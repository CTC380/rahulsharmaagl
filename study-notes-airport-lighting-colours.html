2

RUNWAY LIGHTING COLOURS

Runway edge

Normally white. On instrument runways, FAA guidance uses yellow / amber in the caution zone at the far end, replacing white for the last 2,000 ft or half the runway length, whichever is less.

Threshold and end

The lights at the runway end are two-colour. They emit green outward from the runway end, marking the threshold for landing aircraft, and red toward the runway, marking the end of the runway for a departing aircraft.

ApproachGreen thresholdWhite runway lightingYellow caution zone (edge)Red runway end

Runway centreline lights (FAA sequence)

One of the most common exam questions. FAA AIM describes it as seen from the landing threshold: white, then alternating white / red, then red. The distances below are runway remaining.

PositionColour
More than 3,000 ft remainingWhite
3,000 ft to 1,000 ft remaining (a 2,000 ft zone)Alternating white / red
Last 1,000 ftRed
WhiteWhite + red alternatingRed

Touchdown zone lights are steady-burning white, in two rows of transverse light bars either side of the centreline. They start 100 ft beyond the landing threshold and extend to 3,000 ft beyond it, or to the runway midpoint, whichever is less.

FAA vs ICAO caution zones (Annex 14)

3

TAXIWAY LIGHTING COLOURS

Edge = Blue

Defines the lateral edge of the taxiway.

Centreline = Green

Steady-burning green giving taxi guidance, particularly in low visibility.

Lead-on / lead-off lights

Lead-off lights guide aircraft exiting the runway and lead-on lights guide aircraft entering it. Both alternate green and yellow, starting with green at the runway centreline, to warn that the aircraft is within the runway environment or ILS critical area. The pattern ends one light position beyond the holding position.

This is FAA usage. In ICAO and UK usage, “lead-on lights” usually means the steady green centreline lights that lead an aircraft from a stop bar onto the runway, so confirm which standard a question uses (see Note 03).

4

HOLDING POSITION LIGHTS

Clearance bar

Three steady-burning in-pavement yellow lights that increase conspicuity of a holding position or mark a taxiway intersection in applicable locations.

Runway guard lights (RGL)

Enhance the conspicuity of a runway/taxiway intersection, especially in low visibility. The FAA describes elevated (a pair of flashing yellow lights) and in-pavement (alternately flashing yellow) configurations.

Stop bar

Red, steady-burning, unidirectional in-pavement lights across the taxiway at the runway holding position, with elevated red lights on each side where provided. Controlled stop bars are coordinated with taxiway centreline lead-on lights.

PAPI COLOURS

PAPI uses white and red to show whether the aircraft is above, on or below the intended approach path. The pilot sees the visual output, not the internal lamp or filter technology.

DisplayMeaning
4 whiteToo high
3 white + 1 redSlightly high
2 white + 2 redOn path
1 white + 3 redSlightly low
4 redToo low
Memory
  • More white = high
  • 2W + 2R = on slope
  • More red = low

APAPI (two units) uses the same colours: 2 white = high, 1 white + 1 red = on path, 2 red = low.

See PAPI & APAPI for aiming and installation.

6

APPROACH LIGHTING AND REIL

Approach lighting

The general FAA approach-lighting system uses white for primary guidance. Certain CAT II/III configurations include red side-row elements, providing runway alignment, height perception, roll guidance and horizontal reference. The approach ends at the green threshold lights. ICAO CAT II/III systems also use red side-row barrettes in the inner section; check Annex 14 for the exact extent.

REIL

Runway End Identifier Lights are white flashing lights used to identify a runway end or threshold. They may be unidirectional (facing the approach) or omnidirectional.

7

LIGHTS VS MARKINGS VS SIGNS

Do not apply the light-colour chart to markings or signs.

ItemTypical FAA colour
Runway markingsWhite
Taxiway and holding-position markingsYellow
Mandatory signWhite inscription on red background
Location signYellow inscription on black background
Direction / destination signBlack inscription on yellow background
Runway distance remaining signWhite numeral on black background

Sign colours are written as inscription on background, per FAA AC 150/5340-18H (issued September 2024). Always read the background colour first: red = mandatory, black = location, yellow = direction, destination and information.

8

EXAM TRAPS

Colour alone is never enough. Always ask: what colour, where is it, and what is its function?

Red is not always "stop"

Red can mean runway end, stop bar, FAA runway entrance lights, a PAPI low indication or CAT II/III approach elements. Location and function decide.

Yellow is not automatically taxiway edge

Yellow can be runway caution-zone edge lights, clearance bars, runway guard lights or lead-on/lead-off elements. Taxiway edge is blue.

Blue vs green

Blue = taxiway edge. Green = taxiway centreline. The most important colour pair.

Green is not just "taxiway"

Green is both the runway threshold (from the approach) and the taxiway centreline. It is not the runway centreline.

Threshold vs centreline

A green threshold does not mean a green runway centreline. Runway centreline is white, with the specified colour transition toward the end.

Stop bar vs RGL

Stop bar red = STOP. Runway guard light yellow = warning.

Exam & Interview

RAPID-FIRE QUESTIONS

What colour are runway edge lights?

White, with the specified yellow/amber caution-zone section on applicable instrument runways.

What colour identifies a runway threshold?

Green.

What colour identifies a runway end?

Red.

What colour are runway centreline lights?

Primarily white, then alternating white/red and then red toward the runway end under the FAA sequence.

What colour are touchdown-zone lights?

White.

What colour are taxiway edge lights?

Blue.

What colour are taxiway centreline lights?

Green.

What colour is a stop bar?

Red.

What colour are runway guard lights?

Flashing yellow.

What colour are clearance-bar lights?

Yellow.

What colours does PAPI use?

White and red.

What colour are runway markings?

White.

What colour are taxiway markings?

Yellow.

What does a red stop bar mean?

Stop.

What does blue taxiway edge mean?

Taxiway lateral boundary.

What colours do FAA lead-on / lead-off lights use?

Alternating green and yellow, starting with green at the runway centreline.

What colour are runway entrance lights in the FAA RWSL system?

Red: it is unsafe to enter or cross the runway.

Final Study Takeaway

FINAL EXAM MEMORY

Runway is white, threshold is green, runway end is red. Taxiway edge is blue, taxiway centreline is green. Stop bar is red; runway guard and clearance bar are yellow. PAPI is white + red. The runway centreline goes white → white/red → red. Always confirm the exact application, directionality and colour transitions against the applicable fixture, system and current standard.

ENGINEERING & EXPERT NOTES

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist

Colour Meaning Depends on Position and Pattern

Principle

Learn the colour together with where it appears and what it marks. The same colour can mean different things in different places.

Check Colour From the Pilot's Viewing Direction

Inspection

A light can show a different colour or none from the opposite direction. Verify from the intended view.

Measure Chromaticity, Don't Trust the Eye

Verification

LED colour can drift. Use an instrument for acceptance and periodic checks.

Use Approved Filters and Lenses Only

Spares

A generic coloured part can change both colour and intensity. Replace with the approved part for that fixture.
