Asset Health Monitoring

The condition-intelligence and health-management layer of the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide. Division 07 establishes the commissioning baseline, Division 08 monitors operational performance, and Division 09 establishes the maintenance strategy — Division 10 brings these information streams together to determine the current health of each AGL asset, how fast it is deteriorating, and what action may be required before performance or reliability becomes unacceptable.

HLT-01 Asset Condition Assessment
Purpose

Determines the current physical and technical condition of an AGL asset. It should not rely on one source of information alone.

Assessment Inputs
  • Visual inspection
  • Electrical testing
  • Photometric testing
  • Functional testing
  • Maintenance findings
  • Failure history
  • Operating performance
  • Environmental exposure
  • Age
  • Previous condition assessments
Assess Areas Such As

Physical condition — damage, corrosion, contamination, mechanical deterioration.

Electrical condition — insulation, connections, electrical performance, earthing.

Optical condition — light output, lens condition, alignment, photometric performance.

Operational condition — availability, functional performance, control response, alarm performance.

HLT-02 Asset Health Indicators
Purpose

Asset Health Indicators (AHIs) are measurable parameters used to determine whether an asset is healthy, deteriorating, or approaching an unacceptable condition.

Possible AGL Indicators
  • Inspection condition
  • Electrical test result
  • Photometric result
  • Failure frequency
  • Repeat failures
  • Operating hours
  • Availability
  • Energy performance
  • Maintenance frequency
  • Age
  • Performance deviation from baseline
Principle

An asset health indicator should provide evidence about the current or developing condition of the asset.

HLT-03 Health Scoring
Purpose

Health scoring converts multiple condition/performance inputs into a structured assessment. The exact definitions and calculation method should be formally approved by the airport.

Example Scoring System
  • 5 — Excellent
  • 4 — Good
  • 3 — Fair
  • 2 — Poor
  • 1 — Critical
Scoring Factors Could Include
  • Condition
  • Test results
  • Performance
  • Failures
  • Maintenance history
  • Age
  • Degradation
Important

A health score should not replace the underlying evidence. The supporting inspection and test records should remain available.

HLT-04 Asset Health Index
Purpose

The Asset Health Index (AHI) provides a consolidated representation of asset health. The exact weighting should be defined through the airport's approved asset-health methodology.

Conceptual Inputs
InspectionTestingPerformanceFailure HistoryMaintenance HistoryDegradationAsset Health Index
Important Distinction — Criticality ≠ Health

An asset may be highly critical but healthy, highly critical and unhealthy, low criticality but unhealthy, or low criticality and healthy. Division 03 determines how important the asset is; Division 10 determines how healthy the asset currently is.

HLT-07 Maintenance History
Purpose

Maintenance history provides another important indicator of asset health.

Analyze
  • Number of maintenance events
  • Preventive maintenance
  • Corrective maintenance
  • Emergency maintenance
  • Component replacements
  • Repeat interventions
  • Maintenance frequency
  • Maintenance cost
  • Recurring defects
Example

An asset requiring increasingly frequent intervention may indicate deterioration even if it has not yet experienced complete failure.

HLT-08 Failure History
Purpose

Failure history should be incorporated into health assessment.

Track
  • Failure date
  • Failure mode
  • Failure cause
  • Failure frequency
  • Repair
  • Replacement
  • Downtime
  • Repeat failure
  • Consequence
Important

A single failure does not necessarily mean poor health. However, repeated failures, increasing maintenance, and performance degradation together may indicate a deteriorating asset. This is where Division 10 should connect to the Failure Records & Reliability Engineering framework.

HLT-09 Performance Trends
Purpose

Performance trends compare current performance against historical and baseline values.

Possible Trends
  • CCR performance
  • Electrical performance
  • Photometric performance
  • Availability
  • Energy consumption
  • Operating hours
  • Control performance
  • Alarm frequency
Example
Baseline — NormalSlight DeviationIncreasing DeviationSignificant DeviationHealth Alert

This provides an early-warning mechanism before complete functional failure.

HLT-10 Condition Trends
Purpose

Condition trends track the physical/technical condition of the asset over time.

Example
  • Commissioning — Excellent
  • Year 1 — Excellent
  • Year 2 — Good
  • Year 3 — Good
  • Year 4 — Fair
  • Year 5 — Poor
The Trend Can Then Trigger
  • Detailed inspection
  • Additional testing
  • Maintenance
  • Rehabilitation
  • Replacement assessment

HLT-12 Remaining Useful Life
Purpose

Remaining Useful Life (RUL) represents an estimate of how much useful operational life remains before the asset reaches a defined end-of-life condition or requires major intervention.

RUL Should Consider
  • Current condition
  • Age
  • Operating hours
  • Failure history
  • Performance degradation
  • Maintenance history
  • Environmental exposure
  • Manufacturer/service-life information
  • Criticality
  • Future operating requirements
Important

RUL is an estimate, not a guaranteed replacement date. An asset may have an estimated RUL of five years, but a sudden failure or accelerated deterioration can change that estimate.

HLT-13 RUL Estimation
Purpose

RUL estimation should progressively improve as lifecycle data becomes available.

Initial Estimate (At Commissioning)
Expected Service LifeOperating AssumptionsInitial RUL Estimate
Operational Estimate (Later)
Condition DataOperating HoursFailure HistoryPerformance TrendUpdated RUL
Why It's Stronger

This is much stronger than simply using RUL = Design Life − Age, because actual operating condition may differ substantially from the original assumptions.

HLT-14 Health-Status Classification
Purpose

Each asset should have a standardized health status. The exact classification thresholds should be defined and approved rather than assumed.

A Practical Framework
  • Healthy — normal condition, normal performance, no significant deterioration, routine monitoring.
  • Good — minor deterioration, normal operational performance, no immediate intervention.
  • Watch — detectable deterioration, trend requires monitoring, additional inspection/testing may be appropriate.
  • Poor — significant deterioration, maintenance/rehabilitation planning required.
  • Critical — serious condition/performance issue, significant operational or safety concern, immediate engineering decision required.
HLT-15 Asset-Health Dashboard
Purpose

The asset-health dashboard should provide a high-level view of the airport's AGL asset portfolio. The actual data should come from the controlled asset-management system.

Recommended Dashboard Fields
  • CCR-01 — High criticality, Healthy, Good condition, High availability, 0 failures → Monitor
  • PAPI-01 — Critical criticality, Watch, Fair condition, High availability, 1 failure → Inspect
  • RWY Fixture-01 — High criticality, Poor, Poor condition, Reduced availability, 3 failures → Plan intervention
HLT-16 Critical Asset Monitoring
Purpose

Critical assets should receive enhanced health monitoring. This links directly with Division 03.

Critical Assets May Include
  • Runway lighting assets
  • PAPI
  • Critical CCRs
  • Critical series circuits
  • Critical cable sections
  • Critical ALCMS components
  • Other assets identified through the airport's approved criticality assessment
Monitoring Intensity — Criticality × Health

High Criticality + Poor Health → Immediate attention.

High Criticality + Healthy → Enhanced monitoring.

Low Criticality + Poor Health → Risk-based intervention.

HLT-17 Asset Health Alerts
Purpose

Health alerts identify assets requiring attention.

Alert Triggers May Include
  • Health score below threshold
  • Rapid health deterioration
  • Significant baseline deviation
  • Repeated failures
  • Reduced availability
  • Abnormal energy consumption
  • Poor inspection result
  • Failed test
  • Increasing maintenance frequency
  • RUL below defined planning threshold
Alert Levels
  • Information — normal monitoring.
  • Warning — deterioration detected.
  • High — engineering review required.
  • Critical — immediate action/operational assessment required.
HLT-18 Health-Index Review
Purpose

The health index should be periodically reviewed rather than treated as a permanent number.

Review Inputs
  • New inspection results
  • New test results
  • New failures
  • Maintenance history
  • Performance trends
  • Operating hours
  • Utilization
  • RUL estimate
  • Changes in asset criticality
Review Outcomes
  • Health score unchanged
  • Health score improved
  • Health score reduced
  • Monitoring increased
  • Maintenance required
  • Rehabilitation required
  • Replacement planning required

Most Important Design Principle

Asset health should be determined from evidence accumulated throughout the asset lifecycle — not from age alone. Division 10 takes the raw lifecycle evidence generated by Divisions 02–09 and converts it into a current health status, a deterioration trend, and a forward-looking RUL assessment.

This is the point where the Asset Lifecycle Guide starts becoming a true condition-based asset-management system rather than simply a collection of asset records.

Division 02 — Asset Register
Division 03 — Criticality & Risk
Division 06–07 — Installation & Commissioning Baseline
Division 08–09 — Operational Performance & Maintenance History
Division 10 — Asset Health Index → Degradation → RUL → Health Alert
Division 11 onward — Renewal / Replacement / Capital Planning

Division 03 and Division 10 remain deliberately distinct: criticality tells you how important the asset is, while health tells you how well the asset is doing — the combination drives the engineering decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Asset Health Monitoring in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide.

Q1What is Division 10 — Asset Health Monitoring in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

Division 10 is the condition-intelligence layer. It covers 18 topics on condition assessment, health indicators, health scoring, the asset health index, inspection and testing history, degradation monitoring, RUL estimation and the asset-health dashboard.

Q2What is asset condition assessment?

It determines the current physical and technical condition of an AGL asset and should not rely on one source of information alone. Inputs include visual inspection, electrical, photometric and functional testing, maintenance findings and failure history.

Q3What are asset health indicators?

Asset Health Indicators (AHIs) are measurable parameters used to determine whether an asset is healthy, deteriorating or approaching an unacceptable condition. Examples include inspection condition, electrical and photometric results, failure frequency and repeat failures.

Q4How does health scoring work?

Health scoring converts multiple condition and performance inputs into a structured assessment, for example on a five-level scale from 5 (Excellent) to 1 (Critical). The exact definitions and calculation method should be formally approved by the organization.

Q5What is the Asset Health Index?

The Asset Health Index provides a consolidated representation of asset health. The exact weighting should be defined through the airport's approved asset-health methodology, and the index should be reviewed periodically rather than treated as a permanent number.

Q6Which records feed asset health?

Inspection results, testing history, maintenance history and failure history all feed the health assessment. Performance trends and condition trends compare current values against historical and baseline values.

Q7What is Remaining Useful Life (RUL)?

RUL is an estimate of how much useful operational life remains before the asset reaches a defined end-of-life condition or requires major intervention. It draws on current condition, age, operating hours, failure history and performance degradation, and estimation should improve as lifecycle data becomes available.

Q8How are health dashboards and alerts used?

The asset-health dashboard gives a high-level view of the airport's AGL portfolio, with data drawn from the controlled asset-management system. Health alerts flag assets needing attention, such as a score below threshold, rapid deterioration, significant baseline deviation or repeated failures, and critical assets get enhanced monitoring.
