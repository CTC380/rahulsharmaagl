Product Overview

Runway Edge Light

Runway edge lights are installed in two approximately parallel rows along the runway edges to provide a continuous visual definition of the runway lateral limits. ICAO Annex 14 requires runway edge lights on a runway intended for night use and on a precision approach runway for day or night use. They are placed along the edges of the runway area, or outside it by not more than 3 m. Confirm the clause and edition for the project. Depending on the application, the fixture may be configured as:

  • Elevated runway edge light
  • Base-mounted elevated runway edge light
  • Stake-mounted elevated runway edge light
  • Inset / in-pavement runway edge light
  • Unidirectional configuration
  • Bidirectional configuration
  • LED light source
  • Halogen/incandescent configuration where specified
  • Fixed-intensity or multi-intensity configuration
  • Series-circuit compatible configuration

For instrument runways, the lighting arrangement and colour configuration are particularly important because runway edge lighting works together with the runway centreline, threshold/end, touchdown-zone and approach lighting systems to provide a coherent visual reference to the flight crew.

The final fixture configuration should always be selected against the approved runway lighting design, photometric requirements, electrical circuit characteristics and project technical specification.

Primary Applications

06 — Inset / In-PavementWhere an elevated fixture would create an unacceptable obstruction or the runway design requires a flush pavement installation.
Runway Edge Light Function

What the Fixture Must Do

Primary Functions

  • Define the lateral limits of the runway.
  • Provide continuous visual runway-edge guidance.
  • Assist pilots during take-off and landing.
  • Improve runway recognition during reduced visibility.
  • Provide visual reference during night operations.
  • Support the overall runway lighting pattern.
  • Maintain the required colour and photometric characteristics.
  • Operate reliably as part of the airport series-circuit lighting system.

A System, Not an Isolated Product

Runway edge lights should not be considered as an isolated lighting product. Their performance depends on the complete AGL system, including the CCR, primary circuit, isolating transformers, connectors, secondary wiring, fixture installation, optical alignment and control system.

Configuration

Elevated and Inset Runway Edge Lights

Elevated Runway Edge Light

Elevated fixtures are installed above the pavement surface using an appropriate mounting arrangement. They provide an easily identifiable visual reference and are commonly used where the runway design permits above-ground fixtures.

Typical configurations

  • Stake mounted
  • Base mounted
  • Frangible support arrangement
  • Single fixture
  • Bidirectional fixture
  • Multi-directional optical arrangement where specified

Elevated lights on the movement area must be frangible and low enough to keep clearance for propellers and engine pods of jet aircraft (ICAO Annex 14). Use the fixture manufacturer's certified frangible coupling and mounting method.

Inset / In-Pavement Runway Edge Light

Inset fixtures are installed flush or substantially flush with the pavement. They are selected where:

  • Pavement clearance is critical.
  • Elevated fixtures are undesirable.
  • The runway design requires in-pavement installation.
  • Aircraft wheel clearance must be maintained.
  • High mechanical protection is required.
  • The approved design specifies an inset fixture.

Installation requires attention to

  • Base-can compatibility
  • Fixture orientation
  • Torque requirements
  • Pavement interface
  • Drainage
  • Cable connection
  • Transformer installation
  • Water ingress protection
  • Optical cleanliness
  • Maintenance access

Light Colour

Specify the Colour Configuration, Not Just "White"

The runway edge light colour arrangement shall be selected according to the applicable aerodrome standard and runway configuration.

Under ICAO Annex 14, Volume I, runway edge lights are fixed lights showing variable white, except that they show red in the approach direction between the runway start and a displaced threshold, and a section at the remote end of the runway may show yellow, covering 600 m or one-third of the runway length, whichever is less.

FAA practice differs: on instrument runways yellow replaces white on the last 2,000 ft (610 m) or half the runway length, whichever is less. Use the zone length that matches the governing standard for the project.

The product should therefore not simply be specified as "white runway edge light". The required optical and colour configuration should be established from the runway direction, threshold arrangement and approved lighting design.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices, transformers or fixtures
  • Do not disconnect or reconnect a fixture's secondary connector while the series circuit is energised: an open secondary on a series isolating transformer can develop hazardous voltage and damage the transformer
  • Work on the movement area only under the airport's permit-to-work system, with the runway or taxiway closed as required, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures

FAQ

Runway Edge Light — Frequently Asked Questions

What are runway edge lights and where are they installed?

Runway edge lights are installed in two approximately parallel rows along the runway edges to give continuous visual definition of the runway's lateral limits. ICAO Annex 14 requires them on a runway intended for night use and on a precision approach runway for day or night use, placed along the edges of the runway area or outside it by not more than 3 m. Confirm the clause and edition for the project.

What is the spacing of runway edge lights?

ICAO Annex 14 specifies a spacing of not more than 60 m for an instrument runway and 100 m for a non-instrument runway. FAA practice limits spacing of medium- and high-intensity runway edge lights to 200 ft (61 m). All values are subject to the applicable provisions and the project design.

What colour are runway edge lights?

Under ICAO Annex 14, Volume I, runway edge lights are fixed lights showing variable white, except that they show red in the approach direction between the runway start and a displaced threshold, and a section at the remote end of the runway may show yellow, covering 600 m or one-third of the runway length, whichever is less. FAA practice differs: on instrument runways yellow replaces white on the last 2,000 ft (610 m) or half the runway length, whichever is less. Specify the colour configuration for the governing standard rather than just white.

What is the difference between elevated and inset runway edge lights?

Elevated fixtures sit above the pavement on a stake, base or frangible support, are easy to see and generally easier to maintain, but need aircraft clearance to be considered. Inset fixtures are installed flush with the pavement in a base can, selected where pavement clearance is critical or the approved design specifies in-pavement installation; they have lower mechanical exposure but need attention to drainage, sealing and pavement interface.

Do elevated runway edge lights need to be frangible?

Yes. Elevated lights on the movement area must be frangible and low enough to keep clearance for propellers and engine pods of jet aircraft (ICAO Annex 14). Use the fixture manufacturer's certified frangible coupling and mounting method.

Should I choose LED or halogen runway edge lights?

LED can offer long service life, lower maintenance, consistent optical performance, reduced energy consumption, rapid switching and good compatibility with modern AGL control systems, where specified by the project. Halogen or incandescent versions may be required by the specification or when existing infrastructure is being maintained; consider lamp rating and life, optical system, current setting, transformer and circuit compatibility, and spare-parts availability.

What are FAA L-861, L-862 and L-850C?

FAA AC 150/5340-30J identifies elevated runway edge-light classifications including L-861 (medium intensity) and L-862 (high intensity, typically precision runways), and inset runway edge lights are L-850C. The fixture specification is FAA AC 150/5345-46F. Verify the applicable edition before procurement.

How are runway edge lights connected to the AGL series circuit?

In a conventional series circuit each runway edge light is normally supplied through its own isolating transformer: constant current regulator, primary AGL cable, primary connector, isolating transformer, secondary connector, then the light. The fixture should be specified as part of the complete AGL circuit rather than as an independent electrical load.

What should be checked for series-circuit compatibility before procurement?

Confirm the CCR output current, circuit voltage, transformer rating, fixture electrical input, number of fixtures, cable length, circuit resistance and inductance, connector system, required intensity steps, ALCMS interface and monitoring requirements. This prevents an otherwise suitable fixture from being incorrectly selected for the existing circuit.

What are the common runway edge light faults and how are they troubleshot?

A light that is off may be due to lamp or LED failure, a secondary or primary connector problem, isolating transformer failure or an open series circuit. Other symptoms include low intensity, wrong colour, wrong direction, intermittent operation, water ingress and uneven lighting. Work along the circuit from the CCR, primary circuit, primary connector, isolating transformer, secondary connector, fixture input, lamp or LED, optics and alignment, rather than replacing a light when the fault is in the connector, transformer, cable or circuit. A light is not fully restored merely because it is illuminated: alignment, intensity, colour, electrical connection and physical condition must also be right. See the electrical safety warning before working on any circuit.

What information is needed to request a runway edge light quotation?

Provide the airport name, runway designation, length, width, classification and approach category, elevated or inset requirement, number of fixtures, spacing, required light colour, uni- or bidirectional requirement, LED or halogen requirement, CCR current, existing transformer rating and connector arrangement, base-can and cable details, ALCMS or control requirements, required certifications, project specification and delivery requirement.
