One of the most important decision-making divisions in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide. It converts everything established in the previous divisions — age, condition, health, failure history, reliability, criticality, risk, performance, obsolescence, lifecycle cost and safety — into a single defensible lifecycle decision: continue, repair, refurbish, upgrade, or replace.

REP-01 Asset End-of-Life Assessment
Purpose

Determines whether an AGL asset is approaching or has reached the point where continued operation is no longer the preferred lifecycle strategy. End-of-life should not be determined from age alone.

Should Assess
  • Asset age
  • Original expected service life
  • Current condition
  • Condition trend
  • Failure history
  • MTBF / MTTR
  • Asset health
  • Remaining Useful Life
  • Criticality
  • Performance
  • Obsolescence
  • Spare availability
  • Manufacturer support
  • Maintenance cost
  • Lifecycle cost
  • Safety risk
End-of-Life Condition May Be Reached When
  • Technical performance is no longer acceptable
  • Reliability has deteriorated significantly
  • Critical components are no longer supportable
  • Safety risk becomes unacceptable
  • Maintenance cost becomes excessive
  • Required performance cannot be maintained
  • Technology becomes unsupported
  • Replacement becomes more economical
REP-02 Replacement Triggers
Purpose

Defines the circumstances that should initiate a formal replacement assessment.

Potential Triggers
  • End of expected service life
  • Severe condition deterioration
  • Repeated failures / increasing failure frequency
  • Unacceptable risk or safety concern
  • Regulatory requirement
  • Persistent performance degradation
  • Obsolescence
  • Lack of spare parts
  • Manufacturer support withdrawal
  • Excessive maintenance or energy cost
  • Technology limitations
  • Major airport expansion
  • Incompatibility with future systems
Important Principle

A replacement trigger should initiate an assessment — not automatically mean immediate replacement.

REP-03 Age-Based Replacement
Purpose

Age can be used as an initial screening parameter, not a sole determinant of replacement.

Consider
  • Installation date
  • Commissioning date
  • Expected service life
  • Operating hours
  • Environmental exposure
  • Actual condition
  • Failure history
Screening Sequence
ASSET AGE REACHES EXPECTED SERVICE LIFECONDITION ASSESSMENTRELIABILITY REVIEWRUL ASSESSMENTREPLACEMENT DECISION
Scope Boundary

An asset reaching its nominal design life should trigger a review. It should not automatically be declared unserviceable.

REP-04 Condition-Based Replacement
Purpose

Condition-based replacement is driven by physical and functional deterioration.

Indicators
  • Poor condition grade
  • Severe corrosion
  • Electrical deterioration
  • Structural deterioration
  • Optical degradation
  • Repeated defects
  • Moisture-related deterioration
  • Reduced performance
  • Health-index deterioration
Decision Logic
ConditionAction
AcceptableContinue
DeterioratingMonitor / repair
PoorRehabilitation / replacement assessment
CriticalImmediate intervention assessment
REP-05 Reliability-Based Replacement
Purpose

Reliability-based replacement considers whether continued operation has become unreliable.

Analyze
  • Failure rate
  • MTBF
  • MTTR
  • Repeat failures
  • Failure modes
  • Failure trend
  • Maintenance frequency
  • Unplanned outages
Example

An asset may be physically acceptable but experience repeated failures. In that situation, reliability deterioration may justify replacement even when physical condition alone does not.

REP-06 Risk-Based Replacement
Purpose

Risk-based replacement combines probability of failure and consequence of failure to determine lifecycle risk.

Basic Concept
Risk = Probability of Failure × Consequence of Failure
Priority Principle

High-risk assets should receive greater replacement priority where risk cannot be adequately controlled through maintenance, inspection, monitoring, spare strategy, refurbishment or other mitigation.

REP-07 Performance-Based Replacement
Purpose

An asset may require replacement when it can no longer provide the required performance.

Assess
  • Functional performance
  • Photometric performance
  • Electrical performance
  • Availability
  • Energy performance
  • Control performance
  • Monitoring capability
  • Operational response
AGL Examples
  • Fixture no longer achieves required optical performance
  • CCR cannot provide required operating characteristics
  • PAPI performance cannot be maintained
  • ALCMS cannot support required control/monitoring functions

REP-10 Safety-Based Replacement
Purpose

Safety should receive the highest consideration where continued operation creates unacceptable risk.

Potential Triggers
  • Structural deterioration
  • Electrical safety concerns
  • Critical equipment degradation
  • Repeated dangerous failures
  • Loss of required performance
  • Inability to maintain required safety function
Principle
Unacceptable safety risk overrides economic justification
Scope Boundary

The specific safety and regulatory requirements should be determined from the applicable airport, regulatory and engineering standards.

REP-13 Replace-versus-Repair
Purpose

Repair should be compared with replacement when repeated failures or deterioration occur.

Repair Option — Assess
  • Repair cost
  • Expected repair life
  • Reliability after repair
  • Spare availability
  • Future failures
  • Downtime
  • Maintenance burden
Replacement Option — Assess
  • Replacement cost
  • New service life
  • Reliability
  • Energy
  • Maintenance
  • Technology
  • Warranty
  • Future support
Decision Principle

Repair is preferable when it provides acceptable performance, reliability, risk and lifecycle value.

Replacement Priority Model

A useful strategic approach combines five major dimensions with lifecycle economics to prioritize replacement across the whole asset population — not just asset-by-asset.

1 · Safety
How serious is the consequence of failure?
2 · Criticality
How important is the asset to airport operations?
3 · Condition
How deteriorated is the asset?
4 · Reliability
How frequently is it failing?
5 · Supportability
Can the asset still be supported?
6 · Lifecycle Economics
Is replacement financially preferable?

Replacement Portfolio View

Replacement should not be managed only asset-by-asset — the airport should also look at asset populations sharing common risk.

Runway Fixtures

If hundreds of fixtures use an obsolete technology, the airport may need a portfolio replacement programme rather than replacing individual fixtures after failure.

CCRs

If multiple CCRs use the same obsolete control platform, a coordinated modernization programme may be more economical.

ALCMS

If the control system is approaching end-of-support, replacement should be planned before failure — not after the system becomes unavailable.

Most Important Design Principle

Division 19 should remain the replacement decision gateway. It integrates asset age, condition, health, failure history, reliability, criticality, risk, performance, obsolescence, lifecycle cost, safety and operational requirements — converting them into a single defensible Maintain / Repair / Refurbish / Upgrade / Replace decision. It does not hold the underlying condition data (Division 15), the reliability engineering (Division 11), or the lifecycle-cost methodology (Division 12) — it draws on all of them.

Replacement should be a planned, evidence-based lifecycle decision — not simply a response to asset failure. Economic considerations should never be used to justify continued operation of an asset presenting unacceptable safety risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Replacement Planning & Decision Making in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide.

Q1What is Division 19 — Replacement Planning & Decision Making in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

Division 19 is the replacement decision gateway. It covers 20 topics on end-of-life assessment, replacement triggers, age/condition/reliability/risk/performance/obsolescence/cost/safety-based replacement, priority ranking, decision matrix, repair vs refurbish vs replace, budgeting, forecasting, business case, approval and execution.

Q2What is an asset end-of-life assessment?

It determines whether an AGL asset is approaching or has reached the point where continued operation is no longer the preferred lifecycle strategy. It considers asset age, original expected service life and current condition.

Q3What triggers a replacement assessment?

Replacement triggers define the circumstances that should start a formal assessment, such as end of expected service life, severe condition deterioration and repeated or increasing failures.

Q4Is age enough to decide on replacement?

No. Age can be used as an initial screening parameter but not as the sole determinant of replacement, and it is considered together with operating hours, environmental exposure and actual condition.

Q5Which replacement drivers does Division 19 cover?

It covers condition-based, reliability-based, risk-based, performance-based, obsolescence-based, cost-based and safety-based replacement. Safety should receive the highest consideration where continued operation creates unacceptable risk.

Q6How are replacement priorities and decisions made objectively?

Not every asset can be replaced at once, so a replacement priority ranking should weigh safety, operational and regulatory criticality, condition and failure history. A standard replacement decision matrix should be used to avoid subjective decisions.

Q7How do repair, refurbish and replace compare?

Repair should be compared with replacement when repeated failures or deterioration occur, looking at repair cost, expected repair life, reliability after repair and downtime. Replace-versus-refurbish is especially important for aging AGL systems, comparing cost, extended life, performance, reliability and supportability.

Q8How are replacements budgeted, approved and executed?

Replacement decisions must be converted into financial plans, backed by a multi-year replacement programme and forecasting. Major replacements need a formal business case, a controlled approval process, and replacement execution converts the approved decision into an operational asset.
