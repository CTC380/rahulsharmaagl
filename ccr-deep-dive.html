Constant Current Regulators (CCR) — Deep Dive

Control the Current Control the Circuit Control the Light

A Constant Current Regulator is one of the most important electrical components in an Airfield Ground Lighting system. It supplies and controls the current in the airport series circuit so that connected lighting equipment operates at the intended intensity levels — the controlled electrical source at the beginning of the AGL series circuit, and the interface between the airport electrical supply, the series circuit, the isolation transformers and the individual light fixtures.

Overview

What Is a Constant Current Regulator?

A Constant Current Regulator (CCR) is an electrical power-control device designed to maintain a controlled output current for an AGL series circuit.

Unlike a conventional voltage source, the CCR regulates current as the primary controlled output parameter. A CCR is not simply a power supply — it is a controlled current-regulating system, and the internal technology used to achieve that regulation can vary by design.

The FAA currently lists AC 150/5345-10H as the active specification for Constant Current Regulators and Regulator Monitors.

Working Principle

CCR Working Principle: How Does a CCR Work in Airport Lighting?

The CCR airport lighting working principle comes down to one idea: sense the output current and adjust the voltage to hold it at the target for the selected brightness step.

1. AC Input

The CCR takes its power from the airfield AC supply. The input can vary, and the CCR is designed to keep its output steady regardless.

2. Brightness Step Reference

A local or remote control command selects a brightness step. Each step corresponds to a defined target output current.

3. Regulation

The CCR adjusts its output voltage so that the current stays at the target, whatever the load on the circuit happens to be.

4. Series Loop Output

The regulated current flows around the series loop, so the same current passes through every isolation transformer on the circuit.

5. Feedback

The CCR continuously measures its output current and compares it with the target, correcting any difference.

6. Protection & Monitoring

The CCR detects abnormal conditions such as open circuit, overcurrent, earth fault or overtemperature, and reports them to the operator.

Fundamental Principle

Why Does AGL Use Constant Current?

AGL series circuits may contain a large number of lighting fixtures distributed over long distances. The electrical characteristics of the circuit can change because of the number of fixtures, transformer characteristics, cable impedance, cable length, temperature, fixture condition, open circuits, short circuits, insulation deterioration and component failures. A conventional constant-voltage supply would not provide the same controlled current behaviour under these changing conditions — so the CCR is designed to regulate the series-circuit current instead.

Common Misunderstanding

A CCR Is Not a Conventional Transformer

"A CCR is just a transformer that supplies the runway." — It is not.

A CCR is a controlled current-regulating system. Its function is to regulate the output current despite changes in circuit conditions within its designed operating range. The internal technology can vary according to the CCR design, so it should never be assumed solely from the term "CCR."

Internal Technology

Major CCR Technologies

Historically and commercially, CCR technology has included several distinct approaches.

Saturable Reactor Type

Older CCR designs that use magnetic saturation and reactor control principles.

Ferroresonant Type

Designs that use magnetic components and resonance characteristics to regulate the output.

Static / Electronic CCR

Modern regulators using semiconductor power electronics, control electronics and digital monitoring.

Hybrid Designs

Some systems combine magnetic and electronic technologies.

FAA Classification

L-828, L-829 & AC 150/5345-10H

In FAA terminology, L-828 and L-829 are important CCR classifications. The distinction is primarily associated with the regulator and monitoring/control functionality specified for airport lighting applications.

For any project, the exact equipment type, features and certification requirements should be checked against the applicable current FAA specification and project requirements. FAA AC 150/5345-10H is the current active FAA specification covering CCRs and regulator monitors.

FAA Constant Current Regulator

The FAA constant current regulator specification is AC 150/5345-10H, which covers CCRs and regulator monitors. L-828 and L-829 are the FAA classifications. Check the equipment type and certification against the current circular and the project requirements.

Why It Matters

Why Current Control Is Critical

Consider a runway lighting circuit — if the CCR output drifts from its intended level, the consequences run in both directions.

Output Lower Than Intended

  • Fixtures may appear dim
  • Photometric performance may be reduced
  • System visibility may be affected
  • A fault may exist in the regulator or circuit

Output Higher Than Intended

  • Fixture loading may increase
  • Equipment stress may increase
  • Optical output may exceed its intended operating condition
  • Protection or monitoring functions may operate

CCR current verification is an essential part of AGL testing.

Electrical Behaviour

CCR Output Voltage — Current Controlled, Voltage Adapts

The CCR does not simply provide a fixed output voltage. The required output voltage varies with the electrical characteristics of the connected series circuit — as the circuit load changes, the CCR develops the voltage necessary to maintain the commanded current, within its designed operating limits.

Current vs Voltage as Circuit Load Changes

As circuit resistance (more fixtures, longer cable, ageing connections) increases, the CCR raises its output voltage to hold current flat — the opposite of a constant-voltage supply

Water ingress can create a chain reaction: Insulation degradation → Leakage current → Earth fault → Circuit instability → Fixture failure. Short-circuit investigation should therefore begin at the regulator and progress into the field circuit.

Three Fault Types on the Same Loop

Open circuit breaks the current path entirely; short circuit bypasses the load; earth fault leaks current to ground — each produces a different CCR response

Airport Lighting Open Circuit

An airport lighting open circuit breaks the series loop, for example through a broken cable, a disconnected connector or a failed transformer. The CCR may show a high output voltage, trip or give a fault indication. Locate the break by splitting the loop and testing continuity, and never open an energised circuit.

System Interfaces

CCR, Isolation Transformers, Cable & LED Compatibility

The CCR does not normally connect directly to individual AGL lamps or fixtures — the series circuit feeds isolation transformers, which electrically interface the series circuit with the individual fixture load. The CCR, cable and connected equipment must be treated as one electrical system.

CCR & Isolation Transformers

Typical path: CCR → Primary Series Cable → Isolation Transformer → Secondary Cable → Light Fixture. This allows many individual lighting units to be connected into one series circuit.

CCR & Series-Circuit Cable

Important parameters: cable length, resistance, inductive characteristics, number of transformers, transformer loading, circuit configuration, operating current, insulation condition and environmental conditions.

CCR & LED Lighting

LED fixtures changed the electrical behaviour and loading of many AGL systems. An existing CCR should not automatically be assumed compatible with every LED fixture — check current waveform, control behaviour, minimum load, harmonics and power factor.

The correct engineering question is not "Is this CCR good?" — it is "Is this CCR compatible with the complete AGL series-circuit load?"

CCR → Series Cable → Isolation Transformer → Connector → LED / Lamp Fixture — every link in this chain must be checked together.

Electrical Performance

Efficiency, Power Factor & Thermal Management

Efficiency

Affects input power, heat generation, room temperature, energy consumption and cooling requirements. Should be evaluated from actual manufacturer data rather than assumed from CCR type.

Power Factor

Poor power factor can increase input current, distribution losses, transformer loading and electrical infrastructure requirements. Acceptable limits come from the applicable specification.

Thermal Management

CCR losses become heat. An overloaded or poorly ventilated CCR may show overtemperature alarms, output instability, reduced reliability, protective shutdown or premature failure.

What Is Airport Lighting Earth Testing?

Airport lighting earth testing measures how well the installation is connected to earth, so that fault current has a safe path and protective devices and surge protection work as intended.

Airport Lighting Surge Protector

An airport lighting surge protector limits transient overvoltages on the supply and series circuit, protecting the CCR, transformers and control equipment.

Airport Lighting Lightning Arrester

An airport lighting lightning arrester diverts lightning-induced surges to earth. It depends on a low-resistance earth path, so earth testing and surge protection belong together.

Runway Lighting Earth Test & Taxiway Lighting Earth Test

The runway lighting earth test and taxiway lighting earth test check the earthing of each circuit's equipment, so fault current has a safe path and protection works as intended. Record the result with the circuit and compare it with the specification.

Runway Lighting Megger Test & Airport Cable Megger Test

The runway lighting megger test measures the insulation resistance of the runway series circuit to earth, with the circuit isolated and the CCR disconnected first. The airport cable megger test does the same for the primary cable, for example before equipment is connected, and is repeated periodically and after repairs.

Airport Series Circuit Insulation Test

The airport series circuit insulation test measures the insulation of the whole loop to earth. Trend the results over time, because a falling trend can warn of water ingress before it becomes an earth fault.

Isolating Transformer Insulation Test

The isolating transformer insulation test checks the insulation between the primary and secondary windings and from the windings to earth, with the transformer disconnected from the circuit. Test voltage and acceptable values come from the manufacturer and the specification.

Airport Lighting Cable Insulation

Airport lighting cable insulation keeps the series-circuit conductor separate from earth. It degrades through water ingress, damage during installation or repair, ageing and poor connector or splice work, and insulation resistance tests on the primary cable show its condition.

What Is Airport Lighting Insulation Testing?

Airport lighting insulation testing measures the insulation resistance of cables, connectors and transformers, usually with a megger, to confirm they isolate the series circuit from earth. It is done at commissioning, periodically and after repairs, with the circuit isolated and locked out.

Runway Lighting Circuit Resistance

Runway lighting circuit resistance is the resistance of the series loop conductor, measured with the circuit isolated and compared with the commissioning baseline. A rising trend can show degraded connections, which makes the CCR raise its output voltage to hold the current.

Current & Efficiency

CCR Current Setting, Output Current Test & CCR Efficiency

Getting the output current right at each step is what makes the lights perform as designed.

CCR Current Setting

The CCR current setting at each brightness step defines the current in the series loop. It is set and checked at commissioning and after repair, because a wrong setting changes both light output and lamp life.

CCR Output Current Test

A CCR output current test measures the loop current at each step with a calibrated instrument suited to the waveform, and compares it with the specification. Record each step, as in the table below.

Runway Lighting CCR Current

Runway lighting CCR current at each step determines the light output of the runway fixtures. Record it together with photometry, so that optical results can be tied to a known current.

CCR Efficiency

CCR efficiency is the ratio of output power to input power. It varies with loading and technology, and a CCR run well below its rating is generally less efficient. Correct sizing keeps losses and heat down.

Faults

Airport Series Circuit Faults, Earth Faults & CCR Not Working

A structured approach to faults on the series circuit and the CCR itself. See also the troubleshooting engine.

Airport Series Circuit Fault

An airport series circuit fault is an open circuit, a short circuit or an earth fault on the loop. Each gives a different CCR response, as shown in the fault section above.

Earth Fault

An airport series circuit earth fault, also called an airport lighting earth fault, is leakage from the circuit to earth. It is normally flagged by the CCR earth fault indication, and it is usually caused by insulation damage or water ingress.

Airport Series Circuit Troubleshooting

Airport series circuit troubleshooting runs: confirm the CCR status and fault indication, measure output current and voltage, split the loop at accessible points, insulation-test each section, then repair and retest.

CCR Fault Codes

CCR fault codes are specific to each manufacturer. Read them from the display or the control system, record them with the time and step, and look them up in the manual. Typical categories are overcurrent, open circuit, earth fault, overtemperature and input abnormalities.

CCR Not Working Airport

When a CCR is not working at an airport, check the input supply, the local or remote selector, the control command, any protection trip and the output current. Do not repeatedly re-energise a circuit with a suspected earth fault.

Safety First

Isolate, lock out and prove dead before working on any series circuit or CCR. Series circuits carry constant current at potentially dangerous voltage, and an energised circuit must never be opened.

Airport Lighting Cable Fault

An airport lighting cable fault is damage to the primary cable that causes an earth fault, short circuit or open circuit. Common causes are water ingress, mechanical damage from excavation, insulation ageing and poor splices or connectors. Locate it by splitting the loop and insulation-testing each section, then repair and retest.

CCR Overview

CCR in Airfield Lighting: Types, Sizing & Regulator Cabinet

CCR In Airfield Lighting

A CCR in airfield lighting is the regulator at the head of each series circuit. It takes the airport supply and delivers one controlled current to the loop of lights, set by the brightness step selected.

CCR Types For Airport Lighting

CCR types for airport lighting include ferroresonant and thyristor (SCR) designs, and newer solid-state types, in 3-step or 5-step versions and a range of kW ratings. See Major CCR Technologies above for how they differ.

CCR Sizing For AGL

CCR sizing for AGL adds up fixture wattage, isolating transformer losses and cable losses, then selects a rating with margin so the regulator is neither overloaded nor lightly loaded. Check the standard step current for the circuit.

Airport Lighting Regulator Cabinet

An airport lighting regulator cabinet houses the CCR in the substation. It needs clear access, adequate ventilation, a clean dry environment, correct earthing and tidy input and output cable entry.

Constant Current Regulator Working Principle

Constant current regulator working principle: it measures output current and adjusts its output voltage to hold the set current, whatever the load voltage needs, so every light in the series circuit carries the same current.

Constant Current Regulator Types

Constant current regulator types are grouped by technology (ferroresonant, thyristor, solid-state), by number of brightness steps and by rating, and by whether control is local, remote or networked.

Constant Current Regulator Functions

Constant current regulator functions are to hold the circuit current, provide brightness steps, protect the circuit against faults and report status to the control system.

Constant Current Regulator Components

Constant current regulator components include the power transformer or converter, control board, current sensing, brightness-step selector, protection devices, output terminals and control interface.

Specification & Standards

Constant Current Regulator Specification, Design, Requirements & Standards

Constant Current Regulator Specification

A constant current regulator specification states input supply, output current steps, kW rating, efficiency, power factor, protection, control interface and environmental limits, with the standard it must meet.

Constant Current Regulator Requirements

Constant current regulator requirements include accurate current regulation across the load range, protection against open circuit and earth fault, suitable control and monitoring, and compliance with the project standard.

Constant Current Regulator Design

Constant current regulator design for a project sets the rating, quantity, circuit split, redundancy and control interface, based on connected load and the operational needs of the runway or taxiway.

Constant Current Regulator Selection Guide

Constant current regulator selection guide: match rating and steps to the circuit load and standard, choose the technology for efficiency and maintainability, and check control interface compatibility.

Constant Current Regulator Technical Guide

Constant current regulator technical guide: use this page with the manufacturer manual and the circuit design to verify ratings, settings and protection before commissioning.

Constant Current Regulator Standards

Constant current regulator standards include FAA AC 150/5345-10 (L-828 and L-829), IEC 61822, ICAO Annex 14 and national requirements. See the L-828, L-829 section above.

AGL Regulatory Requirements

AGL regulatory requirements for CCRs come from ICAO Annex 14, national regulators such as DGCA in India, the airport operator, and product standards such as IEC 61822 and FAA AC 150/5345-10.

Install & Service

CCR Installation Procedure, Fault Troubleshooting, Testing & Maintenance

CCR Installation Procedure

A CCR installation procedure covers positioning and levelling the cabinet, connecting supply, output and earth cables with correct torque, checking insulation resistance of the output circuit, and verifying settings before energising.

CCR Fault Troubleshooting

CCR fault troubleshooting starts with the displayed alarm or trip, then checks supply, output current and the series circuit. An open circuit, earth fault or overload trip each point to different parts of the circuit; isolate and lock out before testing.

Constant Current Regulator Commissioning

Constant current regulator commissioning confirms correct installation, output current at each step, protection operation, control and monitoring signals, and records the results. See CCR Commissioning above.

Constant Current Regulator Testing Procedure

Constant current regulator testing procedure covers output current at every step, protection trips, control from local and remote, and insulation of the connected circuit, with readings recorded against limits.

Constant Current Regulator Maintenance

Constant current regulator maintenance includes cleaning, checking connections, verifying output current at each step, testing protection and recording results in the maintenance log.

Constant Current Regulator Preventive Maintenance

Constant current regulator preventive maintenance is a scheduled programme of inspection, cleaning, calibration checks and protection tests that keeps the regulator reliable between faults.

Constant Current Regulator Troubleshooting

Constant current regulator troubleshooting separates regulator faults from circuit faults: check supply and alarms first, then measure output current and test the circuit with the CCR isolated.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is a Constant Current Regulator (CCR)?

A CCR is an electrical power-control device designed to maintain a controlled output current for an AGL series circuit. It sits at the beginning of the series circuit as the interface between the airport electrical supply, the series circuit, the isolation transformers and the individual light fixtures. It is a current-regulating system, not simply a power supply.

Q2Why does AGL use constant current?

Series circuits can contain many fixtures over long distances, and their electrical characteristics change with the number of fixtures, transformer characteristics, cable impedance and length, temperature, fixture condition and faults. A conventional constant-voltage supply would not give the same controlled current behaviour under these changes, so the CCR regulates series-circuit current instead.

Q3Is a CCR just a transformer that supplies the runway?

No. A CCR is a controlled current-regulating system whose function is to regulate output current despite changes in circuit conditions within its designed operating range. The internal technology varies by design, so it should not be assumed from the term CCR alone.

Q4What technologies are used in CCRs?

CCR technology has included saturable reactor types (older designs using magnetic saturation), ferroresonant types (magnetic components and resonance), static or electronic CCRs (semiconductor power electronics, control electronics and digital monitoring) and hybrid designs that combine magnetic and electronic technologies.

Q5What are L-828 and L-829, and which FAA specification covers CCRs?

In FAA terminology L-828 and L-829 are important CCR classifications, with the distinction primarily associated with regulator and monitoring or control functionality. The page states that the FAA currently lists AC 150/5345-10H as the active specification for Constant Current Regulators and Regulator Monitors. Equipment type and certification requirements should be checked against the current FAA specification and project requirements.

Q6How does a CCR control light brightness?

AGL intensity is controlled by changing the series-circuit current: higher current gives higher light output and lower current gives lower output. The actual current at each brightness step must come from the applicable CCR, lighting system and project requirements, and the current-to-intensity relationship is equipment-dependent.

Q7Why does CCR output voltage change with the circuit?

A CCR holds current, not voltage. As circuit resistance increases, for example with more fixtures, longer cable or ageing connections, the CCR raises its output voltage to hold current constant, within its designed operating limits.

Q8What happens if CCR output current drifts from its intended level?

If output is lower than intended, fixtures may appear dim, photometric performance may be reduced and a fault may exist in the regulator or circuit. If it is higher, fixture loading and equipment stress may increase, optical output may exceed its intended condition, and protection or monitoring functions may operate. CCR current verification is an essential part of AGL testing.

Q9What are the open-circuit, short-circuit and earth-fault conditions in a CCR circuit?

An open circuit breaks the current path, for example from a broken cable, disconnected connector or failed transformer, and can cause high output voltage, a fault indication or a regulator trip. A short circuit bypasses the load, for example through cable insulation failure or water ingress. An earth fault leaks current to ground. Each produces a different CCR response, and readings should be confirmed against the CCR manufacturer's documentation.

Q10Can an existing CCR be assumed compatible with every LED fixture?

No. LED fixtures changed the electrical behaviour and loading of many AGL systems. Check current waveform, control behaviour, minimum load, harmonics and power factor. The right question is whether the CCR is compatible with the complete AGL series-circuit load.

Q11How does a CCR work in airport lighting?

A CCR (constant current regulator) takes AC power and delivers a regulated current into a series loop of cable and isolation transformers. It senses its output current, compares it with the target for the selected brightness step, and adjusts its output voltage to hold the current constant whatever the load. Protection and monitoring functions detect open circuit, earth fault and similar conditions.

Q12What is airport lighting earth testing?

Airport lighting earth testing measures the resistance of the earth connections of the CCR installation and the associated equipment, so that fault current has a safe path and protective and surge devices work as intended. It is usually done alongside insulation (megger) testing of the series circuit.

Q13How is an airport lighting series circuit designed?

Design starts by listing the fixtures and loads on each circuit, then selecting the CCR rating with spare capacity, routing and sizing the cable, and splitting the lights into circuits by function and zone. Earthing and protection are specified, and the finished circuit is verified by insulation, continuity and current tests.

Q14What do CCR fault codes mean?

CCR fault codes are manufacturer-specific. They normally indicate conditions such as overcurrent, open circuit, earth fault, overtemperature or input abnormalities. Read the code from the display or control system, record the time and brightness step, and look it up in the manufacturer's manual before acting.

Q15What should I check when a CCR is not working at an airport?

Check the input supply, the local or remote selector, the control command, any protection trip and the output current. If an earth fault is suspected, do not repeatedly re-energise the circuit; isolate it, split the loop and insulation-test each section. Always isolate and lock out before touching the circuit.

Q16What is airport lighting insulation testing?

Airport lighting insulation testing measures the insulation resistance of cables, connectors and transformers, usually with a megger, to confirm they isolate the series circuit from earth. It is done at commissioning, periodically and after repairs, with the circuit isolated and locked out, and the results are trended over time.

Q17How to troubleshoot a constant current regulator?

Check the alarm or trip first, then supply voltage and output current. If current is wrong or zero, isolate and lock out the CCR and test the series circuit for open circuit, earth fault or low insulation. Only then suspect the regulator itself.

Q18How to maintain a CCR?

Clean the cabinet and ventilation, check terminals and earthing, verify output current at every brightness step, test protection and control, and record readings so changes over time show up. Follow the manufacturer's schedule.
