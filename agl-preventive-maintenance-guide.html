Airfield Ground Lighting Maintenance: Preventive, Corrective & Periodic

A structured programme covers visual inspection, electrical testing, photometric verification, component inspection, fault diagnosis, corrective action and documentation. The goal is to find why a light failed and restore the whole circuit, not just swap the lamp.

Overview

What AGL Maintenance Really Covers

Airfield Ground Lighting maintenance keeps a lighting system available when the airport needs it and performing to its required optical, electrical and functional level. It is not simply replacing failed lamps or fittings: the aim is to find the cause of a failure and restore the complete lighting circuit.

An AGL system is a chain of power equipment, series circuits, isolation transformers, connectors, light fittings, control equipment and visual guidance systems. A defect anywhere in that chain can change the final lighting performance, so a sound programme combines preventive maintenance, condition monitoring, corrective maintenance and documented retesting.

A light that turns on but produces inadequate optical output is not equivalent to a fully healthy lighting system.

Preventive Maintenance

Planned Work Before a Failure Becomes Significant

Visual inspection

The foundation of AGL maintenance: find obvious conditions before they become larger failures.

  • Failed lights, damaged lenses, cracked housings
  • Loose hardware, damaged bases, incorrect orientation
  • Contamination, water ingress, exposed wiring
  • Missing covers, abnormal illumination, overheating

Cleaning

Optical contamination directly reduces lighting performance.

  • Lenses, optical windows, filters, lamp envelopes
  • Housings and PAPI optical components
  • Dust, rubber deposits, oil, mud, insects
  • Do not replace a low-output fixture before ruling out contamination

Connectors

The electrical interface between circuit elements, so maintained before they fail.

  • Loose connections, damaged contacts, corrosion
  • Moisture, insulation damage, incorrect assembly
  • Overheating, contamination, mechanical damage
  • Poor connectors cause intermittent lights, reduced output, open circuits, high-resistance connections, transformer stress and repeated failures

Isolation transformers

The interface between the primary series circuit and each fixture in many installations.

  • Enclosure, cable and connector condition
  • Overheating, moisture ingress, insulation
  • Identification and installation integrity
  • Suspect when one fixture differs from healthy neighbours

Cable system

A major part of AGL reliability; faults can produce complex symptoms.

  • Cable condition, continuity, insulation resistance
  • Fault location, earth faults, cable routes
  • Symptoms: several lights out, intermittent or low output
  • Use a systematic test programme

Light fittings

Inspect mechanical and optical condition of each fitting.

  • Housing, lens, optical assembly, light source
  • Mounting, orientation, seals, drainage
  • In-pavement: lens, pavement interface, settlement
  • Evidence of impact or water ingress

CCR

The aim is to confirm the CCR delivers the expected operating condition to the series circuit, not just that it is powered.

  • Enclosure, cooling, connections, grounding
  • Output current, current steps, control settings
  • Alarms, fault indications, remote control, monitoring
  • Internal components per the manufacturer procedure

Task frequency should follow the applicable authority requirements, airport operating conditions, manufacturer instructions, environmental conditions, system criticality and historical failure data. FAA AC 150/5340-26C uses scheduled inspection intervals and states that its routine may be altered to suit local conditions.

Corrective Maintenance

Diagnose the Root Cause, Not Just the Symptom

Click a fault to see what to investigate.

Failed airport light

Possible causes / checks

  • Is the fixture itself faulty?
  • Is power reaching the transformer?
  • Is the isolation transformer healthy?
  • Is the secondary connector intact?
  • Is the light source functional?
  • Is the optical assembly damaged?
  • Is there an upstream series-circuit problem?
  • Are adjacent fixtures normal? One failed light points to a local fault; several point to the common circuit and upstream equipment

Low-intensity light

Possible causes / checks

  • Dirty lens, degraded lamp or LED
  • Damaged optics or optical window
  • Incorrect light source or current setting
  • Transformer or electrical connection problem
  • Beam misalignment
  • Sequence: measure, inspect, clean, check alignment, check electrical condition, repair, retest

Intermittent light

Possible causes / checks

  • Loose connector, damaged cable, poor contact
  • Thermal fault, vibration, unstable connection
  • Transformer, control-system or internal fixture fault
  • Record when it occurs, one or many lights, intensity, weather, circuit condition and recent work
  • Do not close it because it started working again

Series-circuit fault

Possible causes / checks

  • Symptoms: several lights out, unstable operation, circuit trips, incorrect current, low output
  • Path: CCR, primary series cable, isolation transformer, secondary connector, light
  • Troubleshoot through the circuit, not by random replacement

Insulation fault

Possible causes / checks

  • Cable damage, moisture ingress, damaged connectors
  • Transformer insulation deterioration, poor termination, water accumulation
  • Follow electrical safety procedures and approved test methods
  • Interpret readings against cable length, configuration, connected equipment, temperature, moisture and earlier results
  • No single resistance value fits every AGL circuit

Isolation transformer failure

Possible causes / checks

  • Individual light failure, abnormal secondary voltage or current
  • Intermittent operation, overheating, insulation deterioration
  • Repeated lamp or LED failure
  • Separate transformer failure from a fault in the light or secondary connection
  • Compare with adjacent units and historical measurements

Connector failure

Possible causes / checks

  • Corrosion, moisture, loose connection
  • Damaged pins, insulation damage, poor crimping
  • Incorrect assembly, mechanical stress
  • After repair confirm stable operation and retest electrically or photometrically

Light OFF, replace lamp may restore operation temporarily. A better process is: inspect fixture, check transformer, check connector, check circuit, identify root cause, repair, test. This reduces recurring faults and unnecessary maintenance cost.

Photometry

Periodic Photometry and Condition Monitoring

What periodic photometry evaluates

  • Light intensity
  • Beam distribution
  • Colour
  • Alignment
  • Optical degradation
  • Fixture-to-fixture consistency

For relevant precision approach runway systems, ICAO guidance identifies in-field measurement of intensity, beam spread and orientation, electrical-circuit measurements and intensity-setting checks as elements of preventive maintenance.

Photometric data is useful beyond acceptance testing. A trend of baseline intensity, current intensity and rate of change shows which fixtures are degrading. A light still operating but with a sustained drop in output deserves investigation before it becomes a failure.

PAPI

PAPI Maintenance

A PAPI is an optical visual approach guidance system rather than a conventional runway or taxiway light, so its maintenance goes beyond lamp replacement. A PAPI can stay illuminated while its guidance is no longer correctly aligned.

Daily PAPI

Confirm all units operate and look comparable.

  • Comparable brightness, abnormal optical appearance
  • Failed light sources and system status
  • Reported defects

Monthly PAPI

Control, optical and mechanical checks.

  • Control and brightness-control operation
  • Clean optical surfaces, inspect filters and lamps
  • Impact damage, wiring, foundation and unit condition

Alignment and geometry

Check geometry, not only lights.

  • Horizontal alignment, vertical aiming, elevation, spacing
  • Runway reference geometry
  • Foundation movement, impact, civil works

PAPI photometry. Where required, verify intensity, beam coverage, colour, transition characteristics, aiming and consistency between units using calibrated equipment and the applicable procedure. ICAO contingency guidance lists calibration of PAPI among visual-aid status checks.

Special Cases

Maintenance After Impact and After Pavement Works

After impact

Aircraft, vehicles, snowplows, mowing and construction equipment can all damage fixtures. Inspect:

  • Fixture housing, lens, mounting
  • Alignment and cable connection
  • Isolation transformer
  • Foundation and pavement condition

A fixture that stays lit after impact is not automatically unaffected. Alignment and optical performance may have changed while electrical operation stays normal.

After pavement works

Rehabilitation may remove, lower, raise or reinstall in-pavement lights. Verify:

  • Fixture position, elevation, orientation
  • Mechanical seating, drainage and sealing
  • Cable connections and optical condition
  • Photometric performance where required

Especially important for runway centreline, touchdown zone and taxiway centreline lighting.

Records

Documentation, Asset Register and Trending

Maintenance record

A traceable record for every task.

  • Date, area, runway or taxiway, equipment ID and type
  • Task, technician, observed condition, fault
  • Root cause, corrective action, parts replaced
  • Measurements, test and retest result, closure date

Asset register

Links each major component to location and history.

  • Fixture ID, location, type, manufacturer, model
  • Installation date, transformer, connector, circuit, CCR
  • Maintenance, photometric, failure and replacement history

Trending

Patterns that single inspections cannot show.

  • Failures per month, by fixture type, by circuit
  • Transformer, connector and CCR alarm history
  • Photometric degradation, PAPI defects, repeat locations

With a register, the question "Which fixture is this?" becomes: Fixture TW-CN-034, Taxiway B, Circuit 4, Transformer TR-034, previous fault history, last photometric result. Repeated failures in one circuit can point to a common electrical or environmental cause; repeated failures of one fixture type can point to a product, installation or environment problem.

Testing

Electrical Testing and Optical Testing Together

Electrical testing (depending on system)

  • CCR output-current verification, circuit-current checks
  • Insulation-resistance and continuity testing
  • Transformer and connector checks
  • Grounding and bonding verification
  • Control- and monitoring-system checks

Test methods and acceptance values must suit the actual system and the applicable electrical safety requirements.

Optical testing

An electrically healthy fixture may still have low intensity, contamination, damaged optics, incorrect alignment, incorrect colour or degraded beam distribution.

Electrical test + visual inspection + photometry gives a stronger assessment than any one method alone.

Standards

ICAO and FAA Maintenance Guidance

ICAO

Annex 14 provisions for preventive maintenance of visual aids; guidance on inspection and measurement.

FAA

AC 150/5340-26C: preventive-maintenance schedules and troubleshooting, listed as active.

2023 LETTER

FAA guidance on airport-owned approach lighting systems and visual aids.

LOCAL

National rules, airport procedures, project specifications and manufacturer instructions.

FAA AC 150/5340-26C was issued 20 June 2014, cancels AC 150/5340-26B and includes a Maintenance Log Preparation Guide as Appendix B. It covers series lighting circuits, CCRs, fixtures, signs, PAPI, approach lighting, ALCMS and other visual aids. The FAA 2023 letter notes the importance of trained personnel or suitable contracted services for these systems.

ICAO regional guidance reproducing Annex 14 gives specific serviceability objectives for certain Category I and Category II/III runway lighting elements. Use those figures only with the correct runway category, edition and national implementation. They are not universal AGL maintenance percentages.

ICAO requirements are controlled regulatory requirements. The daily, weekly and monthly schedule on this page is an operational planning framework. Do not treat FAA and ICAO guidance as interchangeable.

Keyword Reference

AGL Routine Inspection, Lens Cleaning, Lamp Replacement & Corrective Maintenance

The day-to-day tasks that keep individual lights working.

AGL routine inspection

AGL routine inspection is the regular check of lights, signs, structures and circuits for damage, dirt, misalignment, water ingress and failed units. It is usually done on foot or from a vehicle at set intervals, with findings logged so trends can be seen. Frequency and scope should follow your maintenance plan and the aerodrome's requirements.

Airfield lighting lens cleaning

Airfield lighting lens cleaning removes rubber, dust, oil and grime that reduce light output. Use cleaning methods and agents approved by the fixture manufacturer, and avoid abrasive materials that scratch optical surfaces. Cleaning is often combined with inspection of the seal, gasket and fixture condition.

AGL lamp replacement

AGL lamp replacement means isolating and securing the circuit first, then fitting the correct lamp or LED unit type and rating, checking the seal and gasket, and closing the fixture to the manufacturer's instructions. Never work on an energised series circuit. Record the replacement against the fixture so repeat failures can be identified.

AGL corrective maintenance

AGL corrective maintenance is the repair work done after a fault is found. It starts with locating the fault, then isolating it safely, repairing or replacing the failed part, testing and returning the circuit to service. The cause should be recorded so it can feed preventive planning. See the Preventive, Corrective, Predictive and Emergency section above.

Keyword Reference

Airfield Ground Lighting Preventive Maintenance

Planned work that finds problems before they cause outages, under the three names commonly used for it.

Airfield ground lighting preventive maintenance

Airfield ground lighting preventive maintenance is the planned inspection, cleaning, testing and servicing of the whole AGL system, including fixtures, cables, connectors, isolation transformers and CCRs. The aim is to find ageing and damage before it becomes a failure. See A Repeatable Preventive Maintenance Sequence above.

Airfield lighting preventive maintenance

Airfield lighting preventive maintenance covers the same planned work from the airfield operator's view: scheduled tasks, intervals, responsible staff, records and follow-up. A good programme ties each task to an asset, so nothing is missed and effort goes to the highest-risk equipment.

Airport lighting preventive maintenance

Airport lighting preventive maintenance is the general term for planned care of all airside lighting, including approach, runway, taxiway and PAPI systems. The detailed tasks and intervals should come from the standard you follow and your maintenance plan. See the AGL Maintenance Schedule section above.

Keyword Reference

Reliability, Serviceability, Predictive Maintenance & Spare Parts List

Measuring and protecting how well the system performs over time.

Airfield lighting reliability

Airfield lighting reliability is how consistently the system performs when needed. It depends on design, installation quality, equipment condition and maintenance, and is tracked through failure rates, repeat faults and outage history.

Airfield lighting operational reliability

Airfield lighting operational reliability looks at reliability from the pilot's and operator's side: are the required lights available during operations, and how quickly are faults restored? Higher categories of operation have tighter expectations, so the maintenance response should reflect the category served.

Airfield lighting serviceability

Airfield lighting serviceability is the proportion of lights that are working as required. ICAO Annex 14 sets serviceability expectations by category of operation and type of lighting. Confirm the exact levels in the edition you follow, and record serviceability checks as part of routine inspection.

Airfield lighting predictive maintenance

Airfield lighting predictive maintenance uses condition data to predict failures before they happen. Examples are insulation resistance trends, CCR current and power readings, lamp-out counts, photometric checks and data from the monitoring system. See Periodic Photometry and Condition Monitoring above.

AGL spare parts list

An AGL spare parts list names the items kept in stock so repairs are not delayed. Typical entries are lamps or LED units, isolating transformers, connectors, gaskets and seals, lenses, fasteners and cable repair kits. Quantities should reflect failure history, lead times and how critical each circuit is.

FAQ

AGL Maintenance FAQ

Q1What is AGL maintenance?

AGL maintenance is the preventive, corrective and periodic work required to keep an Airfield Ground Lighting system operational, reliable and within its applicable performance requirements.

Q2What is preventive maintenance in airport lighting?

Preventive maintenance is planned inspection, cleaning, testing and adjustment intended to prevent failure or degradation.

Q3What is corrective airport lighting maintenance?

Corrective maintenance is the repair or replacement work performed after a lighting component has failed or its performance has degraded.

Q4How often should airport lighting be maintained?

There is no single universal interval for every airport. A maintenance schedule should be established according to applicable authority requirements, airport operating conditions, equipment manufacturer recommendations, system criticality and observed performance.

Q5What should be checked daily?

Typical daily checks can include operational status, failed-light identification, critical visual checks, fault reports and other tasks defined by the airport maintenance programme.

Q6What should be checked monthly?

Typical monthly work can include cleaning, fixture inspection, connector checks, PAPI checks, controls and selected equipment inspections. The precise scope should follow the approved maintenance programme.

Q7Is PAPI included in AGL maintenance?

Yes. PAPI requires its own maintenance procedures covering operation, cleanliness, optical condition, mechanical condition, alignment, controls and, where required, photometric verification.

Q8Why is photometry important in AGL maintenance?

Photometry identifies optical degradation that may not be visible during a simple inspection, including reduced intensity, distribution problems and alignment deficiencies.

Q9What causes intermittent airport lights?

Possible causes include loose connectors, cable problems, transformer faults, mechanical movement, thermal effects and internal fixture faults.

Q10What causes low runway or taxiway light intensity?

Possible causes include contamination, degraded light sources, damaged optics, incorrect current or intensity settings, electrical problems and misalignment.

Q11When should an AGL light be replaced?

Replacement should follow the applicable maintenance criteria and troubleshooting results. A low-performing light should first be assessed for contamination, alignment, electrical problems and other correctable causes before unnecessary replacement.

Q12Why should maintenance records be kept?

Historical records allow the airport to identify recurring failures, monitor degradation, plan corrective work and demonstrate maintenance activities.

Q13Does ICAO specify AGL maintenance requirements?

Yes. ICAO Annex 14 contains provisions for preventive maintenance of visual aids, with further guidance addressing inspection, measurement and serviceability objectives. The exact requirements depend on the applicable edition, runway category and national implementation.

Q14Is FAA airport lighting maintenance guidance available?

Yes. FAA AC 150/5340-26C, Maintenance of Airport Visual Aid Facilities, is listed as active and provides preventive-maintenance and troubleshooting guidance.

Trend Test Readings to Catch Slow Degradation
Condition

Insulation resistance and similar readings often decline gradually. Plot them so intervention happens before failure.

Fix the Cause, Not Only the Symptom
Root Cause

Replacing a failed part without understanding why it failed invites repeats. Record the cause found.

Keep Critical Spares Close
Spares

Review spares against failure history and lead times. Waiting weeks for a common part undermines good maintenance.

Share Findings With the Team
