AGL-CS-090 — Sign Photometric Performance Failure

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. An illuminated sign photometric performance investigation where optical-system degradation and LED aging caused luminance reduction and non-uniform illumination — from electrical verification through photometric measurement, optical inspection, and performance restoration.

1Case Study Overview

During a periodic airfield visual aid inspection, inspectors reported that a runway location sign appeared noticeably dim and unevenly illuminated despite being electrically operational.

Pilots also reported that the sign was difficult to identify during night operations, particularly from extended taxi distances.

The sign remained illuminated, the Constant Current Regulator (CCR) was operating normally, and no electrical faults were detected.

Because the sign appeared operational but visually inadequate, a comprehensive investigation was initiated covering luminance measurement, illumination uniformity, color compliance, optical transmission, LED aging, diffuser condition, reflector performance, and internal optical contamination.

The investigation identified optical-system degradation and LED aging, resulting in luminance reduction and non-uniform illumination that failed ICAO photometric performance requirements.

This case demonstrates that an illuminated sign can be electrically healthy while failing operational visual-performance requirements due to photometric deterioration.

3System Background

Illuminated sign performance depends on:

  • LED output.
  • Driver stability.
  • Optical diffuser transmission.
  • Reflector efficiency.
  • Internal cleanliness.
  • Color filters.
  • Uniform light distribution.
  • Optical alignment.

Photometric failures may result from:

  • LED aging.
  • Diffuser contamination.
  • Reflector deterioration.
  • Optical yellowing.
  • Internal dust accumulation.
  • Color-filter degradation.
  • Driver output reduction.
  • Uneven LED performance.

Visual performance depends on optical quality, not only electrical operation.

7Sign Photometry Principle

Healthy optical system → High luminance → Uniform illumination → Correct color → Good legibility
Optical degradation → Reduced transmission → Uneven illumination → Reduced contrast → Poor legibility → Photometric failure

Photometric performance depends on optical efficiency.

Uniformity was restored.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The sign photometric failure was caused by combined optical-system degradation and LED aging, including:

  • LED lumen depreciation.
  • Diffuser transmission loss.
  • Reflector deterioration.
  • Internal contamination.
  • Color degradation.

The electrical system remained healthy.

The primary failure mechanism was loss of optical efficiency.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: Sign illuminated. Noticeably dim. Uneven brightness. Electrical system healthy. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace the transformer.
B. Increase CCR current.
C. Measure luminance, verify illumination uniformity, inspect optical components, assess LED aging, evaluate color compliance, and perform photometric testing.
D. Replace the sign power cable.
Correct answer: C. Dim illumination with healthy electrical operation points to an optical or LED performance problem. Photometric measurement, uniformity verification, optical inspection, LED assessment, and color compliance testing will identify the cause without unnecessary equipment replacement.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about sign photometric performance, luminance and uniformity.

Q1What is AGL-CS-090?

AGL-CS-090 is an engineering training case study. It covers a runway location sign that stayed lit but was dim and unevenly illuminated, so it failed photometric requirements. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2Can an illuminated sign be electrically healthy but still fail?

Yes. Input voltage, driver output, current and transformer output were all normal, yet the sign failed luminance and uniformity requirements.

Q3What did measurements show in AGL-CS-090?

Luminance was 72 cd/m² against a requirement of at least 120 cd/m². It varied across the sign: left 92, centre 74 and right 51 cd/m², so uniformity exceeded acceptable limits.

Q4What colour problems were found?

The red background had shifted toward orange and the white legend had yellowed slightly.

Q5What did optical and LED assessment show?

Inspection found diffuser haze, internal dust, reflector discoloration and surface contamination. The LEDs were producing about 65% of their original luminous flux.

Q6What was the root cause of AGL-CS-090?

Combined optical-system degradation and LED aging: lumen depreciation, diffuser transmission loss, reflector deterioration, internal contamination and colour degradation.

Q7How was performance restored?

Replacing the LED arrays, diffusers and degraded reflectors and cleaning the optical surfaces raised luminance to 138 cd/m² and restored uniformity.

Q8What other checks were made after repair?

The team verified driver performance, measured luminance, uniformity and chromaticity, conducted a night legibility assessment and updated photometric maintenance records.

Q9What is the key lesson from this case?

Illumination alone does not prove compliance. Measure sign luminance, uniformity and colour, not just electrical operation.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

Illuminated sign photometric failures are optical-performance failures caused by LED aging, diffuser contamination, reflector deterioration, and loss of optical transmission, and objective measurement of luminance, uniformity, chromaticity, and legibility is essential for determining ICAO compliance and restoring effective visual guidance.

Systematic photometric measurement, optical inspection, LED-performance assessment, diffuser evaluation, reflector restoration, and post-repair compliance testing provide a complete engineering methodology for restoring ICAO-compliant illuminated sign performance.

In AGL engineering, a sign that is illuminated is not necessarily a sign that is visible, and photometry is the engineering discipline that measures the difference.
