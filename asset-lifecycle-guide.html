AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide

The strategic layer of the AGL Knowledge Hub — not whether an asset is installed correctly, not how to keep it running, not why it failed, but what should be done with it throughout its entire life. Organized across 25 divisions spanning planning, criticality, procurement, installation, commissioning, operations, maintenance strategy, health monitoring, reliability, lifecycle cost, spares, refurbishment, replacement, decommissioning, and digital asset management.

All 25 divisions (495 topics) are structured and published.

How this differs from the other references: the Inspection Handbook asks whether the installation is physically correct. The Testing & Commissioning Manual asks whether it performs as required. The Maintenance Handbook documents how to keep an accepted, in-service asset running. The Failure Database explains why something failed and how to prevent recurrence. This guide sits above all four — it's the strategic layer that decides what to do with an asset from the day it's planned to the day it's recycled, and it's what ties the other four references together.

Five References, Five Questions

The same AGL asset can appear in five different references for five different reasons. This guide is the only one that spans the full life — the other four each own one stage or one lens.

Inspection Handbook

Is it physically correct?

Condition, alignment, and physical state of equipment already in service, against standard.

Testing & Commissioning

Does it perform as required?

Electrical, functional, photometric, and integration proof before acceptance into service.

Maintenance Handbook

How do we keep it running?

The maintenance strategy and specific task that applies to an accepted, in-service asset.

Failure Database

Why did it fail?

Root-cause investigation and recurrence-prevention once something has already gone wrong.

Asset Lifecycle Guide

What should we do with it?

The strategic decision layer — plan, procure, operate, maintain, refurbish, replace, or dispose — across the asset's entire life.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide and how its 25 divisions fit together.

Q1What is the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

It is the strategic asset-management layer of the AGL Knowledge Hub, covering what should be done with every airfield ground lighting asset from planning through disposal. It is organized into 25 divisions and 495 topics, all published.

Q2How does the Asset Lifecycle Guide differ from the other AGL references?

The Inspection Handbook asks whether the installation is physically correct, the Testing & Commissioning Manual asks whether it performs as required, the Maintenance Handbook covers how to keep an in-service asset running, and the Failure Database explains why something failed. The Asset Lifecycle Guide sits above all four and asks what should be done with the asset throughout its entire life.

Q3What lifecycle stages does the guide cover?

Twelve stages: plan, design, procure, install, commission, operate, maintain, monitor health, repair, refurbish or upgrade, replace, decommission, and dispose or recycle. Every division maps onto one or more of them.

Q4How are the 25 divisions organized?

Divisions 01 to 07 cover the front half of the lifecycle, from planning through baseline performance. Divisions 08 to 15 cover operations, maintenance strategy, health, reliability, cost, spares, warranty and inspection. Divisions 16 to 20 cover refurbishment, upgrade, obsolescence, replacement and disposal. Divisions 21 to 25 cover digital asset management, KPIs, audit, case studies and the master asset-record database.

Q5How does the guide connect to the Design Manual, Testing & Commissioning Manual, Maintenance Handbook, Inspection Handbook and Failure Database?

Division 04 feeds lifecycle thinking into design practice, and Division 07 establishes the commissioning baseline for later assessments. Division 09 keeps maintenance strategy at a strategic level while the Handbook owns the procedures, and Division 15 feeds condition grades and defect data into the asset health index. Division 11 is the lifecycle-level home for MTBF, MTTR and failure-trend data, alongside the Failure Database.

Q6What is the master asset record in the guide?

Division 25 defines a 29-field standard asset record that every AGL asset should carry, from Asset ID to disposal status.

Q7Where should I start in the guide?

Division 01, Asset Planning & Strategy, is the starting point of the lifecycle. From there Division 02 covers the asset register and Division 03 covers criticality and risk, and you can follow the lifecycle order or jump straight to the division you need.
