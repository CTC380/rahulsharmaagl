Photometric Fundamentals Comparison

COLORIMETRY VS PHOTOMETRY

Colour vs Quantity — photometry measures how much visible light is produced or received, while colorimetry measures the colour characteristics of that light. In AGL, both are important: photometry verifies intensity and distribution, while colorimetry verifies that the emitted light has the required colour characteristics.

02 · Definitions

What Each One Measures

Colorimetry

Concerned with the colour characteristics of light. It provides an objective method for describing colour rather than simply relying on visual judgement.

  • Is the light red, green, white or blue?
  • Does the measured colour fall within the required limits?
  • Expressed through chromaticity coordinates

Photometry

Concerned with the quantity of visible light as perceived by the human eye — how intense it is, how it is distributed, and how much illumination it produces.

  • Luminous intensity → candela (cd)
  • Illuminance → lux (lx)
  • Luminance → cd/m² · Luminous flux → lumen (lm)
03 · Biggest Difference

Quantity vs Quality

Photometry and colorimetry describe two entirely different aspects of the same emitted light.

AGL Light
Colorimetry
What colour?
Photometry
How much light?

Easy memory: Photometry = QUANTITY of light. Colorimetry = QUALITY/COLOUR of light.

04 · AGL Example

A Green Taxiway Centreline Light

Consider a green taxiway centreline light. Photometry and colorimetry each examine a different question about the same fixture.

Photometry Checks

  • Intensity, in candela
  • Beam distribution
  • Horizontal coverage
  • Vertical coverage

Colorimetry Checks

  • Is the emitted colour green?
  • Does its measured chromaticity fall within the specified colour limits?

Therefore, a light can be correct colour but insufficient intensity, or high intensity but incorrect colour — both characteristics may need verification.

07 · Why Visual Inspection Falls Short

Visual Colour Inspection Is Not Enough

An inspector may say "the light appears green." However, perceived colour can be affected by several factors that a colorimeter is not subject to:

  • Ambient light
  • Viewing conditions
  • Observer position
  • Individual colour perception
  • Background brightness

Colorimetry provides a more objective measurement of a light's colour characteristics than visual judgement alone.

08 · Neither Tells the Full Story

What Each Measurement Misses

Photometry Doesn't Define Colour

A light can have a high luminous intensity — say 10,000 cd — but that number alone does not tell you whether the light is white, red, green or blue. Two different readings of 8,000 cd could theoretically represent lights of different colours.

Colorimetry Doesn't Define Intensity

Colour information alone does not tell you how much light is emitted. Two lights with correct, matching colour characteristics can still have very different photometric output — one at 10,000 cd, another at only 3,000 cd.

10 · Measurement Instruments

How Each Is Measured

Photometric Measurement

A photometer or photodetector measures the light and returns intensity data.

  • Result in cd
  • Result in lx
  • Result in cd/m²

Colorimetric Measurement

A colorimeter or spectroradiometric measurement system measures the light and returns colour data.

  • Measured chromaticity coordinates
  • Colour parameters against specified limits

The exact instrument depends on the applicable measurement method and required accuracy.

12 · Common Mistakes

Myths vs Facts

Myth: "The light is bright, so its colour is correct"
Fact

Brightness does not verify colour. A fixture can have ample intensity while still falling outside the required chromaticity limits.

Myth: "The light is the correct colour, so it meets the photometric requirement"
Fact

Correct colour does not guarantee sufficient intensity or beam coverage. A correctly coloured light can still fail an intensity or distribution requirement.

Myth: "Photometry measures everything about the light"
Fact

Photometry and colorimetry describe different aspects of optical performance. Neither one, alone, fully characterizes an AGL fixture.

13 · Engineering Verdict

Colorimetry vs Photometry

Engineering Verdict

Photometry and colorimetry are complementary, not substitutable. Photometry checks whether an AGL light is strong enough and correctly distributed; colorimetry checks whether the emitted colour meets the required characteristics.

Neither measurement alone provides a complete optical performance check. A light can be the correct colour but insufficiently intense, or intense enough but the wrong colour — for full AGL compliance, both must be verified.

ColorimetryWhat colour? + chromaticity coordinates + primary purpose is colour verification.
PhotometryHow much light? + cd / lx / cd·m⁻² / lm + primary purpose is intensity and distribution.

For AGL: think of photometry when discussing how much light is emitted and how it is distributed, and colorimetry when discussing whether the emitted colour meets the required aviation colour limits.

14 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the simplest way to remember the difference?

Colorimetry → COLOUR (what colour is the light). Photometry → QUANTITY (how much light, and how it is distributed).

Can a light pass photometry but fail colorimetry, or vice versa?

Yes. A fixture can have correct colour but insufficient intensity, or high intensity but incorrect colour. The two checks are independent, so both are typically required.

Why isn't visual inspection sufficient for colour verification?

Perceived colour can be affected by ambient light, viewing conditions, observer position, individual colour perception and background brightness, so a formal colorimetric measurement is more objective than a visual judgement.

Does a high candela rating tell you the colour of a light?

No. Candela tells you about luminous intensity, not the complete colour specification. Two lights with similar candela ratings could, in principle, have different colours.

What instruments are used for each measurement?

Photometry typically uses a photometer or photodetector to produce intensity data in cd, lx or cd/m². Colorimetry typically uses a colorimeter or spectroradiometric system to produce chromaticity/colour data.

Do all AGL lights need both photometric and colorimetric verification?

Both characteristics are commonly relevant since AGL fixtures typically carry both an intensity/distribution requirement and a colour requirement. Always confirm exactly which checks the applicable specification requires for a given fixture.

15 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — sets out both the photometric intensity requirements and the colour/chromaticity requirements for aeronautical ground lights.

FAA AC 150/5340-30

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids — photometric and colour requirements relevant to interpreting AGL fixture performance.

CIE Colorimetry & Photometry Standards

International Commission on Illumination definitions and measurement methods for chromaticity coordinates alongside luminous intensity, illuminance and luminance.

Final Engineering Note

Do not treat a photometric pass as a colour pass, or a colour pass as a photometric pass. Confirm which measurement the governing standard requires before selecting an instrument or acceptance criterion.

Photometry measures how much visible light is produced or received; colorimetry measures the colour characteristics of that light — for AGL compliance, both checks are complementary and generally both are required.
