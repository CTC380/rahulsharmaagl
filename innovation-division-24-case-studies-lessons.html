Division 24 of 25 · CSL Series · Institutional Learning Division

Innovation Case Studies & Lessons Learned

The institutional learning and knowledge-retention division of the AGL Innovation Framework. Its purpose is not simply to collect success stories — it captures what was attempted, why it was attempted, how it was tested, what actually happened, what failed, what was learned, and how that learning should influence future AGL projects. Divisions 18–23 continuously generate prototypes, PoCs, pilots, technology assessments, research and new ideas; without this division, valuable engineering knowledge disappears when a project closes or personnel change.

Standard Case Study Template & ID Scheme

Every major case study should follow this master template, moving from identification through problem, innovation, implementation, results, lessons, final decision and knowledge transfer.

A. Identification — ID, title, category, project/PoC ID, date, team, contributors
B. Problem — original problem, existing condition, engineering gap, business impact
C. Innovation — proposed solution, technology, objective, expected benefits
D. Implementation — design, prototype, testing, pilot, deployment
E. Results — technical, safety, reliability, energy, cost, user performance
F. Lessons — what worked, what failed, unexpected findings, six lesson types
G. Final Decision — adopt, modify, scale, repeat, hold, stop
H. Knowledge Transfer — SOP, design standard, checklist, training, product, research

Most Important Design Principle

Division 24 is the institutional memory of the entire AGL Innovation Programme. It does not compete with Divisions 18–23 — it receives their output. Prototype history stays in Division 18, PoC and pilot mechanics stay in Division 19, technology evaluation in Division 20, and IP/commercialization in Division 21. Division 24's job is to turn all of that raw activity into structured, reusable, classified knowledge.

Its defining feature is the closed loop: a case study is not complete until its lessons have been pushed into an SOP update, a design-standard update, a training update, or a product update. Every case study — successful, failed or inconclusive — should answer one question: "What should the next engineer do differently because we already learned this?"

Especially for failed innovations, case studies should go through technical, safety, commercial, confidentiality and IP review before publication, since sensitive project information needs careful handling even when the failure itself is valuable to record.

Governing philosophy: "Innovate once, learn permanently, and never repeat the same engineering mistake."
