Approach Light Frangible Support

A specialised breakaway mounting system that secures elevated approach light fixtures while ensuring safe separation on aircraft impact. The frangible support is designed to shear or collapse under a predetermined load, minimising damage to the aircraft and preventing catastrophic incidents, while maintaining precise alignment of the light fixture during normal operation.

Overview

What Is It?

An Approach Light Frangible Support is a critical safety component that mounts elevated approach light fixtures to their foundations while providing a controlled breakaway function on impact. The support typically consists of a frangible coupling, a mounting pole or mast, and a base plate, all designed to safely separate when struck by an aircraft. The frangible coupling is the key element — a precisely engineered weak point that shears or fractures under a specific load, allowing the light fixture and pole to break away cleanly without causing structural damage to the aircraft.

The frangible support is a mandatory requirement for all elevated approach lighting fixtures under ICAO Annex 14 and FAA standards. The design must balance two conflicting requirements: sufficient strength to withstand environmental loads (wind, ice, vibration) and predictable breakaway performance at a specified impact force (typically 2,500–5,000 N shear). Modern frangible supports use advanced materials such as aluminium alloys, composites, or specially machined steel couplings with precise fracture characteristics, ensuring consistent and reliable performance across a wide temperature range.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The approach light frangible support's purpose is to resolve the same safety conflict addressed by a frangible coupling on any elevated AGL fixture, but for approach lighting specifically — structures that sit directly in an aircraft's path during the most critical phase of landing, where an undercarriage strike or a low approach is a realistic possibility. Because the support must be strong enough to survive wind, ice, and routine handling yet fracture cleanly at a tightly controlled load if struck, its engineered breakaway point is what prevents an approach light structure from becoming a fixed obstacle capable of damaging a wing, engine, or landing gear during an excursion. This is why the design is a mandatory ICAO/FAA requirement rather than an optional feature, and why the fracture is engineered to produce no sharp edges or large fragments — protecting the aircraft is the entire point of the component, not a secondary benefit.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Approach light frangible supports are deployed wherever elevated approach lighting fixtures are installed. They are used in:

  • ALSF‑1 and ALSF‑2 systems – supporting all elevated centreline, crossbar, barrette, and SFL fixtures for CAT I, II, and III precision approaches.
  • MALSF and MALSR systems – providing frangible support for elevated medium‑intensity approach lighting.
  • Calvert I and II systems – supporting barrette and crossbar configurations with frangible couplings.
  • Runway edge lighting – where elevated runway edge lights are used in snow-prone areas.
  • Taxiway edge lighting – where frangible supports are required for elevated taxiway edge lights.
  • Obstruction lighting – on masts and structures where frangible breakaway is required.
  • Military airbases – where rugged, reliable frangible supports are essential for operational safety.
Placement

Installation & Location

Frangible supports are installed at each fixture station along the approach lighting structure, footed on a base that anchors the support to the ground or to the main structure frame while allowing the coupling itself to shear away cleanly on impact. Height and positioning follow the design drawing for the specific ALS configuration, since the fixture it carries must sit at the correct elevation relative to the approach light structure and the rest of the array. Cabling is run up through or alongside the support to the fixture above, with enough slack left near the coupling that a frangible break doesn't immediately pull the conductor apart, and the support's base is set out so its failure zone doesn't intrude on any area aircraft might traverse.

Function

How Does It Work?

The approach light frangible support operates through a carefully engineered breakaway mechanism:

  • Frangible Coupling: The core component is a precisely machined coupling that connects the pole or mast to the base. The coupling is designed with a specific shear or fracture strength, typically achieved through a reduced cross‑section, score lines, or a frangible insert material. When an impact force exceeds the design threshold, the coupling fractures cleanly.
  • Impact Force Threshold: The breakaway load is carefully specified to be high enough to withstand operational loads (wind, ice, vibration) but low enough to break on aircraft impact without causing significant damage. Typical shear loads range from 2,500 N to 5,000 N, depending on the fixture weight and height.
  • Controlled Separation: On impact, the coupling fractures at the predetermined point, allowing the pole and light fixture to separate from the base. The design ensures the separation is clean — no sharp edges or large fragments are produced — minimising the risk of foreign object debris (FOD) or aircraft damage.
  • Residual Strength: After the frangible coupling breaks, the remaining base and foundation remain intact and can be reused. The pole and fixture are typically replaced, but the base can be quickly re‑fitted with a new frangible coupling and pole.
  • Environmental Resistance: The frangible support is designed to maintain its breakaway characteristics over a wide temperature range (‑40°C to +70°C) and after years of exposure to weather, de‑icing chemicals, and UV radiation. Materials are chosen for corrosion resistance and fatigue endurance.
  • Alignment and Adjustment: The support includes adjustment features (e.g., slotted holes, levelling nuts) that allow precise alignment of the light fixture during installation, ensuring the light beam is correctly directed towards the approach path.
  • Grounding: The frangible support typically includes a grounding terminal or connection point to ensure the light fixture is properly earthed, providing lightning protection and electrical safety.

Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

The frangible support has no electronics of its own, but its mechanical failure mode is deliberately designed to plug into the same loop-level control logic that governs every other fixture on the approach lighting circuit. When the coupling shears, the light fixture it carries separates from its cable connection (or the cable itself parts at a designed weak point), which the CCR sees as an open circuit on its loop — an identical voltage-rise signature to a burnt-out lamp or a failed isolating transformer. The ALCMS therefore reports a sheared support the same way it reports any other loop fault: as a fault on that circuit, not as a structural event at a named location. This is an intentional consequence of keeping the electrical and mechanical failure modes aligned — a broken support never leaves a "live" stub of cable presenting a shock or arcing hazard, and it never fails silently, since any separation immediately registers as a control-system-visible fault. Confirming that the cause was impact rather than an electrical failure still requires a field inspection of the structure itself.

Construction

Physical Construction

The assembly comprises three parts working together: a base plate anchored to a concrete foundation or embedded base can, a short mounting pole or mast typically 0.3–1.0 m tall (up to 3 m for sequenced flashing light towers), and the frangible coupling itself — the engineered weak point machined from aluminium alloy (6061-T6 or 7075-T6), ductile iron, or composite material to shear at a precise 2,500–5,000 N shear load. The coupling's fracture geometry is what distinguishes it from an ordinary joint: it is machined to break cleanly with no sharp edges or fragments that could cause foreign object damage, while still carrying a 200–500 N·m bending moment under normal wind and ice loading without prematurely failing. Anodising, hot-dip galvanising, or a polymer coating protects the base material from corrosion over a service life rated beyond 100,000 fatigue cycles, and a grounding terminal on the base plate ties the structure into the airfield earthing system. The top of the pole carries a standard flange or bracket sized to accept the 45W–200W light fixture it supports.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Frangible Coupling Corrosion and Fatigue

The frangible coupling is exposed to de‑icing chemicals, moisture, and cyclic wind loading. Over time, corrosion can alter the fracture characteristics, causing the coupling to break at a higher or lower load than designed. Regular inspection (visual and non‑destructive testing) is essential, with replacement every 5‑10 years per manufacturer recommendations.

Incorrect Breakaway Load Setting

Using a frangible coupling with an incorrect shear load rating (e.g., too high or too low) compromises safety. A coupling with too high a break load may not separate on impact, causing aircraft damage. A coupling with too low a break load may fail under normal wind loading. Always verify the coupling rating matches the fixture weight and height specifications.

Repeated Breakaway Events and Base Damage

After a frangible coupling breaks, the base plate or foundation may suffer damage from the impact. Repeated breakaway events at the same location can lead to foundation fatigue or cracking. The base should be inspected for damage after each event, and the foundation repaired or reinforced if necessary.

Misalignment After Breakaway Replacement

When a frangible support is replaced after a breakaway event, the new pole and coupling must be carefully aligned to ensure the light beam is correctly directed. Failure to align properly can result in reduced light visibility for pilots. Always re‑align the fixture using a theodolite or laser alignment tool after replacement.

Improper Torqueing of Fasteners

Frangible coupling and base plate fasteners must be torqued to the manufacturer's specified values. Over‑torquing can pre‑stress the coupling and alter its breakaway characteristics. Under‑torquing can allow movement or misalignment under wind loading. Use calibrated torque wrenches and follow the specified torque sequence.

Grounding Connection Failure

The frangible support provides a critical grounding path for the light fixture. Corrosion or loose connections at the grounding terminal can create a high‑resistance path, increasing the risk of lightning damage to the fixture or control electronics. Regular testing of grounding resistance and inspection of connections are essential.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Coupling Breaks at an Unexpected Load, or Fails to Break on Impact
Likely Cause
Corrosion from de-icing chemicals, moisture, and cyclic wind loading has altered the coupling's fracture characteristics over time.
Diagnostic Steps
Conduct a visual and non-destructive inspection of the coupling for corrosion, cracking, or deformation; compare the coupling's age against the manufacturer's replacement interval.
Corrective Action
Replace the coupling per the manufacturer's recommended interval (typically every 5-10 years) or immediately if corrosion or deformation is found.
Coupling Rated Incorrectly for the Fixture
Likely Cause
A frangible coupling with the wrong shear load rating was installed for the fixture's weight and mounting height.
Diagnostic Steps
Verify the installed coupling's rating against the fixture's weight and height specification in the design documentation.
Corrective Action
Replace with the correctly rated coupling for the specific fixture and height combination.
Foundation Cracking or Damage After a Breakaway Event
Likely Cause
The base plate or foundation has absorbed impact damage from a breakaway event, and repeated events at the same location have caused fatigue.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect the base plate and foundation for cracking or displacement after every breakaway event.
Corrective Action
Repair or reinforce the foundation as needed before reinstating the fixture, and flag the location for closer monitoring if breakaway events recur.
Reduced Light Visibility After Support Replacement
Likely Cause
The replacement pole and coupling were not properly re-aligned after a breakaway event, leaving the beam mis-directed.
Diagnostic Steps
Check fixture alignment with a theodolite or laser alignment tool against the design aim point.
Corrective Action
Re-align the fixture to the correct beam direction using the theodolite or laser tool, and re-verify photometrically.
Coupling or Base Plate Shows Movement Under Wind Loading
Likely Cause
Fasteners were under-torqued during installation or a prior service, allowing movement at the coupling or base plate.
Diagnostic Steps
Check fastener torque values against the manufacturer's specification using a calibrated torque wrench.
Corrective Action
Re-torque all fasteners to the specified value and sequence; replace any fastener showing thread damage from prior improper torque.
Elevated Lightning Damage Risk Identified
Likely Cause
Corrosion or a loose connection at the grounding terminal has created a high-resistance earth path through the frangible support.
Diagnostic Steps
Test grounding resistance at the support and inspect the connection for corrosion or looseness.
Corrective Action
Clean and re-terminate the grounding connection, and re-test resistance before returning the fixture to service.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

Premature Fracture Under Normal Loading
High Severity
Root Cause
A coupling engineered or manufactured to a lower fracture threshold than specified, or fatigue from routine wind and ice loading over time bringing it closer to its design breakaway point.
Effect
The support fractures during normal operating conditions rather than only on impact, taking the fixture it holds out of service without any strike having occurred.
Detection Method
Fracture-load verification against the specified design threshold as part of type acceptance, and inspection for signs of fatigue cracking during scheduled maintenance.
Failure to Fracture Cleanly on Impact
High Severity
Root Cause
Corrosion, seizure, or an out-of-specification coupling that no longer fractures at its designed load, defeating the entire safety purpose of the frangible design.
Effect
The structure remains a fixed obstacle after a strike instead of breaking away, risking further damage to the aircraft that struck it — the exact outcome the frangible design exists to prevent.
Detection Method
Periodic fracture-load testing or coupling condition inspection per the manufacturer's schedule, with particular attention to corrosion at the breakaway point.
Corrosion of the Coupling Reducing Fracture-Load Predictability
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Moisture and ground-level corrosion attacking the coupling material over years of exposure, gradually shifting its actual fracture load away from its designed value in either direction.
Effect
Unpredictable fracture behaviour — either premature failure or failure to break away — undermining confidence in the coupling's engineered response.
Detection Method
Visual and, where accessible, material condition inspection of the coupling for corrosion during scheduled maintenance.
Fixture Misalignment From Repeated Wind/Ice Loading Without Failure
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Cyclic loading working the coupling and its fixture connection loose over time without reaching the fracture threshold.
Effect
Gradual tilt or misalignment of the fixture it supports, degrading the visual pattern accuracy the approach lighting design depends on.
Detection Method
Level and alignment check of the supported fixture during scheduled inspection, independent of any impact event.
Sharp Edges or Large Fragments After a Fracture Event
Medium Severity
Root Cause
A manufacturing or material defect causing the coupling to fracture in a way that doesn't meet the no-sharp-edges, no-large-fragments design intent.
Effect
An aircraft that strikes the support sustains more damage than the frangible design is meant to allow, undermining the entire protective purpose of the component.
Detection Method
Post-fracture inspection of the failure pattern against the type's certified fracture behaviour whenever a strike event occurs.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Frangibility Not to Be Defeated
Mechanical

Never brace, guy, or over-torque the frangible coupling beyond its rated shear force (typically 2,500-5,000 N); the support must separate cleanly on aircraft impact to prevent structural damage to the aircraft.

Series Circuit Isolation
Electrical Safety

De-energise and lock out the circuit and verify zero energy before working on the support base, cabling, or the fixture it carries.

Working at Height
Personnel Safety

Use appropriate fall protection and stable access equipment when servicing an elevated approach light fixture or mast, accounting for wind loading during the work.

Off-Airport Approach Corridor Access
Operational

Approach light arrays often extend beyond the airport perimeter over roads, wetlands, or private land; confirm site access, landowner coordination, and any traffic-management needs before dispatching a crew.

NOTAM for Approach Lighting Outage
Operational

Raise a NOTAM before taking a fixture out of service, since a degraded approach lighting system can restrict approach minima or precision approach categories until restored.

Verify Shear Threshold & Bonding After Work
Compliance

Confirm coupling hardware and torque match the manufacturer's specification, and that the bonding/earth jumper across the coupling is intact, before returning the support to service.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
The Two Requirements Are in Genuine Tension, Not Just on Paper
Design

A support stiff enough to hold optical alignment under wind load and weak enough to shear cleanly on impact sit at opposite ends of the same design parameter; verify the specific frangible support model is rated for both the actual wind exposure and the correct impact threshold for its category, rather than assuming a 'standard' unit covers all cases.

A Support That Looks Intact Can Still Have a Compromised Weak Point
Inspection

UV and thermal cycling can degrade a frangible coupling's shear characteristics without any visible sign on the pole or base. Periodic shear-load verification belongs in the maintenance cycle, not just post-impact inspection.

Re-Verify Optical Aim After Any Coupling Replacement
Alignment

Swapping a frangible coupling changes the load path through the assembly slightly; re-check the fixture's beam aim after the replacement rather than assuming it's unchanged from before the coupling was serviced.

De-Icing Fluid Exposure Ages the Coupling Faster Near Traffic Areas
Durability

Supports positioned near taxiway or runway intersections that see heavy de-icing traffic accumulate glycol exposure faster than those further out, accelerating seal and coupling material degradation unevenly across an array. Weight inspection frequency by exposure position.

Coordinate Cable Slack With the Expected Breakaway Travel
Electrical

If the power cable running up through the support doesn't have enough slack to accommodate the coupling's designed breakaway travel, an impact can rip the cable rather than letting the mechanical failure happen cleanly as designed. Verify cable routing against the coupling's actual travel distance at installation.

Never Field-Substitute a Non-Rated Coupling to Save a Delivery Delay
Safety

Under schedule pressure it's tempting to install a visually similar coupling that isn't rated to the specified shear threshold; this silently defeats the entire safety function of the support and typically isn't caught until an incident investigation.

Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about the approach light frangible support: what it is, how it works, specifications, standards, maintenance and common faults.

Q1What is an approach light frangible support?

It is the safety component that mounts an elevated approach light fixture to its foundation while providing a controlled breakaway on impact. It typically consists of a frangible coupling, a mounting pole or mast and a base plate, designed to separate cleanly when struck by an aircraft.

Q2Is a frangible support mandatory for approach lights?

Yes. It is a mandatory requirement for all elevated approach lighting fixtures under ICAO Annex 14 and FAA standards.

Q3What design balance does a frangible support have to achieve?

It must be strong enough to withstand wind, ice and vibration, yet break predictably at a specified load when an aircraft strikes it. The frangible coupling is the engineered weak point that provides this.

Q4Where are approach light frangible supports used?

They support elevated fixtures in ALSF-1 and ALSF-2 systems for CAT I, II and III approaches, in MALSF and MALSR medium-intensity systems, and in Calvert I and II barrette and crossbar configurations.

Q5How does the breakaway mechanism work?

The frangible coupling connects the pole or mast to the base and is machined with a specific shear or fracture strength, typically through a reduced cross-section, score lines or a frangible insert material. When impact force exceeds the design threshold, the coupling fractures cleanly.

Q6What are the typical specifications?

Typical shear load for the frangible coupling is 2,500 N to 5,000 N depending on fixture weight and height, with bending moment capacity of 200 N·m to 500 N·m before the break. Pole height is typically 0.3 m to 1.0 m, up to 3 m for SFL, and fatigue life is over 100,000 wind and vibration cycles.

Q7What materials are used?

Typical materials are aluminium alloy (6061-T6 or 7075-T6), ductile iron or composite materials, protected by anodising, galvanising or a polymer coating. The operating range is -40°C to +70°C.

Q8Which standards apply to frangible supports?

The main references are ICAO Annex 14 (frangibility of approach light supports), FAA AC 150/5345-46, FAA AC 150/5220-23A (frangible connections) and IEC TS 61827. Always verify the current edition and your aerodrome's approved requirements.

Q9How often should a frangible support be inspected or replaced?

Typical intervals are annual for frangible coupling inspection, base plate and foundation inspection, fastener torque and grounding resistance, with scheduled coupling replacement every 5 to 10 years. Your airport's approved maintenance programme takes precedence.

Q10What are the common problems with frangible supports?

Common problems are frangible coupling corrosion and fatigue, an incorrect breakaway load setting, base damage after repeated breakaway events, misalignment after breakaway replacement, improper fastener torque and grounding connection failure. A coupling that breaks at an unexpected load, or fails to break on impact, needs its rating and condition checked.
