Photometric Grid Compliance Analyzer

Import a full measurement grid and classify every point as pass, investigate or replace, with a heat map and a report.

About this calculator

Paste or load a grid of measured intensity at horizontal and vertical angles. Compare it with one required minimum, or with a required grid of your own, and the tool colours each point, lists the worst ones and shows where the peak sits. You can limit the check to the angular zone the standard applies to your light type. It holds no standard tables, so the requirement is always yours to enter and cite.

1 · Requirement & Action Levels

Rule note

Take this from the standard and figure for your light type, category and colour. The default is an example only, not a standard value. If you change the unit, re-enter the minimum and both grids in the new unit.

Rule note

Percent of the required value at each point. Both are placeholders for your own maintenance rules. A point at or above 100 % meets the requirement outright.

2 · Evaluation Zone (optional)

Rule note

Only points inside this window are judged. Points outside are shown greyed and left out of every count. Leave blank to judge the whole grid. For in-pavement fixtures the standard disregards any part of the 10 percent beam that lies below grade, so set the vertical limits to suit.

3 · Measurement Grid

Rule note

Top row: horizontal angles. First column: vertical angles. Use the sign convention of your standard; FAA AC 150/5345-46F takes horizontal angles toward the runway centreline as positive. Comma, tab or semicolon separated, so a block copied from a spreadsheet works. Empty or non-numeric cells are treated as "no reading". The grid shown is illustrative, not manufacturer or test data.

Same layout and same angles as the measured grid. The grid shown is an example only. Enter the values from your standard, using the same unit. A point with no required value is left unjudged.

Method & Reference

How the grid is analysed

Every step is shown so the result can be checked by hand.

1. Reading the grid

The first row gives the horizontal angles and the first column the vertical angles. Each remaining cell is the measured intensity at that direction. Angles must increase or decrease steadily; a repeated angle is reported.

2. Percent of required

Each point is divided by the required value at that point, either your single minimum or the matching cell of your required grid.

% = reading ÷ required × 100

3. Status bands

At or above the investigate level a point passes. Between the replace and investigate levels it needs investigation, and below the replace level it is a replace candidate. Points outside the evaluation zone, with no reading, or with no required value are not judged and are not counted.

4. Peak position

The highest reading and its angles are shown. A peak well away from where the beam should be centred is a prompt to check aiming before blaming the lamp.

5. Relating it to FAA and ICAO

FAA AC 150/5345-46F asks for three things: the average over the main beam meets the tabulated value, every point in the main beam reaches at least half of it, and every point on the 10 percent beam edge reaches 10 percent. To check the half-value rule, set the zone to the main beam, compare each point with the tabulated value and set the replace level to 50 %. The AC takes its average along axis cuts, so the mean shown here is only a guide. ICAO Annex 14 Appendix 2 gives isocandela diagrams for each light type, so use a required grid taken from the figure your project cites.

What this tool does not do

It does not hold standard tables, average intensity over a beam region, interpolate between points or model the beam. The standard's averaging rules, if any, are applied by you. The check is per measured point.

FAQ

Photometric Grid Compliance Analyzer: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on classifying a full measurement grid of intensity readings.

What does the Photometric Grid Compliance Analyzer do?

It imports a full grid of measured intensity at horizontal and vertical angles and classifies every point as pass, investigate or replace. It compares the grid with one required minimum, or with a required grid of your own, and shows a heat map, the worst points and where the peak sits. It holds no standard tables, so the requirement is always yours to enter and cite.

How should the measurement grid be laid out?

The top row gives the horizontal angles and the first column gives the vertical angles, and each remaining cell is the measured intensity in that direction. Separate values with commas, tabs or semicolons, so a block copied from a spreadsheet works, or load a CSV file. Empty or non-numeric cells are treated as no reading. Angles must increase or decrease steadily, and a repeated angle is reported.

What is the sign convention for angles?

Use the convention of your standard. FAA AC 150/5345-46F takes horizontal angles toward the runway centreline as positive.

What is the difference between a single minimum and a required grid?

With a single minimum, every point is divided by the same required value. With a required grid, each point is divided by the matching cell of your own grid, which must use the same layout, angles and unit as the measured grid. A point with no required value is left unjudged. With a single minimum applied to a whole grid, points far off-axis naturally fall low, so use the evaluation zone or a required grid.

What is the evaluation zone?

It is an optional window of horizontal and vertical angles. Only points inside it are judged, and points outside are shown greyed and left out of every count. Leave it blank to judge the whole grid. For in-pavement fixtures the standard disregards any part of the 10 percent beam that lies below grade, so set the vertical limits to suit.

How are the status bands decided?

Each point is divided by its required value, times 100. At or above the investigate level it passes, between the replace and investigate levels it needs investigation, and below the replace level it is a replace candidate. A point at or above 100 percent meets the requirement outright. Points outside the zone, with no reading or with no required value are not judged and not counted.

How does it relate to FAA and ICAO requirements?

FAA AC 150/5345-46F asks for the average over the main beam to meet the tabulated value, every main beam point to reach at least half of it, and every point on the 10 percent beam edge to reach 10 percent. To check the half-value rule, set the zone to the main beam, compare each point with the tabulated value and set the replace level to 50 percent. ICAO Annex 14 Appendix 2 gives isocandela diagrams for each light type, so use a required grid taken from the figure your project cites.

Is the result a final compliance decision?

No. The tool does not hold standard tables, average intensity over a beam region, interpolate between points or model the beam. The mean shown is a guide only and is not the standard's averaging, and the check is per measured point. The required values, evaluation zone and action levels shall be taken from the applicable standard and your maintenance procedure. Confirm any point near a limit with a repeat measurement.
