Screened Cable VS Unscreened Cable

Screened cable is cable built with a conductive screen/shield around the conductor, aimed at electric-field control, EMI management and cable/system protection. Unscreened cable has no conductive screen and relies on its insulation and outer construction alone. Whether an AGL series cable should be screened or unscreened is not a fixed rule — it depends on the circuit design, voltage rating, EMC requirements and the applicable project specification.

02 · Definitions

What Is Screened & Unscreened Cable?

Screened Cable

Cable built with a conductive layer around the insulated conductor, typically copper tape, copper wire, aluminium or another conductive construction, depending on the cable design.

Main characteristics

  • Conductive screen surrounds the insulation
  • Aimed at electric-field control and EMI reduction
  • Screen effectiveness depends on construction, continuity and earthing
  • Requires correct screen termination and bonding

Unscreened Cable

Cable with no conductive screen around the insulated conductor, relying on its insulation and outer protective construction alone.

Main characteristics

  • No conductive shield layer
  • Simpler, lighter construction in most designs
  • No screen-termination or bonding requirement
  • Relies on insulation and sheath for basic protection
03 · Key Difference

The Biggest Difference

The easiest way to remember the difference between screened and unscreened cable:

Screened Cable

Conductor → insulation → conductive screen → outer sheath

Unscreened Cable

Conductor → insulation → outer sheath

Easy memory: Screened → conductor + insulation + shield. Unscreened → conductor + insulation.

05 · What Does the Screen Do?

Field Control & Earthing Function

The screen can help control the electric field surrounding the cable and can provide a controlled path for certain unwanted electrical currents when correctly bonded and grounded. For shield effectiveness, the screen's construction, continuity and termination/earthing arrangement are all important — a screen that is present but poorly terminated does not deliver the intended benefit.

Simply having a screen does not guarantee effective shielding. An incorrectly terminated or poorly bonded screen can reduce the intended effectiveness of the construction.

08 · Do Not Confuse

Electrical Isolation vs Screening

These are completely different concepts, even though both appear in the same AGL circuit context.

Isolation

Provided by an isolation transformer through magnetic coupling between primary and secondary windings — it separates circuits electrically.

Screening

Provided by a conductive layer around the cable — it addresses electric-field control and EMI, not electrical separation.

Screening does not mean electrical isolation, and an isolation transformer does not automatically mean the cable is screened. The two functions are independent of each other.

09 · Installation & Termination

The Extra Step Screened Cable Requires

A screened cable carries an additional installation requirement beyond that of an unscreened cable: the screen itself must be correctly terminated and bonded or earthed according to the cable and system design. An incorrectly terminated screen can reduce its intended effectiveness, so this step is not optional where a screen is present.

Screened Cable

  • Screen must be correctly terminated
  • Requires bonding/earthing per the system design
  • Poor termination reduces effectiveness

Unscreened Cable

  • No screen-termination step required
  • Simpler termination overall
  • Relies on standard insulation/sheath termination practice
10 · Advantages & Trade-offs

What Each Construction Offers

Screened Cable

Potential advantages

  • Better control of electric fields
  • Improved EMC performance
  • Reduced electromagnetic coupling
  • Controlled screen/earth connection where required
  • Useful in electrically demanding environments

Trade-offs

  • Higher cost
  • More complex termination
  • Greater diameter/weight in many designs
  • Requires correct screen bonding

Unscreened Cable

Potential advantages

  • Simpler construction
  • Easier termination
  • Lower cost
  • Smaller/lighter construction in many cases
  • No screen termination required

Trade-off

  • Does not provide the additional shielding/field-control function of a screened construction

12 · AGL Selection

Choosing the Right Construction

For an AGL installation, the choice between screened and unscreened cable should follow this sequence, not a general preference for one construction over the other:

  • AGL circuit design
  • Operating voltage
  • Current
  • Installation environment
  • EMC / screening requirement
  • Cable construction
  • Project specification

The choice should come from the specified cable type, rather than assuming screened is always better.

13 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: Every AGL series cable must be screened
Fact

Whether an AGL cable should be screened depends on the specific circuit design, voltage rating, project specification and EMC requirements — it isn't a fixed rule.

Myth: Screening provides electrical isolation
Fact

Screening addresses electric-field control and EMI; electrical isolation is a separate function provided by an isolation transformer through magnetic coupling.

Myth: A cable with a screen is automatically well shielded
Fact

Shield effectiveness depends on the screen's construction, continuity and correct termination/bonding — an incorrectly terminated screen can reduce its intended effectiveness.

Myth: Screened cable is always the better choice for AGL
Fact

Screened cable carries higher cost, more complex termination and generally greater diameter/weight — the correct choice is whatever the AGL circuit design and project specification call for.

14 · Engineering Verdict

Which Construction Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

Screened cable adds a conductive layer around the insulated conductor for electric-field control and EMI management, at the cost of a more complex, heavier and more expensive construction that also requires correct screen termination and bonding. Unscreened cable is a simpler, lighter and lower-cost construction that relies on its insulation and outer sheath alone, without the additional shielding function.

For AGL, don't select screened cable merely because it offers more protection. Use the cable construction specified for the particular AGL circuit, voltage, installation and applicable project/airport requirements.

ScreenedConductor + insulation + conductive screen — better field/EMC control, more complex termination.
UnscreenedConductor + insulation only — simpler construction, no screen termination required.

15 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the basic difference between screened and unscreened cable?

Screened cable has a conductive layer around the insulated conductor for field control and EMI reduction; unscreened cable has no such layer and relies on its insulation and outer sheath alone.

Does every AGL series cable need to be screened?

No. Whether AGL series cable should be screened depends on the specific circuit design, cable voltage rating, project/airport specification, applicable standard, EMC requirements and installation arrangement.

Does screening provide electrical isolation?

No. Screening and isolation are different concepts — isolation is provided by an isolation transformer through magnetic coupling, while screening is a conductive layer addressing field control and EMI.

Does having a screen guarantee good shielding performance?

Not automatically. Shield effectiveness depends on the screen's construction, continuity and correct termination/bonding arrangement — a poorly terminated screen reduces its intended effectiveness.

What extra installation step does screened cable require?

The screen must be correctly terminated and bonded/earthed according to the cable and system design — a requirement that unscreened cable does not have.

Is screened cable always more expensive and heavier than unscreened cable?

Generally, yes — screened cable typically has a greater diameter/weight and higher cost due to the additional conductive layer and more involved termination, though the exact difference is design-dependent.

How should the screened-vs-unscreened choice be made for an AGL project?

By following the AGL circuit design, operating voltage, current, installation environment, EMC/screening requirement and project specification — not by assuming screened cable is always the better option.

Final Engineering Note

Whether a given AGL cable should be screened or unscreened, and the exact screen construction/earthing arrangement, can vary with the governing standard, equipment manufacturer and approved airport design. For final design, use the current applicable standard and manufacturer datasheets rather than a generic comparison page.

Screened cable adds a conductive shield for field/EMC control at the cost of complexity and price; unscreened cable is simpler and cheaper but has no shielding function — the correct choice for AGL comes from the circuit design and project specification, not a default assumption.
