Product Overview

Back Lit Airfield Sign Boards

What It Is

Back-lit sign boards provide illumination from behind the sign face and suit a wide range of airfield guidance applications. The rear arrangement gives clear, consistent visibility of the approved legend during night-time operations.

Precision AGL Solutions supplies them for taxiways, aprons, runway-holding positions and aircraft stand guidance. Dimensions, legend, illumination system, mounting and environmental protection follow the airport layout and project requirements.

Typical Assembly

  • Sign face and approved legend
  • Internal illumination system, LED or other specified source
  • Reflective or optical internal surfaces
  • Sign enclosure, electrical connection arrangement
  • Mounting supports with frangible or breakaway components
  • Weather and environmental protection

Back-Lit Illumination

Light Source Behind the Sign Face

What It Provides

  • Even illumination of the legend, clear visual identification
  • Controlled light distribution, good night-time visibility
  • Consistent appearance across the sign face
  • Suitable integration with modern LED systems

Design Principle

Under ICAO Annex 14, signs are to be illuminated for night use on instrument runways and on non-instrument runways of code number 3 or 4, and when used in runway visual range below 800 m. Otherwise a retroreflective sign may be acceptable where the authority permits. Sign luminance should follow the airfield brightness steps. The source sits behind the face rather than at the panel edge. Design the internal optics as part of the complete sign assembly to minimize localized bright areas and dark zones.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures

FAQ

Back Lit Airfield Sign Boards — Frequently Asked Questions

What are back-lit airfield sign boards?

Back-lit sign boards provide illumination from behind the sign face and suit a wide range of airfield guidance applications, including taxiways, aprons, runway-holding positions and aircraft stand guidance. The rear arrangement gives clear, consistent visibility of the approved legend at night. A typical assembly has the sign face and approved legend, an internal illumination system, reflective or optical internal surfaces, an enclosure, and mounting supports with frangible or breakaway components.

What colours do the different sign categories use?

Mandatory instruction signs use a white legend on a red background, location signs a yellow legend on black (with a yellow border where required), and direction and information signs a black legend on yellow. Colours are fixed by sign category, not chosen per project, and red is reserved for mandatory instruction signs. The FAA types are L-858R for mandatory instruction, L-858Y for direction, destination and information, and L-858L for location, with size, style and class confirmed per AC 150/5345-44L.

When must airfield signs be illuminated?

Under ICAO Annex 14, signs are to be illuminated for night use on instrument runways and on non-instrument runways of code number 3 or 4, and when used in runway visual range below 800 m. Otherwise a retroreflective sign may be acceptable where the authority permits. Sign luminance should follow the airfield brightness steps.

How does back-lit illumination work, and why use LEDs?

The light source sits behind the sign face rather than at the panel edge, giving even illumination of the legend and background so colours read correctly at night. The internal optics should be designed as part of the complete sign assembly to minimise localised bright areas and dark zones, and matched to the sign size rather than using one configuration for every size. LEDs offer efficient operation, long life, stable illumination, fast switching and lower maintenance, and the modules and drivers should suit the sign dimensions and project electrical architecture.

How are legend, character height and sign size determined?

The legend is established from the approved signage schedule and may show taxiway and location identifiers, directional arrows, holding-position information, stand numbers, gate identifiers and apron information. Character height, spacing and the overall legend configuration come from the required viewing distance and applicable project criteria. Overall dimensions follow from the legend and character configuration, and larger signs may need additional support or structural provisions.

How are back-lit signs mounted, and must they be frangible?

Arrangements include ground-mounted supports, dedicated sign posts, base-mounted assemblies, adjustable supports and frangible or breakaway supports. Mounting must be stable while maintaining required aircraft and vehicle clearances. Signs on the movement area must be frangible and low enough to clear aircraft propellers and engine pods, and the sign height, distance from the pavement edge and frangible point should be confirmed in the airfield safety and clearance assessment.

What electrical arrangements do airfield signs use?

Airfield signs normally run on a series circuit (constant current, through an isolating transformer) or a parallel circuit (constant voltage). The LED driver must suit the transformer secondary or supply voltage and respond correctly to the regulator brightness steps. Also confirm rated power and current, switching, dimming and control interface, surge protection and earthing, and confirm the electrical interface before procurement.

What is checked at commissioning, and how are signs maintained?

Commissioning covers physical inspection of mounting and supports, electrical connections, illumination testing and legend visibility, luminance and uniformity where required, switching, and a night-time check of orientation, with final installation details recorded. Maintenance covers cleaning the sign face, checking for damage, inspecting mounting hardware, cable entries and electrical connections, checking LED modules and drivers, and looking for moisture ingress, legend condition, colour and luminance changes and corrosion. LED systems reduce routine light-source replacement, but the complete sign assembly should still be inspected periodically.

What are the common problems with back-lit signs?

Uneven illumination points to LED or optical component problems, contamination or internal deterioration. Reduced luminance can come from LED degradation, driver faults, supply problems or a contaminated sign face. Moisture ingress comes from damaged seals, cable-entry problems or enclosure deterioration. A damaged sign face reduces legend clarity and should be assessed promptly, and loose, bent or corroded supports should be inspected and repaired.

Is it safe to work on sign boards while the circuit is energised?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage, and opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death. De-energise the circuit at the regulator, apply the site lock-out / tag-out procedure, use the series circuit cut-out switch or an approved isolation device, and work under the airport permit-to-work system. See the electrical safety warning on this page.

Which standards apply to back-lit sign boards?

References include ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I (Chapter 5, signs; Appendix 1, colours; Appendix 4, taxiing guidance sign design), ICAO Doc 9157 Part 4, FAA AC 150/5345-44L and AC 150/5340-18H, DGCA India CAR Section 4, and airport-specific signage specifications and approved signage schedules. Confirm the applicable standard and edition for each project before final technical approval.

What should be provided when requesting or replacing a sign board?

Provide the airport or project name, sign type, approved legend, character height and number of characters, arrow or symbol requirements, required dimensions and installation location, mounting and frangibility requirements, illumination requirements, electrical supply and control requirements, environmental conditions, applicable project standards and required documentation. For replacement or retrofit, check the existing foundations, support arrangements, cable routes and electrical interfaces before selecting the replacement unit.
