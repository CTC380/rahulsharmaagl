AGL-CS-011 — Low Insulation Resistance in an AGL Circuit

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A predictive-maintenance investigation where low IR readings revealed progressive cable insulation deterioration — from trend analysis through sectional testing and cable replacement.

Case ID

AGL-CS-011

Category

Level

Advanced

Case Type

Engineering Simulation

Primary Skill

Important: This is an engineering simulation created for training. It is not presented as an actual airport incident. All readings, values, dates and outcomes are illustrative, and no airport, organisation, person or manufacturer is identified or implied.

1Case Study Overview

During a scheduled preventive maintenance program, the electrical maintenance team performed insulation resistance testing on a runway edge lighting circuit.

The circuit remained operational, and the Constant Current Regulator (CCR) was maintaining the selected current. However, the insulation resistance measurement was significantly lower than the airport's historical maintenance values.

No visible cable damage was observed, and no field lights had yet failed.

The investigation ultimately identified progressive insulation degradation in one underground cable section caused by moisture contamination and aging of the insulation system.

This case demonstrates that low insulation resistance is often an early warning indicator of developing underground electrical failures.

2Learning Objectives

After completing this case study, the learner should be able to:

  • Understand the significance of insulation resistance in AGL systems.
  • Interpret low IR test results.
  • Differentiate localized insulation deterioration from circuit-wide degradation.
  • Apply sectional IR testing procedures.
  • Investigate moisture-related insulation failures.
  • Interpret IR trend data.
  • Perform post-repair insulation verification.

3System Background

The AGL primary circuit consists of:

CCR → Primary Cable → Cable Joints → Isolating Transformers → Light Fixtures → Return

Insulation resistance testing evaluates the integrity of the cable and connected insulation system.

A healthy circuit typically exhibits high insulation resistance, while deterioration may indicate:

  • Moisture ingress.
  • Cable insulation aging.
  • Damaged cable sheath.
  • Contaminated connectors.
  • Defective cable joints.
  • Transformer insulation deterioration.
  • Earth leakage paths.

IR testing is primarily a predictive maintenance tool.

4Problem Statement

The reported condition was:

  • Circuit operational.
  • No CCR alarm.
  • Low insulation resistance.
  • Recent monsoon conditions.
  • Historical IR values previously satisfactory.
  • No visible excavation damage.
Engineering Question

Is the low insulation resistance caused by cable deterioration, moisture ingress, transformer insulation problems, or multiple distributed defects?

7Insulation Resistance Principle

Insulation resistance testing measures leakage current through the insulation system.

A reduction in IR generally indicates increased leakage paths.

Typical interpretation:

Very high Healthy insulation
Moderately reduced Early deterioration
Low Significant contamination or insulation degradation
Very low Serious insulation failure or earth leakage

Trend analysis is usually more valuable than a single measurement.

8Test Equipment

The investigation used:

  • 5 kV insulation resistance tester.
  • True-RMS multimeter.
  • Approved AGL circuit test equipment.
  • Cable sectional isolation equipment.
  • Circuit drawings.
  • Historical maintenance records.
  • Airport-approved PPE.

Testing was performed after approved circuit isolation.

9Investigation

Step 1: Verify Test Procedure

The team confirmed:

  • Circuit isolated.
  • Connected equipment identified.
  • Test voltage appropriate.
  • Instrument calibration valid.
  • Test duration correct.

The measurement was confirmed as valid.

Step 2: Review Historical Trend

Maintenance records showed:

12 months earlier Greater than 1000 MΩ
6 months earlier 650 MΩ
3 months earlier 180 MΩ
Current 12 MΩ

The progressive decline strongly indicated developing insulation deterioration.

Step 3: Sectional Isolation Testing

The circuit was divided into sections.

Results:

CCR to S1 Greater than 500 MΩ
S1 to S2 15 MΩ
S2 to S3 Greater than 500 MΩ

The low insulation resistance was localized to Section S1–S2.

Step 4: Transformer Isolation

Transformers in the affected section were disconnected sequentially. The insulation resistance remained low. This eliminated the transformers as the primary cause.

Step 5: Cable Investigation

Excavation of the affected cable section revealed:

  • Moisture-saturated soil.
  • Damaged cable sheath.
  • Water migration.
  • Insulation discoloration.
  • Early electrical tracking.

The cable insulation had absorbed moisture over an extended period.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

A damaged cable sheath allowed long-term moisture ingress into the underground primary cable.

The moisture progressively reduced insulation resistance, creating increasing leakage current and a developing insulation failure. The fault had not yet progressed to a complete earth fault or open circuit.

11Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Isolated the circuit.
  2. Removed the affected cable section.
  3. Replaced the damaged cable.
  4. Installed new approved cable joints.
  5. Repaired sheath protection.
  6. Improved drainage conditions.
  7. Performed insulation resistance testing.
  8. Conducted continuity testing.
  9. Restored the circuit.
  10. Established periodic IR trend monitoring.

12Post-Repair Verification

CCR operationNormal
Circuit continuityNormal
Insulation resistanceGreater than 1000 MΩ
Leakage currentNormal
Field lightsOperational
Maintenance documentationCompleted

The insulation resistance returned to a healthy range after cable replacement.

14Why This Case Was Important

Low insulation resistance is often the earliest detectable electrical warning in an underground AGL circuit.

If ignored, it may progress to:

  • Earth faults.
  • Cable failure.
  • Connector damage.
  • Transformer stress.
  • CCR protection operation.
  • Operational lighting outages.

Early detection can significantly reduce repair costs.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: Circuit operating normally. CCR healthy. Insulation resistance reduced from 600 MΩ to 15 MΩ over several months. What should you investigate first?

A. Increase CCR current.
B. Replace all light fittings.
C. Perform sectional insulation resistance testing.
D. Replace the CCR.
Correct answer: C. Sectional testing will localize the insulation degradation, allowing targeted investigation of the affected cable section.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about low insulation resistance in an AGL circuit, based on this case study.

Q1What is AGL-CS-011?

AGL-CS-011 is an engineering training case study on a predictive-maintenance investigation in which low insulation resistance revealed progressive cable insulation deterioration before any light had failed. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2Can a circuit have low insulation resistance and still operate normally?

Yes. In this case the circuit was operational, the CCR was maintaining current and no lights had failed, yet insulation resistance was well below the airport's historical values. The fault had not yet progressed to an earth fault or open circuit.

Q3What was the root cause?

A damaged cable sheath allowed long-term moisture ingress into the underground primary cable. The moisture progressively reduced insulation resistance and created increasing leakage current and a developing insulation failure.

Q4Why is sectional insulation resistance testing recommended?

Sectional testing localizes the insulation degradation, so the affected cable section can be investigated and repaired instead of replacing components blindly. It is the recommended step when a circuit shows a significant IR decline but no visible damage.

Q5Why are IR trends important for predictive maintenance?

Insulation resistance trends are essential predictive-maintenance data. A reading compared with historical values shows deterioration long before an operational failure occurs.

Q6How was the circuit repaired?

The team isolated the circuit, removed the affected cable section, replaced the damaged cable and installed new approved joints. They repaired sheath protection, improved drainage, tested insulation resistance and continuity, restored the circuit and set up periodic IR trend monitoring.

Q7What did post-repair verification show?

Insulation resistance returned to greater than 1000 MΩ, with normal leakage current, continuity and CCR operation, and the field lights operational.

Q8What should you do if insulation resistance falls from 600 MΩ to 15 MΩ over several months but the circuit still works?

Perform sectional insulation resistance testing to localize the deterioration. Increasing CCR current, replacing light fittings or replacing the CCR is not the right response.

Trending

Insulation resistance falling year on year is a maintenance trigger even when each reading meets the limit in use.

Section the Circuit to Find the Weak Length

Localisation

Record Temperature and Humidity With Every Reading

Planning

A scheduled repair under controlled conditions is cheaper and safer than an emergency during operations.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

Low insulation resistance is often a progressive underground insulation deterioration problem that should be investigated before operational failures occur.

Systematic IR trend analysis, sectional isolation, cable investigation, and moisture assessment can identify developing insulation failures at an early stage, allowing corrective action before earth faults, cable breakdown, or circuit outages occur.

In AGL engineering, insulation resistance is one of the most powerful indicators of the long-term health of the underground electrical network.
