FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about sizing an AGL isolating transformer with this calculator.

Q1What does the Isolation Transformer Selection Calculator do?

You enter the series circuit current, the fixture's rated power and secondary voltage, and a safety margin. It returns a recommended standard transformer rating, the required power including margin, the approximate secondary current, the approximate turns ratio and the headroom against the fixture rating.

Q2How is the recommended transformer size calculated?

Required power is the fixture's rated power multiplied by one plus the safety margin. The calculator then picks the smallest standard rating that is at least that value. For example, a 45 W fixture with a 15% margin needs 51.75 W, so the recommendation is 65 W.

Q3Which standard transformer ratings does the calculator use?

45 W, 65 W, 100 W, 150 W, 200 W and 300 W. Typical applications range from low-intensity fixtures and small signs at 45 W up to high-intensity approach lighting and sequenced flashing systems at 300 W.

Q4How is the secondary current worked out?

It is the fixture's rated power divided by its secondary voltage. Typical secondary voltages are 6.6 V, 12 V or 24 V depending on the lamp or LED type.

Q5What series circuit currents can I choose?

6.6 A for a standard AGL loop, 20 A for a high-current loop and 2.8 A for low-step or obstruction circuits.

Q6What safety margin should I choose?

The calculator offers 10% for a tight fit, 15% as typical and 20% as conservative. The margin is the headroom above the fixture's rated power before choosing a transformer size.

Q7What happens if the required power is above 300 W?

The calculator flags that the requirement exceeds the largest standard rating listed. Such a fixture needs a custom or higher-capacity transformer, so confirm with the manufacturer before specifying.

Q8Can I use the result as the final transformer selection?

No. It is a first-pass sizing estimate for planning. Final selection must confirm compliance with the applicable specification (ICAO Annex 14, FAA AC 150/5345-53, IEC 61822 or IS 7785), the manufacturer's rated power and insulation class, and site factors such as cable length and voltage drop.

Q9What does the headroom figure show?

It compares the selected standard rating with the fixture's rated power, as a percentage. A positive value means spare capacity, and a negative value would mean the transformer is smaller than the fixture load.
