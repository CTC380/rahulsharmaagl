Side Row Bars

A critical component of precision approach lighting systems, Side Row Bars are rows of lights installed on both sides of the approach centreline. They provide lateral guidance, define the approach corridor, and assist pilots in maintaining proper alignment during final approach — particularly in low visibility conditions.

Overview

What Is It?

Side Row Bars are a critical component of precision approach lighting systems. They consist of rows of lights installed on both sides of the approach centreline, typically at fixed distances from the runway threshold. These lights provide lateral guidance to pilots during final approach by defining the approach corridor and helping pilots maintain proper alignment with the runway centreline.

Side Row Bars are configured as part of the ALSF-1 and ALSF-2 precision approach lighting systems. They occupy the inner 1,000 ft of the system nearest the runway — installed as two rows of red lights, one on each side of the extended centreline, forming a "runway-shaped box" between the threshold and the 1,000 ft decision (roll) bar. The lights are steady-burning red, at an intensity well below the white centreline/flasher stations, and give the pilot a visual reference confirming the aircraft is still in the approach light system and short of the runway threshold — the same red reference required to descend below 100 ft AGL under 14 CFR 91.175 when only the approach lights are in sight.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

Side Row Bars exist to define the outer boundary of the approach corridor from a distance, giving the pilot a clear sense of the safe lateral envelope to stay within, in addition to the fine centreline tracking that the barrettes and centreline lights provide. Because they sit on both sides of the approach path rather than on it, they let a pilot judge overall corridor width and position within it holistically, functioning like a visual "gate" the aircraft flies through, rather than requiring the pilot to reconstruct that boundary purely from a single centreline reference. This dual-reference approach — centreline for fine tracking, side row bars for the broader corridor — is precisely why full precision systems combine both rather than relying on either alone.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Side Row Bars are a standard component of precision approach lighting systems and are used on:

  • CAT I precision approach runways (ALSF-1 configuration)
  • CAT II/III precision approach runways (ALSF-2 configuration)
  • Runways with low visibility operations (RVR below 800m)
  • Military airfields requiring precision approach capability
  • Major international airports with high traffic volumes
  • Runways with complex approach paths requiring enhanced lateral guidance
Placement

Installation & Location

Side row bars are erected on their own poles flanking the centreline barrette piers, offset laterally at the standard approach lighting width either side of the extended runway centreline, with pole spacing matching the centreline barrette interval so each side row light sits level with its corresponding centreline barrette. Poles share the same foundation line and, on approaches over water or uneven terrain, the same raised platform or bridge structure as the centreline piers. Cabling runs along the side row pole line back to the approach lighting control cabinet, with an isolating transformer at each pole base, and the full layout is set out from the same ILS/runway centreline survey used for the centreline barrettes.

Function

How Does It Work?

Side Row Bars operate as an integral part of the approach lighting system:

  • Configuration: Side Row Bars are installed as two rows of lights, one on each side of the approach centreline, confined to the inner 1,000 ft of the approach lighting system nearest the runway threshold — not spaced out beyond it. Each row consists of light stations at longitudinal intervals matching the centreline light stations (typically every 100 ft), with 3 lights per side at each station.
  • Lighting: The lights are steady-burning red, distinguishing this final 1,000 ft "box" from the white high-intensity centreline lights and sequenced flashers that terminate at the 1,000 ft decision (roll) bar. Side Row Bars are not sequenced or flashed.
  • Guidance: The visual "gates" created by the side row bars provide lateral guidance to the pilot, defining the approach corridor. If the aircraft is off-centreline, the pilot will see the bars asymmetrically, indicating the need to correct the approach path.
  • Integration: Side Row Bars are integrated with other approach lighting components — centreline barrettes, crossbar lights, sequenced flashing lights — to provide a comprehensive visual guidance system for precision approaches.
Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

Side Row Bars are not individually addressable — they are wired into the same constant current series circuit as the rest of the approach lighting system (ALS) they belong to, whether that is a CAT I configuration, SSALR, MALSR, or a full CAT II/III system. An Airfield Lighting Control and Monitoring System (ALCMS), driven by RVR category selection or ATC instruction, sets the CCR brightness step for that circuit. Because side row bars typically share a loop (or a dedicated sub-circuit fed from the same regulator bank) with crossbars and centreline barrettes, every bar switches on and dims together with the rest of the ALS — there is no independent control of a single bar or pair. Each fixture draws its supply through its own isolating transformer, tapping a fixed secondary current from the loop. Fault detection remains loop-based: the CCR watches loop voltage against the commanded current, and a voltage rise at constant current is reported to the ALCMS as a circuit fault rather than pinpointed to an individual bar.

ALCMS → CCR → ALS Series Circuit (Side Row Bars + Crossbars + Centreline) → Isolating Transformer → Fixture

Construction

Physical Construction

Side row bar fixtures use the same die-cast aluminium housing family as other high-intensity approach lights, anodised or powder-coated for corrosion resistance and fitted with a hardened glass or polycarbonate prism to shape and focus the beam toward the approach path. Because each bar groups several lights together at a fixed cross-bar spacing, individual fixtures are mounted on a common structural bar or on closely spaced individual poles set to a matched height and aim, with each unit bolted to its own frangible coupling and L-867 base can so that an aircraft impact shears the fixture cleanly without damaging the bar's neighbouring units. The optical assembly is gasket-sealed with an O-ring to achieve an IP67/IP68 rating, and internal wiring from each light in the bar terminates at sealed L-823 connectors feeding its own isolating transformer, keeping the bar's series-circuit wiring independent per fixture for fault isolation. Precise alignment across all lights in a bar is critical, since side row bars work as a lateral pair to define the approach corridor, so mounting stakes and base cans are set and levelled to a common reference during installation. Stainless-steel fasteners are used throughout for durability in the exposed approach lighting environment.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Alignment Drift

Side Row Bars must be precisely aligned with the approach centreline. Over time, mounting structures can shift due to ground settlement, wind, or impact damage, causing the bars to become misaligned. Regular alignment checks using precision optical equipment are essential to maintain guidance accuracy.

Lens Contamination from Jet Blast

Lights near the runway threshold are exposed to jet blast, which can deposit rubber, dust, and oil on the lenses. This is particularly problematic for elevated lights. Regular cleaning with approved solvents and soft brushes is required to maintain light output and beam pattern integrity.

Frangible Coupling Fatigue

Elevated side row bar lights rely on frangible couplings that can fatigue over time due to wind loading and thermal cycling. Regular inspection and replacement of couplings is recommended to ensure breakaway performance in case of aircraft impact.

Flashing Synchronization Failure

In configurations where side row bars flash in sequence, synchronization failure can occur due to control circuit issues or signal timing errors. This can cause confusing visual cues for pilots. Regular testing of the flashing sequence and synchronization is essential.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Single Fixture Dark, Rest of Row Normal
Likely Cause
Failed LED module / lamp, or an open isolating transformer (blown primary fuse-wire link or failed secondary winding).
Diagnostic Steps
Confirm series loop continuity through the fixture's transformer primary; megger the transformer; check secondary voltage at the connector with the CCR energised.
Corrective Action
Replace the LED module/lamp if the transformer checks healthy; otherwise swap the isolating transformer and re-seal the L-823 connectors before backfill.
Entire Row Dark / CCR Will Not Regulate
Likely Cause
Open-circuit fault in the series loop (severed cable, corroded L-823 splice, or a failed transformer acting as an open point).
Diagnostic Steps
Check the CCR fault/open-circuit indication first; loop-resistance test from the CCR output to isolate the faulted segment; sectionalise and megger cable runs to narrow down the break.
Corrective Action
Excavate and repair or replace the faulted cable section and connector; re-test insulation resistance before reinstating the circuit.
CCR Trips on Overcurrent or Ground Fault
Likely Cause
Moisture ingress at a fixture or connector has degraded insulation resistance, creating a short or a partial ground fault on the series loop.
Diagnostic Steps
Megger the full circuit to identify the low-resistance segment; visually inspect suspect fixtures for cracked gaskets, condensation, or damaged secondary cable jackets.
Corrective Action
Dry out or replace the affected fixture/connector, renew O-rings and gaskets, and confirm insulation resistance is back above the acceptable minimum before re-energising.
Flashing Sequence Fails to Synchronise (Where Fitted)
Likely Cause
Control circuit fault or signal timing error between the flasher controller and one or more bar fixtures.
Diagnostic Steps
Observe the flash pattern against the specified timing; check the flasher controller's diagnostics; test signal continuity to the affected fixture.
Corrective Action
Recalibrate or replace the flasher controller, and repair or replace the faulted signal cable.
Reduced or Uneven Light Output
Likely Cause
Lens contaminated by jet blast deposits (rubber, dust, oil), or an aging LED array, particularly on elevated fixtures near the threshold.
Diagnostic Steps
Run a photometric intensity check against ICAO/FAA thresholds; inspect and clean the lens; compare output against a known-good fixture in the row.
Corrective Action
Clean or replace the lens, or replace a degraded LED module; re-verify with a follow-up photometric reading.
Fixture Leaning, Loose, or Misaligned
Likely Cause
Frangible coupling has fatigued from wind loading and thermal cycling, or the base structure has settled or been struck.
Diagnostic Steps
Visually and physically check the frangible coupling for cracking or play; check fixture aim against tolerance; inspect the mounting base for settlement or impact damage.
Corrective Action
Replace the frangible coupling on schedule if fatigued, re-level and re-aim the fixture, and address any base or subgrade settlement to prevent recurrence.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

LED Module / Lamp Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Solder-joint fatigue from repeated thermal cycling, moisture ingress through a compromised LED board seal, or ordinary end-of-life lumen depreciation.
Effect
Single fixture goes dark or drops below minimum intensity while the rest of the row continues to operate normally, thinning the threshold's lateral guidance cue.
Detection Method
Nighttime visual patrol, periodic photometric intensity survey, and continuity/secondary-voltage checks at the fixture connector.
Isolating Transformer Winding Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Moisture ingress leading to insulation breakdown, a manufacturing defect in the winding, or a blown primary fuse-wire link from a transient surge.
Effect
Open-circuit point in the series loop; can darken the single fixture or, if the open extends to the loop itself, take down the entire row.
Detection Method
Megger test of the transformer, secondary voltage check with the CCR energised, and loop-resistance testing from the CCR output.
L-823 Connector / Splice Degradation
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Improper seating or crimping during installation, gasket failure allowing groundwater ingress, or corrosion building up on the pins over time.
Effect
Intermittent operation, flickering, or a partial ground fault that degrades circuit insulation resistance.
Detection Method
Insulation resistance (megger) testing of the circuit and visual inspection of connectors at the fixture base during maintenance.
Series Cable Insulation Breakdown
High Severity
Root Cause
Cable jacket damage from nearby excavation or rodent activity, age-related insulation degradation, or prolonged standing water in the duct run.
Effect
Ground fault or short circuit that trips the CCR or darkens the entire series loop.
Detection Method
Full-circuit megger test to flag the low-resistance segment, followed by sectionalised testing or time-domain reflectometry to pinpoint the fault location.
Flasher Controller Fault (Where Fitted)
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Control circuit fault or signal timing error between the flasher controller and one or more bar fixtures.
Effect
Flashing sequence fails to synchronise, weakening the added conspicuity the flashing bars are meant to provide near the threshold.
Detection Method
Observation of the flash pattern against specified timing, flasher controller diagnostics, and signal continuity testing to the affected fixture.
Frangible Coupling Fatigue / Fracture
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Cyclic loading from wind and thermal cycling, material fatigue over the service interval, or corrosion at the coupling threads on elevated units near the threshold.
Effect
Fixture leans, loosens, or separates entirely from its base, taking the light out of tolerance or out of service.
Detection Method
Physical inspection for cracking or play in the coupling, and periodic fixture-aim/level checks against tolerance.
Jet-Blast Lens Contamination
Low Severity
Root Cause
Rubber, dust, and oil deposits carried by jet blast and prop wash settling on the lens of fixtures positioned close to the threshold.
Effect
Reduced or uneven light output on affected fixtures, diminishing the lateral extension of the threshold guidance.
Detection Method
Photometric intensity check against ICAO/FAA thresholds, and visual inspection and cleaning of the lens.
CCR Regulation Fault
High Severity
Root Cause
Internal component failure (control board or output stage), a load mismatch after circuit modification, or a firmware/setpoint fault.
Effect
Incorrect brightness step, failure to hold regulated current across the loop, or nuisance tripping on healthy circuits.
Detection Method
CCR self-diagnostic/fault indication, output current verification, and a load-bank test to confirm regulation across all intensity steps.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Series Circuit Isolation
Electrical Safety

Side row bar fixtures sit on a constant-current series circuit that can carry several kV across the loop. De-energise and lock out the CCR, then verify zero energy at the pole base before climbing or handling connectors.

Elevated Pole Work
Personnel Safety

Side row bars mount on poles flanking the centreline pier line; use fall protection appropriate to pole height, and follow the pier structure's own access procedure where it is a raised platform or bridge over water.

Frangibility Not to Be Defeated
Mechanical

Never substitute a frangible pole with a rigid fitting or over-torque it during installation or repair; each pole must still shear cleanly if struck by an aircraft on a low approach or go-around.

Approach Surface Coordination
Operational

The side row bar poles sit within the approach protected surface; any work, including crane or lift use, requires ATC coordination, a NOTAM for the affected bar, and confirmation that temporary equipment stays within the height limit for that surface.

Insulation Test Before Re-Energisation
Electrical Safety

After any cable, connector, or transformer work, megger test the affected series loop before restoring power, since a residual fault at re-energisation can trip the CCR or darken the bar.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Do not leave a light dark, out of level with its paired centreline barrette, or at reduced intensity after maintenance; verify alignment and photometric conformance before closing out and cancelling the associated NOTAM.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Lateral Symmetry Matters More Than Absolute Intensity
Photometric

Side row bars work as a matched left/right pair providing a visual 'gate'; a brightness mismatch between the two sides is a more significant guidance problem than either side being slightly outside its own individual tolerance, so check left-right symmetry specifically at each inspection, not just each bar against spec.

Verify Bar Spacing Against the Approach Corridor Width
Installation

Side row bar spacing defines the width of the visual approach corridor a pilot flies through; resurvey lateral spacing after any threshold relocation or ALS reconfiguration, since a corridor that has quietly narrowed or widened from the original design changes the guidance provided without any single fixture reading as faulty.

Match the Flashing/Steady Mode to the ALS Configuration
Configuration

Whether side row bars operate steady-burning or flashing depends on the specific ALSF configuration in use; confirm actual operating mode against the design after any control-cabinet change, since a bar quietly reverting to steady-burning when the design calls for flashing changes the visual cue without an obvious fault.

Trend Loop Insulation on the Longer Side-Row Feeders
Troubleshooting

Side row bars sit further from the centreline than crossbars and often ride a longer feeder run with more splice points; trend insulation resistance readings on these feeders specifically, since a slow-developing fault here is easy to miss until an entire bar goes dark.

Off-Runway Terrain Affects Foundation Stability
Durability

Side row bar structures often sit on softer ground further from the paved runway environment than centreline fixtures; inspect foundation and mounting hardware for corrosion and settlement on a schedule suited to that exposure rather than the standard runway-fixture interval.

LED Retrofit Needs Left-Right Curve Matching
Retrofit

An LED retrofit that matches the original incandescent brightness-step curve on one side of a bar pairing but not the other reintroduces the lateral asymmetry the system depends on avoiding; validate the step curve across both sides of every bar pair, not one side at a time.
