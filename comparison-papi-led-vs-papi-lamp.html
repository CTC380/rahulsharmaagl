Light Source Technology Comparison

PAPI LED VS PAPI Lamp

Same PAPI principle, different light source. PAPI LED replaces the traditional incandescent/halogen lamp with a solid-state LED module and driver, while PAPI Lamp is the conventional filament-based source most legacy installations still use. Both must satisfy the same intensity, chromaticity and transition requirements — the difference is in energy use, source life, heat behaviour and maintenance profile.

The FAA's PAPI programme page cites LED extending source life from roughly 2,000 hours (a typical incandescent PAPI lamp) to at least 40,000 hours. AC 150/5345-28H itself only sets a 1,000-hour minimum for lamps and refers LED performance criteria to FAA Engineering Brief No. 67 — so treat the LED figure as a manufacturer-declared value, not a specification limit.

02 · Definitions

What Is PAPI LED & What Is PAPI Lamp?

PAPI LED

PAPI LED uses an LED array driven by an electronic driver in place of the conventional lamp source. The rest of the optical logic — housing, optics and red/white transition — follows the same PAPI design principle.

What changes

Power supply, light source and driver electronics. The optical output requirements do not change — a PAPI LED unit still has to meet the same intensity, angular coverage and chromaticity criteria as a lamp-based unit.

PAPI Lamp

PAPI Lamp is the conventional configuration, typically using two or three high-power incandescent or halogen lamps per light unit, with the beam shaped by the fixture's optics and colour filter.

What stays familiar

Straightforward electrical architecture, well-understood failure modes, and — because filament sources run hot — a degree of built-in lens anti-condensation heating that an LED unit must otherwise provide by design.

03 · Power Consumption & Source Life

Where LED Makes Its Biggest Case

Energy use and source life are the two figures that dominate any LED-versus-lamp discussion, and they are related: a lamp converts a large share of input power to heat rather than light, and that same thermal stress is what limits filament life.

PAPI LED

  • Lower electrical power for equivalent light output
  • Source life measured in tens of thousands of hours
  • Output degrades gradually rather than failing abruptly
  • Actual savings depend on driver design, intensity setting and CCR configuration

PAPI Lamp

  • Higher electrical power draw per unit
  • Rated life on the order of 1,000–2,000 hours (the FAA minimum is 1,000 hours)
  • Filament ageing gradually reduces output before failure
  • Lamp is treated as a routine consumable component

No fixed percentage saving should be assumed. The actual energy and life advantage of a given LED PAPI depends on the manufacturer's driver design, heater arrangement, operating intensity step and control system.

04 · Heat, Frost & Anti-Icing

The Trade-Off LED Introduces

A lamp's inefficiency is a nuisance electrically, but the resulting heat has one practical benefit: it helps keep the lens free of dew, frost and ice. A PAPI light unit is required to prevent condensation or icing on the lens, whether through thermostatically controlled heating or the intrinsic heat of the source itself — FAA AC 150/5345-28H accepts either approach.

PAPI LED units run cooler at the source, which is good for efficiency but means anti-condensation and anti-icing has to be engineered in deliberately — typically a dedicated lens heater — rather than arriving "for free" as a side effect of the lamp.

05 · Optical Performance

Transition Sharpness & Intensity Stability

PAPI LED

A well-engineered LED optical system can hold a very sharp red/white transition, and because LED output does not fade the way a filament does, intensity stays more consistent across the source's service life.

PAPI Lamp

Transition sharpness is equally achievable, but depends on the lamp/reflector/filter combination staying in good condition — filament ageing reduces output over time and affects maintenance intervals.

LED does not automatically mean better photometry. Whichever source is used, the complete optical assembly still has to satisfy the applicable intensity, angular coverage, chromaticity and transition-angle requirements.

06 · Start-Up Behaviour

Reaching Full Intensity From Cold

FAA AC 150/5345-28H requires PAPI light sources to be at full intensity within 5 seconds of a cold start. A conventional lamp needs a brief filament warm-up to get there; an LED source reaches its operating output essentially as soon as the driver energises it. In both cases, the complete light unit's start-up behaviour also depends on its control, monitoring and heater circuits — not the source alone.

07 · Electrical Architecture

Where the Circuit Actually Differs

The FAA PAPI specification defines two power styles, and either can be built with either light source. Style A is voltage-powered from a utility single-phase supply through a power and control unit (PCU) with photocell day/night control. Style B is current-powered from a 2.8–6.6 A series lighting circuit — the usual AGL arrangement — with the CCR and isolation transformers supplied separately. What LED changes is what sits between that supply and the light source.

PAPI LED Circuit

On a series (Style B) system, CCR power reaches an LED driver through the isolation transformer, and the driver regulates current to the LED module; on a voltage-powered (Style A) system the PCU and driver play the equivalent role. Either way the driver is an active electronic component in its own right, alongside the light unit's monitoring and heater circuits.

PAPI Lamp Circuit

On a series (Style B) system, CCR power passes through an isolation transformer straight to the lamps — a simpler, well-understood arrangement with fewer active electronic components in the light unit itself. A voltage-powered (Style A) system uses the PCU to regulate the lamp-socket voltage instead.

08 · Failure Modes

How Each System Actually Fails

PAPI LED

  • Individual LED failure — often a partial output reduction rather than total loss
  • Driver failure — can take out a full channel or module
  • Control-circuit fault causing light-unit malfunction

Exact behaviour depends on the manufacturer's LED and driver architecture.

PAPI Lamp

  • Filament failure — typically a clean, total loss of that individual lamp
  • Multiple lamps per light unit are normal, and the FAA specification requires the unit to keep operating with one lamp out without stressing the remaining lamps
  • Series (Style B) systems can add an optional lamp-bypass (shorting) device and an optional go/no-go lamp-monitoring output

One failed lamp therefore reduces the unit's output rather than blacking it out — which is why lamp-out checks and monitoring matter.

10 · Lifecycle Cost

Initial Cost vs Long-Term Cost

PAPI LED

Higher initial cost, offset over time by lower energy use, a much longer source life and reduced light-source maintenance — generally the lower total lifecycle cost over the installation's service life.

PAPI Lamp

Lower initial cost, but regular lamp replacement, higher energy consumption and more frequent maintenance visits typically make it the higher long-term cost option.

A real lifecycle comparison should be run against the specific airport's operating hours, CCR intensity settings, local energy cost, lamp/LED-module replacement cost, labour and access-equipment cost — not a generic assumption.

11 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: LED PAPI is automatically better photometry
Fact

No. The complete optical assembly — LED or lamp-based — still has to meet the same intensity, chromaticity and transition requirements.

Myth: Converting a lamp PAPI to LED is a simple "swap the source" job
Fact

No. The optics, power supply/CCR compatibility, thermal management, monitoring and existing mounting/aiming reference all need re-evaluation.

Myth: LED sources never need any lens heating
Fact

No. Because LEDs run cooler at the source, most LED PAPI designs still need a dedicated anti-condensation/anti-icing solution.

Myth: LED PAPI never fails
Fact

No. LED modules, drivers and control circuits can still fail — the failure pattern is just different from a lamp burning out.

Myth: LED and lamp PAPI produce different pilot indications
Fact

No. Both present the same red/white PAPI logic to the pilot — the light source technology is invisible to the flight deck.

13 · Engineering Verdict

PAPI LED vs PAPI Lamp

Engineering Verdict

PAPI LED and PAPI Lamp deliver the identical PAPI indication logic — the difference is entirely in the source technology behind it. LED wins decisively on energy use, source life and light-source maintenance, but it shifts the anti-condensation/anti-icing function onto a dedicated heater and adds driver electronics that lamp systems don't have.

For a new installation, LED is generally the preferred technology, provided the complete system is certified and evaluated against the same photometric, chromaticity and environmental requirements as a lamp-based unit. For an existing lamp PAPI, LED retrofit should be treated as a full system evaluation, not a like-for-like bulb swap.

PAPI LEDLower energy use, much longer source life, less light-source maintenance, more electronics.
PAPI LampSimpler circuit, intrinsic anti-frost heat, but shorter life and regular lamp replacement.

LED is not a different or "better" PAPI principle — it is a more efficient way of producing the same red/white indication. The pilot sees the same PAPI either way.

14 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between PAPI LED and PAPI Lamp?

The light source and its associated optical/electrical design. PAPI LED uses an LED array and driver; PAPI Lamp uses a conventional incandescent or halogen lamp. Both provide the same visual approach-slope indication.

Does LED PAPI use less energy than lamp PAPI?

Generally yes — LED requires less energy to produce the same light output. The exact saving depends on the specific design, operating intensity and control system, so a fixed percentage should not be assumed.

Does LED PAPI need a lens heater?

Usually yes. Incandescent lamps generate enough heat to help prevent dew, frost or ice on the lens as a side effect, while LED sources run cooler and typically need a dedicated anti-condensation/anti-icing heater.

Can an existing lamp PAPI simply be retrofitted with LED units?

Not as a simple bulb swap. The complete optical and electrical system needs evaluation for certification, photometric performance, chromaticity, power supply/CCR compatibility, thermal management and the existing mounting and aiming arrangement.

Does LED PAPI reduce all maintenance?

It reduces light-source replacement specifically. Lens cleaning, alignment, photometric verification and electrical inspection are still required on either technology.

Which technology is preferred for a new PAPI installation?

LED is generally preferred for new installations because of lower energy consumption, longer source life and lower lifecycle cost, provided the complete system meets the applicable photometric and certification requirements.

15 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I · Doc 9157 Part 4

Photometric, chromaticity and configuration requirements for PAPI, applicable regardless of the light source technology used; Doc 9157 Part 4 gives the design guidance.

FAA AC 150/5345-28H — PAPI Systems

Equipment specification for L-880 (4-unit) and L-881 (2-unit) PAPI, in voltage-powered Style A and series-powered Style B. Sets a 1,000-hour minimum lamp life, full intensity within 5 seconds of cold start, and lens dew/frost prevention; LED sources are permitted under the criteria in Engineering Brief No. 67.

IS 12290 · IS 12291 (both 1987)

Indian standards for isolating transformers (IS 12290) and constant current regulators (IS 12291) for airport lighting — relevant to the series-circuit supply, not to PAPI light-unit photometrics. Both are dated, and Indian AGL tenders commonly call up FAA/ICAO requirements for the fixtures themselves.

FAA Engineering Brief No. 67

Light Sources Other Than Incandescent and Xenon for Airport and Obstruction Lighting Fixtures — the performance and chromaticity criteria an LED PAPI is evaluated against.

DGCA CAR Section 4, Series B, Part I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — the Indian regulatory requirement that adopts the ICAO Annex 14 visual-aid provisions; also verify AAI and project-specific specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Do not specify or retrofit "LED PAPI" against a generic web comparison. The final equipment must be evaluated against the current governing standard, manufacturer certification and project-specific requirements.

PAPI LED is not a different indication system from PAPI Lamp — it is the same visual glidepath principle delivered through a more energy-efficient, longer-life light source, with its own set of engineering trade-offs to manage.
