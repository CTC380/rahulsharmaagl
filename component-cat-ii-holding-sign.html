CAT II Holding Sign

A red mandatory instruction sign with white text that marks the Category II holding position, located further from the runway to protect ILS critical areas during low‑visibility operations. Pilots and vehicles must stop and hold at this position before entering the runway.

Overview

What Is It?

A CAT II Holding Sign is a red mandatory instruction sign with white text that identifies the holding position for Category II precision approach operations. This holding position is located further from the runway threshold than the standard (CAT I) holding position to protect the ILS critical area and ensure signal integrity during low‑visibility approaches. The sign typically displays "CAT II" or "CAT II/III" along with the runway designation. It is a critical component for runway safety and incursion prevention during low‑visibility operations, and it is used in conjunction with stop bar lights and holding position markings.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The CAT II holding sign exists because the standard CAT I holding position, adequate for ordinary operations, sits too close to the runway to guarantee ILS signal integrity once low-visibility procedures are active. Its purpose is to mark a second, more conservative stop line so that any aircraft or vehicle still waiting to enter the runway remains clear of the ILS critical area for the duration of a CAT II approach, protecting the accuracy of the guidance the arriving crew is relying on. Because the applicable holding position changes depending on which approach category is in use, the sign also functions as a procedural cue, reminding crews and vehicle drivers that low-visibility procedures are active and that the closer CAT I line no longer applies.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Installed at airports that support CAT II and CAT III precision approach operations. The sign is placed at the CAT II holding position, which is typically located 250–300 metres from the runway threshold, or as defined by the ILS critical area boundary. It is positioned on the side of the taxiway, at an installed height of 0.7 to 1.1 metres above the pavement, on a frangible support. These signs are used at major international airports with low‑visibility capabilities and are essential for ensuring that aircraft and vehicles do not enter the ILS critical area during precision approaches.

Placement

Installation & Location

The sign's post foundation is set on the taxiway shoulder at the CAT II holding position distance defined by the ILS critical area survey for that runway, not a generic setback figure, since the exact distance shifts with localizer siting and runway category. The frangible mount is set back clear of aircraft wingtip clearance but close enough to the pavement edge to stay legible from the cockpit at the holding point. Illuminated panels are fed by cable from the same circuit as the co-located stop bar and holding-position lights so the sign, stop bar, and pavement marking can be commissioned and inspected as one holding-position installation; non-illuminated panels rely on retroreflective sheeting and need only a mechanical fixing.

Function

How Does It Work?

The sign displays white text on a red background, following ICAO Annex 14 and FAA standards. The text typically reads "CAT II" (with or without the runway designator) to indicate the holding position category. The sign may be non‑illuminated (using retroreflective sheeting) or internally illuminated (LED) for nighttime visibility. The sign is positioned to be clearly visible to pilots and vehicle drivers as they approach the holding point. It works in conjunction with CAT II holding position markings on the pavement and stop bar lights to ensure compliance with ATC instructions during low‑visibility operations. The sign is mounted on a frangible support to minimise aircraft damage in case of collision.

Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

An illuminated CAT II holding position sign shares its electrical circuit with the co-located stop bar, so the two are commanded, powered, and fault-monitored as a single loop rather than as separate systems. The Airfield Lighting Control and Monitoring System (ALCMS) selects the intensity step for that holding-position loop based on RVR and ATC instruction, and the CCR regulates the loop to the corresponding fixed current; the sign's isolating transformer taps that same current, so the sign's illumination rises and falls in step with the stop bar it sits beside rather than being switched independently. This matters operationally: an ATC command to illuminate the stop bar to bar traffic at the CAT II position also brings the sign to full intensity at the same time, reinforcing the same instruction visually in two ways at once. Fault detection is loop-based — a voltage rise at constant current flags an open-circuit lamp or failed transformer somewhere on the holding-position loop, without by itself distinguishing whether the fault lies in the sign, the stop bar, or another fixture on the same circuit, so field inspection follows to isolate the specific component. Where the sign is a non-illuminated, retroreflective panel instead, none of this applies — it carries no cable or transformer and depends entirely on aircraft and vehicle lights for visibility, with no loop membership or fault reporting of its own.

The distance isn't arbitrary — it's set by how much of the ILS critical area needs to stay clear of reflective interference for a given approach minimum. CAT II approaches fly to lower decision heights than CAT I, so the critical area (and the buffer protecting it) is larger, pushing the holding position further back. CAT III pushes it back again for the same reason, at an even lower minimum — see the CAT III Holding Sign page for how that position also has to protect a second zone.
Construction

Physical Construction

The panel is built on an extruded aluminium frame carrying a flat face of either engineering-grade retroreflective sheeting (Class 1 or 2) with the "CAT II" legend cut or screen-printed in white on red, or a translucent polycarbonate/acrylic panel with the same legend applied as a perforated film for internally illuminated versions, lit by an LED module run at low voltage from the adjacent circuit. The frame is sealed against the pavement environment and bolted to a frangible coupling — a breakaway post engineered to shear at a defined load if struck by an aircraft or vehicle — set on a base plate anchored below grade, keeping the sign rigid enough to hold its legend flat while still failing safely on impact.

Prerequisites

Installation Requirements

Setting-Out & Siting Survey
Civil

Survey and mark the sign position from the design/siting drawing before excavation, confirming clearance from the taxiway/runway edge safety area and any frangibility height limit.

Foundation / Base Plate Installation
Civil

Excavate and set the base plate or footing to design depth, keeping the finished mount level and within the frangible height limit for the sign's location.

Frangible Mount Fitting
Mechanical

Fit the frangible post or breakaway coupling and torque to the specified value, confirming it shears as designed under lateral load and does not bind.

Cable Route & Connector Termination
Electrical

Where the sign is internally illuminated, lay the duct/cable route to the panel and terminate the L-823 connector per manufacturer procedure, keeping the splice fully submersible-rated before backfill.

Isolating Transformer Matching
Electrical

Where the sign is internally illuminated, confirm the isolating transformer wattage and ratio match the panel and the series circuit's rated current step before connection.

Panel Legend & Content Setting
Quality

Before the panel is fixed and the position closed out, confirm the panel reads "CAT II" (or "CAT II/III") with the correct runway designation, and that the sign is sited at the surveyed CAT II holding position rather than the CAT I position.

Retroreflective Sheeting Application
Mechanical

Apply and inspect the retroreflective sheeting for correct grade, alignment, and freedom from bubbles or edge lifting before the panel is mounted.

Pre-Backfill Sign-off
Documentation

Record base/footing position, frangible coupling torque, cable route, and panel content as-built before backfill, since these are not inspectable once the site is closed.

Verification

Testing & Commissioning

Insulation Resistance (Megger) Test
Pre-Energisation

Where the sign is internally illuminated, megger-test the isolating transformer and internal wiring before the circuit is energised, confirming insulation resistance clears the acceptable minimum ahead of backfill.

Loop Continuity & Resistance Test
Pre-Energisation

For an illuminated installation, verify series loop continuity through the sign and record loop resistance, ruling out open circuits or high-resistance splices before first energisation.

Primary/Secondary Voltage Verification
Energisation

With the CCR energised, confirm secondary voltage at the illuminated sign's L-823 connector matches the transformer's rated ratio at the commissioned current step.

CCR Brightness Step Response
Energisation

Cycle the CCR through every intensity step and confirm an illuminated panel responds correctly at each, ruling out a marginal transformer or a loose connection.

Retroreflectivity / Luminance Verification
Final Acceptance

Measure sheeting retroreflectivity with a retroreflectometer against ICAO/FAA commissioning minimums; where the panel is internally illuminated, also verify luminance and colour uniformity with a luminance meter.

Legend & Content Verification
Final Acceptance

Confirm the panel reads "CAT II" (or "CAT II/III") together with the correct runway designation, and that the sign is sited at the surveyed CAT II holding position rather than the CAT I position.

Frangible Coupling & Mounting Verification
Pre-Energisation

Confirm the frangible coupling, base depth, and panel height meet as-built tolerances before the site is finally backfilled.

Documentation & As-Built Sign-off
Final Acceptance

Record retroreflectivity/luminance readings, and (where illuminated) insulation, continuity, and voltage readings, alongside as-built position in the commissioning dossier for handover.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Retroreflective Sheeting Degradation

UV exposure, weathering, and abrasion can reduce the reflectivity of the red background and white text, making the sign difficult to read at night or in low visibility. Regular inspection and replacement (typically every 5–7 years) is essential.

Internal Illumination Failure

LED modules inside illuminated signs can fail, reducing visibility at night. Regular testing of the illumination system and replacement of failed components is required, especially before low‑visibility seasons.

Frangible Support Fatigue

Breakaway couplings can become brittle over time due to weather and vibration. Regular inspection and replacement as per manufacturer guidelines are required to ensure breakaway performance.

Moisture Ingress

Water ingress through seals can cause corrosion and electrical faults. Routine seal checks and use of desiccants are recommended, especially in wet climates.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Red Background or White Text Unreadable at Night
Likely Cause
Retroreflective sheeting has degraded past its useful reflectivity, or (on an illuminated sign) the internal LED module has failed or dimmed.
Diagnostic Steps
Take a night or low-light reflectivity/luminance reading against the applicable ICAO/FAA minimum; on illuminated signs, confirm supply voltage is present at the fixture before condemning the LED module.
Corrective Action
Replace the retroreflective face panel if sheeting is below minimum, or swap the LED module/driver if illumination has failed; re-verify with a follow-up reading, especially ahead of low-visibility season.
Illuminated Sign Dark or Flickering
Likely Cause
Failed LED module or driver, a loose/corroded cable connection, or an upstream circuit fault feeding the sign.
Diagnostic Steps
Check for supply voltage at the sign's connector; inspect and re-torque cable terminations; if voltage is present but the sign stays dark, isolate the LED module/driver.
Corrective Action
Replace the LED module or driver, or re-terminate corroded connections with fresh crimps and sealant; confirm the upstream circuit if no voltage reaches the sign.
Moisture or Condensation Inside Housing
Likely Cause
Failed gasket or seal at the frame or cable entry, allowing water ingress and risking corrosion of internal electrical contacts.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect gaskets and cable glands for cracking or compression set; check for visible condensation or staining inside the housing; verify desiccant (if fitted) hasn't saturated.
Corrective Action
Replace failed gaskets and cable glands, dry out the housing, refresh or add desiccant, and re-check for recurrence ahead of the next low-visibility operational period.
Sign Leaning, Loose, or Misaligned
Likely Cause
Frangible coupling has fatigued or partially sheared, or the base plate/subgrade has settled, shifting the sign out of its correct orientation.
Diagnostic Steps
Visually and physically check the frangible coupling for cracking or play; confirm the sign face is still square and legible from the approach; inspect the base plate and surrounding pavement for settlement.
Corrective Action
Replace the frangible coupling on schedule if fatigued, re-level and re-orient the sign, and address any base plate or subgrade settlement to prevent recurrence — critical given this sign marks a CAT II holding position.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

LED Panel / Internal Light Source Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Thermal cycling or driver-circuit fault in the LED panel of an illuminated unit, or a manufacturing defect in the light source.
Effect
Sign face goes dark or dim on the illuminated variant, removing the sign's night/low-visibility conspicuity while the panel itself remains structurally intact.
Detection Method
Night or low-light visual check against the panel's expected output, and driver-circuit voltage check at the housing.
Retroreflective Sheeting Degradation
Medium Severity
Root Cause
UV exposure, surface abrasion, or accumulated grime/rubber film reducing the sheeting's coefficient of retroreflection over time.
Effect
Reduced legibility under aircraft or vehicle landing-light illumination at night, most critical for non-illuminated units that depend entirely on retroreflectivity for visibility.
Detection Method
Scheduled retroreflectivity measurement against ICAO Annex 14 / FAA minimums, and visual inspection for sheeting delamination or contamination.
Frangible Coupling Fatigue / Impact Damage
High Severity
Root Cause
Cyclic wind loading fatiguing the frangible coupling over time, or a direct strike from a vehicle, aircraft component, or ground equipment.
Effect
Sign leans, misaligns, or separates from its post, degrading legibility and, if debris remains in the movement area, creating a foreign-object hazard.
Detection Method
Post alignment and coupling condition check during scheduled inspection, and immediate post-incident inspection following any reported strike.
Power/Circuit Connector Degradation
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Moisture ingress at the base can, gasket, or connector feeding an illuminated unit, or a corroded/loose termination from age.
Effect
Intermittent illumination or a partial ground fault on the feeding circuit, affecting the illuminated unit's reliability independent of the panel itself.
Detection Method
Insulation resistance (megger) testing of the feeder circuit, and visual inspection of the base can, gasket, and connector during maintenance.
Legibility Shortfall in Low-Visibility Conditions
High Severity
Root Cause
Reduced photometric output or sheeting reflectivity falling below the margin needed for reliable recognition in the reduced-visibility conditions CAT II operations are conducted under.
Effect
Holding position sign is not reliably read by flight crews in the low-visibility conditions it exists to protect, undermining ILS critical area protection during CAT II approaches.
Detection Method
Photometric/retroreflectivity testing held to a tighter margin than standard signage given the low-visibility operating environment, at a shorter inspection interval.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Circuit Isolation Before Access
Electrical Safety

De-energise and lock out the feeder circuit at the CCR or panel, then verify zero energy at the base can before opening the housing or handling cable connectors.

Airside Work Coordination
Operational

Any work on or near the movement area requires ATC/airside coordination and a NOTAM where the sign will be dark, obscured, or out of tolerance, with vehicle and personnel movement kept clear of the taxiway or runway strip per the aerodrome's safety management procedures.

Frangibility Not to Be Defeated
Mechanical

Never substitute the frangible coupling with a rigid fitting or over-torque it during installation or repair; the coupling must still shear cleanly on aircraft impact to limit damage under the frangibility requirement.

FOD Control at the Base
Operational

Keep tools, fasteners, and removed gaskets accounted for at all times when the base can or housing is open, and secure or remove any loose material before reinstating the sign, to prevent foreign object debris on the movement area.

Insulation Test Before Re-Energisation
Electrical Safety

After any cable, connector, or panel work, megger test the affected circuit before restoring power, since a residual fault at re-energisation can trip the feeder circuit or damage healthy units on the same loop.

PPE & Grounding
Personnel Safety

Use insulated tools and appropriate PPE when working on circuit connections, and confirm the sign housing and base can are bonded/grounded per the electrical design before reconnecting the circuit.

Precision-Approach Critical Area Discipline
Operational

Because this sign marks a CAT II ILS critical/sensitive area boundary, treat any outage or obscuration as operationally significant: notify ATC immediately, NOTAM the condition, and restrict CAT II approaches until the sign is restored or an equivalent procedural safeguard is in place.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Hold This Sign to a Tighter Margin, Not Just a Standard Pass
Inspection

Because this sign protects a CAT II critical area where the visual recognition margin is already reduced, treat photometric and retroreflectivity readings that barely clear the standard minimum as due for replacement soon — not as a pass to file away until the next cycle.

Rule Out Sheeting Fade Before Condemning the LED Driver
Troubleshooting

A housing that “reads dark” on a night patrol isn't automatically a driver or LED fault — take a reflectivity/luminance reading first, since degraded sheeting on a partially-illuminated design can look like an electrical failure when it isn't.

Time Seal Inspection to the Fog Season, Not the Calendar
Maintenance

Gasket and desiccant condition matters most exactly when CAT II operations actually happen. Schedule the housing seal check ahead of the season when low-visibility approaches pick up, rather than on a generic annual date that might land mid-dry-season.

Don't Let Coupling Wear Wait for a Legibility Complaint
Mechanical

A leaning or slightly misaligned frangible coupling on this sign should trigger priority replacement even if the face still reads fine, because the geometry it marks matters as much as its legibility — a tilted sign can misrepresent the boundary it's protecting.

Any Reposition Needs the ILS Survey, Not a Field Estimate
Compliance

After pavement work disturbs the foundation, re-survey the sign's position against the ILS critical area protection survey specifically — a position that looks “close enough” by eye can still sit outside the boundary the survey defines.

Log Marginal Readings, Not Just Fails
Reliability

Keep a running record of readings that pass but trend downward, not only outright failures. A sign heading toward the minimum ahead of the low-visibility season is worth pre-emptive replacement rather than waiting for it to actually fail.

Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about the CAT II holding sign: purpose, siting, power, standards, maintenance and common faults.

Q1What is a CAT II holding sign?

A CAT II holding sign is a red mandatory instruction sign with white text that identifies the holding position for Category II precision approach operations. The position is further from the runway than the standard CAT I holding position, to protect the ILS critical area and signal integrity during low-visibility approaches.

Q2What does a CAT II holding sign display?

White text on a red background reading CAT II, or CAT II/III, with or without the runway designator, following ICAO Annex 14 and FAA standards.

Q3Where is the CAT II holding position?

Typically 250 to 300 metres from the runway threshold, or as defined by the ILS critical area boundary. The sign stands at the side of the taxiway at an installed height of 0.7 to 1.1 metres above the pavement on a frangible support, at airports that support CAT II and CAT III precision approaches.

Q4How does a CAT II holding sign differ from a CAT III holding sign?

The CAT III holding position is further from the runway than both CAT I and CAT II, typically 300 to 400 metres or more from the threshold, to give maximum protection to the ILS critical and sensitive areas in the lowest-visibility (CAT IIIA, IIIB and IIIC) operations. The CAT II position typically sits 250 to 300 metres from the threshold.

Q5How does a CAT II holding sign work with markings and stop bars?

It works with the CAT II holding position markings on the pavement and stop bar lights to ensure compliance with ATC instructions during low-visibility operations. Together they mark the point where pilots and vehicles must stop and hold before entering the runway.

Q6How is an illuminated CAT II holding sign powered?

An illuminated CAT II holding sign is wired onto the constant current series circuit for its section of the airfield and fed through its own isolating transformer rather than directly from the loop. The Constant Current Regulator (CCR) holds the loop at a fixed current (up to 6.6 A, with steps of 2.8, 3.4, 4.1, 5.2 and 6.6 A) and the ALCMS selects the brightness step, so the sign burns at the same intensity as the other fixtures on that loop. The non-illuminated variant is a passive retroreflective panel with no electrical supply.

Q7Which standards apply to CAT II holding signs?

ICAO Annex 14 covers colours, dimensions and placement, and FAA AC 150/5340-18 covers siting, design and colour convention. FAA AC 150/5345-44 is the equipment specification for the sign unit, ASTM D4956 sets retroreflective sheeting performance, and IEC 60598 covers luminaires for illuminated signs.

Q8What are the photometric requirements for a CAT II holding sign?

On the illuminated variant the red background should average at least 30 cd/m² (uniformity no worse than 10:1) and the white legend at least 150 cd/m², giving a 5:1 to 10:1 contrast ratio. The non-illuminated variant needs a retroreflective coefficient of at least 50 cd/lux/m² for white and 15 cd/lux/m² for red per ASTM D4956 Class 1 or 2.

Q9How often should CAT II holding signs be inspected and maintained?

Typical intervals are quarterly for cleaning dirt, de-icing fluid residue and rubber deposits off the sign face, and annual for retroreflectivity measurement, illumination testing, frangible coupling inspection, seal and cable gland checks, and insulation resistance (megger) testing. Retroreflective sheeting is usually budgeted for replacement every 5 to 7 years.

Q10What are the common field issues with CAT II holding signs?

Four issues recur: retroreflective sheeting degradation from UV, weathering and abrasion (replacement is typically due every 5 to 7 years); failure of the internal LED illumination, which cuts night visibility; frangible support fatigue, as breakaway couplings turn brittle with weather and vibration; and moisture ingress through damaged seals or cable glands, which causes corrosion and electrical faults. Routine seal checks and desiccants are recommended.
