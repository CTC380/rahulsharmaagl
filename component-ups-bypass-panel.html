UPS Bypass Panel

A maintenance bypass switchgear assembly that enables the UPS to be isolated and safely serviced without interrupting power to critical AGL loads. The UPS Bypass Panel provides a manual or automatic transfer path around the UPS, ensuring continuous power availability during UPS maintenance, battery replacement, or failure, and is an essential component for high‑availability AGL power systems.

Overview

What Is It?

A UPS Bypass Panel — also known as a maintenance bypass switch, UPS bypass switchgear, or static bypass panel — is a switchgear assembly that provides an alternate power path around an Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS). It typically consists of a combination of manually operated or motorised switches (isolators, circuit breakers, or contactors) that allow the UPS to be taken offline for maintenance, repair, or replacement while the connected critical loads (ALCMS servers, communication equipment, CCR control circuits) remain powered from the mains supply.

Key characteristics of the UPS Bypass Panel include:

  • Maintenance Bypass Path: Provides a direct connection from the utility (mains) input to the UPS output, bypassing the UPS entirely. This allows the UPS to be isolated and de‑energised for safe servicing.
  • Isolation Switching: Includes input and output isolators (switches or circuit breakers) that allow the UPS to be completely disconnected from both the supply and the load.
  • Interlock and Safety Logic: Implements mechanical or electrical interlocks to prevent accidental parallel operation of the UPS and bypass paths, ensuring that the UPS is not connected while the bypass is closed, and vice versa.
  • Indication and Monitoring: Provides status indication (LEDs, meters, or touchscreen) showing the position of the bypass switch, input/output voltage/current, and alarm conditions (e.g., bypass not available).
  • Automatic or Manual Operation: Can be configured as manually operated (for planned maintenance) or automatic (with motorised switches for remote or automated transfer in case of UPS failure).
  • Form Factor: Typically housed in a floor‑standing or wall‑mounted enclosure (IP20 or higher) with lockable doors, suitable for installation in electrical rooms, control centres, or equipment shelters.
  • Integration with ALCMS: May include communication interfaces (Modbus, SNMP) to report bypass status and alarms to the ALCMS for remote monitoring.

In airfield lighting systems, the UPS Bypass Panel is a critical part of the power distribution chain that feeds the ALCMS, communication networks, and control electronics. It ensures that these systems remain operational during UPS maintenance, battery change‑out, or in the event of a UPS fault, preventing downtime that could compromise airfield lighting control and monitoring.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The bypass panel exists to decouple "the UPS needs maintenance" from "the equipment it protects must go dark." ALCMS servers, communication gateways, and CCR control circuits are not things an airfield can simply switch off for an afternoon while a battery is replaced or a UPS module is serviced — doing so would mean losing monitoring and control capability over some or all of the lighting system during that window. By routing power directly from the mains around the UPS, the panel lets that maintenance happen with zero interruption to the loads it protects. The interlock logic is just as important as the bypass path itself: it exists specifically to prevent a technician from accidentally creating a dangerous parallel-source condition, ensuring the convenience of maintenance-without-downtime never comes at the cost of an unsafe switching error.

Application

Where Is It Used?

The UPS Bypass Panel is deployed in all AGL installations where UPS systems protect critical control and communication equipment. Key applications include:

  • ALCMS Server and Workstation Power: Providing a maintenance bypass for UPS units that supply power to the ALCMS servers, workstations, and operator consoles.
  • Communication Equipment Racks: Enabling safe servicing of UPS units that power network switches, routers, communication gateways, and fiber optic equipment.
  • CCR Control Circuits: Protecting the power supply to CCR controllers, monitoring panels, and communication interfaces that are fed from UPS systems.
  • SCADA and RTU Power: Providing bypass capability for UPS units that power SCADA servers, remote terminal units (RTUs), and telemetry equipment.
  • Control Room Power Distribution: Located in the main electrical room or control centre, offering a convenient point for isolating UPS units without affecting the entire facility.
  • Remote Substations and Equipment Shelters: Deployed in remote substations where UPS maintenance would otherwise require a complete shutdown of local AGL control equipment.
  • Airport Operations Centres: Supporting the high‑availability power requirements of airport operations centres that rely on UPS‑protected AGL control systems.
Placement

Installation & Location

The bypass panel is installed immediately adjacent to the UPS it protects, in the same electrical room or equipment shelter, wired in-line between the utility supply, the UPS, and the protected load so that all three paths — mains input, UPS output, and bypass — meet at a single switching point rather than being routed separately. It is mounted in its own floor-standing or wall-mounted enclosure with lockable doors, positioned for clear access to the switch handles and status indication so a technician can safely operate the isolators without needing to reach into the UPS cabinet itself. Cabling in and out of the panel is sized and protected to match the full load current of the equipment it serves, with the interlock wiring between the UPS and bypass switch positions kept short and direct so the mechanical or electrical lockout logic cannot be defeated by a wiring fault. Where the panel reports status to the ALCMS, its communication link is run alongside the general control-room network cabling rather than through the UPS itself, so bypass status remains visible even while the UPS is fully isolated for service.

Function

How Does It Work?

The UPS Bypass Panel operates as a transfer switch that allows the load to be fed directly from the mains supply while isolating the UPS. Key functional aspects include:

  • Normal Operation: In normal operation, the UPS input is fed from the mains, and the UPS output feeds the critical load through the panel. The bypass switch is open (OFF), and the UPS is online, providing conditioned power and battery backup.
  • Maintenance Bypass Procedure: When maintenance is required, the operator performs a controlled transfer:
    • First, ensure the UPS is in bypass mode (if available) or synchronise the UPS output with the mains (if the UPS supports static bypass).
    • Close the maintenance bypass switch (or circuit breaker) to connect the load directly to the mains.
    • Open the UPS output isolator to disconnect the load from the UPS.
    • Open the UPS input isolator to disconnect the UPS from the mains.
    • The UPS is now fully isolated and can be safely serviced, while the load continues to receive mains power via the bypass path.
  • Restoration Procedure: To restore normal operation:
    • Close the UPS input isolator to apply mains power to the UPS.
    • Start the UPS and allow it to synchronise and charge.
    • Close the UPS output isolator to connect the load back to the UPS.
    • Open the maintenance bypass switch to remove the bypass path.
    • The load is now fed from the UPS again.
  • Interlock Logic: To prevent unsafe operation, the panel includes mechanical or electrical interlocks that prevent the bypass switch from being closed while the UPS output isolator is closed, and vice versa. This ensures that the UPS and bypass are never paralleled (which could cause damage).
  • Automatic Transfer (Optional): In advanced panels, the bypass switch can be motorised and integrated with the UPS monitoring system. If the UPS fails (overload, over temperature, or internal fault), the system can automatically transfer the load to bypass to prevent a total power loss.
  • Monitoring and Indication: The panel provides local indication of switch positions (ON/OFF), input and output voltages, and current. It may also include alarm contacts for remote monitoring of bypass status and faults.

The panel is designed for fail‑safe operation: the bypass path is a simple, passive connection (no active electronics) to ensure maximum reliability. In the event of a complete power failure on the mains, the bypass path will not provide power, so the load would rely on the UPS if it is still online or on battery until power is restored.

Construction

Physical Construction

The panel is built as freestanding or wall-mounted switchgear, typically a sheet-steel enclosure with front-access doors, housing the isolators, circuit breakers, or contactors that form the bypass, input, and output switching paths as busbar-connected assemblies rather than loose point-to-point wiring. Manually operated switches use externally accessible handles interlocked so an operator cannot select an unsafe combination of positions (such as isolating the UPS output while still connected to bypass), while motorised or static bypass versions add contactors or solid-state switches driven by the panel's internal control logic. Busbars and connections are sized and spaced to the panel's full-load current rating with clear phase segregation, and the enclosure includes cable gland plates top or bottom for the incoming mains, UPS, and load-side cabling, along with a control-wiring compartment kept physically separated from the power busbars for safety and to reduce electrical noise on any monitoring circuits.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Incorrect Bypass Sequence

Performing the bypass transfer in the wrong sequence (e.g., opening the UPS output before closing the bypass) can cause a complete loss of power to the load. Always follow the manufacturer's documented procedure, which typically involves putting the UPS into static bypass (if available) before closing the maintenance bypass. Use clearly labelled switches and interlock keys to enforce the correct sequence.

Mechanical Interlock Failure

Over time, mechanical interlocks (key‑operated) can wear or become misaligned, potentially allowing both the bypass and UPS output to be closed simultaneously. This can cause back‑feeding and damage to the UPS. Regularly inspect and maintain the interlock mechanism. Consider using electrical interlocks (contactors) as a secondary safeguard.

Loose Connections and Overheating

High current through the bypass path can cause terminal connections to heat up if not properly torqued. Periodic thermographic inspections should be performed, and all connections should be re‑torqued to manufacturer specifications during scheduled maintenance.

Inadequate Short‑Circuit Rating

The bypass switch and associated cabling must be rated for the prospective short‑circuit current of the supply. If the panel is undersized, a fault could cause catastrophic failure. Ensure the panel's short‑circuit withstand rating matches the upstream protection.

Communication Failure with ALCMS

If the panel provides remote monitoring, loss of communication may prevent operators from knowing the bypass status. Implement redundant communication paths and local indication (LEDs) that remain active even if the communication link is lost.

Ageing of Isolating Switches

Mechanical switches have a limited number of operations. After many cycles, contacts can become pitted or eroded, leading to high resistance and heating. Plan for periodic replacement based on the switch's mechanical life and the frequency of bypass operations.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Load Lost Power During a Bypass Transfer
Likely Cause
The bypass sequence was performed out of order (e.g. opening the UPS output before closing the bypass), interrupting supply to the load.
Diagnostic Steps
Review the operation log against the manufacturer's documented bypass sequence to confirm the order of operations used.
Corrective Action
Retrain personnel on the correct documented sequence, and confirm labelling and interlock keys are in place to enforce it going forward.
Both Bypass and UPS Output Found Closed Simultaneously
Likely Cause
A worn or misaligned mechanical (key-operated) interlock has failed to prevent both paths from being closed at once.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect the interlock mechanism for wear, misalignment, or failure to engage; check for evidence of back-feed damage to the UPS.
Corrective Action
Repair or replace the mechanical interlock, and consider adding an electrical (contactor) interlock as a secondary safeguard.
Bypass Path Running Warm
Likely Cause
High current through the bypass path has heated loose or under-torqued terminal connections.
Diagnostic Steps
Perform a thermographic inspection of the bypass path connections during normal load.
Corrective Action
Re-torque all connections to the manufacturer's specification during the next scheduled maintenance window.
Panel or Cabling Damaged Following a Fault
Likely Cause
The bypass switch or cabling was undersized for the supply's prospective short-circuit current.
Diagnostic Steps
Check the panel's short-circuit withstand rating against the upstream protection's let-through energy.
Corrective Action
Replace the panel or cabling with correctly rated equipment matching the upstream protection before returning to service.
Bypass Status Not Visible at the ALCMS
Likely Cause
A communication link failure between the panel and the ALCMS has cut off remote status reporting.
Diagnostic Steps
Check the communication link status and cabling; confirm whether local LED indication is still functioning.
Corrective Action
Restore the communication link, and confirm local indication remains a reliable fallback independent of the link.
Switch Operation Feels Stiff or Shows Heating After Many Cycles
Likely Cause
The isolating switch has accumulated wear from repeated operations, pitting or eroding its contacts.
Diagnostic Steps
Check the switch's operation count against its rated mechanical life; inspect contacts for pitting if accessible.
Corrective Action
Plan for switch replacement based on remaining mechanical life and the site's bypass operation frequency.

Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Confirm Bypass Path Before UPS Isolation
Electrical Safety

Transfer critical loads to the bypass path and confirm they are stable on mains supply before isolating the UPS for maintenance; do not de-energise the UPS while it is still the sole source feeding connected loads.

Follow the Correct Switching Sequence
Electrical Safety

Operate the panel's isolators, breakers, or contactors in the manufacturer's specified sequence when transferring to or from bypass; an out-of-sequence operation can momentarily interrupt the load or create a make-before-break fault condition.

Lock Out the Isolated UPS
Electrical Safety

Once the UPS is isolated on the bypass panel, lock out and tag its input and output isolators before working on the UPS itself, and verify zero energy at its terminals despite the bypass being engaged.

Residual Battery Energy
Electrical Safety

Treat the UPS battery string as a separate, potentially hazardous energy source even after the unit is isolated from the bypass panel; follow the UPS's own isolation and lock-out procedure for the battery circuit before working on it.

Confirm Load Status Before and After Transfer
Operational

Verify which loads are fed through the panel and confirm their status before and after any transfer, since critical loads (ALCMS servers, CCR control circuits) losing power briefly during an uncoordinated switch can disrupt airfield lighting control.

PPE for Switching Operations
Personnel Safety

Use insulated tools and appropriate PPE when operating switchgear on the bypass panel, particularly where the incoming supply has a high prospective fault current.

FOD Control at the Panel
Operational

Keep tools and fasteners accounted for at all times when the panel is open, and secure or remove any loose material before closing it.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Do not leave the panel in bypass mode, with an isolator mid-position, or with a lock-out device still fitted after work is complete and verified safe; confirm the UPS is back in normal online operation before closing out the work order.

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Confirm the Bypass Path Is Actually Carrying Load Before Opening the UPS Isolators
Operational

The whole sequence depends on the bypass switch carrying load before the UPS is isolated. Verify load current on the bypass path itself, not just switch position — a switch that looks closed can still have a poor contact.

Interlocks Prevent Paralleling — Don't Work Around Them
Electrical Safety

The mechanical/electrical interlock exists specifically to stop the UPS output and bypass path from ever being closed simultaneously. Defeating it to speed up a transfer risks paralleling two sources that were never designed to be synchronised.

Follow the Sequence Exactly — Order Matters, Not Just End State
Operational

Closing the bypass switch before opening the UPS output isolator, and the reverse order on restoration, is what keeps the load from ever being dropped. Performing the steps out of order, even reaching the same final positions, can cause a momentary loss of power to critical AGL loads.

Battery Energy Persists After the UPS Is Isolated
Electrical Safety

Isolating the UPS from both mains and load doesn't discharge its battery. Treat the UPS as carrying stored energy until confirmed otherwise, since its internal battery circuit remains live independent of the isolators' position.

Confirm Load Status Immediately After Every Step, Not Just at Sign-Off
Commissioning

Verify voltage and current at the load right after each step of the bypass or restoration sequence — a fault introduced mid-sequence is far easier to isolate to that specific step than to find later during final sign-off.

FOD and Housekeeping Still Apply to Switchgear
Operational

Treat the bypass panel with the same tool and hardware accounting discipline as any other AGL component — a dropped tool inside energised switchgear is a far more serious event than the same mistake at a light fixture.

Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about UPS bypass panels: what they do, transfer sequence, interlocks, ratings, standards, maintenance and common faults.

Q1What is a UPS bypass panel?

A UPS bypass panel, also called a maintenance bypass switch or static bypass panel, is a switchgear assembly that provides an alternate power path around an uninterruptible power supply. It lets the UPS be taken offline for maintenance, repair or replacement while critical loads stay powered from the mains.

Q2Why does an AGL system need a UPS bypass panel?

ALCMS servers, communication gateways and CCR control circuits cannot simply be switched off while a UPS battery or module is serviced, or monitoring and control of some or all of the lighting system would be lost. The panel separates "the UPS needs maintenance" from "the protected equipment must go dark".

Q3What equipment does a bypass panel protect?

ALCMS servers, workstations and operator consoles, communication racks including switches, routers, gateways and fibre equipment, and CCR control circuits such as controllers, monitoring panels and communication interfaces.

Q4How does a UPS bypass panel work?

In normal operation the UPS is fed from the mains and its output feeds the load through the panel, with the bypass switch open. For maintenance the operator makes a controlled transfer to the direct mains-to-load bypass path, then isolates the UPS input and output. The documented manufacturer sequence must be followed.

Q5What happens if the bypass sequence is done wrongly?

Doing it out of order, for example opening the UPS output before closing the bypass, can cause a complete loss of power to the load. Use clearly labelled switches and interlock keys, and retrain personnel on the manufacturer's documented sequence if this has happened.

Q6What is the interlock for?

A mechanical key or electrical interlock stops both the bypass and the UPS output being closed at the same time, which would back-feed the UPS. A worn or misaligned mechanical interlock should be repaired or replaced, and an electrical interlock can be added.

Q7What are the typical ratings?

Voltage of 120 VAC, 230 VAC or 400 VAC three-phase to match the UPS, current ratings from 16 A to 400 A, at 50/60 Hz, with 1- to 4-pole switching. Switching is manual (rotary switch or circuit breaker) or motorised, and Form C relay outputs report bypass status, isolator position and faults.

Q8Where is a UPS bypass panel installed?

Immediately beside the UPS it protects, in the same electrical room or shelter, wired between the utility supply, the UPS and the load so all three paths meet at one switching point. It is mounted in its own lockable floor-standing or wall-mounted enclosure.

Q9Which standards apply to UPS bypass panels?

The main references are IEC 61439 (low-voltage switchgear and controlgear assemblies), IEC 60947 (switches, disconnectors and circuit breakers), UL 508A (industrial control panels) and NFPA 70 (NEC). Always verify the current edition and your approved requirements.

Q10How often should a UPS bypass panel be maintained?

Typical intervals are semi-annual for interlock function tests and communication and alarm function tests, and annual for a live bypass transfer test, contact and switch inspection, overcurrent protection check and terminal and busbar tightness with a thermal scan under load. Your approved maintenance programme takes precedence.
