AGL-CS-045 — Runway Centreline Light Water Ingress

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A moisture-investigation where multiple centreline inset lights failed after heavy rainfall due to deteriorated sealing and blocked drainage — from forensic fixture inspection through seal assessment, cable-entry examination, and drainage restoration.

Primary Skill

1Case Study Overview

Following several days of heavy rainfall, the maintenance team observed that multiple runway centreline inset lights exhibited reduced intensity, intermittent operation, and repeated fixture failures within the same runway section.

The Constant Current Regulator (CCR) remained healthy, and the primary circuit operated normally.

Inspection revealed that several inset fittings contained standing water inside the fixture housing, with visible corrosion, moisture contamination, and insulation deterioration.

A detailed forensic investigation identified failure of the fixture sealing system combined with inadequate drainage around the base can, allowing progressive water ingress into the optical and electrical compartments.

This case demonstrates that water ingress is one of the most destructive long-term failure mechanisms affecting runway centreline inset lighting systems.

3System Background

A runway centreline inset light consists of:

  • Optical assembly.
  • LED or lamp module.
  • Internal terminal assembly.
  • Cable-entry system.
  • Sealing gaskets.
  • O-rings.
  • Pressure-equalization components.
  • Fixture housing.
  • Base can installation.

Because the fixture is installed within the pavement surface, it is exposed to:

  • Rainwater.
  • Standing water.
  • Hydraulic pressure from aircraft traffic.
  • Temperature cycling.
  • Condensation.
  • Chemical contamination.

Long-term reliability depends heavily on effective environmental sealing.

7Water Ingress Mechanism

Normal protection:

Sealed housing → Dry electrical compartment → Stable insulation → Reliable operation

Failure mechanism:

Seal deterioration → Water entry → Corrosion → Insulation deterioration → Leakage current → Intermittent operation → Fixture failure

Water ingress is usually progressive, not sudden.

The affected inset fixtures were removed.

  • Corroded terminals.

  • Discolored insulation.

The moisture level exceeded normal condensation.

Step 3: Seal Examination

Inspection of the sealing system showed:

  • Compressed gasket deformation.
  • Surface cracking.
  • Loss of elasticity.
  • Contamination of sealing surfaces.
  • Improper compression.

The primary gasket had deteriorated.

Step 4: Cable-Entry Inspection

The cable-entry assembly showed:

  • Seal shrinkage.
  • Mechanical wear.
  • Inadequate compression.
  • Moisture migration along the cable.

The cable entry was a secondary ingress path.

Step 5: Drainage Assessment

The base can showed:

  • Sediment accumulation.
  • Blocked drainage path.
  • Standing water around the fixture.
  • Repeated water exposure.

Poor drainage significantly increased water exposure duration.

Step 6: Insulation Resistance Testing

Results:

Dry fixture Greater than 1000 MΩ
Wet fixture 8 MΩ

Moisture had severely reduced insulation performance.

Step 7: Corrosion Analysis

The terminal assembly exhibited:

  • Copper oxidation.
  • Galvanic corrosion.
  • Contact resistance increase.
  • Progressive conductor deterioration.

Corrosion was responsible for the intermittent electrical behavior.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The runway centreline light water ingress was caused by failure of the fixture sealing gasket combined with blocked base-can drainage, allowing standing water to enter the fixture housing through both the primary seal and cable-entry system.

The resulting moisture caused:

  • Terminal corrosion.
  • Insulation deterioration.
  • Increased contact resistance.
  • Leakage current.
  • Intermittent operation.
  • Progressive fixture failure.

11Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Removed all affected fixtures.
  2. Replaced sealing gaskets.
  3. Replaced damaged cable-entry seals.
  4. Cleaned and dried fixture housings.
  5. Replaced corroded terminal assemblies.
  6. Restored base-can drainage.
  7. Performed insulation resistance testing.
  8. Verified optical performance.
  9. Reinstalled the fixtures.
  10. Updated preventive maintenance procedures.

14Why This Case Was Important

Water ingress causes progressive reliability degradation, including:

  • Corrosion.
  • Insulation failure.
  • Optical degradation.
  • Intermittent faults.
  • Repeated maintenance.
  • CAT II/III reliability reduction.

Preventive sealing maintenance is often more valuable than repeated fixture replacement.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: Multiple centreline lights affected. Recent heavy rainfall. CCR healthy. Moisture visible inside the fixture. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace the CCR.
B. Increase the current setting.
C. Inspect fixture seals, cable-entry assemblies, internal moisture, and base-can drainage.
D. Replace all transformers.
Correct answer: C. The visible moisture and rainfall pattern point to an ingress and drainage problem. Seal inspection and drainage assessment will identify the root cause without unnecessary equipment replacement.

Quick Reference: Runway Centreline Light Colour & ICAO Requirements

ICAO runway centerline lights (Annex 14) are fitted on precision approach runways and certain others, along the centreline of the runway. Their colour changes towards the runway end so that a pilot can judge the remaining distance. Water ingress, as in this case, affects inset fixtures in particular, because they sit in the pavement.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about water ingress in runway centreline lights, based on this case study.

Q1What is AGL-CS-045?

AGL-CS-045 is an engineering training case study on water ingress into multiple runway centreline inset lights after several days of heavy rain. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2What symptoms appeared?

Multiple centreline lights showed reduced intensity, intermittent operation and repeated failures within the same runway section, concentrated after heavy rainfall. Several fixtures contained standing water, visible corrosion and insulation deterioration, while the CCR and primary circuit were normal.

Q3What was the root cause?

Failure of the fixture sealing gasket combined with blocked base-can drainage let standing water enter through both the primary seal and the cable-entry system.

Q4What did the moisture cause?

It caused terminal corrosion, insulation deterioration, increased contact resistance, leakage current, intermittent operation and progressive fixture failure.

Q5What should you inspect first when several lights fail after rain with moisture visible?

Inspect fixture seals, cable-entry assemblies, internal moisture and base-can drainage. Replacing the CCR or all transformers, or increasing the current setting, is not the right first step.

Q6How was the section repaired?

The team removed the affected fixtures, replaced sealing gaskets and cable-entry seals, cleaned and dried the housings and replaced corroded terminal assemblies. They restored base-can drainage, tested insulation resistance, verified optical performance, reinstalled the fixtures and updated preventive maintenance procedures.

Q7How was the repair verified?

Fixture insulation was greater than 1000 MΩ, there was no internal moisture, optical output was normal and drainage was restored. All affected centreline lights returned to normal operation.

Q8Which test equipment was used?

The team used a true-RMS multimeter, insulation resistance tester, moisture inspection equipment, thermal imaging camera, optical inspection tools, a low-resistance ohmmeter and pressure and seal inspection tools.

Q9What color are runway centerline lights?

Runway centerline lights are white along most of the runway. Towards the runway end the colour changes, with alternating red and white and then red only for the last section, to warn the pilot of the remaining distance. Confirm the exact lengths against the current ICAO Annex 14.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

Runway centreline light water ingress is primarily a sealing and environmental-control failure that progressively causes corrosion, insulation deterioration, contact resistance increase, and eventual secondary-circuit failure within inset lighting systems.

Systematic CCR verification, fixture removal, seal inspection, cable-entry assessment, drainage evaluation, insulation testing, and corrosion analysis can accurately identify the ingress mechanism and significantly improve the long-term reliability of runway centreline lighting.

In AGL engineering, water inside an inset fixture is not merely a maintenance issue; it is the beginning of a reliability failure that will continue until the sealing and drainage system is restored.
