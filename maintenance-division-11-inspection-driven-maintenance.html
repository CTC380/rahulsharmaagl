Inspection-Driven Maintenance

The operational bridge between the AGL Inspection Handbook and the AGL Maintenance Handbook. Division 11 does not describe what maintenance is performed on an asset — it describes how an inspection finding becomes a maintenance action: identified, classified, prioritized, triggered, repaired, verified, closed, and trended.

Core principle: Inspect → Identify Defect → Classify → Prioritize → Trigger Maintenance → Repair → Verify → Close → Trend. No significant inspection finding should be lost between inspection and maintenance closure — this is what makes Division 11 a closed-loop control system rather than a records log.

Defect Description Standard

A finding is only as useful as the description behind it. The description must be specific enough to act as a maintenance trigger without repeating the inspection.

Weak vs. Usable Description

  • Weak: "Light problem."
  • Better: "Runway edge light at identified location has damaged lens and visible contamination."
  • The second version identifies asset, defect, and condition — giving the maintenance team a usable trigger.

Minimum Finding Record

  • Date, time, location
  • Asset, Asset ID, circuit
  • Inspection type
  • Defect description
  • Photograph / evidence where applicable
  • Inspector and initial condition

Repeat-Defect Escalation Ladder

Repeat repair is not necessarily successful maintenance. The objective is to eliminate the underlying failure mechanism, not to keep repeating the same fix.

1
First Occurrence

Normal corrective maintenance.

2
Second Occurrence

Monitor recurrence.

3
Repeated Occurrence

Engineering review.

4
Persistent Recurrence

Root-cause investigation.

5
Continued Recurrence

Condition / replacement assessment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about inspection-driven maintenance, based on the topics in this division.

8 QUESTIONS
Q1What is Inspection-Driven Maintenance?

It is the operational bridge between the AGL Inspection Handbook and the AGL Maintenance Handbook. It does not describe what maintenance is done on an asset; it describes how an inspection finding becomes a maintenance action.

Q2What does Division 11 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 11 (Inspection-Driven Maintenance) contains 30 topics in four groups: Group A Inspection Finding to Maintenance (8 topics), Group B Inspection-Based Maintenance (10 topics), Group C Closure (6 topics) and Group D Condition Escalation (6 topics).

Q3What is the core principle of Inspection-Driven Maintenance?

Inspect, identify the defect, classify, prioritize, trigger maintenance, repair, verify, close and trend. No significant inspection finding should be lost between inspection and maintenance closure.

Q4What is the closed-loop workflow?

Every finding moves through eleven steps: Inspect, Identify, Classify, Prioritize, Trigger, Work Order, Maintain, Test, Post-Inspect, Close and Trend.

Q5How does defect classification drive the maintenance response?

Critical findings get an immediate or urgent response through emergency or priority corrective maintenance; Major findings are prioritized for corrective maintenance; Minor findings are scheduled for routine corrective maintenance; Observations are monitored with follow-up inspection; and a repeat defect goes to engineering review and root-cause or condition assessment. Categories should ultimately align with the airport's approved operational and safety criteria.

Q6What makes a defect description usable as a maintenance trigger?

It must identify the asset, the defect and the condition. 'Light problem' is weak, whereas 'Runway edge light at identified location has damaged lens and visible contamination' gives the maintenance team a usable trigger without repeating the inspection.

Q7What must a minimum finding record include?

Date, time and location; asset, asset ID and circuit; inspection type; defect description; a photograph or other evidence where applicable; and the inspector and initial condition.

Q8What is the repeat-defect escalation ladder?

A first occurrence gets normal corrective maintenance, a second occurrence is monitored for recurrence, a repeated occurrence goes to engineering review, persistent recurrence to root-cause investigation, and continued recurrence to condition or replacement assessment. Repeat repair is not necessarily successful maintenance; the aim is to eliminate the underlying failure mechanism.
