Frangible Coupling

A critical safety device designed to break under a predetermined load, allowing an elevated AGL fixture to separate cleanly from its base upon aircraft impact. This breakaway mechanism prevents catastrophic damage to aircraft wings and engines while protecting the fixture's mounting infrastructure.

Overview

What Is It?

A Frangible Coupling (also known as a breakaway coupling or shear coupling) is a mechanical safety device engineered to fracture at a specific, predetermined load. It serves as the structural interface between the elevated AGL fixture (or its support pole) and the fixed base plate or base can. The coupling is designed to break cleanly when subjected to a force within a defined range — commonly 250–450 kg (550–1000 lbs) in manufacturer ratings — which is low enough to minimize damage to an aircraft that strikes the fixture, yet high enough to withstand normal operational loads such as wind, vibration, and maintenance handling. Rather than fixing a single load figure, ICAO and FAA frangibility standards (ICAO Doc 9157, Part 6; FAA AC 150/5220-23A) define performance in terms of impact energy — the structure must break, distort, or yield under the collision forces of a reference aircraft (commonly cited as 3,000 kg / 6,600 lb) moving on the ground or airborne at a specified speed — and individual coupling ratings are engineered and tested to satisfy that criterion.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The frangible coupling's purpose is to resolve a direct conflict between two safety needs: an elevated fixture must stand up to everyday wind, vibration, and maintenance handling, yet it must not remain rigidly attached to its base if an aircraft strikes it. Without a deliberately engineered weak point, an elevated light or sign would act as a fixed obstacle capable of tearing into a wing, engine, or landing gear during an excursion or low approach. By fracturing within a tightly controlled load range, the coupling lets the fixture break away and lie down or displace instead of transmitting that impact force back into the airframe, converting a potentially catastrophic strike into a survivable one. This is why frangibility is mandated by ICAO and FAA standards for every elevated fixture in or near the movement area, and why a coupling that has fractured or been damaged must be replaced immediately — its protective function depends on the break point being exactly where it was designed to be.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Frangible couplings are mandatory safety components on all elevated AGL fixtures installed in or adjacent to aircraft movement areas. Common applications include:

  • Elevated Runway Edge Lights: Mounted on frangible poles or pedestals along the runway edge.
  • Elevated Taxiway Lights: Used on taxiway edge fixtures where elevated poles are installed.
  • Approach Lighting Masts: Frangible couplings on approach light structures to protect aircraft during overrun.
  • Obstruction Lights: Mounted on frangible masts for obstacle marking.
  • Wind Cone Masts: Used on frangible wind cone supports adjacent to runways.
  • Signage Structures: Frangible couplings for mandatory and information signs near runways.
Placement

Installation & Location

The coupling is fitted low on the pole or pedestal, close to grade, so the structure shears at (or just above) ground level and minimises the height of debris left standing after an impact. It is bolted between the base can or foundation stub and the pole itself, with the shear plane, weakened section, or friction joint set to the manufacturer's specified break-away load so the coupling fails predictably under aircraft impact but stays rigid under normal wind and service loads. Power cabling is looped with enough slack across the coupling that a break-away event separates the structure without tearing the conductors free, and the coupling is checked and re-torqued on the routine maintenance schedule since a coupling that has loosened or corroded may fail at the wrong load or shear irregularly.

Function

How Does It Work?

The frangible coupling operates on the principle of controlled structural failure. It is engineered with a weak point — typically using shear pins, frangible nuts, or a specifically designed neck section — that will fracture when the applied load exceeds the design threshold.

  • Shear Pins: Precision-machined pins that are designed to shear at a specific lateral force. The fixture is held in place by these pins, which snap under impact.
  • Frangible Nuts/Bolts: Fasteners with a reduced cross-section or specific material hardness that ensures they break before the main structure.
  • Engineered Neck: The coupling itself has a neck with a precise thickness calculated to fracture under a given bending moment.

After the coupling breaks, the fixture or pole separates cleanly from the base, lying flat on the ground or displacing sideways. This prevents the fixture from acting as a rigid obstacle that could tear into an aircraft's wing, engine, or landing gear. The coupling is strictly single-use and must be replaced after any impact event or if it shows signs of fatigue or damage.

Construction

Physical Construction

The coupling is precision-machined from aluminium alloy or a glass-reinforced composite, with a deliberately reduced cross-section (a necked waist or a ring of pre-scored shear points) engineered so the part fractures predictably within its rated load band rather than deforming or bending. Upper and lower flanges bolt to the fixture stem above and the base plate or base can below, so the coupling sits as a discrete, replaceable link in the mechanical load path. Because the fixture's power feed also runs through this joint, most couplings carry a breakaway electrical connector or a slack service loop of cable through the coupling body, so the cable itself separates or pulls free cleanly at the same load rather than tearing out the fixture's wiring. Fasteners are stainless steel and torqued to the manufacturer's spec, since over-torquing can raise the effective shear threshold above its rated range.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Fatigue Failure from Vibration

Continuous vibration from aircraft operations and wind can cause metal fatigue in the coupling, potentially lowering its breakaway threshold. Regular inspection and timely replacement (every 5 years) is critical to ensure it breaks at the correct load.

Corrosion Seizing the Mechanism

Corrosion, especially in coastal or high-moisture environments, can seize the shear pins or frangible nuts, preventing the coupling from breaking properly on impact. This poses a severe safety risk. Regular inspection and anti-corrosion treatment are essential.

Incorrect Shear Pin Replacement

Using non-specified bolts, pins, or nuts (e.g., standard hardware instead of frangible shear pins) can lead to a coupling that is either too weak (breaks under wind load) or too strong (fails to break on impact). Always use the manufacturer's specified frangible kit.

Reusing an Activated Coupling

Frangible couplings are strictly single-use. Even if a coupling appears intact after a minor impact, it is highly likely that its structural integrity is compromised. Reusing it is dangerous and violates safety standards.

Incorrect Torque on Fasteners

Over-tightening or under-tightening the fasteners securing the coupling can affect the breakaway force. Always use a calibrated torque wrench to the specified torque values.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Coupling Failed to Break Away on Impact
Likely Cause
Corrosion has seized the shear pins or frangible nuts, or non-specified hardware was installed in place of the manufacturer's frangible kit, raising the effective breakaway threshold.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect the coupling for corrosion at the shear pin/nut interfaces; verify the installed hardware matches the manufacturer's specified part numbers; treat the coupling as a safety-critical failure and take the fixture out of service pending replacement.
Corrective Action
Replace the entire coupling with a new, correctly specified frangible kit — never reuse or attempt to free a seized coupling — and schedule more frequent inspection if corrosion caused the seizure.
Coupling Broke Under Wind Load, Not Impact
Likely Cause
Incorrect (under-strength) shear pins installed, or fatigue from continuous vibration having lowered the coupling's breakaway threshold below its rated value.
Diagnostic Steps
Confirm the failed pins/nuts match the manufacturer's specified part number and rating; check the coupling's service history and age against the recommended replacement interval.
Corrective Action
Install a new coupling using only the specified frangible kit, and bring the replacement schedule (typically every 5 years) up to date across the site if fatigue was the cause.
Coupling Appears Intact After a Minor Impact
Likely Cause
The coupling has been activated or stressed even though it has not visibly separated — frangible hardware is single-use and can be compromised internally without obvious external damage.
Diagnostic Steps
Treat any known impact event as a trigger for replacement regardless of visual condition; do not load-test the coupling in place to "check" it, as this risks an uncontrolled failure.
Corrective Action
Replace the coupling as standard practice after any impact event, using a new manufacturer-specified frangible kit.
Fixture Loose or Wobbling at the Coupling
Likely Cause
Fasteners securing the coupling installed with incorrect torque, either too loose (allowing play) or over-tightened (risking premature fatigue).
Diagnostic Steps
Check fastener torque against the manufacturer's specified value using a calibrated torque wrench; inspect for elongation or deformation from over-torquing.
Corrective Action
Retorque fasteners to the exact specified value, replacing any hardware showing signs of deformation from incorrect prior torque.

High Severity
Root Cause
Incorrect (under-strength) shear pins installed, or fatigue from continuous vibration lowering the breakaway threshold below its rated value.

Coupling breaks away prematurely under normal wind load rather than only on genuine impact.

Verification of installed pins/nuts against the manufacturer's specified part number and rating, and service-life check against the replacement interval.
Latent Internal Damage After Impact
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Coupling has been stressed or activated internally by a minor impact without visibly separating, since frangible hardware is single-use.
Effect
Reduced strength margin persists undetected, risking uncontrolled failure under a subsequent load.
Detection Method
Treating any known impact event as a mandatory trigger for replacement regardless of visual condition — there is no reliable field test for latent damage.
Fastener Torque Fault
Low Severity
Root Cause
Fasteners securing the coupling installed with incorrect torque, either too loose or over-tightened.
Effect
Fixture is loose or wobbles at the coupling, or the hardware is put at risk of premature fatigue.
Detection Method
Torque check against the manufacturer's specified value using a calibrated wrench, and inspection for deformation from over-torquing.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Frangibility Not to Be Defeated
Mechanical

Never substitute a frangible coupling with a rigid fitting, add reinforcement, or over-torque the fasteners; the coupling must still shear cleanly at its rated load to limit damage on aircraft impact.

Torque to Specification
Mechanical

Use a calibrated torque wrench and the manufacturer's specified value when installing or servicing the coupling; both under- and over-torquing shift the actual shear threshold away from its rated design point.

Electrical Isolation
Electrical Safety

De-energise and lock out the fixture circuit before removing or servicing the coupling, since it sits directly in the fixture's cable and mounting path.

Mandatory Replacement After Impact
Compliance

Replace any coupling known to have absorbed an impact event (mower strike, vehicle contact, snowplough contact) regardless of its visual condition, since latent damage cannot be reliably assessed in the field.

FOD Control
Operational

Account for all fasteners and hardware when replacing a coupling in the movement area, and secure loose parts before leaving the site.

PPE
Personnel Safety

Use insulated tools for any electrical connection work and standard airside PPE when working on or near an active movement area.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Confirm the fixture is level, correctly aimed, and firmly seated on the reinstalled coupling before closing out the work.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Environmental Aging Shifts the Shear Point Without Any Impact
Durability

UV exposure and repeated thermal cycling can change a composite or aluminium coupling's shear characteristics over years of service even without ever absorbing an impact; a coupling that looks visually sound can no longer shear at its rated load, so build periodic torque/shear verification into the overhaul cycle rather than relying on visual inspection alone.

Torque Drift Happens Between Scheduled Checks
Mechanical

Vibration from aircraft rollout or mowing equipment can gradually loosen or, less commonly, work a fastener tighter between maintenance visits; a coupling torqued correctly at installation can drift out of its rated range well before the next scheduled inspection, so weight re-torque checks toward high-vibration or high-traffic positions.

Latent Damage After a Low-Speed Impact Is Easy to Underestimate
Compliance

A coupling struck by a mower or slow-moving vehicle can look completely undamaged while carrying internal stress fractures that only manifest under a later, more significant load; treat any known impact event as mandatory replacement regardless of how minor the contact seemed at the time.

Batch Variation Between Manufacturers Isn't Always Interchangeable
Procurement

Couplings from different manufacturers rated for the same fixture type don't always share identical shear thresholds or mounting patterns; verify compatibility against the specific fixture and mounting hardware before substituting a coupling from a different supplier, rather than assuming "rated for the same fixture" means fully interchangeable.

Snow-Clearing Season Concentrates Impact Risk
Operational

Coupling replacements cluster heavily around snow-clearing season at many sites, since plough blades and drifted snow markers are a common source of low-speed contact; stock spare couplings ahead of the season for fixture types known to be at risk, rather than ordering reactively after each strike is discovered.

Record the Impact Cause When Replacing
Maintenance

Logging what struck a coupling (mower, snowplough, vehicle, unknown) alongside each replacement builds a pattern over time that can point to a specific procedure, route, or piece of equipment worth adjusting, rather than treating each replacement as an isolated, unexplained event.

Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about frangible couplings: breakaway function, ratings, standards, installation checks and maintenance.

Q1What is a frangible coupling?

A frangible coupling, also called a breakaway or shear coupling, is a mechanical safety device engineered to fracture at a predetermined load. It is the structural interface between an elevated AGL fixture or its support pole and its base.

Q2Why do elevated fixtures need a frangible coupling?

The fixture must withstand everyday wind, vibration and maintenance handling, yet must not stay rigidly attached to its base if an aircraft strikes it. The coupling resolves that conflict through controlled structural failure.

Q3Where are frangible couplings used?

They are mandatory on elevated AGL fixtures installed in or adjacent to aircraft movement areas, such as elevated runway edge lights on frangible poles or pedestals.

Q4How does a frangible coupling work?

It has a deliberate weak point, such as shear pins, frangible nuts or a designed neck section, that fractures when the applied load exceeds a set threshold.

Q5What breakaway force is typical?

A typical manufacturer lateral rating is 250-450 kg (550-1000 lbs), engineered to satisfy ICAO/FAA impact-energy criteria. Vertical strength is typically higher to ensure wind resistance.

Q6Can a frangible coupling be reused after impact?

No. It is single-use and must be replaced immediately after activation or a significant impact, even without visible damage. The typical replacement interval is every 5 years.

Q7What materials and mounting interfaces are used?

High-strength aluminium alloy, steel or composite polymer, protected by anodising, zinc plating or powder coating. Mounting uses a flange, threaded studs or a clamp connection.

Q8Which standards apply?

ICAO Annex 14 Vol I (frangibility of elevated fixtures near the movement area), ICAO Doc 9157 Part 6, FAA AC 150/5220-23A (Frangible Connections) and FAA AC 150/5345-42 (light bases, transformer housings, junction boxes and accessories).

Q9How often should frangible couplings be inspected?

Semi-annually for fatigue cracks and deformation; annually for corrosion and free movement, shear pin verification and fastener torque; and after any strike.

Q10Why did a coupling fail to break away on impact?

Corrosion may have seized the shear pins or frangible nuts, or non-specified hardware was installed instead of the manufacturer's frangible kit, raising the effective breakaway threshold.
