Product Overview

Obsolete / Legacy AGL Replacement Unit

What It Is

Older airport lighting systems often contain discontinued or obsolete components no longer readily available through the original supply chain. Precision AGL Solutions supports identifying installed legacy equipment and developing a replacement route.

The assessment can cover runway and taxiway lighting equipment, isolating transformers, connectors, CCR components, illuminated signs and associated airport electrical infrastructure.

Key Benefits

  • Identify discontinued equipment accurately
  • Reduce the risk of incompatible replacement parts
  • Determine whether direct replacement is practical, or identify retrofit alternatives
  • Retain serviceable portions of existing infrastructure
  • Support planned rehabilitation, reduce downtime
  • Improve spare-parts planning
  • Document legacy installations for future maintenance

Assessment Stages

Detail for Each Stage

1. Identify & 2. Verify Application

Identify equipment from nameplate, catalogue or model number, photographs and airport documentation. Where the label is damaged or missing, photographs of the complete equipment and connections still help.

Then establish its actual role: runway, taxiway or approach lighting, series-circuit infrastructure, CCR equipment, illuminated signage, field connection systems or fixture assemblies.

3. Electrical & 4. Mechanical

  • Electrical: voltage, current, frequency, power rating, transformer characteristics, load, control interface, connector pin configuration, circuit arrangement, monitoring
  • Mechanical: dimensions, mounting points, fixing, cable entry, connector position, enclosure, weight, clearances, adjacent components
  • Similar dimensions can still be unsuitable if electrical characteristics differ
  • Critical in existing bases, pits, fixtures, signs or enclosures
Optical & System Requirements, and Selection

Maintain the Intended Function

5. Optical and System

  • Optical characteristics, light distribution, luminous performance
  • Colour requirements, beam characteristics, fixture geometry
  • Sign illumination, system-level performance

6. Possible Outcomes

  • Direct replacement, compatible replacement from an alternative source
  • Replacement assembly, retrofit module
  • Modified installation, technology conversion, complete component upgrade

Direct Replacement vs Retrofit

Not Every Obsolete Component Needs a System Replacement

Direct Replacement May Suit Where

  • The original specification remains available
  • Interfaces unchanged, physical dimensions match
  • Electrical characteristics compatible
  • Required performance can be maintained

Retrofit May Suit Where

  • The original component is discontinued, replacement technology has changed
  • Spare parts are no longer readily available
  • Existing equipment can be retained
  • A newer technology provides a suitable alternative

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures
Series-Circuit Compatibility

Check the Circuit Before Changing the Technology

LED Retrofit on a Series Circuit

LED fixtures draw far less power than halogen ones, so the circuit load falls. Check the regulator's minimum-load and power-factor limits, that the circuit current stays correct at every brightness step (for example 2.8, 3.4, 4.1, 5.2 and 6.6 A on a five-step 6.6 A circuit), and that lamp-out monitoring still works. A replacement must also match the original photometric performance, not just brightness.

Legacy Materials

Some older transformers, capacitors and cables may contain hazardous materials, including PCBs. Test or confirm before handling, and follow the airport and national rules for disposal.

FAQ

Obsolete / Legacy AGL Replacement Unit — Frequently Asked Questions

What is the obsolete / legacy AGL replacement service?

Older airport lighting systems often contain discontinued or obsolete components that are no longer readily available through the original supply chain. Precision AGL Solutions supports identifying the installed legacy equipment and developing a replacement route. The assessment can cover runway and taxiway lighting equipment, isolating transformers, connectors, CCR components, illuminated signs and associated airport electrical infrastructure.

What information is needed to identify legacy equipment?

Useful information includes the original manufacturer, equipment model, catalogue or part number, serial number, equipment rating, manufacturing information, dimensions, connector arrangement, cable interface, photographs, installation location and application within the system. A clear photograph of the nameplate often gives important information, since older equipment may have several versions, revisions or manufacturer-specific interfaces.

How is a replacement route assessed?

The sequence is to identify the installed equipment, verify its application, check electrical compatibility, check mechanical compatibility, review optical and system requirements, and then select the replacement. Each stage helps establish whether the component can be replaced directly or whether a modified or retrofit solution is more appropriate.

Why is a similar-looking replacement not enough?

Similar dimensions can still be unsuitable if electrical characteristics differ. A replacement transformer, for example, should be selected from the installed circuit and application rather than appearance alone, and a replacement connector must give a suitable electrical and mechanical interface with the existing cable and mating component.

When does direct replacement suit, and when does retrofit?

Direct replacement may suit where the original specification remains available, interfaces are unchanged, dimensions match, electrical characteristics are compatible and the required performance can be maintained. A retrofit may suit where the original component is discontinued, spare parts are no longer readily available, the existing equipment can be retained and a newer technology provides a suitable alternative. Outcomes range from direct replacement and compatible replacement from an alternative source to retrofit modules, technology conversion and complete component upgrade.

What is assessed for legacy transformers and connectors?

For isolating transformers, the assessment can include the original model, primary and secondary characteristics, power rating, dimensions, connector arrangement, cable interface, installation method and the associated fixture or circuit. For connectors it covers connector type, cable construction, conductor size, electrical requirements, termination method, sealing arrangement, mating interface and the existing installation configuration.

Can discontinued CCR components be replaced?

The assessment can consider the CCR manufacturer, model, card number and module type, the electrical interface, connector configuration, control requirements and any firmware or configuration requirements, along with physical dimensions. Where direct card replacement is impractical, a broader refurbishment or regulator replacement strategy may be considered.

What options exist for legacy fixtures and signs?

For fixtures, replacement routes include a replacement light source, an LED retrofit module, driver replacement, a halogen-to-LED conversion or complete fixture replacement, preserving the required function and compatibility. For signs, where the enclosure and face remain serviceable, component-level refurbishment of LED boards, drivers, internal wiring, connectors and facia components may be an alternative to complete sign replacement.

Why document legacy replacements and plan spares?

Records of the original and replacement manufacturer, model, part number and rating, the location, date and reason for replacement, the compatibility assessment, photographs and test information give a reference for future maintenance. Spare-parts planning can prioritize safety-critical lighting equipment, components with long procurement times, frequently failing components, equipment installed in large quantities and components with no readily available direct replacement.

Do replacements that change the technology need approval?

Where a replacement changes the original technology or configuration, review it for electrical, mechanical, optical and system-level suitability. Approvals, inspections, testing or documentation may be required by the airport and the authority, and for FAA-funded projects specified equipment must be listed under the FAA Airport Lighting Equipment Certification Program (AC 150/5345-53D). Related references include AC 150/5340-26C, AC 150/5345-10H, AC 150/5345-47C, AC 150/5345-26E and AC 150/5345-46F.

Is it safe to replace legacy components on a live series circuit?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage, and opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death. Any modification to operational airfield lighting should follow the applicable airport safety, maintenance and approval procedures. See the electrical safety warning on this page.
