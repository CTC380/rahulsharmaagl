Primary AGL Cable Installation SOPs

Controlled procedures for the installation, routing, jointing, termination and documentation of AGL series-circuit primary cables — route preparation, underground installation, trenches, ducts, bedding, backfilling, identification and numbering, entries into CCR rooms/transformer pits/base cans, terminations, joints, sealing, route inspection and as-built records.

SOP-100 Primary Cable Route Preparation

Hold Point — Cable installation shall not begin until the route has been inspected and released.

Purpose

To ensure that the approved primary AGL cable route is properly prepared before cable installation.

Pre-Installation Review

Verify:

  • Approved cable route drawing.
  • Cable schedule.
  • Circuit identification.
  • Existing underground services.
  • Duct/trench arrangement.
  • Crossing locations.
  • Cable entry points.
  • Transformer/base-can locations.
  • CCR room entry.
  • Required separation.
  • Drainage requirements.
Procedure
  1. Review the approved cable route.
  2. Conduct site verification.
  3. Establish route reference points.
  4. Identify existing underground utilities.
  5. Mark the cable route.
  6. Confirm excavation requirements.
  7. Verify crossing arrangements.
  8. Prepare ducts/trenches.
  9. Remove sharp objects and debris.
  10. Confirm the route is suitable for cable installation.
SOP-101 Primary Cable Installation

Critical Rule — Never pull an AGL primary cable beyond the manufacturer's specified pulling tension or bend radius.

Purpose

To establish a controlled method for installing AGL primary cables without damaging the cable or compromising future system reliability.

Procedure
  1. Verify cable type and drum identification.
  2. Inspect cable drum.
  3. Check cable length.
  4. Position the drum correctly.
  5. Establish cable pulling direction.
  6. Use suitable rollers/guides.
  7. Maintain the manufacturer's maximum pulling tension.
  8. Maintain the specified minimum bend radius.
  9. Avoid dragging the cable over sharp surfaces.
  10. Avoid twisting the cable.
  11. Avoid excessive mechanical stress.
  12. Position the cable in the approved route.
  13. Protect cable ends.
  14. Identify both ends.
  15. Conduct inspection and required tests.
SOP-102 Underground Cable Installation
Purpose

To ensure underground AGL primary cables are installed with adequate mechanical and environmental protection.

Procedure
  1. Verify route.
  2. Verify excavation/duct condition.
  3. Remove stones and sharp objects.
  4. Provide approved bedding where required.
  5. Install cable.
  6. Maintain required separation.
  7. Protect crossing locations.
  8. Provide warning/marker system where specified.
  9. Complete approved backfill.
  10. Compact in controlled stages.
  11. Record cable route and depth.
Inspection

Check for:

  • Cable damage.
  • Excessive bends.
  • Incorrect depth.
  • Poor bedding.
  • Inadequate protection.
  • Unidentified cable sections.
SOP-103 Cable Trench Preparation

Hold Point — The trench should be inspected before cable installation and before backfilling.

Purpose

To prepare cable trenches suitable for safe AGL cable installation.

Procedure
  1. Establish trench alignment.
  2. Confirm required excavation dimensions.
  3. Verify underground services.
  4. Excavate to approved depth.
  5. Maintain safe trench conditions.
  6. Remove rocks and sharp objects.
  7. Prepare trench bottom.
  8. Provide specified bedding.
  9. Check drainage.
  10. Inspect before cable placement.
SOP-104 Cable Duct Installation
Purpose

To ensure cable ducts provide continuous mechanical protection and maintainable cable routing.

Procedure
  1. Verify duct type and size.
  2. Establish route.
  3. Install ducts according to approved drawings.
  4. Maintain alignment.
  5. Provide appropriate bends.
  6. Prevent sharp edges.
  7. Seal unused duct openings where required.
  8. Provide draw rope where specified.
  9. Verify continuity.
  10. Inspect duct before cable installation.
  11. Record duct route.
Acceptance

The duct shall be free from:

  • Blockages.
  • Collapsed sections.
  • Sharp edges.
  • Excessive deformation.
  • Unapproved joints.

SOP-106 Cable Bedding

Important — Bedding material shall comply with the approved project specification. Improvised materials shall not be used.

Purpose

To provide suitable support and protection beneath and around underground cables.

Procedure
  1. Prepare trench bottom.
  2. Remove sharp stones and debris.
  3. Place approved bedding material.
  4. Level the bedding.
  5. Install cable without disturbing the prepared surface.
  6. Provide additional approved covering material.
  7. Inspect before general backfilling.
SOP-107 Cable Backfilling

Prohibited Practice — Do not dump heavy material directly onto exposed cable.

Purpose

To protect installed cables while restoring the cable route.

Procedure
  1. Inspect cable before backfill.
  2. Confirm cable identification.
  3. Confirm required protection.
  4. Place approved backfill material.
  5. Avoid direct impact on the cable.
  6. Backfill in controlled layers.
  7. Compact as specified.
  8. Maintain required route markers.
  9. Restore the surface.
  10. Record completion.
SOP-108 Cable Identification
Purpose

To ensure every primary cable can be positively identified throughout its service life.

Identification

Cable identification should include, as applicable:

  • Circuit number.
  • Cable ID.
  • From location.
  • To location.
  • Phase/conductor identification where applicable.
  • Installation section.
  • Drawing reference.
Procedure
  1. Assign identification before installation.
  2. Mark both cable ends.
  3. Mark intermediate accessible points where required.
  4. Use durable identification.
  5. Ensure identification remains legible.
  6. Update identification after approved modifications.
SOP-109 Cable Numbering
Purpose

To establish a consistent numbering system for primary AGL cables.

Procedure

Develop a project-specific numbering convention.

Example Structure

AGL-CIR-01-CBL-001

Where:

  • AGL = system.
  • CIR-01 = circuit.
  • CBL-001 = cable identification.

The actual project numbering system shall be defined in the approved document-control procedure.

Cross-Reference

The same cable number shall appear consistently on:

  • Cable schedule.
  • Layout drawings.
  • Circuit drawings.
  • Test reports.
  • As-built drawings.
  • Maintenance records.

  8. Provide sealing against moisture/pests where required.

  12. Inspect before energization.

SOP-115 Cable Jointing
Purpose

To establish controlled procedures for joining AGL primary cables.

Preconditions

Before jointing:

  • Confirm cable identification.
  • Confirm circuit isolation.
  • Obtain required permit.
  • Verify cable compatibility.
  • Check joint kit.
  • Check environmental conditions.
  • Establish a clean work area.
  • Verify qualified jointing personnel.
Procedure
  1. Isolate the circuit.
  2. Apply LOTO.
  3. Verify absence of voltage.
  4. Prepare cable ends.
  5. Maintain cleanliness.
  6. Install joint components according to manufacturer instructions.
  7. Maintain correct dimensions.
  8. Complete electrical connections.
  9. Complete insulation/sealing system.
  10. Allow curing where applicable.
  11. Conduct inspection.
  12. Perform required electrical tests.
  13. Document the joint.

Division-Wide Requirements

The master workflow, quality hold points, critical checks and common failure modes that apply across all 21 Division 05 procedures.

A. Master Workflow

The following sequence should be applied end-to-end for every primary AGL cable installation:

D. Common Installation Failures

The following conditions should receive particular attention during QA/QC:

  • Excessive Cable Pulling ForceMay damage cable insulation or reduce long-term reliability.
  • Excessive BendMay damage the cable or compromise electrical performance.
  • Cable Damage During BackfillingSharp stones or uncontrolled machinery can damage buried cable.
  • Poor Cable SealingCan permit moisture ingress into joints, pits and equipment.
  • Incorrect Cable IdentificationCan cause major maintenance and fault-location problems.
  • Uncontrolled Cable JointingPoor preparation or contamination can result in premature insulation failure.
  • Incorrect Route RecordsCan make future fault finding extremely difficult.
  • Water AccumulationPersistent water around joints, pits or entries can accelerate electrical deterioration.
  • Unsupported Cable EntryMechanical stress at a connector or transformer can cause premature failure.
  • Missing As-Built InformationAn undocumented cable route becomes a major future maintenance risk.

“A primary AGL cable is not simply a wire placed underground. Its reliability depends on the complete installation chain: correct route, correct cable, controlled pulling, correct bend radius, proper jointing, effective sealing, mechanical protection, accurate identification, electrical testing and accurate as-built documentation.”

Division 05 — SOP Approval Principle

Each project should adapt these SOPs to its own approved cable route drawings, cable schedule, cable manufacturer's instructions, jointing/termination kit manufacturer's instructions, applicable national and international cable installation standards, and airport/authority specifications.

Approved project drawings and manufacturer's instructions shall take precedence wherever they impose more restrictive requirements.

Reference Basis

Technical references behind the procedures in this division:

  • FAA AC 150/5345-7, Specification for L-824 Underground Electrical Cable — primary series circuit cable construction and rating.
  • FAA AC 150/5370-10, Item L-108 (Underground Power Cable) and Item L-110 — cable installation, direct burial, ducts and splicing.
  • FAA AC 150/5340-30 — cable depth, routing, marking and splicing details.
  • IEC 61821 — maintenance of series circuits, including insulation-resistance monitoring and trending.
  • IEEE Std 400 series — field testing and evaluation of cable insulation (confirm applicability to the cable construction used).
  • ICAO Annex 14, Vol. I — Aerodrome Design and Operations, Chapter 8, and ICAO Doc 9157, Aerodrome Design Manual, Part 5 — electrical system and circuit requirements.
  • Cable and splice-kit manufacturer instructions — jointing, stress-relief and sealing procedures.

References are given at document or chapter level for technical orientation. Confirm the current edition, amendments and national adoption (for example DGCA CAR Section 4, FAA Advisory Circulars or EASA material) for each project. Approved project specifications, manufacturer instructions and the airport's own procedures take precedence, as stated in the precedence note above.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Primary AGL Cable Installation SOPs in the AGL SOP Library.

Q1What is Division 05 — Primary AGL Cable Installation SOPs?

Division 05 is a set of 21 procedures (SOP-100 to SOP-120, Rev 0) for installing, routing, jointing, terminating and documenting AGL series-circuit primary cables. It covers route preparation, underground installation, trenches, ducts, bedding, backfilling, identification and numbering, entries into CCR rooms, transformer pits and base cans, terminations, joints, sealing, route inspection and as-built records.

Q2Which values must come from the manufacturer or project documents?

Cable types, installation depths, bend radii, pulling tensions, separation distances, jointing systems, termination methods and testing requirements must be based on approved project drawings, the cable manufacturer's instructions, applicable standards and airport specifications.

Q3What limits apply when pulling a primary cable?

Never pull a primary cable beyond the manufacturer's specified pulling tension or bend radius (SOP-101). Use suitable rollers and guides, avoid dragging over sharp surfaces or twisting the cable, verify drum identification and length, protect cable ends and identify both ends.

Q4Where do termination dimensions come from?

From the approved termination manufacturer's instructions, not from an assumed generic dimension (SOP-114). Verify the cable ID and termination kit, inspect the insulation, maintain the required stripping dimensions, apply the specified sealing, perform the required electrical tests and record the termination.

Q5What must be in place before jointing a primary cable?

Confirm cable identification, circuit isolation, the required permit, cable and joint-kit compatibility, environmental conditions, a clean work area and qualified jointing personnel (SOP-115). The procedure then requires isolating, applying LOTO and verifying absence of voltage before preparing the cable ends.

Q6What are the quality hold points for primary cable installation?

HP-01 Route Release, HP-02 Trench/Duct Inspection, HP-03 Cable Inspection, HP-04 Cable Installation, HP-05 Jointing, HP-06 Termination, HP-07 Cable Protection, HP-08 Backfill, HP-09 Electrical Testing and HP-10 As-Built. Electrical testing must be complete before energization.

Q7How is the installed cable route inspected?

SOP-119 checks the correct route, cable, circuit and location, absence of visible damage, bend radius, protection, duct or trench arrangement, bedding and backfill. It also checks identification and the CCR room, pit, base-can, joint and crossing interfaces. Inspection happens during installation, before closure and as a final route verification, with deviations recorded and as-built information updated.
