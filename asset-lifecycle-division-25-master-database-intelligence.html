Asset Lifecycle Master Database & Intelligence

The master data, knowledge and intelligence layer of the entire AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide. Divisions 01–24 generate, update and consume asset information; Division 25 brings that information together into one controlled asset-intelligence structure so every major AGL asset can be traced from identification through installation, operation, maintenance, failure, health, lifecycle decision, replacement and disposal — one asset, one master record, one complete lifecycle history.

Initial Issue

The question this division answers — what is the single controlled identity and complete lifecycle record for every AGL asset, and what does that record tell us to do next?

Where Division 25 Sits in the Lifecycle

Division 25 does not generate new field activity of its own — it is the data, knowledge and intelligence layer that Divisions 01–24 feed into and draw from. Every asset register entry, criticality score, design decision, inspection, test, maintenance job, failure record, cost entry, audit finding and case study ultimately resolves back to one master record, keyed by one Asset ID.

Five Intelligence Layers

Division 25 is more than a database. Each master record is built up through five layers that turn raw asset data into a lifecycle decision.

01
Identity — What is the asset?

Asset ID → Type → Model → Serial → Location

02
History — What has happened to it?

Installation → Commissioning → Maintenance → Inspection → Testing → Failures → Repairs

03
Current State — What condition is it in now?

Condition → Health → Reliability → Performance → RUL

04
Future Risk — What is likely to happen next?

Failure risk → Obsolescence → RUL → Replacement forecast → Lifecycle cost

05
Decision Intelligence — What should we do?

Maintain → Monitor → Repair → Refurbish → Upgrade → Replace → Dispose

MDI-01 Asset ID
Purpose

Every asset must have a unique and permanent Asset ID — the single controlling identity that every other lifecycle record, in every division and every linked database, keys against.

Requirements
  • Unique
  • Non-duplicated
  • Traceable
  • Persistent throughout the lifecycle
  • Linked to physical identification
  • Linked to CMMS / EAM
  • Linked to drawings
  • Linked to maintenance records
  • Linked to failure records
Example
AGL-RWY-01-LGT-0001

The coding structure itself should follow the airport's approved asset-coding standard.

Persistence Rule

The Asset ID should not change simply because the asset is repaired, refurbished or relocated, unless the organization's asset-governance rules explicitly define that event as creation of a new asset.

MDI-02 Asset Type
Purpose

Asset Type defines the standardized asset category so records can be grouped, compared and benchmarked across the entire AGL population.

Examples
  • Runway light
  • Taxiway light
  • Approach light
  • PAPI
  • Airfield sign
  • CCR
  • Isolation transformer
  • Primary cable
  • Secondary cable
  • L-823 connector
  • Base can
  • ALCMS equipment
  • Control equipment
Why Standardization Matters

Standardized asset types enable asset population analysis, failure comparison, maintenance planning, spare forecasting, lifecycle-cost analysis and reliability benchmarking across like-for-like equipment.

MDI-03 Asset Description
Purpose

Provide a clear engineering description of the asset — enough detail to distinguish it from similar assets on the same field.

Recommended Information
  • Functional description
  • Equipment type
  • Application
  • Rating
  • Configuration
  • Associated system / circuit
Standard

The description should be sufficiently specific to distinguish similar assets — not a generic label repeated across hundreds of records.

MDI-04 Manufacturer
Purpose

Record the manufacturer exactly as approved in the controlled asset documentation.

Used For
  • Reliability analysis
  • Warranty management
  • Product comparison
  • Supplier evaluation
  • Obsolescence monitoring
  • Spare-parts planning
Data Discipline

Manufacturer data should be standardized to avoid multiple spellings or abbreviations of the same manufacturer fragmenting the analysis.

MDI-05 Model
Purpose

Record the exact equipment / model designation — the field that lets similar-looking assets be told apart by generation and configuration.

Model Data Supports
  • Product population analysis
  • Failure analysis
  • Spare identification
  • Obsolescence assessment
  • Technology migration
  • Manufacturer comparison
Multiple Revisions

For equipment with multiple revisions, the relevant model / version should be retained rather than overwritten.

MDI-06 Serial Number
Purpose

The serial number provides manufacturer-level traceability beneath the organization's own Asset ID.

Verify Against
  • Physical nameplate
  • Manufacturer documentation
  • Procurement records
  • Warranty records
  • Asset register
Important Distinction

Asset ID = the organization's lifecycle identifier. Serial Number = the manufacturer's equipment identifier. The two are different fields and should never be merged.

MDI-08 Installation Date
Purpose

Record the date the asset was physically installed.

Used For
  • Age calculation
  • Lifecycle planning
  • Service-life analysis
  • Replacement forecasting
  • Reliability studies
Evidence

Installation date should be supported by an appropriate installation or project record where available.

MDI-09 Commissioning Date
Purpose

Record when the asset was formally commissioned / accepted for operational use.

Important Distinction

Installation Date ≠ Commissioning Date. The two dates can support different lifecycle calculations — installation marks physical fitting, commissioning marks operational acceptance — and both should be retained separately.

MDI-10 Expected Service Life
Purpose

Record the expected service life established through the applicable asset / product / design information.

Consider
  • Product design expectations
  • Operating environment
  • Duty cycle
  • Maintenance regime
  • Technology
  • Manufacturer information
  • Applicable requirements
Important Distinction

Expected service life should not automatically be treated as the replacement date. Actual replacement should also weigh condition, reliability, risk, performance, obsolescence and lifecycle cost.

MDI-11 Warranty Period
Purpose

Record the complete warranty position for the asset, not just an expiry date.

Record
  • Warranty start date
  • Warranty duration
  • Warranty expiry
  • Warranty provider
  • Warranty reference
  • Warranty conditions
Intelligence

The database should be able to identify assets approaching warranty expiry, and assets currently under warranty with recorded failures — the two situations that need action.

MDI-12 Criticality
Purpose

Record the approved asset criticality classification.

Potential Dimensions
  • Safety
  • Operational
  • Regulatory
  • Financial
  • Service continuity
Source Methodology

The criticality record should link to the methodology established under Division 03 — Asset Criticality & Risk Assessment rather than duplicating a separate scoring logic here.

Most Important Design Principle

Division 25 should remain the master data, knowledge and intelligence layer — it does not replace the detailed methodologies of the earlier divisions (criticality logic stays in Division 03, audit logic stays in Division 23), it connects them through one governed identity.

One Asset → One Master Record → Complete Lifecycle History → Intelligent Decision Support. The Asset Register tells you what the asset is; the Master Database & Intelligence layer tells you what has happened to it, how it is performing, what risks it carries, what it may need next, and what lifecycle decision should be considered.

Division 02 — ASSET REGISTER (What is the asset?)
Divisions 03–24 — ASSESS · DESIGN · OPERATE · MAINTAIN · AUDIT
Division 25 — CONNECT · ANALYZE · PREDICT · DECIDE

This is why Division 25 closes the guide rather than opening it: every earlier division needs the trusted identity that Division 02 establishes, and every earlier division's output ultimately needs somewhere authoritative to live — Division 25 is that place.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Asset Lifecycle Master Database & Intelligence in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide.

Q1What is Division 25 — Asset Lifecycle Master Database & Intelligence in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

Division 25 is the master data, knowledge and intelligence layer connecting every division. It covers 29 topics on the Standard Asset Record: Asset ID, type, description, manufacturer, model, serial number, location, installation/commissioning dates, service life, warranty, criticality, condition, health index, maintenance/inspection/testing/failure history, operating data, lifecycle cost, repair/refurbishment/upgrade history, replacement forecast, disposal status, related documents, related standards and revision history.

Q2What is the AGL Asset Lifecycle Master Database?

It is the master data, knowledge and intelligence layer of the guide. Divisions 01 to 24 generate, update and consume asset information, and Division 25 brings it together into one controlled asset record.

Q3How many fields does the master asset record have?

It defines 29 fields, MDI-01 to MDI-29, running from Asset ID through to revision history.

Q4What identification fields are in the master record?

Asset ID, asset type, description, manufacturer, model, serial number and location. The Asset ID must be unique and permanent, and it is the single controlling identity that every other lifecycle record refers to.

Q5Which lifecycle and condition fields are included?

Installation date, commissioning date, expected service life, warranty period, criticality, initial condition, current condition and Health Index, plus energy and operating data and lifecycle cost.

Q6Which history fields does the record carry?

Maintenance, inspection, testing, failure, repair, refurbishment and upgrade history. Failure history should connect directly with the AGL Failure Database rather than duplicating its structure, and repair history is recorded separately from general maintenance history.

Q7Why is the replacement forecast an intelligence field?

It should be an intelligence field and not merely a fixed replacement date. It draws on age, expected service life, condition, Health Index, RUL and reliability.

Q8How are documents, standards and changes tracked?

Every master record provides controlled links to related documents such as datasheets, drawings, installation and commissioning records and test reports. Related standards can include ICAO, FAA, DGCA and applicable Indian Standards, and every significant change is traceable through revision history with the previous value, new value and reason.

Q9How is the end of an asset's life tracked?

The disposal status field tracks the final lifecycle status, from active and under maintenance through under refurbishment or replacement to decommissioned and removed.
