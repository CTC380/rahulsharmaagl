Intermediate Holding Position Light

A specialized taxiway lighting system that marks intermediate holding positions on taxiways where aircraft may be required to stop and await clearance. These lights provide visual guidance to pilots at holding points that are not at runway intersections, typically indicating positions where aircraft hold before crossing active taxiways or entering critical areas.

Overview

What Is It?

An Intermediate Holding Position Light is a specialized taxiway lighting system that marks intermediate holding positions on taxiways where aircraft may be required to stop and await clearance. These lights provide visual guidance to pilots at holding points that are not at runway intersections, typically indicating positions where aircraft hold before crossing active taxiways or entering critical areas. The lights are typically a combination of yellow/amber fixtures that define the holding position boundary, helping pilots to accurately position their aircraft and maintain safe separation from other traffic.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

Intermediate holding positions exist away from runway intersections, at points where taxiways cross each other or where an aircraft must wait before entering a busy or restricted area — situations that carry real collision risk even though no runway is involved. The purpose of this light is to give that non-runway holding point the same kind of definite, visible stopping reference a pilot gets at a runway holding position, so ground traffic can be sequenced safely without relying purely on radio calls and markings.

The amber/yellow signature deliberately sets these lights apart from the red of a runway stop bar and the blue/green of edge and centreline lighting, so a pilot immediately recognises the holding point for what it is: a place to stop and confirm clearance before proceeding, not a routing cue to be followed through.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Intermediate holding position lights are installed at various locations on the airfield where holding is required:

  • Intersecting taxiways where holding is required before crossing
  • Approaches to active runways (intermediate holding positions)
  • ILS critical area boundaries where holding is required
  • Apron entry/exit points where traffic flow is managed
  • Maintenance and construction zones where temporary holding is required
  • Complex taxiway layouts requiring intermediate holding points
Placement

Installation & Location

Fixtures are set out across the taxiway at the marked intermediate holding position, in an inset row (or paired elevated fixtures on unpaved shoulders) tied to the same holding-position marking the lights reinforce. Inset units use the standard load-rated base can recessed into the pavement, sealed and fed by armoured cable run in duct back to the taxiway lighting controller; because these lights are switched independently of the plain taxiway centreline circuit, a separate control cable run back to the ALCMS or local control panel is required alongside the series-circuit power feed. Setting-out is tied to the holding-position marking survey so the light row lines up exactly with the painted marking it duplicates.

Function

How Does It Work?

Intermediate holding position lights operate as part of the taxiway guidance system:

  • Power: Powered from the constant current series circuit via an isolating transformer (typically 15W – 45W).
  • Light Source: Utilizes yellow/amber LED arrays or yellow-filtered halogen lamps. The yellow colour indicates a holding position that is not runway-related.
  • Configuration: Typically installed as a row of lights (3 to 5 fixtures) across the taxiway at the holding position.
  • Guidance: The lights provide a clear visual indication of the holding boundary, guiding pilots to stop at the correct position.
  • Intensity Control: CCR-controlled intensity with 3 to 5 steps, matching standard taxiway lighting.
Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

Intermediate holding position lights are wired on a control cable separate from the plain taxiway centreline circuit, which is what lets ground control switch the row on independently of the taxiway lighting around it. That separation is a wiring and command distinction, not an automatic interlock: unlike a true stop bar, which is typically wired to extinguish automatically (or is prohibited from being crossed) based on runway occupancy or ATC clearance logic feeding a selective switching system, an intermediate holding position light row is generally switched manually from the ALCMS or local control panel by ATC/ground control as an operational aid, most commonly energised during low-visibility procedures to reinforce a holding position that would otherwise rely on the painted marking alone. The current step through the row is still set conventionally by the CCR, so brightness follows the same visibility-based selection as the rest of the taxiway system — only the on/off command is independent.

Construction

Physical Construction

Inset units use the same load-rated die-cast aluminium housing and construction as standard inset taxiway fixtures, seated in a base can recessed into the pavement with a wheel-load-rated top plate and a toughened-glass or polycarbonate lens gasket-sealed for an IP67/IP68 rating. Where paired elevated fixtures are used on unpaved shoulders instead, the same optical head mounts on a frangible coupling atop an L-867-type base can. The yellow/amber colour is produced by a filtered lens or coloured LED array rather than a dyed housing, so colour rendering stays consistent over the fixture's service life. Because these lights are switched independently of the plain taxiway centreline circuit, the fixture's base enclosure carries separate sealed cable glands for the series-circuit power feed (via its isolating transformer) and the independent control cable run back to the ALCMS or local control panel, in addition to the L-823 primary connectors and stainless-steel fasteners used throughout.

Installation Requirements

Row Setout Against Marking

Set out each fixture position in the row against the painted holding-position marking it duplicates, holding tolerance so the light row lines up exactly once installed.

Dual Circuit Duct & Cable Route
Electrical

Lay separate duct/cable routes for the series-circuit power feed and the independent control cable back to the ALCMS or local control panel, since the two are switched separately.

Sealed Gland Termination
Electrical

Terminate the series-circuit and control cables through separate sealed glands at each fixture's base enclosure, along with the L-823 primary connectors, before backfill.

Isolating Transformer Matching
Electrical

Confirm the isolating transformer wattage and ratio match each fixture and the series circuit's rated current step before connection.

Mechanical

Confirm the lens-to-housing seal is correctly fitted and the fixture meets its IP67/IP68 rating before it is subjected to standing water or de-icing fluid.

Confirm the independent control cable run terminates correctly at the ALCMS or local control panel, verifying the on/off command path is intact end to end before energisation.

Pre-Energisation Sign-off

Testing & Commissioning

Insulation Resistance (Megger) Test
Pre-Energisation

Megger-test the isolating transformer and the series-circuit run to each fixture in the row before backfill, confirming insulation resistance clears the acceptable minimum ahead of first energisation.

Loop Continuity & Resistance Test
Pre-Energisation

Verify series loop continuity through every fixture in the holding-position row and record total loop resistance, ruling out open circuits or high-resistance splices before commissioning.

Control Cable Continuity Test
Pre-Energisation

Test the independent control cable run back to the ALCMS or local control panel separately from the series-circuit power feed, confirming the on/off command path is intact end to end.

Primary/Secondary Voltage Verification
Energisation

With the CCR energised, confirm secondary voltage at each fixture's L-823 connector matches the isolating transformer's rated ratio at the commissioned current step.

ALCMS On/Off Command Response
Energisation

Issue a manual on/off command from the ALCMS or local control panel and confirm the entire row switches together, verifying the independent control path functions as designed.

CCR Brightness Step Response
Energisation

Cycle the CCR through every intensity step and confirm the row responds correctly at each, ruling out marginal transformers or loose connections.

Row Alignment & Marking Survey
Final Acceptance

Survey each fixture in the row against the painted holding-position marking, correcting any deviation so the light row lines up exactly with the marking it duplicates.

Photometric & Chromaticity Commissioning Test

Measure yellow/amber beam intensity and colour with a calibrated photometer against ICAO/FAA commissioning tolerances prior to operational handover.

Documentation & As-Built Sign-off

Record insulation, continuity, voltage, control-cable, and photometric readings alongside as-built position in the commissioning dossier for handover.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Yellow Filter/LED Degradation

Yellow light sources and filters are susceptible to UV degradation and thermal stress. Over time, output can decrease or colour can shift. Regular photometric testing and replacement is essential.

Lens Contamination

Holding position lights are exposed to rubber deposits, dust, and de-icing fluids. Regular cleaning with approved solvents and soft brushes is required to maintain light output.

Misalignment

Lights can become misaligned due to pavement settlement or impact damage, altering the holding position boundary. Regular alignment checks are essential.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Single Fixture Dark, Rest of Row Normal
Likely Cause
Failed LED array/lamp, or an open isolating transformer (blown primary fuse-wire link or failed secondary winding).
Diagnostic Steps
Confirm series loop continuity through the fixture's transformer primary; megger the transformer; check secondary voltage at the L-823 connector with the CCR energised.
Corrective Action
Replace the LED array/lamp if the transformer checks healthy; otherwise swap the isolating transformer and re-seal the L-823 connectors before backfill.
Entire Row Dark / CCR Will Not Regulate
Likely Cause
Open-circuit fault in the series loop (severed cable, corroded L-823 splice, or a failed transformer acting as an open point) on the shared taxiway series circuit.
Diagnostic Steps
Check the CCR fault/open-circuit indication first; loop-resistance test from the CCR output to isolate the faulted segment; sectionalise and megger cable runs to narrow down the break.
Corrective Action
Excavate and repair or replace the faulted cable section and connector; re-test insulation resistance before reinstating the circuit.
Row Fails to Respond to ALCMS On/Off Command
Likely Cause
A fault in the independent control cable run back to the ALCMS or local control panel, since the on/off command is wired separately from the series-circuit power feed.
Diagnostic Steps
Verify the CCR series circuit is otherwise healthy and energised; test continuity and signal integrity on the independent control cable back to the ALCMS/local panel; check the control relay at the fixture row.
Corrective Action
Repair or replace the faulted control cable or relay, and confirm the row responds correctly to a manual on/off command from the ALCMS before returning to service.
CCR Trips on Overcurrent or Ground Fault
Likely Cause
Moisture ingress at a fixture or connector has degraded insulation resistance, creating a short or a partial ground fault on the series loop.
Diagnostic Steps
Megger the full circuit to identify the low-resistance segment; visually inspect suspect fixtures for cracked lenses, condensation, or damaged secondary cable jackets.
Corrective Action
Dry out or replace the affected fixture/connector, renew O-rings and gaskets, and confirm insulation resistance is back above the acceptable minimum before re-energising.
Reduced or Discoloured Yellow/Amber Output
Likely Cause
Contaminated or eroded lens, or a yellow/amber LED array/filter degraded by UV exposure and thermal stress.
Diagnostic Steps
Run a photometric and chromaticity check against ICAO/FAA thresholds for the selected CCR step; inspect and clean the lens of rubber deposits or de-icing fluid residue.
Corrective Action
Clean or replace the lens, replace a degraded LED array/filter, and re-verify with a follow-up photometric reading.
Row Misaligned With the Holding-Position Marking
Likely Cause
Pavement settlement or impact damage has shifted an inset fixture, or a frangible coupling has fatigued on a paired elevated fixture on an unpaved shoulder.
Diagnostic Steps
Survey the fixture row against the painted holding-position marking; for elevated units, check the frangible coupling for cracking or play; inspect the pavement/subgrade around inset units for settlement.
Corrective Action
Re-set or re-level the affected fixture to align with the marking, replace a fatigued frangible coupling, and address any subgrade settlement to prevent recurrence.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

LED Array / Lamp Failure
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Solder-joint fatigue from repeated thermal cycling, moisture ingress through a compromised board seal, or ordinary end-of-life lumen depreciation.
Effect
Single fixture goes dark or drops below minimum intensity while the rest of the holding-position row continues to operate normally.
Detection Method
Nighttime visual patrol, periodic photometric intensity survey, and continuity/secondary-voltage checks at the L-823 connector.
Independent Control Cable / Relay Fault
High Severity
Root Cause
Damage or degradation in the dedicated control cable run back to the ALCMS or local control panel, or a failed switching relay at the fixture row — this circuit is wired separately from the series-circuit power feed.
Effect
Row fails to respond to a manual on/off command even though the series-circuit power feed remains healthy, undermining the row's use as a low-visibility operational aid.
Detection Method
Continuity and signal integrity testing on the independent control cable, and functional verification of the on/off command from the ALCMS/local panel.
Isolating Transformer Winding Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Moisture ingress leading to insulation breakdown, a manufacturing defect in the winding, or a blown primary fuse-wire link from a transient surge.
Effect
Open-circuit point in the series loop; can darken the single fixture or, if the open extends to the loop itself, take down the entire holding-position row.
Detection Method
Megger test of the transformer, secondary voltage check with the CCR energised, and loop-resistance testing from the CCR output.
L-823 Connector / Splice Degradation
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Improper seating or crimping during installation, gasket failure allowing groundwater ingress, or corrosion building up on the pins over time.
Effect
Intermittent operation, flickering, or a partial ground fault that degrades circuit insulation resistance.
Detection Method
Insulation resistance (megger) testing of the circuit and visual inspection of connectors during maintenance.
Yellow/Amber Filter Degradation
Low Severity
Root Cause
UV exposure and thermal stress causing the yellow/amber filter or LED array to haze, discolour, or shift in output over time.
Effect
Reduced or colour-shifted output that weakens the visual distinction between this holding-point signal and other AGL colours nearby.
Detection Method
Scheduled photometric and chromaticity testing against ICAO/FAA thresholds, and visual lens inspection during routine cleaning.
Lens Contamination
Low Severity
Root Cause
Rubber deposits, dust, and de-icing fluid residue accumulating on the outer lens surface.
Effect
Reduced light output that weakens the holding-position boundary cue, particularly in low-visibility conditions when the row is most relied upon.
Detection Method
Scheduled photometric intensity testing and visual lens inspection during routine cleaning with approved solvents.
Row Misalignment With Holding-Position Marking
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Pavement settlement or impact damage shifting an inset fixture, or frangible coupling fatigue on a paired elevated fixture on an unpaved shoulder.
Effect
The light row no longer lines up with the painted holding-position marking it is meant to reinforce, weakening the boundary reference for pilots.
Detection Method
Survey of the fixture row against the painted marking, and inspection of the frangible coupling or surrounding pavement/subgrade for movement.
Series Cable Insulation Breakdown
High Severity
Root Cause
Cable jacket damage from nearby excavation, age-related insulation degradation, or prolonged standing water in the duct run.
Effect
Ground fault or short circuit that trips the CCR or darkens the entire shared taxiway series circuit feeding the row.
Detection Method
Full-circuit megger test to flag the low-resistance segment, followed by sectionalised testing or time-domain reflectometry to pinpoint the fault location.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Series & Control Circuit Isolation
Electrical Safety

These fixtures are switched independently of the plain taxiway centreline circuit; confirm both the series-circuit power feed and the separate control cable back to the ALCMS or local control panel are isolated before opening a base can or handling connectors.

Holding Position Work Coordination
Operational

This fixture marks a point where aircraft are required to stop and await clearance; work here requires airside coordination and the holding position clearly marked by other means while the lights are out of service.

Flush Fit Where Inset
Mechanical

Where the inset configuration is used, the fixture must sit precisely flush with the pavement; a proud edge or depression becomes a wheel-impact point for every aircraft crossing the position.

Frangibility Where Elevated
Mechanical

Where paired elevated fixtures are used on an unpaved shoulder, never substitute the frangible mounting with a rigid fitting or over-torque it; it must still shear cleanly on impact.

Insulation Test Before Re-Energisation
Electrical Safety

After any cable, connector, or transformer work, megger test the affected loop before restoring power, since a residual fault at re-energisation can trip the circuit or darken the holding position marker.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Do not leave a fixture dark, out of position, or logically inconsistent with the painted holding-position marking after maintenance; verify alignment and control-circuit response before closing out.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Easy to Mistake for a Runway Stop Bar at a Distance
Human Factors

The amber IHP signature is deliberately different from a runway stop bar's red, but at night and from a distance the distinction relies on colour rendering staying accurate; check lens colour and intensity against spec more strictly here than on lower-consequence fixtures, since a misread colour undermines the distinction the system exists to provide.

Confirm Activation Logic After Taxiway Routing Changes
Configuration

Where IHP lights are selectively illuminated based on active taxiway routing, an airfield reconfiguration or new taxiway designation can leave the activation logic pointing at the wrong holding position; re-verify the mapping between physical location and control logic after any taxiway naming or routing change.

Isolate the Circuit Before Any Base Can Work
Electrical Safety

Like other series-circuit taxiway fixtures, IHP lights can carry several kV across the loop; de-energise and lock out the regulator and verify zero energy at the fixture before opening a base can, even though the fixture sits away from the runway.

Trend Loop Insulation on Long Taxiway Runs
Troubleshooting

IHP lights are often positioned well out along a taxiway feeder, and a slow-developing insulation fault upstream can present at the fixture as inconsistent intensity; trend megger readings on the specific loop segment rather than assuming the fixture itself is at fault.

Vehicle Strikes Are More Common Here Than at Runway Fixtures
Mechanical

Because intermediate holding positions see more routine ground vehicle and towing traffic than runway-adjacent fixtures, frangible coupling wear and housing damage from low-speed strikes accumulate faster; inspect IHP fixtures on a schedule matched to actual ground traffic volume rather than a blanket taxiway interval.

Match Intensity to the Surrounding Taxiway Lighting
Photometric

An IHP light that is technically within its own spec but noticeably brighter or dimmer than the taxiway edge lighting around it weakens the visual hierarchy pilots rely on to tell a holding cue from routine guidance; check relative intensity against neighbouring fixtures, not just the IHP light's own tolerance band.
