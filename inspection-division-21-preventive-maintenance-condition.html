Preventive Maintenance Condition Inspection

A standardized methodology for assessing the current condition, deterioration, remaining serviceability, maintenance requirement, and failure risk of AGL assets before functional failure occurs. This division is specifically focused on condition-based maintenance (CBM) and preventive-maintenance decision-making — moving beyond "is it working today?" to "is it deteriorating, and should we intervene before it fails?"

Initial Issue

Core question — "What is the condition of the asset before it actually fails?" The objective is not simply to confirm whether an asset works today, but to determine whether its condition indicates Healthy → Deteriorating → Maintenance Required → High Risk → Replacement Required.

Key difference from other divisions — Division 02 asks "is the fixture physically/optically acceptable today?" Division 20 asks "was the test evidence properly performed and documented?" Division 21 asks "is the fixture deteriorating, and should we intervene before it fails?" Condition rating ≠ maintenance priority — the final decision always weighs Condition × Criticality × Failure Consequence × Redundancy × Availability.

A. Standard Inspection Classification

Every observation should be assigned an appropriate final status:

NormalEquipment/condition satisfactory and operating as expected.

DefectiveAbnormality or defect identified but equipment remains operational or condition does not require immediate withdrawal.

UnserviceableEquipment or system is not available for its intended function.

Immediate Action RequiredCondition presents an operational, electrical, safety, or airside risk requiring immediate escalation/action.

B. Minimum Condition Inspection Record

Each Division 21 condition record should include:

  • Date and inspection time
  • Inspector name and designation
  • Asset ID, type, and location
  • Installation date / age, where known
  • Physical / electrical / optical / mechanical / environmental condition ratings
  • Condition-trend comparison to previous inspection
  • Failure and repair history summary
  • Obsolescence and spare-availability status
  • Overall condition rating (C1–C6)
  • Maintenance and replacement requirement
  • Monitoring frequency recommendation
  • Assigned priority and criticality
  • Final status and inspector's verification/sign-off

D. Maintenance Priority

Condition rating ≠ maintenance priority. A C3 runway centreline light may require higher priority than a C4 non-critical spare component, because asset criticality and operational consequence differ. The final decision therefore always weighs:

Decision FormulaCondition × Criticality × Failure Consequence × Redundancy × Availability
ConsidersAsset maintenance-priority assessment, criticality-based condition assessment, failure-consequence assessment, availability/reliability impact assessment, maintenance-risk assessment, planned-outage requirement assessment, and replacement-planning priority assessment.

E. Worked Example — Runway Edge Light

Illustrates how a condition inspection differs from a pass/fail current inspection, and why deterioration is intervened on before functional failure.

Current InspectionFixture operational → PASS
Condition InspectionLens degradation + corrosion + repeated moisture ingress + increasing maintenance interventions
Condition AssessmentC4 — Poor
DecisionContinue operation only under the applicable operational controls, increase monitoring, and plan replacement before functional failure.

F. The Key Difference from Other Divisions

Division 21 should not duplicate the individual inspection divisions — it asks a fundamentally different question about the same asset.

Division 02

"Is the runway fixture physically/optically acceptable today?"

Division 20

"Was the photometry/test evidence properly performed and documented?"

Division 21

"Is the runway fixture deteriorating, and should we intervene before it fails?"

G. Scope Boundary

Preventive-maintenance condition inspection is intended to

  • Assess the trajectory of an asset's condition — Healthy → Deteriorating → Maintenance Required → High Risk → Replacement Required — so that intervention happens before functional failure.

It does not replace

  • Daily and detailed physical/optical inspection (Divisions 01–19)
  • Photometric, electrical, and test-evidence verification (Division 20)
  • Corrective maintenance carried out in response to an identified failure

Central philosophy

  • Do not wait for failure to discover deterioration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about preventive maintenance condition inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook.

Q1What is Division 21 — Preventive Maintenance Condition Inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook?

Division 21 contains 14 published topics in 17 sections (A–Q). It gives a standardized methodology for assessing the current condition, deterioration, remaining serviceability, maintenance requirement and failure risk of AGL assets before functional failure occurs, focused on condition-based maintenance and preventive-maintenance decision-making.

Q2How does Division 21 differ from the other inspection divisions?

It asks a different question about the same asset. Division 02 asks whether the runway fixture is acceptable today, Division 20 asks whether the test evidence was properly performed and documented, and Division 21 asks whether the fixture is deteriorating and whether to intervene before it fails.

Q3What is the asset condition rating scale?

A single scale applies across the division: C1 Excellent, C2 Good, C3 Fair, C4 Poor, C5 Critical and C6 Failed/Unserviceable. It is used together with asset criticality, not as a standalone decision, and each rating has a typical action from continuing normal inspection through to removal from service or replacement.

Q4Is the condition rating the same as maintenance priority?

No. Condition rating does not equal maintenance priority. The decision weighs condition, criticality, failure consequence, redundancy and availability, so a C3 runway centreline light may need higher priority than a C4 non-critical spare component.

Q5Can an asset pass a current inspection and still need intervention?

Yes. In the division's worked example, a runway edge light is operational and passes a current inspection, but lens degradation, corrosion, repeated moisture ingress and increasing maintenance interventions lead to a C4 Poor rating, with increased monitoring and planned replacement before functional failure.

Q6What does the condition-based maintenance workflow include?

It identifies the asset, reviews history, inspects current condition, reviews previous defects, test results and failure history, assesses deterioration, assigns a condition rating, assesses criticality and failure consequence, determines maintenance, monitoring and replacement requirements, assigns priority, and records follow-up.

Q7Do airport procedures take precedence over this handbook?

Yes. This handbook is an uncontrolled educational reference, not an approved procedure. The airport's approved inspection schedule, SMS procedures, ATC instructions, NOTAM procedures and local aerodrome regulations always take precedence, and the current revision of any official document should be verified before use.

Q8Can I download a printable checklist for each inspection topic?

Yes. Each inspection topic has a Download as Word option that produces an uncontrolled Word checklist template (description, a tick-box inspection table and an inspector sign-off block) for personal reference and internal training, to be reviewed and adopted under the user’s own document control.
