Refurbishment & Life Extension

The asset-renewal and service-life extension layer of the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide, sitting between Condition Assessment and the final Replacement / Renewal Decision. Division 16 determines whether an existing asset should continue in service, be repaired, refurbished, retrofitted, modernized, life-extended — or replaced — covering life-extension strategy, refurbishment-versus-replacement, LED retrofit, CCR/transformer/cable/sign/PAPI modernization, control-system and ALCMS upgrade, obsolescence management, verification and formal refurbishment acceptance.

Initial Issue

The question this division answers — Should this existing AGL asset continue in service, be repaired, refurbished, retrofitted, modernized and life-extended — or should it be replaced?

Where Division 16 Sits in the Lifecycle

Division 16 is the renewal decision bridge. It draws on criticality, asset health, reliability, lifecycle cost and condition assessment to decide whether an asset is refurbished, retrofitted or modernized — before the eventual replacement and renewal decision.

RLE-01 Asset Life-Extension Strategy
Purpose

An asset life-extension strategy defines how the useful service life of an existing asset can be safely and economically extended beyond its current condition or originally expected operating period.

Consider
  • Current asset age
  • Current condition
  • Original design life
  • Remaining useful life
  • Failure history
  • Criticality
  • Obsolescence
  • Spare availability
  • Technology status
  • Maintenance cost
  • Performance
  • Lifecycle cost
  • Future operational requirements

RLE-02 Refurbishment Assessment
Purpose

Refurbishment assessment determines whether the existing asset has sufficient technical and economic potential for refurbishment.

Assess
  • Physical condition
  • Electrical condition
  • Mechanical condition
  • Optical condition
  • Structural condition
  • Performance
  • Failure history
  • Component availability
  • Obsolescence
  • Upgrade compatibility
  • Expected extended life
  • Refurbishment cost
  • Replacement cost
Key Question

Can the asset be restored or improved to an acceptable level of performance and reliability without replacing the complete asset?

RLE-03 Refurbishment Versus Replacement
Why This Matters

This is one of the most important decision areas in Division 16 — the formal comparison of continuing, refurbishing or replacing an existing asset.

Option A — Continue Existing Asset
  • Maintenance cost
  • Failure risk
  • Spare availability
  • Performance
  • RUL
Option B — Refurbish
  • Refurbishment cost
  • Extended service life
  • Performance improvement
  • Reliability improvement
  • Maintenance reduction
Option C — Replace
  • New equipment
  • Installation
  • Commissioning
  • Energy
  • Maintenance
  • Expected service life
  • Future support
Decision Inputs & Link

Condition, criticality, RUL, reliability, obsolescence, lifecycle cost, operational risk, technology and future requirements.

The decision should connect directly with Division 12 — Asset Lifecycle Cost Management.

RLE-04 Component Replacement
Purpose

Life extension does not necessarily require replacing the complete asset. Individual components may be replaced to restore performance or reliability.

Examples
  • Electronic modules
  • LED modules
  • Drivers
  • Connectors
  • Wiring
  • Control components
  • Fans
  • Relays
  • Protection components
  • Mechanical components
Strategic Objective

Replace the deteriorated or obsolete component while retaining serviceable portions of the existing asset where technically justified.

RLE-06 CCR Modernization
Purpose

CCR modernization may involve upgrading an existing regulator rather than replacing the complete system.

Consider
  • CCR age
  • Failure history
  • Control technology
  • Output performance
  • Protection
  • Monitoring capability
  • Communication capability
  • Component availability
  • Manufacturer support
  • Energy efficiency
  • Compatibility with ALCMS
Potential Objectives

Improve reliability, improve monitoring, improve control, improve diagnostics, reduce obsolescence and improve maintainability.

RLE-07 Transformer Refurbishment
Purpose

Isolation transformers may require refurbishment depending on their condition and lifecycle status.

Assess
  • Physical condition
  • Insulation condition
  • Moisture
  • Corrosion
  • Connections
  • Failure history
  • Test results
  • Age
  • Manufacturer support
  • Availability of replacement units
Decision

Continue vs. Refurbish vs. Replace — based on condition, risk, performance and lifecycle economics.

RLE-08 Cable Rehabilitation
Purpose

Cable rehabilitation should be considered when the existing primary/secondary cable infrastructure remains potentially serviceable but shows deterioration.

Assess
  • Insulation resistance
  • Continuity
  • Failure history
  • Joint condition
  • Termination condition
  • Moisture exposure
  • Environmental conditions
  • Cable age
  • Circuit performance
Potential Interventions
  • Localized repair
  • Joint replacement
  • Termination rehabilitation
  • Section replacement
  • Cable-system rehabilitation
Limit

Complete replacement may be required where deterioration is widespread or risk becomes unacceptable.

RLE-10 PAPI Modernization
Purpose

PAPI modernization should evaluate the existing system's technical condition and future supportability.

Assess
  • Optical system
  • Light source
  • Housing
  • Mechanical alignment
  • Structural condition
  • Electrical system
  • Control/interface
  • Performance
  • Calibration requirements
  • Manufacturer support
  • Obsolescence

RLE-11 Control-System Upgrade
Purpose

Control-system upgrades may be required where existing systems are becoming difficult to maintain or support.

Assess
  • Hardware condition
  • PLC/controller status
  • Communication architecture
  • HMI
  • Software
  • Firmware
  • Interfaces
  • Cyber/security requirements where applicable
  • Spare availability
  • Vendor support
  • Obsolescence
Objective

Maintain or improve availability, reliability, monitoring, diagnostics, maintainability and future supportability.

RLE-12 ALCMS Upgrade
Purpose

ALCMS modernization should consider both existing system performance and future airport requirements.

Assess
  • Hardware
  • Software
  • PLC
  • HMI
  • Communication
  • Monitoring
  • Alarm functionality
  • Interface with AGL equipment
  • Expansion capability
  • Obsolescence
  • Manufacturer support
Lifecycle Objective

Avoid a situation where an otherwise serviceable AGL field system becomes difficult to operate because the control/monitoring platform is obsolete.

RLE-13 Technology Retrofit
Purpose

Technology retrofit is broader than a single component upgrade. It may involve introducing newer technology into an existing asset/system while retaining suitable existing infrastructure.

Examples
  • LED technology
  • Modern CCR controls
  • Digital monitoring
  • Modern communication
  • Electronic protection
  • Advanced diagnostics
  • Improved control interfaces
Evaluate

Technology benefit vs. retrofit cost vs. remaining asset life vs. replacement alternative.

RLE-14 Performance Improvement
Purpose

Refurbishment should not only restore the asset to its original condition where an approved improvement is technically justified.

Performance Objectives May Include
  • Improved reliability
  • Improved availability
  • Improved optical performance
  • Reduced energy consumption
  • Improved control
  • Improved diagnostics
  • Reduced maintenance
  • Improved maintainability
Important

Any performance improvement should be demonstrated through appropriate testing and comparison with the established baseline.

RLE-17 Obsolescence Management
Purpose

Obsolescence is one of the strongest reasons for refurbishment or modernization.

Identify
  • End-of-life components
  • Discontinued products
  • Unsupported software
  • Legacy electronics
  • Unavailable spare parts
  • Manufacturer withdrawal
  • Long procurement lead times
  • Proprietary technology
Possible Responses
MONITORSTRATEGIC SPARE PURCHASECOMPONENT REPLACEMENTRETROFITSYSTEM MODERNIZATIONCOMPLETE REPLACEMENT
Principle

Obsolescence should be identified before it becomes an operational failure.

RLE-18 Life-Extension Verification
Purpose

After refurbishment, the asset should not automatically be considered life-extended. The extension should be verified through technical evidence.

Verify
  • Electrical performance
  • Functional performance
  • Optical performance where applicable
  • Mechanical condition
  • Structural condition
  • Control performance
  • Reliability indicators
  • Energy performance
  • Safety requirements
  • Applicable regulatory requirements
Compare

Pre-refurbishment condition vs. post-refurbishment condition — the result should demonstrate that the refurbishment achieved its intended objectives.

RLE-20 Refurbishment Acceptance
Purpose

Refurbishment acceptance is the formal gateway between refurbishment execution and return to the asset lifecycle — confirming the refurbished asset is technically, operationally and documentarily acceptable for continued service.

A. Scope
  • Approved refurbishment scope completed
  • All specified components replaced/refurbished
  • Deviations documented and approved
B. Physical Condition
  • Equipment correctly assembled
  • Mounting secure
  • Enclosures complete
  • No unacceptable damage
  • Labels/tags updated
C. Electrical Condition
  • Electrical connections verified
  • Insulation tests where applicable
  • Continuity verified
  • Earthing verified
  • Protection verified
  • Functional electrical performance verified
D–F. Optical, Functional & Documentation

Optical (where applicable): Light output, photometric performance, alignment, optical characteristics.

Functional: Normal operation, control operation, monitoring, alarms, intensity/control functions, communication interfaces.

Documentation: Updated asset register, as-built information, test records, refurbishment records, component records, serial numbers, manufacturer documentation, warranty information, updated maintenance requirements.

G. Baseline
  • New post-refurbishment baseline established
  • Condition score updated
  • Health index updated where applicable
  • RUL assessment updated
H. Final Acceptance Status
ACCEPTED FOR CONTINUED SERVICEACCEPTED WITH CONDITIONSNOT ACCEPTED — CORRECTIVE ACTION REQUIRED

Division 16 — Core Principle

Refurbishment and life extension should be treated as evidence-based lifecycle decisions that restore or improve asset performance, reliability and supportability while providing a technically and economically justified extension of useful service life.

The renewal decision should consider: Condition + Criticality + Reliability + Remaining Useful Life + Obsolescence + Lifecycle Cost. The resulting strategy is one of: Maintain / Refurbish / Retrofit / Modernize / Replace.

This makes Division 16 the formal renewal and life-extension decision layer between the AGL Inspection/Condition Assessment system and the eventual Asset Replacement & Renewal strategy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Refurbishment & Life Extension in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide.

Q1What is Division 16 — Refurbishment & Life Extension in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

Division 16 is the asset-renewal and service-life extension layer between condition assessment and the replacement decision. It covers 20 topics on life-extension strategy, refurbishment assessment, refurbishment-versus-replacement, component replacement, LED retrofit, CCR modernization, transformer refurbishment, cable rehabilitation, sign and PAPI modernization, control-system and ALCMS upgrade, technology retrofit, performance and reliability improvement, energy-efficiency upgrade, obsolescence management, life-extension verification, post-refurbishment baseline and refurbishment acceptance.

Q2What is an asset life-extension strategy?

It defines how the useful service life of an existing asset can be safely and economically extended beyond its current condition or original expected life, considering current age, condition and original design life.

Q3How is refurbishment assessed against replacement?

Refurbishment assessment determines whether the asset has enough technical and economic potential for refurbishment. Refurbishment versus replacement is described as one of the most important decision areas, formally comparing continuing, refurbishing or replacing using factors such as maintenance cost, failure risk, spare availability and performance.

Q4Does life extension require replacing the whole asset?

No. Individual components such as electronic modules, LED modules, drivers, connectors, wiring and control components may be replaced to restore performance or reliability.

Q5Which AGL assets have specific refurbishment or modernization topics?

Division 16 covers LED retrofit, CCR modernization, transformer refurbishment, cable rehabilitation, sign modernization, PAPI modernization, control-system upgrades and ALCMS upgrades, as well as broader technology retrofit.

Q6Should refurbishment only restore original condition?

Not necessarily. Where an approved improvement is technically justified, refurbishment can also improve reliability, availability, optical performance and energy use. Life extension should ideally improve reliability rather than simply postpone replacement.

Q7How is life extension verified?

After refurbishment the asset should not automatically be considered life-extended. The extension must be verified through technical evidence such as electrical, functional and optical performance, and a new baseline is established, connecting to Division 07.

Q8What is refurbishment acceptance?

It is the formal gateway between refurbishment execution and return to the asset lifecycle, confirming the refurbished asset is technically and operationally acceptable once the approved scope is completed and specified requirements are met.
