Division 12 of 25 · INS-CIV Series

Civil Works & Base Can Inspection

A standardized methodology for assessing the civil, structural, drainage, mounting, alignment, and pavement-interface condition of AGL base cans, light foundations, pits, mounting hardware, and associated civil works. Covers base-can condition, foundation, grouting, level, alignment, drainage, pit covers, bolts, frangible couplings, cable entries, pavement interfaces, settlement, cracks, cleanliness, and water accumulation.

Initial Issue

Critical technical control — The civil installation should be treated as an integral part of AGL equipment reliability and photometric performance. The inspection chain is Pavement → Foundation → Base Can → Frangible Coupling → Fixture → Cable Entry → Pit → Drainage. A fixture that is electrically healthy can still become operationally unacceptable because of incorrect base-can level, settlement, fixture misalignment, damaged mounting hardware, compromised frangible components, pavement movement, inadequate grouting, water accumulation, blocked drainage, damaged cable entries, or foundation deterioration. Particular attention should be given to level, alignment, pavement interface, drainage, water, and mounting security. Any condition affecting fixture elevation, alignment, frangibility, structural stability, cable protection, or safe pavement operation should be escalated according to the applicable airport engineering and maintenance procedure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about civil works & base can inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook.

Q1What is Division 12 — Civil Works & Base Can Inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook?

Division 12 contains 85 published topics in 16 sections (A–P). It gives a standardized methodology for assessing the civil, structural, drainage, mounting, alignment and pavement-interface condition of AGL base cans, light foundations, pits, mounting hardware and associated civil works.

Q2What does the civil works and base can inspection cover?

It covers base-can level and alignment, foundation condition, grouting, drainage and water management, pit covers, mounting hardware, frangible couplings, cable entry into the base can, pavement interface, surface settlement, cracks and concrete damage, pit cleanliness, the civil interface with AGL equipment, and post-civil-work inspection.

Q3What parameters are recorded for base cans and civil works?

The checklist includes base-can condition, level, elevation and alignment, foundation, grouting, cracks, settlement and differential settlement, drainage, pit water, pit cleanliness, pit cover and cover level, mounting bolts and hardware, frangible coupling, cable entry and sealing, pavement interface, cracks and spalling, water accumulation, FOD, vegetation, corrosion and civil work impact.

Q4What is the standard civil and base-can inspection workflow?

The sequence identifies the location and fixture or base can, checks level, elevation and alignment, and inspects the foundation, grouting, mounting hardware, frangible coupling, cable entry, pit, cover, drainage and water. It then checks the pavement interface, settlement, cracks and spalling, and cleanliness and FOD, before recording the defect, classifying, correcting, reinspecting and closing.

Q5Are inspections required after civil work near AGL equipment?

Yes. Section O covers post-civil-work inspection, and the checklist records civil work impact as None or Present.

Q6How are civil and base-can defects classified?

Defect status is recorded using the four-level scheme Normal, Defective, Unserviceable and Immediate Action Required, so every observation ends with a clear classification before it is recorded and escalated.

Q7Why is civil work treated as part of AGL equipment reliability?

The civil installation is treated as an integral part of AGL equipment reliability and photometric performance. An electrically healthy fixture can still become operationally unacceptable because of base-can level, settlement, misalignment, damaged mounting hardware, compromised frangible components, pavement movement, inadequate grouting, water accumulation, blocked drainage, damaged cable entries or foundation deterioration.

Q8Do airport procedures take precedence over this handbook?

Yes. This handbook is an uncontrolled educational reference, not an approved procedure. The airport's approved inspection schedule, SMS procedures, ATC instructions, NOTAM procedures and local aerodrome regulations always take precedence, and the current revision of any official document should be verified before use.

Q9Can I download a printable checklist for each inspection topic?

Yes. Each inspection topic has a Download as Word option that produces an uncontrolled Word checklist template (description, a tick-box inspection table and an inspector sign-off block) for personal reference and internal training, to be reviewed and adopted under the user’s own document control.
