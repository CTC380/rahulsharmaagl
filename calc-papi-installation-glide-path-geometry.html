About this calculator

PAPI system, PAPI unit, aiming angle and glide-path angle are related but distinct parameters — this calculator keeps them separate throughout. A geometric 3° calculation alone does not establish complete PAPI compliance; the exact standard-specific dimensional database and obstacle limitation-surface criteria must be verified against the applicable ICAO/FAA/DGCA requirements, approved aerodrome design, and actual site survey.

Individual Unit Aiming Angles & Indication Sectors

This does not equate "3 white = exactly X°" — it models the transition-sector boundaries generated by the individual optical aiming angles you enter, per the governing specification. P1 is the unit nearest the runway and carries the highest setting; the defaults follow the common pattern θ+30′, +10′, −10′, −30′ for four units and θ±15′ for two (edit to the approved design).

UnitAiming Angle θ (°)Δθ to next unitRed/white transition angle (°)
Indication model (standard 4-unit PAPI): 4 white = well above the glide path · 3 white/1 red = slightly above · 2 white/2 red = on the glide path · 1 white/3 red = slightly below · 4 red = well below (plain-language labels; ICAO Annex 14 words these as further above, above, on, below and further below). Each unit changes from white to red at its own aiming (setting) angle θi, so the transition angle column equals the entered setting angle. As the aircraft descends, the unit with the highest setting angle (P1, nearest the runway) turns red first and the lowest-setting unit (the farthest) turns red last.

Obstacle Clearance — Preliminary Geometric Screening

Measured along the approach, before the threshold (positive). The screening adds DPT to reach the distance from the PAPI.

Obstacle protection surface slope = reference angle − this offset. ICAO Annex 14 sets the surface 0.57° (about 34′) below the setting of the lowest unit (unit A, the unit farthest from the runway) for a four-unit PAPI and 0.9° (54′) below unit A for an APAPI. The default offset follows the number of units until you edit it. Verify against the edition you design to, or enter the project value.

Using α instead of the lowest unit setting makes the surface steeper, which is the less conservative choice. Use it only when the governing standard defines the surface that way, and enter that standard's offset above.

This is a preliminary screening of the obstacle against an obstacle protection slope set below the unit settings (the reference angle and offset entered above, measured from the PAPI elevation at the PAPI position), and does not model the OPS origin distance, width or splay. It is not a complete ICAO obstacle-limitation-surface / approach-surface compliance determination.

Method & Reference

How This Calculator Works

PAPI system, PAPI unit, aiming angle, glide-path angle and threshold crossing height are calculated as distinct, related parameters — never collapsed into a single "3° angle" assumption.

Array width

Warray = (N − 1) × SPAPI

Centre-to-centre distance from the first to the last unit — not the physical housing width.

Basic glide-path equation

H = D × tan(α)

Example: D = 300 m, α = 3° → H ≈ 15.72 m, the geometric height difference of a 3° line over 300 m.

PAPI-to-threshold geometry

gradient = tan−¹(ΔE / DPT)

ΔE = EPAPI − ETH. This is the ground gradient between the two reference points. It is informational only: it is not the glide angle or the optical aiming angle and is not compared against the nominal angle.

Forward / reverse TCH

Forward: HTCH = DPT × tan(α) + ΔE     Reverse: D = (HTCH − ΔE) / tan(α)

Mode A calculates TCH from position & elevation; Mode B derives the theoretical PAPI position/elevation requirement from a target TCH — a design-analysis tool in both directions. Siting standards fix the PAPI distance from the lowest on-course angle and a minimum eye height over threshold (for ICAO, the setting of unit B minus 2′); this tool works from the nominal angle α, so treat the reverse result as a first estimate and confirm it against the siting table in the applicable standard.

Check-stick geometry

HGP = DCS × tan(α)     αmeasured = tan−¹(ΔH / D)

Expected vs. measured height difference (ΔH) is checked against the applicable tolerance for a PASS/FAIL result, plus a measured glide-path angle for comparison against the required angle.

Terminology kept separate

What this calculator does not do

It does not maintain the exact ICAO/FAA/DGCA PAPI dimensional database, does not perform a complete obstacle-limitation-surface / approach-surface compliance determination, and does not treat a passed geometric 3° check as full PAPI compliance. Geometry, TCH, aiming-angle framework, check-stick, preliminary obstacle screening, unit elevation and foundation slope are fully calculated; exact figure-level acceptance must be confirmed against the approved design and site survey.

FAQ

PAPI Installation and Glide-Path Geometry Calculator: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on PAPI placement, threshold crossing height, aiming angles and the geometric checks.

What does the PAPI Installation and Glide-Path Geometry Calculator do?

It calculates PAPI array geometry, lateral and longitudinal location, threshold crossing height (TCH), unit-by-unit aiming angles and indication sectors, check-stick verification, a preliminary obstacle screening, and unit elevation and foundation slope. It also records an existing installation and produces a PDF report.

Does a passed geometric 3° check mean the PAPI is compliant?

No. The calculator states that a geometric 3° calculation alone does not establish complete PAPI compliance. PAPI geometry is safety-critical, and the results shall be verified against the applicable ICAO, FAA or DGCA requirements, the approved aerodrome design and actual site survey data. The calculator does not perform a complete obstacle-limitation-surface or approach-surface compliance determination.

How is the threshold crossing height calculated?

In forward mode, HTCH = DPT × tan(α) + ΔE, where DPT is the PAPI distance from the threshold, α the nominal glide-path angle and ΔE = EPAPI − ETH. In reverse mode, D = (HTCH − ΔE) ÷ tan(α) gives the theoretical position needed for a target TCH. Siting standards fix the PAPI distance from the lowest on-course angle and a minimum eye height over the threshold, so treat the reverse result as a first estimate and confirm it against the governing standard. In forward mode the page also shows the eye height over the threshold at the lowest on-course angle (unit B − 2′ for a four-unit PAPI, unit A − 2′ for an APAPI) for comparison with the minimum eye height over threshold (MEHT) in ICAO Annex 14.

What is the difference between the aiming angle and the glide-path angle?

The aiming angle is the optical setting of one unit. The glide-path angle is the resulting visual approach path. They are related but not identical, and the calculator keeps the PAPI system, unit, box or housing, aiming angle, glide-path angle and threshold crossing height as separate terms. The default pattern follows θ + 30′, +10′, −10′ and −30′ for four units and θ ± 15′ for two, and you should edit it to the approved design.

How does the check-stick verification work?

The expected height on the glide path is HGP = DCS × tan(α) at the check-stick distance. You enter the measured height, and the difference ΔH is compared with the tolerance for a PASS or FAIL result. The calculator also gives a measured glide-path angle, αmeasured = tan⁻¹(ΔH ÷ D), to compare with the required angle.

How does the obstacle screening work?

It is a preliminary geometric screening of an obstacle against an obstacle protection slope set below the unit settings. ICAO Annex 14 sets the surface 0.57° (about 34′) below the setting of the lowest unit (unit A, the unit farthest from the runway) for a four-unit PAPI and 0.9° (54′) below unit A for an APAPI. The default offset follows the number of units until you edit it. It does not model the surface origin distance, width or splay, so it is not a complete compliance determination.

Are the spacing, distance and tolerance values hard-coded to a standard?

No. The standard-specific figures are project-entered. The values pre-filled on the page (9 m spacing, 300 m from the threshold, 3° angle, ±0.5 m lateral tolerance, ±0.03 m check-stick tolerance, 0.5 % slope tolerance and 0.57° OPS offset) are editable placeholders, not standard figures. The exact dimensional database and obstacle limitation criteria are not incorporated.

Why enter the elevation of each PAPI unit?

A PAPI installation cannot be evaluated using horizontal distance alone, so each unit's individual elevation is entered if the foundation is not perfectly level. The calculator then reports the elevation difference between units and the foundation slope against your tolerance. The existing-installation inspection records measured units for reference only, and measured aiming angles are checked in the Survey and Aiming Verification tool.
