AGL Fixture Installation Comparison

Surface Installation VS Recessed Installation

Surface installation places equipment on or above the mounting surface, while recessed installation places equipment partially or fully below the surface level. The key distinction is physical position relative to the finished surface, which in turn drives visibility, civil work, maintenance access and suitability for aircraft movement areas.

02 · Definitions

What Is Surface & Recessed Installation?

Surface Installation

In a surface installation, the equipment is mounted on top of the pavement, ground, structure, or support. It projects above the installation surface and is therefore easier to identify and access.

Typical AGL examples

  • Elevated runway edge lights
  • Elevated taxiway edge lights
  • Surface-mounted signs
  • Electrical control cabinets
  • Equipment mounted on foundations or supports

Recessed Installation

In a recessed installation, the equipment is installed inside a prepared opening or housing below the finished surface. It is integrated into the surface rather than projecting above it.

Typical AGL examples

  • Inset runway centreline lights
  • Inset taxiway centreline lights
  • Inset stop-bar lights
  • Inset touchdown-zone lights
03 · Key Difference

Above the Surface vs Below/Within the Surface

The simplest way to remember the difference between surface and recessed installation:

Surface

Above the surface

Recessed

Below/within the surface

Engineering point: Surface-mounted equipment projects above the installation surface, whereas recessed equipment is integrated into the surface. The lowest installation cost does not always mean the correct AGL solution — operational and aircraft-clearance requirements determine whether an elevated or inset/recessed fixture is appropriate.

05 · Installation Requirements

Civil & Electrical Work

Surface Installation

SELECT LOCATION ↓ PREPARE FOUNDATION / SUPPORT ↓ INSTALL FIXTURE ↓ CONNECT CABLE ↓ ALIGN ↓ TEST

Usually involves less pavement excavation.

Recessed Installation

SELECT LOCATION ↓ CUT / PREPARE PAVEMENT ↓ INSTALL LIGHT BASE ↓ INSTALL DUCT / CABLE ↓ INSTALL FIXTURE ↓ ALIGN ↓ SEAL ↓ TEST

Generally requires greater coordination between civil and electrical works.

07 · Maintenance

Maintenance Comparison

Surface Installation

Advantages include:

  • Easier visual inspection
  • Easier physical access
  • Faster replacement
  • Less pavement work

Recessed Installation

Typically requires more attention to:

  • Sealing
  • Water ingress
  • Light-base condition
  • Connector condition
  • Drainage
  • Torque of mounting bolts

Recessed maintenance may require: remove fixture → access light base → check connection → reinstall and seal. Surface maintenance is generally: inspect → replace.

08 · Mechanical Protection

Exposure vs Integration

Surface Installation

The fixture is more exposed to:

  • Vehicle contact
  • Aircraft blast
  • Accidental impact
  • Weather exposure

However, elevated AGL fixtures may use frangible mounting arrangements where required.

Recessed Installation

The pavement and light base provide additional physical integration and protection. However, recessed fixtures must withstand:

  • Aircraft wheel loads
  • Pavement movement
  • Water ingress
  • Contamination
  • Mechanical stress

11 · Typical Engineering Selection

Choosing Between Surface & Recessed

Choose Surface When

  • Easy maintenance is important
  • Aircraft do not pass directly over the fixture
  • Elevated fixtures are operationally permitted
  • Lower civil-work complexity is preferred
  • The location allows an above-ground fixture

Choose Recessed When

  • A low-profile installation is required
  • Aircraft wheels may pass over the location
  • Surface obstruction must be minimized
  • The applicable AGL design requires inset lighting

13 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: Surface installation is always the cheaper option overall
Fact

Not necessarily over the fixture's lifecycle. Surface installation is usually lower in initial civil-work cost, but recessed fixtures reduce obstruction risk and can lower long-term exposure to impact damage in aircraft movement areas.

Myth: Recessed fixtures never need to be removed for maintenance
Fact

Incorrect. Recessed/inset fixtures may still require fixture removal to access the light base, check connections and reseal — maintenance is generally more involved than a surface fixture, not eliminated.

Myth: A surface-mounted fixture can be used anywhere an inset fixture is used
Fact

Not true. Where aircraft wheels pass directly over a location, a low-profile recessed/inset fixture is normally required; an elevated surface fixture would create an obstruction.

14 · Engineering Verdict

Which Installation Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

Surface installation places equipment on or above the mounting surface, providing easier access and generally simpler civil work but creating a physical projection above the surface. Recessed installation places equipment partially or fully below the surface, providing a low-profile arrangement suited to aircraft movement areas but requiring more civil work and installation complexity.

For AGL systems, the correct choice depends primarily on whether aircraft traffic passes directly over the location, obstruction-clearance requirements, maintenance strategy and civil-work budget — not simply on which option has the lower upfront cost.

SurfacePrioritizes accessibility and simpler installation, at the cost of surface projection and exposure.
RecessedPrioritizes a low profile and suitability for aircraft movement areas, at the cost of civil-work complexity.

15 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is surface installation always the right choice for edge lights?

No. Surface/elevated edge lights are common, but the choice still depends on aircraft clearance, obstruction limits and the applicable AGL design requirements for the specific location.

Why are centreline and stop-bar lights typically recessed?

Because aircraft wheels commonly pass directly over these locations, a low-profile inset fixture minimizes obstruction and impact risk compared with a surface-mounted fixture.

Does recessed installation always cost more than surface installation?

Usually the initial cost is higher due to pavement cutting, light-base installation and greater civil/electrical coordination, though this must be weighed against operational suitability rather than cost alone.

Is maintenance always harder for recessed fixtures?

Generally, yes — recessed fixtures may require removal to access the light base, along with closer attention to sealing, water ingress and connector condition, compared with the easier visual inspection and access of surface fixtures.

Can a surface-mounted AGL fixture still be protected against impact?

Yes. Elevated AGL fixtures may use frangible mounting arrangements where required, so that the fixture breaks away safely under load rather than posing a rigid obstruction.

What is the single deciding factor between surface and recessed installation?

There is no single factor — but whether aircraft wheels pass directly over the fixture location is usually the primary operational driver, alongside obstruction clearance and applicable AGL design requirements.

Applicable material covering surface and recessed fixture mounting, light-base design and pavement integration guidance.

National CAA / DGCA / AAI Requirements

Final Engineering Note
