Green Threshold Light

A specialized runway threshold lighting fixture that provides green visual guidance to pilots approaching a runway. Green threshold lights are a critical component of the runway lighting system, marking the landing threshold and providing a clear visual reference during final approach and landing.

Overview

What Is It?

A Green Threshold Light is a specialized runway threshold lighting fixture that provides green visual guidance to pilots approaching a runway. These lights are installed at the runway threshold and are part of the approach lighting system. The green colour indicates to pilots that they are aligned with the runway centreline and are on the correct approach path. Green threshold lights are a critical component of the runway lighting system, marking the landing threshold and providing a clear visual reference during final approach and landing.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The green colour of this fixture exists to give pilots an unambiguous "start of usable landing surface" cue that cannot be mistaken for any other signal on the field, since green is reserved specifically for this role across the runway lighting system. This is critical because the runway threshold does not always coincide with the physical start of the pavement — a displaced threshold, overrun, or blast pad may extend beyond it — so a purely visual judgement of where the pavement begins is not reliable enough on its own. During final approach, particularly at night or in reduced visibility, this dedicated marker lets the pilot confirm correct alignment with the landing surface and time the flare and touchdown against a fixed, trustworthy reference rather than an estimate.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Green threshold lights are installed at the runway threshold on precision approach runways. They are used on:

  • Precision approach runways (CAT II and III) where approach guidance is critical
  • Runways with approach lighting systems requiring threshold identification
  • Low visibility operations where visual cues are essential
  • Runways with complex approach paths requiring enhanced guidance
  • Military airfields requiring precise approach guidance
Placement

Installation & Location

Fixtures are set out in a single row across the full width of the runway at the threshold, spaced evenly on each side of the centreline in line with the runway edge lights, with additional units forming the wing bars where fitted. Each light mounts on an L-868 base can recessed flush with the pavement or shoulder, sealed against surface water, with cabling run in pavement ducting or shoulder trenching back to the isolating transformer serving the threshold circuit. Where the runway is used in both directions, the fixtures are unidirectional units angled so the green beam faces only the approach direction, with the reverse face fitted with a red lens or blanked so it does not confuse traffic departing from the opposite end. Setting-out is tied to the threshold survey mark so the light line remains square to the runway centreline, and the row is coordinated with the approach lighting system so the last approach light bar and the threshold row read as optically continuous to the approaching pilot.

Function

How Does It Work?

Green threshold lights operate as part of the approach and runway lighting system:

  • Installation: Green threshold lights are installed at the runway threshold, typically in a row across the runway width.
  • Lighting: The lights are green and project a uni-directional beam towards the approaching aircraft, providing a clear visual reference of the runway threshold.
  • Guidance: The green lights indicate to the pilot that they are aligned with the runway centreline and are on the correct approach path.
  • Integration: Green threshold lights are integrated with approach lighting systems, runway edge lights, and other runway lighting components.
Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

Electrically, green threshold lights are controlled exactly like runway edge lights — they are not individually addressable, and control is exercised at the loop level. An ALCMS, operated by the tower or an automated visibility-linked routine, selects the required brightness step and passes that command to the CCR, which is commonly wired in series with the adjacent runway edge circuit so the threshold row and the edge lights change intensity together. Every threshold fixture on that loop draws from the same fixed current through its own isolating transformer and therefore burns at the same intensity simultaneously; there is no way to dim or brighten one fixture independently of the row. Fault detection is likewise loop-based — a voltage rise at constant current signals increased impedance, typically an open-circuit lamp or a failed isolating transformer, and is reported to the ALCMS as a loop fault rather than pinpointed to an exact fixture. The unidirectional beam described in the section below is a purely optical property of the fixture housing; it plays no part in how the light is switched or dimmed.

Construction

Physical Construction

The fixture body is typically a die-cast aluminium alloy housing, machined and anodised or powder-coated for corrosion resistance against runway de-icing fluids, fuel spillage, and UV exposure. The green uni-directional lens is a borosilicate glass or high-strength polycarbonate prism, gasket-sealed to the housing with an O-ring or silicone seal to achieve an IP67/IP68 rating against water and dust ingress, and shaped internally to filter light to the approach side only. Elevated units (FAA Type L-862E on precision-approach runways, L-861E on non-precision) bolt to a frangible coupling engineered to shear cleanly under aircraft impact loads, seated on an L-867 base can set flush with the surrounding grade; flush-mounted (inset) units (FAA Type L-850D) instead seat directly into an L-868 base can recessed into the pavement, with the top surface load-rated for in-pavement aircraft wheel and jet-blast loading per the FAA AC 150/5345-42 light base specification, and its retaining bolts torqued to the correct clamping force per FAA Engineering Brief 83A (In-Pavement Light Fixture Bolts). Internal wiring terminates at L-823 primary connectors, sealed splice connectors rated for direct burial and continuous immersion, which mate to the secondary leads of the isolating transformer housed in its own base can nearby. Stainless-steel fasteners are used throughout to resist corrosion, and the base can assembly includes a drainage provision to prevent water pooling around the transformer and cable entry points.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Green Filter Degradation

The green filters in threshold lights are susceptible to UV degradation and thermal stress from halogen lamps. Over time, the colour can fade or shift toward yellow, reducing the effectiveness of the visual cue. Regular photometric testing and filter replacement is essential.

Lens Contamination

Green threshold lights are exposed to rubber deposits from landing aircraft, tyre debris, and de-icing fluids. Regular cleaning with approved solvents and soft brushes is required to maintain light output and colour fidelity.

Misalignment from Pavement Settlement

Pavement settlement can cause threshold lights to tilt, altering the beam pattern and reducing visibility on approach. Regular alignment checks using a digital level or specialized alignment jig are recommended.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Single Fixture Dark, Rest of Row Normal
Likely Cause
Failed LED array / halogen lamp, or an open isolating transformer (blown primary fuse-wire link or failed secondary winding) at that fixture.
Diagnostic Steps
Confirm series loop continuity is unbroken through the fixture's transformer primary; megger the transformer; check secondary voltage at the L-823 connector with the CCR energised.
Corrective Action
Replace the LED array/lamp if the transformer checks healthy; otherwise swap the isolating transformer and re-seal the L-823 connectors before backfill.
Entire Series Circuit Dark / CCR Will Not Regulate
Likely Cause
Open-circuit fault in the series loop (severed cable, corroded L-823 splice, or a failed transformer acting as an open point) — often shared with the adjacent runway edge circuit.
Diagnostic Steps
Check the CCR fault/open-circuit indication first; loop-resistance test from the CCR output to isolate the faulted segment; sectionalise and megger cable runs to narrow down the break.
Corrective Action
Excavate and repair or replace the faulted cable section and connector; re-test insulation resistance before reinstating the circuit.
CCR Trips on Overcurrent or Ground Fault
Likely Cause
Moisture ingress at a fixture or connector — more common on flush-mounted (inset) units subject to standing water — has degraded insulation resistance, creating a short or partial ground fault on the series loop.
Diagnostic Steps
Megger the full circuit to identify the low-resistance segment; visually inspect suspect fixtures for cracked gaskets, condensation inside the lens, or damaged secondary cable jackets.
Corrective Action
Dry out or replace the affected fixture/connector, renew O-rings and gaskets, and confirm insulation resistance is back above the acceptable minimum before re-energising.
Green Colour Faded or Shifted Toward Yellow/White
Likely Cause
UV degradation and thermal stress from a halogen source have faded or shifted the green filter/prism, or an incorrect LED array has been fitted, weakening the colour cue used to identify the threshold.
Diagnostic Steps
Perform a chromaticity/colour check against ICAO Annex 14 green boundary limits; inspect the lens for haze, yellowing, or filter delamination; confirm lamp/LED type matches the specified fixture.
Corrective Action
Replace the green lens/filter or LED array, and re-verify chromaticity and intensity before returning the fixture to service.
Beam Visible from Wrong Direction or Misaligned
Likely Cause
Pavement or subgrade settlement has tilted the fixture, or the uni-directional optic has been rotated during maintenance, altering the beam so it no longer projects cleanly toward the approach side only.
Diagnostic Steps
Check fixture aim and orientation with a digital level or alignment jig; confirm the lens/prism is correctly indexed toward the approach direction; inspect surrounding pavement or turf for settlement or heave.
Corrective Action
Re-level and re-orient the fixture so the beam is confined to the approach side, and address any base can or subgrade settlement to prevent recurrence.
Cracked Lens or Housing Damage Under Wheel/Impact Load
Likely Cause
Flush-mounted (inset) units: repeated aircraft wheel loading has fatigued the lens or top seal. Elevated units: frangible coupling has begun to shear, or the fixture has been struck or has settled.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect the lens and top-cap seal for cracking, chips, or seal failure; on elevated units, check the frangible coupling for cracking or play and verify the fixture still sits level.
Corrective Action
Replace the lens, gasket, or frangible coupling as required, re-level the fixture, and confirm the L-867/L-868 base can and surrounding pavement remain sound.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

Green Filter / Lens Chromaticity Shift
High Severity
Root Cause
UV exposure and thermal stress from a halogen source fading or shifting the green filter/prism, delamination of the coloured coating, or an incorrectly specified LED array fitted at the fixture.
Effect
Colour cue drifts toward yellow or white, weakening or invalidating the visual threshold identification on approach even while the fixture still appears to be lit correctly.
Detection Method
Chromaticity check against ICAO Annex 14 green boundary limits during scheduled photometric testing, and visual inspection of the lens for haze or yellowing.
Uni-Directional Optic Misalignment
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Pavement or subgrade settlement tilting the fixture, or the indexed lens/prism being rotated out of its correct orientation during maintenance or lamp replacement.
Effect
Beam no longer confines cleanly to the approach side, degrading the threshold cue for arriving aircraft or, if rotated far enough, becoming visible from the wrong direction.
Detection Method
Fixture aim and orientation check with a digital level or alignment jig, confirming the lens/prism indexing against the approach direction.
LED Array / Isolating Transformer Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Thermal cycling or a manufacturing defect in the LED array or halogen source, or moisture-driven insulation breakdown in the isolating transformer winding.
Effect
Open-circuit point in the series loop; darkens the single fixture or, if the open extends to the loop itself, takes down the shared threshold/edge circuit.
Detection Method
Megger test of the transformer, secondary voltage check at the L-823 connector with the CCR energised, and loop-resistance testing from the CCR output.
L-823 Connector / Series Circuit Insulation Breakdown
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Moisture ingress at the base can, gaskets, or connectors — more common on flush-mounted (inset) units subject to standing water — and improper seating or crimping at installation.
Effect
Intermittent operation or a partial ground fault that degrades insulation resistance and can trip the CCR on overcurrent or ground fault.
Detection Method
Megger testing of the circuit and visual inspection of connectors, gaskets, and top-cap O-rings during maintenance.
Lens / Housing Fatigue Under Wheel or Impact Load
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Flush-mounted units: repeated aircraft wheel loading fatigues the lens or top seal. Elevated units: frangible coupling fatigue, or a strike/settlement event at the fixture.
Effect
Cracked lens with moisture ingress on inset units, or a leaning, misaligned, or separated fixture on elevated units.
Detection Method
Lens and top-cap seal inspection on inset units; frangible coupling and level checks on elevated units; surrounding pavement or turf inspection for settlement in both cases.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Series Circuit Isolation
Electrical Safety

Threshold lights sit on a constant-current series circuit that can carry several kV across the loop. De-energise and lock out the CCR, then verify zero energy at the base can before opening it or handling connectors.

Airside Work Coordination
Operational

The threshold row marks the start of the usable landing surface and sits inside the touchdown zone; any work here requires ATC coordination and a NOTAM where fixtures will be dark, with personnel clear of live traffic.

Touchdown Zone Loading
Mechanical

This fixture sits in a heavily loaded pavement area; confirm the base can lid and lens are seated flush and fully secured after any work, since a proud edge here is a wheel-load and FOD risk on every landing.

Base Can Seal Integrity
Mechanical

As an inset fitting, reseal the base can and cable gland fully watertight after any work to keep surface water and rubber-deposit contamination away from the connector and optics.

Insulation Test Before Re-Energisation
Electrical Safety

After any cable, connector, or transformer work, megger test the affected series loop before restoring power, since a residual fault at re-energisation can trip the CCR or darken the whole loop.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Do not leave a fixture dark, misaligned, or at reduced intensity after maintenance; verify beam orientation and photometric conformance before closing out and cancelling the associated NOTAM.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Colour and Alignment Are the Same Signal
Optical

A green threshold light communicates correct alignment and threshold location at once — a discoloured lens or degraded filter doesn't just dim the light, it can shift the perceived colour enough to undermine the alignment cue itself. Verify colour, not just intensity, when assessing fixture condition.

Uni-Directional Filtering Depends on Exact Orientation
Mechanical

The lens is internally shaped to filter light to the approach side only. A fixture reinstated even a few degrees off orientation after base-can work can leak light to the wrong side or dim the intended approach-side output without tripping an obvious fault.

Frangible Couplings Age, Even Without Impact
Mechanical

On elevated units, UV and thermal cycling can shift a frangible coupling's shear characteristics over years of service. A coupling that looks visually fine can no longer shear at its rated load, so periodic torque/shear verification belongs in the overhaul cycle, not just the post-impact inspection.

Size the Transformer to the Load, Not the Legacy Lamp
Retrofit

Reusing an isolating transformer sized for the old incandescent wattage on a lower-draw LED retrofit is a common source of premature transformer failure and nuisance loop trips. Re-select VA rating against the actual LED load.

Trend Loop Readings Rather Than Spot-Checking
Troubleshooting

Connector moisture ingress at the threshold tends to show up first as a slow insulation-resistance drift before it becomes a hard fault. Logging megger readings over successive cycles catches a failing splice months before it darkens the fixture.

De-Icing Fluid Ages Seals Faster Than UV Alone
Durability

Repeated exposure to fuel drip and glycol-based de-icing fluid at the threshold degrades gaskets and lens seals faster than sun and weather alone. Specify seal materials rated for fuel/glycol contact and check gasket condition at overhaul, not only at install.
