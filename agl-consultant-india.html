01 · Overview

What Does an AGL Consultant in India Do?

An AGL consultant in India provides specialist engineering support for the planning, design, specification, testing, commissioning, modernization and maintenance of Aeronautical Ground Lighting (AGL) systems at airports and aerodromes.

AGL consultancy combines airport engineering, electrical engineering, optics, pavement interfaces, control systems and aviation regulatory requirements. The consultant acts as the specialist technical interface between the airport's operational requirement and the installed lighting system — from initial concept through final commissioning.

In India, the work has to consider the applicable DGCA requirements, the ICAO framework, airport / operator requirements and the approved project specifications. The DGCA aerodrome framework was amended during 2025, including amendments to CAR Section 4, Series B, Part I, to incorporate ICAO changes and requirements associated with airport development.[1]

Why Airport Projects Need Specialist AGL Consultancy

AGL is not ordinary external lighting. A runway light has to perform a defined visual function from a specific aircraft viewing geometry, and a series-circuit system behaves electrically very differently from conventional parallel lighting.

ICAO

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I is the principal international aerodrome reference. ICAO's catalogue lists the 9th Edition (July 2022), including Amendment 18 dated 4 August 2025 and subsequent corrigenda issued in 2026.[2] For visual aids, ICAO also publishes Aerodrome Design Manual Part 4 — Visual Aids (Doc 9157). ICAO's AGL training material describes AGL design as implemented in accordance with Annex 14, Doc 9157 and industry best practices.[3]

DGCA

An AGL consultant in India should identify the current DGCA requirements applicable to the aerodrome before beginning detailed design. ICAO should not be treated as the only controlling document — DGCA requirements and airport-specific approved documents must also be checked.

Operational requirement first

DGCA approvals for CAT-II low-visibility operations at Raipur and Bhopal during 2025, and an approach-lighting upgrade at IGI Airport for CAT-II/III operations, show why design must follow the operational requirement, not merely the existing installation.[1]

CCR Selection

Selection requires more than matching the nominal current. Consider circuit current and load, fixture load, transformer characteristics, number of fixtures, circuit length, regulation, brightness stages, control interface, monitoring and LED compatibility. Where an FAA equipment specification is used, treat it separately from ICAO/DGCA aerodrome design requirements.

Isolation Transformers

Specified and verified for runway, taxiway, approach, centreline and threshold lights, PAPI and signs. Selection considers primary current, secondary requirement, frequency, load, installation environment, connector compatibility and fixture technology.

Cables & Connectors

Develop the cable and connector philosophy together with the series-circuit design: primary cable, route, joints, terminations, transformer and fixture connections, spare capacity, insulation and mechanical protection. Connector quality matters — one small fault can affect a fixture's reliability.

Light Bases & Housings

Coordinate the mechanical interface — light bases (deep and shallow), transformer housings, junction boxes and mounting hardware — addressing finished elevation, drainage, pavement interface, cable access, mechanical loading and maintainability.

17 · Common Mistakes

Common AGL Consulting Mistakes

Seven errors that specialist consultancy is designed to prevent.

Treating ICAO and FAA as interchangeable

They are different regulatory and technical frameworks.

Designing from equipment backwards

The correct sequence is operational requirement → lighting design → electrical design → equipment selection.

Using generic PAPI dimensions

PAPI location and geometry must be derived from the applicable design.

Accepting functional tests as photometry

A light being ON does not establish complete optical compliance.

Ignoring secondary power

Critical lighting availability depends on power-system design.

Ignoring control integration

Stop bars and critical AGL may depend on control and monitoring functions.

Producing incomplete as-builts

Future maintenance requires accurate asset and circuit information.

20 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an AGL consultant?
An AGL consultant is a specialist airport-lighting engineer or consultancy organization that supports the design, specification, testing, commissioning, maintenance and modernization of Aeronautical Ground Lighting systems.
What does an AGL consultant do in India?
An AGL consultant can provide runway and taxiway lighting design, PAPI engineering, electrical design, CCR and transformer selection, ALCMS/ILCMS consultancy, photometry, testing, commissioning, BOQ preparation, technical specifications and maintenance support.
Which standards does an AGL consultant use?
The consultant identifies the requirements applicable to the particular project, which may include ICAO Annex 14, ICAO Doc 9157, DGCA CARs, airport/AAI specifications and equipment standards.
Is ICAO Annex 14 enough for an Indian airport?
No. Indian projects also need to consider the applicable DGCA requirements and airport-specific approved documents. India's aerodrome regulatory framework was amended during 2025 to incorporate ICAO changes and airport development requirements.
Can an AGL consultant prepare a BOQ?
Yes. AGL consultants can develop or review BOQs based on the approved lighting design, electrical system, control architecture and project specifications.
Can an AGL consultant perform photometric testing?
Yes. Specialist AGL consultancy can include field photometry. Current AAI procurement records show dedicated AGL photometric field-testing requirements at Indian airports.
Can an AGL consultant design PAPI?
Yes. PAPI consultancy can include siting, elevation, aiming, alignment, geometry, photometry and commissioning.
Does an AGL consultant need to be an equipment manufacturer?
No. An independent AGL consultant can provide design, specification, inspection and evaluation services without being the equipment manufacturer.
Can an AGL consultant evaluate AGL equipment vendors?
Yes. The consultant can prepare technical compliance matrices and evaluate equipment against the project's applicable requirements.
What is the difference between an AGL consultant and an AGL contractor?
An AGL consultant primarily provides engineering, design, technical review, testing and advisory services. An AGL contractor generally executes supply, installation, construction, testing or maintenance according to the project scope.
Why is AGL photometry important?
Photometry provides objective evidence of the optical performance of the installed lighting system. A fixture can operate electrically while still having problems with intensity, colour, coverage or alignment.
Is AGL important for CAT-II/III operations?
Yes. Low-visibility operations depend on the availability and performance of critical visual aids, power and related airport systems. Current Indian AIP material documents specific AGL serviceability and standby-power provisions for low-visibility procedures.
Can an AGL consultant help with an existing airport?
Yes. Consultancy can include condition assessment, compliance gap analysis, photometry, troubleshooting, rehabilitation design, modernization planning and maintenance optimization.

Approach lighting design

Approach lighting design sets the layout, light types, circuits and power for the approach lighting system of a runway. It follows the approach category, the ICAO and DGCA requirements and the airport's operating needs, and it must be coordinated with the runway lighting, CCRs and control system.

An AGL technical proposal sets out the scope, design basis, applicable standards, equipment, testing and commissioning approach for a project. A clear proposal helps the airport compare bids and see how each requirement will be met.
