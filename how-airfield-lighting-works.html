Overview

What Is Airfield Ground Lighting and How Does It Work?

Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) is an interconnected system of electrical, optical, mechanical and control components that gives aircraft visual guidance on and around an airport. Airport power feeds a Constant Current Regulator (CCR), which controls the current in a primary series circuit. Isolating transformers then pass power from that circuit to individual light fixtures, and control equipment such as an ALCMS operates and monitors the whole thing.

      Power

      The Series Circuit and the CCR

      Why a series circuit

      A runway has many lights spread over long distances. A series circuit lets them share one controlled current source: CCR, series cable, transformer 1, transformer 2, transformer 3 and so on, back to the return. Each transformer secondary feeds its own fixture. This differs from building lighting, where loads usually sit in parallel across a fixed voltage.

      What a CCR does

      It maintains the selected current (for example 6.6 A) through the series circuit despite changes in load. The CCR controls current; the series-circuit load determines the voltage needed to maintain it.

      If circuit impedance…CCR output voltage may…Series current…
      IncreasesIncreaseStays controlled
      DecreasesDecreaseStays controlled

      Brightness control

      Generally done by changing the CCR's operating current: lower current gives lower fixture output, higher current gives higher output. Systems can have different numbers of intensity steps, and FAA examples include three-step and five-step CCRs.

      Monitoring

      A monitored system can watch selected parameters and raise alarms: CCR status, series-circuit status, selected current, regulator fault, circuit load change, lighting failure and communication status. Actual functionality depends on the CCR, monitoring system and airport architecture.

      Transformer and Cables

      Isolation Transformer, Primary Cable and Secondary Connector

      Isolation transformer

      Primary current produces magnetic flux, which produces secondary voltage for the fixture. Primary and secondary windings are electrically separate, so it isolates the fixture from the high-voltage series circuit.

      Primary cable

      Part of the series circuit: CCR to a transformer, on to the next transformer. Must suit operating voltage, series current, insulation, environment, circuit length and mechanical conditions.

      Secondary connector

      Connects the transformer secondary to the fixture. Must give reliable contact and suit the installation environment.

      Why the transformer is needed

      PurposeWhat it gives
      Electrical isolationSeparates the individual fixture from the series primary circuit
      Power transferMoves the required energy to the fixture
      Series-circuit continuityIn applicable AGL designs, the primary arrangement keeps the series circuit continuous even if an individual lamp circuit opens
      Environmental installationPurpose-designed for airport lighting, including underground or exposed installation depending on product and specification

      PAPI

      How PAPI Works

      A four-unit PAPI uses individually aimed light units, so the pilot sees different white and red combinations. It converts aircraft position into a visual indication through the geometry of its optical beams. Each unit is designed with separate red and white sectors, and the colour transition changes with the aircraft's elevation relative to the selected approach path.

      What the pilot seesAircraft position
      4 whiteHigh
      3 white + 1 redSlightly high
      2 white + 2 redOn the approach path
      1 white + 3 redSlightly low
      4 redToo low
      Important optical characteristics: intensity, colour, beam coverage, red/white transition, aiming angle and horizontal coverage. That is why PAPI testing needs both alignment and photometry.

      Control

      How ALCMS and Remote Control Work

      ALCMS

      The Airfield Lighting Control and Monitoring System gives centralized control and monitoring of lighting circuits. The operator selects a circuit or intensity state, and the field system returns status or alarm information.

      Feedback matters

      Status travels back so staff can see whether the commanded lighting state was actually achieved: field equipment, monitoring, ALCMS, operator.

      The control system sets the operating command. The CCR provides the electrical regulation that carries out the selected series-circuit condition.

      Resilience

      Low Visibility and Secondary Power

      Low-visibility operations

      Place extra weight on the reliability and availability of visual aids. Depending on runway and operational category, systems may include approach, edge, centreline and touchdown zone lighting, taxiway centreline lights, stop bars, PAPI, enhanced monitoring, redundant power and rapid or controlled power transfer. This is why AGL commissioning and maintenance are critical at major airports.

      Secondary power

      Standby arrangements keep critical visual aids available after loss of the normal supply, and can involve generators, UPS, automatic transfer equipment, independent feeders and dedicated AGL distribution. Transfer characteristics depend on operational and regulatory requirements.

      A problem anywhere shows up in the output

      Low CCR current can give low fixture output. Correct CCR current with dirty optics can also give low output. So test electrical and photometric performance together.

      How photometry works

      A calibrated photometer in controlled geometry measures the fixture, giving intensity, colour and distribution data. Compare with the applicable requirement, correct, and retest. It can reveal low intensity, poor beam distribution, incorrect alignment, colour issues and degradation.

      TestQuestion it answers
      Functional testDoes the light operate?
      Photometric testDoes the light provide the required optical performance?
      A light can be on, electrically healthy and photometrically deficient: dirty optics, damaged lens, degraded LED or lamp, wrong alignment or wrong colour. Commissioning and maintenance often need both.

      Runway Lighting Work Permit

      Work on runway lighting is normally carried out under a work permit issued by the airport. The permit sets out where the work is, what it covers and how long it will take, and the work is coordinated with airport operations and air traffic control before anyone goes airside.

      Because a series circuit can carry current even when the lights look off, the circuit should be made safe under the airport's own isolation procedure before work starts. After the work, the lighting is tested, retested and recorded before it is handed back for use. The exact permit and isolation requirements are set by the airport, so always follow its approved procedure.

      Why AGL is not normal building lighting

      AGL may involve long series circuits, controlled current, isolation transformers, specialized optical fixtures, airside installation, aircraft loading, frangibility, strict visual guidance requirements, multiple intensity steps, centralized control, monitoring and photometric verification. A runway lighting system is an integrated aviation visual-aid system, not a large outdoor installation.

      Final Engineering Perspective

      The aim is not to illuminate the airport. It is to give the pilot the correct visual information for approach, landing, taxiing and runway movement.

      Power controls the circuit. Optics provide the guidance. Control systems manage the operation. Testing proves the performance.

      FAQ

      How Airfield Lighting Works FAQ

      Q1How does airfield lighting work?

      Through electrical power, controlled series circuits, isolation transformers, light fixtures, optical systems and centralized control. In a typical series-circuit installation the CCR controls current, transformers feed individual fixtures and the fixtures produce the required visual guidance.

      Q2How does runway lighting work?

      Different visual-aid fixtures (edge, threshold, end, centreline, touchdown-zone and approach lights) give pilots visual guidance during approach and runway operations.

      Q3How does taxiway lighting work?

      Edge lights, centreline lights, stop bars, runway guard lights and associated signs guide aircraft during ground movement.

      Q4How does a CCR work?

      A CCR regulates the current in the airport series circuit. Its output voltage changes as needed according to the circuit's electrical load.

      Q5Why does airport lighting use a CCR?

      It lets many fixtures spread over a long series circuit operate at a controlled current and selectable intensity levels.

      Q6What does an isolation transformer do in airport lighting?

      It electrically isolates the individual fixture from the primary series circuit while transferring power to the fixture.

      Q7Why are airport lights connected through isolation transformers?

      They separate the fixture-side circuit from the airport's high-voltage series circuit and let each light receive the required secondary supply.

      Q8How does airport lighting brightness change?

      In a series-circuit AGL system, brightness is generally controlled by changing the CCR's output-current level.

      Q9How does ALCMS control airport lights?

      The ALCMS sends commands to lighting-control equipment such as CCR interfaces, which operate the appropriate circuit. It can also receive status and fault information from field equipment.

      Q10How does PAPI work?

      Individually aimed optical units give different red/white indications depending on the aircraft's position relative to the desired approach path.

      Q11How does airport lighting work during low visibility?

      Critical visual aids can be supported by dedicated circuits, intensity control, monitoring and secondary power, according to the airport's operational requirements.

      Q12What happens if an airport lighting circuit fails?

      It depends on the fault. One failed light may need fixture, transformer or connector troubleshooting, while multiple failures may need investigation of the CCR or primary series circuit.

      Q13Why does an airport light stay on but look dim?

      Possible causes are optical contamination, light-source degradation, incorrect current, electrical problems or optical misalignment.

      Q14What is the difference between functional and photometric testing?

      Functional testing confirms that equipment operates. Photometric testing quantitatively evaluates optical performance such as intensity, distribution and colour where required.

      Q15Why is AGL photometry important?

      It gives measured evidence of light performance and can find degradation or alignment problems that visual inspection alone may miss.

      Q16How is airfield lighting maintained?

      Visual inspection, cleaning, electrical testing, component inspection, photometry where required, troubleshooting, corrective action and retesting.

      Q17What are the main AGL components?

      CCRs, primary cables, isolation transformers, connectors, light fixtures, light bases, PAPI, signs, control systems and monitoring systems.

      Q18Is airport lighting connected directly to the airport power supply?

      Usually the airport supply feeds the AGL electrical system, often through a CCR for series-circuit lighting. The detailed architecture depends on the airport design.

      Q19Is airport lighting AC or DC?

      It depends on the equipment and system. Many conventional series-circuit systems use AC-powered CCRs and isolation transformers, while specialized systems can differ.

      Q20Can LED lights be used in an AGL series circuit?

      Yes, where the LED fixture is designed and approved for the specific AGL system. Verify compatibility with the CCR, transformer and circuit characteristics.
