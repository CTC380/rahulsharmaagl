Runway Lighting Installation, Alignment & Verification SOPs

Controlled procedures for runway edge, centreline, threshold, threshold wing-bar, runway-end and touchdown-zone lighting — fixture installation, height and optical alignment, secondary connection, fixture and lamp/LED module replacement, prism and lens cleaning, and mechanical, electrical, optical and operational verification of runway light fixtures. Applicable SOP range: SOP-165 to SOP-184.

SOP-165 Runway Edge Light Installation
Purpose

To ensure runway edge lights are installed at the approved locations, elevations and orientations and provide the required visual guidance.

Pre-Installation Checks

Verify:

  • Approved runway lighting layout.
  • Fixture type.
  • Fixture location.
  • Circuit identification.
  • Base can condition.
  • Isolating transformer.
  • Secondary cable.
  • Frangible coupling.
  • Mounting hardware.
  • Fixture elevation.
  • Orientation.
  • Required color/optical configuration.
Procedure
  1. Identify the approved fixture location.
  2. Verify base can and foundation condition.
  3. Inspect fixture for damage.
  4. Verify transformer and secondary connection.
  5. Install frangible coupling/mounting arrangement.
  6. Install fixture.
  7. Maintain specified mounting height.
  8. Align fixture with runway geometry.
  9. Apply specified torque to mounting hardware.
  10. Connect secondary lead.
  11. Verify connector condition.
  12. Conduct visual inspection.
  13. Perform operational verification.
Acceptance

The fixture shall be:

  • Correctly located.
  • Correctly oriented.
  • Correctly elevated.
  • Mechanically secure.
  • Properly connected.
  • Free of optical contamination.
  • Operational.

Division-Wide Requirements

The master installation workflow, quality hold points, common failure modes, the master inspection checklist and the golden rule that governs all 20 Division 08 procedures.

Runway Light Installation Master Workflow

For runway edge, centreline, threshold, wing-bar, runway-end and touchdown-zone fixtures:

APPROVED DRAWING LOCATION VERIFICATION BASE/CAN INSPECTION TRANSFORMER & SECONDARY CABLE VERIFICATION FIXTURE INSPECTION MOUNTING / INSTALLATION HEIGHT VERIFICATION ALIGNMENT VERIFICATION CONNECTOR CONNECTION TORQUE VERIFICATION OPTICAL INSPECTION MECHANICAL INSPECTION ELECTRICAL INSPECTION FOD INSPECTION CONTROLLED ENERGIZATION OPERATIONAL VERIFICATION PHOTOMETRIC VERIFICATION WHERE REQUIRED DOCUMENTATION & RELEASE

Runway Light Quality Hold Points

  • HP-01 — Location — Verify fixture location against approved runway lighting drawings.
  • HP-02 — Base/Can — Verify foundation/base condition before fixture installation.
  • HP-03 — Fixture — Verify correct model, optical configuration and physical condition.
  • HP-04 — Height — Verify elevation before final release.
  • HP-05 — Alignment — Verify optical/runway alignment.
  • HP-06 — Connection — Verify transformer, secondary cable and connector.
  • HP-07 — Torque — Verify mounting hardware torque.
  • HP-08 — Optical Condition — Verify prism/lens/LED/lamp condition.
  • HP-09 — FOD — Complete FOD inspection before operational testing.
  • HP-10 — Operational Test — Verify correct operation at the required control/intensity settings.
  • HP-11 — Photometry — Where required, perform formal photometric verification using the approved measurement procedure.
  • HP-12 — Final Release — Release the runway lighting system only after all applicable defects are closed.

Common Failure Modes

  • 1. Misalignment — A physically working fixture can still provide inadequate visual guidance if its optical system is incorrectly aligned.
  • 2. Incorrect Height — Uneven elevated fixtures can affect the visual appearance of the runway edge lighting system and may create operational concerns.
  • 3. Contaminated Prism — A dirty optical surface can reduce useful light output.
  • 4. Damaged Prism — Cracks, chips or severe scratches can alter optical performance.
  • 5. Loose Mounting — Mechanical movement can change fixture alignment.
  • 6. Connector Failure — Poor secondary connections can produce intermittent or failed fixtures.
  • 7. Water Ingress — Moisture can cause corrosion, insulation deterioration and electrical failure.
  • 8. Incorrect Fixture — Installing an incorrect optical configuration can produce an apparently functional but technically non-compliant lighting system.
  • 9. Incorrect Torque — Under-torquing can permit movement; over-torquing can damage components.
  • 10. Incorrect Operational Verification — Checking only whether the lamp/LED turns ON is insufficient. Correct operation also requires checking color, intensity behavior, optical direction, stability and system response as applicable.

Quick Reference

A short answer on installation depth, taken from the Division 08 procedures. Confirm all values against the approved drawing and the manufacturer's instructions.

Runway light installation depth

Runway light installation depth is the depth at which the base can and fixture mounting sit below the finished surface, so that the fixture ends up at the approved elevation. The depth is set by the approved design and the fixture manufacturer, not by a generic figure, and this division gives no universal value. In practice it is controlled through the checks in SOP-165 and SOP-167: verify the base can and foundation condition before fitting, install the frangible coupling and fixture, maintain the specified mounting height, and then measure the finished height against the project tolerance (HP-02 Base/Can and HP-04 Height). A fixture installed too deep or too shallow shows up as an uneven height against its neighbours and is corrected before final release.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Runway Lighting SOPs in the AGL SOP Library.

Q1What is Division 08 — Runway Lighting SOPs?

Division 08 is a set of 20 procedures (SOP-165 to SOP-184, Rev 0) for runway edge, centreline, threshold, threshold wing-bar, runway-end and touchdown-zone lighting. It covers fixture installation, height and optical alignment, secondary connection, fixture and lamp or LED module replacement, prism and lens cleaning, and mechanical, electrical, optical and operational verification.

Q2Is a runway light satisfactory just because it illuminates?

No. The Golden Rule says correct location, elevation, alignment, optical performance, color, intensity, electrical connection, mechanical security and operational reliability must all be verified against the approved aerodrome lighting requirements.

Q3What is the runway light installation workflow?

Approved drawing, location verification, base/can inspection, transformer and secondary cable verification, fixture inspection, mounting, then height, alignment, connector and torque verification. Then optical, mechanical and electrical inspection, FOD inspection, controlled energization, operational verification, photometric verification where required, and documentation and release.

Q4What are the quality hold points for runway lights?

HP-01 Location, HP-02 Base/Can, HP-03 Fixture, HP-04 Height, HP-05 Alignment, HP-06 Connection, HP-07 Torque, HP-08 Optical Condition, then FOD, Operational Test, Photometry and Final Release.

Q5How is runway edge light height verified?

SOP-167 requires verifying the approved height, using calibrated measuring equipment and a reference surface, measuring the fixture, comparing with project tolerance, checking neighboring fixtures, correcting deviations and recording measurements. Inspection looks for uneven elevations, settlement, foundation movement, incorrect hardware and damaged couplings.

Q6What is the rule for replacing lamps?

Never install a lamp with an incorrect rating or optical specification (SOP-178). Confirm the lamp type, isolate the circuit, verify a safe condition, allow the lamp and fixture to cool, replace per manufacturer instructions, check the optical assembly and sealing and perform an operational test.

Q7Can a generic torque value be used for runway fixtures?

No. Never use a generic torque value when the manufacturer specifies a different one (SOP-183). Use a calibrated torque wrench, do not exceed the specified torque and record verification where required.

Q8What is checked during operational verification of runway lighting?

After confirming the work area, personnel and tools are clear, FOD inspection is done and permission to energize is obtained, the circuit is energized through the approved control system at the required intensity step. The team checks ON/OFF condition, color, intensity behavior, flickering, intermittent operation, abnormal brightness and optical direction (SOP-184).

Q9What is the runway light installation depth?

Runway light installation depth is how deep the base can and fixture mounting sit so that the fixture reaches the approved elevation. It comes from the approved design and the manufacturer, not a generic figure. It is checked through base/can verification and then finished-height measurement against project tolerance (SOP-165, SOP-167).
