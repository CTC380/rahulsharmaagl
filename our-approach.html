A Systems Approach to Airfield Ground Lighting

Airfield Ground Lighting is not a collection of independent products. Precision AGL Solutions combines technical understanding, structured procedures, testing, documentation, knowledge development and continuous improvement into one connected approach.

The Core Principle

Understand the Complete System Before Solving the Individual Problem

A runway light depends on its circuit.
A circuit depends on its CCR, cables and isolating transformers.
A PAPI depends on correct siting, alignment and verification.
A testing result depends on the right procedure and accurate measurement.
A maintenance decision depends on inspection, failure history and documentation.

Our Approach to Standards

ICAO Approach Lighting System

The approach lighting system is a good example of how we treat a standard. ICAO Annex 14 is the starting point for the approach lighting required for a runway, and the configuration that applies depends on the runway type, the operational category and the airport's approved design.

We then move from that requirement to its engineering meaning, covering layout, series circuits and CCR loading, and on to installation, testing and documentation. Always confirm the requirement against the current edition of Annex 14. Approach Lighting Systems →

Approach Lighting System Diagram

The same chain applied to approach lighting runs in this order:

  1. Requirement from the approved approach lighting configuration
  2. Layout drawing, with elevation, azimuth and aiming of the fixtures
  3. Series circuit and CCR arrangement
  4. Installation, then electrical and photometric testing
  5. Test report and documentation

Each step is covered on the Approach Lighting Systems page.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is the Precision AGL approach to Airfield Ground Lighting?

A systems approach. Its core principle is to understand the complete system before solving the individual problem, because a runway light depends on its circuit, the circuit on its CCR, cables and isolating transformers, and a maintenance decision on inspection, failure history and documentation.

Q2What is the 5-step working model?

Understand, Assess, Engineer, Verify, Deliver. Understand covers project objective, airport environment and requirements. Assess covers system review, site inspection, testing and compliance review. Engineer covers architecture, layouts, equipment and circuit planning. Verify covers inspection, electrical testing, photometry and compliance checks. Deliver covers reports, records, documentation, SOPs, checklists and training material.

Q3What is the AGL system chain?

Light Fitting, Isolating Transformer, Series Circuit, CCR, Control & Monitoring, and Airport Operations. The approach looks at how each component fits into the complete airport lighting system rather than in isolation.

Q4What does a knowledge-first approach mean?

A solution to one problem can become reusable knowledge instead of staying in one document. It can become a procedure, checklist, training lesson, troubleshooting case, technical article, failure record, component reference, standards reference or future improvement.

Q5How are resources connected around one topic?

The page uses the isolating transformer as an example. It connects to AGL Fundamentals, the Component Library, Standards Companion, Engineering Resources, SOP Library, Testing & Commissioning Manual, Inspection Handbook, Maintenance Handbook, Troubleshooting Atlas, Failure Database, Case Studies and the Quiz Center.

Q6How does the approach treat standards?

Standards are a starting point, not the entire answer. The approach moves from requirement to engineering meaning, then implementation, testing and documentation. The Standards Companion connects ICAO, FAA, IS and related requirements with practical engineering interpretation.

Q7How does the approach treat testing and photometry?

Testing is not treated as an isolated end-of-project activity. It supports design verification, equipment evaluation, commissioning, maintenance, fault diagnosis, performance monitoring and compliance documentation. Photometry connects measurement and calibration, beam alignment, intensity and chromaticity, data analysis, pass/fail evaluation and technical reporting.
