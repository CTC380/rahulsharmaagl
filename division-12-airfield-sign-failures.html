Initial Issue

Key principle — A sign being electrically energized does not necessarily mean that it is operationally satisfactory. A sign may remain ON while suffering from uneven illumination, low brightness, incorrect colour, message damage, optical degradation or structural misalignment. Every record should distinguish cause, component failure, performance failure and service-level failure, so the database does not record only the final symptom.

ASF-01 LED Module Failure
Failure Definition

Failure of one or more LED modules used to illuminate the airfield sign.

Possible Causes
  • LED degradation
  • Electrical stress
  • Thermal stress
  • Ageing
  • Moisture
  • Driver failure
Possible Consequences
  • Dark areas
  • Partial sign outage
  • Uneven illumination
  • Reduced brightness
  • Complete sign outage
Investigation

Check:

  • LED module condition
  • Number of failed modules
  • Illumination uniformity
  • Driver output
  • Electrical connections
  • Thermal condition
ASF-02 LED Driver Failure
Failure Definition

Failure of the electronic driver supplying or controlling the LED modules.

Possible Symptoms
  • Complete loss of illumination
  • Partial illumination
  • Flickering
  • Uneven brightness
  • Intermittent operation
Investigation

Trace: Power supply → driver → LED modules → optical output.

ASF-03 Complete Sign Outage
Failure Definition

Complete loss of required illumination of the airfield sign.

Possible Causes
  • Power-supply failure
  • Driver failure
  • Internal wiring failure
  • Connector failure
  • Multiple LED-module failure
  • External circuit failure
Investigation

Check the complete electrical path: Supply → protection → wiring → driver → LED modules.

Important Distinction

"Complete sign outage" is the service-level failure, while the underlying cause should be recorded separately.

ASF-04 Partial Sign Outage
Failure Definition

Loss of illumination from part of an otherwise functioning sign.

Possible Causes
  • LED module failure
  • Driver failure
  • Internal wiring failure
  • Connector problem
  • Local electrical fault
Possible Consequences
  • Reduced legibility
  • Uneven illumination
  • Reduced operational effectiveness
Investigation

Identify:

  • Affected section
  • Number of failed modules
  • Remaining illumination
  • Electrical circuit arrangement
ASF-05 Uneven Illumination
Failure Definition

Illumination across the sign face is visibly or measurably non-uniform.

Possible Causes
  • LED module failure
  • LED ageing
  • Driver degradation
  • Optical contamination
  • Internal wiring issue
  • Component deterioration
Possible Consequences
  • Reduced readability
  • Poor visual uniformity
  • Reduced operational effectiveness
Investigation

Compare: Illuminated area → dark/bright areas → module condition → driver condition → optical condition.

ASF-06 Low Brightness
Failure Definition

Sign illumination is present but brightness is below the expected/required level.

Possible Causes
  • LED degradation
  • Driver degradation
  • Power-supply problem
  • Optical contamination
  • Thermal effects
  • Ageing
Important Distinction

Complete outage: no required illumination. Partial outage: some illumination areas unavailable. Low brightness: illumination remains but is insufficient. Uneven illumination: brightness distribution is inconsistent. These should remain separate failure categories.

Investigation

C. Classification Groups

The 20 topics are organised into four logical groups while retaining the original numbering:

  • A — Illumination & Electrical Failures (Topics 1–10)
  • B — Environmental & Enclosure Failures (Topics 11–14)
  • C — Structural & Mechanical Failures (Topics 15–18)
  • D — Thermal & Impact Failures (Topics 19–20)

This gives Division 12 a complete airfield-sign reliability structure — not only whether the sign is ON/OFF, but whether it is properly illuminated, bright, uniform, correctly coloured, physically intact, correctly aligned and capable of conveying the intended operational information.

D. Failure Severity

Every failure receives a severity classification, plus operational-impact tagging:

Critical / Safety-Critical — immediate operational or safety risk

Major / Operationally Critical — significant availability impact

Moderate — reduced performance, not yet critical

Minor / Non-Critical — limited or no operational effect

Degradation / Intermittent / Latent — developing or hidden fault

F. Classification Principle — Cause, Component, Performance, Service-Level

Division 12 distinguishes between cause, component failure, performance failure and service-level failure so the database does not record only the final symptom. Example: vehicle impact (cause) → housing damage + mounting failure (physical failure) → sign misalignment (position failure) → reduced visibility (functional consequence) → airfield sign unavailable/ineffective (operational consequence). A sign that is electrically energized is not necessarily operationally satisfactory — uneven illumination, low brightness, incorrect colour, message damage, optical degradation and structural misalignment can all coexist with a sign that appears "ON".

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about illuminated airfield sign failures, as covered in Division 12 of the AGL Failure Database.

Q1What is Division 12 — Airfield Sign Failures in the AGL Failure Database?

Division 12 covers failures of illuminated airfield signs. It contains 20 published topics (ASF-01 to ASF-20) spanning LED module and driver failure, sign outage, illumination and colour performance, enclosure and environmental condition, structural condition, message integrity and vehicle-impact damage.

Q2Which airfield sign failures does Division 12 cover?

LED module failure, LED driver failure, complete and partial sign outage, uneven illumination, low brightness, incorrect colour, internal wiring failure, connector failure, power-supply failure, moisture ingress, seal failure, housing damage, structural damage, mounting failure, sign misalignment, message/component damage, corrosion, overheating and vehicle-impact damage.

Q3How are the 20 airfield sign topics grouped?

They fall into four groups while keeping the original numbering: Illumination & Electrical Failures (10 topics), Environmental & Enclosure Failures (4), Structural & Mechanical Failures (4) and Thermal & Impact Failures (2).

Q4Is an energised sign necessarily satisfactory?

No. A sign that is electrically energised is not necessarily operationally satisfactory. Uneven illumination, low brightness, incorrect colour, message damage, optical degradation and structural misalignment can all coexist with a sign that appears to be ON.

Q5What four levels does Division 12 use to classify a failure?

Division 12 distinguishes cause, component failure, performance failure and service-level failure so the database does not record only the final symptom. For example, a vehicle impact (cause) leads to housing damage and mounting failure (physical failure), then sign misalignment (position failure), reduced visibility (functional consequence) and an airfield sign that is unavailable or ineffective (operational consequence).

Q6What airfield sign failure chains does Division 12 capture?

In the LED chain, LED module failure leads to partial sign outage, uneven illumination, reduced readability and operational impact. In the driver chain, LED driver failure leads to LED output loss, complete sign outage and an unavailable sign. Moisture chains starting from seal failure are also captured.

Q7What should be recorded for an airfield sign failure?

Records cover identification (failure ID, airport, location, sign identification and type, date/time, circuit or asset reference), failure category and mode, LED, driver and power-supply condition, optical and functional condition, environmental and physical condition, RCA, corrective action, verification and reliability.

Q8How are failure severity and operational impact classified?

Every failure receives a severity classification, plus operational-impact tagging. The classes are Critical / Safety-Critical (immediate operational or safety risk), Major / Operationally Critical (significant availability impact), Moderate (reduced performance, not yet critical), Minor / Non-Critical (limited or no operational effect), and Degradation / Intermittent / Latent (a developing or hidden fault).
