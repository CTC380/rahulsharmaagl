Appearance Is Not Compatibility

Red / White Optical Characteristics

Replacement Parts Must Preserve the Configuration

How the Indication Works

The PAPI indication depends on the relationship between the white and red portions of the emitted light. The assembly is designed so the observer sees different colour indications according to the aircraft's position relative to the visual approach path.

Changes That May Affect Distribution or Transition

  • Filter characteristics
  • Lens geometry
  • Lamp position
  • Optical alignment
  • Reflector position
  • Optical component thickness

Select components against the applicable PAPI equipment documentation. ICAO Annex 14 requires the red-to-white transition, seen from not less than 300 m, to occur within an angle of not more than three minutes of arc. A wrong filter, lens or lamp position can widen it.

Replacement Procedure

A Typical Optical-Component Replacement Process

  1. Identify the PAPI unit — manufacturer, model, serial
  2. Identify the component — all lamp, filter or lens markings
  3. Review documentation — drawings, manuals, spare-parts lists
  4. Confirm compatibility — against the original requirements
  5. Isolate the equipment — de-energise the circuit at the regulator under lock-out / tag-out, and never open an energised series circuit (Style B / current-powered units); for voltage-powered (Style A) units, isolate the supply feeding the unit; also per airfield electrical and maintenance safety procedure
  6. Remove the existing component — without damaging adjacent parts
  7. Inspect the assembly — holders, seals, reflectors, wiring
  8. Install the replacement — specified orientation and position
  9. Clean and secure — clean optical surfaces, retaining parts fitted
  10. Functional verification — per maintenance / commissioning procedure
  11. Verify alignment where required — if beam geometry or aiming may be affected, confirm the setting angles and the red/white transition before return to service, and arrange a flight check where the authority requires it
  12. Record the replacement — component, equipment ID and activity
Replacement Considerations

Lamps, Filters and Lenses

Lamp

Treat as an optical maintenance activity. Where a unit uses more than one lamp, replace them as a matched set. Where the unit is LED-based, the light engine may be replaced as a module instead of a lamp. Potential issues:

  • Incorrect voltage, wattage or base
  • Incorrect dimensions
  • Incorrect light-source position
  • Incorrect optical output
  • Incompatible lamp technology
  • Poor seating in the holder
  • Contamination of optical surfaces

The manufacturer's approved procedure takes precedence over a generic method.

Filter

Handle carefully to prevent:

  • Scratches
  • Finger contamination
  • Incorrect orientation
  • Improper seating
  • Optical distortion
  • Damage to retaining components

Install in the defined orientation or mounting direction.

Lens

Confirm both mechanical and optical compatibility. Before installation, inspect:

  • Lens surface
  • Retaining ring and gasket
  • Mounting seat
  • Adjacent optical components
  • Fasteners and sealing surfaces

After installation, verify correct seating with no mechanical stress.

FAQ

PAPI Lamp, Filter & Lens Spares — Frequently Asked Questions

What PAPI optical spares are available?

Replaceable components may include lamps or light sources, optical and coloured filters, lenses and front optical elements, reflectors, lamp holders, optical retaining components, seals and gaskets, and optical mounting hardware. Availability depends on the PAPI manufacturer, model, generation and optical design.

Can I use a lamp with the same electrical ratings as the original?

Not necessarily. A lamp with similar electrical ratings is not acceptable if its physical light-source position or optical characteristics differ from the original specification. Consider lamp technology, rated voltage, power and current, base or socket type, dimensions, filament or light-source position, luminous and colour characteristics, and manufacturer and PAPI model compatibility.

Is a filter that looks the same acceptable as a replacement?

No. A visually similar filter is not automatically acceptable, because depending on the PAPI design the filters establish or control the intended optical characteristics of the beam. Selection should consider filter type, optical transmission, colour characteristics, dimensions and thickness, mounting orientation and the PAPI model.

Why do replacement optical parts affect the red/white transition?

The indication depends on the relationship between the white and red portions of the emitted light, so filter characteristics, lens geometry, lamp position, optical alignment, reflector position and component thickness can all affect distribution or transition. ICAO Annex 14 requires the red-to-white transition, seen from not less than 300 m, to occur within an angle of not more than three minutes of arc, and a wrong filter, lens or lamp position can widen it.

What information is needed to identify the correct replacement part?

Provide the PAPI manufacturer, model and serial number, revision or generation, and the lamp, filter and lens part numbers, voltage and wattage, base type, colour designation, dimensions and markings. Photographs of the nameplate and optical assembly, drawings, the spare-parts list or maintenance manual and previous purchase references also help, and for older or unidentified equipment nameplate and optical-assembly photographs are particularly useful.

Are PAPI optical components interchangeable between manufacturers or models?

Not necessarily. Compatibility should be established at equipment level, not component level, especially for older systems where parts from different generations can look similar but differ optically or electrically. A replacement should not be represented as compliant solely because it physically fits, and optical parts not specified by the manufacturer can invalidate the equipment's type certification (for FAA-listed PAPI, see AC 150/5345-53D).

How should the circuit be isolated before replacing a PAPI lamp, filter or lens?

De-energise the circuit at the regulator under lock-out / tag-out, and never open an energised series circuit (Style B, current-powered units). For voltage-powered (Style A) units, isolate the supply feeding the unit. Also follow the airfield electrical and maintenance safety procedure, and the manufacturer's approved procedure takes precedence over a generic method.

Does a PAPI need re-alignment after replacing an optical component?

It can, depending on the design and procedure. If beam geometry or aiming may be affected, confirm the setting angles and the red/white transition before return to service, follow the manufacturer's post-maintenance alignment-verification requirements, and arrange a flight check where the authority requires it.

Should PAPI lamps be replaced individually?

Where a unit uses more than one lamp, replace them as a matched set. Where the unit is LED-based, the light engine may be replaced as a module instead of a lamp. Check for the correct voltage, wattage and base, dimensions, light-source position, optical output and lamp technology, and seat the lamp properly in the holder.

What are the common problems with replacement parts?

Typical problems are a lamp that does not fit (incorrect base, model, equipment generation or dimensions), a lamp that fits but gives incorrect optical performance, a filter with correct dimensions but different appearance, and a lens that fits mechanically but does not match the optical assembly. Identify the fault before substituting parts by comparing the symptom with the manufacturer's troubleshooting procedure, rather than replacing components first.

Which PAPI optical spares should be stocked?

Stocking depends on the number of installed PAPI units, equipment model and age, lamp replacement frequency, availability of parts, operational requirements, manufacturer lead times and the planned maintenance schedule. Potential stock items are replacement lamps, filters and lenses, lamp holders, seals, optical retaining components and other model-specific service parts. Keep identification labels so model-specific parts are not mixed, and protect optical surfaces from impact, scratching and contamination in storage.

Engineering Note
