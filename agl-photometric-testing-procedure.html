AGL Photometric Testing Procedure Runway, Taxiway & PAPI

The practical field procedure: preparation, equipment set-up, measurement, evaluation, rectification, retesting and the final report, for runway, taxiway and PAPI lighting.

Overview

What Is AGL Photometric Testing?

AGL photometric testing measures the actual optical output of installed airport lighting and compares it with the applicable design, regulatory and project criteria. It is used after installation, during commissioning, periodic inspection and maintenance. A fitting can be electrically healthy, mechanically intact and lit during a visual inspection, and still fail photometrically through low intensity, wrong beam direction, inadequate coverage, wrong colour, abnormal distribution, incorrect aiming or inconsistency with adjacent fittings.

A functional test asks

Does the light operate?

A photometric test asks

Does the light produce the required optical output in the required direction and configuration?

The 4Cs in AAI specifications

Configuration

Does the installed visual-aid arrangement match the required configuration?

Colour

Does the light have the required colour?

Candela

Measured luminous intensity.

Coverage

Is sufficient light provided over the specified field of view?

AAI procurement documents show in-field AGL testing can include intensity, beam spread, orientation, the 4Cs and chromaticity, plus field alignment and reporting. They also distinguish vehicle-mounted mobile testing from individual fitting measurement with portable or frame-based equipment.

Photometry is required for

  • New installation and commissioning
  • Periodic inspection and preventive maintenance
  • Corrective maintenance (confirming a repair)
  • LED conversion baselines
  • Airport rehabilitation
  • Regulatory or airport inspection evidence

Five principles of a good programme

  • Correct equipment for the fitting type
  • Correct geometry per the approved method
  • Correct operating condition (current and intensity step)
  • Traceable equipment and results
  • Actionable reporting: which fittings passed, failed or need investigation
Equipment

Equipment, Calibration and Conditions

Photometer and sensor

An airport photometer or AGL field measurement system suited to the light category, with a calibrated sensor of suitable spectral and photometric characteristics.

Positioning and frame

Equipment to establish test location, angular position, sensor height, alignment, distance and orientation, plus a measurement frame for individual fittings.

Data and software

Data acquisition and software for photometric curves, intensity, aiming, pass/fail analysis, asset identification and reporting.

MethodBest for
Vehicle-mountedLong runways, extensive taxiways, large fitting populations, systematic surveys and high-volume periodic inspection
Handheld or frame-basedPAPI, individual lights, isolated defects, detailed investigation, post-maintenance retesting, workshop measurements
AAI's portable-equipment specification describes a standalone system for photometric and chromatic measurement of AGL and PAPI, with photometric diagrams, mean intensity and aiming angles, for field and laboratory use.

Verify before a formal survey

  • Instrument ID, serial number, calibration certificate and dates
  • Sensor configuration and software version
  • Condition of sensor lens, cables, connectors, frame, mounting hardware, battery, logger, communication links and vehicle installation

Record conditions the method requires

  • Date and time, temperature, weather
  • Visibility and precipitation
  • Pavement condition, ambient lighting, wind where relevant
Do not begin a formal acceptance survey with equipment whose calibration or verification status is unclear.

Runway

Runway Photometry Procedure

Divide the runway into identifiable lighting groups and set the test route before measuring. The detailed test matrix follows the runway category and project specification.

01

Review runway data

Designation, layout, lighting type, fitting count, circuit identification and interleaving, locations, brightness levels, previous data. Create or verify the asset list.

02

Inspect the lighting

Missing fixtures, damaged or cracked lenses, contamination, wrong orientation, damaged housings, displaced elevated fittings, water ingress, damaged mountings.

03

Establish the measurement reference

Reference the system to runway geometry: centreline, longitudinal station, fixture location, test-track position, sensor orientation. A positioning error can look like an optical failure.

04

Set the lighting circuit

Energize at the specified operating condition; record test intensity, CCR setting and circuit condition.

05

Align the measurement system

Verify vehicle position, sensor height and orientation, frame geometry and reference direction. Start collecting only when the set-up is stable.

06

Measure each fitting or segment

Intensity, angular distribution, aiming, colour, chromaticity, coverage. Vehicle systems tie data to runway position; portable testing needs a unique ID per fitting.

07

Check abnormal measurements

Verify the set-up, repeat the reading, inspect the fitting, check the circuit, compare with neighbours, then consider electrical and mechanical causes. Do not classify an isolated odd reading as failure before confirming it.

By lighting type

Edge: output, colour, orientation, coverage. Threshold: directional performance and colour. Runway end: output, colour, orientation. Centreline: intensity, colour, configuration. Touchdown zone: intensity and direction where installed. Approach: the applicable optical and configuration parameters.

Circuit interleaving and LED

On redundant systems, test circuit A and B in normal condition and, where required, with each unavailable, to see whether the remaining pattern keeps the required characteristics. For LED fittings, measure rather than compare by eye: replacements, conversions, brightness stages, colour consistency and unit-to-unit variation.

The measurement grid and acceptance criteria come from the governing specification. FAA research on LED airfield lighting shows the value of photometric measurement for intensity and colour.

Taxiway

Taxiway Photometry Procedure

Same principle, adapted to taxiway geometry and the visual aid: edge lights, centreline lights, stop bars and runway guard lights.

01

Review the taxiway layout

Taxiway name, light type, circuit, directionality, sequence, intersections, stop bars, special lighting.

02

Visual inspection

Fixture damage, contamination, orientation, elevation, pavement around inset fittings, missing units.

03

Establish the test route

Define the route first on large networks to avoid missed fittings, duplicates and wrong asset IDs.

04

Energize the circuit

Set the required state. For dynamic systems such as controlled centreline segments, record which segments are commanded on.

05

Measure

Record the required photometric data.

06

Investigate abnormal units

Sensor set-up, fitting condition, aiming, connector, transformer, circuit condition.

Centreline lights

Verify optical output, colour, orientation, configuration and consistency. Where they support a Follow the Greens or A-SMGCS function, commissioning must also verify that the correct segment responds to the correct command. That control test is separate from photometry.

Stop bars and runway guard lights

Stop bars need two elements: an optical test (does the light give the required output?) and a functional or control test (does the right stop bar switch correctly?). A photometer cannot verify interlocking logic. Guard lights: operation, output, colour, orientation, and sequence or configuration where applicable.

Advanced taxiway guidance needs three different tests

TestQuestion
PhotometricDoes the green centreline fitting give the required optical output?
ControlDoes the correct green segment respond to the route command?
OperationalDoes the route indication behave correctly for the aircraft movement?

PAPI

PAPI Photometry Procedure

PAPI output is deliberately split into precise white and red elevation sectors, so the survey combines physical inspection, aiming verification, photometry, colour measurement and functional testing.

01

Review PAPI information

Runway, PAPI position, unit IDs, design approach angle, approved elevations, MEHT/TCH where relevant, equipment type, previous measurements. AAI eAIP publishes position, angle and MEHT per runway direction: these are runway-specific design data, not generic equipment values.

02

Inspect physically

Lenses, optical surfaces, housing, mounting, foundation, drainage, connectors, transformer, alignment, surrounding vegetation or obstructions.

03

Verify horizontal alignment

All units correctly related to the runway reference.

04

Verify elevation and aiming

Measure each unit independently and confirm the optical axes match the approved aiming. ICAO material includes procedures for measuring PAPI settings and addresses UAS as an alternative method.

05

Energize the PAPI

Operate under the prescribed test condition.

06

Measure optical performance

Per unit: intensity, colour, angular characteristics, white/red transition, beam alignment, unit-to-unit consistency.

07

Compare the four units

Evaluate as a system too. A unit that looks normal can still be inconsistent with the other three.

Geometry

Control distance, elevation reference, azimuth, sensor alignment, unit reference and measurement position. A geometry error can mimic an aiming or intensity error on a healthy fitting.

Intensity

Compare against the applicable PAPI photometric requirement. Never apply a generic threshold without identifying the governing standard, equipment specification and operating condition. Low output can come from dirty lenses, optical deterioration, lamp or LED degradation, damaged reflector, electronic failure, wrong current, internal failure or a measurement geometry error: separate measurement error from equipment failure.

White/red transition

Shows whether the transition sits at the intended angle. Abnormal transition can come from incorrect aiming, optical damage, internal assembly movement, a filter or optical element problem, or mechanical displacement.

Reporting

Record Unit 1 to Unit 4 individually and give a system-level interpretation.

Colour and Alignment

Colour, Chromaticity, Beam Alignment and Coverage

Colour versus chromaticity

Colour is the perceived or defined colour characteristic. Chromaticity specifies colour quantitatively in a defined colour space. Objective measurement matters most for formal acceptance. Changes can come from damaged filters, ageing optics, wrong replacement components, contamination, LED degradation or internal optical damage, so investigate rather than treating it as cosmetic.

Alignment and coverage

Alignment decides whether the useful output goes into the right angular region. A misaimed fitting can give reduced effective intensity, poor coverage, inconsistent guidance, glare in the wrong direction and non-uniform appearance. Coverage asks whether the light reaches the required region: peak intensity does not equal complete photometric compliance.

Elevated fittings

Alignment can shift through support movement, loose fasteners, impact, foundation movement or maintenance work. A light can pass an electrical test and fail alignment.

Inset fittings

Incorrect installation, pavement movement, displaced fixtures, damaged mounting or poor seating can leave the optics working but misoriented.

Acceptance

Photometric Acceptance

Acceptance must rest on the correct governing criteria: ICAO requirements, DGCA requirements, AAI specifications, approved project specifications, manufacturer requirements, and FAA criteria only where the project explicitly invokes them. Do not present FAA criteria as ICAO or DGCA requirements.

Pass

The relevant measured parameters comply with the applicable criteria.

Fail

Intensity, colour or chromaticity, coverage, aiming, configuration, distribution or another specified parameter is outside criteria.

Investigation required

Abnormal results needing measurement verification or engineering assessment before final classification.

No single intensity value applies to every AGL fitting: it varies with light type, colour, direction, beam, runway or taxiway function, operating condition, brightness setting and standard. State that measured values are compared with the applicable criteria for the specific system and project. For LED fittings, report input condition, brightness stage, measured intensity, colour, alignment and result to create a baseline.

      Project Types

      Commissioning, Periodic Inspection and Maintenance

      New commissioning

      Material inspection, installation inspection, electrical testing, functional testing, photometric testing, defect correction, retesting, final acceptance. This stops optical acceptance before basic installation and electrical problems are resolved.

      Periodic inspection

      Review the previous report and current asset inventory, visual inspection, photometric survey, trend comparison, defect classification, maintenance, retest.

      Trend analysis

      A history reveals progressive deterioration before failure. Illustrative only: baseline 100%, then 95%, 91%, 86% over three years, then maintenance required. The value is the ability to spot decline, not the particular numbers.

      Condition-based maintenance

      Instead of replacing by age, measure, evaluate, prioritize: clean, align, repair, replace a component or replace the fitting. Link results to maintenance records and a digital asset database (asset ID, location, type, manufacturer, circuit, photometric, failure and maintenance history).

      Integration

      Photometry and the Rest of the AGL System

      Electrical testing

      Run electrical, functional, then photometric tests. A dim fitting leads to transformer, secondary cable, connector, fitting, optics.

      CCR and operating condition

      Record the CCR and circuit condition. A failure at one intensity level may not read the same at another.

      Redundancy

      Test normal configuration and, where required, a defined failure configuration to show the remaining system keeps required guidance.

      Emergency power

      Normal power, power failure, UPS or DG transition, AGL stabilization, photometric verification.

      Lightning events

      After a significant event: visual inspection, electrical testing, surge investigation, functional test, photometry.

      A-SMGCS / Follow the Greens

      Photometry verifies the physical lighting; control and operational tests are separate.

      Asset management

      Link results to an asset database: asset ID, location, fitting type, manufacturer, installation date, circuit, and photometric, failure and maintenance history. This is the foundation for predictive maintenance.

      Common Errors, Safety and Quality

      1

      Incorrect measurement geometry

      The sensor is not positioned correctly.

      2

      Wrong circuit condition

      Fitting measured at an unintended operating condition.

      3

      Poor asset identification

      A reading assigned to the wrong light.

      4

      Unverified calibration

      Traceability is unclear.

      5

      Dirty sensor or lens

      The instrument itself affects the result.

      6

      Not repeating abnormal results

      A measurement error gets classified as an equipment failure.

      7

      No visual inspection

      A simple contamination or alignment problem is missed.

      8

      No retest

      "Repaired" is reported without confirming optical performance.

      9

      Generic acceptance criteria

      A universal value applied to different fitting types.

      Airside safety

      Coordinate runway or taxiway access, ATC, airport operations, vehicle permits, radio, restricted-area access, work windows, circuit switching and escorts per the airport's approved airside-work requirements. It is not a roadside lighting inspection.

      Quality assurance

      Calibrated equipment, trained people, standardized set-up, documented method, repeatability checks, asset traceability, controlled data processing, report review, and second-level review of abnormal data for important surveys.

      Team competence

      Operators should understand AGL architecture, visual aids, CCR operation, transformers, optical alignment, PAPI, airport safety, measurement principles and data interpretation, so a low reading is investigated, not just reported.

      ICAO reference

      Aerodrome Design Manual Part 4, Visual Aids (Doc 9157 Part 4), covers the characteristics of visual aids including approach, runway and taxiway lighting. The ICAO Store lists the 5th Edition, 2021, with Amendment 2 dated 15 September 2025.

      Final Engineering Perspective

      A photometric test is a controlled engineering process, and a low reading can come from contamination, aiming, electrical problems, optical deterioration or measurement error. Find the cause before you act.

      FAQ

      AGL Photometric Testing Procedure FAQ

      Q1What is the AGL photometric testing procedure?

      A typical procedure runs: document review, visual inspection, equipment verification, measurement set-up, circuit preparation, photometric measurement, data analysis, defect identification, rectification, retesting and a final report.

      Q2What equipment is required for AGL photometry?

      Typically an airport photometer, calibrated sensor, measurement frame, positioning equipment and data-acquisition software. Large airports may also use vehicle-mounted mobile photometric systems. AAI specifications show both vehicle-mounted and portable approaches.

      Q3What parameters are measured during AGL photometry?

      Depending on the fitting: intensity, beam spread, orientation, colour, candela, coverage, chromaticity, configuration and other applicable parameters.

      Q4How is runway photometry performed?

      Review the lighting layout, align the measurement system to the runway reference, energize the circuit at the required operating condition, then measure and evaluate each required fitting or segment.

      Q5How is taxiway photometry different?

      The principle is the same, but the programme follows the taxiway configuration and may include edge lights, centreline lights, stop bars and runway guard lights.

      Q6How is PAPI photometry performed?

      It combines physical inspection, horizontal and vertical aiming verification, intensity measurement, colour assessment and evaluation of the white/red transition using suitable equipment.

      Q7Is PAPI intensity testing the same as PAPI aiming?

      No. Intensity measures optical output, while aiming establishes the angular direction of the beam. Both affect the final visual guidance.

      Q8Can a PAPI be on but still fail photometry?

      Yes. Electrical operation does not prove correct intensity, colour, beam distribution or aiming.

      Q9What is 4C AGL testing?

      In AAI technical specifications, 4C means Configuration, Colour, Candela and Coverage. Those specifications may also require chromaticity and other measurements.

      Q10What happens when an AGL light fails photometry?

      First confirm the measurement. Then inspect the fitting, electrical circuit, transformer, connector, optical assembly and alignment. Clean, realign, repair or replace as appropriate, then retest.

      Q11Should every failed light be replaced?

      No. A failure can come from contamination, aiming or other correctable conditions. Identify the cause before replacing.

      Q12Why is retesting important?

      It proves that the corrective action restored the required optical performance and closes the defect with measured evidence.

      Q13Can photometry be done using a vehicle?

      Yes. Vehicle-mounted systems suit large runway and taxiway surveys, and AAI procurement documents provide for them.

      Q14Can PAPI be tested using a handheld instrument?

      Yes, if the instrument and method suit PAPI. AAI's portable photometric specification covers AGL and PAPI measurements.

      Q15Is colour measured during AGL photometry?

      Where required. Colour and chromaticity are recognized field-testing parameters in AAI specifications.

      Q16What is the difference between photometry and electrical testing?

      Electrical testing evaluates the condition and behaviour of the electrical system. Photometry evaluates the optical performance of the installed light.

      Q17What is the acceptance limit for runway lights?

      There is no single universal value. It depends on the specific light, operating condition, runway function and governing specification.

      Q18How often should AGL photometry be performed?

      It depends on the approved maintenance programme, airport requirements, operational category, authority requirements and contract specification. AAI procurement examples include annual and multi-year testing, but those are not a universal frequency.

      Field Insight

      Engineering & Expert Notes

      RS
      By Rahul Sharma
      Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
      Plan the Test Before Going Out
      Preparation

      Agree locations, instruments, step settings and access arrangements in advance. A prepared plan reduces runway occupancy and rework.

      Measure at the Intensity Step Stated in the Requirement
      Settings

      Confirm CCR step and current before each reading, because output changes with setting. Record the step used.

      Position the Sensor Consistently
      Positioning

      Small changes in sensor position or angle can alter results. Use marked points or fixtures to repeat the geometry.

      Handle a Failed Result Methodically
      Failures

      Check the setup, clean the optic and verify alignment before declaring a failure. Then rectify and retest, keeping both records.

      Do Not Skip the Colour Check
      Colour

      Colour compliance is part of acceptance. Measure chromaticity where required rather than relying on appearance.

      Archive Raw Data With the Report
      Records

      Keep readings and photos alongside the summary so findings can be reviewed or challenged later.
