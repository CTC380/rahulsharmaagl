AGL-CS-080 — PAPI Photometry and Alignment Investigation

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. An integrated PAPI investigation where photometric degradation and geometric misalignment combined to produce unstable glide-path indication — from electrical verification through photometric measurement, beam-angle calibration, survey alignment, foundation inspection, and operational glide-path validation.

1Case Study Overview

During a routine ICAO visual aid inspection, flight crews reported that the PAPI indication appeared inconsistent during final approach, with unstable red/white transitions and uneven brightness between units.

Ground inspection confirmed that all four PAPI units were operational, but maintenance records showed that Unit 2 had undergone recent optical maintenance, Unit 3 had experienced foundation settlement, and Unit 4 had accumulated significant optical contamination.

Because the problem involved both photometric and geometric symptoms, a comprehensive investigation was initiated covering:

  • Beam-angle calibration.
  • Unit intensity balance.
  • Chromaticity.
  • Optical transmission.
  • Mechanical alignment.
  • Foundation stability.
  • Survey geometry.
  • Operational glide-path verification.

The investigation identified combined photometric and alignment defects affecting multiple PAPI units, resulting in degraded visual guidance and failure to meet ICAO Annex 14 performance requirements.

This case demonstrates that PAPI performance must be evaluated as a complete integrated visual guidance system rather than as four independent light units.

3System Background

A PAPI system requires simultaneous compliance of:

  • Beam angles.
  • Unit intensities.
  • Chromaticity.
  • Optical transmission.
  • Mechanical alignment.
  • Foundation elevation.
  • Runway reference geometry.
  • Transition sharpness.
  • Unit spacing.

Failure of any one parameter can degrade operational guidance.

21Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: All PAPI units ON. Uneven brightness. Incorrect transition sequence. Recent maintenance and construction activity. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace all PAPI units.
B. Increase light intensity.
C. Perform a complete integrated investigation including photometry, beam-angle calibration, survey alignment, foundation verification, intensity balancing, and operational glide-path validation.
D. Replace the power supply.
Correct answer: C. Uneven brightness and incorrect transition with healthy electrical operation indicate combined photometric and geometric defects. A complete integrated investigation will identify all contributing factors without unnecessary equipment replacement.

24Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about integrated PAPI photometric and alignment investigation.

Q1What is AGL-CS-080?

AGL-CS-080 is an engineering training case study. It covers a PAPI with several interacting photometric and alignment defects across multiple units, investigated as one integrated system. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2Why should a PAPI be evaluated as a system and not four separate lights?

The pilot sees one combined pattern. In AGL-CS-080, defects in different units and in the overall geometry combined to degrade the whole indication.

Q3What defects were found in AGL-CS-080?

Unit 3 had beam-angle drift (3.26° against 3.17°) from foundation settlement. Unit 4 was about 31% low in intensity (17,200 cd against roughly 25,000 cd) from optical contamination. The installation also had a lateral offset of 15.8 m against 15.0 m and a +96 mm elevation error.

Q4Was the electrical system involved?

No. Supply voltage, control system, driver outputs and unit operation were all normal.

Q5What did operational simulation show?

A 3W/1R indication on the published glide path, reduced transition sharpness, uneven brightness and reduced night conspicuity, which matched the pilot reports.

Q6What did the foundation inspection find?

Differential settlement beneath Unit 3, base movement, adjustment mechanism stress and mounting instability. The foundation movement had altered beam geometry.

Q7What optical problem affected Unit 4?

Internal lens haze, reflector contamination and reduced optical transmission, which caused the intensity degradation and unbalanced the units.

Q8How was the PAPI corrected?

The team re-established survey control points, corrected installation geometry, re-leveled Unit 3 and recalibrated beam angles. They replaced Unit 4's optical assembly, restored intensity balance and verified chromaticity, performed photometric testing and operational glide-path validation, then updated calibration documentation.

Q9What was verified before returning the PAPI to service?

Beam angles, intensity balance, chromaticity, transition sharpness, survey alignment and operational guidance all passed, restoring ICAO-compliant performance.

25Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

PAPI performance is an integrated photometric and geometric system, and correct visual glide-path guidance requires simultaneous compliance of beam-angle calibration, unit intensity balance, optical transmission, survey alignment, foundation stability, and operational validation, because a PAPI can be electrically healthy while still providing incorrect approach guidance if either the photometric or alignment components are degraded.

Systematic photometric measurement, beam-angle verification, survey alignment inspection, foundation assessment, intensity balancing, optical restoration, and operational glide-path validation provide a complete engineering methodology for restoring ICAO-compliant PAPI performance.

In AGL engineering, a PAPI is not merely four lights beside a runway; it is a precision optical surveying system whose purpose is to place every pilot on the correct glide path with confidence, consistency, and compliance.
