Product Overview

Edge Lit Airfield Sign Boards

What It Is

Edge-lit sign boards use an illumination arrangement designed to distribute light through the sign face, giving clear night-time visual information where signs must stay visible during night operations and changing ambient-light conditions.

Precision AGL Solutions supplies them for taxiway identification, location information, directional guidance and apron applications. Dimensions, legend, illumination performance and mounting are selected from the airport layout and project requirements.

Typical System

  • Sign face and legend
  • Edge-lighting or internal optical assembly
  • LED illumination system
  • Sign enclosure and electrical connection arrangement
  • Mounting supports with frangible or breakaway components
  • Sealing and environmental protection features

Edge-Lit Illumination System

A Complete-Sign Design Approach

System Aims

  • Consistent legend illumination
  • Good visibility at the required viewing distance
  • Controlled brightness, reduced localized hot spots
  • Suitable contrast between legend and background
  • Reliable night-time operation, long service life with maintenance

Design Principle

Under ICAO Annex 14, signs are to be illuminated for night use on instrument runways and on non-instrument runways of code number 3 or 4, and when used in runway visual range below 800 m; otherwise a retroreflective sign may be acceptable where the authority permits. Sign luminance should follow the airfield brightness steps so that signs match the surrounding lighting. LEDs are positioned and coupled with suitable optics to distribute light across the face. Evaluate the optical design as a complete sign assembly, not the LED source independently of the sign face and legend.

LED Technology

Efficient Illumination for Modern Signage

Potential Advantages

  • Low electrical consumption, long operating life
  • Reduced lamp replacement and maintenance
  • Stable illumination, rapid switching
  • Compatibility with modern control systems

Electrical Interface

Select LED configuration, driver arrangement, operating voltage and control interface to suit the project electrical architecture. Where signs integrate with an airfield lighting system, confirm the interface during project design.

Support and Clearance

Safety

Mounting must be stable while keeping the required clearance from aircraft and operating areas. Signs on the movement area must be frangible and low enough to clear aircraft propellers and engine pods. Confirm sign height, distance from the pavement edge and the frangible point in the airfield safety and clearance assessment, against the sign location plan.

Electrical Configuration

Engineered for the Project Power Architecture

Relevant Parameters

  • Series or parallel circuit, transformer rating, LED driver configuration
  • Rated power, current requirements
  • Cable connection, switching, dimming

  • Earthing/grounding arrangement

Control Integration

Airfield signs normally run on a series circuit (constant current, through an isolating transformer) or a parallel circuit (constant voltage). The LED driver must suit the transformer secondary or supply voltage and respond correctly to the regulator brightness steps. Confirm the electrical interface before procurement.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures

FAQ

Edge-Lit Airfield Sign Boards — Frequently Asked Questions

What are edge-lit airfield sign boards?

Edge-lit sign boards use an illumination arrangement designed to distribute light through the sign face, giving clear night-time visual information where signs must stay visible during night operations and changing ambient-light conditions. They are used for taxiway identification, location information, directional guidance and apron applications. A typical system has the sign face and legend, an edge-lighting or internal optical assembly, an LED illumination system, an enclosure and electrical connection arrangement, frangible or breakaway mounting supports, and sealing and environmental protection.

What colours and FAA types apply to the different sign categories?

Colour follows the sign category and is not chosen per project. Mandatory instruction signs are white on red, location signs are yellow on black, and direction and information signs are black on yellow. Red is reserved for mandatory instruction signs. FAA types are L-858R for mandatory instruction, L-858Y for direction, destination and information, and L-858L for location, with size, style and class confirmed per AC 150/5345-44L. Each sign face must meet the chromaticity and luminance limits for its colour.

Does an airfield sign have to be illuminated, and what are the benefits of LED edge-lighting?

Under ICAO Annex 14, signs are to be illuminated for night use on instrument runways and on non-instrument runways of code number 3 or 4, and when used where runway visual range is below 800 m. Otherwise a retroreflective sign may be acceptable where the authority permits. LED illumination offers low consumption, long life, reduced lamp replacement, stable output and compatibility with modern control systems. Light is positioned and coupled with optics to spread it across the face, and the optical design should be evaluated as a complete sign assembly.

How are legend, character height and sign size decided?

The legend comes from the airport signage plan and may include taxiway identifiers, runway-related information, arrows, location identifiers and stand or apron references. Character height and legend size are selected from the required viewing distance and the applicable signage criteria, with enough legend and background contrast. Overall dimensions depend on character height, legend spacing, arrow size, internal optical clearance, mounting clearance and cable entry position, and should be taken from the approved sign schedule. Large signs may need additional structural support or multiple mounting points.

How is an edge-lit sign mounted, and why must it be frangible?

Mounting options include ground-mounted supports, dedicated sign posts, breakaway or frangible supports, base plates and project-specific structures. Signs on the movement area must be frangible and low enough to clear aircraft propellers and engine pods. Confirm the sign height, distance from the pavement edge and the frangible point in the airfield safety and clearance assessment, against the sign location plan.

How is an edge-lit sign powered?

Airfield signs normally run on a series circuit (constant current, through an isolating transformer) or a parallel circuit (constant voltage). The LED driver must suit the transformer secondary or supply voltage and respond correctly to the regulator brightness steps. Confirm the electrical interface before procurement, including transformer rating, rated power and current, switching and dimming, control interface, surge protection and earthing.

How are edge-lit signs protected against the environment?

Construction may include corrosion-resistant materials and protective finishes, sealed electrical compartments, weather-resistant sign faces, durable optics, protected cable entries and robust mounting hardware. Consider rain and moisture, dust, UV exposure, temperature cycling, wind loading, corrosion, condensation and cleaning activity. Confirm the required ingress protection and environmental classification against the actual installation environment and project specification.

What are the common problems with edge-lit signs?

Typical problems are uneven illumination (an optical component issue, LED failure, contamination or internal deterioration), reduced brightness (LED or driver problems, supply issues, contamination or ageing), moisture inside the sign (compromised sealing, cable-entry problems or enclosure damage), a damaged legend or sign face, which reduces readability and should be assessed promptly, and damaged, loose or corroded mounting supports, which should be inspected before continued operation.

What should be provided when requesting or replacing an edge-lit sign?

Provide the airport or project name, sign type, approved legend, character height, number of characters, arrow or symbol requirements, required dimensions, installation location, mounting arrangement, frangibility requirements, illumination requirements, electrical supply, control requirements, environmental conditions, applicable project standards and required documentation. When replacing an existing sign, check the new unit against the existing foundation, cable routing, electrical interface and sign schedule.

Which standards apply to edge-lit airfield signs?

References include ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I (Chapter 5 on signs, Appendix 1 on colours and Appendix 4 on taxiing guidance sign design), ICAO Doc 9157 Part 4, FAA AC 150/5345-44L for runway and taxiway signs, FAA AC 150/5340-18H for sign standards, DGCA India CAR Section 4 and project-specific airfield lighting specifications, signage schedules and layout drawings. Confirm the applicable standard and edition for each project before final technical approval.

Is it safe to work on an edge-lit sign while the circuit is energised?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage, and opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death. De-energise the circuit at the regulator, apply the site lock-out / tag-out procedure, use the series circuit cut-out switch or an approved isolation device and work under the airport’s permit-to-work system. See the electrical safety warning on this page.
