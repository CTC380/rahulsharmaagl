Standards & Compliance Comparison

ICAO VS FAA

Annex 14, Volume I vs the FAA AC 150/5340 series — ICAO and FAA both define detailed requirements for airport visual aids, but they are not interchangeable standards. ICAO provides internationally applicable SARPs, while FAA requirements are primarily U.S.-specific and are implemented through regulations, Advisory Circulars, engineering briefs and equipment specifications.

02 · Definitions

What Is ICAO & FAA?

ICAO

ICAO = international aerodrome requirements. The principal AGL reference is Annex 14, Volume I — Aerodrome Design and Operations, which establishes specifications for aerodrome physical characteristics, facilities and technical services, including visual aids. ICAO also provides additional design guidance through Doc 9157, Aerodrome Design Manual, Part 4: Visual Aids.

Annex 14 covers

  • Runway, taxiway & approach lighting
  • PAPI & aerodrome beacons
  • Signs
  • Light characteristics, colour & intensity
  • Location, secondary power & maintenance performance

FAA

FAA = U.S. airport design, equipment, installation and maintenance requirements. Rather than one document equivalent to Annex 14, FAA requirements are distributed across AC 150/5340-30J (design and installation of visual aids), AC 150/5340-26C (maintenance), engineering briefs and equipment specifications.

FAA structure covers

  • Regulations
  • AC 150/5340 series
  • FAA equipment specifications
  • Engineering Briefs
  • Project-specific requirements
03 · Key Difference

International Basis vs U.S. Implementation Detail

The easiest way to remember the difference between ICAO and FAA:

ICAO

Tells the international aviation community what aerodrome visual aids need to achieve

FAA

Provides detailed U.S. requirements and implementation guidance for design, equipment, installation and maintenance

Engineering point: This is a difference in structure and emphasis, not a strict separation. ICAO is not without technical detail, and FAA is not without operational requirements.

04 · Lighting Philosophy

Same Objective, Different Structure

Both systems are fundamentally concerned with the same operational objective: provide pilots with reliable, standardized visual guidance for safe aircraft movement. The exact technical implementation, however, can differ.

ICAO structure

International operational standardization → visual characteristics → location/geometry → intensity/colour → operational requirements.

FAA structure

U.S. airport requirements → visual characteristics → location/geometry → intensity/colour → detailed design → installation → equipment specifications → maintenance.

06 · Runway & Taxiway Lighting

Shared Parameters, Separate Verification

Both ICAO and FAA specify requirements for runway lighting — fixture type, location, spacing, colour, light distribution, intensity, orientation, runway classification, approach category and operational conditions. Do not assume that an ICAO-compliant fixture automatically satisfies FAA requirements. The actual photometric tables, fixture characteristics, tolerances and installation details must be checked against the applicable standard.

Both systems also use taxiway lighting — edge lights, centreline lights, stop bars, clearance bars and signs — for ground guidance. Fixture colour, location, spacing, intensity and installation details can differ between ICAO and FAA requirements.

Do not simply write "ICAO/FAA compliant" on a project specification without identifying the exact applicable requirements.

07 · PAPI

Similar Concept, Requirements Verified Separately

PAPI is a good example of where the overall concept is similar but detailed requirements need separate verification — optical system, colour transition, vertical beam characteristics, intensity, installation geometry, elevation and alignment. PAPI photometric compliance and PAPI installation/alignment compliance should be checked against the selected standard, not assumed to be identical between ICAO and FAA.

08 · CCR & Series Circuit

Operational Performance vs Design + Equipment

ICAO focuses heavily on the operational requirements and performance of the aerodrome lighting system, including lighting characteristics and power-supply considerations. FAA documents add more detailed U.S.-specific guidance for design, installation and maintenance of airport visual aids.

ICAO

AGL system performance → operational requirement.

FAA

AGL system performance + design/installation + equipment/maintenance.

09 · Secondary Power Supply

Reliable Power for Critical Visual Aids

Both ICAO and FAA recognize the importance of reliable power for critical visual aids. ICAO Annex 14 specifically includes requirements concerning secondary power supply and associated switching characteristics — the practical objective being that if normal power fails, secondary power takes over and AGL continues. The actual required transfer times, loads, circuits and categories must be taken from the applicable authority/project requirements.

10 · Maintenance

Performance Objectives vs Dedicated Maintenance AC

Aspect ICAO FAA
ApproachMaintenance performance objectivesDedicated maintenance guidance
Governing documentAnnex 14AC 150/5340-26C
FramingAerodrome operator programmeInspection + maintenance practices

Annex 14 material describes the relevant specifications as defining maintenance performance level objectives, rather than simply defining whether a lighting system is operational or out of service. FAA's dedicated document, AC 150/5340-26C – Maintenance of Airport Visual Aid Facilities, is currently listed as active by FAA.

11 · LED Requirements

LED Is Not Just a Lamp Replacement

Both ICAO and FAA support modern lighting technologies, but the compliance route differs. FAA specifically identifies Engineering Brief 67 for light sources other than incandescent and xenon for airport and obstruction lighting fixtures. ICAO Annex 14 also addresses LED-related considerations — for example, noting that LED fixtures do not produce the infrared heat signature associated with incandescent lamps, which can have implications for snow/ice and certain operational considerations.

LED should not be treated simply as a lamp replacement. The complete fixture must satisfy the applicable photometric, colour, electrical, mechanical and environmental requirements.

12 · Photometry

Same Categories, Different Tables

Both ICAO and FAA specify photometric characteristics — intensity, beam distribution, beam spread, colour, vertical and horizontal distribution — for different visual aids. But the measurement method, test conditions, photometric tables and acceptance limits can differ.

Not sufficient

"Fixture is ICAO/FAA compliant."

Better

"Fixture photometry tested against the applicable ICAO Annex 14 requirements and FAA requirements identified in the project specification."

13 · Installation & Frangibility

Installation Considerations

FAA AC 150/5340-30J specifically addresses design and installation details for airport visual aids. Typical installation considerations for both frameworks include fixture location, elevation, alignment, mounting, frangibility, bases, cable installation, transformers, connectors, in-pavement installation and equipment clearances. ICAO also addresses these matters at the aerodrome-system level.

Both ICAO and FAA address safety considerations for equipment located near runways and taxiways, but use the specific authority's requirements for the project instead of assuming one frangibility requirement is automatically equivalent to the other.

17 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: "ICAO/FAA compliant" is a sufficient technical statement
Fact

Photometry, fixture geometry, intensity, colour, PAPI, approach lighting, frangibility, CCR, power supply, installation and maintenance requirements should each be compared against the exact applicable reference, not assumed equivalent.

Myth: An ICAO-compliant fixture automatically satisfies FAA requirements
Fact

The actual photometric tables, fixture characteristics, tolerances and installation details must be checked against the applicable standard for each framework separately.

Myth: FAA documents are mandatory on any project using FAA-compliant equipment
Fact

What is actually mandatory is determined by the local aviation authority and the contract/project specification — not by the origin of the equipment's compliance testing.

18 · Engineering Verdict

Which Framework Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

ICAO tells the international aviation community what aerodrome visual aids need to achieve. FAA provides detailed U.S. requirements and implementation guidance for airport visual aids, equipment, installation and maintenance.

For an AGL project, do not assume ICAO and FAA requirements are identical. Compare the exact requirement for each item, especially photometry, fixture geometry, intensity, colour, PAPI, approach lighting, frangibility, CCR, power supply, installation and maintenance.

19 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Are ICAO and FAA interchangeable standards for AGL?

No. ICAO provides internationally applicable SARPs, while FAA requirements are primarily U.S.-specific and implemented through regulations, Advisory Circulars, engineering briefs and equipment specifications.

Does an ICAO-compliant fixture automatically satisfy FAA requirements?

No. The photometric tables, fixture characteristics, tolerances and installation details must be checked against each applicable standard separately.

What is the single ICAO document equivalent to the FAA framework?

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I is the principal reference, supplemented by Doc 9157 Part 4. FAA requirements, by contrast, are distributed across the AC 150/5340 series, engineering briefs and equipment specifications rather than one equivalent document.

Which standard applies on an Indian airport project?

The applicable Indian regulatory requirements and project/airport specification govern first, with ICAO requirements typically the next reference. FAA documents are not automatically mandatory merely because equipment is FAA compliant.

Do both frameworks cover LED light sources?

Yes, both support modern lighting technologies, but the compliance route differs — FAA identifies Engineering Brief 67, while ICAO Annex 14 addresses LED-related considerations separately.

Is it enough to write "ICAO/FAA compliant" in a technical submittal?

No. A compliance matrix identifying the ICAO reference, FAA reference and project requirement for each item is much stronger for tender compliance, technical submittals and airport authority approval.

What is the difference between ICAO and FAA airport lighting?

ICAO Annex 14 sets international standards and recommended practices that States adopt for airport lighting layout, colour and performance. The FAA publishes detailed advisory circulars covering equipment specifications, installation and maintenance for US airports. Airports outside the US often follow ICAO for requirements and may cite FAA specifications for equipment, so the project specification decides which applies.

20 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual airport project, verify the current applicable editions of the relevant standards before using this comparison for project design.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — visual aids, lighting characteristics, colour, intensity, location, secondary power and maintenance performance objectives.

ICAO Doc 9157, Part 4 — Visual Aids

Functional requirements, approach lighting, runway/taxiway lighting, light characteristics, signs and other visual aids.

FAA AC 150/5340-30J

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids — currently listed as active by FAA.

FAA AC 150/5340-26C

Maintenance of Airport Visual Aid Facilities — currently listed as active by FAA.

FAA Engineering Brief 67

Light sources other than incandescent and xenon for airport and obstruction lighting fixtures.

Final Engineering Note

The local aviation authority and contract/project specification determine what is actually mandatory — verify DGCA, AAI or the equivalent national CAA requirements for Indian projects.

ICAO = international aerodrome visual-aid framework. FAA = U.S.-specific regulatory/technical framework with detailed airport design, installation, equipment and maintenance guidance. Do not assume the two are identical — compare each requirement against the exact applicable standard.
