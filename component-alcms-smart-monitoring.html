ALCMS & Smart Airfield Monitoring

The central nervous system of the airfield — a sophisticated SCADA-based platform that controls every CCR, monitors every circuit, logs every event, and gives air traffic controllers and maintenance teams real-time visibility of the entire AGL infrastructure.

Overview

What Is It?

Airfield Lighting Control and Monitoring System (ALCMS) is the integrated software and hardware platform that allows operators to control, monitor, and diagnose the entire AGL network from a central location. It replaces manual switching panels and local control boxes with a computerised system that communicates with every CCR, distribution panel, and, in modern implementations, even individual fixtures.

Smart airfield monitoring extends this concept with predictive analytics, asset health tracking, and integration with other airfield systems (e.g., ASDE-X, weather sensors, BMS) to create a holistic view of airfield operations. The goal is not just remote control, but intelligent, data-driven decision support for operations and maintenance.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The ALCMS's purpose is to give a single operator, from a single location, both the reach and the awareness to run an airfield's entire lighting system safely — turning what would otherwise be dozens or hundreds of separately switched circuits and local control panels into one coherent picture that can be commanded and diagnosed centrally. Its value shows up most clearly in exactly the situations where manual, distributed control would be too slow or too fragmented to trust: bringing an approach lighting system up to full intensity the moment weather deteriorates, isolating a faulted circuit before it affects surrounding operations, or pinpointing which single fixture in a long series loop has failed from a CCR's current signature alone, rather than sending a technician to walk the runway. The step from ALCMS toward "smart" monitoring extends this same purpose forward in time — using the same data stream not just to show what is happening now, but to flag what is likely to fail next, so maintenance can be planned before a fault ever reaches the control room as an alarm.

Application

Where Is It Used?

ALCMS is deployed wherever AGL infrastructure needs to be controlled and monitored:

  • Air Traffic Control Tower: Primary control interface for switching circuits and intensity steps in real time.
  • Maintenance Control Centre: Detailed status displays, fault logs, and diagnostic tools for engineering staff.
  • Remote Monitoring Stations: Secondary displays for off-site or backup monitoring (e.g., airport operations centre).
  • Mobile Devices (Tablets): Increasingly, field technicians use mobile applications to view circuit status and receive fault alerts while on the airfield.
  • Integration with Other Systems: Data exchanged with surface movement radars, weather systems, and building management systems for coordinated decision-making.

The system typically includes a redundant architecture (dual servers, dual network paths) to ensure high availability.

Placement

Installation & Location

The ALCMS server infrastructure is installed in the airfield's main equipment room or data centre, with the primary operator workstation sited in the ATC tower or control room where the switching decisions are actually made, and secondary displays or a maintenance control centre workstation installed wherever engineering staff need real-time visibility. Where a redundant, dual-server architecture is specified, the standby server is installed in a physically separate room or building on its own UPS-protected power feed, connected to the primary by dedicated replication links, so a single room-level event cannot take down both. Field-side connectivity — from CCR communication interfaces, remote I/O, and smart sensors back to the ALCMS server — rides on the airfield's existing redundant fibre and Ethernet backbone rather than requiring new cabling specific to the ALCMS itself, and integration with third-party systems (surface radar, weather, BMS) is commissioned through the ALCMS's own interface ports without those external systems needing to be co-located. Mobile access for field technicians is provisioned through the facility's Wi-Fi or cellular network rather than a dedicated installation, since the mobile application itself has no physical siting requirement beyond normal network coverage.

Function

How Does It Work?

ALCMS is built on a SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition) framework. It communicates with remote terminal units (RTUs) located in each CCR or distribution panel over a dedicated communication network (fiber optic, Ethernet, or serial). The RTUs acquire data from the CCRs (current, voltage, fault status, intensity setting) and execute commands sent from the control centre.

Key functional aspects include:

  • Circuit Control: Turn individual series circuits on/off, select intensity steps, and change operating modes (e.g., normal, emergency, test).
  • Real-Time Monitoring: Display current, voltage, power, and status of each CCR and circuit. Alarms trigger when parameters deviate from setpoints.
  • Event Logging & Reporting: All operator actions, faults, and system events are logged with timestamps, providing an audit trail and data for trend analysis.
  • Fault Diagnosis: Advanced systems provide fault location assistance by analysing current signatures and comparing with expected values (e.g., detecting a single fixture failure from the CCR current profile).
  • Predictive Maintenance: By tracking lamp hours, switching cycles, and performance drift, the system can alert maintenance crews to impending failures before they occur.
  • Cybersecurity: Modern ALCMS includes role-based access control, encrypted communications, and intrusion detection to protect critical infrastructure.
Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

ALCMS sits at the top of the airfield lighting control hierarchy, and its own architecture is essentially the control loop the rest of the system feeds into. At the field level, remote terminal units (RTUs) inside each CCR or distribution panel acquire real-time current, voltage, fault and intensity data, and carry out on/off and step commands issued from above. That data travels over a dedicated communication network — fibre, Ethernet, or serial — back to the central SCADA server, which aggregates it, runs alarm and fault-diagnosis logic, and presents it to the controller or maintenance operator through the HMI. Commands flow the other way along the same path: an operator selecting a new intensity step or switching a circuit off issues that instruction from the HMI, the server validates and routes it to the correct RTU, and the RTU executes it at the CCR — with the resulting change reported back up the same loop to confirm it took effect.

CCR / RTU → Communication Network → SCADA Server → HMI → Operator Command (closed loop)

System Architecture

One Server, Every Circuit, Every Screen

HMI & data layer
Command / field layer

Every human interface — tower, maintenance centre, remote monitoring, field tablets — talks to a single redundant ALCMS server, never directly to the field. The server pushes commands down to RTUs installed at each CCR room or distribution panel over a dedicated network, and pulls back current, voltage and fault data from the same link. External systems (weather, ASDE-X, building management) plug into the server through open interfaces rather than the control network, keeping the safety-critical command path isolated from general IT integration.

Construction

Physical Construction

The ALCMS platform is built as a distributed hardware and software system rather than a single physical unit: server hardware, typically rack-mounted in a control-room or equipment-room cabinet, hosts the core monitoring and control software, often duplicated as a hot-standby pair housed in separate cabinets or even separate buildings for redundancy. Field-level hardware, including remote terminal units (RTUs) or protocol converters installed within CCR cabinets and distribution panels, physically interfaces with the underlying AGL equipment, translating local signals into the Ethernet, fibre-optic, or serial communication protocols the central servers use to poll and command the field. These field units are housed in sealed, DIN-rail-mounted enclosures within the same cabinets as the CCRs and switchgear they monitor, wired directly to current sensors, contactors, and status relays. The communication backbone connecting field units to the central server, whether fibre-optic cable, structured Ethernet cabling, or a dedicated RS-485 network, is installed in conduit or duct runs alongside the primary AGL power cabling, with redundant physical paths provided on critical circuits so a single cable fault does not isolate a section of the airfield from central monitoring.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Communication Network Failures

Fiber cuts, switch failures, or electromagnetic interference can disrupt communication between the control centre and remote RTUs, resulting in loss of visibility and control.

Software / Firmware Bugs

Like any complex software, ALCMS can have bugs that cause incorrect status displays, command failures, or system crashes. Regular updates and thorough testing are essential.

Cybersecurity Vulnerabilities

As a critical infrastructure system, ALCMS is a potential target for cyber attacks. Weak passwords, unpatched systems, and insecure interfaces can expose the airfield to disruption.

RTU Hardware Failures

The remote terminal units at each CCR or panel are subject to heat, humidity, and power surges, leading to hardware failures that knock out monitoring for that location.

Data Integrity / Calibration Drift

Measurement sensors (current, voltage) can drift over time, causing the system to display inaccurate values, which may lead to incorrect maintenance decisions.

Integration Complexity

Connecting ALCMS to other airfield systems (e.g., ASDE, weather, or ATC automation) often requires custom interfaces that can be brittle and difficult to maintain.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Single RTU / CCR Goes Dark on the Control Screen
Likely Cause
A failed RTU (heat, humidity, or power-surge damage), a severed or degraded fiber/copper drop to that one location, or a local network switch fault serving only that panel.
Diagnostic Steps
Check the RTU's local status LEDs and power supply at the equipment room; test the communication link back to the head-end with a loopback or link-status check; confirm the local switch port is up and passing traffic.
Corrective Action
Reset or replace the RTU if it's unresponsive locally, repair or splice the damaged link, or replace the faulted switch port; confirm the point returns to the control screen before closing out.
Multiple Points or an Entire Zone Lost Simultaneously
Likely Cause
A fiber cut or aggregation-switch failure upstream of several RTUs, or electromagnetic interference disrupting a shared communication trunk rather than a single-point hardware fault.
Diagnostic Steps
Map which points went dark together to identify the shared trunk or switch; check the aggregation/core switch for link and power status; inspect the fiber run for physical damage at accessible splice points and pull cabinets.
Corrective Action
Repair or re-splice the damaged fiber run, replace the failed switch, and reroute through a redundant path if the network architecture supports it; verify all affected points recover before standing down.
Displayed Status Doesn't Match Actual Field Condition
Likely Cause
Sensor calibration drift on the current/voltage measurement feeding that RTU, a software/firmware bug in the status-processing logic, or stale data from a communication link that dropped without alarming.
Diagnostic Steps
Cross-check the displayed value against a hand-held meter reading at the CCR or panel; check the RTU's last-update timestamp to rule out a silently stale link; review recent firmware change logs for the affected module.
Corrective Action
Recalibrate or replace the drifting sensor, apply the vendor's firmware patch if a known bug is confirmed, and fix the alarming logic so a dropped link is flagged rather than displayed as last-known-good.
Operator Commands Not Reaching the Field (No Response to Step/On/Off)
Likely Cause
The target CCR or RTU is in local control rather than remote, a command-path fault between the server and that RTU, or a software fault on the server queuing commands without executing them.
Diagnostic Steps
Confirm the local/remote selector at the CCR panel; issue a test command to a known-good point on the same link to isolate whether the fault is link-wide or specific to one device; check the server's command queue and event log for rejected or stuck commands.
Corrective Action
Return the selector to remote, restore the communication path to the affected RTU, or restart the affected server process/service if commands are stuck in queue; re-verify command-and-response on the affected point.
System Flags Unauthorised Access Attempts or Unexpected Configuration Changes
Likely Cause
A cybersecurity event such as a brute-force login attempt, an unpatched vulnerability being probed, or weak/shared credentials on an operator or interface account.
Diagnostic Steps
Review the security/audit log for the source and account involved; confirm which systems and interfaces are network-reachable from that source; check patch status of the server and any exposed interfaces against the vendor's current advisories.
Corrective Action
Lock out or reset the affected account, apply outstanding security patches, and tighten network segmentation or firewall rules around the ALCMS; follow the airfield's incident-response procedure and notify the relevant security authority if compromise is confirmed.
Integrated System Feed Missing or Erratic (Weather, ATC, ASDE)
Likely Cause
A brittle custom interface to the third-party system has broken after an update on either side, or the external system itself is down or has changed its data format.
Diagnostic Steps
Check the interface's connection and error log for parsing or timeout failures; confirm the external system is online and delivering data in its expected format; review recent version or configuration changes on either side of the interface.
Corrective Action
Update or repair the custom interface to match the current data format, coordinate with the external system's administrators on any breaking change, and add monitoring on the interface itself so a future break is flagged rather than discovered by an operator.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

Communication Network Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Cable damage, a failed network switch, or a fault in the field communication path between the ALCMS server and its RTUs or CCRs.
Effect
Multiple points or an entire zone are lost simultaneously on the control screen, removing supervisory visibility over that section of the system.
Detection Method
Communication protocol health check, and a redundancy/failover test to confirm the backup path picks up automatically.
Software / Firmware Bug
High Severity
Root Cause
A defect introduced in an ALCMS software or RTU firmware update that wasn't caught before deployment.
Effect
Displayed status doesn't match the actual field condition, or operator commands stop reaching the field even though the connection itself is healthy.
Detection Method
Response time verification and a circuit status polling verification comparing displayed status against a direct field check.
Cybersecurity Vulnerability
High Severity
Root Cause
An unpatched software component, weak access control, or a misconfigured network boundary exposing the ALCMS to unauthorised access.
Effect
The system flags unauthorised access attempts or unexpected configuration changes, or worse, such an event goes undetected.
Detection Method
Cybersecurity patch review and configuration check performed on a scheduled basis, not just after an incident.
RTU Hardware Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Component ageing, a power supply fault, or environmental stress at an outdoor-mounted RTU cabinet.
Effect
A single RTU or CCR goes dark on the control screen while the rest of the system continues reporting normally.
Detection Method
RTU/field unit communication test, and a check of the affected unit's power and hardware status.
Data Integrity / Calibration Drift
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Sensor or RTU calibration drifting out of tolerance without a corresponding update to the ALCMS data model.
Effect
The system's data logging and historian record values that no longer accurately reflect true field conditions.
Detection Method
Data logging and historian verification, cross-checked against a direct field measurement at the same point.
Redundancy / Failover Fault
High Severity
Root Cause
A configuration error or hardware fault in the standby server or communication path meant to take over automatically if the primary fails.
Effect
A primary system fault results in a full outage instead of an automatic, low-disruption changeover to redundant equipment.
Detection Method
Redundancy and failover test performed on a scheduled basis to confirm the standby path actually takes over correctly.
Integration Complexity Fault
Medium Severity
Root Cause
A change on a third-party system — weather, ATC, or ASDE feed — that breaks the interface without a corresponding update on the ALCMS side.
Effect
An integrated system feed goes missing or becomes erratic, denying operators one of the external data sources the monitoring system is meant to consolidate.
Detection Method
Integration interface check performed against each connected third-party system, particularly after any known change on their side.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Confirm UPS/Redundant Power Before Maintenance
Electrical Safety

Verify servers, RTUs, and network equipment are on protected power and that redundant paths are genuinely independent before taking any single component out of service for maintenance.

Backup Configuration and Data First
Data Integrity

Take a verified backup of system configuration, historical data, and calibration settings before applying software updates, firmware changes, or hardware replacement.

Cybersecurity Hygiene
Compliance

Keep credentials, firewall rules, and patch levels current, avoid connecting unauthorised devices to the control network, and never disable security controls to work around a nuisance issue.

Coordinate Maintenance Windows
Operational

Schedule software updates, failover tests, and RTU replacements during an approved window with operators notified, since the platform provides live control and monitoring of the AGL network.

Verify Failover Before Isolating a Node
Operational

Confirm redundant servers or communication paths have successfully taken over before powering down or disconnecting the primary path for service.

Validate Data and Control After Any Change
Compliance

After an update or component replacement, confirm real-time status, alarms, and control commands are all functioning correctly across the affected circuits before closing out the work.

Maintain a Manual Fallback Plan
Operational

Ensure local/manual control fallback procedures remain available and known to operators in case the monitoring platform itself becomes unavailable during an incident.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Alarm Fatigue Undermines the Whole Platform
Design

A monitoring system that generates frequent low-value alerts trains operators to acknowledge and dismiss quickly, which delays response when a genuinely critical alarm appears among the noise. Tune thresholds and suppress known-benign conditions deliberately rather than letting nuisance alarms accumulate.

A Silent Field Device Isn't the Same as a Confirmed Healthy One
Diagnostics

A field device that stops reporting because of a communications failure can look identical on the dashboard to one that is genuinely fine and simply has nothing to report. Build heartbeat or last-seen monitoring into the platform so silence itself triggers investigation rather than being read as "all clear."

Time Sync Across the Platform Matters for Incident Reconstruction
Troubleshooting

If field controllers and the central ALCMS platform drift out of time sync, event logs from different parts of the system can appear out of sequence during a post-incident review, making root-cause analysis harder than it needs to be. Verify time sync health across the whole system, not just at the server.

Historical Trend Data Is Only as Good as Its Retention Policy
Process

A platform that only retains detailed data for a short rolling window can lose the very trend history needed to diagnose a slow-developing fault once it finally surfaces. Confirm retention settings match the timescale of the failure modes you actually need to catch, such as seasonal drift.

Treat the Platform's Own Health as a Monitored Item
Reliability

Operators can develop unwarranted confidence in an "all green" dashboard if the monitoring platform itself has a partial outage that isn't visible from within its own interface. Maintain an independent, external check on platform availability rather than relying solely on the platform to report its own health.

Document Every Threshold Change With Its Rationale
Compliance

An alarm threshold adjusted during troubleshooting and left unrecorded can confuse a future technician who doesn't know why the value differs from the original commissioning baseline. Log the reason for any threshold change alongside the change itself.
