Initial Issue

Key principle — Do not classify every lighting outage as a fixture failure. First determine whether the actual failure is in the cable, circuit, connector, termination, insulation, electrical connection, protection system, or associated equipment. Every electrical failure record should capture the full failure chain — one electrical failure can lead to another.

Core categories — Cable insulation breakdown, open circuit, short circuit, earth fault, connector failure, high-resistance joint, voltage imbalance and circuit overload are the eight starting electrical-failure categories of Division 02. The remaining twelve topics are expanded diagnostic and failure-intelligence categories, not replacements for the original eight.

Relationship to Division 05 — ELF-01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 09 and 10 (cable insulation breakdown, open circuit, short circuit, earth fault, connector failure, cable termination failure and cable joint failure) are treated here at the general series-circuit/electrical level. For the cable- and connector-hardware-specific version of the same failure modes — physical construction, installation cause and connector-interface detail — see Division 05 — Cable & Connector Failures.

ELF-01 Cable Insulation Breakdown Core
Failure Definition

Failure or deterioration of cable insulation resulting in reduced insulation integrity, leakage, earth fault, short circuit or eventual circuit failure.

Typical Symptoms
  • Low insulation resistance
  • Earth-fault alarm
  • Repeated CCR protection trips
  • Intermittent operation
  • Complete circuit outage
  • Increasing leakage current
  • Cable heating in severe cases
Possible Causes
  • Cable ageing
  • Mechanical damage
  • Moisture ingress
  • Thermal stress
  • Improper installation
  • Excessive bending
  • Chemical/environmental deterioration
  • Electrical overstress
Investigation
  • Isolate the circuit safely
  • Perform insulation-resistance testing
  • Compare phase/series-circuit readings with historical values
  • Segment the circuit where necessary
  • Locate the defective section
  • Inspect cable physically
  • Check joints and terminations
Evidence
  • IR test readings
  • Cable-test report
  • Fault location
  • Cable photographs
  • Maintenance history
  • Environmental conditions
ELF-02 Open Circuit Core
Failure Definition

Complete interruption of the electrical path in the AGL circuit.

Possible Locations
  • Primary cable
  • Cable joint
  • Connector
  • Transformer connection
  • Secondary lead
  • Terminal
  • Internal fixture connection
Symptoms
  • Circuit outage
  • Multiple fixtures extinguished
  • CCR unable to establish normal output
  • Circuit continuity failure
  • Abnormal CCR indication
Investigation

Trace the circuit systematically rather than replacing individual fixtures without confirmation.

Important Distinction

An open circuit is a failure mode, not necessarily the root cause. For example:

OPEN CIRCUITBROKEN CABLE CONDUCTORMECHANICAL DAMAGE

or

OPEN CIRCUITLOOSE TERMINALINCORRECT MAINTENANCE TORQUE

  • Internal equipment fault
Symptoms

  • Protection alarm

  • Repeated trip condition
Investigation — Check
  • Cable insulation
  • Connectors
  • Joints
  • Transformers
  • Fixture secondary circuits
  • Internal equipment
  • Moisture

ELF-06 High-Resistance Joint Core
Failure Definition

A connection having abnormally high electrical resistance.

Causes

  • Poor crimp
  • Corrosion
  • Contamination
  • Incorrect connector assembly
  • Damaged contact
  • Poor termination
Symptoms
  • Voltage drop
  • Localized heating
  • Flickering
  • Reduced fixture output
  • Intermittent operation
  • Progressive joint deterioration

ELF-08 Circuit Overload Core
Failure Definition

Electrical loading exceeding the intended operating capacity of the circuit or equipment. This is the final category among the eight starting electrical-failure categories.

Possible Causes
  • Incorrect circuit design
  • Incorrect fixture quantity
  • Incorrect load calculation
  • Equipment/component failure
  • Incorrect configuration
  • Abnormal operating condition
  • Incorrect replacement component
Symptoms
  • Excessive CCR output demand
  • Protection operation
  • Overheating
  • Cable temperature increase
  • Equipment shutdown
  • Repeated protection trips
Investigation — Verify
  • Circuit load
  • Fixture quantity
  • Transformer ratings
  • CCR capacity
  • Current
  • Voltage
  • Cable condition
  • Circuit configuration
  • Recent modifications

  • Transformer insulation degradation
Symptoms

  • Reduced circuit reliability
Investigation

Compare leakage/current readings against previous records, normal operating condition, circuit condition and environmental conditions.

ELF-14 Loose Terminal
Failure Definition

Terminal connection that has lost the required mechanical/electrical tightness.

Symptoms
  • Intermittent operation
  • Heating
  • Voltage fluctuation
  • High resistance
  • Open circuit
Investigation — Check
  • Terminal tightness
  • Signs of overheating
  • Discolouration
  • Corrosion
  • Conductor condition
  • Correct termination
  • Torque where specified
Important Connection

A loose terminal can be both failure mode: loose terminal, and root cause: incorrect maintenance torque. The database should keep those fields separate.

ELF-17 Circuit Continuity Failure
Failure Definition

Failure to maintain a continuous electrical path through the intended circuit.

Possible Causes
  • Open cable
  • Failed joint
  • Failed connector
  • Loose terminal
  • Broken conductor
  • Component disconnection
Investigation

Perform continuity testing systematically after safe isolation.

Important Distinction

Continuity failure describes the test/result condition. The actual root cause could be broken conductor, failed joint, loose connector or incorrect termination.

ELF-18 Intermittent Electrical Fault
Failure Definition

Electrical fault that appears and disappears rather than remaining continuously present.

Typical Symptoms
  • Flickering fixture
  • Intermittent circuit outage
  • Repeated CCR alarms
  • Fault disappears after switching
  • Fault appears during vibration or temperature change
Difficulties

Intermittent faults are often difficult to reproduce.

Investigation — Consider
  • Loose connections
  • High-resistance joints
  • Cable damage
  • Moisture
  • Thermal effects
  • Connector problems
  • Mechanical movement
  • Intermittent insulation breakdown
Evidence

Record the exact operating conditions under which the fault occurred.

ELF-19 Electrical Surge Damage
Failure Definition

Damage resulting from an abnormal transient electrical event.

Possible Effects
  • LED driver failure
  • Electronic component damage
  • Protection-component failure
  • Insulation damage
  • Control equipment failure
  • Repeated fixture failure
Possible Sources
  • Lightning-related transient
  • Switching transient
  • Supply disturbance
  • Protection-system inadequacy
Evidence

Record date/time of failure, weather condition, lightning activity if known, equipment affected, protection condition, surge-protection status and number of simultaneously failed components.

Important Pattern

If multiple fixtures/components fail simultaneously, investigate whether the event represents a systemic electrical transient rather than individual component failure.

Division 02 vs. Division 01

Division 01 captures failures at the fixture level — LED, driver, lamp, optics, housing. Division 02 captures failures in the electrical path feeding those fixtures — cable, circuit, connector, termination, insulation, protection and associated equipment. Before recording a lighting outage in Division 01, first rule out the Division 02 causes covered here: a fixture that appears failed may in fact be starved by an open circuit, an earth fault, or a high-resistance joint upstream.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about electrical failures in the AGL series circuit, as covered in Division 02 of the AGL Failure Database.

Q1What is Division 02 — Electrical Failures in the AGL Failure Database?

Division 02 covers the electrical path that feeds AGL fixtures: the series circuit and its associated electrical connections. It contains 20 published topics (ELF-01 to ELF-20) covering cable, circuit, connector, termination, insulation, protection and associated-equipment failures.

Q2Which electrical failures does Division 02 cover?

Cable insulation breakdown, open circuit, short circuit, earth fault, connector failure, high-resistance joint, voltage imbalance, circuit overload, cable termination failure, cable joint failure, insulation deterioration, leakage current, poor electrical connection, loose terminal, incorrect wiring, polarity error, circuit continuity failure, intermittent electrical fault, electrical surge damage and electrical protection failure.

Q3How are the 20 electrical failure topics organised?

The first eight topics are the primary, core electrical-failure categories, and the remaining twelve are expanded diagnostic and failure-intelligence categories that do not replace the original eight. The topics are grouped into five logical sets while keeping the original numbering: Cable & Circuit Integrity (8 topics), Connection & Termination (6 topics), and three two-topic groups covering electrical performance, wiring and configuration, and transient and protection.

Q4What failure chains does Division 02 capture?

Division 02 records the full chain because one electrical failure can lead to another. For example, cable ageing leads to insulation deterioration, low insulation resistance, leakage current, an earth fault, a CCR trip and a lighting outage. In a connection chain, a loose terminal leads to high contact resistance, heating, connection deterioration, an intermittent fault and finally an open circuit. Moisture, surge and incorrect-wiring chains are also captured.

Q5Why rule out electrical causes before recording a fixture failure in Division 01?

Division 01 captures failures at the fixture level, while Division 02 captures failures in the electrical path feeding those fixtures. A fixture that appears failed may in fact be starved by an open circuit, an earth fault or a high-resistance joint upstream, so these Division 02 causes should be ruled out first.

Q6What should be recorded for an electrical failure?

Each electrical failure should become a standard failure record. Fields include failure ID, airport/project, circuit and cable identification, equipment affected, failure date, operating condition, failure category and mode, symptoms, test results, fault location, physical evidence, environmental condition, previous failure history, root and contributing cause, corrective and preventive action, retesting, operational impact, downtime, severity, related SOP, drawing and test report, photographs, lessons learned and recurrence status.

Q7How is a cable insulation or earth fault investigated?

The circuit is isolated safely and insulation-resistance testing is performed. Readings are compared with historical values, and the circuit is segmented where necessary to locate the defective section. The cable is then inspected physically, and joints and terminations are checked. Evidence includes IR test readings, the cable-test report, fault location, cable photographs, maintenance history and environmental conditions.

Q8How are failure severity and operational impact classified?

Every failure receives a severity classification, plus operational-impact tagging. The classes are Critical / Safety-Critical (immediate operational or safety risk), Major / Operationally Critical (significant availability impact), Moderate (reduced performance, not yet critical), Minor / Non-Critical (limited or no operational effect), and Degradation / Intermittent / Latent (a developing or hidden fault).

Q9Which RCA categories are most relevant to electrical failures?

Division 02 links every failure to the Root Cause Analysis Database. The most relevant RCA categories are Electrical, Thermal, Environmental, Installation, Maintenance, Material, Component and Human/Procedural.
