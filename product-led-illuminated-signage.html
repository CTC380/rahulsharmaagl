Product Overview

LED Illuminated Airfield Signage

What It Is

LED illuminated signage gives clear, consistent visual information across movement areas and supports modern airfield lighting and signage upgrades, improving visibility during night operations, reduced visibility and low-light conditions.

Sign construction, illumination method, luminance, electrical interface, dimensions, mounting and environmental protection are configured to the airport layout, operational requirement, applicable standard and project specification.

Principal Components

  • Sign face — legend, symbol or identifier
  • LED lighting system and optical system for uniformity
  • Power supply — converts and regulates the input
  • Enclosure — protects internal components
  • Mounting structure and electrical interface
Sign Categories

Configured for Each Airfield Function

Mandatory instructionMandatory instructions at designated locations.
LocationCurrent taxiway, runway or movement-area location.
DirectionRouting toward taxiways, runways, aprons, gates.
InformationAdditional operational or navigational information.
Stand identificationAircraft stands, gates and apron parking positions.
Replacement & upgradeUpgrading conventional illuminated signs to LED, any category.

Also applicable at taxiway intersections, runway holding positions, apron routing and terminal aprons. Each category has its own functional and visual requirements: select the sign face and illumination arrangement for the intended application. The standard colour scheme is white on red for mandatory instruction, yellow on black for location, and black on yellow for direction and information signs. Annex 14 deals with stand identification mainly through apron markings, so the airport signage plan sets the legend and format of stand signs. Under Annex 14, signs must be illuminated where used in runway visual range conditions below 800 m, at night with instrument runways, and at night with non-instrument runways of code number 3 or 4; elsewhere they may be retroreflective.

Luminance & Optical Uniformity

Readable Without Glare

Luminance

Provide sufficient contrast and readability without excessive glare. Requirements depend on:

  • Sign category, airport lighting environment
  • Day/night operation, viewing distance and angle
  • Background illumination, sign-face construction
  • Applicable standard and project requirements

Luminance and contrast are set by ICAO Annex 14 Appendix 4 and FAA AC 150/5345-44L, and must follow the airfield brightness steps so the sign never looks brighter or dimmer than the surrounding lights. Verify the finished sign against the applicable standard.

Optical Uniformity

Poor uniformity causes dark areas, bright spots, uneven character visibility, reduced contrast and inconsistent appearance between signs. Consider:

  • LED positioning, sign depth, reflective surfaces
  • Diffuser characteristics, legend construction
  • Internal geometry, LED output, power-supply regulation

Constant-Current Integration

Where signs operate within centralized airfield lighting infrastructure, the interface must be specifically engineered:

  • Series (constant-current, via isolating transformer) or parallel (constant-voltage) circuit supply
  • Constant-current regulator interfaces and brightness-step response
  • Sign load matched to the isolating-transformer rating, with its effect on circuit loading and power factor checked
  • Dedicated sign power supplies
  • Circuit-level and brightness control
  • Switching interfaces, isolation and protection

A conventional commercial LED supply should not be assumed connectable directly to an airfield lighting circuit.

Mounting & Frangibility

Support and Clearance

Mounting Configurations

  • Single- or multi-post mounting
  • Ground-mounted structures, bracket mounting
  • Project-specific support frames
  • Frangible support systems for all movement-area signs

Provide structural support while meeting aircraft and vehicle clearance requirements.

Frangibility

Signs in the movement area must be frangible and low enough to keep clearance for the propellers and engine pods of aircraft. Evaluate the complete support, not the sign face alone:

  • Enclosure, support posts, base assemblies
  • Fasteners, breakaway components
  • Foundation, cable routing
  • Sign height and edge clearance from the pavement

Height and distance from the pavement edge depend on sign type, runway or taxiway code and aerodrome criteria: confirm them with the approved signage plan.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures

FAQ

LED Illuminated Airfield Signage — Frequently Asked Questions

What is LED illuminated airfield signage?

LED illuminated signage gives clear, consistent visual information across movement areas and improves visibility during night operations, reduced visibility and low-light conditions. The range covers mandatory instruction, location, direction, information and stand identification signs, configured for luminance, electrical interface, dimensions, mounting and environmental protection. Conventional illuminated signs can be upgraded to LED, retaining suitable existing infrastructure where practical.

What colours do the different sign categories use?

The standard colour scheme is white on red for mandatory instruction signs, yellow on black for location signs, and black on yellow for direction and information signs. Sign colours and legend format are fixed by sign category under Annex 14 and FAA requirements and are not project choices. Annex 14 deals with stand identification mainly through apron markings, so the airport signage plan sets the legend and format of stand signs.

When must an airfield sign be illuminated?

Under Annex 14, signs must be illuminated where used in runway visual range conditions below 800 m, at night with instrument runways, and at night with non-instrument runways of code number 3 or 4. Elsewhere they may be retroreflective.

Which standards set sign luminance and uniformity?

Luminance and contrast are set by ICAO Annex 14 Appendix 4 and FAA AC 150/5345-44L, and they must follow the airfield brightness steps so the sign never looks brighter or dimmer than the surrounding lights. Poor optical uniformity causes dark areas, bright spots and uneven character visibility, so verify the finished sign against the applicable standard.

How is an LED sign connected to the airfield lighting circuit?

The supply may be a series (constant-current) circuit through an isolating transformer or a parallel (constant-voltage) circuit. The sign load must be matched to the isolating-transformer rating, with its effect on circuit loading and power factor checked, and dedicated sign power supplies, brightness response, switching and protection must be considered. A conventional commercial LED supply should not be assumed connectable directly to an airfield lighting circuit.

How are character height and sign size decided?

Dimensions are engineered from function and viewing distance, covering character height, number of characters and lines, arrows or symbols, border and mounting arrangement. Character height depends on the cockpit and vehicle-operator viewing position, sign location, taxiway or apron geometry, ambient lighting and the airport signage plan, so it is not a universal value.

What do mounting, frangibility and environmental construction involve?

Signs in the movement area must be frangible and low enough to keep clearance for the propellers and engine pods of aircraft. Evaluate the complete support, including enclosure, posts, base assemblies, fasteners, breakaway components and foundation, and confirm sign height and edge clearance from the pavement with the approved signage plan. The enclosure should also resist rain, dust, UV, temperature variation, humidity, wind and cleaning chemicals, with corrosion-resistant materials and sealed cable entries.

How are LED signs commissioned and maintained?

Commissioning checks the correct category, legend, orientation and location, character visibility, luminance and optical uniformity, electrical input and control operation, and mounting stability. Night-time testing is particularly useful for judging practical illumination and contrast. LED reduces lamp-replacement needs, but periodic cleaning and inspection remain necessary, and any reduction in illumination should be investigated before it affects operational visibility.

What are the common problems with LED illuminated signs?

Uneven illumination can come from LED module failure, optical degradation or power-supply problems, and reduced luminance from LED ageing or connection problems. A complete sign failure points to loss of supply, a power-supply failure, a circuit fault or a control-system fault. Excessive glare can come from incorrect luminance or optical design, and moisture ingress from damaged seals or poor cable entry, which should be addressed promptly.

What should be confirmed before specifying an LED sign?

Begin with the operational function: the sign category, the exact approved legend, the character height and dimensions, the LED illumination configuration, the electrical interface and control architecture, the mounting, height, foundation and frangibility, and the environment, including temperature, humidity, rain, dust, UV, wind, corrosion and aircraft-engine airflow.

Is it safe to work on a sign on an energised series circuit?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage, and opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death. De-energise the circuit at the regulator, apply the site lock-out / tag-out procedure, use the series circuit cut-out switch or an approved isolation device and work under the permit-to-work system. See the electrical safety warning on this page.
