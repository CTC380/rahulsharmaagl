SOP-341 Insulation Resistance Testing
Purpose

To verify the insulation condition of AGL electrical circuits, cables or equipment using an approved insulation-resistance test method.

Pre-Test Requirements

Verify:

  • Approved test procedure.
  • Circuit identification.
  • Electrical isolation.
  • LOTO.
  • Equipment disconnected/protected where required.
  • Suitable calibrated insulation tester.
  • Correct test voltage.
  • Test points identified.
  • Sensitive electronic equipment isolated/protected.
Procedure
  1. Obtain electrical work authorization.
  2. Identify circuit/equipment.
  3. Isolate circuit.
  4. Apply LOTO.
  5. Prove dead.
  6. Disconnect components that could be damaged by the test.
  7. Connect insulation tester.
  8. Apply approved test voltage.
  9. Record stabilized measurement according to the approved method.
  10. Stop test.
  11. Discharge stored electrical energy.
  12. Restore connections.
  13. Compare result with approved criterion.
  14. Record test result.

Important — Never apply an insulation-resistance test to connected electronic equipment unless the manufacturer specifically permits it.

SOP-352 CCR Current Testing
Purpose

To verify actual series-circuit current generated by the CCR.

Procedure
  1. Identify CCR/circuit.
  2. Verify safe test configuration.
  3. Never break the series loop or open a current-transformer secondary while energized to insert an instrument.
  4. Use calibrated suitable instrument.
  5. Operate CCR at approved intensity steps.
  6. Measure current at each required step.
  7. Record actual values.
  8. Compare against approved CCR settings/tolerances.
  9. Investigate abnormal deviations.

Important — For a series AGL circuit, current regulation is a fundamental operating parameter. A current reading must be interpreted together with the CCR setting and circuit condition.

B. Testing Quality Hold Points (HP-01 → HP-12)

Work shall not proceed past a hold point until the associated inspection or verification has been completed and released.

  • HP-01 — Test Documentation: Approved drawings, specifications and test procedures available.
  • HP-02 — Instrument Calibration: All measuring instruments have valid calibration status.
  • HP-03 — Electrical Safety: Isolation/LOTO/proving-dead requirements completed before applicable tests.
  • HP-04 — Cable Testing: Cable test results accepted before energization.
  • HP-05 — Transformer Testing: Transformer test results accepted.
  • HP-06 — CCR Testing: Input/output/current/voltage/protection verified.
  • HP-07 — Functional Testing: Equipment performs its intended function.
  • HP-08 — Photometry Setup: Measurement geometry and instrument setup verified.
  • HP-09 — Photometric Results: Results reviewed against the applicable criteria.
  • HP-10 — Corrective Action: Failed items repaired and retested.
  • HP-11 — Integrated Testing: Complete AGL chain successfully verified.
  • HP-12 — Final Acceptance: Controlled test report approved.

D. Common Testing Failures to Monitor

The following conditions should be specifically monitored during Division 17 testing:

  1. Testing Without Correct Isolation — Can expose personnel or equipment to hazardous energy.
  2. Insulation Testing With Electronics Connected — Can damage controllers, drivers, communication interfaces and electronic modules.
  3. Incorrect Earth Resistance Method — Parallel earth paths can produce misleading measurements.
  4. Wrong CCR Operating Step — Photometric measurements taken at the wrong intensity setting can produce invalid conclusions.
  5. Incorrect Photometry Geometry — Even a calibrated instrument can produce unreliable results if distance, height, orientation or reference geometry is wrong.
  6. Expired Calibration — Measurements may not be technically defensible if the instrument's calibration status is invalid.
  7. One-Point Photometry — One intensity measurement cannot necessarily establish full compliance with an applicable photometric distribution requirement.
  8. Ignoring Chromaticity — A light may appear visually acceptable while its measured chromaticity falls outside the applicable requirement.
  9. Testing Without Traceability — A result without Equipment ID + Location + Instrument + Date + Test Method + Acceptance Criterion has limited engineering value.
  10. No Retest After Repair — A repaired component should not automatically be considered compliant without the required verification.

Division 17 — AGL Testing Golden Rule

“A test result is meaningful only when the equipment, test method, instrument, calibration status, test condition, measurement geometry and acceptance criterion are all controlled and traceable. AGL testing shall progress from electrical integrity → equipment performance → control response → field operation → photometric performance → integrated system verification.”

Division 17 — SOP Approval Principle

Test methods, acceptance criteria, test voltages, measurement locations, instrument requirements and photometric limits shall be taken from the approved project specifications, applicable ICAO/FAA/DGCA requirements, relevant IS/IEC standards, equipment manufacturer's instructions and approved airport maintenance procedures.

Generic numerical acceptance values shall not be substituted where the applicable standard or approved design specifies a different criterion — approved project specifications and applicable standards shall always take precedence.

Reference Basis

Technical references behind the procedures in this division:

  • ICAO Annex 14, Vol. I — Aerodrome Design and Operations, Appendix 1 (colours) and Appendix 2 (light characteristics) — colour and intensity criteria against which measurements are assessed.
  • ICAO Doc 9157, Aerodrome Design Manual, Part 4 — photometric measurement guidance.
  • FAA AC 150/5345-46, AC 150/5345-28 and AC 150/5345-44 — equipment photometric requirements.
  • FAA AC 150/5340-26 and IEC 61821 — maintenance testing of series circuits.
  • CIE 121 and CIE 15 — photometry and goniophotometry; colorimetry.
  • IEC 61557 series and IEC 60364-6 — insulation, continuity and earth-resistance test equipment; verification of installations.
  • ISO/IEC 17025 — competence and traceability of calibration and test laboratories.
  • FAA AC 150/5345-53 — certified equipment.

References are given at document or chapter level for technical orientation. Confirm the current edition, amendments and national adoption (for example DGCA CAR Section 4, FAA Advisory Circulars or EASA material) for each project. Approved project specifications, manufacturer instructions and the airport's own procedures take precedence, as stated in the precedence note above.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Testing & Photometry SOPs in the AGL SOP Library.

Q1What is Division 17 — Testing & Photometry SOPs?

Division 17 is a set of 27 procedures (SOP-341 to SOP-367, Rev 0) for AGL electrical testing, cable and transformer testing, CCR input, output, current and voltage testing, and functional testing of runway, taxiway, approach, PAPI and signage circuits. It also covers photometric testing (intensity, beam alignment, chromaticity), instrumentation, calibration verification, test reporting and integrated AGL system testing.

Q2Where do test methods and acceptance criteria come from?

The approved project specifications, applicable ICAO, FAA and DGCA requirements, relevant IS/IEC standards, manufacturer's instructions and approved airport maintenance procedures. A generic numerical acceptance value must not be used where the applicable standard or approved design specifies a different criterion.

Q3What must be in place before an insulation resistance test?

An approved test procedure, circuit identification, electrical isolation and LOTO, equipment disconnected or protected where required, a calibrated insulation tester, the correct test voltage, identified test points and protection for sensitive electronic equipment (SOP-341). The circuit is proved dead before testing begins.

Q4What does photometric testing involve?

SOP-360 measures the photometric performance of AGL light sources such as runway, taxiway and approach lights, PAPI and signs where applicable. Before testing, verify the approved method, applicable standard, test geometry, instrument and its calibration status, ambient conditions, fixture condition, CCR setting and test location. Then set the approved CCR intensity, position the instrument and record the measurements.

Q5Is a valid calibration certificate enough before using a photometric instrument?

No. Calibration validity and pre-use verification are different controls (SOP-365). Check the instrument ID, certificate, calibration and due dates, measurement range and detector condition, perform a pre-use reference check if required, and remove the instrument from service if calibration is expired or questionable.

Q6What does integrated AGL system testing cover?

SOP-367 verifies the complete chain from incoming supply and CCR through series circuits, cables, transformers, secondary circuits and earthing to the control scope (ALCMS, PLC, HMI, remote control and communication) and the field equipment. Performance checks include operation, intensity, alignment, chromaticity where applicable, alarm response and feedback.
