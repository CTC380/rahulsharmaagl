Initial Issue

Important — Commissioning shall be performed only against the approved IFC/as-built design, approved equipment submittals, project specifications, approved test procedures, manufacturer's instructions and applicable aerodrome/regulatory requirements. Commissioning is not simply "switching the system ON"; it is the controlled demonstration that the installed AGL system is safe, correctly installed, electrically sound, functionally operational, photometrically compliant and fully documented.

SOP-402 Circuit Energization
Purpose

To safely energize an AGL circuit for commissioning.

Preconditions

Confirm:

  • Circuit identification.
  • Test results accepted.
  • Personnel clear.
  • Tools removed.
  • Covers installed.
  • Connections complete.
  • Earthing complete.
  • Correct CCR assignment.
  • ATC/Operations coordination.
  • Energization authorization.
Procedure
  1. Conduct pre-energization briefing.
  2. Confirm circuit.
  3. Verify work area.
  4. Verify the series loop is complete (no open circuit) and that insulation-resistance results are accepted.
  5. Remove LOTO through approved procedure.
  6. Energize CCR/circuit under controlled conditions.
  7. Monitor current/voltage.
  8. Check abnormal alarms.
  9. Observe field equipment.
  10. De-energize if abnormal condition occurs.
  11. Record result.

Division 19 — AGL Commissioning Golden Rule

"Commissioning is the controlled demonstration that the installed AGL system is exactly what was designed, safely energized, electrically sound, correctly controlled, operationally functional, photometrically compliant, properly documented and ready for long-term maintenance. No system should be considered fully commissioned merely because it can be switched ON."

Division 19 — SOP Approval Principle

Commissioning shall be performed only against the approved IFC/as-built design, approved equipment submittals, project specifications, approved test procedures, manufacturer's instructions and applicable aerodrome/regulatory requirements.

Commissioning is not simply "switching the system ON" — it is the controlled demonstration that the installed AGL system is safe, correctly installed, electrically sound, functionally operational, photometrically compliant and fully documented.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Commissioning & Acceptance SOPs in the AGL SOP Library.

Q1What is Division 19 — Commissioning & Acceptance SOPs?

Division 19 is a set of 25 procedures (SOP-393 to SOP-417, Rev 0) for taking an installed AGL system from pre-commissioning readiness through verification of equipment, cables, transformers, fixtures, signs, CCRs and earthing. It continues through controlled energization, individual and integrated system testing, functional, photometric, ALCMS, PAPI and signage acceptance, punch-point management, final acceptance, as-built verification and client and O&M handover.

Q2Is commissioning just switching the system on?

No. Commissioning is the controlled demonstration that the installed AGL system is safe, correctly installed, electrically sound, functionally operational, photometrically compliant and fully documented. It is performed only against the approved IFC/as-built design, approved submittals, project specifications, approved test procedures, manufacturer's instructions and aerodrome or regulatory requirements.

Q3What must be confirmed before energizing a circuit for commissioning?

Circuit identification, accepted test results, personnel clear, tools removed, covers installed, connections and earthing complete, correct CCR assignment, ATC/Operations coordination and energization authorization (SOP-402). The series loop must be verified complete with no open circuit and insulation-resistance results accepted before LOTO is removed through the approved procedure.

Q4When must controlled energization stop?

Stop immediately for abnormal current, unexpected voltage, an earth fault, smoke or odor, equipment overheating, unexpected fixture behavior, an abnormal CCR alarm, or a communication or control failure that creates an unsafe condition (SOP-403).

Q5How are punch points categorized?

Category A (Critical) means potential safety or operational impact. Category B (Major) is a significant technical or functional deficiency. Category C (Minor) is a cosmetic, documentary or minor technical deficiency that does not prevent operation, subject to approved acceptance. The punch list records item number, location, equipment, description, category, responsible party, target closure date, evidence required and status (SOP-411).

Q6What is required before final acceptance?

Installation and testing complete; photometry, ALCMS, PAPI and signs accepted; punch points closed or approved; as-built and O&M documents complete; training completed where required; flight inspection completed where required (for example PAPI and approach lighting); and regulatory or aerodrome approval for operational use, such as DGCA/NOTAM, obtained where required (SOP-413).

Important Notice, Disclaimer & Limitation of Liability

Please read before relying on any content on this page or on any SOP downloaded from it.

Specific to this division: Testing, inspection, measurement and commissioning content here is generic. It is not a certification, calibration, accredited test, flight inspection or acceptance. Test voltages, pass/fail criteria and photometric or electrical limits are indicative; use the approved test procedure, the current regulatory requirements and the manufacturer’s data. Acceptance, handover and declaration of serviceability rest with the aerodrome operator and competent authority, and a completed template is not evidence of compliance.
  1. Purpose and intended users. These SOPs are independent educational and reference templates for competent, suitably trained and authorised airfield ground lighting (AGL) and aerodrome electrical personnel. They are not professional engineering, legal, safety or regulatory advice, and using them does not create any professional or client relationship.
  2. Safety. Work on series circuits, constant current regulators (CCRs), isolating transformers, cables and connectors can be fatal. Always apply the aerodrome’s permit-to-work and isolation/LOTO procedures, prove dead, use correctly rated instruments and PPE, and follow the manufacturer’s instructions. Nothing here authorises work on live equipment or the bypassing of any safety device or interlock.
  3. Not approved or controlled documents. The SOPs, their numbers and their revision labels are generic templates. They are not approved, issued or controlled documents of any aerodrome, airline, contractor or regulator. Before use, your organisation must review them against its own equipment, hazards and legal duties, adapt them, and approve and issue them under its own document-control and safety management system.   4. Aviation operations. Nothing on this page authorises return to service, closure or opening of any aerodrome area, a change of operating category, a NOTAM or any aerodrome certification decision.
