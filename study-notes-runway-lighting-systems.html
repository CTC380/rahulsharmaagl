RUNWAY LIGHTING SYSTEMS COMPLETE NOTES

Runway lighting provides visual guidance associated with the runway during aircraft take-off and landing operations. Different runway lights perform different functions, and the easiest way to study them is to connect four things:

1

RUNWAY EDGE LIGHTS

Runway edge lights define the lateral limits of the runway and help pilots identify the usable runway width. In FAA guidance they are classified by intensity:

  • HIRL: High Intensity Runway Lights
  • MIRL: Medium Intensity Runway Lights
  • LIRL: Low Intensity Runway Lights

FAA guidance states that they are normally white, with yellow replacing white on the last 2,000 ft or half the runway length, whichever is less, on instrument runways. ICAO Annex 14 uses a different rule: the section 600 m or one-third of the runway length, whichever is less, at the remote end (from where the take-off run starts) shows yellow, and the lights between the runway start and a displaced threshold show red in the approach direction. Indian aerodromes follow ICAO-based provisions, so check the applicable AIP before assuming the FAA distance.

Exam point
  • White = runway edge guidance
  • FAA yellow caution zone (instrument runways) = last 2,000 ft or half the runway, whichever is less
  • ICAO yellow caution zone = last 600 m or one-third of the runway, whichever is less
  • ICAO displaced threshold: edge lights between the runway start and the displaced threshold show red in the approach direction
  • The yellow edge lights are bidirectional: white to the aircraft that has the yellow section behind it, yellow to the aircraft rolling toward it

Study method: Runway Edge → Lateral Limits → White. The intensity classification tells you the system's intensity level, while the colour provides visual runway guidance.

Runway lights are normally supplied from CCR-fed series circuits through isolation transformers; see Note 05, Note 07 and Note 08.

2

THRESHOLD LIGHTS

Threshold lights identify the beginning of the landing portion of the runway for an approaching aircraft. For FAA configurations they are green when viewed from the approach side.

Remember
  • Green = Threshold
  • Threshold → Beginning of landing portion → Green

ICAO Annex 14 specifies threshold lights as fixed, unidirectional green lights in a line at right angles to the runway axis at the threshold (at the displaced threshold where one exists). Wing bars, extra green lights on each side of the threshold, may be added for conspicuity, particularly on precision approach runways. The FAA achieves the same result with bidirectional green/red fixtures (see runway end lights below).

Study the threshold as both a physical runway reference point and a visual lighting indication.

3

RUNWAY END LIGHTS

Runway end lighting identifies the end of the usable runway. FAA practice uses bidirectional fixtures whose colour depends on viewing direction, so one fixture serves as the threshold light for one direction and the end light for the other:

Red when viewed from the runway

Seen by an aircraft on the runway, looking toward the runway end.

Green when viewed from the approach side

Seen by an aircraft approaching, where it serves as the threshold indication.

Exam point
  • Red = Runway End
  • Green = Threshold

ICAO Annex 14 specifies runway end lights as fixed, unidirectional red lights visible in the direction of the runway, in a line at right angles to the axis, consisting of at least six lights. ICAO threshold (green) and end (red) lights may be separate or combined in one fixture. Where a stopway is provided, ICAO also specifies stopway lights: fixed, unidirectional red lights facing the runway.

Memory link: think of the runway-end fixture as a bidirectional visual indication, not one colour from every direction.

4

RUNWAY CENTRELINE LIGHTS

Centreline lights provide accurate runway alignment and a distance-remaining cue in the final part of the runway, particularly in reduced visibility. They are required on CAT II and CAT III precision approach runways and may be provided on other runways, for example where RVR for take-off is low.

Spacing: FAA centreline lights are spaced at 50 ft (15 m). ICAO specifies approximately 15 m, and approximately 30 m is permitted on runways intended for use in RVR conditions of 350 m or greater.

Centreline colour sequence (FAA and ICAO)

Viewed from the landing threshold toward the far end: White → Alternating Red/White → Red. The FAA and ICAO sequences are essentially the same pattern, because 3,000 ft ≈ 914 m and 1,000 ft ≈ 305 m. Only the units and rounding differ.

ZoneFAA (distance remaining)ICAO Annex 14 / India (distance remaining)Colour
Main sectionMore than 3,000 ftMore than 900 mWhite
Caution section3,000 ft to 1,000 ft (2,000 ft long)900 m to 300 m (600 m long)Alternating Red/White
Final sectionLast 1,000 ftLast 300 mRed
Exam tip
  • FAA: 3,000 ft white, then 2,000 ft alternating, then 1,000 ft red
  • ICAO / India: white to 900 m from the end, alternating 900 m to 300 m, red for the last 300 m
  • ICAO exception: on runways shorter than 1,800 m, the alternating red/white section starts at the midpoint of the runway and runs to 300 m from the end
  • Seen from the opposite direction the same lights give the sequence for that direction, so the fixtures are bidirectional white/white, white/red or red/red
Standard note. Do not mix units or standards in an answer. Name the reference (FAA AC 150/5340-30 and AIM, ICAO Annex 14, the aerodrome's AIP, or the project specification) and quote its values. Indian aerodromes follow ICAO Annex 14 through DGCA CAR Section 4, so the metric 900/300 m values are the ICAO standard as applied in India, not a separate Indian rule. Always confirm against the current AIP entry for the aerodrome.

Always identify what the question is asking for: FAA requirement? ICAO requirement? Indian AIP? Airport specification? Project-specific requirement? This distinction is particularly important in examinations and technical documentation.

5

TOUCHDOWN ZONE LIGHTS (TDZL)

TDZL provide visual identification of the touchdown zone on applicable precision approach runways. FAA guidance describes rows of transverse light bars symmetrically disposed around the centreline, using steady-burning white lights, giving a clear indication of the touchdown-zone area during landing.

Extent: FAA TDZL start 100 ft (30 m) beyond the threshold and extend to 3,000 ft (900 m) from the threshold, or to the runway midpoint where the runway is shorter than 6,000 ft (1,800 m). ICAO Annex 14 uses the same 900 m / midpoint rule, with short barrettes of three fixed white lights. TDZL are unidirectional, facing the landing threshold, and are required on CAT II and CAT III runways.

Remember
  • TDZL = Touchdown Zone Lights
  • TDZL → Touchdown Zone → White
6

REIL

Runway End Identifier Lights. REILs are a pair of synchronised white flashing lights, one on each side of the runway at the threshold and facing the approach, giving rapid and positive identification of the approach end of a runway. They are especially useful where the runway has little contrast with surrounding terrain or lights, or in reduced visibility. ICAO calls the equivalent aid runway threshold identification lights (white flashing, 60 to 120 flashes per minute).

Exam point
  • REIL = Rapid Runway-End Identification
  • REIL → Identify runway end quickly

Do not confuse REIL with the runway-end lights themselves. Despite the name, REIL marks the approach end (threshold) of the runway, not the far end. It is a separate visual aid, white and flashing, intended specifically for rapid identification of the runway being approached.

7

RETIL

Rapid Exit Taxiway Indicator Lights. RETILs give distance-to-go information to the nearest rapid exit taxiway and can improve situational awareness during landing and rollout in applicable low-visibility or heavy-traffic conditions.

RETIL is an ICAO Annex 14 aid. The lights are fixed, unidirectional yellow lights installed in the runway next to the centreline, on the same side as the associated rapid exit taxiway. They form a 3-2-1 sequence of sets about 100 m apart (six lights in total), counting down to the point of tangency of the exit taxiway centreline. ICAO recommends them on runways intended for use in RVR below 350 m and/or where traffic density is heavy. Check the aerodrome's AIP entry to see whether RETILs are installed on a given runway.

REIL

Runway End Identification: white flashing lights at the threshold that identify the approach end.

RETIL

Rapid Exit Taxiway Indicator: steady yellow 3-2-1 sets that indicate distance to go to the rapid exit.

Exam Questions

EXAM QUESTIONS

What is the function of runway edge lights?

Runway edge lights define the lateral limits of the runway and provide visual runway-edge guidance.

What colour are runway threshold lights when viewed from the approach side?

Green, in both FAA and ICAO practice.

What do runway-end lights identify?

The end of the usable runway. They show red to an aircraft on the runway looking toward the runway end.

What is the runway centreline colour sequence?

White, then alternating red and white, then red. FAA: white until the last 3,000 ft, alternating for the next 2,000 ft, red for the last 1,000 ft. ICAO Annex 14 (followed in India): white to 900 m from the runway end, alternating from 900 m to 300 m, red for the last 300 m.

What are TDZL?

Touchdown Zone Lights: steady white lights in the touchdown zone of precision approach runways, extending 900 m (3,000 ft) from the threshold or to the runway midpoint, whichever is less.

What does REIL stand for?

Runway End Identifier Lights.

What is the purpose of REIL?

To provide rapid and positive identification of the approach end (threshold) of a runway using synchronised white flashing lights.

What does RETIL stand for?

Rapid Exit Taxiway Indicator Lights.

What is the purpose of RETIL?

To give distance-to-go information to the nearest rapid exit taxiway, using yellow unidirectional lights in a 3-2-1 pattern, and to support situational awareness during landing and rollout in low visibility or heavy traffic.

What is the difference between REIL and RETIL?

REIL identifies the approach end of the runway with white flashes. RETIL gives distance-to-go to a rapid exit taxiway using yellow lights in a 3-2-1 pattern.
