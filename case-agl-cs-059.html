AGL-CS-059 — Blue Taxiway Light Photometric Failure

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A photometric investigation where a blue taxiway edge light failed to meet ICAO intensity and chromaticity requirements despite normal electrical operation — from photometer verification through optical inspection, LED module analysis, lens contamination assessment, and corrective optical restoration.

1Case Study Overview

During a scheduled photometric inspection of taxiway edge lighting, one blue taxiway edge light was found to have failed photometric performance requirements under ICAO Annex 14, Volume I, Chapter 5.

The Constant Current Regulator (CCR) was operating normally at the selected 6.6 A current, the fixture was electrically functional and appeared visually blue, but photometric measurements showed insufficient intensity and incorrect chromaticity coordinates.

A comprehensive optical investigation was initiated covering photometer calibration, fixture positioning, lens condition, LED module performance, optical-system contamination, and chromaticity verification.

The investigation identified inadvertent use of an incorrect LED module during a previous replacement, combined with progressive optical degradation of the lens assembly, which reduced both output intensity and shifted the chromaticity outside the specified blue color band.

This case demonstrates that a blue taxiway light can appear visually correct while failing photometric requirements — and electrical functionality does not guarantee optical compliance.

3System Background

A blue taxiway edge light provides:

  • Blue taxiway edge delineation.
  • ICAO-compliant chromaticity.
  • Minimum intensity requirements.
  • Uniform beam pattern.
  • Omnidirectional or directional guidance.

The optical system consists of:

LED Module → Optical Lens → Light-Emitting Aperture → Blue Chromatic Output

Photometric requirements for blue taxiway edge lights under ICAO Annex 14 include:

  • Chromaticity coordinates within the defined blue color band.
  • Minimum intensity values at specified angles.
  • Beam spread and uniformity.
  • Intensity ratios between adjacent lights.

Common photometric failure causes include:

  • Incorrect LED module.
  • Lens contamination.
  • Optical degradation.
  • Incorrect fixture alignment.
  • Photometer calibration error.
  • Incorrect current setting.

  • Photometric test failed.
  • Intensity below ICAO minimum.
  • Chromaticity outside blue color band.
  • Adjacent blue taxiway lights passing.

7Photometric Diagnostic Principle

Normal photometric performance:

Correct LED module → Clean lens → Correct alignment → ICAO-compliant intensity and chromaticity

Photometric failure:

Incorrect LED module → Contaminated lens → Optical degradation → Reduced intensity → Chromaticity shift → ICAO non-compliance

Visual appearance is not a reliable indicator of photometric compliance.

The photometer was:

Step 4: Measure Chromaticity

Measured chromaticity coordinates (x, y):

x = 0.210, y = 0.160

ICAO blue color band requirements:

x between 0.136 and 0.220, y between 0.070 and 0.200

The chromaticity shifted outside the acceptable blue color band.

Step 5: LED Module Inspection

Inspection revealed:

  • LED module part number did not match the approved specification.
  • Module was an unapproved replacement type.
  • Primary wavelength was shifted toward cyan.
  • Output flux was lower than the original specification.

An incorrect LED module had been installed during a previous maintenance action.

Step 6: Lens and Optical Assembly Inspection

The lens showed:

  • Surface contamination.
  • Fine particulate deposits.
  • Minor abrasions.
  • Reduced optical transmission.
  • UV degradation.

The optical path was partially obstructed and degraded.

Step 7: Fixture Alignment Verification

Alignment was found to be:

  • Within acceptable tolerances.
  • Not contributing to the photometric failure.

The alignment was correct.

Step 8: Reference Comparison

Testing an adjacent blue taxiway light with the same photometer:

  • Intensity: 12.4 cd (PASS).
  • Chromaticity: within ICAO blue color band (PASS).

The adjacent fixture confirmed that the problem was localized to the failed fixture.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The blue taxiway light photometric failure was caused by installation of an incorrect, unapproved LED module during a previous replacement, combined with progressive optical degradation of the lens assembly through contamination and UV exposure.

The combined effects produced:

  • Reduced luminous flux.
  • Wavelength shift away from ICAO blue.
  • Optical transmission loss.
  • Chromaticity shift outside the blue color band.
  • Intensity below ICAO minimum.

The fixture was electrically functional but optically non-compliant.

11Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Removed the incorrect LED module.
  2. Cleaned the lens and optical assembly.
  3. Installed an approved ICAO-compliant blue LED module.
  4. Replaced the degraded lens assembly.
  5. Verified photometric intensity.
  6. Verified chromaticity coordinates.
  7. Checked optical alignment.
  8. Performed full photometric testing.
  9. Updated maintenance records and parts inventory.
  10. Implemented LED module verification procedure.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: Blue taxiway edge light operational. Visually appears blue. Photometric test shows intensity below ICAO minimum and chromaticity outside blue color band. CCR healthy. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace the CCR.
B. Increase the current setting.
C. Verify LED module specification, inspect the optical assembly for contamination and degradation, and confirm photometer calibration.
D. Replace the entire taxiway light fixture.
Correct answer: C. The photometric failure with a functional fixture and healthy CCR points to an optical-system problem. LED module verification, optical inspection, and photometer verification will identify the cause without unnecessary equipment replacement.

Quick Reference: Taxiway Lighting Photometric Test

A taxiway lighting photometric test measures the intensity and colour of a taxiway light against the required values. For blue taxiway edge lights, a lens or filter that has faded or is dirty can cause a failure even when the light is on, so check the lens condition before replacing the unit.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about blue taxiway light photometric failure, based on this case study.

Q1What is AGL-CS-059?

AGL-CS-059 is an engineering training case study on a blue taxiway edge light that looked normal but failed photometric testing under ICAO Annex 14, Volume I, Chapter 5. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2How can a light look blue and still fail?

The fixture was electrically functional and visually blue, but measurements showed insufficient intensity and chromaticity coordinates outside the blue color band. Adjacent blue lights passed.

Q3What was the root cause?

An incorrect, unapproved LED module installed during a previous replacement, combined with progressive lens degradation from contamination and UV exposure. This reduced luminous flux, shifted the wavelength away from ICAO blue and lowered optical transmission.

Q4What should you investigate first?

Verify the LED module specification, inspect the optical assembly for contamination and degradation, and confirm photometer calibration. Replacing the CCR or the whole fixture, or increasing the current setting, is not the right first step.

Q5Which test equipment was used?

The team used a calibrated photometer, colorimeter or spectrometer, lens inspection equipment, LED module test equipment, a true-RMS multimeter, current measurement equipment and alignment verification tools.

Q6How was the light corrected?

The team removed the incorrect LED module, cleaned the optical assembly, installed an approved ICAO-compliant blue LED module and replaced the degraded lens assembly. They verified intensity, chromaticity and alignment, performed full photometric testing, updated records and implemented an LED module verification procedure.

Q7How was the correction verified?

The module specification was correct, the lens was restored, intensity was at least 10.0 cd and chromaticity was within the ICAO blue band. The light passed all photometric requirements.

Q8Why verify LED module specification during replacement?

The failure began with an unapproved module installed during a previous replacement. The case therefore added an LED module verification procedure to stop incorrect modules entering service.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

A blue taxiway light photometric failure is an optical-performance problem caused by incorrect LED modules, optical contamination, or lens degradation, and it can create ICAO non-compliance and operational guidance hazards even when the electrical system is fully functional and the fixture appears visually normal.

Systematic photometer calibration verification, intensity measurement, chromaticity testing, LED module specification verification, optical assembly inspection, and corrective optical restoration can identify and eliminate blue taxiway light photometric failures.

In AGL engineering, a light that looks blue to the human eye can still fail ICAO chromaticity — the photometer is the only reliable witness.
