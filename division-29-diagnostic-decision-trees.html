Diagnostic Decision Trees

A consolidated set of structured, symptom-to-fix decision trees spanning the full AGL system — CCR start-up, trip, earth-fault, overtemperature and output-current diagnosis; series circuit, cable, insulation, transformer, connector and fixture-level fault isolation; and sign, PAPI, approach lighting, stop bar, photometry, and ALCMS communication troubleshooting. Each tree follows the same structured logic as the rest of the Atlas: symptom → observation → measurement → decision → fault location → root cause → verification → corrective action → preventive action, so a technician can walk straight from a reported fault to a confirmed fix.

DDT-661 CCR Not Starting Decision Tree

Symptom — CCR fails to start when commanded from ALCMS or the local panel — no output, no run indication, and the unit remains dead or trips immediately on the start command.

Equipment / System

Constant Current Regulator (CCR) — SCR or IGBT-based — its control/logic board, main incoming supply, and start/enable circuitry.

Possible Causes
HighMain incoming supply breaker tripped or missing phase upstream of the CCR
HighControl fuse blown on the CCR logic/control power supply
MedLocal/Remote selector switch in the wrong position or an E-stop engaged
MedALCMS start command not reaching the CCR due to a comms fault
LowInternal control board fault preventing the start sequence
LowOutput contactor or relay failed to pull in
Required Tools
Multimeter (CAT III)CCR technical manual and wiring diagramALCMS status screenClamp-on ammeter
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the CCR before any internal access.
  • Verify the incoming supply is de-energised before opening the cabinet.
  • Treat the CCR as live until confirmed isolated.
Decision Tree / Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCheck ALCMS for the CCR's reported status and any active alarm/fault code before touching the unit.
  2. ObservationConfirm the local/remote selector position and that no E-stop or interlock is engaged at the panel.
  3. MeasurementWith the cabinet open and supply confirmed present, verify incoming supply voltage at the main breaker and confirm the control power supply fuse/breaker is intact.
  4. MeasurementAttempt a local start command and observe whether the control logic energises (indicator lamps, display) even if the output contactor does not close.
  5. DecisionControl logic does not power up at all → incoming supply/control fuse fault. Control logic powers up but the output does not close → contactor/output stage or start-sequence fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the incoming supply, control fuse, selector/interlock circuit, or output contactor/relay.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific failed component before replacement.
Test Points
Incoming supply voltage at the main breaker
Control power supply fuse continuity
Selector switch / E-stop circuit continuity
Output contactor coil voltage on start command
Root Cause Verification

After repair, start the CCR through a full run cycle at each intensity step and confirm ALCMS reports normal running status.

Corrective Action
  • Reset or replace the tripped breaker after confirming no downstream fault.
  • Replace the blown control fuse.
  • Correct the selector switch or E-stop position.
  • Repair the ALCMS-to-CCR comms link.
  • Replace a faulty output contactor or control board.
Preventive Action
  • Include a CCR start-sequence test in the periodic PM cycle.
  • Verify the comms link integrity periodically.
  • Check E-stop/interlock wiring for damage during PM.
Related References

SOP: SOP-CCR-01 (CCR Start-up and Verification Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-10, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

Case Study: Division 01 — CCR Troubleshooting

Rev 0 — Initial issue
DDT-662 CCR Trip Decision Tree

Symptom — CCR starts but trips (shuts down) within seconds to minutes of energising, either on start or during the run, without an operator command.

Equipment / System

CCR overcurrent, short-circuit, and general protection trip circuitry, output stage, and the series circuit it feeds.

Possible Causes
HighHard short circuit or ground fault on the downstream series circuit
HighOutput stage (SCR/IGBT) overcurrent trip from a genuine circuit fault
MedNuisance trip from an undersized or misconfigured protection setpoint
MedA failing output component causing the CCR to self-protect
LowCooling fan failure allowing internal temperature to rise and trip on secondary protection
LowLoose internal connection causing a transient that trips protection
Required Tools
Clamp-on ammeterInsulation resistance tester (megger)CCR trip/event logCCR technical manualThermal imaging camera
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before investigating internal trip circuitry.
  • Treat the series circuit as a possible short/ground fault until proven otherwise.
  • Do not repeatedly reset the CCR without investigating the trip cause.
Decision Tree / Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the CCR trip log/event history to identify which protection function (overcurrent, ground fault, overtemperature) initiated the trip and whether it is immediate or delayed.
  2. ObservationNote whether the trip occurs on every start attempt or is intermittent.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the full series loop to check for a hard short or ground fault before re-energising.
  4. MeasurementIf insulation resistance is acceptable, re-energise briefly under supervision and clamp-meter the output current against the circuit's expected load.
  5. DecisionMegger reveals a hard fault on the loop → route to the Series Circuit Failure or Cable Earth Fault trees. Megger is clean and output current is within range but the trip persists → CCR internal protection/output stage fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the downstream circuit or to the CCR's own protection/output stage.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific fault — external short vs. internal CCR fault — before returning the unit to service.
Test Points
Series loop insulation resistance
Output current at the moment of trip
Trip log timestamp and protection function
Cooling fan operation
Root Cause Verification

After repair, run the CCR through a full brightness-step cycle without a repeat trip and confirm the event log is clear.

Corrective Action
  • Repair the identified circuit short or ground fault.
  • Replace the faulty output component.
  • Adjust the protection setpoint per manufacturer specification if confirmed a nuisance trip.
  • Replace a failed cooling fan.
Preventive Action
  • Trend CCR trip logs to catch developing faults before hard failure.
  • Include protection setpoint verification in commissioning and PM.
  • Keep the cooling system clean and unobstructed.
Related References

SOP: SOP-CCR-02 (CCR Trip Investigation Procedure), SOP-CCR-04

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-10, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

Case Study: Division 29 — Series Circuit Failure Decision Tree

Rev 0 — Initial issue
DDT-663 CCR Earth Fault Decision Tree

Symptom — CCR earth-fault (ground-fault) protection trips or alarms, indicating current leakage to ground somewhere on the series circuit or within the CCR itself.

Equipment / System

CCR earth-fault/ground-fault detection circuitry and the series circuit loop (cable, joints, isolation transformers) it protects.

Possible Causes
HighMoisture ingress into a cable joint, pull box, or base can degrading insulation to ground
HighA failing isolation transformer winding with insulation breakdown to its case/ground
MedDamaged cable jacket exposing the conductor to a grounded path
MedThe CCR's own internal earth-fault detection circuit malfunctioning (false trip)
LowA recently disturbed section (excavation, base can work) with a marginal connection to ground
LowCumulative insulation ageing across an older section of the circuit
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)CCR earth-fault trend logCable route and joint location drawingTDR / fault locatorMoisture detection meter
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the CCR before any insulation testing or cable work.
  • Treat a genuine earth-fault indication as a real hazard, not a nuisance, and do not simply reset without investigating.
Decision Tree / Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the CCR's earth-fault trend log to see whether the fault is a hard trip or a gradually worsening marginal reading.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the primary loop in segments from accessible pull boxes or base cans.
  3. MeasurementCompare each segment's reading against its baseline/previous test value to identify the segment with the greatest drop.
  4. DecisionDrop isolated to one segment, joint, or transformer → localised repair. Drop appears at the CCR terminals with the field loop disconnected → CCR internal detection fault.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific cable segment, joint, or transformer winding.
  6. Root CauseConfirm with a full-loop re-test that insulation resistance is restored to, or above, the acceptable threshold.
Test Points
Segment-by-segment insulation resistance vs. baseline
Individual transformer winding insulation
CCR earth-fault trip log
Joint/base can condition in the lowest-reading segment
Root Cause Verification

Re-megger the full loop after repair and monitor the CCR earth-fault trend over subsequent operating cycles.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the degraded cable segment or joint.
  • Replace the transformer with the failing winding.
  • Reseal an identified moisture entry point.
  • Replace the CCR earth-fault detection module if confirmed internally faulty.
Preventive Action
  • Schedule periodic full-loop insulation resistance testing and trend results.
  • Prioritise ageing sections for proactive replacement.
  • Seal known moisture entry points during PM.
Related References

SOP: SOP-CCR-03 (Earth Fault Investigation Procedure), SOP-CCR-04

Standards: IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

DDT-664 CCR Overtemperature Decision Tree

Symptom — CCR alarms or trips on overtemperature protection, either shortly after starting or progressively during extended operation.

Equipment / System

CCR cooling system (fans, vents, heat sinks), output stage, and enclosure/cabinet environment.

Possible Causes
HighCooling fan failure or a blocked/dirty air filter restricting airflow
HighEnclosure installed or operating in an ambient environment exceeding design temperature (poor ventilation, direct sun, blocked louvres)
MedAn output stage component (SCR/IGBT) degrading and generating excess heat at normal load
MedA loose high-current connection creating a localised hot spot
LowTemperature sensor/thermostat miscalibrated, causing a false trip
LowCCR operating at a load/duty beyond its rated capacity for the circuit
Required Tools
Thermal imaging cameraClamp-on ammeterCCR technical manualCabinet thermometerTorque wrench for connection checks
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before internal inspection.
  • Allow the unit to cool before opening if recently tripped on overtemperature.
  • Use insulated tools when checking live connections.
Decision Tree / Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the trip/alarm log to confirm overtemperature is the actual protection function and note the time-to-trip from start.
  2. ObservationCheck the cabinet/enclosure environment for blocked vents, direct heat sources, or inadequate clearance.
  3. MeasurementWith the unit isolated, inspect and test the cooling fan(s) and check the air filter for blockage.
  4. MeasurementThermal-image the output stage and all high-current connections during a supervised run to locate a hot spot.
  5. DecisionHot spot at a specific connection or component → localised fault, repair/replace. No localised hot spot but overall cabinet temperature is high → ventilation/ambient issue.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the cooling system, a specific connection/component, or the installation environment.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the temperature returns to normal operating range under full load after correction.
Test Points
Cooling fan operation and airflow
Air filter condition
Thermal image of output stage and connections
Cabinet ambient temperature
Root Cause Verification

Run the CCR at full rated load for an extended period post-repair and confirm temperature stabilises within normal range without re-tripping.

Corrective Action
  • Replace a failed fan or clean/replace the air filter.
  • Re-torque or replace a loose high-current connection.
  • Replace a degraded output component.
  • Improve enclosure ventilation or shading.
  • Recalibrate or replace the temperature sensor.
Preventive Action
  • Clean and inspect the cooling system and filters on a defined PM interval.
  • Include thermal imaging of connections in PM.
  • Verify enclosure ventilation clearances at commissioning.
Related References

SOP: SOP-CCR-05 (Overtemperature Investigation Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-10

Case Study: Division 29 — CCR Output Current Decision Tree

Rev 0 — Initial issue
DDT-665 CCR Output Current Decision Tree

Symptom — CCR output current reads incorrect (too high, too low, unstable, or zero) at a given intensity step, without necessarily tripping.

Equipment / System

CCR output regulation and current feedback circuitry, and the series circuit it supplies.

Possible Causes
HighSeries circuit has fewer or more load points than expected (fixture added/removed, or a fault reducing effective load)
HighCurrent feedback/sensing transformer or shunt inaccurate or failed
MedOutput regulation control loop fault (SCR firing/IGBT control) preventing correct current output
MedA partial short or high-resistance fault on the circuit affecting current distribution
LowIntensity step command from ALCMS not matching what is set locally
LowCCR calibration drift since last verification
Required Tools
Clamp-on ammeter (true-RMS)CCR technical manualALCMS intensity step logCalibrated reference meter
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before any internal connection changes.
  • Treat the output circuit as energised at the commanded current until confirmed isolated.
Decision Tree / Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompare the CCR's reported output current against the ALCMS-commanded intensity step and the circuit's expected rated current for that step.
  2. MeasurementIndependently verify actual output current with a calibrated clamp meter at the CCR output terminals.
  3. MeasurementIf the independent reading differs from the CCR's displayed value, suspect the feedback/sensing path; if they agree but are wrong for the step, suspect the regulation control loop or the circuit itself.
  4. DecisionDisplayed and measured current agree but differ from the expected value for the circuit load → circuit-side issue, route to the Series Circuit Failure tree. Displayed and measured current disagree → CCR feedback/sensing fault.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the current feedback/sensing component, the output regulation control loop, or the downstream circuit.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the specific component or circuit condition responsible for the discrepancy.
Test Points
CCR output terminals (independent ammeter reading)
Current feedback/sensing transformer output
ALCMS commanded step vs. actual output
Circuit load count
Root Cause Verification

Step the CCR through its full intensity range and confirm output current tracks the expected value at each step, matching an independent reference measurement.

Corrective Action
  • Replace a faulty current sensing transformer or shunt.
  • Repair or recalibrate the output regulation control loop.
  • Correct the circuit load discrepancy.
  • Recalibrate the CCR against a reference standard.
Preventive Action
  • Include CCR output current calibration verification in the periodic PM/calibration cycle.
  • Maintain an accurate as-built fixture count per circuit.
Related References

SOP: SOP-CCR-06 (CCR Calibration and Output Verification)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-10

Case Study: Division 29 — Series Circuit Failure Decision Tree

Rev 0 — Initial issue
DDT-666 Series Circuit Failure Decision Tree

Symptom — An entire series circuit (a run of fixtures) is dark or shows abnormal output, distinct from a single-fixture fault, reported via ALCMS, night inspection, or pilot/vehicle report.

Equipment / System

The complete series loop for a given circuit — CCR output, primary cable, isolation transformers, fixtures, and return path to the CCR.

Possible Causes
HighOpen circuit somewhere in the primary cable loop (a break, failed joint, or disconnected termination)
HighCCR at reduced or zero output feeding the circuit
MedA hard short or ground fault causing the CCR to trip before establishing normal output
MedA section isolator or circuit breaker for this specific loop left open after previous work
LowMultiple simultaneous fixture-level faults coincidentally affecting the same run
LowMiswiring following recent circuit modification or repair work
Required Tools
Clamp-on series circuit ammeterInsulation resistance tester (megger)TDR / fault locatorAs-built circuit and base-can layout drawingCCR panel / ALCMS status
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate all airside access through ATC/apron control.
  • Treat the circuit as energised until confirmed isolated and LOTO applied at the CCR.
Decision Tree / Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCheck the CCR/ALCMS panel to confirm whether the CCR itself is producing normal output current for this circuit.
  2. ObservationConfirm by field inspection whether the entire run is dark or only a section/group of fixtures.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the full loop from the CCR output to identify whether an open circuit or a short/ground fault is present.
  4. MeasurementIf an open circuit is indicated, use a TDR or sectional testing from accessible base cans/pull boxes to localise the break.
  5. DecisionCCR output is abnormal → route to the CCR Output Current or CCR Trip trees. CCR output is normal but the loop shows an open or short → cable/circuit-level fault, proceed to isolate.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate the open circuit or short/ground fault to a specific cable segment, joint, or transformer.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the fault location by direct inspection or excavation before repair.
Test Points
CCR output current and voltage
Full-loop insulation resistance and continuity
Sectional resistance from accessible access points
Isolator/breaker positions for this circuit
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-energise the circuit and confirm every fixture on the run illuminates correctly at all intensity steps during a night inspection.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the open cable section or failed joint.
  • Restore CCR output if the root cause was upstream.
  • Close any isolator/breaker inadvertently left open.
  • Correct miswiring from recent work.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain an accurate, current circuit and base-can layout drawing.
  • Verify circuit continuity as a standard step after any excavation or joint work near the loop.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TCL-11 (Circuit Fault Location Procedure), SOP-CCR-04

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

Case Study: Division 29 — Cable Open Circuit Decision Tree

Rev 0 — Initial issue
DDT-667 Single Light Failure Decision Tree

Symptom — A single fixture on an otherwise normally operating series circuit fails to illuminate, while the rest of the run lights correctly.

Equipment / System

The individual fixture, its isolation transformer, L-823 connector, and base can.

Possible Causes
HighIsolation transformer failure at the fixture (open primary/secondary winding)
HighLamp/LED source failure inside the fixture
MedL-823 connector failure — moisture ingress, corroded pin, or plug not fully seated
MedBase can flooded or contaminated, affecting the connector or transformer
LowFixture physically damaged or displaced by vehicle/aircraft wheel loading
LowA localised short cable segment between this fixture and the next, isolated to this connection only
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeter1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)L-823 connector test adaptorBase-can lifting tool and PPE
Safety Precautions
  • Confirm the circuit is isolated and LOTO applied before opening the base can, even though the rest of the run remains lit.
  • Use FOD-safe tools and account for every item before leaving the pavement.
Decision Tree / Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm via field inspection that only this single fixture is dark and the rest of the run is lit normally.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, disconnect the fixture's L-823 plug and megger the isolation transformer primary and secondary windings.
  3. MeasurementInspect the base can for water or debris and check the lamp/LED module for visible failure.
  4. DecisionTransformer or lamp confirmed faulty → replace at this fixture only. Transformer and lamp test good → suspect the connector or a short local cable run to the adjacent fixture.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the transformer, lamp/LED module, connector, or the short cable segment feeding this fixture.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the specific failed component before replacement rather than swapping parts speculatively.
Test Points
Isolation transformer primary/secondary insulation
L-823 connector contact condition
Lamp/LED module continuity
Base can water/debris condition
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-energise the circuit and confirm the fixture lights at all intensity steps, then inspect the full run at night to confirm no adjacent lights were disturbed.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed isolation transformer or lamp/LED module.
  • Reseat or replace a corroded/damaged connector.
  • Drain and reseal the base can.
Preventive Action
  • Include fixture, transformer, and connector inspection in the routine night flight-check and PM cycle.
  • Maintain an accurate base-can schedule to speed fault location.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TCL-01 (Fault Isolation Procedure), SOP-CCR-04

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-46, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

Case Study: Division 29 — Multiple Light Failure Decision Tree

Rev 0 — Initial issue
DDT-668 Multiple Light Failure Decision Tree

Symptom — Two or more fixtures on the same circuit are dark or abnormal simultaneously, more than a single isolated fixture fault would explain.

Equipment / System

The affected group of fixtures, the cable section(s) connecting them, and the upstream CCR/circuit.

Possible Causes
HighAn open circuit or failed joint in the cable section immediately upstream of the first affected fixture, taking out everything downstream on that leg
HighCCR at reduced/zero output affecting the whole circuit, with only some fixtures visibly darker due to loading effects
MedA common cable section shared by the affected group damaged during recent excavation or ground work
MedCoincidental independent failures at each fixture (less likely but must be ruled out)
LowA section isolator/breaker for a sub-group left open after previous work
LowSeasonal water table rise affecting multiple base cans in a low-lying area simultaneously
Required Tools
Clamp-on series circuit ammeterInsulation resistance tester (megger)TDR / fault locatorAs-built circuit and base-can layout drawing
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate airside access through ATC/apron control.
  • Treat the circuit as energised until confirmed isolated and LOTO applied.
Decision Tree / Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationMap exactly which fixtures are affected and confirm whether they are contiguous along the circuit or scattered.
  2. ObservationCheck the CCR/ALCMS panel to confirm whether overall circuit output current is normal, reduced, or zero.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the loop between the last known-good fixture and the first affected fixture to check for an open or short.
  4. DecisionAffected fixtures are contiguous downstream of a single point → localise the fault to the cable/joint at that point. Affected fixtures are scattered with no common cable section → investigate CCR output or independent fixture-level faults for each.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific cable segment or joint feeding the affected group, or to individual fixtures if scattered.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the fault location by direct inspection before repair.
Test Points
Circuit output current at the CCR
Insulation resistance/continuity between the last known-good and first affected fixture
Base can condition in the affected area
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-energise the circuit and confirm every fixture in the previously affected group illuminates correctly at all intensity steps during a night inspection.

Corrective Action
  • Repair the identified cable section or joint.
  • Restore CCR output if the root cause was upstream.
  • Repair any individually faulted fixtures identified.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain accurate circuit mapping to speed identification of contiguous vs. scattered failures.
  • Monitor low-lying base can areas for seasonal water table effects.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TCL-11, SOP-CCR-04

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

Case Study: Division 29 — Series Circuit Failure Decision Tree

Rev 0 — Initial issue
DDT-669 Cable Open Circuit Decision Tree

Symptom — Continuity testing or fault symptoms indicate a break in the primary series cable loop, interrupting current flow to some or all downstream fixtures.

Equipment / System

Primary series circuit cable, joints, and terminations between the CCR and the affected fixtures.

Possible Causes
HighA cable break from excavation damage (third-party dig-in or airfield works)
HighA failed or corroded splice/joint that has separated
MedA termination that has worked loose or corroded at a base can or pull box
MedCable pulled taut and separated during pavement settlement or frost heave
LowRodent or wildlife damage to the cable jacket and conductor
LowA manufacturing/installation defect in a joint that has finally failed after prolonged service
Required Tools
TDR (time domain reflectometer) or fault locatorInsulation resistance tester (megger)Cable route drawingContinuity testerExcavation/access equipment as needed
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before any cable testing or excavation.
  • Call for utility/cable locates before any digging.
  • Treat exposed conductors as live until confirmed isolated.
Decision Tree / Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm from the failure pattern (which fixtures are dark) which cable section is implicated.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, use a TDR to estimate the distance to the open from the nearest accessible termination.
  3. MeasurementCross-check the TDR estimate against the as-built cable route drawing to identify the likely physical location (pull box, joint, or mid-run).
  4. DecisionBreak located at an accessible joint/pull box → repair without excavation. Break located mid-run in buried cable → coordinate excavation and locates before repair.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the exact break location by direct inspection once access is achieved.
  6. Root CauseDetermine whether the break resulted from mechanical damage, corrosion, or ground movement to inform the repair method and prevent recurrence.
Test Points
TDR distance-to-fault reading
Cable route drawing correlation
Joint/pull box condition at the estimated location
Continuity across the repaired section after splicing
Root Cause Verification

After repair, megger and continuity-test the full loop, then re-energise and confirm all previously affected fixtures illuminate correctly.

Corrective Action
  • Splice and properly seal the break using an approved airfield-rated splice kit.
  • Replace the damaged cable section if the jacket is compromised over a length rather than at a single point.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain accurate cable route drawings and provide them to any party performing ground works near the circuit.
  • Include cable route condition in periodic PM where accessible.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TCL-11 (Circuit Fault Location Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

DDT-670 Cable Earth Fault Decision Tree

Symptom — Insulation testing or CCR earth-fault protection indicates current leakage from the primary cable to ground, distinct from a fixture-level or transformer-level fault.

Equipment / System

Primary series circuit cable, its jacket, joints, and route between the CCR and the fixtures it feeds.

Possible Causes
HighA nicked or cracked cable jacket exposing the conductor to moist soil or a conductive pathway
HighA failed joint allowing moisture ingress directly onto the conductor
MedMechanical damage from a previous excavation that has since begun to fail under moisture exposure
MedLong-term UV or thermal degradation of jacket material at an exposed section
LowA manufacturing defect in the cable insulation
LowRodent damage exposing the conductor
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)TDR / fault locatorCable route and joint drawingMoisture detection meter
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before any insulation testing or cable work.
  • Treat a marginal insulation reading as a developing hazard and investigate rather than resetting protection.
Decision Tree / Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the CCR's earth-fault trend to establish whether this is a step-change (likely mechanical damage) or gradual (likely moisture/ageing).
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the cable in segments from accessible pull boxes to identify the affected segment.
  3. MeasurementWithin the identified segment, narrow the location using a TDR referenced against the route drawing.
  4. DecisionFault localises to a specific accessible point → repair without excavation. Fault requires excavation to access → coordinate locates and dig.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the exact fault point by direct inspection.
  6. Root CauseDetermine whether mechanical damage, joint failure, or ageing caused the fault to guide the appropriate repair and prevention.
Test Points
Segment-by-segment insulation resistance
TDR distance-to-fault
Joint/pull box condition
Moisture presence at the suspected location
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-megger the full loop and confirm the reading is restored to, or above, the acceptable threshold, then monitor the earth-fault trend over subsequent cycles.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the damaged cable section or joint using an approved airfield-rated splice/repair kit.
  • Reseal an identified moisture entry point.
Preventive Action
  • Schedule periodic insulation resistance testing and trend results over time.
  • Prioritise ageing sections for proactive replacement.
  • Provide accurate route drawings to any party performing ground works nearby.

Case Study: Division 29 — Low Insulation Resistance Decision Tree

Rev 0 — Initial issue
DDT-671 Low Insulation Resistance Decision Tree

Symptom — Periodic or diagnostic insulation resistance (megger) testing of a circuit returns a reading below the acceptable threshold, without necessarily having caused a trip or visible fault yet.

Equipment / System

The full series circuit loop under test — cable, joints, isolation transformers — and the test equipment itself.

Possible Causes
HighGradual moisture ingress into one or more cable joints or pull boxes
HighAged cable jacket cracking from UV/thermal cycling
MedA single degrading isolation transformer winding
MedMechanical damage from previous ground works beginning to fail under moisture exposure
LowCumulative insulation ageing across an older section of the circuit
LowA test equipment or test lead fault producing an artificially low reading
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)CCR earth-fault trend logCable route and joint drawingSpare/calibrated megger for cross-check
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before any insulation testing.
  • Treat a marginal reading as a developing hazard rather than a nuisance.
Decision Tree / Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the reading with a second, calibrated megger and a known-good test lead set to rule out a test equipment error before proceeding.
  2. MeasurementMegger the loop in segments from accessible pull boxes/base cans, comparing each against its baseline/previous test value.
  3. MeasurementIdentify the segment or transformer contributing the greatest drop in insulation resistance.
  4. DecisionDrop isolated to one segment/joint → localised repair. Drop distributed across many segments → treat as cumulative circuit ageing and plan proactive replacement.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific cable segment, joint, or transformer winding contributing most to the reduced reading.
  6. Root CauseConfirm with a full-loop re-test that the reading is restored to, or above, the acceptable threshold.
Test Points
Segment-by-segment insulation resistance vs. baseline
Individual transformer winding insulation
Test equipment calibration status
Joint/pull box condition in the lowest-reading segment
Root Cause Verification

Re-megger the full loop after repair and confirm the reading is restored to, or above, the acceptable threshold; trend over subsequent test cycles.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the identified degraded cable segment or joint.
  • Replace the transformer with the failing winding.
  • Reseal an identified moisture entry point.
Preventive Action
  • Schedule periodic full-loop insulation resistance testing on a defined interval and trend results year over year.
  • Prioritise ageing circuit sections for proactive cable replacement.

Equipment / System

Stop bar fixture(s), transformer/connector, the dedicated stop bar circuit, and the ALCMS/CCR interface controlling its on/off state.

Possible Causes

MedInterlock/logic fault between the stop bar and adjacent taxiway/runway lighting control
LowIn-pavement fixture physically damaged by aircraft/vehicle wheel loading given the stop bar's location at a runway holding position

Comms link status between ALCMS and the CCR

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the failed fixture component.
  • Repair the CCR/relay interlock logic fault.
  • Restore the comms link between ALCMS and the CCR.
Preventive Action
  • Include stop bar state-verification (commanded vs. actual) in the routine inspection cycle.

The fixture(s) under test, their lamp/LED source, lens/filter, alignment, and the CCR output feeding them.

ALCMS server/workstation, communication network (fibre, copper, or wireless links), and the CCR/field device communication interfaces.

ALCMS event log for the affected device

  • Repair or replace the damaged cable/fibre/connector.
  • Replace the failed network switch/media converter/communication card.
  • Correct the device's communication configuration/address.
Preventive Action
  • Include communication link testing (cable/fibre condition, network component health) in the periodic PM cycle, separate from lighting-circuit PM.
  • Maintain an accurate, current network architecture drawing.
