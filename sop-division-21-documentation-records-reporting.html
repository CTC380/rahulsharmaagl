Initial Issue

Golden Rule — If it was not recorded, it cannot be reliably demonstrated. If it was recorded incorrectly, it cannot be reliably trusted. Every AGL test, inspection, failure, repair, replacement and modification shall therefore be recorded with sufficient technical detail to provide complete traceability from the field activity to the final approved record.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Documentation, Records & Reporting SOPs in the AGL SOP Library.

Q1What is Division 21 — Documentation, Records & Reporting SOPs?

Division 21 is a set of 22 procedures (SOP-439 to SOP-460, Rev 0) for AGL test, inspection, maintenance, failure and incident reports, calibration control, asset registers, equipment history, transformer, CCR, cable and photometry records, daily and shift logs, fault logs, spare parts and replacement records, document control, as-built records and technical handover.

Q2What is the Golden Rule of AGL documentation?

If it was not recorded, it cannot be reliably demonstrated; if it was recorded incorrectly, it cannot be reliably trusted. Every AGL test, inspection, failure, repair, replacement and modification is recorded with enough technical detail for complete traceability from the field activity to the final approved record.

Q3What should every controlled AGL record include?

As applicable: document title and number, project or airport, equipment or system, location, circuit ID, asset ID, date and time, revision number, prepared, checked and approved by, test or inspection status, attachments and evidence, reference drawing or specification, and the record retention requirement.

Q4What must a test report contain?

Project or airport, equipment or circuit identification, test date, time and location, instrument and serial number, calibration status, method, conditions, results, acceptance criteria, pass or fail, observations, corrective action, technician, witness and approval (SOP-439). Raw test data must be retained wherever required, not only the final pass or fail result.

Q5Can an expired instrument be used for acceptance testing?

Not unless the approved quality procedure specifically addresses the situation (SOP-457). The certificate register tracks instrument ID, model, serial number, certificate number, calibration and due dates, provider, status and location, with statuses Valid, Due Soon, Expired and Out of Service.

Q6What happens when the installed system differs from the drawing?

The drawing must be corrected through controlled revision (SOP-459). Any field modification is reflected through the approved document and change-control process so that layout drawings, circuit diagrams, cable and equipment schedules, earthing and ALCMS records stay accurate.

Q7What does a failure report structure include?

Failure identification (date, equipment, location, circuit, type), detection source, investigation (symptoms, tests, measurements, fault location), corrective action (repair, replacement or temporary restoration) and final status: restored, restricted, temporary repair or pending permanent repair (SOP-442).
