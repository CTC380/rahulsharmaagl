Why Isolating Transformers Are the Backbone of AGL Systems

Most people see isolating transformers as simple voltage step-down devices. In reality, they provide electrical isolation, series circuit continuity, and fault tolerance — and understanding how they fail is just as important as understanding how they work.

01

Introduction – The Critical Role of Reliability

Aircraft operations depend on consistent and uniform lighting, especially during low-visibility conditions. Most people see isolating transformers simply as voltage step-down devices. In reality, their role is far more important and directly impacts the safety and performance of the entire AGL circuit.

An isolating transformer is not merely a supporting electrical component — it is essential to the architecture that keeps every fitting on the circuit reliable.

02

What Makes Isolating Transformers Critical?

An AGL system operates on a series circuit powered by a Constant Current Regulator (CCR). Each light fitting is connected through an isolating transformer, and this configuration allows the AGL system to function reliably under demanding airfield conditions. Isolating transformers perform three key functions:

Electrical Isolation

Isolating transformers electrically isolate individual fittings from the primary circuit. This ensures that a fault at the fitting level does not directly impact the high-voltage primary loop — a separation that is fundamental to the safety and reliability of the AGL system. The primary loop itself stays lethal, so it must always be isolated and locked out before work.

Maintaining Series Circuit Continuity

In a series circuit, continuity is everything. Isolating transformers allow each fitting to operate independently while still being part of a continuous loop. Even if a lamp fails, the circuit remains intact — the failure of a single light should never automatically result in the failure of the complete lighting circuit.

Protection Against Localized Failures

A failure at one point should never bring down the entire circuit. Isolating transformers help limit the impact of faults, ensuring that issues remain localized rather than cascading through the system.

03

The Real Advantage — Fault Tolerance

A well-designed AGL system ensures that a failed lamp, secondary lead or fitting does not affect the rest of the circuit. This is not simply a design preference — it is consistent with the series-circuit practice described in ICAO Doc 9157 Part 5 and FAA guidance. The limit of this protection matters: a fault on the secondary side is contained, but an open primary winding, a failed primary connector or a primary-side insulation fault is not. It interrupts the loop or shows up as an earth fault, and is handled at circuit level by the CCR’s open-circuit and earth-fault protection.

Without isolating transformers, even a minor fault could potentially lead to a complete circuit failure. For an airfield lighting system supporting aircraft operations, such a condition is unacceptable. The ability to isolate individual fittings and contain localized failures is therefore a fundamental part of system reliability.

04

Common Issues in Isolating Transformers

Despite their robustness, isolating transformers are not immune to problems. Some common issues observed in the field include:

OverloadingConnected load above the transformer rating leads to overheating & insulation stress
Moisture IngressPoor sealing allows moisture into the assembly, degrading insulation
Incorrect RatingA wrong rating or current ratio for the lamp or LED load can over- or under-drive the fitting and accelerate degradation

Proper transformer selection ensures the unit operates within its intended electrical and thermal conditions. For underground AGL installations in particular, protection against environmental conditions is an important aspect of long-term reliability.

05

Failure Is a Process, Not an Event

One important point that is often overlooked is that isolating transformers often degrade gradually rather than failing suddenly, although lightning surges and mechanical damage can cause abrupt failure. Typical degradation can involve:

  • Insulation resistance reducing over time
  • Thermal stress building up
  • Internal winding degradation beginning

By the time a transformer fails completely, it may already have been under stress for a long period. A transformer that appears operational today may already be progressing toward failure if its electrical or environmental condition has deteriorated.

06

Why Monitoring Matters

Because degradation is progressive, regular monitoring becomes essential. Key practices include:

01
Insulation Resistance TestingIdentifies deterioration before complete failure (circuit isolated and locked out, under the permit-to-work)
02
Load VerificationChecks load against rated capacity
03
Visual InspectionChecks for moisture ingress & physical damage
04
Performance ChecksEvaluated at the rated circuit current (6.6 A at full brightness)

Together, these practices support a proactive maintenance approach. The objective is not simply to replace a transformer after failure, but to identify early warning signs and prevent unexpected outages.

07

The Importance of Proper Selection, Installation & Monitoring

The reliability of an isolating transformer does not depend on the transformer alone. Three factors are particularly important:

FactorWhy It Matters
Proper SelectionThe transformer must be appropriately matched to the connected lamp load and application, and to the standard called up in the project specification (commonly IS 12290, IEC 61823 or FAA AC 150/5345-47, L-830).
Proper InstallationCorrect installation, sealing, and connector condition protect against environmental and mechanical stress.
Proper MonitoringRegular inspection and performance checks identify degradation before it becomes a complete failure.

A weakness in any one of these areas can affect the reliability of the overall AGL circuit.

08

Isolating Transformers and Overall AGL Reliability

An AGL system is made up of many individual components, but its performance ultimately depends on how reliably those components work together. The isolating transformer sits at an important interface between the series circuit and the individual light fitting.

Its ability to provide electrical isolation, maintain circuit continuity, and contain localized failures makes it a critical part of the system architecture. This is why the condition of isolating transformers should never be overlooked during AGL maintenance, commissioning, and troubleshooting activities.

Conclusion

Isolating transformers are not just supporting components — they are the backbone of AGL systems. They ensure electrical isolation, series circuit continuity, protection against localized failures, and reliable operation of individual AGL fittings.

Their proper selection, installation, and monitoring directly influence the reliability of the entire airfield lighting system. In aviation, reliability cannot be treated as an afterthought. It has to be built into every component and every stage of the system.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does an isolating transformer do in an AGL series circuit?

Each light fitting is connected to the CCR-fed series circuit through an isolating transformer. The transformer provides electrical isolation between the fitting and the primary loop, keeps the series circuit continuous even when a lamp fails, and limits the impact of faults so they stay localised instead of cascading through the system.

Why is electrical isolation important in AGL?

Isolation means a fault at the fitting level does not directly affect the high-voltage primary loop, which is fundamental to the safety and reliability of the system. The primary loop itself stays lethal, so it must always be isolated and locked out before work.

What happens to the circuit if one lamp fails?

The circuit remains intact. In a series circuit continuity is everything, and the isolating transformer lets each fitting operate independently while remaining part of a continuous loop. The failure of a single light should never automatically cause the failure of the complete lighting circuit.

Does an isolating transformer protect against every kind of fault?

No. A fault on the secondary side (lamp, lead or fitting) is contained. An open primary winding, a failed primary connector or a primary-side insulation fault is not. It interrupts the loop or shows up as an earth fault, and is handled at circuit level by the CCR's open-circuit and earth-fault protection.

What are the common problems found in isolating transformers?

Common field issues are overloading (connected load above the transformer rating, causing overheating and insulation stress), moisture ingress from poor sealing that degrades insulation, and an incorrect rating or current ratio for the lamp or LED load, which can over- or under-drive the fitting and accelerate degradation.

Do isolating transformers fail suddenly?

Often they degrade gradually: insulation resistance falls over time, thermal stress builds up and internal winding degradation begins. By the time a transformer fails completely it may already have been under stress for a long period, although lightning surges and mechanical damage can cause abrupt failure.

How should isolating transformers be monitored?

Four practices are described: insulation resistance testing to identify deterioration before complete failure (with the circuit isolated and locked out under the permit-to-work), load verification against rated capacity, visual inspection for moisture ingress and physical damage, and performance checks at the rated circuit current (6.6 A at full brightness). The aim is to catch early warning signs rather than replace a transformer only after failure.

What matters when selecting and installing an isolating transformer?

Reliability depends on proper selection, installation and monitoring together. The transformer must be matched to the connected lamp load and application, and to the standard called up in the project specification (commonly IS 12290, IEC 61823 or FAA AC 150/5345-47, L-830). Correct installation, sealing and connector condition protect against environmental and mechanical stress. A weakness in any one of these can affect the reliability of the whole AGL circuit.
