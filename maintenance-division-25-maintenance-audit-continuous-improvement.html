Maintenance Audit & Continuous Improvement

The management, reliability, performance-assurance and continuous-improvement division of the handbook. Its purpose is not to perform maintenance — it determines whether maintenance was performed correctly, on time, whether the defect was actually eliminated, whether equipment is becoming more or less reliable, and what should change to improve future performance.

Audit philosophy: an audit should not simply ask "was the checklist filled in?" It should establish whether the maintenance system actually controlled the asset condition. A completed form without effective maintenance should not automatically be considered successful maintenance.

Reliability

9 topics
MNT-AUD-022MTBF Monitoring
Mean Time Between Failures
  • MTBF = Operating Time ÷ Number of Relevant Failures
  • Monitor where meaningful and supported by reliable data
  • Trend MTBF rather than one reporting period
Acceptance / Record

Increasing MTBF generally indicates improved reliability.

MNT-AUD-023MTTR Monitoring
Mean Time To Repair
  • MTTR = Total Repair Time ÷ Number of Repairs
  • Monitor by equipment, failure type, team, location, period
Acceptance / Record

High MTTR may indicate: spare shortages, difficult access, poor diagnostics, lack of tools/competency, complex restoration.

MNT-AUD-024Failure-Rate Monitoring

Maintenance Audit Checklist

Five requirement groups closed out on every audit.

Planning

  • Maintenance plan available
  • Work order available
  • Correct priority assigned
  • Required resources planned

Execution

  • Correct SOP used
  • Correct checklist used
  • Competent personnel
  • Safety requirements and LOTO followed

Testing

  • Required tests performed
  • Test results recorded
  • Instruments identified
  • Calibration status valid where applicable

Documentation

  • Maintenance record complete
  • Components recorded
  • Test records traceable
  • Asset history updated

Effectiveness

  • Defect eliminated
  • No immediate recurrence
  • Repeat failure reviewed
  • Corrective action effective

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about maintenance audit & continuous improvement, based on the topics in this division.

8 QUESTIONS
Q1What is Maintenance Audit & Continuous Improvement?

It is the management, reliability, performance-assurance and continuous-improvement division of the handbook. Its purpose is not to perform maintenance but to determine whether maintenance was performed correctly, on time, and effectively.

Q2What does Division 25 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 25 (Maintenance Audit & Continuous Improvement) contains 40 topics in four groups: Group A Maintenance Audit (12 topics), Group B Failure Analysis (9 topics), Group C Reliability (9 topics) and Group D Continuous Improvement (10 topics).

Q3What should a maintenance audit establish?

An audit should not simply ask whether the checklist was filled in. It should establish whether the maintenance system actually controlled the asset condition, because a completed form without effective maintenance is not assurance.

Q4What is the Division 25 management cycle?

Eight steps: Audit, Analyze, Identify Root Cause, Measure Reliability, Improve, Implement, Verify and Standardize. Each audited activity is examined along the chain of requirement, planning, execution, testing, record, defect closure and reliability outcome.

Q5What does the maintenance audit checklist cover?

Five requirement groups on every audit: planning (plan, work order, priority, resources), execution (correct SOP and checklist, competent personnel, safety and LOTO followed), testing (required tests performed and recorded, instruments identified, valid calibration), documentation (complete record, components recorded, traceable test records, asset history updated) and effectiveness (defect eliminated, no immediate recurrence, repeat failure reviewed, corrective action effective).

Q6How do the audit and failure-analysis workflows differ?

The audit workflow runs from audit plan, document review, field verification and record verification through non-conformity or observation, root-cause analysis, corrective action, implementation, effectiveness verification and closure. The failure-analysis workflow, for significant or recurring failures, runs from failure and initial defect assessment through data collection, failure-mode identification, root-cause analysis, corrective and preventive action, implementation, monitoring and effectiveness verification.

Q7Which KPIs are on the reliability dashboard?

Thirteen management KPIs: PM compliance, PM on-time rate, corrective maintenance, emergency maintenance, backlog, MTBF, MTTR, failure rate, repeat defects, maintenance cost, spare consumption, audit findings and corrective-action closure.

Q8How does Division 25 turn the handbook into a reliability system?

Through a permanent feedback loop: inspection, maintenance and testing generate failure and condition data, which feeds reliability analysis and root-cause analysis, leading to improvement, an updated strategy and improved asset reliability. Division 25 is the highest-level management layer, feeding lessons back into inspection, preventive, predictive and condition-based maintenance.
