Product Overview

Approach Runway End Light for Airfield Ground Lighting

What It Does

Approach runway-end lighting forms part of the visual-aid arrangement at the runway approach and runway-end interface.

The runway end is a critical reference. The lighting must distinguish the landing threshold from the physical runway extremity and give the required information to aircraft approaching, landing, taking off or moving on the runway.

Depending on configuration, the arrangement may combine threshold identification, runway-end identification, direction-specific optical output, a green approach-side indication and a red runway-side indication.

  • Whether threshold coincides with runway extremity

  • Elevated or inset installation
  • Electrical circuit and isolating transformer
  • Mounting, environment and project specification

The final configuration should follow the approved airport lighting layout and applicable aerodrome standard.

The Key Distinction

Threshold = Runway End or Threshold ≠ Runway End

The exact function depends on this relationship, which matters most for displaced-threshold arrangements.

Threshold light: identifies the beginning of the landing portion. Runway end light: identifies the physical extremity. ICAO specifies threshold lights as fixed unidirectional green in the approach direction and runway-end lights as fixed unidirectional red in the runway direction.

Threshold & Runway-End Arrangements

Coincident vs Displaced

Threshold Coincident With Runway End

A combined fixture may give different indications by viewing direction. FAA guidance describes it as green on the approach side and red on the runway side, using separate optical paths, lenses or filters in one physical fixture.

Verify this characteristic when selecting a combined fixture.

Displaced Runway Threshold

A displaced threshold changes the relationship between physical runway end, landing threshold, approach area, runway-end lights and threshold lights.

FAA guidance identifies separate colour arrangements: lights at the displaced threshold emit green outward from the threshold, while runway edge lights in the displaced area show red toward the approach, as applicable to the approved design.

A displaced-threshold application should never be treated as a simple copy of a threshold-at-runway-end arrangement.

Combined Fixture — Direction-Specific Output

Colour & Optical Directionality

Direction Is the Defining Characteristic

Colour Configuration

  • Approach direction — Green: identifies the threshold to an approaching aircraft
  • Runway direction — Red: identifies the runway end to aircraft operating in the runway direction

FAA notes special lenses or filters create the required direction-specific coverage. Confirm exact colours against the approved runway arrangement.

Optical Characteristics

A combined fixture may have green output toward the approach, red toward the runway, independent optical paths, separate LED/lamp channels, special lenses, filters, reflector/lens assemblies and direction-specific beam distributions.

The submittal should therefore give approach-side photometry and runway-side photometry, not one generic curve.

Optical Parameters & Risks

  • Light colour
  • Horizontal and vertical beam spread
  • Main beam direction and optical axis
  • Intensity and viewing sector
  • Colour separation
  • Directional cutoff and uniformity

Incorrect optical configuration can cause:

  • Green visible from an unintended direction
  • Red visible in the wrong direction
  • Reduced threshold conspicuity
  • Confusion between threshold and runway end
  • Photometric non-compliance
ICAO Runway-End Lighting Arrangement

System-Layout Requirements

Runway-End Lights Under ICAO

  • Provided for a runway equipped with runway edge lights
  • On a line at right angles to the runway axis
  • As near to the runway end as possible, and not more than 3 m outside it
  • At least six lights
  • Evenly spaced between edge-light rows, or in two symmetrical groups
  • Fixed unidirectional red in the direction of the runway

Category III & Quantity

For Category III precision approach runways, ICAO limits runway-end light spacing to 6 m, except for the innermost pair when a central gap is used.

Treat these as system-layout requirements, not generic product dimensions. For FAA arrangements, count and spacing vary with runway width and the approved threshold configuration, with fixtures grouped symmetrically about the centreline.

Runway geometry + threshold configuration + governing standard = final quantity

Standards & References

Governing Standards

Select and install against the applicable aerodrome standard, approved runway lighting layout and project-specific technical specification.

ICAO

Annex 14 Vol. I is the 9th Edition (July 2022); Amendment 18 took effect on 4 August 2025; its aerodrome-design and visual-aids provisions apply from 27 November 2025 (ground handling from 26 November 2026, obstacle limitation surfaces from 21 November 2030). Confirm any later amendments before procurement. Provisions state:

  • Runway end lights required where edge lights are provided
  • On a line at right angles to the axis, as close to the end as practicable, not more than 3 m outside it
  • At least six lights
  • Fixed unidirectional red in the runway direction
  • Threshold fittings may serve as runway-end lights when the threshold is at the extremity
  • Threshold lights: fixed unidirectional green toward the approach

FAA

AC 150/5340-30J, Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids, is listed as active. Combined threshold/runway-end fixtures emit green toward the approach and red toward the runway, usually via special lenses or filters.

Fixtures within each threshold-light group must be consistently elevated or in-pavement. FAA survey/design guidance explains how the threshold/end relationship changes with displaced thresholds. AC 150/5345-46F covers the fixture classes, including elevated L-861E (medium-intensity) and L-862E (high-intensity) threshold/end lights and in-pavement L-850D bidirectional threshold/end lights; confirm the class against the approved design. Use the current fixture specification and approved drawings for classification, photometry, spacing, mounting and electrical configuration.

FAQ

Approach Runway End Light — Frequently Asked Questions

What is an approach runway end light?

Approach runway-end lighting forms part of the visual-aid arrangement at the runway approach and runway-end interface, distinguishing the landing threshold from the physical runway extremity. Depending on configuration, the arrangement may combine threshold identification, runway-end identification, direction-specific optical output, a green approach-side indication and a red runway-side indication.

What is the difference between a threshold light and a runway end light?

A threshold light identifies the beginning of the landing portion of the runway, and a runway-end light identifies the physical extremity. ICAO specifies threshold lights as fixed unidirectional green in the approach direction and runway-end lights as fixed unidirectional red in the runway direction. The exact function depends on whether the threshold coincides with the runway end, which matters most for displaced thresholds.

Can one fixture serve as both a threshold light and a runway end light?

Yes, where the threshold is at the runway extremity. ICAO notes that fittings serving as threshold lights may also be used as runway-end lights in that case, and FAA guidance describes a combined fixture that is green on the approach side and red on the runway side, using separate optical paths, lenses or filters in one physical fixture. Verify this characteristic when selecting a combined fixture.

What does ICAO require for runway end lights?

ICAO requires runway-end lights on a runway equipped with runway edge lights. They are placed on a line at right angles to the runway axis, as near to the runway end as possible and not more than 3 m outside it, with at least six lights evenly spaced between the edge-light rows or in two symmetrical groups, fixed unidirectional red in the direction of the runway. For Category III precision approach runways, spacing is limited to 6 m, except for the innermost pair when a central gap is used.

How are displaced thresholds handled?

A displaced threshold changes the relationship between the physical runway end, landing threshold, approach area, runway-end lights and threshold lights. FAA guidance identifies separate colour arrangements: lights at the displaced threshold emit green outward from the threshold, while runway edge lights in the displaced area show red toward the approach, as applicable to the approved design. It should never be treated as a simple copy of a threshold-at-runway-end arrangement.

Should the fixtures be elevated or inset?

Choose by the approved design. FAA guidance says fixtures in each group on both sides of a threshold should be all elevated or all in-pavement, because mixing them in one group gives inconsistent light output. Elevated fixtures use a frangible base and need aircraft clearance considered, while inset fixtures use a load-bearing housing and need careful coordination of the pavement interface, sealing and drainage.

Which FAA fixture classes cover threshold and runway-end lights?

FAA AC 150/5345-46F covers elevated L-861E (medium intensity) and L-862E (high intensity) threshold/end lights and in-pavement L-850D bidirectional threshold/end lights. Confirm the class against the approved design, and use the current fixture specification and approved drawings for classification, photometry, spacing, mounting and electrical configuration. AC 150/5340-30J gives design and installation details for airport visual aids.

What photometric data is needed for a combined green/red fixture?

The submittal should give approach-side photometry and runway-side photometry, not one generic curve: green and red photometric reports, beam distribution diagrams, chromaticity data and a directional optical diagram. Verification from the approach should show a green indication with correct intensity, beam spread and alignment and no unintended red; from the runway it should show red with no unintended green. Incorrect optical configuration can cause green visible from an unintended direction, red visible in the wrong direction, or confusion between threshold and runway end.

How are approach runway end lights powered?

Typical arrangements are series-circuit operation with an individual isolating transformer, a dedicated approach or runway circuit, or an LED driver with multi-channel colour output: CCR, primary series circuit, isolating transformer, secondary cable, then the combined fixture. Green and red functions may be controlled internally by the fixture's optical arrangement rather than by separate external circuits, so verify against the approved wiring diagram. Before replacement or installation, confirm CCR current, fixture input, transformer rating, number of fixtures, cables, connectors, intensity levels and monitoring.

What are the common faults in approach and runway-end lighting?

Typical symptoms are no green output, no red output, the wrong colour from the wrong direction, an uneven array, and an incorrect threshold or end position. Work from control to photometry: AGL control, CCR or power, primary circuit, isolating transformer, secondary connection, fixture, green channel, red channel, optical direction, geometry and alignment, and photometry. For the runway-end system also check threshold location, physical runway end, marking, edge lights and end lights.

What should be confirmed before ordering approach runway end lights?

Confirm the runway width, physical runway end, landing threshold and whether the threshold is coincident or displaced, approach category, the combined-fixture requirement, elevated or inset configuration, green approach-side and red runway-side output, light quantity and spacing, runway-centreline symmetry and edge-light relationship, series-circuit current, transformer and connector type, and the applicable standard. Request the green and red photometric data, combined-fixture GA drawing, wiring diagram and compliance documents.

Engineering Note
