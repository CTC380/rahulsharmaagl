Airfield Signage Installation, Message & Illumination SOPs

Controlled procedures for internally and externally illuminated airfield guidance signs — foundation, mounting, positioning, orientation and message verification, transformer/driver and LED module installation, internal wiring, gasket and sealing, cleaning, illumination and brightness testing, and mechanical, electrical and message inspection. Applicable SOP range: SOP-236 to SOP-255.

Initial Issue

Golden Rule — An airfield sign is not merely an illuminated box—it is an operational navigation aid. Correct message, location, orientation, height, structural integrity, electrical reliability, sealing and illumination must all be verified together so that the pilot receives the intended information at the intended location and viewing direction.

SOP-236 Airfield Sign Installation
Purpose

To establish a controlled procedure for installation of internally or externally illuminated airfield signs at their approved aerodrome locations.

Pre-Installation Verification

Verify:

  • Approved airfield signage layout.
  • Sign type.
  • Sign identification.
  • Message/legend.
  • Location.
  • Orientation.
  • Mounting arrangement.
  • Foundation.
  • Sign dimensions.
  • Colour configuration.
  • LED/lighting system.
  • Power supply.
  • Transformer.
  • Driver.
  • Cable route.
  • Frangibility requirements.
Procedure
  1. Review approved sign drawing.
  2. Confirm sign identification and message.
  3. Verify installation location.
  4. Inspect foundation.
  5. Verify mounting hardware.
  6. Position sign.
  7. Install support/mounting system.
  8. Verify orientation.
  9. Connect electrical system.
  10. Install transformer/driver where applicable.
  11. Inspect internal wiring.
  12. Complete sealing.
  13. Verify sign message.
  14. Conduct illumination testing.
  15. Conduct electrical/mechanical inspection.
  16. Complete final documentation.
Acceptance

The installed sign shall comply with the approved Location + Message + Orientation + Height + Structure + Electrical System + Illumination + Frangibility requirements.

SOP-237 Sign Foundation Installation
Purpose

To ensure the foundation provides adequate support and stability for the airfield sign.

Pre-Installation

Verify:

  • Approved foundation drawing.
  • Foundation dimensions.
  • Location.
  • Elevation.
  • Anchor arrangement.
  • Concrete specification.
  • Drainage.
  • Cable entry.
  • Frangible interface.
Procedure
  1. Establish approved sign location.
  2. Prepare excavation/formwork.
  3. Install reinforcement where specified.
  4. Install cable ducts/entries.
  5. Position anchor bolts/embedded hardware.
  6. Verify alignment.
  7. Place concrete.
  8. Cure according to approved requirements.
  9. Inspect finished foundation.
  10. Verify elevation.
  11. Verify anchor positions.
  12. Record inspection.
Critical Check

Foundation installation shall not interfere with:

  • Drainage.
  • Pavement.
  • Cable routes.
  • Frangible mounting.
  • Maintenance access.

SOP-239 Sign Orientation

Critical Principle — The sign shall be evaluated from the pilot's intended viewing direction, not merely from the maintenance access direction.

Purpose

To ensure the sign is correctly oriented toward the intended aircraft movement direction.

Procedure

  2. Establish taxiway/runway reference.
  3. Verify sign face direction.
  4. Check horizontal orientation.
  5. Verify viewing angle.
  6. Check sign visibility from the intended route.

  8. Secure mounting hardware.
  9. Record final orientation.

  7. Record measurements.

SOP-241 Sign Message Verification

Critical Rule — Never accept an airfield sign solely because it illuminates. The message itself must be verified against the approved signage schedule.

Purpose

To ensure the installed sign displays the correct approved message.

Procedure
  1. Obtain approved sign schedule.
  2. Identify sign number.
  3. Verify sign message.
  4. Verify legend characters.
  5. Verify sign type and colour scheme (mandatory, information, location, direction, destination) against ICAO Annex 14 and the approved schedule.
  6. Verify colour/background configuration.
  7. Check directional arrow where applicable.
  8. Check character sequence.
  9. Check sign face condition.
  10. Verify visibility from operational direction.
  11. Record acceptance.
SOP-242 Sign Electrical Connection
Purpose

To ensure safe and reliable electrical connection of illuminated airfield signs.

Procedure
  1. Obtain work authorization.
  2. Isolate circuit under LOTO (Division 02).
  3. Verify isolation and test for absence of voltage/current (for series-fed signs, confirm the CCR is isolated and locked out — an unlit sign is not proof of isolation).
  4. Identify supply cable.
  5. Inspect cable.
  6. Verify cable entry.
  7. Connect supply.
  8. Verify polarity where applicable.
  9. Verify protective earthing.
  10. Check connector/termination.
  11. Check cable sealing.
  12. Perform applicable electrical tests.
  13. Restore power through controlled energization.
SOP-243 Sign Transformer Connection
Purpose

To ensure the correct transformer is installed and connected where the sign uses an isolating transformer.

Procedure
  1. Never open the series-circuit primary while the CCR is energized.
  2. Verify transformer type/rating.
  3. Inspect transformer.
  4. Verify primary connection.
  5. Verify secondary connection.
  6. Confirm cable identification.
  7. Inspect connectors.
  8. Verify insulation/continuity as applicable.
  9. Verify sealing.
  10. Confirm transformer loading.
  11. Energize under controlled conditions.
  12. Verify sign operation.

SOP-247 Sign Gasket Installation

SOP-251 Sign Brightness Verification
Purpose

To verify the sign's illumination level against applicable approved requirements.

Pre-Test

Verify:

  • Calibrated test equipment.
  • Approved measurement method.
  • Correct sign configuration.
  • Correct intensity setting.
  • Environmental conditions where relevant.
Procedure
  1. Identify sign.
  2. Confirm operating intensity.
  3. Establish measurement points according to approved test method.
  4. Measure sign luminance (cd/m²) using the approved method.
  5. Record values.
  6. Compare with applicable criteria.
  7. Identify non-uniform areas.
  8. Investigate low output.
  9. Correct defects.
  10. Repeat test.
Possible Causes of Low Brightness
  • LED degradation.
  • Driver failure.
  • Dirty sign face.
  • Internal contamination.
  • Incorrect supply.
  • Loose wiring.
  • Optical deterioration.

Airfield Signage Master Workflow

For internally and externally illuminated airfield guidance signs:

APPROVED SIGNAGE DRAWING & SCHEDULE SIGN TYPE / MESSAGE VERIFICATION LOCATION SURVEY FOUNDATION VERIFICATION SIGN SUPPORT INSTALLATION SIGN BODY INSTALLATION HEIGHT & ORIENTATION VERIFICATION TRANSFORMER / DRIVER INSTALLATION INTERNAL WIRING LED MODULE INSTALLATION GASKET & SEALING ELECTRICAL TESTING MECHANICAL INSPECTION MESSAGE VERIFICATION ILLUMINATION TEST BRIGHTNESS VERIFICATION FOD INSPECTION FINAL ACCEPTANCE ASSET / SIGNAGE RECORD UPDATE

Signage Quality Hold Points

  • HP-01 — Sign Schedule — Verify sign type and message against the approved schedule.
  • HP-02 — Location — Verify exact installation position.
  • HP-03 — Foundation — Verify foundation, anchor and cable-entry arrangement.
  • HP-04 — Mounting — Verify sign height, level and mechanical stability.
  • HP-05 — Orientation — Verify viewing direction and operational visibility.
  • HP-06 — Electrical — Verify transformer/driver, wiring, connections and earthing.
  • HP-07 — Sealing — Verify gasket, cable entries and enclosure sealing.
  • HP-08 — Message — Verify the actual sign face against the approved message.
  • HP-09 — Illumination — Verify complete and uniform illumination.
  • HP-10 — Brightness — Verify applicable illumination/brightness requirements.
  • HP-11 — FOD — Complete final FOD inspection.
  • HP-12 — Final Release — Release the sign only after all applicable defects are closed.

Common Failure Modes

  • 1. Wrong Sign Message — The sign physically works but provides incorrect operational information.
  • 2. Incorrect Location — Even a correct sign can become misleading if installed at the wrong location.
  • 3. Incorrect Orientation — A correctly functioning sign may not be adequately visible from the intended aircraft route.
  • 4. Uneven Illumination — Failed LED modules or poor internal optical distribution can create dark areas.
  • 5. Driver Failure — Can cause complete or partial loss of illumination.
  • 6. Moisture Ingress — Can damage LED modules, drivers and internal wiring.
  • 7. Gasket Failure — A degraded gasket can lead to repeated condensation and corrosion.
  • 8. Loose Internal Wiring — Can produce intermittent operation.
  • 9. Foundation/Support Movement — Can change sign orientation and elevation.
  • 10. Corrosion — Can weaken mounting hardware and electrical connections.
  • 11. Damaged Legend — Physical damage, fading or contamination can reduce message legibility.
  • 12. Incorrect Replacement Component — An incompatible LED module or driver can alter brightness, colour or optical performance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Airfield Signage SOPs in the AGL SOP Library.

Q1What is Division 12 — Airfield Signage SOPs?

Division 12 is a set of 20 procedures (SOP-236 to SOP-255, Rev 0) for internally and externally illuminated airfield guidance signs. It covers foundation, mounting, positioning, orientation and message verification, transformer/driver and LED module installation, internal wiring, gasket and sealing, cleaning, illumination and brightness testing, and mechanical, electrical and message inspection.

Q2Is an airfield sign correct just because it lights up?

No. Never accept an airfield sign solely because it illuminates (SOP-241). The Golden Rule says message, location, orientation, height, structural integrity, electrical reliability, sealing and illumination must all be verified together, because a sign is an operational navigation aid.

Q3How is a sign message verified?

Against the approved signage schedule (SOP-241). Identify the sign number, verify legend characters, sign type and colour scheme (mandatory, information, location, direction, destination) against ICAO Annex 14 and the schedule, check the arrow and character sequence, and check visibility from the operational direction.

Q4From which direction is sign orientation judged?

From the pilot's intended viewing direction, not merely the maintenance access direction (SOP-239). Verify the sign face direction, horizontal orientation, viewing angle and visibility from the intended route.

Q5How are airfield signs sealed against moisture and dust?

SOP-248 inspects the enclosure, gasket, cable entry and connector entry, verifies drain or vent arrangements, replaces damaged seals, tightens fasteners uniformly and inspects the full perimeter. Inspection looks for water droplets, condensation, corrosion, dust ingress, cracked seals and loose panels.

Q6How is sign brightness verified?

Sign luminance is measured in cd/m² using the approved method with calibrated test equipment and compared with applicable criteria (SOP-251). Non-uniform areas and low output are investigated; possible causes include LED degradation, driver failure and a dirty sign face.

Q7What are the quality hold points for airfield signs?

They include sign schedule, location, foundation, mounting, orientation, electrical, sealing and message checks. The installation workflow runs from approved signage drawing and schedule through location survey, foundation, mounting, wiring, LED installation, sealing, testing, message verification, illumination and brightness tests, FOD inspection and final acceptance.
