WHAT ARE PAPI AND APAPI?

PAPI (Precision Approach Path Indicator) and APAPI (Abbreviated PAPI) are visual approach slope indicators. Aimed red and white beams tell the pilot whether the aircraft is above, on or below the approach slope. They are not radio aids like an ILS glide slope.

FAA AC 150/5345-28H classifies the four-unit system as L-880 and the two-unit system as L-881. ICAO likewise defines PAPI as a four-unit wing bar and APAPI as a two-unit wing bar, normally on the left side of the runway unless impracticable.

Suitability is set by the aerodrome requirements, aircraft, approach design and project specification. PAPI can be harmonised with an ILS but does not replace it.

3

PRINCIPLE: THE RED/WHITE TRANSITION

Each unit projects one beam split horizontally: aviation white above, aviation red below. The boundary is the transition band, and AC 150/5345-28H defines the beam centre as that transition. As the aircraft changes angle it crosses each unit's transition, so the pilot sees a different white/red mix.

Viewed at 1,000 ft (300 m), the transition must be within 3 minutes of arc at beam centre and 5 minutes at the beam edges. Colours must meet SAE-AS25050 and, for LED systems, FAA Engineering Brief #67.

4

APPROACH PATH, TCH, EYE-TO-WHEEL AND OCS

The visual approach path is normally 3° (FAA Order 6850.2C) but is set by the approved aerodrome design; do not assume 3° everywhere. Slope: θ = arctan(H / D).

TCH

Threshold Crossing Height. Raising PAPI units also raises the TCH, which may require relocation.

Eye-to-wheel height

Pilot eye to main gear. The design uses the aircraft height group and the required wheel clearance over the threshold.

MEHT

Minimum Eye Height over Threshold, tied to obstacle clearance and approach geometry.

OCS

Obstacle Clearance Surface (ICAO: obstacle protection surface, OPS). Obstacles must not penetrate it; fix by removal, changing the angle, moving the PAPI or recalculating. The FAA OCS rises at 1° below the lowest on-course aiming angle (the third unit from the far end, e.g. 2°50′ on a 3° L-880) and is evaluated out to 8 NM for an L-880 and 4 NM for an L-881 (AC 150/5340-30J, FAA Engineering Brief #95).

PAPI is not simply "1,000 ft from the threshold". Location depends on slope, TCH, eye-to-wheel height, obstacles, runway profile, PAPI elevation, ILS and aircraft group. Always use the approved siting calculation. In ICAO-style siting the distance from the threshold is D = (EWH + WTH) / tan M, where EWH is the eye-to-wheel height of the critical aircraft group, WTH is the required wheel clearance over the threshold and M is the lowest on-course angle minus 2′ (2°48′ for a 3° PAPI). Runway profile and PAPI elevation then adjust the result.
5

LAYOUT AND UNIT ORDER

Units form a straight line (wing bar) perpendicular to the runway edge. In a left-side installation, unit D is nearest the runway (inboard) and unit A is farthest.

Use the approved project siting document rather than spacing from memory. ICAO allows small height adjustments between units to suit transverse slope.

6

AIMING ANGLES

The units are deliberately aimed at different angles around the selected path. If all were aimed alike you would not get the stepped red/white sequence.

Unit (3° path)4-unit offsetAngle
D (nearest runway)+30′3°30′
C+10′3°10′
B−10′2°50′
A (farthest)−30′2°30′
Unit (3° path)2-unit offsetAngle
Nearest runway+15′3°15′
Farthest−15′2°45′

Other arrangements use wider offsets, for example 3°35′, 3°15′, 2°45′, 2°25′ on a 3° path (+35′, +15′, −15′, −35′), which ICAO-based documents give for some ILS-harmonised or steeper-slope designs. The 4-unit offsets above are the usual FAA and ICAO standard for a 3° path. These are not universal settings: the approved design, and the standard it follows, decides.

Built-in adjustment covers 2° to 8°, and the manufacturer's aiming tool reads in 10-minute increments with about ±3′ repeatability. Aiming is a safety-critical adjustment: errors change the pilot indication, TCH, obstacle clearance and ILS agreement.

7

OPTICS AND CONSTRUCTION

Light source

Incandescent or LED. LED gives long life, stable output and low power.

Environment

Class I: −35 °C to +55 °C · Class II: −55 °C to +55 °C; up to 100% RH and 100 mph (161 km/h) wind; rain, dust, salt, sun.

Lens and anti-ice

Must resist dew, frost and ice, by heating or intrinsic heat management. A dirty lens affects intensity, beam and transition: not cosmetic.

Horizontal coverage

Baffles or blanking devices limit beam spread to avoid obstacles.

Frangibility and rigidity

Frangible couplings and fittings; optical pattern must not shift under specified wind/static load.

Tilt protection

Fail-safe: if one unit drops more than ¼° or rises more than ½°, the whole system is de-energised after a 10–30 s delay that prevents nuisance trips. A tilt trip therefore turns every unit off, not just the one that moved.

Verify the classes, temperatures and tolerances against AC 150/5345-28H before you rely on them.

Type (L-880/L-881) counts the units, Style (A/B) is the powering method, and Class (I/II) is the temperature range. The L-828 regulator and isolation transformer are not supplied with a Style B PAPI and must be compatible.

CCRPrimary series circuitIsolation transformerL-823 connectionPAPI unit

Style B specifies Class A Style 1 or Style 6 plugs under AC 150/5345-26 to mate with the transformer output lead (see Note 10, Note 08 and Note 09). In both styles a lamp failure must not damage the power supply or the remaining lamps; for Style B a lamp bypass (shorting device) is available on request.

9

CONTROL AND DAY/NIGHT OPERATION

A Style A PCU may be separate or integrated and can hold power supply, control, intensity control, monitoring and protection. Style A requirements include full-intensity day mode, automatic photoelectric switching with hysteresis and a delay of about 45–75 s, defaulting to night mode if the photocell fails, night modes of roughly 5% and 20% of day intensity, and remote ON/OFF by hardwire or radio. An optional go/no-go lamp-monitor output can report status. Wrong intensity causes poor visibility, glare or reduced contrast.

SITING AND INSTALLATION

Approved designSurveyAircraft dataTCH / MEHT / OCSLocationFoundationLevel and azimuthVertical aimingElectricalPhotometric checkCommissioning

Survey data (see FAA Engineering Brief #95 for additional siting and survey considerations): runway centreline and threshold elevation, PAPI foundation elevation, longitudinal and transverse slope, obstacles, ILS reference, aircraft group and existing visual aids. A longitudinal gradient means siting cannot assume flat ground; distance, elevation and aiming may need to change.

After resurfacing

Elevation changes affect TCH, path, wheel clearance and OCS. Reassess; do not just restore the old angle.

After threshold displacement

New location, TCH, obstacle and aiming assessment.

Vegetation and obstructions

Trees, buildings, cranes, poles, terrain and temporary equipment must be checked; keep beams clear and avoid disturbing alignment.

After an incident

Recheck after impact, lightning, strong wind, foundation disturbance or survey changes.

11

PHOTOMETRY AND MAINTENANCE

"All lamps ON" does not mean "PAPI compliant." A PAPI can be lit yet have wrong intensity, transition, aiming or colour. Photometry checks intensity, beam distribution, chromaticity, transition quality, beam geometry and horizontal coverage.

AreaCheck
OpticalLens, colour, beam pattern, transition, intensity
MechanicalFoundation, mounting, frangible fittings, level, alignment, tilt
ElectricalPower, current/voltage, transformer, connectors, control, monitoring
EnvironmentalWater, dust, ice, frost, condensation, corrosion

Tools: precision inclinometer and aiming tool, survey level, true-RMS and clamp meters, insulation tester where applicable, photometric equipment, torque and cleaning tools, all calibrated where required. Record designed vs actual angle, difference, elevation, alignment and condition per unit, and keep a maintenance log (date, runway, unit, fault, action, final angle, test result, technician, return to service). Documentation should include the instruction book, drawings, aiming and calibration procedures, FAT and qualification reports and photometric data.

14

COMMON MISTAKES AND COMMISSIONING CHECKLIST

Treating PAPI as four lights

It is an optical, mechanical, electrical and geometric system.

Setting all four units to 3°

Standard units use different angles around the path.

Reusing APAPI angles

The selected slope sets the final angles.

Fixed location assumption

TCH, obstacles, profile and aircraft matter.

Checking only electrical operation

It can be healthy and optically non-compliant.

Mixing Style A and B

Voltage-powered vs series-current powered.

Commissioning checklist

Location and geometry

Threshold reference, runway offset, distance, spacing, elevation, TCH, eye-to-wheel group, OCS, obstacles.

Alignment

Horizontal, vertical aiming, unit level, stable foundation.

Electrical

Supply, CCR/PCU, transformer, connectors, cable, grounding.

Optical and control

Colours, transition, intensity, beam distribution, photometry; local and remote control, day/night, monitoring, tilt protection. Record final settings and hand over.

Exam & Interview

EXAM / INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

What does PAPI stand for?

Precision Approach Path Indicator.

What does APAPI stand for?

Abbreviated Precision Approach Path Indicator.

How many units are in PAPI and APAPI?

Four and two.

What colours does PAPI use?

Aviation white (upper sector) and aviation red (lower sector).

What does 2 white + 2 red mean?

On slope, for a standard four-unit PAPI.

What does all white mean? All red?

Above the slope; below the slope.

What does 1 white + 1 red mean on APAPI?

On slope.

What is L-880? L-881?

A four-unit PAPI; a two-unit APAPI.

What are Style A and Style B?

Style A is voltage-powered through a PCU; Style B is current-powered from a series circuit via isolation transformers.

What is the nominal visual approach path?

Normally 3°, unless another approved angle is set.

Why are the four units aimed at different angles?

To create the progressive red/white sequence around the selected path.

What are the standard 3° four-unit aiming angles?

3°30′, 3°10′, 2°50′, 2°30′ from nearest to farthest runway.

What is the APAPI aiming on a 3° path?

3°15′ and 2°45′.

What is TCH and why does it matter?

Threshold Crossing Height; it affects approach geometry and wheel clearance.

What is OCS?

Obstacle Clearance Surface.

What is the Style B current range?

2.8–6.6 A (AC 150/5345-28H).

Why use an isolation transformer on Style B?

It interfaces the PAPI to the series circuit and provides isolation.

Does Style A need a CCR?

No. Style A is voltage-powered and uses a PCU.

Why is PAPI aiming checked?

Incorrect aiming gives wrong visual guidance.

Why is photometry required?

To verify intensity, colour, transition and beam performance.

Why are PAPI units frangible?

To reduce hazard to aircraft if struck.

Why have tilt protection?

To de-energise the whole PAPI if any unit moves beyond its aiming limits, so pilots never get a false slope indication.

What should you check after impact?

Location, foundation, level, alignment, aiming, electrical condition and photometry.

Final Study Takeaway

FINAL STUDY TAKEAWAY

PAPI tells the pilot high, on slope or low by changing the ratio of white to red through precisely aimed beams. Understand it as optics + geometry + electrical + mechanical + photometry + maintenance.

PAPI engineering = aircraft geometry + runway geometry + optical aiming + electrical system + photometry + obstacle clearance + maintenance.
