Product Overview

Taxiway Centreline Light

What It Is

Taxiway centreline lights provide longitudinal visual guidance along taxiway routes, helping pilots maintain alignment through straight sections, curves, intersections, rapid-exit taxiways and selected runway-entry areas.

Unlike taxiway edge lights, which identify the lateral limits of the pavement, centreline lights provide a visual reference along the intended aircraft movement path.

Selected According To

  • Taxiway geometry
  • Straight or curved section
  • Taxiway intersection arrangement
  • Rapid-exit configuration
  • Low-visibility operating requirements
  • Runway proximity
  • Light colour sequence
  • Optical beam width
  • Directionality
  • Fixture spacing
  • Inset housing requirements
  • Series-circuit characteristics
  • CCR / intensity-control requirements
  • Project technical specification

Final configuration should always be selected against the approved airport lighting layout, applicable aerodrome standards and project-specific requirements.

Inset Configuration

In-Pavement Centreline Fixtures

Why Inset

Taxiway centreline lighting is commonly implemented using in-pavement / inset fixtures, allowing the lighting system to operate along the aircraft movement path without creating an unacceptable surface obstruction.

The light fixture, housing and pavement interface should be treated as a coordinated installation.

Straight Taxiway Sections

Optical Distribution for Straight Routes

Fixture Selection

Straight taxiway sections generally require a centreline fixture with an optical distribution suited to the intended aircraft viewing geometry. FAA guidance identifies:

  • L-852A for applicable straight sections where RVR is at least 1,200 ft
  • L-852C for applicable straight sections on low-visibility taxi routes below 1,200 ft RVR

Project Design Governs

The required beam configuration, intensity and colour arrangement should follow the applicable project design.

Curved Taxiway Sections

Guidance Through Continuous Heading Change

Why Curves Need Attention

Curves require special attention because the aircraft changes heading continuously. The system must provide enough visual information for the pilot to understand the path and direction of the turn.

FAA design guidance identifies wide-beam centreline fixtures for curved applications, including L-852B for applicable higher-RVR conditions and L-852D for low-visibility applications. The current FAA table also lists L-852J (RVR at or above 1,200 ft) and L-852K (below 1,200 ft) as wide-beam curve fixtures; confirm each fixture's beam handing and orientation against the curve direction on the approved drawing and the fixture photometric report, and check the beam angles in Table 1 of the current AC against the curve direction on the approved drawing.

Design Considerations

  • Radius of curvature
  • Fixture spacing
  • Beam width
  • Fixture orientation
  • Number of visible lights
  • Centreline geometry
  • Aircraft cockpit viewing position
  • Bidirectional vs unidirectional configuration

FAA research shows centreline performance on curves depends strongly on spacing and beam characteristics; a standard L-852D arrangement at specific curved geometry must preserve visibility of at least three lights from the cockpit.

Light Colour

Green Centreline, With Yellow Where Required

Colour Principle

For conventional taxiway centreline guidance, green is the principal colour. At specific runway-proximity locations, the configuration may incorporate yellow and green to provide additional visual information.

ICAO provisions specify green taxiway centreline lights for ordinary applications, with special green / yellow arrangements for exit taxiways and where proximity to a runway needs to be indicated.

Runway Proximity

Taxi Guidance Without Ambiguity

Control of Light Distribution

Centreline lights installed near a runway require careful control of light distribution. The purpose is to provide useful taxi guidance without creating ambiguity with runway visual aids.

ICAO notes the need to limit green light distribution near runways so that it does not create confusion with runway threshold lighting.

Low-Visibility Operations

Standard vs Low-Visibility Centreline Lighting

Why It Matters

Taxiway centreline lighting is particularly important on designated low-visibility taxi routes.

FAA AC 150/5340-30J states that all low-visibility lighting systems needed for operations below 1,200 ft RVR are to be operated in accordance with the applicable low-visibility procedures, and identifies dedicated taxiway centreline fixture types for these routes.

Do Not Treat Every Centreline Fixture as Equivalent

Distinguish standard taxiway centreline lighting from low-visibility taxiway centreline lighting.

Low-visibility fixtures identified by FAA: L-852C (straight), L-852D and L-852K (curved) and L-852F (intersections).

ICAO Annex 14 requires taxiway centre line lights on taxiways intended for use in runway visual range conditions below 350 m, and recommends them on taxiways used at night in better visibility, particularly at complex intersections and exit taxiways, except where traffic density is light and edge lights and centreline marking give adequate guidance (see Chapter 5 of the applicable edition).

Installation Spacing

Centreline fixture spacing is not a single universal value. It depends on taxiway geometry, straight versus curved section, RVR requirement, fixture beam width, governing standard, taxiway function and airport design.

FAA research demonstrates that curved-centreline spacing and beam width must be considered together to maintain sufficient cockpit visibility of successive fixtures. As an ICAO reference point (confirm in your edition), straight-section spacing is normally not more than 30 m (up to 60 m only where meteorological conditions allow), and not more than 15 m on taxiways intended for use below 350 m RVR, with closer spacing on curves. Spacing should therefore be taken from the approved lighting layout, not a generic product catalogue.

FAQ

Taxiway Centreline Light — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a taxiway centreline light?

Taxiway centreline lights provide longitudinal visual guidance along taxiway routes, helping pilots maintain alignment through straight sections, curves, intersections, rapid-exit taxiways and selected runway-entry areas. Unlike taxiway edge lights, which identify the lateral limits of the pavement, they give a visual reference along the intended aircraft movement path. They are commonly implemented as in-pavement (inset) fixtures so that they do not create an unacceptable surface obstruction.

Where are taxiway centreline lights used?

They are used for continuous taxi route guidance, straight sections (narrow beam) and curved sections (wide beam), taxiway intersections, rapid-exit taxiways, acute-angle exits, runway-entry routes, low-visibility taxi routes, and holding and manoeuvring areas, as well as in rehabilitation, new taxiway construction and AGL modernisation.

Which FAA fixture types are used for straight, curved and intersection sections?

FAA AC 150/5345-46F lists L-852A (straight, narrow beam) and L-852C (straight, low visibility, narrow beam), L-852B and L-852J (curves, wide beam), L-852D and L-852K (curves, low visibility, wide beam), and L-852E and L-852F (taxiway intersections, omnidirectional yellow, with F for low visibility). Confirm each fixture's beam handing and orientation against the curve direction on the approved drawing and the fixture photometric report.

What changes on low-visibility taxi routes?

For low-visibility taxi routes below 1,200 ft RVR, FAA guidance identifies L-852C, L-852D, L-852F and L-852K, and other configurations are used where RVR is at or above 1,200 ft. 1,200 ft is about 366 m, while ICAO uses a 350 m threshold, so confirm which applies to your project. ICAO Annex 14 requires taxiway centre line lights on taxiways intended for use in runway visual range conditions below 350 m, and recommends them on taxiways used at night in better visibility, particularly at complex intersections and exit taxiways. Do not treat every centreline fixture as equivalent.

What colours do taxiway centreline lights use?

For conventional guidance, green is the principal colour. ICAO provisions specify green centreline lights for ordinary applications, with special green and yellow arrangements for exit taxiways and where proximity to a runway needs to be indicated. FAA's fixture specification lists combinations such as green-green, green-yellow and yellow-yellow for certain bidirectional arrangements, as well as unidirectional green or yellow configurations. The sequence comes from the approved lighting layout and applicable standard.

How are exit taxiways and lights near runways handled?

On acute-angled exits, FAA guidance states unidirectional L-852A / L-852C fixtures are normally used, while bidirectional fixtures may be used where additional guidance for emergency vehicles approaching the runway is required. Centreline lights near a runway need careful control of light distribution so they give useful taxi guidance without creating ambiguity with runway visual aids, and ICAO notes the need to limit green light distribution near runways so it does not create confusion with runway threshold lighting.

How are centreline lights spaced, especially on curves?

Centreline spacing is not a single universal value. It depends on taxiway geometry, straight versus curved section, RVR requirement, fixture beam width, governing standard, taxiway function and airport design. As an ICAO reference point (confirm in your edition), straight-section spacing is normally not more than 30 m (up to 60 m only where meteorological conditions allow), and not more than 15 m on taxiways intended for use below 350 m RVR, with closer spacing on curves. On curves, spacing and beam width must be considered together, and a standard L-852D arrangement at specific curved geometry must preserve visibility of at least three lights from the cockpit. Take spacing from the approved lighting layout.

What electrical arrangement and CCR do centreline lights use?

Conventional AGL systems commonly connect centreline lights through a series circuit with isolating transformers, running from the CCR through the primary cable and isolating transformer to the secondary cable and the inset fixture. FAA guidance states that series circuits for taxiway centreline and clearance-bar systems should be powered from an appropriately sized L-828 or L-829, Class 1, Style 2, five-step CCR, with brightness controlled by varying output current. Before replacing or adding a fixture, verify the CCR output current, fixture electrical characteristics, transformer rating, number of lights, cable, connector type, intensity steps and monitoring.

What should be verified for inset installation and alignment?

Verify the pavement construction, housing and fixture dimensions, cut-out dimensions, installation depth, drainage and sealing, mounting hardware, cable routing and maintenance access, with the approved installation drawing controlling all dimensional requirements. For alignment, check the fixture position relative to the centreline, optical direction, spacing, curve and intersection geometry, colour sequence, fixture level, housing orientation and runway interface.

How are centreline lights commissioned and maintained?

Commissioning includes visual inspection (fixture, lens, colour, alignment and spacing, housing, gaskets, drainage, cable and connector), electrical tests (continuity, insulation resistance, transformer verification, series-circuit current, CCR output, intensity steps, monitoring) and photometric verification where required. Inspection and maintenance cover optical condition, mechanical condition, the isolating transformer, secondary connector and cable, and the system layout, including spacing, centreline alignment, curve continuity, intersection arrangement and runway-proximity colour sequence.

What are common centreline light faults?

Typical symptoms are a light off, low light output, wrong colour, incorrect beam direction, poor curve guidance and an uneven centreline appearance. Poor curve guidance can come from incorrect spacing, beam width or orientation, missing or degraded fixtures, incorrect curve geometry or the wrong fixture type. The diagnostic sequence runs from the CCR through the primary circuit, isolating transformer, secondary cable, connector, fixture, light source, optical assembly, alignment and photometric verification.

How is a centreline light different from a taxiway edge light?

A centreline light gives longitudinal route guidance, is typically green or sequence-dependent, is typically inset, and has a high role in low visibility. An edge light defines the lateral boundary, is blue, may be elevated or inset, and has a supporting role. The two systems perform complementary but different functions and should not be substituted for one another.

Which standards apply to taxiway centreline lights?

References include ICAO Annex 14 (taxiway centreline lights, with special runway-proximity and exit-taxiway colour arrangements), FAA AC 150/5340-30J (design and installation details, including fixture selection for straight, curved and low-visibility sections) and FAA AC 150/5345-46F (the L-852A to L-852F, L-852J and L-852K fixture types). In India, also verify DGCA requirements, airport-specific AGL specifications, the approved taxiway lighting layout and approved low-visibility procedures. Verify the applicable edition and amendments, including ICAO corrigenda to Volume I, before procurement.

What should be confirmed before ordering centreline lights?

Confirm the airport and project, taxiway identification and classification, whether the section is straight, curved or an intersection, rapid-exit and runway-proximity requirements, RVR and low-visibility requirements, fixture type, narrow or wide beam, unidirectional or bidirectional, colour configuration, intensity and steps, and fixture spacing. Also confirm series-circuit current, isolating transformer, connector type, housing type, installation depth, pavement cut-out, drainage arrangement, environmental rating, applicable standard and the manufacturer datasheet, photometric and compliance documentation.

Taxiway centreline lights are precision visual-aid equipment. Correct performance depends on the relationship between taxiway geometry, fixture type, beam width, colour, spacing, optical direction, pavement installation, electrical circuit and operational visibility requirements.

For curved routes and low-visibility applications especially, the fixture should be selected as part of the complete lighting layout rather than as a generic centreline light.
