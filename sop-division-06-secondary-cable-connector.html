SOP-121 Secondary Cable Installation
Purpose

To establish a controlled procedure for installing secondary AGL cables between isolating transformers and lighting fixtures.

Pre-Installation Checks

Verify:

  • Correct secondary cable type.
  • Correct cable length.
  • Correct transformer.
  • Correct fixture.
  • Correct connector system.
  • Cable identification.
  • Approved routing.
  • Required bend radius.
  • Cable protection.
  • Environmental condition.
Procedure
  1. Verify the approved circuit drawing.
  2. Identify transformer and fixture.
  3. Inspect secondary cable.
  4. Confirm cable length.
  5. Route cable using the approved path.
  6. Avoid sharp bends and mechanical damage.
  7. Maintain the manufacturer's minimum bend radius.
  8. Prevent unnecessary tension.
  9. Protect the cable from sharp edges.
  10. Secure/support where specified.
  11. Complete connector/termination installation.
  12. Identify the cable.
  13. Perform required continuity and inspection checks.
Acceptance

The installed secondary cable shall be:

  • Correctly routed.
  • Free from visible damage.
  • Properly identified.
  • Properly terminated.
  • Mechanically protected.
  • Suitable for the intended service.

SOP-126 Connector Crimping

Critical Rule — Never re-crimp a defective contact unless the manufacturer specifically permits the procedure. Replace it when required.

Purpose

To establish a controlled process for crimping AGL connector contacts.

Tools

Use:

  • Approved crimping tool.
  • Manufacturer-specified die.
  • Suitable stripping tool.
  • Inspection equipment where specified.
Procedure
  1. Verify tool calibration/condition.
  2. Confirm correct contact.
  3. Prepare cable to specified dimensions.
  4. Insert conductor fully into the contact.
  5. Position the contact correctly.
  6. Perform the specified crimp.
  7. Complete the tool cycle as required.
  8. Inspect the crimp.
  9. Verify conductor retention using the approved method.
  10. Reject defective crimps.

SOP-128 Connector Sealing

Purpose

Procedure

  3. Clean mating/sealing surfaces.
  4. Install seal according to manufacturer instructions.
  5. Ensure proper seating.
  6. Verify no damage or displacement.
  7. Complete connector assembly.
  8. Conduct final visual inspection.

  • Confirm stored-energy conditions.

  3. Inspect connector.

SOP-132 Connector Replacement
Purpose

To ensure defective connectors are replaced correctly and the circuit is restored safely.

Replacement Criteria

Replace when there is:

  • Physical damage.
  • Severe corrosion.
  • Failed contact.
  • Damaged sealing.
  • Poor crimp.
  • Loose connection.
  • Evidence of moisture ingress.
  • Electrical failure.
  • Manufacturer-recommended replacement condition.
Procedure
  1. Identify defective connector.
  2. Isolate the circuit.
  3. Apply LOTO where applicable.
  4. Verify absence of voltage.
  5. Remove defective connector.
  6. Inspect cable.
  7. Cut back damaged cable if required.
  8. Prepare cable.
  9. Install approved replacement connector.
  10. Inspect crimp and sealing.
  11. Perform continuity verification.
  12. Reconnect.
  13. Conduct operational testing.
  14. Update maintenance records.
SOP-133 Connector Cleaning

Important — Do not use aggressive solvents or abrasive materials unless specifically approved by the connector manufacturer.

Purpose

To remove contamination that may affect connector reliability.

Contamination Examples
  • Dust.
  • Dirt.
  • Moisture.
  • Oil.
  • Oxidation.
  • Corrosion products.
  • Foreign material.
Procedure
  1. Isolate equipment as required.
  2. Disconnect connector safely.
  3. Inspect condition.
  4. Use only approved cleaning materials.
  5. Clean mating surfaces carefully.
  6. Allow components to dry where required.
  7. Inspect again.
  8. Replace damaged seals/components.
  9. Reassemble.
  10. Perform required verification.

D. Connector Failure Modes

The following conditions should be specifically monitored during AGL maintenance:

  1. Moisture Ingress — Water entering the connector can contribute to corrosion and insulation degradation.
  2. Poor Crimp — An incorrect crimp can increase electrical resistance and create a weak mechanical connection.
  3. Incomplete Mating — A connector that appears connected but is not fully engaged can create intermittent faults.
  4. Damaged Seal — A damaged or incorrectly seated seal can permit moisture ingress.
  5. Corroded Contact — Corrosion can increase resistance and cause intermittent or complete circuit failure.
  6. Contamination — Dust, dirt and foreign material can interfere with reliable connector mating.
  7. Cable Stress — Excessive tension or bending close to the connector can eventually cause mechanical or electrical failure.
  8. Incorrect Connector Identification — Misidentification can lead to incorrect circuit connections and prolonged fault-finding.

Division 06 — AGL Secondary Connector Golden Rule

"The L-823 connector is a critical part of the AGL circuit — not merely a plug. Correct cable preparation, approved crimping, clean contacts, proper sealing, complete mating, mechanical protection and documented continuity are essential for long-term AGL reliability."

Division 06 — SOP Approval Principle

Final crimp dimensions, conductor preparation, insertion depth, sealing components, tooling and acceptance criteria for L-823/L-830 components shall be based on the approved connector manufacturer's instructions and the applicable project specification.

Approved project specifications and the connector manufacturer's instructions shall take precedence wherever they impose more restrictive requirements than a generic crimp dimension or torque value.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Secondary AGL Cable & Connector SOPs in the AGL SOP Library.

Q1What is Division 06 — Secondary AGL Cable, L-823 Connector & Fixture Connection SOPs?

Division 06 is a set of 20 procedures (SOP-121 to SOP-140, Rev 0) for AGL secondary leads, isolating-transformer secondary connections and L-823 plug and receptacle assembly and crimping. It also covers connector sealing, identification, mating and disconnection, fixture-to-transformer connections, cable polarity verification and secondary circuit continuity testing.

Q2Can generic crimp dimensions or torque values be used for L-823 connectors?

No. Crimp dimensions, conductor preparation, insertion depth, sealing components, tooling and acceptance criteria must follow the approved connector manufacturer's instructions and the project specification. A generic crimp dimension or torque value must not be used where the manufacturer specifies one.

Q3Can a defective crimped contact be re-crimped?

Not unless the manufacturer specifically permits it (SOP-126). Otherwise replace the contact. Use the approved crimping tool and manufacturer-specified die, verify tool condition, insert the conductor fully, inspect the crimp, verify conductor retention and reject defective crimps.

Q4Can a connector seal be reused?

Not where the manufacturer specifies replacement after disassembly (SOP-128). Inspect the sealing components, confirm the correct seal type, clean the surfaces, seat the seal per manufacturer instructions and make a final visual inspection.

Q5How should cable polarity be verified on AGL circuits?

AGL series-circuit arrangements differ from conventional low-voltage distribution, so conventional AC polarity concepts should not be assumed for every connection (SOP-139). Verification is based on the actual equipment, connector system and approved circuit design, using an approved instrument, and results are recorded.

Q6Does a continuity test prove a secondary circuit is healthy?

No. Continuity alone does not prove insulation integrity or overall circuit health (SOP-140). Additional tests must be performed when the commissioning or maintenance procedure specifies them.

Q7What are the quality hold points for secondary connections?

Cable identification, connector kit, crimp, connector assembly, connector mating, continuity, final connection and operational test.
