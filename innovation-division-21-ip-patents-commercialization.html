Innovation IP, Patents & Commercialization

Division 21 is the intellectual-property protection, invention-management, technology-ownership and commercialization division of the AGL Innovation Hub. Where Division 20 determines which technologies are valuable and mature enough to pursue, Division 21 ensures that valuable AGL innovations are identified, documented, protected, owned, validated, commercialized, transferred and adopted — anchored by the Patent Idea Register and a formal 100+ Patent Concepts programme.

IP-03 Patentability Assessment
Purpose

Determine whether an innovation may justify patent protection. The assessment should be performed through an appropriate IP professional/legal process before making a filing decision.

Initial Engineering Screening
  • What is new?
  • What technical problem is solved?
  • What is technically different?
  • What is the inventive contribution?
  • Can the invention be clearly described?
  • Is there evidence of implementation?
  • Is relevant prior art known?
  • Is there potential industrial application?
Key Concepts

A patentability assessment commonly considers matters such as novelty, inventive step/non-obviousness, industrial applicability/utility and patentable subject matter under applicable law. The exact legal test depends on the jurisdiction.

IP-12 Prototype Commercialization
Purpose

Not every prototype needs to become a mass-market product. Each prototype should be routed to the outcome that fits it.

Potential Routes
  • Route A — Product: Develop into a commercial product.
  • Route B — Internal Technology: Use within engineering/maintenance operations.
  • Route C — Licensing: License technology to another organization.
  • Route D — Technology Transfer: Transfer to a manufacturer or technology partner.
  • Route E — Strategic IP: Protect the concept for future development.
  • Route F — Stop: If commercial or strategic value is insufficient, close the project while preserving lessons learned and appropriate records.

Most Important Design Principle

Division 21 should remain the commercial and intellectual-property bridge, not another engineering division. It takes what Division 18 (Prototype Development) has built, what Division 19 (PoC & Pilot Projects) has field-tested and what Division 20 (Technology Evaluation & Benchmarking) has compared, and carries it through identification, documentation, protection, ownership, confidentiality, patentability assessment, commercialization assessment, licensing, technology transfer and industry adoption.

Every major invention should have a full IP evidence chain — Problem → Existing Technology → Technical Gap → Innovation → Invention Record → Design → Prototype → Test → PoC → Performance Evidence → IP Assessment → Protection → Commercialization — connected through a central AGL Innovation & IP Master Database that links Innovation ID, Patent Idea ID, Prototype ID, PoC ID, Technology ID, Product ID and Asset/Application.

The 100+ Patent Concepts programme should be measured not by the number of ideas generated, but by the quality of the pipeline: 100+ Concepts → Screened Concepts → High-Potential Inventions → Validated Innovations → Protected IP → Commercialization Candidates → Licensed / Transferred / Productized Technologies → Industry Adoption. That is what makes Division 21 the bridge between the AGL Innovation Programme and real-world economic and industry impact.
