AWOS Sensor

What It Is

AWOS (Automated Weather Observing System; ICAO documents call it an automatic meteorological observing system) sensors are measurement devices within the observing system. They give measured inputs to the AWOS processing system, where parameters are monitored, processed, displayed, recorded and made available to associated airport weather and information systems.

Precision AGL Solutions supports AWOS sensor configurations selected for required parameters, accuracy, environmental conditions, communications interface, power, mounting and project specification.

Typical Uses

  • Airport and runway weather monitoring
  • Automated weather observation and aviation weather reporting
  • Integrated airport weather networks, airfield environmental monitoring
  • General aviation weather stations
  • Expansion, modernization, replacement and upgrade of weather sensors

Sensor Selection

Begin With the Measured Parameter

Selection Criteria

Principle

Do not select sensors independently of the overall AWOS architecture, because communication, power, data processing and mounting interfaces must operate together.

Wind Sensor

Wind Speed and Direction

Provides

  • Wind speed and wind direction
  • Inputs from which the AWOS processor derives mean wind, gusts and direction variation
  • Response and measurement performance suited to the aviation application

Mounting

Provide appropriate exposure to the wind while avoiding interference from buildings, structures, trees or other obstructions. Wind sensors are normally mounted about 10 m above ground and sited to represent conditions along the runway and at the touchdown zone.

Reporting basis: ICAO Annex 3 uses a 2-minute average for local routine and special reports and ATS displays, and a 10-minute average for METAR and SPECI. Wind direction is reported in degrees magnetic in local reports and in degrees true in METAR and SPECI, so the sensor north alignment and the magnetic variation entered in the processor must both be correct. Confirm against your current Annex 3 edition.

Temperature & Humidity Sensor

Environmental Information

May Measure

  • Air temperature
  • Relative humidity
  • Associated calculated meteorological parameters

Positioning

Position and shield the assembly to minimise the influence of direct solar radiation and nearby heat sources. Sensors normally sit in a radiation shield, naturally or mechanically aspirated, at about 1.25 to 2 m above ground, away from paved surfaces and exhaust sources.

Atmospheric Pressure Sensor

Pressure Information for the AWOS

Use

Pressure measurement may be used for aviation meteorological reporting and associated calculations.

Configuration

Install and configure according to the required reference elevation and system design. QNH and QFE are computed from station pressure, so the barometer elevation and the aerodrome or threshold elevation must be entered correctly. Annex 3 reports both in hectopascals, rounded down to the whole hectopascal.

Visibility Sensor

Optical Measurement Where Required

Optical Measurement Can Be Affected By

  • Obstructions, local light sources
  • Dust, contamination
  • Precipitation, environmental conditions

Installation

The sensor provides data for AWOS processing. Follow the applicable system and airport requirements for the installation arrangement. A visibility sensor measures meteorological optical range and is not a substitute for RVR, which is derived from the extinction coefficient, runway light intensity and background luminance (ICAO Doc 9328). Where the runway needs RVR, specify an RVR system.

Present Weather Sensor

Precipitation and Atmospheric Conditions

Capability

Mounting Arrangement

Support for Reliable Measurement

Mounting May Include

  • Dedicated sensor support, equipment platform

Separation From

  • Buildings, hangars, trees, lighting structures
  • Exhaust sources, large paved surfaces

Siting on the aerodrome: assess every mast and sensor support against the Annex 14 obstacle limitation surfaces. Equipment on the runway strip or other operational areas must be frangible and mounted as low as its function allows, and may need obstacle marking or lighting under the aerodrome obstacle plan. Agree the location with the aerodrome operator before installation.

FAQ

AWOS Sensor — Frequently Asked Questions

What is an AWOS sensor?

AWOS (Automated Weather Observing System; ICAO documents call it an automatic meteorological observing system) sensors are measurement devices within the observing system. They give measured inputs to the AWOS processing system, where parameters are monitored, processed, displayed, recorded and made available to associated airport weather and information systems.

Which parameters can AWOS sensors measure?

Typical parameters are wind speed and direction, temperature and relative humidity, atmospheric pressure, visibility, present weather and precipitation, and cloud information and other site-specific parameters. Not every installation needs all of them, so base the selection on the approved system architecture and project requirements.

How should an AWOS sensor be selected?

Begin with the measured parameter, then consider the required range, accuracy and resolution, environmental conditions, response time, data output, communication protocol, power supply, mounting arrangement, cable length, maintenance and calibration requirements, and compatibility with the AWOS processor. Do not select sensors independently of the overall AWOS architecture, because communication, power, data processing and mounting interfaces must operate together.

Where is the wind sensor mounted and how is wind reported?

Wind sensors are normally mounted about 10 m above ground and sited to represent conditions along the runway and at the touchdown zone, with exposure to the wind and no interference from buildings, structures, trees or other obstructions. ICAO Annex 3 uses a 2-minute average for local routine and special reports and ATS displays, and a 10-minute average for METAR and SPECI. Direction is in degrees magnetic in local reports and degrees true in METAR and SPECI, so the sensor north alignment and the magnetic variation entered in the processor must both be correct. Confirm against your current Annex 3 edition.

How are temperature, humidity and pressure sensors positioned and configured?

Temperature and humidity sensors normally sit in a radiation shield, naturally or mechanically aspirated, at about 1.25 to 2 m above ground, away from paved surfaces and exhaust sources, to minimise the influence of direct solar radiation and nearby heat sources. For pressure, QNH and QFE are computed from station pressure, so the barometer elevation and the aerodrome or threshold elevation must be entered correctly. Annex 3 reports both in hectopascals, rounded down to the whole hectopascal.

Can a visibility sensor replace an RVR system?

No. A visibility sensor measures meteorological optical range and is not a substitute for RVR, which is derived from the extinction coefficient, runway light intensity and background luminance (ICAO Doc 9328). Where the runway needs RVR, specify an RVR system. Optical measurement can also be affected by obstructions, local light sources, dust, contamination, precipitation and other environmental conditions.

How do AWOS sensors communicate and how are they powered?

Check the communication protocol, data format, data update rate, transmission distance, cable type, interface hardware, system compatibility, electrical isolation and surge protection, and verify the interface against the AWOS processor before procurement. For power, consider supply voltage, current consumption, power source, cable length, voltage drop, protection, earthing and bonding, surge protection and backup power, and review power availability and cable routing at the system design stage for remote installations.

Where should AWOS masts and sensors be sited on an aerodrome?

Keep sensors separated from buildings, hangars, trees, lighting structures, exhaust sources, large paved surfaces and other meteorological sensors. Assess every mast and sensor support against the Annex 14 obstacle limitation surfaces. Equipment on the runway strip or other operational areas must be frangible and mounted as low as its function allows, and may need obstacle marking or lighting under the aerodrome obstacle plan. Agree the location with the aerodrome operator before installation.

What surge and lightning protection is needed?

Consider surge protection, earthing, bonding, lightning protection of masts and equipment (IEC 62305 series, adopted in India as IS/IEC 62305), cable shielding, electrical isolation, protective devices and proper cable routing. Coordinate the protection arrangement with the airport electrical system and the AWOS equipment design.

How are AWOS sensors calibrated and maintained?

Activities may include calibration verification traceable to national or international measurement standards, functional testing, comparison with reference equipment, cleaning and inspection of sensor surfaces, cable and connector inspection, and data-output and system alarm verification. Set intervals from manufacturer recommendations, airport maintenance procedures and applicable regulatory requirements. At commissioning, accept the sensor only after the required system-level checks are complete.

What are common AWOS sensor problems?

If sensor data is not available, check the power and communication path before replacing the sensor. For unstable measurement, compare with reference information and inspect the complete installation. For incorrect wind direction, verify the physical orientation, the true or magnetic north reference, the magnetic variation and the system configuration first. For poor visibility measurement, inspect optical surfaces and the installation environment before replacing parts.

What information is needed to replace or add an AWOS sensor?

Provide the existing sensor make and model, the existing AWOS make and model, the measured parameter, communication interface, power supply, existing mounting arrangement and cable details, and installation photographs and calibration information where available. Check replacement sensors for mechanical, electrical, communication and software compatibility with the existing AWOS. A block diagram or existing AWOS datasheet speeds the compatibility review.

Which standards apply to AWOS sensors?

References may include ICAO Annex 3 (also published as WMO Technical Regulations, Vol. II), ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I for siting and frangibility of equipment on operational areas, ICAO Doc 9837 and Doc 9328, WMO-No. 8, FAA AC 150/5220-16E, India Meteorological Department (IMD) and DGCA requirements, airport authority specifications, and manufacturer and project AWOS specifications. Confirm the applicable editions and local regulatory requirements before procurement.
