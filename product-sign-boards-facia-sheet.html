Product Overview

Sign Board Facia Sheet

What It Is

The facia sheet is the visible front surface of a sign assembly, and may form part of an illuminated or non-illuminated airfield sign. Precision AGL Solutions supplies facia sheets for new sign assemblies, refurbishment projects and maintenance requirements.

Material, thickness, colour, reflectivity, legend process, dimensions and compatibility follow the existing sign construction and project requirements.

Typical Facia Arrangement

  • Facia sheet, approved legend, background finish
  • Reflective or light-transmitting elements where specified
  • Protective surface treatment
  • Mounting or retention arrangement
  • Internal illumination interface where applicable

Colour & Contrast

Based on the Airport Signage Specification

Facia May Incorporate

  • Specified background colour, contrasting legend
  • Directional arrows, characters and numerals
  • Symbols, borders and other approved graphic elements

Principle

Base colour selection on the applicable airport signage specification, not a generic colour reference.

As a general reference, ICAO Annex 14 and FAA practice use white on red for mandatory instruction signs, yellow on black for location signs, and black on yellow for direction and information signs. Facia colours should meet the chromaticity and luminance requirements for the sign type, including the transmitted colour of illuminated faces.

FAQ

Sign Board Facia Sheet — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a sign board facia sheet?

The facia sheet is the visible front surface of a sign assembly and may form part of an illuminated or non-illuminated airfield sign. It is the visible interface through which taxiway, location, direction, apron and other approved information reaches pilots and ground personnel. Material, thickness, colour, reflectivity, legend process and dimensions follow the existing sign construction and project requirements.

When is a facia-only replacement appropriate?

It is useful when the enclosure and structure remain serviceable but the sign face is damaged or deteriorated, and it may be more appropriate than replacing the whole sign when the remaining components suit continued service. It may not be appropriate if the enclosure or internals are deteriorated, and complete sign replacement may still be preferable where the enclosure, illumination system or electrical components are no longer suitable.

What colours should a facia use?

Base colour selection on the applicable airport signage specification rather than a generic colour reference. As a general reference, ICAO Annex 14 and FAA practice use white on red for mandatory instruction signs, yellow on black for location signs, and black on yellow for direction and information signs. Facia colours should meet the chromaticity and luminance requirements for the sign type, including the transmitted colour of illuminated faces.

How is the facia matched to edge-lit, back-lit and LED signs?

On edge-lit signs the facia works with the optics distributing light through or across the face. On back-lit signs it needs appropriate light transmission and contrast for illumination behind it, and on LED illuminated signs the material and legend construction must be compatible with the specified LED system. Incorrect facia material or graphic construction can cause uneven illumination, poor contrast or reduced legend visibility.

What material and thickness should be used?

Consider sheet rigidity, surface durability, dimensional stability, weather, UV and corrosion resistance, compatibility with the existing enclosure and illumination components and ease of replacement. Thickness depends on the overall sign dimensions, support arrangement, enclosure design, retention method, wind exposure and illumination arrangement. Confirm the correct thickness from the existing sign dimensions or original equipment documentation before manufacture.

How is the legend produced?

The legend process may be printed, applied film, cut lettering, reflective or translucent material or another graphic production process. Before production, check the character accuracy, height, spacing and alignment, the arrow orientation and symbols, the background colour and the overall sign dimensions. Reproduce the approved operational information rather than a visually similar existing panel if the airport layout has since changed.

What reflectivity requirements apply?

Reflectivity applies where specified for the sign application. Considerations include reflective performance, viewing angle, surface durability, environmental exposure, legend and background compatibility and the existing sign construction. Confirm the requirements against the approved project specification.

What information is needed to order a facia sheet?

Provide the existing sign type and application, overall width and height, existing thickness, exact legend, character height, background and legend colour and reflectivity requirements. Also provide the illumination type, existing sign manufacturer or model where available, the mounting or retention arrangement, photographs of the existing sign, the quantity and the project or airport specification. Accurately measure the existing sign before fabrication.

How is a facia replaced safely?

Isolate illuminated signs, and for series-fed signs de-energise at the CCR and apply lock-out. Record the existing sign configuration, remove the existing facia, inspect the enclosure and internal illumination, clean the mounting interface, install the replacement facia checking the legend orientation, and then verify illumination and test the completed sign. Assess any damaged seals, fasteners, optical components or electrical connections before returning the sign to service.

What are the common problems with replacement facias?

Incorrect dimensions can create installation or sealing problems, an incorrect legend, even a small character or arrow error, can make the sign operationally incorrect, and a facia not matching the optical arrangement can give uneven or reduced illumination. A colour mismatch reduces consistency with the airport signage system, and incorrect thickness may prevent correct installation into the existing enclosure.

How is a facia checked after installation, and which standards apply?

Check the legend and orientation, character alignment, colour and contrast, reflective performance where applicable, illumination compatibility and sign-face uniformity, physical security, enclosure condition and visibility from the intended viewing direction. For illuminated signs a night-time inspection helps confirm the result. Requirements may be influenced by ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I (Chapter 5, Appendices 1 and 4), ICAO Doc 9157 Part 4, FAA AC 150/5345-44L and AC 150/5340-18H where applicable, and airport-specific signage standards. Confirm the applicable standard and edition before final production.
