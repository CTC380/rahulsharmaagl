05 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about the AGL Fundamentals question bank, how the drill and scoring work, and the core concepts it covers.

Q1What is the AGL Fundamentals question bank?

It is a 400-question reference and drill covering the first-principles knowledge every AGL engineer starts from: aerodrome terminology, visual aids, runway and taxiway basics, lighting colour coding and basic electrical concepts. It is built beginner-first before moving into technical and interview-level depth, and every question comes with a model answer, explanation and key terms.

Q2What topics does the question bank cover?

Eight topics: Introduction to AGL (50 questions), Airport Fundamentals (50), Runway Fundamentals (50), Taxiway Fundamentals (50), Visual Aids (50), Lighting Colours (30), Lighting Terminology (70) and Basic Electrical Concepts (50).

Q3What difficulty levels are included?

Four levels: Beginner (135 questions), Intermediate (193), Advanced (70) and Interview (2). The explorer and drill can both be filtered by level and by topic.

Q4What question formats does the bank use?

Technical explanation (197 questions), short answer (87), multiple choice (47), scenario-based (38), troubleshooting (20) and interview-style (11).

Q5How does Drill Mode work?

Drill Mode gives a shuffled run through the fundamentals bank, filtered by level and topic if you choose. You can type your own answer and check it for a score, or simply reveal the model answer, then move to the next question.

Q6How is my drill answer scored?

Marking uses weighted key-term matching, with stemming and typo tolerance, plus overall wording overlap. It is a close local approximation of a marker, not true AI grading, so use it to spot missing key terms rather than as an exact grade.

Q7What is Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL)?

Airfield Ground Lighting is a system of lights, visual aids, electrical circuits and associated equipment installed at an aerodrome to provide visual guidance for aircraft operations. It gives pilots essential visual references when natural visual cues are insufficient.

Q8What is an aerodrome?

An aerodrome is a defined area on land or water intended to be used wholly or partly for the arrival, departure and surface movement of aircraft.

Q9Why are different colours used in AGL systems?

Colours help pilots distinguish the function, location and operational meaning of different aerodrome lights. For example, blue is normally used for taxiway edge lights, green for taxiway centreline lights and red for stop bars.

Q10What is the difference between luminous intensity, luminous flux and illuminance?

Luminous intensity is the visible light emitted by a source in a particular direction, measured in candela (cd). Luminous flux is the total visible light a source emits, measured in lumens (lm). Illuminance is the luminous flux falling on a surface per unit area, measured in lux.

Q11Why are runway lighting systems designed as series circuits?

A series circuit lets the lighting system operate at a regulated circuit current, so every light on the circuit carries the same current and gives consistent intensity, with each light load isolated through a suitable transformer. The isolating transformer provides electrical isolation between the series circuit and the individual secondary load, and a Constant Current Regulator normally regulates the current.
