About this calculator

Enter the fixture groups and primary cable, set your expansion and loading limits, and the tool compares every standard CCR size side by side. It shows the smallest size that fits, how heavily it is loaded now and after expansion, the voltage it must supply, and the load on each brightness step.

Method & Reference

How the CCR is selected and loaded

Every step is shown so the result can be checked by hand. For a quick rating on its own, CCR Capacity (#16) follows the same sizing logic. This tool adds the size-by-size comparison and the brightness-step loading.

1. Real load

Fixture watts are divided by the transformer efficiency, then the primary cable loss at full current and any other load are added.

P = Σ(n × W) ÷ η + I² × R_cable + P_other

2. Apparent load and expansion

S = √(P² + Q²), Q = (P_fixtures + P_other) × tan(cos⁻¹ PF)    S_future = S × (1 + g)

Cable loss is resistive, so it carries no reactive part. This is the same treatment as Calculator #16.

3. Required rating and selection

The CCR must carry the future load without passing the maximum loading you set. The smallest ticked standard size at or above that rating is recommended. Beyond the largest size the load is split across circuits. A size is only recommended if its rated output voltage (kVA ÷ class rated current) stays within 5 kV: at 6.6 A that excludes 50 kVA, while on a 20 A circuit 50 kVA is acceptable.

S_required = S_future ÷ max loading

Each size is then given a status: above 5 kV circuit rating, overloaded, above the maximum now, expansion exceeds the limit, lightly loaded, or suitable.

4. Loading at each brightness step

The fixture load is scaled by the step current ratio raised to a power n (2 for constant impedance, 1 for linear, 0 for constant power). Cable loss always scales with I².

P_step = P_fixtures × (I ÷ I_full)ⁿ + I² × R    V_step = S_step ÷ I

The rated output voltage is the CCR kVA divided by full current. If a step needs more voltage than that, the table flags it. This mostly affects constant-power loads at low current.

What this tool does not do

It does not model lamp-out conditions, CCR efficiency or input power factor at low steps, or input supply sizing. Real lamp and LED behaviour varies by product, so the step scaling is an approximation. Isolating transformer no-load losses do not fall with current, so real loading at low steps is usually somewhat higher than shown. A lightly loaded CCR can perform poorly, so check the maker's minimum-load guidance. The sizes offered are nominal, and the CCR data sheet governs.

FAQ

CCR Selection & Loading Calculator: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on how the calculator selects the smallest standard CCR and checks its loading at every brightness step.

What does the CCR Selection & Loading calculator do?

It selects the smallest standard CCR for a series circuit and checks its loading at every brightness step. It includes cable loss, future expansion and output voltage, and it compares every standard size side by side. It produces a PDF calculation report. For a quick rating on its own, Calculator #16 CCR Capacity follows the same sizing logic.

How is the circuit load calculated?

Fixture watts are divided by the isolating transformer efficiency, and then the primary cable loss at full current and any other circuit-level load are added. The formula is P = sum of (n x W) divided by efficiency, plus I squared x R for the cable, plus other load. Apparent load is the vector sum of the real load and the reactive load of the fixtures, because cable loss is purely resistive, and expansion multiplies it by one plus the allowance you enter.

How does the calculator choose the CCR size?

The required rating is the load after expansion divided by the maximum CCR loading you set. The smallest ticked standard size at or above that rating is recommended, and you can untick sizes you cannot buy. If the load is larger than the biggest size, it should be split across circuits. The maximum and minimum loading are your project's own limits, not standard values.

Why is a CCR sometimes rejected on output voltage?

A size is only recommended if its rated output voltage, which is kVA divided by the class rated current (6.6 A or 20 A), stays within 5 kV. At 6.6 A that excludes 50 kVA, while on a 20 A circuit 50 kVA is acceptable. The comparison table gives each size a status such as above 5 kV circuit rating, overloaded, expansion exceeds the limit, lightly loaded or suitable.

How is loading calculated at lower brightness steps?

The fixture load is scaled by the step current ratio raised to a power n, where 2 is constant impedance such as incandescent or halogen, 1 is a linear approximation for LED, and 0 is constant power as a worst case. Cable loss always scales with the current squared. The table flags any step that needs more voltage than the CCR's rated output voltage, which mostly affects constant-power loads at low current.

Can you give a worked example?

Take 80 fixtures of 45 W and 24 of 100 W at 90 percent transformer efficiency, which is 6,667 W, on 4,000 m of #8 AWG at 6.6 A, which is 359 W of cable loss. The real load is 7,026 W, or 7.36 kVA at a power factor of 0.95, and 8.10 kVA with 10 percent expansion. At 90 percent maximum loading the required rating is 9.00 kVA, so the smallest standard CCR is 10 kVA, loaded 74 percent now and 81 percent after expansion.

What does the calculator not model?

It does not model lamp-out conditions, CCR efficiency or input power factor at low steps, or input supply sizing. Real lamp and LED behaviour varies by product, so the step scaling is an approximation. Isolating transformer no-load losses do not fall with current, so real loading at low steps is usually somewhat higher than shown. A lightly loaded CCR can perform poorly, so check the maker's minimum-load guidance.

Is the selected size the final CCR choice?

No. It is an engineering reference tool, and the sizes offered are nominal and read as kVA capacity, the conservative reading. Final selection shall be verified against the CCR manufacturer's rating, minimum-load and output-voltage data for the equipment chosen.
