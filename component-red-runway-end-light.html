Red Runway End Light

A critical runway lighting fixture that provides red visual guidance to pilots approaching the end of the runway. Red runway end lights are an essential safety component, clearly marking the runway terminus and providing a definitive visual cue that the runway surface is ending.

Overview

What Is It?

A Red Runway End Light is a critical runway lighting fixture that provides red visual guidance to pilots approaching the end of the runway. These lights are installed at the runway end and are part of the runway lighting system. The red colour indicates to pilots that they are approaching the end of the runway and that the runway surface is about to end. Red runway end lights are an essential safety component, clearly marking the runway terminus and providing a definitive visual cue that the runway surface is ending.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The red colour of this fixture exists to give pilots a terminal warning that is impossible to confuse with any other signal on the airfield: red is reserved specifically for "runway ends here" across the lighting system, so its meaning is unambiguous the moment it comes into view. This matters most during rollout after landing, when a pilot needs an immediate, reliable cue of how much stopping distance remains, and during takeoff, where it marks the absolute limit of usable pavement if the takeoff must be rejected. Because judging the physical end of a runway visually is difficult at night or in poor visibility, this dedicated red marker removes the guesswork from what is otherwise a critical safety margin.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Red runway end lights are installed at the runway end on all paved runways that support night operations. They are used on:

  • All paved runways supporting night operations
  • Precision approach runways (CAT I, II, and III)
  • Non-precision runways where night operations are conducted
  • Runways with displaced thresholds requiring end identification
  • Military airfields requiring clear runway terminus identification
Placement

Installation & Location

Fixtures are installed in a row across the full width of the runway at the runway end, spaced evenly on each side of the centreline in line with the runway edge lights, so the row reads as a continuous red bar marking the end of the usable pavement. Each light mounts on an L-868 base can recessed flush with the pavement or shoulder (or on a frangible elevated stem where the end abuts a soft surface), sealed against surface water, with cabling run in pavement ducting or shoulder trenching back to the isolating transformer serving the runway end circuit. Fixtures are unidirectional, angled so the red beam faces back along the runway toward an aircraft on its landing or take-off roll, with the reverse face blanked or fitted with a green lens where the same base can also serves as the opposite runway's threshold. Setting-out is tied to the runway end survey mark so the light line remains square to the centreline and coincides exactly with the edge of the usable landing surface.

Function

How Does It Work?

Red runway end lights operate as part of the runway lighting system:

  • Installation: Red runway end lights are installed at the runway end, typically in a row across the runway width, with spacing not exceeding 3 metres between lights.
  • Lighting: The lights are red and project a uni-directional beam towards the approaching aircraft, providing a clear visual reference of the runway end.
  • Guidance: The red lights indicate to the pilot that they are approaching the end of the runway and that the runway surface is about to end.
  • Integration: Red runway end lights are integrated with threshold lights, edge lights, and other runway lighting components.
Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

Red runway end lights sit on the same constant current series circuit as the runway edge lights, and control is exercised at exactly the same loop level: the ALCMS or tower selects a brightness step, the CCR regulates the whole loop to that fixed current, and every fixture — edge lights and end lights alike — burns at the same intensity simultaneously. There is no switch that energises the red lens independently of the loop. Where the same base can also serves as the opposite runway's threshold, colour separation is done optically rather than electrically: a single lamp or LED source sits behind a two-way lens, red facing back along the runway and green facing the opposite direction, so both colours are lit together whenever the loop is energised — there is no separate drive circuit per lens face. This has a direct consequence for fault diagnosis: because a shared fixture has one lamp element and one isolating transformer, a lamp or transformer failure darkens both the red and green faces at once, and the CCR sees it as the same loop-voltage-rise fault it would report for a plain edge light, with no way to tell which lens face has actually failed without a physical inspection.

Loop-level control, identical to the runway edge light; colour separation happens optically at the lens, not in the circuit.

Construction

Physical Construction

The fixture body is a die-cast aluminium alloy housing, machined and anodised or powder-coated for corrosion resistance against runway de-icing fluids, fuel spillage, and UV exposure. A red-filtered borosilicate glass or high-strength polycarbonate lens sits over the optical assembly, gasket-sealed to the housing with an O-ring or silicone seal to achieve an IP67/IP68 rating against water and dust ingress, and internally baffled so the beam is visible only from the approach side. Elevated units bolt to a frangible coupling engineered to shear cleanly under aircraft impact loads, seating on an L-867 base can flush with the surrounding grade; inset (flush) units instead seat directly into an L-868 base can recessed into the pavement, with the top surface load-rated for aircraft wheel loading. Internal wiring terminates at L-823 primary connectors, sealed splice connectors rated for direct burial and continuous immersion, which mate to the secondary leads of the isolating transformer housed in its own nearby base can. Stainless-steel fasteners are used throughout, and the base can assembly includes a drainage provision to prevent water pooling around the transformer and cable entry points.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Red Filter Degradation

The red filters in runway end lights are susceptible to UV degradation and thermal stress from halogen lamps. Over time, the colour can fade or shift toward orange, reducing the effectiveness of the visual cue. Regular photometric testing and filter replacement is essential.

Lens Contamination

Red runway end lights are exposed to rubber deposits from aircraft, tyre debris, and de-icing fluids. Regular cleaning with approved solvents and soft brushes is required to maintain light output and colour fidelity.

Misalignment from Pavement Settlement

Pavement settlement can cause runway end lights to tilt, altering the beam pattern and reducing visibility on approach. Regular alignment checks using a digital level or specialized alignment jig are recommended.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Single Fixture Dark, Rest of Circuit Normal
Likely Cause
Failed lamp/LED module, or an open isolating transformer (blown primary fuse-wire link or failed secondary winding).
Diagnostic Steps
Confirm series loop continuity is unbroken through the fixture's transformer primary; megger the transformer; check secondary voltage at the connector with the CCR energised.
Corrective Action
Replace the lamp/LED module if the transformer checks healthy; otherwise swap the isolating transformer and re-seal the L-823 connectors before backfill.
Entire Series Circuit Dark / CCR Will Not Regulate
Likely Cause
Open-circuit fault in the series loop (severed cable, corroded L-823 splice, or a failed transformer acting as an open point).
Diagnostic Steps
Check the CCR fault/open-circuit indication first; loop-resistance test from the CCR output to isolate the faulted segment; sectionalise and megger cable runs to narrow down the break.
Corrective Action
Excavate and repair or replace the faulted cable section and connector; re-test insulation resistance before reinstating the circuit.
CCR Trips on Overcurrent or Ground Fault
Likely Cause
Moisture ingress at a fixture or connector has degraded insulation resistance, creating a short or a partial ground fault on the series loop.
Diagnostic Steps
Megger the full circuit to identify the low-resistance segment; visually inspect suspect fixtures for cracked gaskets, condensation, or damaged secondary cable jackets.
Corrective Action
Dry out or replace the affected fixture/connector, renew O-rings and gaskets, and confirm insulation resistance is back above the acceptable minimum before re-energising.
Light Appears Orange or Faded Instead of Red
Likely Cause
UV degradation and thermal stress have caused the red filter to fade or shift toward orange, weakening the colour cue.
Diagnostic Steps
Run a photometric chromaticity check against ICAO/FAA colour thresholds; visually compare the filter against a known-good reference unit.
Corrective Action
Replace the red filter/lens assembly and re-verify colour and intensity with a follow-up photometric reading.
Reduced Light Output
Likely Cause
Rubber deposits, tyre debris, or de-icing fluid residue built up on the lens is blocking or scattering light output.
Diagnostic Steps
Visually inspect the lens for contamination; run a photometric intensity check against ICAO/FAA thresholds to quantify the loss.
Corrective Action
Clean the lens with approved solvents and a soft brush; re-verify intensity with a follow-up photometric reading.
Fixture Leaning, Loose, or Misaligned
Likely Cause
Pavement or subgrade settlement has tilted the fixture, altering the beam pattern away from the approach path.
Diagnostic Steps
Check fixture aim and tilt using a digital level or alignment jig; inspect the base can and surrounding pavement/turf for settlement or heave.
Corrective Action
Re-level and re-aim the fixture, and address any base can or subgrade settlement to prevent recurrence.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

Lamp / LED Module Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Solder-joint fatigue from repeated thermal cycling, moisture ingress through a compromised lamp/LED board seal, or ordinary end-of-life lumen depreciation.
Effect
Single fixture goes dark or drops below minimum intensity while the rest of the series circuit continues to operate normally.
Detection Method
Nighttime visual patrol, periodic photometric intensity survey, and continuity/secondary-voltage checks at the fixture connector.
Isolating Transformer Winding Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Moisture ingress leading to insulation breakdown, a manufacturing defect in the winding, or a blown primary fuse-wire link from a transient surge.
Effect
Open-circuit point in the series loop; can darken the single fixture or, if the open extends to the loop itself, take down the entire circuit.
Detection Method
Megger test of the transformer, secondary voltage check with the CCR energised, and loop-resistance testing from the CCR output.
L-823 Connector / Splice Degradation
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Improper seating or crimping during installation, gasket failure allowing groundwater ingress, or corrosion building up on the pins over time.
Effect
Intermittent operation, flickering, or a partial ground fault that degrades circuit insulation resistance.
Detection Method
Insulation resistance (megger) testing of the circuit and visual inspection of connectors at the fixture base can during maintenance.
Series Cable Insulation Breakdown
High Severity
Root Cause
Cable jacket damage from nearby excavation or rodent activity, age-related insulation degradation, or prolonged standing water in the duct run.
Effect
Ground fault or short circuit that trips the CCR or darkens the entire series loop.
Detection Method
Full-circuit megger test to flag the low-resistance segment, followed by sectionalised testing or time-domain reflectometry to pinpoint the fault location.
Red Filter / Lens Degradation
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Prolonged UV exposure and thermal stress from the lamp causing the red filter material to fade or shift toward orange over time.
Effect
Weakened colour cue and reduced chromaticity margin, with the fixture appearing orange or washed-out rather than true red.
Detection Method
Scheduled photometric chromaticity testing and visual comparison against a known-good reference filter during inspection rounds.
Lens Contamination
Low Severity
Root Cause
Rubber deposits from aircraft tyres, tyre debris, and de-icing fluid residue accumulating on the lens surface.
Effect
Reduced light output as contamination blocks or scatters the beam, potentially dropping intensity below ICAO/FAA thresholds.
Detection Method
Visual inspection during scheduled cleaning rounds and periodic photometric intensity testing.
CCR Regulation Fault
High Severity
Root Cause
Internal component failure (control board or output stage), a load mismatch after circuit modification, or a firmware/setpoint fault.
Effect
Incorrect brightness step, failure to hold regulated current across the loop, or nuisance tripping on healthy circuits.
Detection Method
CCR self-diagnostic/fault indication, output current verification, and a load-bank test to confirm regulation across all intensity steps.
Base Can / Foundation Settlement
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Poor sub-grade compaction at installation, frost heave, or drainage failure allowing water to pool and erode the surrounding fill.
Effect
Fixture tilts out of aim tolerance, altering the beam pattern away from the approach path, and cable connections come under mechanical stress.
Detection Method
Visual and level inspection of the base can and surrounding pavement or turf, and a check of drainage paths during annual maintenance.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Series Circuit Isolation
Electrical Safety

Runway end lights sit on a constant-current series circuit that can carry several kV across the loop. De-energise and lock out the CCR, then verify zero energy at the base can before opening it or handling connectors.

Airside Work Coordination
Operational

The runway end row marks the critical overrun/stop boundary pilots rely on; any work here requires ATC coordination and a NOTAM where fixtures will be dark, with personnel and vehicles kept clear of the runway strip.

Base Can Seal Integrity
Mechanical

Reseal the base can lid and cable gland fully watertight after any work, preventing water ingress and jet-blast debris from reaching the connector at this heavily loaded, high-airflow location.

FOD Control at the Base Can
Operational

Keep tools, fasteners, and gaskets accounted for at all times when the base can is open, and secure or remove any loose material before closing out, given the direct jet-blast and reverse-thrust exposure at the runway end.

Insulation Test Before Re-Energisation
Electrical Safety

After any cable, connector, or transformer work, megger test the affected series loop before restoring power, since a residual fault at re-energisation can trip the CCR or darken the whole loop.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Do not leave a fixture dark, misaligned, or at reduced intensity after maintenance; verify beam orientation and photometric conformance before closing out and cancelling the associated NOTAM.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
A Dark Shared Fixture Doesn't Tell You Which Face Failed
Troubleshooting

Where a two-way lens serves the runway end and the opposite threshold from one lamp, the CCR reports the same loop-voltage-rise fault regardless of which face was reported dark. Log which end complained (red or green) before dispatch, since it narrows the physical inspection but never substitutes for one.

LED Retrofit Changes the Colour-Separation Risk
Retrofit

Legacy two-way fixtures rely on a single lamp behind a red/green lens; retrofitting to dual-LED sources per face is worth doing deliberately rather than as a like-for-like swap, since it removes the single-point-of-failure that darkens both colours together and gives independent life-cycle tracking per face.

Verify the Row Against the End Survey Mark, Not the Last Light
Installation

Setting out end-row fixtures by measuring off the previously installed light lets small errors accumulate across the row until the line is no longer square to the centreline. Re-check spacing and squareness against the original runway-end survey mark periodically during installation, not just at the row's midpoint.

Size the Isolating Transformer to the Actual Face Load
Electrical

On shared red/green fixtures, the transformer sees whichever face is drawing current, but retrofit LED loads are far lower than the legacy lamp the transformer was originally sized for. Re-select VA rating against the real LED draw rather than reusing the incumbent transformer, or expect nuisance loop trips.

Frangibility Still Applies at Soft-Surface Ends
Mechanical

Where the runway end abuts a soft surface and the fixture sits on an elevated frangible stem rather than a flush base can, treat the coupling's shear rating as a maintenance item in its own right; UV and thermal cycling can shift shear characteristics over years even with no impact history.

Row-Wide Aim Drift Shows Up Only From the Threshold
Design

Every fixture in the end row can individually measure within photometric tolerance yet still read as a ragged red bar to an arriving pilot if aim angles drift unevenly across the row. Check aim consistency row-to-row from the approach perspective at commissioning, not just fixture-by-fixture at the base can.
