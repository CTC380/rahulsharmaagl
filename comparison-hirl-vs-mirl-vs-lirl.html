Runway Edge Lighting Comparison

HIRL VS MIRL VS LIRL

High, Medium and Low Intensity Runway Lights — HIRL, MIRL and LIRL describe the intensity classification of runway edge lighting in FAA terminology, not a different fixture type, colour, or runway classification. The difference is primarily light output, intensity-step control and the operating environment the system is designed to support — HIRL for demanding/poor-visibility conditions, MIRL for moderate conditions, and LIRL for basic visual runway environments.

02 · The Concept

What These Terms Actually Classify

HIRL, MIRL and LIRL generally describe the intensity category of runway edge lighting — not a separate physical light type, not a fixture mounting style, and not a colour. The same basic runway-edge-light concept can exist at different intensity levels depending on the applicable specification.

HIRL

High intensity → highest visual conspicuity → suited to demanding operating conditions and instrument runway operations.

MIRL

Medium intensity → moderate visual conspicuity → a compromise between high visual performance and lower system complexity/power.

LIRL

Low intensity → basic runway edge identification → suited to environments where HIRL performance is not required.

03 · HIRL

HIRL — High Intensity Runway Lights

HIRL systems provide a high level of runway visual conspicuity and are commonly associated with runways requiring stronger lighting performance, particularly instrument runway operations.

  • Highest maximum light output of the three categories
  • Multiple intensity steps for adjustment to conditions
  • Suited to poor visibility and demanding operating conditions
  • Higher electrical load, cabling/transformer loading and system cost
04 · MIRL

MIRL — Medium Intensity Runway Lights

MIRL provides an intermediate lighting level — a compromise between high visual performance and lower system complexity/power.

  • Medium maximum light output
  • Multiple intensity steps, typically with CCR control
  • Suited to non-precision or selected instrument runways
  • Moderate electrical load and system cost
05 · LIRL

LIRL — Low Intensity Runway Lights

LIRL provides lower-intensity runway edge lighting, generally suited to runway environments where the high-intensity performance of HIRL is not required.

  • Low maximum light output
  • Usually simpler brightness control
  • Suited to visual runways and lower-demand applications
  • Lower electrical load, cost and maintenance complexity
06 · Intensity Comparison

Intensity Comparison

Conceptually, maximum light output follows a clear order:

HIRL > MIRL > LIRL in terms of maximum light intensity. The purpose of increasing intensity is to maintain adequate visual conspicuity as environmental conditions become more demanding — LIRL may be adequate in good visibility, MIRL for moderate conditions, and HIRL for poor or demanding conditions.

HIRL/MIRL/LIRL should not be treated as direct substitutes for runway classification. The applicable runway lighting system depends on the runway type, operational requirements and governing standard.

08 · Intensity Steps & Control

Intensity Steps

A key characteristic of HIRL and MIRL systems is that their intensity can normally be adjusted to suit conditions, typically across a range from low through medium to high. The actual number of intensity steps and their values depend on the applicable FAA specification and equipment. This is normally achieved through the AGL Constant Current Regulator (CCR).

Simplified architecture: AGL control → CCR → series circuit → runway lights. The CCR controls the current supplied to the series circuit: higher circuit current produces higher light output, and lower circuit current produces lower light output. Exact current levels depend on the equipment and applicable standard.

09 · Common Misunderstandings

Myths vs Facts

Myth: HIRL, MIRL and LIRL are different colours of light
Fact

No. All three are classifications based on intensity, not colour. Colour (white, yellow, red, green) is a separate characteristic entirely.

Myth: HIRL is the same as runway centreline lighting
Fact

No. HIRL normally refers to the high-intensity runway edge lighting system. Runway centreline lights have their own photometric, colour and spacing requirements.

Myth: The intensity category alone determines what runway a system belongs to
Fact

No. HIRL/MIRL/LIRL should not be treated as a direct substitute for runway classification — the applicable system depends on runway type, operational requirements and the governing standard.

12 · Engineering Workflow

Engineering Workflow

Sequence: Determine runway type & operational requirements → identify governing standard → determine required edge-light intensity category (HIRL/MIRL/LIRL) → select CCR & intensity-step configuration → design series circuit → calculate circuit loading → select isolation transformers → design primary & secondary cabling → design control & monitoring → installation → electrical testing → photometric testing → operational approval.

13 · Engineering Verdict

HIRL vs MIRL vs LIRL

Engineering Verdict

HIRL, MIRL and LIRL classify the intensity level of runway edge lighting — they should not be confused with runway type, fixture mounting type, or light colour.

HIRLHigh intensity → highest visual conspicuity. Best suited to instrument operations and poor/demanding visibility conditions.
MIRLMedium intensity → intermediate visual conspicuity. A compromise between visual performance and system complexity/power.
LIRLLow intensity → basic/lower visual conspicuity. Suited to visual runways and less demanding operations.

Easy memory: HIRL → HIGH. MIRL → MEDIUM. LIRL → LOW. All three describe intensity, not colour, fixture type, or runway classification.

Quick Answer

Runway Light Intensity Testing & Intensity Steps

Runway light intensity testing means measuring the light output of a runway light in candela, in the specified direction, and comparing it with the required value for its class (high, medium or low intensity). The check is made at the brightness settings in use, so it is tied to the runway lighting intensity steps of the circuit.

Intensity steps are the brightness settings of the CCR, which change the series current and therefore the light output. High-intensity circuits normally have more steps than medium or low-intensity ones. Each step should be tested, because a light that passes at full brightness can still fail at a lower step.

14 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What do HIRL, MIRL and LIRL stand for?

High Intensity Runway Lights, Medium Intensity Runway Lights, and Low Intensity Runway Lights — FAA terminology describing runway edge light intensity categories.

Is HIRL a different type of fixture than MIRL or LIRL?

Generally no — the same basic runway-edge-light concept can exist at different intensity levels. The terms classify intensity, not a distinct physical light type.

Do HIRL and MIRL always have adjustable intensity steps?

Both are normally adjustable across multiple intensity steps via a CCR. LIRL usually has simpler control. Exact step counts and values depend on the applicable FAA specification and equipment.

Does HIRL automatically mean a runway is used for precision approaches?

No. HIRL/MIRL/LIRL should not be treated as a direct substitute for runway classification. The applicable runway lighting system depends on runway type, operational requirements and the governing standard.

What controls the intensity of HIRL and MIRL systems?

The AGL Constant Current Regulator (CCR) controls current supplied to the series circuit — higher current produces higher light output, and lower current produces lower light output.

Is HIRL the same as runway centreline lighting?

No. HIRL normally refers to the high-intensity runway edge lighting system. Runway centreline lights have separate photometric, colour and spacing requirements.

15 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

FAA AC 150/5340-30

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids — HIRL/MIRL/LIRL configuration, installation, photometric requirements and electrical systems. (Supersedes the now-cancelled AC 150/5340-24.)

FAA AC 150/5345-46

Specification for Runway, Taxiway, Heliport, and Vertiport Light Fixtures — covers the L-861/L-861T edge-light fixtures used across HIRL, MIRL and LIRL installations.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — runway lighting intensity, spacing and operational requirements.

DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Do not select HIRL, MIRL or LIRL based on intensity alone. Verify runway type, operational requirements, visibility conditions, CCR/circuit design, power demand and the current governing standard.

HIRL → high intensity for demanding conditions. MIRL → medium intensity, moderate conditions. LIRL → low intensity, basic visual identification.
