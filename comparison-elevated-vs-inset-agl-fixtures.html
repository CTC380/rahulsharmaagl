Installation & Construction Comparison

Elevated VS Inset AGL Fixtures

Elevated AGL fixtures are mounted above the pavement surface, while inset AGL fixtures are installed within the pavement so that their optical surface sits close to flush with the surrounding surface. The choice between the two is fundamentally a question of where the light must be installed and what loading and clearance conditions it must survive there.

02 · Definitions

What Is an Elevated & an Inset Fixture?

Elevated

An elevated AGL fixture is one whose body extends above the pavement surface. The fixture is carried on a base or stake through a frangible coupling (a designed break point), so it yields cleanly if struck by an aircraft or vehicle, and its optical assembly sits clear of the surrounding ground.

Where this matters

  • Fixture is more physically accessible
  • Must be frangible and low enough to preserve clearance for propellers and engine pods
  • Suited to areas outside direct wheel paths

Inset

An inset AGL fixture is installed within the pavement so that its optical window or low-profile top is close to flush with the surrounding surface. Most of the fixture body sits below the surface in a prepared base or can.

Where this matters

  • Minimal obstruction to aircraft movement
  • Requires a pavement opening and mounting can
  • Suited to runways and other high-traffic surfaces
03 · Key Difference

Above the Pavement vs Inside the Pavement

The easiest way to remember the difference between elevated and inset fixtures:

Elevated

Above the pavement

Inset

Inside the pavement

Engineering point: Elevated vs inset describes the fixture's installation and mechanical configuration — not its light-source technology. Both types can use LED sources or, in older installations, halogen.

04 · Installation Arrangement

How Each Fixture Is Installed

Elevated installation

A typical elevated installation involves a base or stake, a frangible coupling (designed break point), and a cable connection. The fixture body remains relatively accessible above ground once installed.

Inset installation

A typical inset installation places the fixture into a prepared pavement opening, seated in a base or can, connected to the cable system below the surface. The pavement is modified to accept the unit.

05 · Aircraft Movement & Clearance

Why This Is the Main Selection Driver

This is one of the primary reasons for choosing one configuration over the other. A protruding fixture can represent a physical obstruction where aircraft wheels or low-clearance aircraft could encounter it, so elevated fixtures are generally used only where their height and location are compatible with operational clearance requirements. The low profile of an inset fixture minimises obstruction and allows installation in locations subject to direct aircraft movement. ICAO Annex 14 also specifies that elevated lights be frangible and mounted low enough to preserve clearance for the propellers and engine pods of jet aircraft.

Selection driver: Whether the fixture location sits in or near a direct aircraft movement path is usually what decides between elevated and inset, more than any other single factor.

06 · Mechanical Requirements

Loading & Structural Considerations

Elevated fixture

Generally positioned to avoid direct wheel loading. However, an elevated fixture must still withstand wind, vibration, jet blast, environmental exposure and, depending on location, accidental impact.

Inset fixture

May be exposed to direct aircraft-wheel loads and therefore requires robust mechanical construction, pavement mounting, optical windows and sealing designed for applicable pavement loads.

07 · Optical Considerations

Beam Geometry Within the Physical Constraints

Both elevated and inset fixtures can be designed to provide a controlled AGL beam; the physical geometry available to achieve it differs. An elevated fixture has more freedom to project its optics above the surface. An inset fixture's low-profile installation requires the optical system to generate the required beam while remaining within the physical constraints of the pavement-mounted housing.

09 · Maintenance

Maintenance Comparison

Elevated fixture

Maintenance is comparatively straightforward because the fixture is accessible above the pavement, allowing more direct inspection and replacement.

Inset fixture

Maintenance may require removal of the fixture from its pavement base. Typical concerns include optical-window contamination, seal condition, water ingress, mounting bolts and internal components.

On active runways and taxiways this typically means a closure or a coordinated work window, so maintenance planning is an operational matter as well as a technical one.

10 · Environmental & Thermal Considerations

Exposure, Drainage & Heat Dissipation

Elevated fixtures are directly exposed to the environment and must withstand rain, dust, wind, jet blast and UV exposure — but that same exposure to surrounding air also assists heat dissipation. Inset fixtures face particular challenges with water ingress, drainage, contamination, seal integrity and debris accumulation, and their thermal design must account for the restricted, enclosed environment within the pavement structure. In snow conditions, elevated fixtures are exposed to snow banks and clearance equipment, while inset fixtures can be damaged by plough blades or covered and contaminated by slush and de-icing fluids.

Sealing is the critical inset detail: because an inset fixture sits within the pavement, sealing and drainage performance directly determine service life and reliability.

11 · Typical Applications

Where Each Type Is Normally Used

Elevated AGL fixtures

  • Runway edge areas
  • Taxiway edge areas
  • Approach-lighting systems (typically on frangible masts)
  • Guidance locations outside direct wheel paths

Inset AGL fixtures

  • Runway centreline lighting
  • Touchdown-zone lighting
  • Stop bars
  • Taxiway centreline lighting
  • Runway threshold/end lighting in applicable installations

The exact application depends on the applicable airfield-lighting design standard and operational requirements.

13 · Selection Logic

How to Choose Between Elevated and Inset

Core question: Is the fixture location in or near a direct aircraft movement path? If yes, inset is often preferred or required. If no, elevated may be suitable.

Other selection factors include:

  • Required lighting function
  • Aircraft clearance
  • Pavement loading
  • Photometric requirements
  • Installation method
  • Maintenance strategy
  • Environmental conditions

15 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: Elevated vs inset determines whether a fixture is LED or halogen
Fact

Elevated vs inset describes installation and mechanical configuration only. Both fixture types can use LED light sources or, in older installations, halogen.

Myth: Inset fixtures are simply a lower-cost version of elevated fixtures
Fact

Inset fixtures are generally more expensive to install because of the pavement modification, base/can, and sealing requirements involved.

Myth: Elevated fixtures are never used on runways
Fact

Elevated fixtures are used on runways and taxiways in areas where their profile and location are compatible with operational clearance requirements, such as some edge-lighting applications.

16 · Engineering Verdict

Which Configuration Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

The fundamental choice is not about which fixture type is "better." It is about where the light must be installed: elevated fixtures prioritise accessibility and above-ground installation, while inset fixtures prioritise low profile and compatibility with demanding aircraft movement areas.

The final selection should always be based on the current applicable ICAO/national standard, the specific lighting function, and the approved airport design — not on a generic comparison alone.

ElevatedAbove-pavement installation, generally easier to access and maintain, but with a higher physical profile.
InsetLow-profile installation within the pavement, suited to locations where minimal protrusion and resistance to aircraft/pavement loads are required.

17 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an inset fixture always stronger than an elevated fixture?

Not universally — both must meet the mechanical requirements of their installation environment. Inset fixtures are specifically designed for direct pavement-area loading, while elevated fixtures are generally positioned to avoid it.

Does elevated vs inset affect the light source used?

No. Elevated and inset describe the installation and mechanical configuration, not the light-source technology. Both can use LED sources or, in older installations, halogen.

Do elevated AGL fixtures have to be frangible?

Yes. ICAO Annex 14 specifies that elevated lights be frangible and mounted low, and FAA elevated fixtures are built with a yield point that separates cleanly if struck. For approach-light structures beyond 300 m from the threshold that are taller than 12 m, ICAO limits the frangibility requirement to the top 12 m.

Why aren't all fixtures made inset for a flush, obstruction-free surface?

Inset installation requires pavement modification, a base or can, and demanding sealing performance, which raises installation and maintenance complexity and cost. Where protrusion is acceptable, elevated fixtures are simpler and more accessible.

What is the single biggest factor in choosing between them?

Whether the location is in or near a direct aircraft movement path. This determines whether a low, flush profile is required.

Are inset fixtures harder to maintain?

Generally yes — maintenance often requires removing the fixture from its pavement base, compared with the more direct access available for elevated fixtures.

Do runway centreline and touchdown-zone lights use elevated or inset fixtures?

These typically use inset fixtures because they sit within the direct aircraft movement area and require a flush, low-profile installation.

18 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual airport project, verify the current applicable editions of the relevant standards before using this comparison for project design.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — lighting characteristics and siting, and the requirement that elevated lights be frangible and mounted low. Verify the amendment level adopted by your State (Amendment 18 became applicable on 27 November 2025).

ICAO Aerodrome Design Manual (Doc 9157)

Part 4 (Visual Aids), Part 5 (Electrical Systems) and Part 6 (Frangibility) — design guidance on aerodrome lighting, its electrical supply and frangible mounting.

FAA AC 150/5345-46

Specification for Runway, Taxiway, Heliport, and Vertiport Light Fixtures (current edition: 150/5345-46F, September 2024) — the equipment specification for both elevated and in-pavement (inset) fixtures, including yield-point requirements for elevated fixtures and load testing of inset fixtures.

FAA AC 150/5345-42

Specification for Airport Light Bases, Transformer Housings, Junction Boxes, and Accessories — the bases and mountings used with both elevated and inset fixtures.

FAA AC 150/5340-30

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids — siting and installation details for airfield lighting. Equipment performance (photometrics, load testing, frangibility) is set by the fixture and base specifications, AC 150/5345-46 and AC 150/5345-42.

FAA AC 150/5220-23A

Frangible Connections — covers frangible connections for objects in airfield safety areas. It does not apply to ALECP-certified light fixtures, whose yield-point requirements are in AC 150/5345-46.

DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Exact mounting, base and sealing requirements can vary with the governing standard and manufacturer specification. For final design, use the approved drawings and dimensional criteria from the applicable current standard rather than a generic comparison page.

Elevated and inset are two mounting configurations for AGL fixtures. Their key distinction is where the fixture sits relative to the pavement surface, and the resulting clearance, loading, sealing and maintenance implications that follow from that position.
