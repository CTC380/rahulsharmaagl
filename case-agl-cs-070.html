AGL-CS-070 — Incorrect Photometer Alignment

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A photometric measurement error case where incorrect detector alignment caused a false 15% intensity failure — from calibration verification through optical-axis alignment, sensitivity analysis, and procedural correction.

Case ID

AGL-CS-070

Category

AGL Photometric Measurement Error & Laboratory Quality Assurance

Level

Expert

Case Type

Engineering Simulation

Primary Skill

Investigation of photometric measurement errors caused by incorrect photometer alignment

Important: This is an engineering simulation created for training. It is not presented as an actual airport incident. All readings, values, dates and outcomes are illustrative, and no airport, organisation, person or manufacturer is identified or implied.

1Case Study Overview

During a laboratory goniophotometric acceptance test, a newly manufactured runway edge light failed the required peak-intensity specification by approximately 15%.

The manufacturer challenged the result because the same fixture had passed internal testing before shipment.

A repeat test performed by another laboratory produced fully compliant photometric results.

A detailed investigation was initiated covering photometer alignment, optical-axis verification, angular positioning, measurement distance, detector height, and reference-axis calibration.

The investigation identified incorrect photometer alignment with the fixture optical axis, causing the detector to measure the beam slightly off-center, resulting in artificially reduced measured intensity and distorted beam-distribution data.

This case demonstrates that incorrect photometer alignment can create false photometric failures even when the AGL fixture is fully compliant.

2Learning Objectives

After completing this case study, the learner should be able to:

  • Verify photometer optical-axis alignment.

  • Evaluate distance and height alignment.
  • Understand systematic measurement errors.
  • Perform alignment verification before testing.
  • Prevent false photometric failures.

3System Background

Photometric measurements require precise alignment of:

  • Fixture optical axis.
  • Photometer optical axis.
  • Measurement distance.
  • Detector height.
  • Horizontal angle.
  • Vertical angle.
  • Reference coordinate system.

Alignment errors may occur due to:

  • Detector displacement.
  • Angular offset.
  • Height error.
  • Distance error.
  • Fixture rotation.
  • Incorrect reference-axis setup.
  • Goniophotometer calibration error.

Photometric testing is fundamentally a geometric measurement process.

7Alignment Error Principle

Correct alignment → Detector on optical axis → True peak intensity → Correct beam distribution → Accurate photometric result
Incorrect alignment → Detector off optical axis → Reduced measured intensity → Distorted beam distribution → False photometric failure

Small angular errors can produce large intensity errors.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The photometric failure was caused by incorrect photometer alignment relative to the fixture optical axis, resulting in:

  • Reduced measured peak intensity.
  • Distorted beam-distribution data.
  • Apparent beam asymmetry.
  • False photometric non-compliance.

The fixture itself was fully compliant.

11Corrective Action

The laboratory:

  1. Realigned the photometer.
  2. Verified optical-axis coincidence.
  3. Calibrated detector position.
  4. Verified detector height.
  5. Verified measurement distance.
  6. Repeated the photometric test.
  7. Confirmed beam-distribution accuracy.
  8. Updated alignment procedures.
  9. Implemented mandatory pre-test alignment verification.
  10. Updated laboratory quality procedures.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: A fixture fails peak intensity by 15%. Calibration records are valid. Another laboratory reports a passing result. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace the fixture.
B. Increase LED power.
C. Verify photometer alignment, optical-axis coincidence, detector height, measurement distance, and angular positioning.
D. Reject the manufacturer.
Correct answer: C. Conflicting laboratory results with valid calibration point to a measurement geometry problem. Alignment verification will identify the cause without unnecessary fixture replacement or manufacturer rejection.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about photometer alignment errors and false photometric failures.

Q1What is AGL-CS-070?

AGL-CS-070 is an engineering training case study. It covers a laboratory acceptance test where a compliant runway edge light appeared to fail because the photometer was misaligned. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2Can incorrect photometer alignment cause a false photometric failure?

Yes. In AGL-CS-070 a fully compliant fixture failed by about 15% because the detector measured the beam slightly off-centre.

Q3Why did two laboratories get different results?

Laboratory A measured 43,500 cd with a narrow, depressed beam, while Laboratory B measured 51,200 cd with a normal beam. Laboratory A's photometer was not aligned with the fixture optical axis.

Q4How large was the alignment error?

Laser testing showed a 0.32° horizontal and 0.27° vertical offset. A 0.3° misalignment reproduced the discrepancy: 51,180 cd aligned versus 43,620 cd misaligned, a 14.8% reduction.

Q5How sensitive is measured intensity to alignment?

In the sensitivity study, intensity fell from 51,200 cd at 0.0° to 49,800 cd at 0.1°, 47,600 cd at 0.2°, 43,600 cd at 0.3° and 39,900 cd at 0.4°. Small angular errors cause large intensity errors.

Q6Did calibration, distance or height contribute?

Calibration records were valid, so calibration was not the cause. The distance error (24.82 m against 25.00 m) was small and not the primary cause. An 18 mm detector height error contributed slightly to vertical error.

Q7Why did the beam look defective when the fixture was healthy?

Misaligned measurements showed lower peak intensity, narrower beam width, vertical depression and horizontal asymmetry, all of which came from measuring off-axis.

Q8How did the laboratory correct the problem?

It realigned the photometer, verified optical-axis coincidence, detector position, height and distance, and repeated the test. It also made pre-test alignment verification mandatory and updated its quality procedures.

Q9What is the key lesson from this case?

Before declaring a fixture non-compliant, verify the measurement setup, especially alignment with the optical axis. A discrepancy between laboratories is a reason to check the method first.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

Incorrect photometer alignment is a geometric measurement error that can significantly reduce measured intensity, distort beam distribution, and create false ICAO photometric failures, even when the AGL fixture is fully compliant.

Systematic optical-axis verification, detector alignment, height verification, distance verification, angular positioning control, and repeat measurement validation can eliminate alignment-related photometric errors and ensure reliable AGL photometric certification.

In AGL engineering, a photometer measures exactly where it is pointed, not necessarily where the light is designed to shine, and alignment accuracy is therefore a fundamental requirement of every photometric measurement.
