Division 12 · CAB/CON · Cable & Connector Test Layer

Cable & Connector Testing

Every AGL circuit ultimately depends on buried, submerged, or embedded cable and connector infrastructure. Division 12 provides the detailed cable-and-connector test layer supporting the equipment-specific commissioning divisions — verifying identification, routing, electrical integrity, mechanical installation, termination and environmental integrity before any circuit is released for acceptance.

Philosophy: this division follows the general pre-commissioning philosophy established in Division 01 and supports the circuit-level tests in Divisions 02–11: Identification → Route → Continuity → Insulation → Resistance → Joint → Termination → Sheath/Condition → Polarity → Analysis → Connector Identification → Installation → Continuity → Contact → Mating → Locking → Sealing → Cable Lead → Electrical Verification → Acceptance.

Chain of Acceptance

For AGL, cable and connector acceptance is treated as one continuous electrical and environmental integrity chain. A cable can pass an insulation-resistance test while still having a defective termination; a connector can show continuity while having poor locking, inadequate sealing, or intermittent contact.

LinkWhat It Confirms
Correct CableThe right cable, correctly identified, is installed and traceable from source to destination
Correct RouteThe installed route matches the approved cable layout with no unauthorized deviation
Correct JointJoints are correctly located, kitted, sealed, and electrically continuous
Correct TerminationTerminations are correctly made, tight, insulated, and mechanically supported
Correct ConnectorThe right connector, correctly identified, is installed at the right point
Correct ContactConnector contacts are clean, seated, engaged, and free of corrosion
Correct MatingConnector halves are fully inserted, aligned, and correctly engaged
Correct LockingThe locking mechanism is fully engaged and cannot unintentionally separate
Correct SealingEnvironmental sealing protects against moisture, water ingress, and contamination
Correct Electrical PerformanceContinuity, contact, resistance, and polarity all evaluate to PASS against the applicable criterion
Accepted Cable/Connector SystemIdentification, route, electrical integrity, mechanical integrity, termination quality, connector integrity, environmental sealing, and test-result analysis all combine to formal acceptance

Before Testing Begins

Two principles should be established before cable and connector testing begins on any AGL circuit.

Electrical PASS ≠ Complete Acceptance

  • A measurement is not an acceptance decision until it has been evaluated against the applicable acceptance criterion
  • A cable can pass an insulation-resistance test while still having a defective termination
  • A connector can show continuity while having poor mechanical locking, inadequate sealing, or intermittent contact
  • The insulation-resistance test voltage must be compatible with all equipment connected to the circuit — sensitive electronic equipment should not be subjected to an inappropriate megger test

Division Boundary

  • Division 01 establishes the general pre-commissioning philosophy that Division 12 follows
  • Division 12 provides the detailed cable-and-connector test layer supporting the equipment-specific commissioning divisions (02–11)
  • Where conventional polarity terminology does not directly apply to AGL circuits, use the appropriate circuit identification/connection verification method rather than forcing a polarity test where it is not technically applicable
  • No cable or connector is released for circuit acceptance until every applicable item in this division is closed

PART A — CABLE
A

Cable Identification & Routing

2 topics
TC-CAB-001Cable Identification Verification
Verification Checks
  • Verify cable ID, type, size, and voltage rating
  • Verify cable circuit, origin, and destination
  • Verify drum/batch information where required
  • Verify cable identification markers and phase/polarity identification where applicable
  • Trace: Source → Cable → Joint → Termination → Equipment
  • Cross-check: Cable schedule → Drawing → Cable marker → Physical cable
  • Check for missing, incorrect, or duplicate identification
  • Check for damaged/unreadable marker or incorrect circuit assignment
Note

For AGL series circuits, the cable identification should allow the complete circuit to be traced from the CCR through the primary circuit and associated components.

Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: every cable is correctly identified, marked, and traceable against the cable schedule and approved drawing.

TC-CAB-002Cable Route Verification
Verification Checks
  • Verify starting point, ending point, and route
  • Verify cable trench/duct, crossing locations, and joint locations
  • Verify equipment entry, cable separation where applicable, and protection arrangements
  • Cross-check: Approved cable route drawing → As-built drawing → Physical route
  • Verify cable does not terminate at the wrong equipment or bypass approved components
  • Verify joint locations correspond with approved records
  • Verify cable is not subjected to unauthorized mechanical damage or routing conditions
  • Record cable ID, design route, actual route, deviation, and result
Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: the installed cable route corresponds with the approved cable layout, with any deviation formally recorded and dispositioned.

B

Cable Electrical Testing

3 topics
TC-CAB-003Cable Continuity Testing
Verification Checks
  • Verify no open circuit and correct conductor
  • Verify correct circuit and correct termination
  • Verify correct joint continuity
  • Test sequence: Isolate → Identify → Test → Record → Restore
  • For AGL circuits, verify continuity through the intended circuit path per the approved test procedure
  • Record cable ID, from, to, test method, result, and status
Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: the conductor is electrically continuous from one designated termination point to the other with no open circuit.

TC-CAB-004Cable Insulation-Resistance Testing
Verification Checks
  • Verify insulation resistance and insulation condition
  • Verify leakage indication where applicable
  • Verify conductor-to-earth and conductor-to-conductor condition where applicable
  • Before testing: isolate the circuit and confirm it is safe
  • Before testing: identify connected equipment and disconnect sensitive equipment where required
  • Before testing: use the approved test voltage and confirm test equipment calibration
  • Record cable ID, test configuration, test voltage, measured IR, acceptance criterion, and result
Hold Point

Hold Point: the insulation-resistance test voltage must be compatible with all equipment connected to the circuit — sensitive electronic equipment must not be subjected to an inappropriate megger test.

TC-CAB-005Cable Resistance Measurement
Verification Checks
  • Verify conductor resistance and circuit resistance
  • Verify resistance consistency and any unexpected resistance increase
  • Verify joint/termination contribution where applicable
  • Evaluate abnormally high resistance for poor termination, poor joint, or damaged conductor
  • Evaluate abnormally high resistance for incorrect cable, corrosion, or loose connection
  • Evaluate abnormally high resistance for excessive cable length compared with design
  • Record cable ID, expected resistance, measured resistance, test condition, and result
Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: measured resistance is consistent with expected values and shows no abnormal increase attributable to a defective connection.

C

Cable Physical Integrity

4 topics
TC-CAB-006Cable Joint Testing
Verification Checks
  • Verify correct joint location and joint identification
  • Verify correct joint kit/type and joint installation
  • Verify joint continuity, insulation, and sealing
  • Verify mechanical and environmental protection
  • Inspect for moisture ingress, poor sealing, and improper installation
  • Inspect for damaged joint, loose connection, incorrect joint kit, or visible contamination
  • Test continuity, insulation resistance, resistance, visual condition, and sealing where applicable
Note

Joint acceptance should consider both electrical integrity and environmental/mechanical integrity.

Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: joints demonstrate correct electrical continuity together with sound mechanical and environmental protection.

TC-CAB-007Cable Termination Testing
Verification Checks
  • Verify correct termination point, correct cable, and correct conductor
  • Verify termination method and contact/terminal condition
  • Verify tightness/torque where specified and insulation
  • Verify identification, strain relief, and sealing
  • Check for loose termination, exposed conductor, or damaged insulation
  • Check for incorrect terminal, wrong cable, or missing ferrule/marker where applicable
  • Check for mechanical strain
  • Trace: Cable ID → Termination ID → Equipment ID
Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: the termination is correctly made at the correct point, mechanically sound, correctly identified, and free of exposed conductor.

TC-CAB-008Cable Sheath/Condition Verification Where Applicable
Verification Checks
  • Verify cable sheath condition for cuts, abrasion, or crushing
  • Verify cable sheath condition for cracking, deformation, or chemical damage
  • Verify cable sheath condition for heat damage, moisture ingress, or mechanical damage
  • Inspect cable entry points, trench sections, and duct entries
  • Inspect base-can entries, joint areas, and equipment interfaces
  • Inspect areas exposed to mechanical stress
Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: any physical condition capable of compromising electrical or environmental integrity is investigated and resolved before acceptance.

TC-CAB-009Cable Polarity Verification
Verification Checks
  • Verify correct positive/negative identification where applicable
  • Verify correct conductor assignment and correct termination
  • Verify correct equipment connection and correct polarity through the circuit
  • Cross-check: Drawing → Cable identification → Termination → Equipment
  • Check for reversed polarity, incorrect conductor, or wrong terminal
  • Check for incorrect cable identification
Note

For AGL circuits where conventional polarity terminology does not directly apply, use the appropriate circuit identification/connection verification method rather than forcing a polarity test where it is not technically applicable.

Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: conductor assignment, termination, and connection are correctly verified against the approved drawing wherever polarity applies.

D

Cable Result Analysis

1 topic
TC-CAB-010Cable Test-Result Analysis
Verification Checks
  • Analyse continuity, insulation resistance, and cable resistance
  • Analyse joint condition, termination condition, and sheath condition
  • Analyse polarity/connection and historical results where available
  • Compare against project requirements, equipment requirements, and manufacturer requirements
  • Compare against the approved test procedure and previous test results where meaningful
  • Identify trends: falling insulation resistance, increasing resistance, repeated joint/connector failures
  • Identify trends: abnormal circuit resistance, intermittent continuity, recurrent moisture ingress
  • Classify each result as PASS, FAIL, RETEST REQUIRED, or INVESTIGATION REQUIRED
Hold Point

Hold Point: a measurement is not an acceptance decision until it has been evaluated against the applicable acceptance criterion.

PART B — CONNECTOR
E

Connector Identification & Installation

2 topics
TC-CON-011Connector Identification
Verification Checks
  • Verify connector ID, connector type, and manufacturer
  • Verify model/type, associated cable, and associated equipment
  • Verify circuit, location, and serial/batch information where applicable
  • Trace: Cable → Connector → Transformer/Fixture/Equipment
  • Check for incorrect connector or missing identification
  • Check for incorrect circuit, untraceable connector, or damaged marker
Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: every connector is correctly identified, of the correct type, and fully traceable from cable through to equipment.

TC-CON-012Connector Installation Verification
Verification Checks
  • Verify correct connector type and correct cable
  • Verify correct installation method and correct termination
  • Verify correct assembly and correct orientation
  • Verify mechanical condition, strain relief, and environmental sealing
  • Check for improper assembly or damaged connector
  • Check for incorrect cable preparation, poor termination, or mechanical stress
Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: the connector is installed according to the approved installation procedure with no assembly, termination, or mechanical defect.

F

Connector Electrical & Mechanical Testing

4 topics
TC-CON-013Connector Continuity Testing
Verification Checks
  • Verify contact continuity through the connector
  • Verify cable-to-connector continuity
  • Verify connector-to-equipment continuity
  • Confirm no open contact and no intermittent connection
  • Test principle: Cable → Connector → Equipment
  • Record connector ID, from, to, test, result, and status
Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: continuity is confirmed through the connector with no open or intermittent contact.

TC-CON-014Connector Contact Verification
Verification Checks
  • Verify contact condition, cleanliness, and position
  • Verify contact seating, engagement, and damage
  • Verify corrosion condition
  • Check for bent, damaged, or corroded contact
  • Check for contamination, loose contact, or poor contact engagement
  • Give particular attention to connectors exposed to moisture, condensation, water ingress, or corrosive environments
Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: contacts are clean, correctly seated and engaged, and free of corrosion or mechanical damage.

TC-CON-015Connector Mating Verification
Verification Checks
  • Verify correct connector pair and complete insertion
  • Verify correct alignment and proper engagement
  • Verify correct mating depth and no abnormal resistance during mating
  • Check for partial mating, cross-mating, or wrong connector
  • Check for misalignment or damaged locking mechanism
Note

Acceptance principle: a connector that appears connected is not necessarily correctly mated.

Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: connector halves are fully inserted, correctly aligned, and properly engaged to the correct mating depth.

TC-CON-016Connector Locking Verification
Verification Checks
  • Verify the lock is fully engaged
  • Verify the locking mechanism is intact
  • Verify the connector cannot unintentionally separate
  • Verify the locking arrangement is correctly installed
  • Check for broken locking mechanism or incomplete engagement
  • Check for loose connector, missing locking component, or damaged latch
  • Perform an appropriate mechanical verification where permitted by the manufacturer, without applying excessive force
Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: the locking mechanism is fully engaged, intact, and prevents unintentional separation.

G

Connector Environmental & Final Verification

3 topics
TC-CON-017Connector Sealing Verification
Verification Checks
  • Verify seal/gasket condition and cable-entry seal
  • Verify connector-body seal and proper mating seal
  • Verify water-ingress protection and environmental protection
  • Inspect for cracked seal or missing gasket
  • Inspect for damaged sealing surface or incorrect assembly
  • Inspect for moisture, water ingress, or dirt contamination
Note

Connector sealing is especially important for field-installed AGL connections exposed to underground, wet, and contaminated environments.

Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: environmental sealing is intact and protects the connector against moisture and water ingress.

TC-CON-018Connector Cable-Lead Verification
Verification Checks
  • Verify cable lead identification and correct cable
  • Verify correct length where specified and correct termination
  • Verify lead condition, insulation, strain relief, and mechanical protection
  • Check for excessive bending or mechanical strain
  • Check for damaged insulation, incorrect cable lead, or poor termination
  • Check for unsupported cable
Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: the cable lead between connector and equipment is correctly identified, terminated, and mechanically protected.

TC-CON-019Connector Electrical Verification
Verification Checks
  • Verify continuity, contact integrity, and cable-to-connector continuity
  • Verify connector-to-equipment continuity
  • Verify polarity where applicable and resistance where required
  • Verify insulation where applicable
  • Evaluate stable contact and no intermittent connection
  • Evaluate no abnormal resistance, no open circuit, and no incorrect connection
Note

The test method must be appropriate to the connector and the equipment to which it is connected.

Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria: final electrical verification confirms stable contact with no intermittent connection, abnormal resistance, or incorrect connection.

H

Connector Acceptance

1 topic
TC-CON-020Connector Acceptance Test
Acceptance Requirements
  • Identification: connector identified, correct type, correct circuit, correct cable
  • Installation: correct installation, correct termination, mechanical condition acceptable, strain relief verified
  • Electrical: continuity, contact, electrical connection, polarity, and resistance verified as applicable
  • Mechanical: correct mating, locking verified, no mechanical damage
  • Environmental: sealing verified, no moisture ingress, no corrosion, environmental protection acceptable
  • Final: cable lead verified, corrective actions completed, retesting completed, connector accepted
Acceptance / Record

Acceptance Criteria (final logic): Identification + Route + Electrical Integrity + Mechanical Integrity + Termination Quality + Connector Integrity + Environmental Sealing + Test-Result Analysis = ACCEPTED CABLE/CONNECTOR SYSTEM. Electrical PASS alone does not equal complete cable/connector acceptance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Division 12 — Cable & Connector Testing in the AGL Testing & Commissioning Manual.

Q1What is Division 12 — Cable & Connector Testing in the AGL Testing & Commissioning Manual?

Division 12 of the AGL Testing & Commissioning Manual covers Cable & Connector Testing. Every AGL circuit ultimately depends on buried, submerged, or embedded cable and connector infrastructure. Division 12 provides the detailed cable-and-connector test layer supporting the equipment-specific commissioning divisions — verifying identification, routing, electrical integrity, mechanical installation, termination and environmental integrity before any circuit is released for acceptance. Q2What is the philosophy behind Division 12 — Cable & Connector Testing?

This division follows the general pre-commissioning philosophy established in Division 01 and supports the circuit-level tests in Divisions 02–11: Identification → Route → Continuity → Insulation → Resistance → Joint → Termination → Sheath/Condition → Polarity → Analysis → Connector Identification → Installation → Continuity → Contact → Mating → Locking → Sealing → Cable Lead → Electrical Verification → Acceptance.

Q3What is the commissioning sequence for cables and connectors in Division 12?

Cable and connector testing runs as two parallel, complementary chains — every link in each chain has to hold before the item is accepted. Cable chain: Identification → Route Verification → Continuity Testing → Insulation-Resistance Testing → Resistance Measurement → Joint & Termination Testing → Sheath/Condition & Polarity Verification → Test-Result Analysis → cable accepted. Connector chain: Identification → Installation Verification → Continuity Testing → Contact Verification → Mating & Locking Verification → Sealing Verification → Cable-Lead Verification → Electrical Verification → connector acceptance test. Both chains are checked for: Identification & traceability; Electrical integrity; Mechanical & termination quality; Environmental sealing; Test-result analysis before acceptance.

B — Cable Electrical Testing: Cable Continuity Testing, Cable Insulation-Resistance Testing, Cable Resistance Measurement. C — Cable Physical Integrity: Cable Joint Testing, Cable Termination Testing, Cable Sheath/Condition Verification Where Applicable, Cable Polarity Verification. D — Cable Result Analysis: Cable Test-Result Analysis. E — Connector Identification & Installation: Connector Identification, Connector Installation Verification. F — Connector Electrical & Mechanical Testing: Connector Continuity Testing, Connector Contact Verification, Connector Mating Verification, Connector Locking Verification. G — Connector Environmental & Final Verification: Connector Sealing Verification, Connector Cable-Lead Verification, Connector Electrical Verification. H — Connector Acceptance: Connector Acceptance Test.
