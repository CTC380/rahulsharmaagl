Digital Asset Management

The digital backbone of the entire AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide. Divisions 01–20 establish how AGL assets are planned, identified, assessed, designed, procured, installed, operated, maintained, monitored, modernized, replaced and retired. Division 21 establishes how all of that lifecycle information is digitally structured, connected, monitored, analyzed and reported — the progression from digital records, to connected assets, to analytics, to intelligent lifecycle decisions.

The question this division answers — How is every AGL asset's lifecycle information digitally structured, connected, monitored, analyzed and reported so that maintenance, reliability, health, cost and replacement decisions are based on accurate, traceable data rather than isolated records?

DAM-01 Digital Asset Register
Purpose

The digital asset register becomes the single authoritative digital record for every AGL asset — the controlled destination that every other digital process ultimately writes back to.

Core Information Held
  • Asset ID & asset tag
  • Asset type & hierarchy
  • Manufacturer, model, serial number
  • Location
  • Installation & commissioning date
  • Warranty & expected service life
  • Condition & criticality
  • Health index
  • Maintenance, failure, testing, inspection & replacement history
  • Obsolescence status
  • Current operational status
Digital Principle

One asset should have one controlled digital identity throughout its complete lifecycle — not a separate, disconnected record in every system that touches it.

DAM-02 CMMS Implementation
Purpose

A Computerized Maintenance Management System (CMMS) provides the digital foundation for maintenance management — the day-to-day engine that runs preventive and corrective work.

CMMS Should Support
  • Asset records
  • Preventive & corrective maintenance
  • Work orders
  • Inspections
  • Maintenance schedules
  • Spare parts
  • Maintenance & failure history
  • Technician activities
  • Completion records
  • Maintenance KPIs
AGL Examples

A CMMS can maintain maintenance history for runway fixtures, taxiway fixtures, PAPI, CCR, isolation transformers, signs, AGL circuits and control equipment.

DAM-03 EAM Implementation
Purpose

An Enterprise Asset Management (EAM) platform provides a broader lifecycle-management environment than maintenance-only systems.

EAM May Integrate
  • Asset planning
  • Procurement
  • Asset register
  • Maintenance
  • Inspection
  • Reliability
  • Inventory
  • Cost
  • Warranty
  • Lifecycle planning
  • Replacement
  • Decommissioning
Strategic Difference
CMMS — Primarily Maintenance-FocusedEAM — Broader Asset Lifecycle Management

For the AGL framework, the important objective is not the software label but ensuring the selected digital platform supports the required lifecycle processes.

DAM-06 Digital Work Orders
Purpose

Work orders should provide a controlled digital record of maintenance activity, from the moment work is requested through to closure.

Work-Order Information
  • Work-order number
  • Asset ID & location
  • Problem & failure mode
  • Work requested / work performed
  • Technician, date & time
  • Parts used
  • Test results
  • Restoration status & downtime
  • Closure approval
Lifecycle Benefit

Every completed work order becomes part of the asset's digital maintenance history — evidence that feeds directly into DAM-07.

DAM-07 Digital Maintenance History
Purpose

Each asset should have a chronological digital maintenance history built up automatically from closed work orders.

Record
  • Preventive maintenance
  • Corrective maintenance
  • Emergency maintenance
  • Repairs
  • Component replacements
  • Adjustments
  • Refurbishment
  • Major interventions
Provides Evidence For

Reliability analysis, cost analysis, replacement decisions, RUL (remaining useful life) assessment, and warranty claims.

DAM-09 Digital Testing Records
Purpose

Testing records should be digitally linked to the asset and retained throughout its lifecycle.

Potential AGL Test Records
  • Insulation resistance
  • Continuity
  • Earth resistance
  • CCR output
  • Transformer tests
  • Fixture tests
  • PAPI tests
  • Photometric tests
  • Functional tests

DAM-10 Digital Failure Records
Purpose

Every significant failure should generate a structured digital failure record, not just a closed work order.

Record
  • Asset ID & failure date
  • Failure type & mode
  • Failure cause & consequence
  • Downtime
  • Repair action & parts used
  • Root cause
  • Recurrence
  • Cost
Connection

This connects directly to the AGL Failure Database and to Division 11 — Reliability Engineering & Failure Management.

DAM-11 Digital Twins

Potential Digital-Twin Levels
Asset Level — CCR / Transformer / Fixture / PAPI / SignSystem Level — Runway Lighting SystemAirport Level — Integrated AGL System
Potential Information
  • Asset identity & location
  • Design information
  • Operational status
  • Condition
  • Maintenance & testing
  • Performance
  • Failure history
Important

A digital twin should have a defined purpose. Creating a 3D model alone does not automatically create a useful digital twin.

DAM-13 Sensor-Based Asset Monitoring
Purpose

Sensors can provide continuous or periodic condition information beyond what periodic inspection alone can capture.

Possible Applications
  • CCR monitoring
  • Equipment temperature
  • Energy monitoring
  • Control-system status
  • Environmental monitoring
  • Selected equipment-health parameters
Important Principle

Sensor deployment should be based on Criticality + Risk + Decision Value, rather than simply installing sensors everywhere.

DAM-14 Predictive Analytics
Purpose

Predictive analytics uses historical and current information to identify patterns that may indicate future failures or deterioration.

Potential Inputs
  • Failure history
  • Maintenance history
  • Testing history
  • Inspection results
  • Operating hours
  • Condition data
  • Energy data
  • Sensor data
  • Performance trends
Potential Outputs
  • Failure probability
  • Deterioration trend
  • Maintenance priority
  • RUL estimate
  • Abnormal-condition alert
Connection

This connects directly with Division 10 — Asset Health Monitoring and Division 11 — Reliability Engineering.

DAM-15 AI-Based Asset Optimization
Purpose

AI can be used as an advanced analytical layer above the asset-management data environment, built on top of predictive analytics.

Potential Applications
  • Failure-pattern recognition
  • Maintenance optimization
  • Anomaly detection
  • RUL estimation
  • Spare forecasting
  • Replacement prioritization
  • Energy optimization
  • Asset-risk prediction
Important Governance Principle

AI outputs should be treated as decision-support information unless the application has been appropriately validated and approved for the relevant operational use. The engineer remains responsible for appropriate technical judgement.

DAM-16 Asset Dashboards
Purpose

Dashboards should convert large volumes of asset information into actionable management information.

Possible Dashboard Views
  • Asset Portfolio — total, active, decommissioned, critical & obsolete assets
  • Maintenance — PM compliance, open work orders, backlog, emergency failures, cost
  • Reliability — MTBF, MTTR, failure frequency, repeat failures
  • Health — health index, condition distribution, deterioration trends, critical alerts
  • Replacement — assets due for replacement, priority, planned CAPEX, obsolescence exposure
DAM-17 Digital Health Index
Purpose

The digital health index should provide a consolidated indication of asset condition built from multiple underlying data sources.

Possible Inputs
  • Inspection results
  • Testing results
  • Failure history
  • Maintenance history
  • Performance
  • Age
  • RUL
  • Obsolescence
  • Operating environment
Example Conceptual Classification

H1 Healthy · H2 Good · H3 Deteriorating · H4 Poor · H5 Critical. The exact scoring methodology should be standardized before implementation.

Important

The health index should be traceable to its underlying evidence — not a black-box score.

DAM-18 Data Integration
Purpose

Digital asset management becomes powerful when different information sources are connected rather than left as separate silos.

Potential Integration

Asset Register ↔ CMMS/EAM ↔ Inspection ↔ Testing ↔ Failure Database ↔ Inventory ↔ Warranty ↔ ALCMS ↔ IoT/Sensors ↔ Analytics.

Result

A single asset can have an integrated digital lifecycle history spanning every system that touches it.

Purpose

Data-Quality Dimensions
  • Accuracy
  • Completeness
  • Consistency
  • Timeliness
  • Uniqueness
  • Validity
  • Traceability
Common AGL Data Problems
  • Duplicate asset IDs
  • Missing serial numbers
  • Incorrect locations
  • Missing commissioning dates
  • Inconsistent equipment names
  • Missing test results
  • Missing warranty information
  • Incorrect asset status
  • Unlinked documents
Principle

Digital transformation without data quality simply digitizes inaccurate information.

Digital Asset Lifecycle Architecture

Division 21 is best understood as eight connected layers, running from the physical asset through to the decisions made about it.

Layer 1 — Physical Asset (CCR / Transformer / Cable / Fixture / PAPI / Sign / ALCMS)
Layer 2 — Asset Identification (Tag / ID / Location / Hierarchy)
Layer 3 — Digital Records (Register / Drawings / Manuals / Testing / Inspection / Maintenance)
Layer 4 — Operational Data (Status / Operating Hours / Energy / Performance)
Layer 5 — Connected Data (Sensors / IoT / ALCMS / Monitoring)
Layer 6 — Analytics (Condition / Reliability / Failure / RUL / Risk)
Layer 7 — Intelligence (Predictive Analytics / AI / Optimization)
Layer 8 — Lifecycle Decisions (Maintain / Repair / Refurbish / Upgrade / Replace)

Each layer depends on the integrity of the one beneath it — analytics built on poor Layer 3 records will produce poor Layer 8 decisions, however advanced the tooling in between.

Most Important Design Principle

Division 21 should not operate independently. It is the digital layer connecting the previous twenty divisions — not a replacement for them, and not limited to simply computerizing the asset register.

The maturity progression is deliberate: Digital Records → Connected Assets → Integrated Asset Data → Asset Health Monitoring → Predictive Analytics → AI-Assisted Optimization → Data-Driven AGL Lifecycle Decisions. An organization should not attempt Layer 7 before Layer 3 is trustworthy.

This makes Division 21 the digital nervous system of the entire AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide, connecting the physical AGL asset portfolio with its engineering, maintenance, reliability, cost and lifecycle decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Digital Asset Management in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide.

Q1What is Division 21 — Digital Asset Management in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

Division 21 is the digital backbone of the entire framework. It covers 20 topics on the digital asset register, CMMS/EAM, asset data architecture, digital work orders, maintenance/inspection/testing/failure history, digital twins, IoT monitoring, predictive analytics, AI-based asset optimization, dashboards, health index, data integration, data quality and lifecycle reporting.

Q2What is a digital asset register?

The digital asset register is the single authoritative digital record for every AGL asset, and the controlled destination that other digital processes ultimately write to. It sits on top of the asset data architecture, which defines how AGL information is structured and connected across every digital system.

Q3What is the difference between a CMMS and an EAM platform?

A CMMS is the digital foundation for maintenance management, running preventive and corrective work day to day. An EAM platform provides a broader lifecycle-management environment covering asset planning, procurement, the asset register, maintenance, inspection and reliability.

Q4Which records are managed digitally?

Division 21 covers digital work orders, maintenance history, inspection records, testing records and failure records. Each is linked to the relevant asset rather than left as standalone paper or PDF reports, and each significant failure should generate a structured failure record, not just a closed work order.

Q5What is a digital twin in AGL asset management?

A digital twin is a digital representation of an asset, system or facility that can be connected to relevant operational and lifecycle information, such as asset identity and location, design information and operational status.

Q6How do IoT and sensor-based monitoring support AGL assets?

IoT monitoring connects physical AGL assets to digital systems, turning equipment state into continuously available data such as status, temperature, current, voltage, energy and operating hours. Sensors add condition information beyond what periodic inspection alone can capture, for example CCR monitoring and control-system status.

Q7How are predictive analytics and AI used?

Predictive analytics uses historical and current information, such as failure, maintenance, testing and inspection history, to identify patterns that may indicate future failure or deterioration. AI is an advanced analytical layer built on top of it, for tasks such as failure-pattern recognition, maintenance optimization and anomaly detection.

Q8Why does data quality matter?

Poor data will produce poor asset-management decisions, however sophisticated the analytics layered on top. Data quality is judged on accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, uniqueness and validity, and integration connects information sources so they are not left as silos.

Q9What do dashboards, the digital health index and lifecycle reports provide?

Dashboards convert large volumes of asset information into actionable management information, such as portfolio and maintenance views. The digital health index consolidates condition from multiple data sources, and digital lifecycle reporting turns lifecycle data into information that different audiences can act on.
