AGL Technology Comparison

MANUAL VS REMOTE

Manual monitoring requires personnel to physically inspect or measure the AGL system. Remote monitoring continuously collects system information and makes it available from a control station without requiring the technician to be at the equipment. The distinction matters most for CCRs, series circuits, LED fixtures, control systems, alarms and maintenance planning.

03 · Definitions

What Each One Actually Is

Manual Monitoring

The technician physically checks the equipment. Example: a technician goes to the CCR room, observes the CCR indication and records output current — that is manual monitoring.

Typical activities

  • CCR display inspection
  • Local current measurement
  • Visual, fixture, cable, transformer & connector inspection
  • Functional testing & field photometry
  • Insulation resistance testing

Remote Monitoring

Sensors, controllers and communication systems transmit equipment information to a central location. Example: the operator sees "CCR-03 — ON — Step 4 — Current Normal — No Alarm" without physically visiting the CCR.

Typically reports

  • Output current & voltage
  • Power & intensity step
  • Alarms & circuit status
  • Event timestamps
  • Communication/network health

06 · CCR Failure Scenario

What Happens Between Inspections

Manual monitoring

If the CCR fails between inspections, the failure may remain unnoticed until someone checks the system or an operational report is received.

Remote monitoring

With suitable monitoring, the control system can generate an alarm when the CCR becomes unavailable or detects an abnormal condition — one of the major benefits of remote monitoring.

08 · What Remote Monitoring Can't Replace

Manual Inspection Is Still Necessary

Remote monitoring does not eliminate physical inspection. A monitoring system reporting "CCR current = normal" does not prove that:

  • A fixture is correctly aligned
  • A lens is clean
  • A fixture is physically damaged
  • A connector is mechanically sound
  • A transformer is physically intact
  • Vegetation is obstructing the light
  • Pavement construction has affected fixture elevation

Remote monitoring monitors system data; physical inspection monitors physical condition. Neither one replaces the other.

15 · Advantages & Disadvantages

Weighing the Trade-Offs

Manual Monitoring

Advantages

  • Simple, low infrastructure cost
  • No communication network required
  • Easy to understand
  • Useful for physical inspection, commissioning & troubleshooting

Disadvantages

  • Labour intensive
  • Periodic rather than continuous
  • Faults can remain unnoticed
  • Slower response; difficult at scale

Remote Monitoring

Advantages

  • Continuous visibility & faster fault notification
  • Centralized information & automatic alarms
  • Event logging & historical trends
  • Reduced routine field visits; better maintenance planning

Disadvantages

  • Higher initial cost
  • Communication infrastructure & software integration required
  • Cybersecurity considerations
  • Sensors/communication equipment can themselves fail

18 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: Remote monitoring makes physical inspection optional
Fact

Remote monitoring reports system data — current, voltage, alarms. It cannot confirm fixture alignment, lens cleanliness, physical damage or vegetation obstruction. Field inspection is still required.

Myth: A Smart CCR is the same thing as remote monitoring
Fact

A Smart CCR facilitates remote monitoring through built-in measurement and digital communication, but a conventional CCR can also gain remote monitoring via external add-on equipment.

Myth: Monitoring and testing are the same activity
Fact

Monitoring is continuous or periodic observation of operating condition. Testing is deliberate verification against a specified requirement — e.g. FAT, insulation resistance or field photometry.

19 · Engineering Verdict

Which One Do You Actually Need?

Engineering Verdict

Manual monitoring tells you what you find when you inspect the system. Remote monitoring tells you what the system is reporting while it is operating. Neither one replaces the other — remote monitoring covers operating parameters continuously, while physical inspection remains essential for physical condition.

For a modern AGL installation, the best approach is usually not Manual vs Remote, but Remote Monitoring + Scheduled Physical Inspection + Periodic Electrical/Photometric Testing working together.

ManualPerson → equipment → information. Best for physical condition, commissioning & troubleshooting.
RemoteEquipment → network → person. Best for continuous operating-parameter visibility & fault awareness.

20 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Does remote monitoring eliminate the need for manual inspection?

No. Remote monitoring reports operating data such as current, voltage and alarms, but it cannot confirm physical condition — fixture alignment, lens cleanliness, mechanical damage or vegetation obstruction still require field inspection.

Is a Smart CCR required for remote monitoring?

Not strictly. A Smart CCR makes remote monitoring straightforward through built-in measurement and digital communication, but a conventional CCR can also be fitted with external equipment to enable remote monitoring.

Is monitoring the same as testing?

No. Monitoring is continuous or periodic observation of operating condition — a displayed current or an alarm. Testing is deliberate verification against a specified requirement, such as FAT measurements or field photometry.

Does remote monitoring introduce cybersecurity risk?

Yes, this is an important consideration once a communication network is introduced, unlike manual monitoring where cybersecurity concern is low.

What should FAT verify for a remote-monitored CCR?

Beyond current measurement, FAT should verify alarm simulation, communication integrity, correct control-system indication, intensity-step indication and failure/event-logging behaviour.

Which approach is best for a modern AGL installation?

Usually a combination rather than either alone: remote monitoring for continuous operational visibility, plus scheduled physical inspection and periodic electrical/photometric testing.

21 · Standards & Reference Basis

Related Standards Context

Manual vs remote monitoring is primarily an engineering-practice distinction rather than a single-document standards comparison. For an actual project, verify what the applicable authority and project specification require.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome operational and maintenance-performance provisions that any monitoring approach must ultimately support.

FAA AC 150/5340 Series

Design, installation and maintenance guidance for airport visual aids, including CCR and control-system considerations.

Cybersecurity Practice

Once a communication network is introduced for remote monitoring, applicable OT/ICS cybersecurity practice becomes relevant and should be assessed for the project.

Final Engineering Note

The airport authority and project specification determine the required monitoring architecture — verify DGCA, AAI or the equivalent national CAA requirements for Indian projects.
