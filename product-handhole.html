Overview

AGL Handhole

What It Is

Handholes provide accessible underground cable-routing and maintenance points within an airfield lighting installation.

They support organised cable management and provide practical access for inspection, cable jointing, testing and maintenance without requiring extensive excavation of the surrounding airfield infrastructure.

Where They Are Used

  • Cable crossings and route transitions
  • Branch cable routes and circuit distribution points
  • Maintenance and inspection locations
  • Cable jointing and connection points
  • Field electrical infrastructure access points
  • Locations needing an accessible underground service point
  • Interfaces between cable ducts, conduits and field equipment

Handhole Construction

A Protected Underground Space

Protected Space for Cables

The handhole provides a protected underground space for airfield lighting cables and associated connections. Construction should be selected to withstand the environmental and mechanical conditions at the installation location.

Adaptable Configuration

The overall configuration can be adapted to accommodate cable quantity, routing direction, available installation space and access requirements.

Cable Entry & Routing

Controlled Routing In and Out

Entry Arrangement

Cable entries are arranged to provide controlled routing into and out of the handhole. The configuration may include multiple cable or duct entries where circuits branch or change direction.

Routing Practice

Proper routing should maintain suitable bend radii and prevent excessive mechanical stress on primary and secondary cables.

Unused entries should be sealed or protected as required by the installation design.

Cover Loading

Matched to the Installation Location

Why It Matters

The cover is an important part of the installation, particularly where the unit is located near aircraft movement areas, vehicle routes or other trafficked surfaces.

Selection

Cover loading should be selected according to the installation location and applicable project requirements. A cover in an area trafficked by aircraft must carry the design aircraft wheel loading and sit flush with the surrounding pavement. State the load class on the order (for example EN 124 classes, where Class F 900 is intended for aircraft pavements, or the applicable Indian Standard) and confirm it against the design aircraft load.

In the runway strip and other graded areas the cover must be flush with the surrounding grade so it does not become an obstacle.

The cover should provide secure access while maintaining the required mechanical protection for the underground cable infrastructure.

Drainage

Prevent Standing Water

Drainage Provisions

Drainage should prevent the accumulation of standing water within the handhole. The appropriate arrangement depends on the surrounding ground conditions, water table, pavement construction and site drainage system.

Coordination

Where required, drainage may be coordinated with the surrounding sub-base or a dedicated drainage arrangement to maintain a suitable environment for cable inspection and maintenance.

Cable Jointing & Maintenance Access

Space to Work

Access for Work

Handholes provide convenient access where cables require inspection, testing, jointing or future replacement. Maintaining sufficient internal working space is important where connections or joints are located within the handhole.

Sizing

The final dimensions should consider not only cable capacity but also the access required for installation and maintenance personnel.

Installation Considerations

Stable, Accessible Underground Infrastructure

Important Considerations

  • Excavation and foundation preparation
  • Finished installation level
  • Cable entry and exit positions
  • Duct alignment and cable bend radius
  • Cover loading requirements
  • Drainage and groundwater conditions
  • Separation from other underground services
  • Protection against cable damage during installation
  • Accessibility for inspection and maintenance

Coordination

Handholes should be installed to maintain the intended cable route.

Installation details should be coordinated with the surrounding pavement, cable duct and airfield electrical infrastructure.

Cable Duct Interface

Accessible Points Along Longer Routes

Integration with Duct Systems

Handholes can be integrated with underground cable duct systems to provide accessible points along longer cable routes.

Duct entries should be aligned with the handhole and securely terminated to prevent damage to cables during installation or subsequent maintenance.

Transition

The transition between ducted and handhole cable routing should maintain suitable mechanical protection and cable support.

Inspection & Commissioning

Confirm After Installation

  • Cover seating and accessibility
  • Correct cable and duct entry arrangement
  • Adequate cable routing and bend radius
  • Secure cable positioning
  • Drainage provisions
  • Protection of cable joints and connectors
  • Correct finished level relative to the surrounding installation
  • Cable and circuit identification labels in place
  • As-built position and level recorded

Any cable joints or electrical connections within the handhole should be inspected and tested in accordance with the applicable project commissioning procedure.

Replacement & Rehabilitation

Upgrading Existing Cable Networks

Replacement Configurations

Handholes can be used when upgrading or rehabilitating existing airfield lighting cable networks. Replacement configurations can be selected for revised cable quantities, new duct routes or changes to the electrical distribution arrangement.

Assess the Surroundings

Where an existing handhole is being replaced, the surrounding pavement, drainage and cable infrastructure should be assessed to determine the appropriate replacement configuration.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Pump out standing water before working in a handhole: wet joints and connectors raise the risk of insulation failure and shock
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures

FAQ

AGL Handhole — Frequently Asked Questions

What is an AGL handhole?

A handhole is an accessible underground cable-routing and maintenance point within an airfield lighting installation. It supports organised cable management and gives practical access for inspection, cable jointing, testing and maintenance without extensive excavation of the surrounding airfield infrastructure.

Where are AGL handholes used?

Typical locations are cable crossings and route transitions, branch cable routes and circuit distribution points, maintenance and inspection locations, cable jointing and connection points, interfaces between cable ducts, conduits and field equipment, and any location that needs an accessible underground service point.

How is the cover loading selected?

Cover loading is selected according to the installation location and the applicable loading requirements. A cover in an area trafficked by aircraft must carry the design aircraft wheel loading and sit flush with the surrounding pavement. State the load class on the order (for example EN 124 classes, where Class F 900 is intended for aircraft pavements, or the applicable Indian Standard) and confirm it against the design aircraft load. In the runway strip and other graded areas the cover must be flush with the surrounding grade so it does not become an obstacle.

What size is a handhole and how deep is it installed?

Handhole dimensions are project and cable-routing dependent, and installation depth is determined by the cable routing, duct arrangement and site requirements. Cable capacity is selected according to the number and size of cables. Final dimensions should consider not only cable capacity but also the access needed by installation and maintenance personnel, with enough internal working space where joints or connections sit inside the handhole.

How are cables routed into a handhole?

Cable entries are arranged for controlled routing into and out of the handhole, and may include multiple cable or duct entries where circuits branch or change direction. Routing should maintain suitable bend radii and prevent excessive mechanical stress on primary and secondary cables. Unused entries should be sealed or protected as required by the installation design.

How does a handhole connect to the cable duct system?

Handholes can be integrated with underground cable duct systems to provide accessible points along longer cable routes. Duct entries should be aligned with the handhole and securely terminated to prevent damage to cables during installation or later maintenance, and the transition should maintain suitable mechanical protection and cable support.

Why does handhole drainage matter?

Drainage should prevent standing water accumulating inside the handhole. The right arrangement depends on the ground conditions, water table, pavement construction and site drainage system, and it may be coordinated with the surrounding sub-base or a dedicated drainage arrangement. Standing water is also a safety issue: wet joints and connectors raise the risk of insulation failure and shock, so water should be pumped out before work.

What should be checked after installation?

Check cover seating and accessibility, the cable and duct entry arrangement, cable routing and bend radius, secure cable positioning, drainage provisions, protection of cable joints and connectors, the finished level relative to the surrounding installation, and that cable and circuit identification labels are in place. Record the as-built position and level, and inspect and test any joints or connections inside the handhole under the project commissioning procedure.

Can handholes be used when upgrading an existing cable network?

Yes. Handholes can be used when upgrading or rehabilitating existing airfield lighting cable networks, with replacement configurations selected for revised cable quantities, new duct routes or changes to the electrical distribution arrangement. Where an existing handhole is being replaced, assess the surrounding pavement, drainage and cable infrastructure first to determine the right replacement configuration.

Is it safe to work in a handhole while the circuit is energised?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage, and opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death. De-energise the circuit at the regulator, apply the site lock-out / tag-out procedure, use the series circuit cut-out switch or an approved isolation device, and verify the circuit is de-energised before touching conductors, connectors or splices. See the electrical safety warning on this page.

Which standards and references apply to AGL handholes?

Principal references are FAA AC 150/5370-10 (Item L-110 for underground electrical ducts and conduits and Item L-108 for underground cable for airports), FAA AC 150/5340-30J, ICAO Doc 9157 Aerodrome Design Manual Part 5 (Electrical Systems), and DGCA India CAR Section 4 with airport specifications. Selection and installation should also be coordinated with the airfield lighting design, pavement loading requirements and local project specifications.

What should be confirmed before ordering a handhole?

Confirm the dimensions, materials, cover loading, drainage and cable-entry arrangements against the requirements of the specific installation before procurement. The cable quantity and size, routing directions, available installation space, access requirements, and the surrounding pavement, verge or underground service conditions all feed into the configuration.
