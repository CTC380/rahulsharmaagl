FAT VS ROUTINE TEST

Two different levels of factory verification for AGL equipment such as CCRs and control panels. A Factory Routine Test asks whether the individual manufactured unit was built correctly, while a Factory Acceptance Test (FAT) asks whether that unit meets the specific project's contract, drawings and functional requirements before it ships.

02 · Core Definitions

Two Different Questions

FAT verifies that the equipment meets the project-specific requirements before shipment, usually with client/consultant witnessing. A Factory Routine Test verifies that each manufactured unit has passed the manufacturer's standard production tests.

In one line, each test answers a different question:

Routine Test"Was this unit manufactured and tested correctly?"
FAT"Does this unit meet our specific project requirements and acceptance criteria?"
03 · Factory Routine Test

Production-Stage Verification

A routine test is essentially a production test. Its objective is to confirm that the individual manufactured unit is free from defects that can be detected through the specified routine tests.

MANUFACTURING

     ↓
ROUTINE TEST
     ↓
PASS?

 ↓       ↓

For example, an AGL CCR may undergo routine checks such as:

  • Visual inspection
  • Wiring verification
  • Insulation-related tests
  • Dielectric/withstand test where applicable
  • Functional checks
  • Protection checks
  • Output verification
  • Metering verification

The exact routine tests depend on the equipment type and applicable product standard.

  • Approved drawings
  • Nameplate details

  • Remote-control and monitoring interfaces
  • Communication and interlocks
  • Performance requirements

08 · Coverage

Is Every Unit Tested?

Generally, each manufactured unit is subjected to the specified routine tests, while FAT sampling or quantity is project-specific.

13 · Where It Fits in the Project

Three Layers of Verification

For equipment such as CCRs, AGL control panels, remote monitoring systems, control and communication panels and packaged AGL equipment, the routine test and FAT should not be treated as identical tests. A good sequence carries the equipment through three distinct stages before it ever reaches site.

MANUFACTURING
      ↓
ROUTINE TEST
      ↓
ROUTINE TEST CERTIFICATE
      ↓
FAT
      ↓
CLIENT / CONSULTANT ACCEPTANCE
      ↓
DISPATCH
      ↓
SITE INSTALLATION
      ↓
SAT

This gives three different levels of verification: Routine Test → FAT → SAT.

  • Routine Test: Manufacturing quality
  • FAT: Project/equipment acceptance
  • SAT: Installed system acceptance
14 · Engineering Verdict

FAT vs Factory Routine Test

Engineering Verdict

Factory Routine Test = standard production test performed to verify the quality and functional condition of the individual manufactured unit.

FAT = project-specific acceptance test performed at the factory to confirm that the equipment meets the contract, approved design and functional requirements before shipment. For an AGL project, the strongest QA/QC approach is to treat Routine Test, FAT and SAT as three separate acceptance layers rather than using the FAT as a substitute for routine testing or SAT.

Routine Test"Did we manufacture it correctly?"
FAT"Does it meet the customer's project requirements?"
SAT"Does the installed system work correctly at site?"

Routine Test and FAT are complementary, not interchangeable — a FAT that only re-runs routine checks has not actually confirmed project acceptance, and a routine test can never substitute for witnessed, project-specific verification.

15 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fundamental difference between FAT and a Factory Routine Test?

A Factory Routine Test verifies that an individual manufactured unit was built and tested correctly against the product standard and manufacturer's procedure. FAT verifies that the unit meets project-specific requirements and acceptance criteria, typically witnessed by the client or consultant.

Is a routine test performed on every unit?

Generally, yes — each manufactured unit is subjected to the specified routine tests as part of normal production. FAT quantity or sampling, by contrast, is project-specific and should not be assumed unless stated in the contract or approved inspection and test plan.

Does FAT repeat every routine test?

Not necessarily. A FAT procedure may require review of the routine test certificates rather than repeating every production test, then add project-specific tests and witnessed functional checks on top.

Who normally witnesses a FAT versus a routine test?

Routine tests are usually witnessed by the manufacturer's own QA/QC function, with client attendance not normally required unless the contract specifies it. FAT is typically witnessed by the manufacturer together with the client and/or an appointed consultant or inspector.

Does passing FAT mean routine testing can be skipped?

No. Routine testing and FAT serve different purposes. The recommended approach is to treat Routine Test, FAT and SAT as three separate acceptance layers rather than substituting one for another.

How does FAT relate to SAT?

FAT is performed at the factory before shipment to confirm project acceptance of the equipment itself. SAT (Site Acceptance Test) is performed after installation to confirm that the installed system works correctly at site. They are sequential, not interchangeable, stages of verification.

16 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents and the project's own approved inspection and test plan.

Equipment Product Standards

The applicable product standard for the equipment type (e.g. low-voltage switchgear/CCR panel standards) defines the routine tests every manufactured unit must pass.

Project FAT Procedure & ITP

The approved project specification, contract, approved drawings and Inspection and Test Plan (ITP) define the FAT scope, sampling and witnessing requirements.

ICAO Annex 14 / FAA AC 150/5345 Series

Aerodrome design and equipment advisory documents that inform the overall AGL equipment acceptance and testing framework referenced by many project specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Do not assume a generic FAT scope or sampling rate. The final test procedure should always be developed from the current governing standard, the approved project specification and the client/consultant-agreed ITP.

A Factory Routine Test is not a substitute for FAT, and a FAT is not simply a repeat of routine testing — each verifies a different question at a different stage of acceptance.
