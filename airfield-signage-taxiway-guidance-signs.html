Airfield Signage & Taxiway Guidance Signs

Airfield signs are an essential part of the airport visual guidance system. They communicate runway, taxiway, location, direction, routing and operational information to pilots while aircraft are moving on the aerodrome. A sign is therefore not simply a board installed beside a taxiway — it is a complete engineered system involving message, colour, character, visibility, location, structure, illumination, electrical supply, control, inspection and operational performance.

Overview

What Is Airfield Signage?

Airfield signage consists of visual signs installed on an aerodrome to provide pilots with information needed for safe aircraft movement. Depending on their function, signs can communicate taxiway direction, taxiway location, runway identification, runway exit information, holding position information, runway approach information, mandatory instructions, operational information, geographic location and routing information.

The sign must present the correct information in a form that can be identified quickly from the aircraft. Message → Colour → Character → Visibility → Location → Structure → Illumination → Electrical Supply → Control → Inspection → Operational Performance.

Not Just a Board

A sign is a complete engineered system, not simply a board installed beside a taxiway.

Where, Where To, Which Way

Why It Matters

Why Airfield Signs Matter

Lighting systems show pilots where to move. Signs tell pilots where they are, where they need to go, which route they are following, and where they must stop or hold. A properly functioning taxiway sign reduces navigation uncertainty and ground movement risk — particularly in complex taxiway networks, intersections, runway exits and low-visibility operations.

Navigation Uncertainty

Reduces confusion about position and routing across the taxiway network.

Wrong Turns

Clear direction and location messaging reduces incorrect taxi routing.

Runway Entry Errors

Mandatory instruction signage supports correct holding behaviour at runway boundaries.

Taxiway ID Errors

Location signs help pilots correctly identify their position on the surface.

Ground Movement Confusion

Especially significant at intersections and complex layouts.

Low-Visibility Operations

Signage becomes particularly important during reduced-visibility ground movement.

Sign Categories

Main Airfield Sign Categories

Airfield signs can broadly be understood by their operational function. The exact sign categories, colours, message formats and installation requirements must be determined from the applicable aerodrome standard and approved airport design.

Location Signs

Identify the taxiway or other location where the aircraft is positioned — particularly useful at intersections, runway exits and complex layouts.

Direction Signs

Provide directional information for taxi routes at intersections.

Destination Signs

Identify destinations such as terminals, aprons or other operational areas where applicable.

Runway Signs

Provide runway identification and associated information.

Mandatory Instruction Signs

Provide information that requires compliance, such as runway holding-related instructions.

Information Signs

Provide additional operational or routing information.

Classification note: The six cards above describe operational functions. In ICAO Annex 14 the formal grouping is two classes — mandatory instruction signs (including runway designation and holding-position signs) and information signs (including location, direction, destination and runway-exit/vacated signs). In common ICAO/FAA practice mandatory signs are white-on-red, location signs yellow-on-black and direction/destination signs black-on-yellow — always confirm against the current applicable standard and the approved project design.
Location & Direction

Taxiway Location & Direction Signs

Location Signs

Identify the taxiway on which the aircraft is located — particularly useful at taxiway intersections, runway exits, complex taxiway layouts, junctions and changes in routing.

The pilot should be able to associate the sign message with the aircraft's actual position. Incorrect location information can create a navigation risk.

Direction Signs

Provide routing information at intersections and may indicate the taxiway direction an aircraft should follow. Engineering considerations include correct message, correct orientation, correct location, visibility from the intended movement direction, character legibility, sign illumination and structural stability.

A perfectly illuminated sign with the wrong message is still a failed operational asset.
Mandatory & Runway Signage

Mandatory Instruction & Runway Identification Signs

Mandatory Instruction Signs

Communicate information that requires particular pilot attention or compliance, and can be associated with runway holding positions, runway identification and other mandatory operational instructions.

Their colour, message format, location and visibility are highly controlled by applicable standards — these must not be treated as ordinary information boards.

Runway Identification Signage

Supports identification of runway environments and associated movement areas, and must be coordinated with runway designation, taxiway geometry, holding positions, runway entry points, runway lighting and markings.

The sign message should always correspond with the approved aerodrome layout and operational configuration.

Engineering the Message

Sign Message, Colour, Character & Arrow Engineering

Message design is one of the most important parts of airfield signage. A sign should never be fabricated directly from an informal instruction without checking the approved drawing or airport information.

Message Engineering

Before fabrication, verify correct designation, spelling, abbreviation, arrow direction, destination, runway/taxiway identification, character format, sign category and orientation.

Colour Engineering

Sign colours are function-specific — the combination communicates the type of information presented. Colour is part of the operational meaning of the sign, not an aesthetic choice.

Character & Arrow Engineering

Characters must be designed for rapid recognition from the intended viewing distance — height, width, stroke, spacing, message length and arrow geometry all matter. An incorrect arrow can be more dangerous than a missing sign.

Construction & Illumination

Sign Face Construction & Illumination

A sign face consists of engineered materials selected for structural strength, weather resistance, colour stability, character visibility, illumination compatibility and mechanical durability. Construction details depend on the approved sign type and manufacturer design.

Internally Illuminated Signs

Electrical Energy → Light Source → Internal Optical System → Sign Face → Visible Message. The engineering objective is uniform and compliant illumination of the required message.

Externally Illuminated Signs

Considerations include light-source positioning, illumination uniformity, glare, shadowing, maintenance access, weather protection, structural arrangement and electrical supply.

LED Airfield Signs

Potential advantages: lower energy consumption, long source life, reduced maintenance, fast switching, improved monitoring. LED does not automatically mean better performance — colour, luminance, uniformity and reliability must still be verified.

Where a sign is designed for connection to an AGL series circuit, the complete interface must be considered. The transformer rating, sign load and electrical interface must be compatible with the approved system.

Control

Sign Control & ALCMS

Signs may be integrated into the airport lighting control architecture. ALCMS may provide centralized control and monitoring, but ALCMS indication is not automatically proof of optical compliance — a sign may report electrically healthy while still having a dirty or damaged face, wrong colour, reduced illumination or mechanical misalignment.

Installation Fundamentals

Placement, Orientation & Foundations

Sign Placement

Verify approved location, offset from taxiway, orientation, clearance, height, viewing direction and relationship with holding positions and taxiway geometry. Check placement against approved drawings and applicable standards.

Sign Orientation

A sign may be mechanically intact but operationally ineffective if it faces the wrong direction. Aircraft Direction of Travel → Pilot Viewing Position → Sign Face → Message Recognition. Intersections require particular attention.

Foundations & Frangibility

Foundations must provide stability, alignment, load resistance and drainage. Supports must be compatible with the required aerodrome safety philosophy — including frangibility where required. Do not replace approved supports with ordinary commercial sign posts.

Sign Placement & Orientation — Plan View

Correct offset and orientation put the sign face square to the approaching pilot's line of sight at the intended reading point — not merely near the taxiway

Environmental Protection

Environmental Exposure & Water Ingress

Airfield signs operate in harsh environments — rain, dust, sand, UV radiation, heat, cold, humidity, chemicals, jet blast, wind and flooding. The enclosure and electrical system must provide appropriate protection.

Water Ingress

Common entry points include housing joints, access covers, cable entries, connectors, damaged seals and condensation paths. Water can cause insulation deterioration, corrosion, driver failure, connector failure, short circuits and intermittent operation.

Sign Cleaning

Dirty sign faces reduce visual performance. Maintenance may require removal of dust, mud, oil, rubber contamination, insects and other surface deposits. Cleaning methods must be compatible with the sign face and manufacturer requirements.

Verification

Photometry & Electrical Testing

A sign can be electrically operational but visually non-compliant. Photometric verification confirms optical performance where applicable, addressing luminance, colour, uniformity, brightness and optical distribution.

Photometric Verification

Prepare → Identify Sign → Verify Test Configuration → Measure → Compare with Applicable Criteria → PASS/FAIL → Record.

Colour & Uniformity

Colour verification uses appropriate measurement equipment against applicable criteria. Uniformity means the illuminated area should not contain unacceptable dark or excessively bright regions.

Electrical Testing

Visual inspection, continuity, insulation resistance where applicable, supply verification, current measurement, driver and transformer verification, connector inspection, functional operation and control verification.

Keyword Reference

Mandatory Instruction Signs at an Airport

Mandatory instruction signs airport

Mandatory instruction signs at an airport tell pilots and drivers where they must stop or may not enter, such as at runway holding positions and no-entry areas. They are placed beside the holding position and must be readable day and night, so they are internally lit or retro-reflective as the standard requires. Sign colours, sizes and positions follow the applicable standard and the approved sign schedule.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is airfield signage?

Airfield signage consists of visual signs installed on an aerodrome to give pilots the information needed for safe aircraft movement, such as taxiway direction and location, runway identification, runway exit, holding position, mandatory instructions and routing information. A sign is a complete engineered system involving message, colour, character, visibility, location, structure, illumination, electrical supply, control and inspection, not simply a board beside a taxiway.

Q2Why do airfield signs matter?

Lighting shows pilots where to move, while signs tell them where they are, where they need to go, which route they are following and where they must stop or hold. A properly functioning taxiway sign reduces navigation uncertainty and ground movement risk, especially in complex networks, intersections, runway exits and low-visibility operations.

Q3What are the main categories of airfield signs?

Operationally they are location signs, direction signs, destination signs, runway signs, mandatory instruction signs and information signs. In ICAO Annex 14 the formal grouping is two classes: mandatory instruction signs and information signs, which include location, direction, destination and runway-exit or vacated signs.

Q4What colours are airfield signs?

In common ICAO and FAA practice, mandatory signs are white on red, location signs are yellow on black, and direction and destination signs are black on yellow. Always confirm against the current applicable standard and approved project design.

Q5What is the difference between location signs and direction signs?

Location signs identify the taxiway or other location where the aircraft is positioned, which is particularly useful at intersections and runway exits. Direction signs provide routing information at intersections. The pilot should be able to associate a location message with the aircraft's actual position, since incorrect location information can create a navigation risk.

Q6What do mandatory instruction signs do?

They communicate information requiring particular pilot attention or compliance, and can be associated with runway holding positions, runway identification and other mandatory operational instructions. Their colour, message format, location and visibility are highly controlled by applicable standards, so they must not be treated as ordinary information boards.

Q7Does a healthy ALCMS status mean an airfield sign is compliant?

No. A sign reporting electrically healthy on ALCMS is not automatic proof of optical compliance. Signs are verified, not assumed, and a perfectly illuminated sign with the wrong message is still a failed operational asset.

Q8How do signs fit into the visual guidance system?

Signage works together with runway lighting, taxiway edge and centreline lights, stop bars, runway guard lights, approach lighting, PAPI, ALCMS, markings and aerodrome layout. The chain runs from airport geometry through markings, lighting and signs to pilot visual information, aircraft navigation and safe ground movement.

Q9Can a sign be made from an informal instruction?

No. A sign should never be fabricated directly from an informal instruction without checking the approved drawing or airport information. The sign message should always correspond with the approved aerodrome layout and operational configuration.

Q10What should be considered when engineering a direction sign?

Engineering considerations include the correct message, orientation and location, visibility from the intended movement direction, character legibility, sign illumination and structural stability.
