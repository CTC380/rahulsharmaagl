AGL-CS-078 — PAPI Alignment Error

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A PAPI alignment investigation where post-construction geometry errors caused systematic glide-path indication errors — from pilot reports through survey verification, foundation assessment, repositioning, and operational validation.

1Case Study Overview

Following runway resurfacing and shoulder rehabilitation, pilots reported that the PAPI appeared consistently high, with aircraft receiving three white and one red indication while flying the published glide path.

The PAPI units were operational, beam intensities were acceptable, and previous maintenance records indicated that beam-angle calibration had been completed.

Because the reported error suggested a systematic alignment problem, a detailed investigation was initiated covering horizontal alignment, vertical alignment, runway reference geometry, foundation levels, unit spacing, survey control points, and beam-angle verification.

The investigation identified PAPI alignment errors introduced during post-construction reinstallation, causing the entire PAPI geometry to shift relative to the runway approach path.

This case demonstrates that PAPI units can be electrically and photometrically healthy while providing incorrect glide-path guidance due to alignment errors in the overall installation geometry.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The PAPI alignment error was caused by incorrect installation geometry following runway rehabilitation, including:

  • Lateral displacement.
  • Longitudinal displacement.
  • Foundation elevation error.
  • Survey reference error.

The optical, electrical, and beam-angle systems remained healthy.

The primary failure mechanism was incorrect geometric relationship between the PAPI installation and the runway reference system.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: PAPI units ON. Beam angles correct. Intensities normal. Pilots consistently report 3W/1R after runway rehabilitation. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace the PAPI lamps.
B. Increase light intensity.
C. Perform a complete survey verification of runway reference geometry, horizontal alignment, longitudinal position, foundation elevation, and PAPI installation coordinates.
D. Replace the power supply.
Correct answer: C. Consistent 3W/1R after construction with correct beam angles and intensities points to an installation geometry problem. Survey verification will identify alignment errors without unnecessary equipment replacement.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about PAPI alignment errors after runway rehabilitation.

Q1What is AGL-CS-078?

AGL-CS-078 is an engineering training case study. It covers a PAPI that showed three white and one red on the glide path after runway resurfacing, caused by installation geometry errors. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2Why did the PAPI appear consistently high after resurfacing?

The whole installation had shifted relative to the runway reference system during post-construction reinstallation. This gave a systematic 3W/1R indication even though units, intensity and beam angles were healthy.

Q3What did the survey measure in AGL-CS-078?

Lateral offset was 16.1 m against 15.0 m required (1.1 m error). Distance from the threshold was 297.8 m against 300.0 m (2.2 m closer), and foundation elevation was +142 mm too high.

Q4Were the individual units or beam angles faulty?

No. Beam angles were acceptable, intensities were correct and unit spacing was within requirements. The error was in the overall installation geometry.

Q5Why does PAPI accuracy depend on survey accuracy?

The red/white transitions are only correct relative to the runway reference. A lateral, longitudinal or elevation error shifts the beam reference and creates a systematic glide-path error.

Q6How was the cause confirmed?

Geometry simulation reproduced the pilot reports: higher apparent glide path, earlier white indication and reduced transition accuracy. After repositioning, operational verification restored 2W/2R.

Q7What was the root cause?

Incorrect installation geometry after runway rehabilitation: lateral and longitudinal displacement, foundation elevation error and survey reference error.

Q8How was the PAPI corrected?

The team re-established survey control points, corrected runway reference geometry, repositioned the installation and corrected foundation elevations. They verified horizontal and longitudinal position, recalibrated beam angles, ran operational verification and completed post-construction certification.

Q9What is the key lesson from this case?

After runway works, verify PAPI position and elevation by survey, not only unit calibration. Perfect beam angles cannot compensate for a shifted installation.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

PAPI alignment errors are installation-geometry failures caused by incorrect runway reference positioning, foundation elevation changes, and survey inaccuracies, and a PAPI system can be electrically and photometrically healthy while providing systematically incorrect glide-path guidance due to geometric misalignment.

Systematic survey verification, runway-reference measurement, installation-coordinate confirmation, foundation assessment, beam-angle validation, and operational glide-path verification can identify alignment errors and restore ICAO-compliant PAPI performance.

In AGL engineering, PAPI is ultimately a surveying instrument built from lights, and geometric accuracy is the foundation of every correct glide-path indication.
