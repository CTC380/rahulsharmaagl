Product Overview

AGL Isolating Transformer (L-831)

What It Is

AGL Isolating Transformer (L-831) couples individual airfield light fixtures to the primary series circuit while providing the required electrical isolation between the primary circuit and the fixture circuit.

It transfers energy from the constant-current series circuit to the individual fixture, helping isolate the fixture and secondary circuit from the primary high-voltage series circuit. It also lets the series circuit stay closed if a fixture lamp or its secondary fails open, so one failed light does not interrupt the whole circuit.

What We Supply

Precision AGL Solutions supplies isolating transformers selected according to fixture wattage, series-circuit current, operating frequency, insulation requirements, cable interfaces and project specifications.

Each fixture is supplied through its associated transformer, so individual fixtures connect to the common series circuit without being directly exposed to the primary circuit voltage. The arrangement is widely used for runway, taxiway, approach and other airfield lighting.

L-831 is the 50 Hz version of the FAA L-830. FAA AC 150/5345-47C designates "type L-830 for 60 Hz operation and type L-831 for 50 Hz operation", so it is the designation to look for on series circuits fed from a 50 Hz supply, as in India.

L-830 vs L-831

Same Family, Different Frequency

FAA AC 150/5345-47C covers both designations. They are paired one-to-one, and the numerical suffix gives the wattage and current rating.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Do not disconnect a transformer secondary or fixture lead while the primary circuit is energised: with the secondary open the transformer can develop a high open-circuit voltage
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures

FAQ

AGL Isolating Transformer (L-831) — Frequently Asked Questions

What is an L-831 isolating transformer?

An L-831 isolating transformer is the 50 Hz version of the FAA L-830. It couples an individual airfield light fixture to the primary series circuit with electrical isolation, and it lets the series circuit stay closed if a lamp or its secondary fails open. FAA AC 150/5345-47C designates type L-830 for 60 Hz operation and type L-831 for 50 Hz operation.

What is the difference between the L-830 and the L-831?

The rated frequency: 60 Hz for the L-830 and 50 Hz for the L-831. The two are paired one-to-one, with type numbers L-830-1 to L-830-19 and L-831-1 to L-831-19, and the same suffix means the same watt and current rating. For example, both the L-830-1 and the L-831-1 are 30/45 W with 6.6 A primary and 6.6 A secondary. See the L-830 isolating transformer page for the 60 Hz designation.

Which isolating transformer should I use on a 50 Hz series circuit?

Use the L-831 on series circuits fed from a 50 Hz supply, as in India, Europe and most of Asia. A 60 Hz L-830 on a 50 Hz circuit is not an equivalent substitute, so confirm the nameplate frequency. IEC 61823 transformers are an alternative reference where the project specifies IEC.

What ratings does the L-831 cover?

Under AC 150/5345-47C the types cover about 10/15 W up to 500 W, with primary and secondary current of 6.6 A or 20 A depending on the type. The primary winding is rated 5,000 V and the secondary winding 600 V, and the maximum secondary open-circuit voltage varies by type. Always confirm against the model datasheet and the project specification.

How do I select the right transformer rating?

Select against the fixture wattage, the series-circuit current, the operating frequency, the secondary requirements, the insulation requirements and the installation environment. Avoid heavily under-loaded transformers, for example a high-wattage unit feeding a low-power LED fixture, because this can distort circuit current and affect regulator loading. Check LED fixture and transformer pairing with the manufacturer.

Where are L-831 isolating transformers used?

They form part of series circuits for runway edge, threshold, end, centreline and touchdown zone lighting, taxiway edge and centreline lighting, stop bars, runway guard and intermediate holding position lighting, approach lighting and apron lighting, as well as replacement and rehabilitation work. Approach lighting use is subject to the electrical requirements of the individual fixture and project.

How is an L-831 installed and commissioned?

Install it to the approved design, confirming the rating, the primary circuit current and the connected fixture wattage, checking cable identification, using compatible connectors and protecting connections against moisture. At commissioning, verify insulation and electrical integrity as required, energise the circuit under controlled conditions, confirm fixture operation at the required intensity levels, check for abnormal heating or leakage and record the results.

What are common causes when a fixture does not operate?

Possible causes include an open primary series circuit, a damaged primary connector, a transformer fault, a damaged secondary lead or connector, a fixture fault, an incorrect transformer rating, or a CCR or circuit-control issue. A complete circuit assessment may be needed before replacing the transformer again, because repeated failures are not always transformer-only problems.

What should I confirm before replacing an existing transformer?

Confirm the existing transformer designation, rating, connected fixture wattage, series-circuit current, frequency, primary and secondary connector types, cable requirements, physical installation constraints and the current project specification. Check the nameplate, which is marked with the specification and the type, L-830 or L-831.

Which standards apply to the L-831?

FAA AC 150/5345-47C covers both the L-830 and the L-831, with installation details in AC 150/5340-30J. Depending on the project, IEC 61823, ICAO Doc 9157 Part 5, ICAO Annex 14 Volume I and airport authority specifications may also apply. Confirm the current edition and the rating required by the circuit design on the manufacturer datasheet.

Is it safe to disconnect a transformer secondary while the circuit is energised?

No. With the secondary open the transformer can develop a high open-circuit voltage, and opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised series circuit can cause serious injury or death. De-energise the circuit at the regulator, apply the site lock-out / tag-out procedure, use the series circuit cut-out switch or an approved isolation device and work under the permit-to-work system. See the electrical safety warning on this page.

What information should I provide when requesting an L-831?

Provide the fixture type and wattage, the existing transformer rating if replacing, the series-circuit current, the operating frequency, primary and secondary connector requirements, the required cable length, the installation environment, the airport or project specification and the quantity. Photographs or nameplate information of the existing transformer help identify the correct configuration.
