How to Inspect and Replace Airfield Lights

Plan the inspection, inspect inset and elevated fixtures, record defects properly, replace a fixture under control and verify the work before handback. General guidance only; the approved airport procedure and manufacturer instructions always govern.

Start with Preparation Replacement Workflow
Overview

Inspection Finds Problems. Replacement Must Be Controlled.

Airfield lighting site inspection helps identify defective fixtures, physical damage, contamination, installation issues and conditions that may affect lighting performance.

AGL systems include runway edge, centreline, threshold and runway-end lights, taxiway edge and centreline lights, approach lights and other visual aids. Each equipment type has specific installation, inspection and maintenance requirements.

The inspection method depends on the fixture design, location, operating environment, electrical configuration and airport maintenance procedure. Inset runway lights and elevated taxiway lights, for example, have different mounting arrangements and may require different inspection approaches.

This guide explains how to plan an AGL inspection, identify common defects, inspect inset and elevated fixtures, coordinate a fixture replacement, conduct night inspections and document the required verification.

Important safety notice: All work on operational airfield lighting must be performed by authorized personnel following the airport's approved access, operational coordination, electrical isolation, maintenance and handback procedures. The steps in this guide are general guidance, not a substitute for the equipment manufacturer's instructions or approved maintenance procedure.

1 · Prepare

Prepare for the AGL Site Inspection

Preparation helps ensure that the inspection is performed on the correct equipment, within the authorized area and using the appropriate inspection method.

Before visiting the site, review the inspection scope, relevant drawings, equipment identification, previous defect reports and applicable maintenance instructions.

1.2 – 1.3 Identification and Objective

1.2 Check the Equipment Identification

Confirm the light's location and intended function using the approved layout and equipment records. A fixture can look similar to another type while having a different optical arrangement, electrical rating or installation requirement; physical appearance alone is not sufficient to select a replacement. Record the asset or fixture identification wherever available. If the identification or circuit association is uncertain, resolve it through the authorized process before proceeding.

1.3 Understand the Inspection Objective

An AGL inspection may be intended to:

  • Identify visible damage or contamination.
  • Check the physical condition of an installation.
  • Investigate a reported lighting failure.
  • Review a recurring defect.
  • Verify work completed during maintenance.
  • Assess a replacement fixture.
  • Support a formal testing or acceptance activity.
  • Document the condition of the lighting system at a particular time.
  • The objective determines what must be checked and whether additional testing is required.
2 · Inset Lights

How to Inspect Inset Runway Lights

Inset lights are installed within the pavement surface. They are used in applications such as runway centreline lighting, touchdown zone lighting and specified runway or taxiway configurations. Inspection should consider both the fixture itself and the surrounding pavement and mounting interface.

2.2 – 2.5 Inspection Points

2.2 Inspect the Optical Surface

The lens or optical surface affects the distribution of light emitted by the fixture. Contamination, cracks, damage or deterioration may affect performance and may require corrective action. Record the observed condition and location; cleaning or replacement decisions should follow the manufacturer's maintenance instructions. A surface that appears clean does not prove that the fixture meets its specified optical performance.

2.3 Inspect the Surrounding Pavement

The condition of the pavement around an inset fitting can be relevant to the installation's structural condition and serviceability. Record visible cracking, deterioration, damage or other abnormalities. Where the scope requires it, the mounting arrangement and civil interface may need further examination. Do not assume that replacing the light alone will resolve a problem involving the pavement or mounting arrangement.

2.4 Inspect for Moisture and Physical Damage

Where observable, note water ingress indicators, corrosion, damaged seals and other conditions identified by the approved procedure. The actual cause may involve the fitting, connector, sealing arrangement, installation condition or another component. Confirm the cause through appropriate investigation rather than treating every moisture-related observation as the same failure.

2.5 Verify Inset Light Performance

Visual inspection is only one part of assessing an inset light. Where required by the applicable procedure, additional verification may include electrical or functional testing, alignment checks, photometric assessment or other specified acceptance activities. The method and acceptance criteria depend on the equipment type, application and governing documents.

3 · Elevated Lights

How to Inspect Elevated Runway and Taxiway Lights

Elevated fittings are mounted above the surrounding surface using the specified base, support or frangible mounting arrangement. Inspection should cover the optical surface, physical housing, mounting arrangement, visible connections and the fixture's intended orientation.

3.2 – 3.3 Orientation and Mounting

3.2 Check Orientation and Installation Condition

The correct orientation of a light can be important to its intended visual function. Verify the applicable direction, alignment and installation details against the approved drawing and manufacturer instructions. If the light appears misaligned or incorrectly positioned, record the observation and arrange the required assessment. Do not adjust a fixture solely by eye where the approved procedure specifies a defined alignment or measurement method.

3.3 Inspect Accessible Mounting Components

Record relevant visible damage, looseness, corrosion or deterioration according to the inspection procedure. The required action depends on the component and manufacturer instructions. Do not assume that a mounting component can be tightened, adjusted or reused without checking the applicable maintenance requirements.

5 · Defects

How to Identify and Record Airfield Light Defects

A good inspection record separates the observed symptom from the suspected cause and the confirmed diagnosis.

For example, “light not operating” describes a symptom. A defective fixture component, connector problem, transformer fault or circuit issue may be possible causes, but the cause should not be recorded as confirmed without evidence.

6 · Replacement

How to Replace an Airfield Light Fixture

Fixture replacement must be carried out by competent, authorized personnel using the approved maintenance procedure and the manufacturer's instructions. The correct method depends on the fixture design, mounting arrangement, electrical configuration, location and reason for replacement. Inset and elevated lights may require different tools, components and acceptance checks.

6.1 General Replacement Workflow

Step 1: Verify the asset and replacement specification

Confirm the fixture identity, intended function, required model and relevant technical characteristics using approved records. Verify that the proposed replacement is compatible with the specified system.

Step 2: Obtain the necessary authorization

Confirm the permitted work scope, airport access authorization, work-zone arrangements and operational coordination requirements.

Step 3: Establish the required safe condition

Apply the approved electrical isolation and hazardous-energy control procedure applicable to the task. Verify the required condition as defined by that procedure.

Step 4: Inspect the surrounding installation

Before replacement, record relevant visible conditions involving the mounting arrangement, accessible connections and surrounding pavement or structure.

Step 5: Remove the defective fixture

Use the manufacturer-approved removal method for the specific fixture. Protect components and associated installation features from damage.

Step 6: Assess associated components

Inspect the relevant connector, mounting interface, sealing components and associated hardware as required by the maintenance procedure. Any further work must remain within the authorized scope.

Step 7: Confirm the replacement component

Check the replacement's identification, configuration, specified ratings and compatibility with the intended application.

Step 8: Install the replacement

Follow the approved manufacturer instructions and project requirements for installation, mounting, connections and any specified sealing or assembly requirements.

Step 9: Complete required inspections and tests

Perform the checks specified for the replacement and affected equipment. The appropriate scope depends on the product, work performed and applicable acceptance procedure.

Step 10: Record the work and findings

Document the old and new equipment identification where required, parts used, work performed, observations, tests, defects and supporting records.

Step 11: Complete the required handback

Confirm that personnel, tools and materials are accounted for, the work area has been cleared, and the required technical and operational handback process is complete.

This workflow is an overview, not a detailed fixture removal or installation method. Specific assembly, torque, sealing, connection and test requirements must come from the relevant manufacturer instructions and approved project documents.

6.2 – 6.3 Compatibility and Root Cause

6.2 Confirm Replacement Compatibility

A physically similar fixture is not automatically a suitable replacement. Before installation, verify applicable factors such as:

  • Manufacturer and model, where specified.
  • Fixture type and intended application.
  • Optical arrangement and directionality.
  • Electrical and control compatibility.
  • Mounting arrangement and required accessories.
  • Environmental and mechanical requirements.
  • Applicable qualification, documentation and approvals.
  • Required post-installation verification.
  • Where an alternative model is proposed, document the technical review and obtain the required approval before use.

6.3 Do Not Assume Fixture Replacement Resolves Every Fault

A failed light may be associated with a transformer, connector, secondary connection, primary circuit or control issue. If the failure symptoms or test results suggest a wider problem, follow the approved fault-investigation process. Repeatedly replacing fixtures without evaluating the underlying cause may lead to recurring defects and unnecessary maintenance. Related reading: Why Airfield Lights Fail.

7 · Verification

What Should Be Checked After Fixture Replacement?

After replacement, the applicable inspection and testing process should confirm that the work meets the specified requirements. The scope must correspond to the equipment and work performed. A successful visual inspection does not automatically demonstrate that every electrical, functional or optical requirement has been met.

Use the acceptance criteria defined by the approved procedure. Do not apply generic electrical test values or mechanical assembly settings to all fixture types.

Technical Completion Versus Operational Release

Completing the replacement and passing the required technical checks does not automatically authorize an affected runway or taxiway for operational use.

Any required maintenance handback, lighting availability update, operational confirmation or formal acceptance must follow the airport's approved process.

8 · Night Inspection

How to Perform an AGL Night Inspection

Night inspection can help identify lighting outages, inconsistent appearance, contamination, alignment concerns and other visible abnormalities that may be difficult to assess during daylight. It is a useful inspection method, but it does not replace every required physical, electrical or photometric check.

8.1 Preparation for Night Inspection

Before the inspection

  • Confirm the approved inspection scope and time.
  • Obtain the required movement-area access authorization.
  • Review the approved inspection route and relevant lighting layouts.
  • Confirm communication with the designated airport authority.
  • Verify the vehicle and work-zone arrangements where applicable.
  • Check weather, visibility and other environmental conditions.
  • Ensure the required PPE and inspection equipment are available.
  • Review open defects and the required reporting priority.
  • Confirm the procedure for stopping work or withdrawing from the area.

8.2 Night Inspection Checklist

During the authorized inspection, record the following where applicable:

8.3 – 8.4 Limits and Safety

8.3 What Night Inspection Can and Cannot Confirm

Night inspection can help identify visible outages, obvious changes in light appearance and selected alignment or distribution concerns. However, visual observations alone do not establish all electrical characteristics, photometric compliance, sealing integrity, internal component condition or mounting requirements. Further assessment may be needed under the applicable inspection, testing and maintenance procedure.

8.4 Movement-Area Safety During Night Inspection

Night inspections must remain within the authorized access and operational arrangements. Personnel should use the approved communication and vehicle procedures, remain inside the permitted work area, maintain awareness of other movement and stop or withdraw when required by the airport's instructions. Do not create unofficial work zones, obstruct movement areas or alter equipment status without authorization.

10 · Frequency

How Often Should Airfield Lights Be Inspected?

Inspection frequency depends on the airport's maintenance programme, the applicable requirements, equipment instructions, operating conditions and observed reliability. A single frequency should not be assumed to apply to all lighting systems or all types of inspection.

Factors Affecting Inspection Frequency

  • Applicable regulatory and airport requirements.
  • Lighting system type and operational importance.
  • Equipment manufacturer's maintenance recommendations.
  • Operating environment and exposure to contamination or physical damage.
  • History of failures and recurring defects.
  • Results of previous inspections and testing.
  • Construction, pavement or maintenance activities near the equipment.
  • Specific inspection requirements following repair or replacement.
  • The airport's approved preventive and corrective maintenance programme.

Scheduled inspection should be supplemented by investigation of reported faults and by any additional checks required after maintenance or modification.

Maintain records of inspections, failures, replacement history and corrective actions so the maintenance team can identify recurring issues and review the effectiveness of the programme.

11 · Mistakes

Common Mistakes During Airfield Light Inspection

Inspecting the light without confirming its identity.

Similar-looking fixtures can have different applications, ratings or optical arrangements.

Replacing the fitting without investigating the symptom.

A circuit or connection issue can produce the same apparent failure as a defective fixture.

Ignoring the surrounding pavement.

Inset light issues may involve the mounting interface or pavement condition as well as the fitting itself.

Treating night inspection as complete acceptance.

Visual inspection does not demonstrate every electrical, optical, mechanical or photometric requirement.

Using an unverified replacement.

Physical fit alone does not establish compatibility or compliance with the approved specification.

Adjusting the fixture by eye.

Where a defined alignment method or reference is required, follow the approved procedure.

Using generic test values or assembly settings.

Apply only the specified methods, limits and instructions for the equipment and task.

Failing to record the defect.

Incomplete records make recurring failures, warranty issues and maintenance history difficult to evaluate.

Ignoring outstanding findings.

Unresolved defects and restrictions must be recorded and handled through the airport's approved process.

Skipping the handback.

Completing the physical repair does not automatically complete the technical and operational restoration process.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1How often should runway lights be inspected?

The frequency depends on the airport's approved maintenance programme, applicable requirements, operating environment, equipment instructions and history of defects. Use the schedule established for the specific airport and lighting system.

Q2What should be checked during an inset runway light inspection?

Typical inspection items include the lens or optical surface, physical condition, evidence of moisture, mounting and pavement interface, accessible connections, identification and operating condition. Additional electrical or photometric testing may be required.

Q3How do I inspect an elevated taxiway light?

Verify the fixture identity and approved location, then inspect its optical surface, housing, mounting arrangement, orientation and accessible connections according to the applicable procedure. Record defects and arrange the required follow-up.

Q4Can an inset light be replaced without checking its transformer?

The required scope depends on the reported fault and approved maintenance procedure. A fixture failure may involve the transformer, connectors, associated wiring or another circuit component, so the investigation should consider relevant evidence rather than assume that the fixture alone is defective.

Q5Is a night inspection sufficient for acceptance?

Not necessarily. Night inspection provides visual evidence but may need to be supplemented by the prescribed electrical, functional, alignment or photometric checks.

Q6Can I use any fixture that fits the existing mounting arrangement?

No. Confirm that the replacement meets the specified equipment, optical, electrical, mechanical and environmental requirements and has the necessary approvals for the project.

Q7What should be recorded after replacing an airfield light?

Record the asset and location, original and replacement fixture identification, work performed, relevant associated components inspected, test procedures and results, outstanding defects, and required review or handback.

Q8What should happen if multiple lights fail on the same circuit?

Report the condition through the approved airport process, establish the scope of the fault and investigate the relevant circuit and associated equipment using authorized procedures. Do not automatically assume that every affected fixture must be replaced.
