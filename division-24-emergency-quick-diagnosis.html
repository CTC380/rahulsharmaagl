Emergency Quick-Diagnosis

Rapid-response diagnostic guidance for safety-critical, high-impact AGL events — CCR trip triage, complete runway/taxiway lighting loss, runway centreline, approach lighting, and stop bar failures, multiple circuit and major earth-fault/cable-fault events, major water ingress, lightning damage, flood-related failure, airport-wide communication and ALCMS system failures, and backup lighting failure. Each topic is structured for speed under operational pressure, while still following the full engineering diagnostic record: symptom → observation → measurement → decision → fault location → root cause → verification → corrective action → preventive action.

EMR-566 CCR Trip Emergency Diagnosis

Symptom — A CCR has tripped and the associated runway or taxiway lighting circuit is dark during active or imminent operations, requiring the fastest possible determination of whether a safe reset is appropriate or the circuit must remain out of service.

Equipment / System

Constant Current Regulator and its protection system, assessed under time pressure to support an immediate operational decision rather than a full root-cause investigation.

Possible Causes
HighA genuine, still-present fault on the circuit (earth fault, short, open) that will re-trip immediately on reset
HighA transient event (lightning-induced surge, brief external short) that has cleared and a reset will hold
MedCCR-internal protection relay or control card fault rather than a circuit-side fault
MedAn operator or automated ALCMS command misread as a fault trip
LowNuisance trip from a marginal protection setting under normal operating conditions
Required Tools
CCR panel indicators and event log (immediate read, no disassembly)True-RMS multimeter (CAT III) for a quick isolation checkATC/apron control communication link1000V insulation resistance tester (megger) if time permits before reset
Safety Precautions
  • Do not reset a tripped CCR more than once without at least a basic isolation check — repeated resets onto a live fault risk equipment damage and delay a safe outcome.
  • Notify ATC/airfield operations of the affected circuit's status before and immediately after any reset attempt so NOTAM action can be issued without delay if needed.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationRead the CCR's trip indicator/fault code immediately to determine the trip type (overcurrent, earth fault, thermal, manual) without waiting for a full log review.
  2. DecisionTrip type indicates a likely transient (e.g. a single earth-fault trip with no prior history) → proceed to a single controlled reset attempt. Trip type indicates a hard fault (overcurrent, repeated trips) → do not reset; escalate directly to fault isolation.
  3. MeasurementIf a reset is attempted, monitor CCR output immediately after reset for a clean, stable start with no immediate re-trip.
  4. Fault LocationIf reset holds, continue monitoring and schedule full root-cause investigation per Division 01 without further delay to normal operations. If reset fails or re-trips, keep the circuit out of service and begin systematic fault isolation.
  5. Root CauseCommunicate the confirmed operational status (restored, or out of service) to ATC/airfield operations immediately so any required NOTAM can be issued or lifted without delay.
Test Points
CCR trip type/fault code
CCR output immediately after any reset attempt
Circuit status communicated to ATC
Time from trip event to operational decision
Root Cause Verification

Confirm circuit status is correctly reflected in ATC/NOTAM systems and hand off to the standard CCR troubleshooting procedure for full root-cause investigation once the immediate operational decision is made.

Corrective Action
  • Perform a single controlled reset only where the trip pattern indicates a likely transient.
  • Keep the circuit out of service and proceed to systematic fault isolation where the trip indicates a hard or repeated fault.
  • Escalate to backup/alternate lighting configuration per local contingency procedure if the affected circuit is safety-critical and cannot be quickly restored.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain an up-to-date, quick-reference trip-response decision aid at every CCR location for use under time pressure.
  • Ensure ATC/airfield operations communication protocols for lighting status changes are rehearsed and readily actionable.
  • Feed every emergency reset event back into the full Division 01 root-cause process rather than closing out on the reset alone.

EMR-567 Complete Runway Lighting Failure

Symptom — All or substantially all lighting on an active runway (edge, centreline, threshold) is reported dark simultaneously, requiring immediate assessment of scope, cause category, and operational impact ahead of any detailed fault-finding.

Equipment / System

The full runway lighting circuit set (edge lights, centreline, threshold/end lights) and their common upstream supply, assessed at the system level for a total-loss event.

Possible Causes
HighLoss of the common upstream power feed to the equipment vault (utility supply failure or main breaker trip)
HighA vault-level event (fire, flood, major equipment fault) affecting all CCRs serving that runway simultaneously
MedAn ALCMS/control system fault issuing an unintended all-off command to the runway's circuits
MedA common conduit/duct bank failure (e.g. from excavation damage) severing multiple circuits at once
LowCoincidental simultaneous individual circuit trips, statistically unlikely but not impossible
LowA deliberate maintenance action or test in progress that was not communicated to the reporting party
Required Tools
Vault/CCR room visual and instrument checkMain incoming power supply status indicatorALCMS command/status logImmediate ATC/airfield operations communication link
Safety Precautions
  • Notify ATC and issue or request a NOTAM/runway closure action immediately given the safety-critical nature of a total runway lighting loss, before completing detailed diagnosis.
  • LOTO and follow standard electrical safety procedures once on-site, but treat the initial notification and closure action as the first priority.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationImmediately confirm with ATC/airfield operations the full scope of the outage — which runway, which light types, and whether it is total or partial — and ensure the runway is taken out of service for the affected operation type if required.
  2. MeasurementOn arrival at the vault, check the main incoming power supply status before checking individual CCRs, since a total loss most commonly traces to a common upstream cause.
  3. MeasurementIf incoming power is confirmed present, check ALCMS command/status log for an all-off command, then check each CCR's individual status.
  4. DecisionUpstream power loss confirmed → escalate to the utility/facility electrical team while keeping the runway closed. Power present but ALCMS/CCR-level cause found → proceed to targeted restoration of the specific fault.
  5. Fault LocationPrioritise identifying whether the cause is common (vault/power/ALCMS) or coincidental (multiple independent circuit faults) to guide the restoration approach.
  6. Root CauseConfirm full restoration of all affected lighting and coordinate with ATC to lift the NOTAM/closure only once verified.
Test Points
Main incoming power supply status
ALCMS command/status log for an all-off event
Individual CCR status for each affected circuit
Confirmed full restoration before NOTAM lift
Root Cause Verification

Conduct a full visual/night verification that all previously affected lighting is restored and operating correctly before coordinating with ATC to lift any closure or NOTAM.

Corrective Action
  • Restore upstream power supply or escalate to the responsible utility/facility team if the cause is external.
  • Correct the ALCMS command/control fault if identified as the cause.
  • Repair the specific vault-level or duct-bank fault identified.
  • Coordinate with ATC throughout to keep the runway status accurately reflected until full restoration is confirmed.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a documented, rehearsed total-lighting-loss response procedure with clear roles for notification, diagnosis, and restoration.
  • Ensure backup/emergency power provisions for the vault are tested on a defined schedule.
  • Review common-cause single points of failure (shared power feed, shared conduit) in system design to reduce total-loss risk.
Related References

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

EMR-569 Runway Centreline Failure

Symptom — The runway centreline lighting circuit is reported dark or significantly degraded during active or imminent low-visibility operations, requiring immediate assessment given its role in guiding aircraft during reduced visibility.

Equipment / System

Runway centreline lighting circuit, its serving CCR, and its criticality to Category II/III or low-visibility operations at that runway.

Possible Causes
HighA circuit-level fault (open, short, earth fault) has tripped the CCR serving the centreline circuit specifically
HighA cable fault has developed at a specific point, taking out the circuit downstream of that point in a series configuration
MedCCR feeding the centreline circuit has an internal fault distinct from the circuit itself
MedAn ALCMS command error has switched off the centreline circuit specifically
LowA partial failure (some segments dark) rather than a complete circuit loss, from a localised fault
LowPhotometric degradation below the required intensity for low-visibility operations without a complete outage
Required Tools
CCR panel indicators and event log for the centreline circuit specificallyTrue-RMS multimeter (CAT III)Series circuit fault locator / TDRImmediate ATC communication link given Cat II/III operational impact
Safety Precautions
  • Notify ATC immediately given the direct impact on low-visibility operational minima — centreline failure typically requires an immediate NOTAM restricting Cat II/III operations until restored.
  • LOTO the circuit before any fault investigation.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm with ATC the exact extent of the centreline outage (full circuit or specific segments) and its immediate impact on published operational minima.
  2. MeasurementCheck the CCR serving the centreline circuit specifically for its trip status and fault code, isolating it from other runway circuits that may be operating normally.
  3. MeasurementIf the CCR shows a circuit-side fault, proceed directly to segment-by-segment fault location using the series circuit fault locator, prioritising speed given the operational impact.
  4. DecisionFault isolated to a specific segment → proceed to targeted repair at that point. CCR-internal fault → escalate to CCR-level troubleshooting while considering a backup CCR or alternate feed if available.
  5. Fault LocationUse the fastest available fault-location method (TDR, segment isolation) given the operational urgency of restoring centreline guidance.
  6. Root CauseConfirm full restoration and photometric compliance before coordinating with ATC to lift the Cat II/III restriction.
Test Points
CCR status specific to the centreline circuit
Fault location result from TDR/segment isolation
Photometric intensity after restoration
Confirmed restoration before minima restriction lift
Root Cause Verification

Verify photometric intensity meets the required standard for the operational category being restored, and confirm with ATC before the Cat II/III restriction is lifted.

Corrective Action
  • Repair the specific cable, connector, or fixture fault identified at the fault-located segment.
  • Switch to a backup CCR or alternate feed if available and the primary CCR requires extended repair.
  • Restore the ALCMS command state if an erroneous command was the cause.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a rapid fault-location capability (TDR, trained crew) specifically available for centreline and other Cat II/III-critical circuits given their operational sensitivity.
  • Ensure backup CCR or alternate feed arrangements exist and are tested for centreline circuits at runways with low-visibility operations.
  • Prioritise centreline circuit PM and insulation trending given the operational consequence of any failure.
Related References

EMR-570 Approach Lighting Failure

Symptom — The Approach Lighting System serving a runway is reported dark or significantly degraded ahead of or during operations, requiring immediate assessment given its critical role in the approach and landing phase, particularly in reduced visibility.

Equipment / System

Approach Lighting System (ALS) circuits, including centreline bars, crossbars, and sequenced flashers, and their serving CCR(s).

Possible Causes
HighA circuit-level fault has tripped the CCR serving the ALS, taking out all or part of the system
HighA cable fault at a specific point in the ALS series circuit, particularly vulnerable given the extended run length typical of approach lighting
MedSequenced flasher controller fault, distinct from the steady-burning bar/crossbar circuit
MedCCR feeding the ALS has an internal fault
LowA partial failure affecting only the outer bars/crossbars rather than the full system
LowAn ALCMS command error has switched off the ALS
Required Tools
CCR panel indicators and event log for the ALS circuit specificallyTrue-RMS multimeter (CAT III)Series circuit fault locator / TDR suitable for extended cable runsImmediate ATC communication link given approach-phase operational impact
Safety Precautions
  • Notify ATC immediately given the direct impact on approach minima — ALS failure typically requires an immediate NOTAM restricting the approach category until restored.
  • LOTO the circuit before any fault investigation, noting the ALS structure may extend into an area requiring additional access coordination.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm with ATC the exact extent of the ALS outage (full system, specific bars, or the sequenced flashers only) and its immediate impact on published approach minima.
  2. MeasurementCheck the CCR(s) serving the ALS for trip status and fault code, and separately check the sequenced flasher controller if flashers are affected.
  3. MeasurementIf a circuit-side fault is indicated, proceed directly to segment-by-segment fault location given the extended cable run typical of ALS installations, prioritising speed given the operational impact.
  4. DecisionFault isolated to a specific segment or bar → proceed to targeted repair. CCR or flasher-controller-internal fault → escalate to the relevant equipment-level troubleshooting while considering any available backup configuration.
  5. Fault LocationUse the fastest available fault-location method given the operational urgency of restoring approach guidance.
  6. Root CauseConfirm full restoration before coordinating with ATC to lift the approach-minima restriction.
Test Points
CCR status specific to the ALS circuit(s)
Sequenced flasher controller status if applicable
Fault location result from TDR/segment isolation
Confirmed restoration before minima restriction lift
Root Cause Verification

Verify all bars/crossbars and, if applicable, the sequenced flashers are operating correctly, and confirm with ATC before the approach-minima restriction is lifted.

Corrective Action
  • Repair the specific cable, connector, or fixture fault identified at the fault-located segment.
  • Repair or reset the sequenced flasher controller if that was the cause.
  • Switch to a backup CCR or alternate feed if available and the primary requires extended repair.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a rapid fault-location capability specifically available for ALS circuits given their operational sensitivity and extended cable runs.
  • Ensure backup CCR arrangements exist and are tested for ALS circuits at runways with precision approach operations.
  • Prioritise ALS circuit PM and insulation trending given the operational consequence of any failure.

EMR-571 Stop Bar Failure

Symptom — A runway-holding-position stop bar is reported dark, stuck illuminated, or otherwise malfunctioning, requiring immediate assessment given its direct role in preventing runway incursions, particularly during low-visibility ground movement operations.

Equipment / System

Stop bar lighting circuit (typically red, elevated or in-pavement, at a runway-holding position), its controller/interlock logic, and its serving CCR.

Possible Causes
HighA circuit-level fault has caused the stop bar to fail dark, removing the visual stop indication entirely
HighA controller/interlock fault has caused the stop bar to fail illuminated (stuck on) when it should have been selectively extinguished, or vice versa, creating a conflicting indication
MedCCR feeding the stop bar circuit has an internal fault
MedA communication fault between the ALCMS/tower control and the stop bar controller, causing it to not respond to a selection command
LowA single fixture failure within the stop bar rather than a full circuit loss
LowAn interlock logic fault with an associated lead-on light, causing an inconsistent overall indication at the holding position
Required Tools
CCR panel indicators and event log for the stop bar circuitStop bar controller/interlock status panelTrue-RMS multimeter (CAT III)Immediate ATC communication link given direct runway incursion risk
Safety Precautions
  • Notify ATC immediately — a stop bar failure, whether dark or stuck in the wrong state, is a direct runway incursion risk and normally requires immediate alternate traffic control measures (e.g. positive ATC control of that holding position) until restored.
  • LOTO the circuit before any fault investigation, coordinating timing with ATC given the safety-critical nature of the location.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm with ATC the exact nature of the fault — fully dark, stuck illuminated, or an inconsistent/conflicting indication with the associated lead-on light — and ensure alternate traffic control measures are in place at that holding position.
  2. MeasurementCheck the CCR serving the stop bar circuit for trip status, and separately check the controller/interlock panel for its logic state and any communication fault with ALCMS/tower control.
  3. MeasurementIf a circuit-side fault is indicated, proceed to fault location prioritising speed given the direct safety impact.
  4. DecisionFault isolated to the circuit/CCR → proceed to targeted repair. Fault isolated to the controller/interlock or communication link → escalate to controller-level troubleshooting while alternate traffic control measures remain in place.
  5. Fault LocationPrioritise identifying whether the fault is circuit-level (dark) or logic-level (stuck/conflicting), as these require different corrective paths.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the stop bar responds correctly to a test selection command in both states before coordinating with ATC to remove alternate traffic control measures.
Test Points
CCR status for the stop bar circuit
Controller/interlock logic state and communication status
Fixture-level status within the stop bar
Confirmed correct response to test selection before restriction lift
Root Cause Verification

Test the stop bar through both selection states (on/off) and confirm correct, consistent response before coordinating with ATC to remove alternate traffic control measures at that holding position.

Corrective Action
  • Repair the specific circuit, connector, or fixture fault identified.
  • Repair or reset the controller/interlock logic fault, or restore the ALCMS/tower communication link.
  • Verify and correct the associated lead-on light interlock if an inconsistent indication was found.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a rapid-response capability specifically for stop bar faults given their direct runway incursion safety role.
  • Test stop bar interlock logic and communication links on a defined PM schedule, not only when a fault is reported.
  • Ensure ATC has a clear, rehearsed alternate traffic control procedure for a stop bar failure at every runway-holding position.
Related References

EMR-572 Multiple Circuit Failure

Symptom — Two or more independent lighting circuits are reported dark or faulted at approximately the same time, requiring immediate assessment of whether a common cause links them before treating each as a separate, unrelated fault.

Equipment / System

Multiple independent lighting circuits and their common upstream infrastructure (shared vault, shared power feed, shared control system), assessed collectively for a coincident-failure event.

Possible Causes
HighA common upstream cause — shared power feed, shared vault event, or shared control system fault — affecting multiple circuits simultaneously
HighA single physical event (excavation strike, vehicle impact, fire) damaging a shared duct bank or vault serving multiple circuits
MedA lightning strike or major power-quality event affecting multiple, but not necessarily all, circuits sharing a common exposure
MedCoincidental but genuinely independent faults on unrelated circuits, statistically less likely but requiring confirmation rather than assumption
LowAn ALCMS-level fault issuing unintended commands to multiple circuits
LowA shared component (e.g. a common protective relay) affecting multiple circuits it serves
Required Tools
Vault/CCR room visual and instrument check across all affected circuitsALCMS command/status log for all affected circuitsSite/vault single-line diagram showing shared infrastructureImmediate ATC/apron control communication link
Safety Precautions
  • Notify ATC/airfield operations of the full scope of affected circuits immediately, since a multi-circuit failure often has greater operational impact than any single circuit loss would suggest.
  • LOTO all affected circuits before investigation, and treat the possibility of a common hazardous cause (fire, major fault) as requiring extra caution on initial approach.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationList every circuit reported affected and cross-reference against the site single-line diagram to identify any shared upstream infrastructure (power feed, vault, duct bank, control system).
  2. MeasurementCheck the shared infrastructure first — main power supply, vault condition, ALCMS command log — before investigating each circuit individually.
  3. MeasurementIf no common cause is found at the shared-infrastructure level, proceed to independently investigate each affected circuit per its relevant division procedure.
  4. DecisionCommon cause identified → prioritise restoring the shared infrastructure, which should restore multiple circuits simultaneously. No common cause found → treat as coincidental independent faults and allocate resources to investigate each separately.
  5. Fault LocationUse the single-line diagram systematically to avoid missing a shared point that is not immediately obvious from the affected circuit list alone.
  6. Root CauseConfirm all affected circuits are individually verified restored, since a common-cause fix does not guarantee every downstream circuit recovers without an additional local issue.
Test Points
Shared infrastructure status (power, vault, ALCMS)
Individual status of each affected circuit
Correlation against the site single-line diagram
Confirmed restoration of every affected circuit individually
Root Cause Verification

Individually verify and photometrically/functionally check every circuit initially reported affected, even after a common cause is corrected, to confirm each has fully recovered.

Corrective Action
  • Restore the identified shared infrastructure fault (power feed, vault equipment, ALCMS/control system).
  • Investigate and repair each circuit individually if no common cause is found.
  • Coordinate with ATC/apron control to reflect the true operational status of each affected area throughout.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain an up-to-date single-line diagram showing shared infrastructure for rapid reference during a multi-circuit event.
  • Review system design for excessive common-cause risk (e.g. too many critical circuits sharing a single feed) and address through capital planning where practical.
  • Rehearse a multi-circuit failure response scenario as part of emergency response training.

EMR-573 Major Earth Fault

Symptom — A severe earth-fault event trips CCR protection with a fault magnitude, sound, or visible indication (smoke, arcing) suggesting a serious rather than marginal fault, requiring an emergency rather than routine response.

Equipment / System

Circuit insulation system and earthing/bonding path, assessed under emergency conditions for a severe, actively hazardous earth-fault event rather than a marginal or trending fault.

Possible Causes
HighA cable or connector has suffered severe insulation breakdown, potentially with visible arcing, smoke, or fire risk at the fault point
HighWater/flood ingress has caused a sudden, severe insulation failure across a section of the circuit
MedThird-party excavation or mechanical damage has severed and exposed a live conductor
MedA lightning strike has caused severe, localised insulation breakdown at one or more points
LowA major equipment failure (transformer, CCR internal fault) has caused a severe fault rather than the field cable itself
LowA pre-existing marginal fault has finally failed catastrophically after a period of gradual insulation degradation
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)Series circuit fault locator / TDRThermal imaging camera for arcing/fire risk assessmentFire extinguisher and emergency response coordination per site procedure
Safety Precautions
  • Treat any indication of smoke, arcing, or burning smell as a potential fire hazard and follow site fire/emergency response procedure before or in parallel with electrical isolation.
  • LOTO the circuit immediately and do not attempt any reset given the severity indicators; verify zero energy before any physical approach to the suspected fault point.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationAssess the severity indicators reported (trip type, any smoke/arcing/smell, location) to confirm this is a major event requiring emergency-level response rather than routine earth-fault investigation.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit confirmed isolated and safe to approach, megger the circuit segment-by-segment to localise the fault as quickly as possible given the operational and safety urgency.
  3. MeasurementUse TDR/fault-locating equipment in parallel to cross-confirm the approximate fault distance and speed up physical location.
  4. DecisionFault localised to an accessible point → proceed directly to excavation/repair. Fault location remains ambiguous → escalate for additional specialist fault-location resource given the urgency.
  5. Fault LocationPrioritise speed and safety together — do not compromise isolation verification even under operational pressure to restore the circuit quickly.
  6. Root CauseConfirm full insulation resistance recovery and clean re-energisation before returning the circuit to service, and coordinate with ATC on the restored status.
Test Points
Insulation resistance segment-by-segment
TDR/fault-locator distance estimate
Physical evidence at the fault point (arcing, water, mechanical damage)
Post-repair insulation resistance and clean re-energisation
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-megger the full circuit and confirm a clean, stable re-energisation with no immediate re-trip before returning the circuit to full service and notifying ATC.

Corrective Action
  • Excavate and repair the located cable/connector fault.
  • Address any fire-risk conditions found before re-energising.
  • Escalate to specialist fault-location resource if the fault cannot be localised promptly with standard equipment.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain rapid-response fault-location equipment and trained personnel available for major earth-fault events, distinct from routine PM resourcing.
  • Ensure fire/emergency response coordination procedures are rehearsed for electrical fault scenarios.
  • Investigate whether the severity of the event indicates a broader condition (e.g. a flood or lightning event) requiring wider system checks beyond the single fault point.
Related References

EMR-574 Major Cable Fault

Symptom — A cable fault of significant scale — a confirmed physical severance, a fault affecting a long circuit segment, or one caused by a known major event (excavation strike, flood, fire) — requires emergency-level fault location and repair resourcing beyond routine troubleshooting.

Equipment / System

Primary series or feeder cable, assessed under emergency conditions where the scale or cause of the fault (severance, major excavation damage, widespread event) requires a coordinated rapid response.

Possible Causes
HighA confirmed physical cable severance from third-party excavation, requiring both electrical fault confirmation and physical/civil coordination for repair access
HighFire or explosion damage to a cable run, requiring safety clearance before any electrical work can begin
MedFlood or major water event affecting an extended cable run, requiring assessment of the full affected length rather than a single point
MedA vehicle or aircraft strike on above-ground or shallow-buried cable infrastructure
LowA latent, extensive cable batch defect manifesting as multiple near-simultaneous faults along one run
LowDeliberate sabotage or vandalism, requiring coordination with security/law enforcement alongside the electrical response
Required Tools
Series circuit fault locator / TDRExcavation equipment and civil works coordination1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)Emergency response coordination with airport operations, civil, and (where relevant) security teams
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate with civil/excavation teams and confirm no other utilities are at risk before any excavation begins, particularly following a known excavation strike event.
  • LOTO the circuit and confirm zero energy before any physical access to a severed or damaged cable, treating any exposed conductor as live until proven otherwise.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationEstablish the known cause and approximate location of the major fault from the reporting party (excavation crew, fire response, flood report) to focus the initial response.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, use TDR/fault-locating equipment to confirm the fault distance and cross-check against the reported location and the as-built cable route drawing.
  3. MeasurementAssess the full extent of affected cable — a single point severance versus an extended damaged length — before committing to a repair scope.
  4. DecisionFault confirmed as a single, accessible point → proceed to excavation and splice/repair. Fault extends over a significant length or the cable is otherwise compromised along its run → plan for a full segment replacement rather than a single splice.
  5. Fault LocationCoordinate fault-location results with civil/excavation teams to minimise excavation extent while ensuring the full damaged length is addressed.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the repaired or replaced segment passes full insulation and continuity testing before re-energising and returning the circuit to service.
Test Points
TDR/fault-locator distance vs. reported/as-built location
Extent of cable damage along the affected length
Insulation resistance and continuity after repair
Confirmed clean re-energisation
Root Cause Verification

Fully test the repaired or replaced cable segment (insulation resistance, continuity, and a clean re-energisation with no immediate fault) before returning the circuit to full service.

Corrective Action
  • Splice-repair a single confirmed fault point using a rated below-grade splice kit.
  • Replace an extended damaged segment rather than attempting multiple splices where the damage is widespread.
  • Coordinate with civil teams to properly reinstate pavement/surface following excavation.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain emergency cable repair materials (splice kits, spare cable reels) in stock for rapid major-fault response.
  • Establish clear coordination protocols with civil/excavation contractors and utility locate services to reduce the risk and speed the response to future strikes.
  • Review as-built cable route accuracy periodically so emergency fault-location efforts are not delayed by inaccurate records.

EMR-575 Major Water Ingress Event

Symptom — A significant water ingress event — affecting multiple base cans, a section of vault, or triggered by a burst water main or major storm — is reported, distinct from a routine single-connector water-ingress fault, requiring a coordinated emergency response.

Equipment / System

Multiple base cans, pull boxes, and potentially the equipment vault, assessed collectively following a confirmed significant water ingress event.

Possible Causes
HighA burst water main or major utility failure has released a large volume of water into the AGL infrastructure area
HighExtreme rainfall or storm surge has overwhelmed drainage across a section of the field
MedA construction dewatering activity nearby has directed unexpected water volume toward AGL infrastructure
MedIrrigation system malfunction has caused sustained water release into an area with base cans
LowA slow-developing but eventually significant leak from an unrelated buried utility
LowSnow melt combined with inadequate drainage causing a significant but slower-developing water event
Required Tools
Wet/dry vacuum and pumping equipment for large-scale water removal1000V insulation resistance tester (megger) for post-event circuit assessmentWater source identification/coordination with utility or civil teamsEmergency response coordination with airport operations
Safety Precautions
  • Treat all affected circuits and equipment as energised hazards until isolation is confirmed at the CCR/vault level, given the scale of the event.
  • Coordinate with the responsible utility or civil team to stop the water source, in parallel with electrical isolation, since continued inflow will undermine any electrical remediation.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationMap the full extent of the water-affected area — which base cans, pull boxes, or vault areas — before beginning individual fault investigation, and identify and address the water source.
  2. MeasurementWith all affected circuits confirmed isolated, systematically megger each affected circuit segment before attempting to re-energise anything.
  3. MeasurementAssess whether water reached the equipment vault/CCR room and inspect that equipment separately and thoroughly before considering system-wide re-energisation.
  4. DecisionCircuit-level ingress only, vault unaffected → proceed with systematic pump-out, drying, and insulation verification circuit by circuit. Vault/CCR affected → treat as a higher-priority, system-wide event requiring full equipment assessment before any re-energisation.
  5. Fault LocationPrioritise fault investigation and restoration by circuit criticality given the scale of the event.
  6. Root CauseConfirm every affected circuit passes insulation testing before re-energising, and monitor for a secondary fault wave as residual moisture continues to affect components over subsequent days.
Test Points
Extent of water-affected base cans/circuits/vault
Insulation resistance for every affected circuit before re-energising
Vault/CCR equipment condition if affected
Water source identification and confirmation it has been stopped
Root Cause Verification

Systematically re-verify insulation resistance for every affected circuit before and after re-energising, and continue monitoring for a secondary fault wave over the following days.

Corrective Action
  • Stop the water source in coordination with the responsible utility or civil team.
  • Pump out and dry all affected base cans, pull boxes, and vault areas.
  • Replace components confirmed damaged by water exposure rather than only drying and re-testing marginal readings.
  • Prioritise restoration by circuit criticality.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a water-event emergency response procedure specific to the AGL system, including coordination steps with utility/civil teams to address the source.
  • Identify water-vulnerable base cans and vault areas in advance and consider protective measures proactively.
  • Coordinate with facility utility management to reduce the risk and response time for burst-main-type events near AGL infrastructure.

EMR-576 Lightning Damage Event

Symptom — Multiple circuits, CCRs, or ALCMS components show simultaneous or near-simultaneous faults following a reported or confirmed lightning strike in the vicinity, requiring a coordinated survey rather than isolated single-fault troubleshooting.

Equipment / System

AGL system components across potentially multiple circuits, CCRs, surge protection devices, and control system equipment, assessed collectively for lightning-induced damage.

Possible Causes
HighA direct or near-direct lightning strike has caused surge damage across multiple CCRs, control cards, or surge protection devices simultaneously
HighInduced surge on multiple cable runs from a nearby strike has damaged connector or fixture-level electronics at various points without a direct hit
MedSurge protection devices have functioned as designed and sacrificed themselves, requiring systematic replacement across affected locations
MedA single severe strike point has caused localised but severe cable insulation damage requiring standard major-cable-fault response in addition to the wider survey
LowSome reported faults following the lightning event are unrelated and coincidental, requiring differentiation from genuine strike-related damage
LowALCMS or communication equipment damaged by the surge, affecting monitoring/control capability alongside the field damage
Required Tools
Surge protection device inspection kit1000V insulation resistance tester (megger) for systematic post-strike surveyCCR and control card diagnostic toolsSite-wide equipment inventory for systematic survey tracking
Safety Precautions
  • Confirm no ongoing lightning risk before sending personnel to survey outdoor equipment; follow site lightning safety procedures for timing of the response.
  • LOTO all circuits before internal inspection of CCRs, surge protection devices, or control equipment suspected of strike damage.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationEstablish the approximate strike location/time from available lightning detection data or reports, and identify which circuits/equipment are within the likely affected radius.
  2. MeasurementSystematically survey CCRs, surge protection devices, and control equipment across the likely affected area for damage indicators (blown surge devices, tripped protection, control card faults) rather than waiting for each to be individually reported.
  3. MeasurementMegger circuits within the affected area systematically to catch induced insulation damage that may not have caused an immediate trip.
  4. DecisionDamage pattern is widespread across the affected area → proceed with a full systematic replacement/repair campaign rather than treating each as an isolated fault. Damage is isolated to one or two points → handle as standard single-fault troubleshooting but keep the wider area on watch.
  5. Fault LocationPrioritise the systematic survey over reactive one-at-a-time troubleshooting, since lightning damage often presents as multiple simultaneous minor faults rather than one obvious major fault.
  6. Root CauseConfirm all systematically surveyed equipment within the affected area is tested and restored or replaced before considering the event closed out.
Test Points
Surge protection device condition across the affected area
Insulation resistance across circuits in the affected area
CCR/control card diagnostic results
Confirmed restoration across the full surveyed area
Root Cause Verification

Conduct a follow-up functional and photometric check across the full affected area over the following days, since some lightning-induced degradation can manifest as delayed rather than immediate failure.

Corrective Action
  • Replace sacrificed surge protection devices across all affected locations.
  • Repair or replace CCR control cards or other equipment confirmed damaged by the surge.
  • Repair localised severe cable damage at the direct or near-direct strike point using the major cable fault procedure.
  • Restore ALCMS/communication equipment if affected.
Preventive Action
  • Ensure surge protection devices are installed and rated appropriately at all CCRs and vulnerable equipment locations given the site's lightning exposure.
  • Maintain a lightning-event response procedure that defaults to a systematic area survey rather than isolated fault response.
  • Monitor local lightning detection data to trigger a proactive survey promptly after a confirmed nearby strike.

EMR-577 Flooding-Related AGL Failure

Symptom — A broad flooding event (from extreme weather, storm surge, or a major water infrastructure failure) has affected AGL infrastructure across a significant portion of the airfield, requiring an emergency-scale, coordinated multi-agency response distinct from a localised major water ingress event.

Equipment / System

AGL infrastructure across a significant portion of the airfield — multiple base cans, cable routes, vaults, and potentially above-ground equipment — assessed collectively during and after a broad flooding event.

Possible Causes
HighStorm surge or extreme rainfall has inundated a large area of the airfield, affecting AGL infrastructure across multiple runways/taxiways simultaneously
HighA major water infrastructure failure (dam, levee, large water main) has released a significant, uncontrolled volume of water onto the airfield
MedDrainage system capacity across the site has been overwhelmed well beyond its design basis
MedFlood water has reached one or more equipment vaults, posing a system-wide electrical safety risk
LowFlood water carrying debris has caused widespread mechanical damage to above-ground fixtures and signs in addition to the electrical impact
LowThe flooding event coincides with and compounds other emergency conditions (e.g. loss of external power) requiring integrated emergency management
Required Tools
Large-scale pumping and water removal equipment1000V insulation resistance tester (megger) for systematic post-flood surveyAirfield emergency management coordination (multi-agency)Flood extent mapping/GIS tools where available
Safety Precautions
  • Follow the airport's overall emergency management plan for a flooding event; AGL response is one component of a broader coordinated response involving airport operations, emergency services, and possibly civil authorities.
  • Treat all flood-affected AGL infrastructure as an electrical hazard until systematically isolated and confirmed safe, given the scale and potential for vault-level impact.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCoordinate with the airport emergency management structure to understand the full scope of the flooding event and its impact on operational areas before beginning AGL-specific assessment.
  2. MeasurementSystematically isolate and assess all AGL circuits within the flood-affected area, prioritising by operational criticality (active runway circuits first) rather than by convenience of access.
  3. MeasurementAssess vault/equipment room impact separately and thoroughly, since this can pose a broader system risk beyond any single circuit.
  4. DecisionFlooding is receding and localised → proceed with systematic circuit-by-circuit recovery per the major water ingress procedure, prioritised by criticality.   5. Fault LocationPrioritise safety and operational criticality over completeness of individual fault diagnosis during the acute phase of the event.
  6. Root CauseConfirm systematic insulation testing and restoration of every affected circuit as flood water recedes, coordinating the sequence with airport emergency management and ATC.

Flood extent and impact on AGL infrastructure by area
Vault/equipment room impact assessment
Insulation resistance for every affected circuit, tested systematically
Phased restoration status coordinated with emergency management
Root Cause Verification

As flood water recedes, systematically re-verify insulation resistance and functional/photometric performance for every affected circuit, coordinating the restoration sequence and any required NOTAM actions with airport emergency management and ATC.

Corrective Action
  • Execute a phased, criticality-prioritised recovery of AGL circuits as conditions allow.
  • Pump out and dry affected vaults, base cans, and pull boxes systematically across the affected area.
  • Replace components confirmed damaged by flood exposure.
  • Coordinate all restoration milestones with airport emergency management and ATC.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain an AGL-specific annex within the airport's overall emergency management plan for major flooding events.
  • Identify flood-vulnerable AGL infrastructure across the site in advance for prioritised protective measures where practical.
  • Participate in airport-wide flood emergency exercises to ensure the AGL response is integrated with the broader emergency management structure.

EMR-578 Airport-Wide AGL Communication Failure

Symptom — The communication link between the ALCMS and multiple or all CCRs/field equipment across the airport is lost, leaving controllers unable to monitor or command lighting status even though the lighting itself may still be functioning normally at the field level.

Equipment / System

ALCMS-to-CCR communication network (fibre, copper, or wireless link and associated network equipment) across the site, assessed for a widespread or total communication loss distinct from a lighting-circuit fault.

Possible Causes
HighA failure at the central communication network switch/hub or ALCMS server affecting the link to all or most CCRs simultaneously
HighPhysical damage to a shared communication backbone (fibre cut, major cable damage) serving multiple CCRs
MedA software/firmware fault in the ALCMS or network equipment causing a widespread communication failure without physical damage
MedA power loss to the central network equipment or ALCMS server room, distinct from a loss of lighting power itself
LowA cyber/network security event affecting communication availability
LowA configuration error pushed to multiple CCRs simultaneously during a maintenance update, disrupting communication rather than lighting function
Required Tools
ALCMS server and network equipment status panelNetwork diagnostic tools (ping, link status) for the communication backboneIndividual CCR local control panel for manual status verificationIT/network support coordination contact
Safety Precautions
  • Confirm the actual lighting status at the field level directly (via CCR local panels or visual check) rather than relying solely on ALCMS, since lighting may still be functioning correctly despite the communication loss.
  • Coordinate with ATC to clarify that this is a monitoring/control communication issue and confirm whether operational lighting status can still be managed via local CCR control if needed.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the scope of the communication loss — all CCRs, a specific zone, or intermittent — and cross-check whether the underlying lighting circuits are still functioning normally at the field level.
  2. MeasurementCheck the central ALCMS server and network equipment status first, since a widespread loss usually traces to a central point rather than many simultaneous field-level link failures.
  3. MeasurementIf the central equipment appears healthy, use network diagnostic tools to trace the communication path toward the affected CCRs to isolate a backbone break.
  4. DecisionCentral ALCMS/server or network equipment fault confirmed → escalate to IT/network support for restoration while confirming lighting itself remains locally controllable. Backbone break confirmed → proceed to physical fault location on the communication cable/network path.
  5. Fault LocationDistinguish between a central-equipment cause and a distributed backbone/cable cause before committing resources to physical fault-finding.
  6. Root CauseConfirm ALCMS visibility and control is restored to all previously affected CCRs and cross-check reported status against local panel status before closing out.
Test Points
ALCMS server/network equipment status
Local CCR status vs. ALCMS-reported status for affected units
Network diagnostic trace results
Confirmed restored ALCMS visibility/control across all affected CCRs
Root Cause Verification

After restoration, cross-check ALCMS-reported status against local CCR panel status for every previously affected unit to confirm accurate communication has been restored, not just that the link is technically up.

Corrective Action
  • Restore or repair the central ALCMS server/network equipment fault.
  • Repair a physically damaged communication backbone/cable.
  • Escalate to IT/network support for a software/firmware or configuration fault.
  • Maintain local CCR control and manual status reporting to ATC throughout the outage as a contingency.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a documented contingency procedure for continuing safe lighting operations via local CCR control during an ALCMS communication outage.
  • Ensure redundant communication paths exist for critical CCRs where practical to reduce single-point-of-failure risk.
  • Coordinate with IT/network support on a rapid-response protocol specific to ALCMS communication given its operational criticality.

Symptom — The Airfield Lighting Control and Monitoring System itself is reported down, unresponsive, or providing clearly erroneous status/control information across multiple circuits, distinct from a communication link loss where the ALCMS server itself is at fault.

Equipment / System

ALCMS server, application software, and its central hardware, assessed for a system-level failure affecting monitoring/control capability across the airport.

Possible Causes
HighALCMS server hardware failure (disk, power supply, main board) taking the application fully offline
HighA software crash or critical application fault rendering the ALCMS unresponsive or providing corrupted status/control data
MedA failed software update or patch has left the ALCMS in a degraded or non-functional state
MedDatabase corruption affecting the ALCMS's ability to correctly track and display circuit status
LowA cyber security event affecting ALCMS availability or integrity
LowA licensing or authentication system fault preventing normal ALCMS operator access despite the underlying server being otherwise healthy
Required Tools

Safety Precautions
  • Immediately confirm and communicate to ATC that lighting circuits should be verified via local CCR control/manual observation, since ALCMS status cannot be trusted during a confirmed system failure.
  • Follow manufacturer-specified safe shutdown/restart procedures for the ALCMS server rather than attempting an uncontrolled restart, which risks further data corruption.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the nature of the failure — fully offline, unresponsive but powered, or providing erroneous data — through direct observation of the server and application.
  2. MeasurementCheck server hardware status/diagnostics first to rule in or out a hardware-level cause before assuming a software fault.
  3. MeasurementIf hardware appears healthy, review recent update/patch history and application logs for the point of failure.
  4. DecisionHardware fault confirmed → escalate for hardware repair/replacement or failover to a backup/redundant server if available. Software/application fault confirmed → follow manufacturer-specified recovery procedure, restoring from backup if database corruption is involved.
  5. Fault LocationPrioritise restoring basic monitoring/control capability, even in a degraded form, over pursuing a complete root-cause diagnosis during the acute outage.
  6. Root CauseConfirm ALCMS status accurately reflects verified local CCR status for every circuit before returning to normal ALCMS-based operation, given the risk of residual data corruption after a major failure.

ALCMS server hardware diagnostic results
Application/database integrity after recovery
ALCMS-reported status vs. verified local CCR status for all circuits
Confirmed stable operation over a subsequent monitoring period
Root Cause Verification

After recovery, cross-check ALCMS status against local CCR panel status for every circuit airport-wide before fully trusting ALCMS-based operation again, and monitor system stability over the following operating period.

Corrective Action
  • Fail over to a backup/redundant ALCMS server if available.
  • Restore the ALCMS application/database from a verified backup following manufacturer procedure.
  • Escalate to the manufacturer for hardware or software support where in-house recovery is not possible.
  • Maintain manual/local CCR control and status reporting to ATC throughout the outage.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a tested backup/redundant ALCMS server configuration for critical airports.
  • Verify ALCMS backup integrity on a defined schedule so a recovery can be trusted when needed.
  • Maintain a current manufacturer support contract and escalation contact for rapid response to a major ALCMS failure.

EMR-580 Backup Lighting Failure

Symptom — A backup/emergency lighting system (standby generator-fed circuits, battery-backed portable/emergency runway lighting, or a secondary CCR configuration) fails to activate or perform correctly when called upon following a primary system failure.

Equipment / System

Backup/emergency lighting provision — standby generator, battery backup, or secondary CCR/feed configuration — assessed specifically for its failure to perform its intended role during a primary system outage.

Possible Causes
HighStandby generator failed to start or failed to reach stable output within the required transfer time
HighAutomatic transfer switch failed to correctly transfer load to the backup source
MedBattery backup system depleted or failed due to inadequate maintenance/testing since the last verified full-capacity test
MedBackup lighting circuits themselves have an independent fault (unrelated to the backup power source) that was not detected because the backup system is rarely energised
LowBackup system activated correctly but was not sized/configured for the full load actually required during this specific outage
LowManual backup activation procedure was not followed correctly by on-site personnel during the emergency
Required Tools
Standby generator status panel and manual start capabilityAutomatic transfer switch diagnostic toolsBattery backup system load-test equipmentBackup lighting circuit-specific test equipment (megger, multimeter)
Safety Precautions
  • Notify ATC immediately that backup lighting has failed to activate, since this compounds the operational impact of the original primary failure and may require additional restrictions.
  • Follow manufacturer safety procedures for generator and transfer switch inspection, particularly if a failed start or transfer is suspected to involve a fuel, mechanical, or electrical fault.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm exactly what backup element failed — generator start, transfer switch operation, battery capacity, or the backup circuit itself — rather than treating the whole backup system as a single fault.
  2. MeasurementCheck the generator start sequence and transfer switch operation logs (if available) for the specific point of failure during the attempted activation.
  3. MeasurementIf the power source and transfer functioned correctly, test the backup lighting circuits themselves for an independent fault.
  4. DecisionGenerator/transfer switch fault confirmed → escalate to the responsible mechanical/electrical backup power team for repair. Backup circuit-specific fault confirmed → treat as a standard lighting circuit fault per the relevant division, noting it went undetected due to infrequent energisation.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate whether the failure is in the power source, the transfer mechanism, or the lighting circuit itself, since each requires a different responsible team and repair approach.
  6. Root CauseConfirm a full functional test of the backup system — start, transfer, and lighting circuit operation together — before considering it restored to service, not just a fix of the single component that failed.
Test Points
Generator start sequence log/result
Transfer switch operation status
Battery backup capacity test result
Backup lighting circuit test result (insulation, continuity, functional)
Root Cause Verification

Conduct a full end-to-end functional test of the backup system (start, transfer, and lighting operation together) and confirm it performs correctly before considering the backup provision restored.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or service the standby generator if it failed to start or reach stable output.
  • Repair or replace a failed automatic transfer switch.
  • Recharge, service, or replace a depleted/failed battery backup system.
  • Repair the independent backup circuit fault per the relevant division procedure.
Preventive Action
  • Conduct full-load, end-to-end backup system tests (not just a generator start test) on a defined regular schedule so latent faults are caught before an actual emergency.
  • Include backup lighting circuits in the routine PM cycle despite their infrequent energisation, to catch independent faults proactively.
  • Verify backup system sizing/configuration against actual required load periodically, particularly after any system expansion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Division 24 — Emergency Quick-Diagnosis in the AGL Troubleshooting Atlas.

Q1What does Division 24 of the AGL Troubleshooting Atlas cover?

Division 24 is the Emergency Quick-Diagnosis reference. It publishes 15 diagnostic topics, EMR-566 to EMR-580, in a single subsection.

Q2What information does each topic include?

Every topic follows the same diagnostic record: the symptom, equipment/system, possible causes ranked by likelihood (high, medium or low), required tools, safety precautions, diagnostic procedure, test points, root cause verification, corrective action, preventive action and related references (SOPs, standards, diagrams and case studies).

Q3Which topics are in Division 24?

The 15 topics are EMR-566 to EMR-580: CCR Trip Emergency Diagnosis; Complete Runway Lighting Failure; Complete Taxiway Lighting Failure; Runway Centreline Failure; Approach Lighting Failure; Stop Bar Failure; Multiple Circuit Failure; Major Earth Fault; Major Cable Fault; Major Water Ingress Event; Lightning Damage Event; Flooding-Related AGL Failure; Airport-Wide AGL Communication Failure; ALCMS Failure; Backup Lighting Failure.

Q4How does a diagnostic procedure begin in Division 24?

Each procedure starts with observation before any intervention. For example, EMR-566 (CCR Trip Emergency Diagnosis) begins: Read the CCR's trip indicator/fault code immediately to determine the trip type (overcurrent, earth fault, thermal, manual) without waiting for a full log review.

Q5What safety precautions apply in Division 24?

Each topic lists its own precautions. For example, EMR-566 (CCR Trip Emergency Diagnosis) starts with: Do not reset a tripped CCR more than once without at least a basic isolation check — repeated resets onto a live fault risk equipment damage and delay a safe outcome.

Q6Which SOPs are referenced in Division 24?
