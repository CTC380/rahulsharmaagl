AGL Preventive & Corrective Maintenance, Component Replacement & Reliability SOPs

Controlled procedures for planned and condition-based AGL maintenance, corrective repair, runway/taxiway/approach/PAPI/sign maintenance, CCR and transformer maintenance, cable and connector repair, lamp/LED/driver replacement, prism and fixture cleaning, fixture alignment, maintenance scheduling and maintenance record traceability.

Initial Issue

Technical Control — Maintenance activities shall be performed under the airport's approved maintenance program, work-permit system, electrical safety/LOTO procedure, equipment manufacturer's instructions and applicable aerodrome requirements. No maintenance activity shall compromise the operational status of an active runway/taxiway or introduce FOD or electrical hazards.

SOP-368 Preventive Maintenance
Purpose

To establish a planned maintenance process for detecting deterioration and correcting defects before they result in AGL failure or reduced operational availability.

Scope

Applicable to:

  • CCRs.
  • Series circuits.
  • Primary cables.
  • Isolation transformers.
  • Connectors.
  • Runway lights.
  • Taxiway lights.
  • Approach lights.
  • PAPI.
  • Signs.
  • ALCMS/control systems.
  • Earthing.
  • Civil interfaces.
Procedure
  1. Review approved preventive-maintenance schedule.
  2. Obtain work authorization.
  3. Review previous maintenance history.
  4. Identify equipment to be serviced.
  5. Coordinate with Operations/ATC where required.
  6. Establish safe work area.
  7. Apply LOTO/electrical isolation where required.
  8. Perform inspection and maintenance.
  9. Clean equipment.
  10. Tighten/replace components according to approved requirements.
  11. Perform required tests.
  12. Restore equipment.
  13. Conduct operational verification.
  14. Update maintenance records.

Principle — Preventive maintenance shall be condition-based where practical, not merely calendar-based.

SOP-379 Connector Maintenance
Purpose

To maintain AGL connectors and prevent moisture, corrosion and contact-related failures.

Inspect
  • Connector body.
  • Contact.
  • Seal.
  • Cable jacket.
  • Strain relief.
  • Moisture.
  • Corrosion.
  • Mechanical damage.
Procedure
  1. Isolate circuit under LOTO (Division 02); prove dead before work.
  2. Disconnect connector where required (never on an energized series circuit).
  3. Inspect contact.
  4. Clean only using approved materials/method.

  6. Replace defective components.
  7. Reconnect correctly.
  8. Verify connection.
  9. Perform continuity/functional testing as required.

  5. Open fixture according to manufacturer's instructions.

  9. Verify correct seating.
  10. Inspect optical components.
  11. Close fixture.

  13. Energize/test.
  14. Record replacement.

SOP-383 Prism Cleaning
Purpose

To maintain optical performance by removing contamination from prisms/lenses.

Procedure
  1. Isolate fixture where required.
  2. Open fixture using approved method.
  3. Inspect optical surface.
  4. Remove loose contamination.
  5. Clean using manufacturer-approved material.
  6. Do not scratch or abrade optical surfaces.
  7. Inspect for cracks/damage.
  8. Reassemble fixture.
  9. Verify sealing.
  10. Perform functional/photometric verification where required.

Prohibited — Do not use unapproved abrasive materials or chemicals that can damage optical surfaces.

SOP-387 Cable Repair
Purpose

To repair damaged AGL cable while maintaining electrical and mechanical integrity.

Procedure
  1. Locate defect.
  2. Identify cable.
  3. Isolate circuit under LOTO (Division 02); prove dead and apply safety earthing at the work location.
  4. Positively identify the correct cable (route records/cable identification method) before cutting.
  5. Excavate/access cable safely where required.
  6. Inspect damage.
  7. Determine repair method.
  8. Use approved repair materials.
  9. Complete repair.
  10. Seal/protect repaired area.
  11. Perform electrical tests.
  12. Restore cable route.
  13. Document repair.

B. Maintenance Quality Hold Points (HP-01 → HP-10)

Work shall not proceed past a hold point until the associated inspection or verification has been completed and released.

  • HP-01 — Correct Equipment: Verify correct equipment/circuit before work.
  • HP-02 — Correct Spare: Verify approved replacement part before installation.
  • HP-03 — Isolation: Verify electrical/operational isolation before maintenance.
  • HP-04 — Installation Quality: Verify component installation and torque/sealing where applicable.
  • HP-05 — Electrical Test: Perform applicable continuity/insulation/earth tests.
  • HP-06 — Functional Test: Verify equipment operation.
  • HP-07 — Optical Verification: Verify alignment/photometry where maintenance could affect optical performance.
  • HP-08 — FOD Clearance: Confirm tools, materials and waste have been removed.
  • HP-09 — Documentation: Complete maintenance record.
  • HP-10 — Return to Service: Obtain authorized operational release.

Division 18 — AGL Maintenance Golden Rule

“AGL maintenance is not complete when the failed component is replaced. Maintenance is complete only when the defect has been corrected, the installation has been inspected, the required electrical and functional tests have passed, optical performance has been verified where applicable, the work area is FOD-free, the equipment has been safely returned to service, and the complete maintenance history has been recorded.”

Division 18 — SOP Approval Principle

Maintenance activities shall be performed under the airport's approved maintenance program, work-permit system, electrical safety/LOTO procedure, equipment manufacturer's instructions and applicable aerodrome requirements.

No maintenance activity shall compromise the operational status of an active runway/taxiway or introduce FOD or electrical hazards — approved airport procedures shall always take precedence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Preventive & Corrective Maintenance SOPs in the AGL SOP Library.

Q1What is Division 18 — Preventive & Corrective Maintenance SOPs?

Division 18 is a set of 25 procedures (SOP-368 to SOP-392, Rev 0) for planned and condition-based AGL maintenance and corrective repair. It covers runway, taxiway, approach, PAPI and sign maintenance, CCR and transformer maintenance, cable and connector repair, lamp, LED and driver replacement, cleaning, fixture alignment, maintenance scheduling and record traceability.

Q2What rules govern all AGL maintenance?

Maintenance is performed under the airport's approved maintenance program, work-permit system, electrical safety and LOTO procedure, manufacturer's instructions and applicable aerodrome requirements. No maintenance activity may compromise the operational status of an active runway or taxiway or introduce FOD or electrical hazards.

Q3What does preventive maintenance involve?

SOP-368 aims to detect deterioration and correct defects before they cause failure. It starts from the approved preventive-maintenance schedule, work authorization and previous maintenance history, coordinates with Operations/ATC where required, and applies to CCRs, series circuits, cables, transformers, connectors, fixtures, PAPI, signs, ALCMS, earthing and civil interfaces.

Q4What is the corrective maintenance sequence?

Defect identified, defect classification, work order, risk assessment, isolation or permit, repair or replacement, testing, operational verification and close work order (SOP-369). Safety-critical defects are escalated immediately under the airport's approved operational process.

Q5Can a CCR protection device be reset repeatedly after it trips?

No. Do not repeatedly reset a CCR protection device without determining the cause of the trip (SOP-376). Corrective work follows alarm or fault diagnosis, isolation, repair, test, functional verification and return to service.

Q6What must a maintenance record contain?

Work order number, date and time, location, equipment and circuit IDs, defect description, work performed, parts replaced with old and new component information, technician and reviewer, test and photometry results, before and after photographs where required, equipment status, outstanding defects and return-to-service authorization (SOP-392). It should let an engineer answer what failed, where, when, why and what was done.
