Calculator #18

Isolating Transformer Efficiency & Loss

Split transformer loss into core and copper, find efficiency at your actual loading, and see what the loss costs across a circuit.

About this calculator

A series isolating transformer is never lossless. Core loss follows the voltage across the transformer and copper loss follows the current through it, and on a constant-current circuit these two behave differently from a mains transformer. This tool uses the data-sheet efficiency to rebuild both losses, then shows efficiency against loading, the best-efficiency point, the heat each unit gives off and the yearly energy it wastes.

Method & Reference

How efficiency and loss are worked out

Every step is shown so the result can be checked by hand.

1. Rated losses

At full rated load (power factor 1) the output equals the VA rating, so the data-sheet efficiency fixes the total loss. It is then split into core and copper.

L_rated = VA × (1 ÷ η − 1)    Core = share × L    Cu = L − Core

If you have test figures, choose the watts option and enter them directly.

2. Series circuit model

The circuit current is fixed, so copper loss stays at I²R. The voltage across the secondary rises and falls with the load, and core loss follows the square of that voltage.

Cu = Cu_rated × (I ÷ I_rated)²    Core = Core_rated × (V ÷ V_rated)²

V is the secondary load VA divided by the current, and V_rated is the rated VA divided by the rated current.

3. Constant-voltage model

For a transformer fed at fixed voltage, core loss is constant and copper loss follows the square of loading x.

Core = Core_rated    Cu = Cu_rated × x²    x = S ÷ VA_rated

4. Efficiency and best point

η = P_out ÷ (P_out + Core + Cu)

Efficiency peaks where the variable loss equals the fixed one. In the series model the best loading is the square root of Cu ÷ Core. In the constant-voltage model it is the square root of Core ÷ Cu.

5. Circuit totals

Circuit loss = loss × N    kWh = kW × hours    Cost = kWh × tariff

What this tool does not do

It does not model temperature rise, magnetising current, saturation, secondary open-circuit behaviour or lamp-out conditions, and it treats the lamp load as fixed in resistance. Size is nominal, and efficiency is a two-point fit, not a measured curve. Use the manufacturer's test report for acceptance values. To size the transformer first, use Calculator #17.

FAQ

Isolating Transformer Efficiency & Loss Calculator: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on core and copper loss, efficiency at your actual loading, and the cost of the loss.

What does the Isolating Transformer Efficiency & Loss calculator do?

It splits the loss of a series isolating transformer into core and copper, finds the efficiency at your actual loading, and shows what the loss costs across a circuit. It uses the data-sheet efficiency to rebuild both losses, then shows efficiency against loading, the best-efficiency point, the heat each unit gives off and the yearly energy it wastes.

How are the rated losses worked out?

At full rated load with a power factor of 1, the output equals the VA rating, so the data-sheet efficiency fixes the total loss. The rated loss is the VA rating times (1 divided by the efficiency, minus 1). It is then split into core and copper using a core share, which is a planning assumption. If you have test figures, choose the watts option and enter the core and copper loss directly from the transformer test report.

What is the difference between the series and constant-voltage models?

In the series model the circuit current is fixed, so copper loss stays at the rated copper loss times the square of the current ratio, while core loss follows the square of the secondary voltage. In the constant-voltage model, for a transformer fed at fixed voltage, core loss is constant and copper loss follows the square of the loading.

How are efficiency and the best-efficiency point calculated?

Efficiency is the output power divided by the output power plus the core and copper loss. It peaks where the variable loss equals the fixed one. In the series model the best loading is the square root of the copper loss divided by the core loss, and in the constant-voltage model it is the square root of the core loss divided by the copper loss.

What efficiency should I enter for the transformer?

Use the efficiency at full rated load and power factor 1 from the data sheet. FAA AC 150/5345-47C sets minimums of 80 percent for 30/45 W and 65 W, 85 percent for 100 W and 150 W, 90 percent for 200 W and above, and 70 percent for 10/15 W and 20/25 W. For a 20/6.6 A transformer, enter 6.6 A as the rated secondary current, not the 20 A primary current. The 300 W and 500 W types with a 20 A secondary are the exception: enter 20 A.

How are the circuit heat, energy and cost worked out?

The circuit loss is the loss per transformer times the number of transformers on the circuit. Yearly energy is that loss in kW times the operating hours, and the cost is the energy times your tariff. Set the number of transformers to 0 to skip the circuit totals.

Can you give a worked example?

A 45 VA transformer at 6.6 A with 90 percent rated efficiency and a 40 percent core share has a rated loss of 5 W, split into 2 W core and 3 W copper. With one 30 W fixture at a power factor of 1.00, the loading is 0.667, so the core loss is 0.89 W and the copper loss stays at 3.00 W. The total loss is 3.89 W and the efficiency is 88.5 percent. For 100 transformers that is 0.39 kW, or 1,167 kWh over 3,000 hours, which costs 9,333 rupees at 8 rupees per kWh.

What does the tool not do?

It does not model temperature rise, magnetising current, saturation, secondary open-circuit behaviour or lamp-out conditions, and it treats the lamp load as fixed in resistance. The size is nominal, and the efficiency is a two-point fit and not a measured curve, so treat the result as a planning figure. Use the manufacturer's test report for acceptance values. To size the transformer first, use Calculator #17.
