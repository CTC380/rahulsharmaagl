Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL): The Backbone of Safe Aircraft Operations

Many airports operate around the clock, often under challenging visibility conditions such as night operations, fog, rain, or low cloud cover. During these situations, pilots depend heavily on Airfield Ground Lighting systems to safely land, taxi, and depart.

AGL is a fundamental part of airport infrastructure. It provides precise visual guidance to pilots and ensures safe aircraft movement both in the air during approach and on the ground during taxi operations.

In this article, I share a practical overview of how Airfield Ground Lighting systems support aircraft operations and why they remain a critical element of modern airport safety.

Key Takeaway

AGL gives pilots clear, reliable visual guidance at night and in reduced visibility — it is the backbone that supports safe take-off, landing, and taxi movement within the aerodrome’s approved operating minima.

01

What Is Airfield Ground Lighting?

Airfield Ground Lighting refers to the system of lighting installed on runways, taxiways, aprons, and approach paths to support aircraft operations. These lighting systems are designed and installed in accordance with international aviation standards such as ICAO Annex 14 and FAA specifications.

The main objective of AGL systems is to provide pilots with clear and reliable visual references during the most critical phases of flight.

02

Key Components of AGL Systems

A typical AGL system consists of several specialized lighting elements, each designed for a specific operational purpose.

Runway Lighting

Runway edge lights, threshold and runway end lights, runway centreline lights, and touchdown zone lights help pilots maintain correct alignment and positioning during landing and take-off. Stop bars and runway guard lights at holding positions help prevent inadvertent runway entry.

Taxiway Lighting

Taxiway edge lights and centreline lights guide aircraft safely between the runway, taxiways, and apron areas, and rapid exit taxiway indicator lights help pilots on the roll-out.

Approach Lighting Systems

Approach lights assist pilots during the final stage of landing, especially in low visibility and at night. Visual approach slope indicators such as PAPI are separate systems that complement approach lighting by giving the pilot glide-path guidance.

Airfield Guidance Signs

Guidance signs (illuminated or retroreflective, depending on the runway and the requirement) provide mandatory instruction, location and directional information to pilots while taxiing on the airfield.

Series Lighting Circuits

Most AGL systems operate on constant-current series circuits using isolating transformers. The regulator drives the same current through every fixture on the circuit, so brightness stays uniform regardless of cable length or the number of fixtures, within the rated load and voltage capability of the regulator. The isolating transformer also keeps the series circuit complete if a lamp fails, and separates the fixture from the high-voltage primary circuit.

03

Importance of AGL Systems in Aviation Safety

Airfield lighting plays a direct role in maintaining safe airport operations. A well-designed AGL system provides:

  • Clear runway identification
  • Accurate guidance for aircraft movement
  • Improved visibility during night and low-visibility operations
  • Reduced risk of runway incursions or navigation errors (for example, through stop bars and runway guard lights)

Without reliable lighting infrastructure, operations at night or in poor visibility would be severely restricted or not permitted.

04

Role of Compliance and International Standards

AGL equipment and installations must meet strict technical requirements set by ICAO Annex 14, national regulators (in India, the DGCA), and equipment specifications such as the FAA AC 150/5345 series and relevant IEC standards. Together, these cover:

Light IntensityPrecise output levels for each fixture
Beam Distribution & AimingHorizontal and vertical beam spread and alignment
Colour SpecificationsCorrect colour for each lighting type
Environmental PerformanceBehaviour under field conditions (weather, temperature, loading)
Serviceability & PowerMaintenance limits and secondary power switch-over times

Compliance ensures that lighting systems deliver consistent visual information to pilots regardless of the airport or region of operation.

05

Reliability of the Electrical System

Beyond the light fixtures themselves, the electrical infrastructure behind AGL is equally important. Key elements include:

01ALCMSAirfield Lighting Control & Monitoring System: selects brightness, switches circuits, reports status
02CCRConstant Current Regulator: holds the series circuit current at each brightness step
03Series CablingPrimary circuit loop feeding every isolating transformer
04Isolating TransformersIsolate each fixture and keep the circuit complete if a lamp fails

Together with a secondary (standby) power supply that meets the required switch-over times, these components ensure stable operation, controlled brightness levels, and reliable performance across the entire airfield lighting network.

06

Key Takeaways

  • AGL provides pilots with the visual guidance needed to land, taxi, and depart safely at night and in low visibility, within approved operating minima
  • Runway, taxiway, and approach lighting, along with guidance signs, each serve a distinct operational purpose
  • Constant-current series circuits with isolating transformers keep brightness uniform across the circuit, within the regulator’s rated capacity
  • ICAO Annex 14 and FAA specifications define the intensity, beam distribution and colour every fixture must meet, and, with equipment standards, the durability and serviceability expected of the system
  • CCRs, isolating transformers, cabling, standby power and monitoring systems form the electrical backbone that keeps the network stable
  • As airports expand and traffic increases, reliable and compliant AGL infrastructure remains essential to safe operations

Conclusion

Airfield Ground Lighting systems form the backbone of safe aircraft operations at any airport. From guiding aircraft during final approach to supporting safe taxi movement on the ground, AGL systems provide the visual references that pilots rely on every day.

As airports expand and traffic increases, maintaining reliable, compliant, and well-designed AGL infrastructure remains essential for safe and efficient aviation operations.

In simple terms, AGL transforms an airport into a clearly visible and safely navigable environment for pilots at night and in reduced visibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is airfield ground lighting (AGL)?

AGL is the system of lighting installed on runways, taxiways, aprons and approach paths to support aircraft operations. It is designed and installed in accordance with international aviation standards such as ICAO Annex 14 and FAA specifications, and its main objective is to give pilots clear and reliable visual references during the most critical phases of flight.

What are the main components of an AGL system?

A typical system includes runway lighting (edge, threshold and end, centreline and touchdown zone lights, plus stop bars and runway guard lights), taxiway lighting (edge and centreline lights and rapid exit taxiway indicator lights), approach lighting systems, airfield guidance signs and the series lighting circuits that power them. PAPI is a separate system that complements approach lighting by giving glide-path guidance.

Why do AGL systems use constant-current series circuits?

The regulator drives the same current through every fixture on the circuit, so brightness stays uniform regardless of cable length or the number of fixtures, within the rated load and voltage capability of the regulator. The isolating transformer also keeps the series circuit complete if a lamp fails and separates the fixture from the high-voltage primary circuit.

How does AGL support aviation safety?

A well-designed system gives clear runway identification, accurate guidance for aircraft movement and improved visibility at night and in low visibility. It also reduces the risk of runway incursions or navigation errors, for example through stop bars and runway guard lights. Without reliable lighting, operations at night or in poor visibility would be severely restricted or not permitted.

Which standards govern AGL equipment and installations?

They must meet technical requirements set by ICAO Annex 14, national regulators (in India, the DGCA), and equipment specifications such as the FAA AC 150/5345 series and relevant IEC standards. Together these cover light intensity, beam distribution and aiming, colour, environmental performance, and serviceability and secondary power switch-over times.

What electrical equipment sits behind the lights?

The article names four elements: the ALCMS (selects brightness, switches circuits and reports status), the CCR (holds the series circuit current at each brightness step), the series cabling (the primary loop feeding every isolating transformer) and the isolating transformers. A secondary (standby) power supply that meets the required switch-over times completes the picture.

Why does compliance with standards matter?

Compliance ensures that lighting systems deliver consistent visual information to pilots regardless of the airport or region of operation. The article stresses that it covers more than light intensity: beam distribution and aiming, colour, environmental performance and serviceability all have defined requirements.
