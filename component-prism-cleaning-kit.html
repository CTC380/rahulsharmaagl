Prism Cleaning Kit

A specialized maintenance kit designed for the safe and effective cleaning of optical prisms, lenses, and windows in AGL fixtures. Regular cleaning preserves light output, maintains color fidelity, and extends the service life of runway, taxiway, and approach lighting systems by removing contaminants such as rubber deposits, dust, oil, and de-icing fluid residues.

Overview

What Is It?

A Prism Cleaning Kit is a purpose-assembled set of cleaning materials and tools used to maintain the optical clarity of AGL fixture prisms, lenses, and optical windows. The kit typically includes:

  • Optical-grade cleaning solution (non-abrasive, streak-free, compatible with glass and acrylic)
  • Lint-free microfiber cloths for gentle wiping without scratching
  • Foam-tipped swabs for reaching recessed optical surfaces
  • Soft-bristled brushes for removing loose debris before liquid cleaning
  • Anti-static wipes to reduce dust attraction after cleaning
  • Storage case to keep all items clean and organized

Proper cleaning of prisms is critical for maintaining the photometric performance of AGL fixtures, ensuring that light beams meet ICAO and FAA intensity and color specifications.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The prism cleaning kit exists to protect the photometric integrity of the fixture over its service life. Dust, oil film, rubber deposits from tyre contact, and de-icing chemical residue all scatter or absorb light at the optical surface, silently reducing the intensity a pilot actually sees even when the lamp or LED array itself is performing to specification — a fixture can fail a photometric inspection purely due to a dirty lens. Because the kit is purpose-matched to the optic (non-abrasive solution, lint-free and foam-tipped applicators), it removes contamination without introducing the scratches or haze that a generic cleaner or cloth would cause, which would otherwise create permanent, uncorrectable light loss. In this way, routine use of the kit is what keeps the beam pattern and colour a fixture was designed to produce actually reaching the pilot's eye in service, not just at the point of manufacture.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Prism cleaning kits are used during routine and corrective maintenance on all AGL fixtures with optical components:

  • Runway Lights: Edge, centreline, threshold, end, and touchdown zone lights
  • Taxiway Lights: Edge, centreline, and stop bar lights
  • Approach Lighting: Barrette lights, crossbar lights, and sequenced flashing lights
  • Visual Approach Slope Indicators: PAPI, VASI, and APAPI units
  • Obstruction Lights: Beacons and obstruction markers with optical windows
  • Sign Boards: Internally illuminated sign faces

Cleaning is performed as part of scheduled preventive maintenance (e.g., quarterly or bi-annually) and also after events that cause heavy contamination, such as rubber deposit buildup from aircraft landings or de-icing operations.

Care

Storage & Handling

The kit is kept in its own padded case in the maintenance vehicle or workshop tool store, with solutions, cloths, and brushes segregated to prevent cross-contamination between abrasive and non-abrasive materials. Cleaning solutions are stored within their manufacturer-stated shelf life and away from direct sunlight or freezing temperatures, which can affect their chemical stability, and microfibre cloths are laundered separately from other shop rags to avoid picking up grit that would scratch optical surfaces on the next use. Crews carry the kit out to the fixture location rather than the fixture being brought to the kit, and consumable items (cloths, solution) are restocked after each maintenance round so the kit is always ready for the next scheduled or corrective cleaning task.

Function

How Does It Work?

The cleaning process involves several steps to safely remove contaminants without damaging the optical surfaces. The technician first uses a soft brush to remove loose dust and debris. Then, a small amount of optical-grade cleaning solution is applied to a lint-free cloth or swab. The cloth is gently wiped across the prism or lens surface in a single direction, avoiding circular motions that can trap particles and cause scratches. For recessed or delicate optics, foam-tipped swabs are used to reach tight areas. After cleaning, a dry lint-free cloth is used to buff the surface to a streak-free finish. The entire process is designed to restore the original light transmission and beam pattern, ensuring that the fixture meets its photometric specification.

The cleaning solution is specifically formulated to dissolve oils, rubber residues, and de-icing chemicals without attacking the optical coating or the substrate (glass or acrylic). Anti-static wipes are then used to reduce the attraction of new dust particles.

Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

The cleaning kit has no control system of its own — it is a manual maintenance tool, and any "logic" involved is procedural rather than electronic: a fixed sequence of steps (debris removal, solvent application, one-directional wiping, dry buffing) is followed in a specific order because reversing or skipping a step risks trapping grit against the optic and scratching it. Its place in the wider architecture is as an input to the maintenance and quality-assurance workflow rather than to the electrical control system: cleaning is typically triggered by a scheduled maintenance interval or by a photometric test bench flagging reduced output, and the result is verified afterwards by re-measuring the fixture's intensity on a photometer or goniophotometer rather than by any feedback from the kit itself.

Construction

Physical Construction

The kit is carried in a rigid, moulded polypropylene or aluminium case with a foam cut-out insert that holds each item in a fixed slot, so contents can be checked at a glance and nothing rattles loose in a service vehicle. The cleaning solution ships in a sealed, light-resistant bottle with a fine-mist or dropper applicator to control the amount applied to a lens without over-wetting the fixture's seals. Microfiber cloths are packed in a resealable, lint-trapping sleeve to keep them free of grit between uses, since a single trapped particle can scratch a prism during wiping. Foam swabs, soft-bristle brushes, and anti-static wipes are individually wrapped, and the case includes a small compartment for a torch or inspection mirror used to check the cleaned surface for residual film or lint before the fixture is closed back up.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Using Abrasive or Ammoniated Cleaners

Technicians sometimes use household glass cleaners containing ammonia or abrasive compounds. These can damage optical coatings, cause crazing on acrylic, and leave residues that reduce light output. Always use the recommended optical-grade solution supplied in the kit.

Scratching from Improper Wiping

Wiping with dry cloths or using circular motions can trap abrasive particles and scratch the optical surface. Use a gentle one-way motion with a damp cloth, and always remove loose debris first with a brush or compressed air.

Cross-Contamination of Swabs

Reusing swabs or cloths between fixtures can transfer contaminants and cause streaking. Use fresh swabs and cloths for each fixture, and avoid touching the cleaning surface with bare fingers.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Streaking Remains After Cleaning
Likely Cause
Loose debris was not removed before wiping, so grit was dragged across the surface, or a used/contaminated swab or cloth redeposited residue rather than lifting it off.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect the streaked area under angled light to confirm whether it's fine scratching or a residue film; check whether loose debris was cleared with a brush or compressed air before wiping; confirm a fresh swab/cloth was used for this fixture.
Corrective Action
Re-clean using the kit's approved optical-grade solution with a fresh cloth, clearing loose debris first with a brush or compressed air, and finish with a gentle one-way wiping motion rather than circular strokes.
Hazy or Cloudy Film After Cleaning
Likely Cause
A household or ammoniated glass cleaner was used instead of the kit's optical-grade solution, leaving a residue film or attacking the optical coating, or the solution was left to dry on the surface rather than being wiped off while still damp.
Diagnostic Steps
Confirm which cleaning solution was actually used; inspect the surface for a uniform haze consistent with dried residue versus a permanent coating change.
Corrective Action
Re-clean strictly with the kit's supplied optical-grade solution, wiping before it dries; if the coating itself has been degraded by an incorrect cleaner, escalate to fixture inspection since coating damage isn't reversible by cleaning.
Fine Scratches Visible on the Optical Surface
Likely Cause
Wiping with a dry cloth or using circular motions trapped abrasive particles against the surface instead of lifting them away.
Diagnostic Steps
Examine the scratch pattern — fine, multi-directional scratching is consistent with dry or circular wiping technique rather than a single incident of damage.
Corrective Action
Retrain technique: always clear loose debris first, use a damp cloth with the approved solution, and wipe in a single gentle direction; if scratching is already significant, assess whether the fixture's light output has been affected enough to warrant lens or window replacement.
Kit Solution Bottle Empty or Contaminated
Likely Cause
Normal consumption during routine cleaning rounds, or a bottle left uncapped has allowed dust or debris to contaminate the remaining solution.
Diagnostic Steps
Check remaining solution volume against the kit's inventory checklist; visually inspect for discolouration or visible particulate in the solution.
Corrective Action
Replace with a fresh bottle of the manufacturer-approved optical-grade solution; never substitute with an unapproved cleaner, and keep the bottle capped between uses.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

Solution Contamination or Expiry
Medium Severity
Root Cause
A cleaning solution bottle left open between uses, or the kit kept in service past its stated shelf life.
Effect
The kit solution bottle is found empty or contaminated, or cleaning leaves a hazy or cloudy film instead of a clear optical surface.
Detection Method
Shelf life and storage condition check, and a solution compatibility/pH verification before use on a live fixture.
Swab / Cloth Cross-Contamination
Medium Severity
Root Cause
A swab or cloth reused across fixtures without proper segregation, carrying grit or residue from one lens to the next.
Effect
Fine scratches appear on the optical surface, or streaking remains after cleaning that a fresh swab wouldn't have caused.
Detection Method
Swab and cloth inventory check, confirming single-use or properly laundered items are used per fixture.
Incompatible Solvent Substitution
High Severity
Root Cause
An abrasive or ammoniated household cleaner substituted for the kit's specified solution when the correct consumable runs out in the field.
Effect
The optical surface is scratched or chemically etched, permanently degrading the fixture's photometric output.
Detection Method
Material and residue check, and a trial clean on a low-risk fixture before the substituted product is used more broadly.
Kit Component Degradation
Low Severity
Root Cause
Normal wear on cloths, applicator tips, or container seals from repeated use over the kit's service life.
Effect
Reduced cleaning effectiveness or a compromised seal that lets the solution degrade faster than expected.
Detection Method
Kit component inspection as part of scheduled restocking, checking cloths, applicators, and container seals for wear.
Incomplete Kit / Missing Components
Low Severity
Root Cause
An item not replenished after the previous use, or a component left behind at a different work site.
Effect
A technician reaches for the kit in the field and finds it missing a required item, forcing an improvised substitute mid-task.
Detection Method
Kit completeness and sign-off check performed before the kit is returned to storage or issued for a new task.
Improper Storage Conditions
Medium Severity
Root Cause
The kit stored in a vehicle or store subject to temperature extremes or direct sunlight outside its rated storage range.
Effect
Accelerated degradation of the cleaning solution and swab materials, shortening the kit's effective service life.
Detection Method
Shelf life and storage condition check comparing actual storage conditions against the manufacturer's specification.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Electrical Isolation Before Cleaning
Electrical Safety

De-energise and lock out the fixture's series circuit at the CCR and verify zero energy before opening the housing to access the prism or lens, since cleaning is normally carried out with the fixture powered down rather than live.

Hot Lamp / Housing Contact
Personnel Safety

Allow the fixture housing and lamp/LED module to cool before starting cleaning after an active period; cleaning solution applied to a hot optical surface can also cause thermal shock and cracking.

Cleaning Solution Handling
Personnel Safety

Use only the optical-grade solution supplied with the kit, avoid skin and eye contact, and follow the solution's safety data sheet for ventilation and spill handling; do not substitute general-purpose glass cleaners, which can leave residue or attack lens coatings.

FOD Control at the Base Can
Operational

Keep swabs, cloths, and brushes accounted for at all times when the fixture is open, and secure or remove any loose material before reinstating the fixture, to prevent foreign object debris on the movement area.

Airside Work Coordination
Operational

Cleaning fixtures on or near an active runway or taxiway requires ATC/airside coordination and a NOTAM where the fixture will be dark or out of tolerance during the work.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Inspect the cleaned optic for streaking or residue before closing out the work and re-energising the circuit, since a poorly cleaned surface can leave photometric output non-compliant despite the maintenance being logged as complete.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Match Solution to Lens Material, Not Just "Optical Grade"
Technique

A cleaning solution safe for glass prisms can craze or cloud certain acrylic lenses with repeated use; confirm material compatibility for each fixture type before standardising a single solution across a mixed glass-and-acrylic inventory.

Track Cloth Laundering, Not Just Cloth Count
Maintenance

A microfiber cloth reused without proper laundering gradually embeds grit that scratches optics on subsequent use, even though it still looks clean; track a laundering or replacement cycle for cloths rather than judging them by appearance alone.

Dry Climates Need an Anti-Static Step
Technique

In arid environments, static buildup during cleaning attracts dust back onto the lens almost immediately after wiping; include an anti-static wipe or technique in dry-climate procedures rather than using the same method as humid sites.

Date-Tag Solution Once Opened
Maintenance

Cleaning solution left open in a field kit can degrade or pick up contaminants that leave streaking on the next use; mark the open date on the bottle and retire it after a defined period rather than using it until it visibly looks wrong.

Match Technique to the Lens Finish, Not a Blanket Method
Technique

Aggressive wiping on a textured or frosted lens element can polish away the designed diffusion pattern over repeated cleanings, subtly changing the fixture's beam characteristics; adjust cleaning pressure and method to the specific lens finish rather than applying one technique fleet-wide.

Track Any Substitution of Kit Components
Consistency

Swapping a kit's original solution or cloth type for a generic substitute changes cleaning results in ways that are hard to trace later; log any component substitution site-wide so an unexplained change in lens condition can be traced back to a supply change.

Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about prism cleaning kits: contents, use, standards and common mistakes.

Q1What is a prism cleaning kit?

A prism cleaning kit is a set of cleaning materials and tools used to maintain the optical clarity of AGL fixture prisms, lenses and optical windows. It includes optical-grade cleaning solution, cloths, swabs and soft brushes.

Q2What fixtures is a prism cleaning kit used on?

Runway, taxiway and approach lights with optical surfaces, plus visual approach slope indicators such as PAPI, VASI and APAPI units, during routine and corrective maintenance.

Q3How do you clean an AGL prism or lens?

Brush off loose dust with a soft brush, apply a small amount of optical-grade solution to dissolve oils, rubber residue and de-icing chemicals, wipe with a clean microfiber cloth without circular scrubbing, then use anti-static wipes to reduce dust attraction.

Q4What cleaning solution is suitable for AGL optics?

An alcohol-free, pH-neutral (about 6.5 to 7.5), non-ammoniated optical cleaner that is non-abrasive and compatible with glass and acrylic.

Q5Why not use household glass cleaner on AGL lenses?

Ammoniated or abrasive cleaners can attack optical coatings and acrylic surfaces and scratch or haze the lens, reducing light output.

Q6Which standards apply to prism cleaning?

The main references are FAA AC 150/5340-26 (maintenance of airport visual aid facilities), OSHA 1910.133 for eye and face protection, and ISO 14644 as a cleanliness reference.

Q7How often should the kit itself be checked?

Check the solution and consumables before each use, and inspect the whole kit quarterly for expired or degraded materials. The unopened solution has a stated shelf life of about 2 years.

Q8What are the common mistakes when cleaning AGL optics?

Using abrasive or ammoniated cleaners, wiping dry or in circular motions that trap abrasive particles, and reusing swabs or cloths between fixtures, which cross-contaminates them.
