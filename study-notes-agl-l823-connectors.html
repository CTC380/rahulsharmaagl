WHAT IS AN L-823 CONNECTOR?

The L-823 connector is a specialised airport-lighting plug or receptacle cable connector. It makes reliable electrical connections in AGL systems without ordinary open terminals at the field interface.

It is used with underground airport-lighting power cables, isolation-transformer leads, light-fixture leads, and primary and secondary circuit connections. The current specification is FAA AC 150/5345-26E (16 December 2021), which replaced -26D.

WHY THE CONNECTOR MATTERS

A connector must do far more than "make contact". It must provide:

  • Electrical continuity
  • Electrical insulation
  • Mechanical strength
  • Watertight sealing
  • Environmental resistance
  • Reliable repeated mating where applicable

The specification requires a watertight seal on the mated connector and defines electrical, mechanical and environmental performance. A connector is one of the critical interfaces between the CCR, cable, transformer and fixture.

3

TYPE, CLASS AND STYLE

AC 150/5345-26E classifies connectors by Type, Class and Style. It defines two types, two classes, and six plug styles plus six receptacle styles.

L-823
 ├── TYPE   : Type I / Type II        = electrical classification
 ├── CLASS  : Class A / Class B       = how it is attached
 └── STYLE  : Plug 1–6 / Receptacle 7–12 = physical configuration
Three-part memory
  • TYPE: what electrical category? (I or II)
  • CLASS: how is it attached? (A = factory molded, B = field attached)
  • STYLE: what physical plug or receptacle configuration? (1–12)
A style number is not a voltage or current class. It identifies the housing and interface configuration only.

6

CONSTRUCTION: HOUSING, PIN AND SOCKET

A plug carries the contacts that mate with a corresponding receptacle. The electrical interface is a pin and a socket.

Housing

Elastomeric material that serves as insulation and sheath and fully encloses the pins or sockets. Gives mechanical protection, insulation, sealing and the cable interface.

Pin and socket material

At least 98% copper, or free-cutting brass alloy 360 (C36000), with required hardness and plating.

Plating

Tin or another suitable plating: reduces corrosion and helps keep contact resistance low.

Spring-loaded / slotted socket

Maintains contact pressure, which limits contact resistance, voltage drop, heating and intermittent connection.

11

COMPATIBILITY: NEVER SELECT BY APPEARANCE

Two connectors can look similar yet differ in type, class, style, contacts, voltage, current, cable size or mating geometry. Physical similarity does not prove electrical or mechanical compatibility. A connector and its cable form one assembly.

Compatibility check before ordering

  1. Type: I or II?
  2. Class: A or B?
  3. Style: correct plug or receptacle style?
  4. Cable: correct conductor and construction?
  5. Current and voltage rating
  6. Does the plug mate with the receptacle?
  7. Does it match the transformer or fixture?
12

CLASS B ASSEMBLY AND WORKMANSHIP

Strip / prepare cableConductor connectionPin / socket assemblyHousing assemblyInsulating / sealing compoundCompleted connector

Class B pins and sockets must allow crimping to the conductor, and the contact must not be unacceptably damaged by crimping. For some configurations, silicone insulating compound fills internal spaces; assembly provisions guard against voids and compound intrusion.

Poor crimp

High resistance, loose contact, heating, mechanical failure, intermittent operation.

Voids

Moisture pathways, electrical stress concentration and reduced insulation performance.

Typical errors

Wrong strip length, wrong conductor size, wrong pin position, too little or contaminated compound, damaged housing, poor sealing.

Remember

Connector certification does not remove the need for correct installation workmanship.

13

CAPS AND SHORT-CIRCUITING CAPS

Caps protect connectors before mating from dirt, moisture, contamination and transport or storage damage. They are supplied with Class A connectors unless the buyer requests otherwise.

For certain Type II receptacles used in series applications, a short-circuiting plug-type cap may be required. It has internal jumpers on the proper pins, the same mating dimensions as the corresponding plug, and is permanently marked "S".

Never fit a shorting cap just because it fits. It must be the correct approved cap, on the correct receptacle, for the correct circuit application.

15

FIELD TESTING, HANDLING AND INSPECTION

Field tests may include continuity, insulation resistance and voltage-drop measurements, plus visual mating, connector condition, strain and sealing checks. Continuity is not insulation integrity: a connector can pass continuity and fail insulation.

Storage

Keep caps on; protect mating surfaces, oil, chemicals, heat and sunlight; no heavy objects on leads; avoid sharp bends; keep markings intact.

Never

Pull by the contacts, drag on the ground, strike with tools, force mismatched halves, mate contaminated contacts, cut molded cable, or modify a certified assembly without an approved process.

Inspect pin

Straight, clean, undamaged, correct geometry.

Inspect socket and housing

Socket not expanded, blocked or corroded, with good spring action; housing without cracks, cuts or deformation.

Cleanliness is part of electrical reliability: dirt or moisture on contacts causes resistance, tracking and intermittent operation. Do not force a connector that will not mate naturally; it may be the wrong style, damaged or contaminated.

FAILURE MODES AND FAULT-FINDING

Failure modeCause / effect
High contact resistanceHeating and voltage drop; can become self-reinforcing
Open circuitBroken conductor or poor contact
Insulation failureMoisture, damage or ageing
Water ingressLeakage and corrosion
Mechanical damageHousing compromised
Incorrect assemblyEspecially Class B

An open primary connector breaks the series loop, so every light on that circuit goes out and the CCR may shut down on open-circuit protection; an open secondary connector usually affects one fixture.

One light off

  • Check fixture
  • Secondary connector
  • Secondary cable
  • Transformer
  • Primary connector

Many lights off

  • Check CCR
  • Primary circuit and cable
  • Primary connectors
  • Transformers

Connector heating: inspect the connector and contacts, check the electrical connection, then current and cable. Likely causes are high contact resistance, poor crimp, wrong contact, contamination, corrosion, loose mating or damage. Low insulation: investigate cable, connector, transformer and moisture. A bad connector also affects LED fixtures (voltage drop, flicker, driver malfunction) and, indirectly, photometric output.

17

L-823 VS GENERIC CONNECTORS

A terminal block belongs inside protected equipment; an L-823 is designed for field AGL cable interfaces. A connector sold as "waterproof" is not automatically L-823 compliant: compliance covers ratings, dimensions, contact performance, environmental tests, cable compatibility, qualification and production requirements. Commercial terms such as inline connector, coupler or cable connector do not replace the formal Type + Class + Style; verify the manufacturer's exact FAA designation.

18

COMMON MISTAKES

Calling every connector "L-823"

Identify Type + Class + Style.

Selecting by cable size only

Cable size alone does not determine the connector.

Mixing plug styles

A plug must mate with the correct receptacle.

Ignoring Type I vs II

Fundamentally different electrical classifications.

Using an ordinary waterproof connector

Waterproof does not establish compliance.

Ignoring Class A vs B

Different installation considerations.

Reusing damaged connectors

Seals and contacts may no longer protect.

Leaving connectors uncapped, forcing mating, poor crimping

Contamination, damaged pins and heating.

Procurement: do not write only "L-823 connector – Qty 100"

L-823 + Type + Class + Style + cable size + cable type
 + mating half + current + voltage + application + standard / approval

Quantity: ____ | Compliance: FAA / project specification

On receipt

Check datasheet, compliance and certification documents, test documentation, L-823 marking, manufacturer, style number, type and part number. Then check for cracks, cuts, deformation, damaged cable sheath, pins or sockets, corrosion, contamination, correct cap and dimensions. Before assembling Class B: right kit, cable, cable size, conductor preparation, crimp tooling, contact, housing, compound and instructions; afterwards check pin position, crimp, compound fill, housing closure, continuity and insulation as required.

Exam & Interview

EXAM / INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

What is an L-823 connector?

An airport-lighting plug/receptacle cable connector for underground power cables, isolation-transformer leads and light-fixture leads.

What is Type I?

1 conductor, 25 A, 5,000 V.

What is Type II?

2 conductors, 20 A, 600 V.

What is Class A?

Factory molded to the cable.

What is Class B?

Field attached to the cable.

What do style numbers identify?

The specified physical plug or receptacle interface configuration.

How many plug and receptacle styles are there?

Six plug styles (1–6) and six receptacle styles (7–12).

Why must an L-823 connector be watertight?

To protect the connection from the moisture, groundwater and flooding met in airfield installations.

Why is contact resistance important?

High resistance causes voltage drop and I²R heating.

What is the difference between Type and Class?

Type is the electrical classification; Class is the attachment method.

Can any Type I plug mate with any Type I receptacle?

No. The correct mating style/configuration must be used.

Can a connector be selected only by current?

No. Type, class, style, cable, voltage, mating half and application must all be verified.

Why do connector faults matter in the primary circuit?

An open or faulty primary connector breaks the series loop, so the whole circuit is affected, not one fitting, and the CCR may shut down on open-circuit protection.

Why does a bad secondary connector usually fail only one light?

The secondary normally supplies one individual fixture.

Why are Class B connectors more sensitive to workmanship?

They are assembled onto the cable in the field.

Why is continuity not enough?

It does not prove insulation integrity, sealing, contact resistance or mechanical condition.

What is the short-circuiting cap marking?

"S", with the same mating dimensions as the corresponding plug.

Can I use qualification test voltages in the field?

No. Use the approved maintenance or commissioning procedure.

Final Study Takeaway

FINAL STUDY TAKEAWAY

The L-823 connector is a critical electrical and environmental interface in an AGL system. Remember three words: Type (electrical: I = 1 conductor, 25 A, 5,000 V; II = 2 conductors, 20 A, 600 V), Class (attachment: A = factory molded, B = field attached) and Style (physical configuration: plugs 1–6, receptacles 7–12).

L-823 knowledge = Type + Class + Style + Cable + Contact + Insulation + Sealing + Installation + Testing + Compatibility.

Field Insight

ENGINEERING & EXPERT NOTES

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist

Workmanship Decides Whether the Connector Lasts

Assembly

Class B connectors depend on clean preparation, correct tools and complete sealing. Training and recorded procedure matter as much as the part.

Compatibility Must Be Verified, Not Assumed

Selection

A connector that mates physically may not be the right type, class or style for the circuit. Check the full designation and the cable it was designed for.

Look for the Water Path, Not Just the Corroded Contact

Failure Mode

Corrosion at a pin is the result. The seal, the pit drainage and the cable entry are usually the cause.

Heat and Intermittency Point to Contact Resistance

Diagnosis

A warm connector or a fault that comes and goes with load or weather suggests a marginal contact. Re-make it with new parts and seals.
