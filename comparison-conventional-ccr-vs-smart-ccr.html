AGL Technology Comparison

Conventional CCR vs Smart CCR

Conventional CCR
VS
Smart CCR

CCR = Constant Current Regulator. A Conventional CCR primarily provides regulated constant current to the AGL series circuit; a Smart CCR adds digital control, monitoring, diagnostics, communication and often condition/status information on top of the basic current-regulation function.

03 · Definitions

What Each CCR Actually Does

Conventional CCR

Generally focuses on maintaining the selected output current despite changes in the series circuit.

Typical functions

  • Constant-current regulation
  • Multiple intensity levels
  • Local ON/OFF control
  • Overcurrent / overload protection
  • Open-circuit protection
  • Basic fault indication

Smart CCR

Performs the basic CCR function but adds measurement, digital processing and communication. Exact capabilities depend on the manufacturer and model.

May monitor

  • Output current, voltage, power
  • Circuit status & CCR temperature
  • Insulation/fault condition where supported
  • Internal alarms & operating hours
  • Fault history & intensity level
  • Communication status

Terminology and standards: "Conventional" and "Smart" are industry terms, not standard designations. FAA AC 150/5345-10 defines CCR types (L-828 regulator without monitoring, L-829 regulator with monitoring, L-827 regulator monitor), classes (Class 1: 6.6 A, Class 2: 20 A) and brightness styles (3 or 5 steps). A Smart CCR may add configuration, measurement and communication, but the regulated output must still meet the specified step currents, tolerance and protection requirements. Verify the current edition and the manufacturer's declared conformity.

04 · Control Difference

Open Loop vs Closed Information Loop

Conventional

Control panel → intensity command → CCR → AGL circuit. Feedback may be relatively limited.

Smart

AGL control system → digital command → Smart CCR → AGL circuit → measurements → diagnostics → feedback to control system. This creates a closed information loop.

05 · Monitoring

Probably the Biggest Practical Difference

Conventional CCR

An operator may know only: "CCR is ON." Detailed information about the circuit may require local measurements or additional equipment.

Smart CCR

The operator may see: CCR ON → Step 3 → Output current normal → Voltage normal → No alarm. Depending on the system, alarms and measurements can be displayed remotely.

06 · Fault Detection

Basic Alarm vs Classified, Logged Event

Conventional CCR

CCR fault → alarm → technician investigates. Fault indication may be relatively basic.

Smart CCR

Fault detected → fault classified → alarm generated → event recorded → displayed remotely → maintenance action. This can significantly reduce troubleshooting time.

07 · Example — Open Circuit

How Each System Responds to the Same Fault

Suppose an AGL series circuit develops an open circuit.

Conventional CCR

Open-circuit protection de-energises the output and indicates a fault, so the circuit does not run at unsafe voltage. The technician then needs to investigate the field circuit.

Smart CCR

Can potentially provide circuit abnormality details, output voltage/current behaviour, fault alarm, time of occurrence and event history.

Some systems can provide more sophisticated circuit-fault localization, but the CCR should not automatically be assumed to identify the exact failed fixture or cable location unless that capability is specifically provided.

08 · Remote Control

Where the Intelligence Sits

Conventional CCR remote operation can be possible through hardwired contacts, external control systems or remote interface modules — but intelligence may reside outside the CCR. Smart CCR communication and digital control are typically integrated more deeply, with possible interfaces including Ethernet, serial communication, fieldbus, proprietary protocols and airport lighting control systems. The actual interface depends on the selected CCR.

09 · Smart Is Not Simply "Better"

The Core Job Still Has to Be Done Well

A Smart CCR still has to perform the core CCR function correctly. If the current regulation is poor, sophisticated communication doesn't make it a good CCR.

Therefore, evaluate in this order:

  • Current regulation
  • Electrical performance
  • Protection
  • AGL compatibility
  • Control
  • Monitoring
  • Communication
  • Diagnostics
10 · LED AGL Compatibility

What Changes With LED

This becomes particularly important with the move from halogen to LED AGL. LED fixtures can have different electrical characteristics from traditional filament lamps (published FAA research has examined LED taxiway lighting effects on CCR stability), so the CCR should be evaluated for minimum/maximum load, current regulation, output voltage, power factor, harmonics where applicable, LED driver compatibility, intensity-step behaviour and fault detection behaviour.

Myth: LED AGL requires a Smart CCR. Not necessarily. A properly designed conventional CCR can operate LED fixtures if the CCR and LED fixtures are compatible and the complete circuit meets the applicable requirements. Smart CCR adds control/monitoring capability — it isn't inherently required merely because the fixtures are LED.

11 · Smart CCR + LED

Where the Combination Pays Off

LED fixture → lower power demand → Smart CCR → current monitoring → voltage/power monitoring → fault diagnostics → remote AGL monitoring. This combination can be particularly valuable for airports seeking condition-based maintenance and centralized monitoring.

12 · Maintenance Philosophy

Reactive vs Condition-Based

Conventional CCR

More reactive/manual: alarm or failure → technician attends → measurements → troubleshooting → repair.

Smart CCR

Can become more condition-based: continuous monitoring → abnormal trend/alarm → early investigation → planned maintenance → reduced troubleshooting time.

This doesn't automatically make the maintenance predictive; that depends on whether the system actually collects and analyzes meaningful condition data.

14 · SAT / Commissioning

What a Smart CCR SAT Must Cover

For a Smart CCR, SAT should go beyond proving that the CCR produces the correct current. Verify each of the following:

Electrical

  • Input supply
  • Output current & voltage
  • Insulation & protection
  • Correct intensity steps

Functional

  • Local & remote control
  • ON/OFF, step selection
  • Interlocks
  • Alarm operation

Communication

  • Network connection
  • Communication with AGL control system
  • Correct status indication
  • Correct command response

Diagnostics

  • Alarm generation & fault indication
  • Event logging
  • Recovery after fault

16 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: LED AGL always requires a Smart CCR
Fact

A properly designed conventional CCR can operate LED fixtures if the CCR and fixtures are compatible and the complete circuit meets the applicable requirements.

Myth: A Smart CCR's remote monitoring replaces field inspection
Fact

Remote monitoring is not a substitute for all required electrical, photometric and field inspections — "output current = normal" doesn't prove alignment, photometry, insulation, transformer connections or spacing are correct.

Myth: Smart CCR condition monitoring is automatically predictive maintenance
Fact

That depends on whether the system actually collects and analyzes meaningful condition data — the capability alone doesn't make maintenance predictive.

17 · Field Testing Still Required

Online Monitoring ≠ Complete SAT/Inspection

Even with extensive monitoring, a Smart CCR reporting "output current = normal" doesn't necessarily prove:

  • Every fixture is correctly aligned
  • Every fixture produces required photometry
  • Cable insulation is satisfactory
  • Transformer connections are correct
  • Runway spacing is correct
  • Physical damage is absent
18 · Engineering Verdict

Which CCR Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

A conventional CCR is primarily a power-regulation device, whereas a Smart CCR is a power-regulation device combined with digital monitoring, control, communication and diagnostics.

For a modern LED AGL system, the key selection question is not merely "Smart or conventional?" but whether the CCR provides the required current regulation and is fully compatible with the LED fixtures, series circuit, control system and required monitoring/diagnostic functions.

ConventionalBest suited to simple/mature AGL systems where lower initial cost and simpler maintenance are priorities.
SmartBest suited to modern integrated AGL systems seeking condition-based maintenance and centralized monitoring.

19 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Does LED AGL always require a Smart CCR?

No. A properly designed conventional CCR can operate LED fixtures if the CCR and fixtures are compatible and the complete circuit meets the applicable requirements.

Can a Smart CCR identify the exact failed fixture or cable location?

Not automatically. Some systems provide more sophisticated fault localization, but this should not be assumed unless the capability is specifically provided.

Does remote monitoring replace field electrical and photometric testing?

No. Online monitoring is not a substitute for the required electrical, photometric and field inspections.

Is a Smart CCR always the better choice?

Not necessarily. The core current-regulation performance still has to be correct first; sophisticated communication doesn't make a CCR good if the underlying regulation is poor. The economic benefit also depends on airport size, control architecture and maintenance philosophy.

Does Smart CCR monitoring automatically mean predictive maintenance?

No. That depends on whether the system actually collects and analyzes meaningful condition data over time.

What is the single biggest practical difference between the two?

Monitoring. A conventional CCR typically tells an operator only that it is on; a Smart CCR can report step, output current, voltage and alarm status, often remotely.

20 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing References

For an actual airport project, verify the current applicable editions of the relevant standards and the specific CCR manufacturer's documentation before using this comparison for project design.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I — Chapters 8 & 10

Aerodrome Design and Operations: electrical systems for visual aids (power supply, system design and monitoring of lighting systems, particularly for low-visibility operations) and maintenance, including lighting serviceability levels. Verify exact provisions in the current edition.

ICAO Aerodrome Design Manual, Doc 9157 Part 5

Electrical Systems: guidance on electrical practices and installations for aerodromes. Guidance material, not a Standard.

FAA AC 150/5345-10

Specification for Constant Current Regulators and Regulator Monitors: defines CCR types (L-828 regulator without monitoring, L-829 regulator with monitoring, L-827 monitor), classes (6.6 A and 20 A) and 3- or 5-step brightness styles.

FAA AC 150/5345-56

Specification for L-890 Airport Lighting Control and Monitoring System (ALCMS): minimum requirements for control and monitoring of airport lighting.

FAA AC 150/5340-26

Maintenance of Airport Visual Aid Facilities: recommended maintenance practice for lighting circuits, CCRs and ALCMS.

IEC 61820 series & IEC 61821

Electrical installations for AGL at aerodromes (including requirements for series circuits), and maintenance of AGL constant current series circuits.

IEC 62443 / Applicable Cybersecurity Frameworks

Security of industrial automation and control systems: relevant wherever gateways, networks or platforms are added around AGL control and monitoring.

DGCA / AAI Requirements (India)

For Indian airport projects, verify applicable DGCA aerodrome requirements, AAI technical requirements, airport-specific specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

CCR terminology, exact monitoring/diagnostic capability and communication protocols vary by manufacturer. For final design, use the manufacturer's specification and the approved control-system architecture rather than a generic comparison page.

A conventional CCR regulates current. A Smart CCR regulates current and adds monitoring, diagnostics and communication. Evaluate current regulation and electrical performance first, in both cases.
