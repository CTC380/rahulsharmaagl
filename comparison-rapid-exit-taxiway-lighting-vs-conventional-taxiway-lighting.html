Taxiway Lighting Comparison

Rapid Exit Taxiway Lighting VS Conventional Taxiway Lighting

Runway Vacation vs Normal Taxi Guidance — rapid exit taxiway lighting provides enhanced visual guidance specifically for aircraft using a high-speed exit to leave the runway efficiently, while conventional taxiway lighting provides normal visual guidance for taxiing along ordinary taxiways. Both may use green centreline lighting; what sets them apart is the operational application, not a different colour or fixture family.

02 · Definitions

What Is Rapid Exit & Conventional Taxiway Lighting?

Rapid Exit Taxiway Lighting

A rapid-exit taxiway is designed to allow an aircraft to leave the runway at a relatively high speed. The lighting provides conspicuous guidance through the curved runway-exit path.

Provides a reference for

  • Quick identification of the exit route after landing
  • Guidance through the curved high-speed exit geometry
  • The transition from runway to taxiway environment
  • Reduced runway occupancy time

Conventional Taxiway Lighting

Provides normal visual guidance for taxiing along ordinary taxiways, whether straight or curved, that are not specifically designed as high-speed runway exits.

Provides a reference for

  • The designated taxi route between movement areas
  • Ordinary taxiing, not runway vacation
  • Continuous route guidance day and night
  • Standard edge and centreline referencing
03 · Biggest Difference

Get Off the Runway vs Move Along the Taxiway

The comparison is not about brightness or a different colour family — both commonly use green centreline lighting. The real distinction is operational application: one is defined by high-speed runway vacation, the other by ordinary taxi movement.

Rapid-exit lighting guides the pilot through a curved, high-speed transition from the runway to the taxiway. Conventional taxiway lighting supports ordinary taxiing — straight or curved — once the aircraft is established on the taxiway.

05 · Pilot Perspective After Landing

What the Pilot Recognises

On the Rapid Exit

The pilot needs to recognise: "This is the rapid-exit route" — and follow the conspicuous guidance leading off the runway at speed.

On a Conventional Taxiway

The pilot needs to recognise: "Follow the taxiway" — the normal taxi-route guidance takes over once the aircraft is off the runway.

Why it matters: rapid runway exits are intended to reduce the time an aircraft occupies the runway. Conspicuous lighting helps the pilot identify the intended exit route, particularly during night or reduced-visibility operations.

06 · Centreline Guidance

Same Colour, Different Operational Purpose

Both systems can use centreline lighting, but the operational purpose differs. On the rapid-exit taxiway, the centreline leads the aircraft through the exit curve. On a conventional taxiway, the centreline provides ordinary taxi guidance along the route.

07 · Relationship to Runway Centreline

Runway Guidance → Exit Guidance → Taxiway Guidance

Rapid-exit lighting becomes important at the transition between runway and taxiway. It helps the pilot move from one guidance system to the next in sequence:

  • Runway centreline guidance during the landing rollout
  • Rapid-exit lighting guidance through the curved vacation path
  • Conventional taxiway lighting guidance once established on the taxiway

Conventional taxiway lighting does not play a role in this runway-to-taxiway transition — its guidance begins once the aircraft is already operating within the ordinary taxiway environment.

08 · Composition of Conventional Taxiway Lighting

What the Normal System Includes

Typical Elements

  • Blue taxiway edge lighting
  • Green taxiway centreline lighting
  • Other applicable taxiway guidance appropriate to the airport

What Rapid Exit Adds

Rapid-exit lighting is not a separate colour family — it is the same general lighting elements applied with emphasis on conspicuous guidance through a specific, high-speed exit geometry.

11 · Myth vs Fact

Common Misunderstanding

Myth: One type of light is brighter than the other
Fact

Brightness is not the defining distinction. The more important difference is that rapid-exit lighting is defined by its operational application at a rapid-exit taxiway, while conventional taxiway lighting is the normal lighting used for ordinary taxiway guidance.

Myth: Rapid-exit lighting is a completely different colour system
Fact

Both systems commonly rely on the same taxiway lighting colours, including green centreline lighting. What differs is the geometry and operational purpose, not the colour palette.

Myth: Conventional taxiway lighting guides the runway-to-taxiway transition
Fact

That transition is specifically the role of rapid-exit lighting. Conventional taxiway lighting takes over as the normal visual guidance environment only once the aircraft is established on the taxiway.

12 · Engineering Verdict

Rapid Exit Taxiway Lighting vs Conventional Taxiway Lighting

Engineering Verdict

Rapid exit and conventional taxiway lighting are the same lighting family applied to two different operational situations, not two competing colour systems. One is defined by its role in a high-speed runway vacation; the other is defined by its role in ordinary taxi movement.

Together, they form a continuous chain of guidance — runway centreline into rapid-exit lighting into conventional taxiway lighting — that carries the aircraft smoothly from landing rollout to routine taxiing.

Rapid ExitRunway vacation + curved high-speed guidance + "get off the runway."
ConventionalNormal taxi movement + continuous route guidance + "follow the taxiway."

Easy memory: Rapid Exit = runway vacation guidance. Conventional = normal taxi guidance.

13 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between rapid exit and conventional taxiway lighting?

Operational application, not colour — rapid-exit lighting guides aircraft through a high-speed curved exit from the runway, while conventional taxiway lighting provides normal guidance for ordinary taxiing.

Is rapid-exit lighting a different colour to conventional taxiway lighting?

No. Both commonly use the same general taxiway lighting colours, including green centreline lighting. The distinction is the operational application, not the colour family.

Why do rapid-exit taxiways matter for runway operations?

Rapid runway exits are intended to reduce the time an aircraft occupies the runway, which supports overall runway capacity and operational efficiency.

When does conventional taxiway lighting take over from rapid-exit lighting?

Once the aircraft is established on the taxiway after vacating the runway, conventional taxiway lighting becomes the normal visual guidance environment.

Does conventional taxiway lighting ever involve curves?

Yes, a conventional taxiway may be straight or curved, but it is not specifically designed as a high-speed runway exit the way a rapid-exit taxiway is.

Which system is used first after landing?

Rapid-exit lighting is used first, guiding the aircraft off the runway, followed by conventional taxiway lighting once the aircraft is established on the taxiway.

14 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — rapid-exit taxiway design principles and taxiway lighting colour, siting, intensity and operational requirements.

FAA AC 150/5340-30

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids — taxiway lighting configuration, installation, photometric requirements and electrical systems.

FAA AC 150/5345-46

Specification for Runway, Taxiway, Heliport, and Vertiport Light Fixtures — the fixture equipment spec governing the taxiway edge and centreline fixtures used on both rapid-exit and conventional taxiways.

DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Do not design a rapid-exit or taxiway lighting arrangement from a generic web diagram. The final installation drawing should always be developed from the current governing standard, approved airport geometry and project-specific requirements.

Rapid exit lighting gets the aircraft off the runway. Conventional taxiway lighting keeps it moving once it's on.
