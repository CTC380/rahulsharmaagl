New Materials & Manufacturing Innovation

Division 17 is the materials science, component manufacturing and production-technology division of the AGL Innovation Hub. It investigates how new materials, advanced manufacturing methods, sustainable materials, automated production and digital quality control can improve AGL equipment performance, reliability, environmental resistance, manufacturability and lifecycle cost. Its core philosophy: Better Materials → Better Components → Better Manufacturing → Better Quality → Better Reliability → Better Lifecycle Performance.

Material Qualification Framework

Every new material should ideally pass through this eight-stage structured evaluation before operational adoption.

STAGE 1

Material Identification

Material, manufacturer, grade, properties, intended application.

STAGE 2

Engineering Screening

Mechanical, electrical, thermal, optical and environmental properties.

STAGE 3

Compatibility

Existing materials, sealants, fasteners, coatings, electronics, optical components.

STAGE 4

Laboratory Testing

Environmental, mechanical, electrical, thermal, and optical where applicable.

STAGE 5

Prototype

Manufacture a representative component.

STAGE 6

Validation

Evaluate actual component performance.

STAGE 7

Field Evaluation

Assess long-term performance under real operating conditions.

STAGE 8

Engineering Approval

Document suitability and limitations.

Additive Manufacturing Maturity Model

A useful way to develop additive manufacturing capability is progressively, level by level.

LEVEL 1

Prototyping

3D printing used mainly for concepts.

LEVEL 2

Engineering Validation

Prototype components used for fit and design verification.

LEVEL 3

Manufacturing Support

Jigs, fixtures and tooling produced through additive manufacturing.

LEVEL 4

Qualified Low-Volume Parts

Selected components produced under controlled quality processes.

LEVEL 5

Digital Spare-Part Manufacturing

Approved digital part designs support controlled spare-part production.

LEVEL 6

Distributed Manufacturing

Future possibility of producing qualified components closer to the point of need, subject to engineering, quality and regulatory controls.

Intelligent Manufacturing Maturity

A parallel maturity model tracking how manufacturing becomes progressively automated, connected and adaptive.

LEVEL 1

Manual Manufacturing

Human-operated production.

LEVEL 2

Mechanized Manufacturing

Machines assist production.

LEVEL 3

Automated Manufacturing

Machines perform defined production tasks.

LEVEL 4

Digitally Connected Manufacturing

Production and quality information are digitally connected.

LEVEL 5

Intelligent Manufacturing

AI/analytics optimize production and detect defects.

LEVEL 6

Adaptive Manufacturing

Production processes dynamically respond to controlled production and quality data.

Most Important Design Principle

Division 17 should remain the materials-and-manufacturing foundation, not the product or reliability layer itself. It defines what components are made of and how they are manufactured, inspected and traced — while the product requirements sit in Division 08 — AGL Product Innovation, the reliability and safety requirements sit in Division 16 — Advanced Safety & Reliability, the optical requirements sit in Division 14 — Advanced Photometry & Optical Innovation, and the sustainability direction comes from Division 07 — Sustainable AGL & Green Airports.

Its central philosophy: design better materials → manufacture better components → inspect intelligently → validate performance → capture digital traceability → monitor lifecycle performance → continuously improve the product.

This makes Division 17 the materials-and-manufacturing foundation of the AGL Product Innovation programme, while creating a direct bridge from new material research to actual AGL component development, qualification, production and lifecycle reliability — feeding directly into the AGL Component/Product Encyclopedia and the Asset Lifecycle Master Database.
