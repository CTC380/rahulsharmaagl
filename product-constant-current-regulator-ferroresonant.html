Product Overview

The Controlled Power Source for a Series Circuit

In a typical series-circuit AGL installation the regulator supplies controlled current through the primary circuit, and individual fixtures connect to it through isolating transformers. The CCR, cable, transformers and fixtures should be considered as one coordinated electrical system.

Ferroresonant CCR → Primary Cable → Primary Connectors → Isolating Transformers → AGL Light Fixtures

The regulator forms an important part of the AGL power system and should be selected together with the connected fixtures, isolating transformers, primary cable and other series-circuit components.

Ferroresonant CCR equipment produces heat during operation and must be installed with suitable thermal management. Cooling requirements depend on:

  • Regulator rating

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Where the site procedure requires it, short and earth the circuit at the work location with approved equipment to discharge residual or induced voltage before touching conductors
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures
Standards

CCR Specification and Ratings

FAA Specification

Constant current regulators and regulator monitors for airport lighting series circuits are covered by FAA AC 150/5345-10H — Specification for Constant Current Regulators and Regulator Monitors. Confirm the current edition and the applicable class/style designation with the manufacturer datasheet.

Ferroresonant CCRs are designated L-828 (60 Hz) and L-829 (50 Hz); for 50 Hz networks such as India, specify L-829. Class 1 covers 6.6 A circuits and Class 2 covers 20 A circuits; Style 1 is 3-step and Style 2 is 5-step.

Also review IEC 61822, ICAO Annex 14 / Doc 9157 Part 5 and the applicable national electrical requirements for the project.

Ratings to Specify

  • Regulator rating (kVA) matched to the connected circuit load
  • Rated output current and available current steps (for example 3-step or 5-step brightness control)
  • Input supply voltage and frequency
  • Regulator class/style per the applicable specification
  • Circuit load range: ferroresonant regulators hold output current only within a defined load range, so check the circuit stays inside it after any fixture change
  • Open-circuit, over-current and over-voltage protection, and a regulator monitor where required
  • Monitoring and remote-control interface

Exact kVA and current ranges offered are supplied on the manufacturer datasheet for the project.

FAQ

Ferroresonant Constant Current Regulator — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a ferroresonant constant current regulator?

A ferroresonant CCR uses a ferroresonant magnetic circuit to provide controlled current to series-circuit Airfield Ground Lighting, supplying runway, taxiway, approach and associated circuits at defined operating current levels. The regulator, primary cable, transformers and fixtures should be considered as one coordinated electrical system.

What are the L-828 and L-829 designations?

Ferroresonant CCRs are designated L-828 for 60 Hz and L-829 for 50 Hz under FAA AC 150/5345-10H. For 50 Hz networks such as India, specify L-829. Class 1 covers 6.6 A circuits and Class 2 covers 20 A circuits, while Style 1 is 3-step and Style 2 is 5-step. See also the thyristor CCR page.

What are the standard intensity step currents?

On FAA 6.6 A circuits, a 3-step regulator uses 4.8 / 5.5 / 6.6 A and a 5-step regulator uses 2.8 / 3.4 / 4.1 / 5.2 / 6.6 A. On FAA 20 A circuits, a 5-step regulator uses 8.5 / 10.3 / 12.4 / 15.8 / 20 A. The number and values of steps should be established from the project lighting design and control requirements.

How is a CCR sized for a series circuit?

Size it on the actual electrical characteristics of the complete circuit, not nominal fixture voltage: the application, the number and type of fixtures, isolating transformer ratings, primary cable length and impedance, required current and intensity steps, input supply, and control requirements. Changes to fixture count, transformer ratings or cable arrangement can affect the CCR requirement.

What cooling does a ferroresonant CCR need?

It produces heat during operation and needs suitable thermal management. Cooling depends on the regulator rating, operating load, ambient temperature, enclosure configuration, ventilation and duty cycle. For enclosed CCR rooms or cabinets, consider available heat dissipation capacity during design.

What control and protection functions are available?

Depending on configuration, local operation, remote intensity selection, run/stop and step selection, status and alarm indication, a control-system interface, and protection of input, output, internal components, temperature, overload, faults and control circuits. Coordinate alarm interfaces with the airport AGL control and monitoring system.

What should be reviewed before replacing an existing regulator?

Review the existing regulator rating, input supply, output current, connected series-circuit load, number of intensity steps, control interface, alarm requirements, installation dimensions, cable terminations, cooling arrangement and circuit condition. Select the replacement from the actual circuit requirements, not simply by matching the nameplate rating of the old regulator.

What is checked at commissioning?

Verify the incoming supply, power and control connections, earthing, series-circuit connections, continuity and insulation condition, measure the regulator output current, verify the intensity steps, test local and remote controls and alarm and status interfaces, and carry out functional testing of the connected lighting circuit.

What should I investigate when the circuit does not operate or alarms repeat?

For a circuit not operating, check incoming power, CCR status, control command, protection status, output connection, series-circuit continuity, primary cable and isolating transformers. For incorrect intensity, check the selected step, output current, connected load and transformer configuration. For repeated protection or alarms, investigate rather than repeatedly reset.

Is it safe to disconnect part of an energised series circuit?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage, and opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death. De-energise at the regulator, apply lock-out / tag-out, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device and work under the permit-to-work system. See the electrical safety warning on this page.

What information should I provide for a CCR quotation?

Provide the application and circuit type, required rated current, input voltage and frequency, number of connected fixtures and fixture type, isolating transformer ratings, primary cable information, required intensity steps, control interface, existing CCR make, model and rating for replacements, installation location, cooling arrangement, project specification and quantity. Photographs of the existing CCR nameplate and control connections help.
