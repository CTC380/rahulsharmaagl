Stop Bar Inset Fixture

A flush-mounted stop bar light fixture installed directly into the pavement at runway holding positions, providing a low-profile, high-durability visual barrier of red lights that indicates to pilots when it is unsafe to proceed onto the runway. The inset design eliminates protrusion above the pavement, making it ideal for snowplough operations, low-clearance areas, and high-traffic taxiways.

Overview

What Is It?

A Stop Bar Inset Fixture is a flush-mounted stop bar light fixture installed directly into the pavement at runway holding positions, providing a low-profile, high-durability visual barrier of red lights that indicates to pilots when it is unsafe to proceed onto the runway. The inset design eliminates protrusion above the pavement, making it ideal for snowplough operations, low-clearance areas, and high-traffic taxiways. These fixtures are typically installed in a row across the taxiway at the runway holding position, creating a continuous line of red lights that forms a clear visual stop barrier.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The inset stop bar fixture exists to give a runway holding position an unmistakable red barrier without adding a raised obstruction to a taxiway that also has to accommodate snowploughs, ground vehicles, and continuous aircraft traffic. Because the holding position sees heavy repeated use, a fixture that sits proud of the pavement here would be far more exposed to being struck or damaged than an elevated light in a lower-traffic location, undermining the reliability of a safety barrier that has to work every single time.

By keeping the same unidirectional red beam and safety function as any stop bar light while eliminating the physical protrusion, the inset design lets airports maintain this critical incursion defence at exactly the high-traffic locations where it's needed most, without introducing the maintenance burden and strike risk an elevated fixture would carry in that environment.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Stop bar inset fixtures are installed at runway holding positions on taxiways where a flush-mounted solution is preferred or required. They are used on:

  • Precision approach runways (CAT II and III) where low-profile lighting is required
  • Snowplough-prone airports where elevated fixtures would be damaged
  • Busy taxiways with high aircraft traffic where protrusions are a hazard
  • Runway holding positions at the intersection of taxiways and runways
  • Low visibility operations where clear visual cues are essential
  • Military airfields requiring robust, damage-resistant fixtures
Placement

Installation & Location

Fixtures are set into the pavement in a row across the full taxiway width at the runway holding position, on a line perpendicular to the taxiway centreline, spaced to match the airport's stop bar lighting scheme so the completed row forms a single, unbroken red barrier flush with the surrounding surface. Each unit is seated into an L-868-style base can recessed into the pavement, sealed against surface water and de-icing fluid ingress, with the fixture's low profile specifically chosen to withstand the repeated snowplough and heavy traffic loading this location sees. Power and control cabling is fed from the stop bar's own switchable circuit, run in duct back to the stop bar control module, kept independent of the general taxiway edge lighting so the bar can be illuminated or extinguished under direct ATC or ALCMS control without affecting other taxiway lighting. Setting-out is tied to the taxiway's surveyed holding position so the row of lights sits exactly on the boundary line it's marking, rather than offset from it.

Function

How Does It Work?

Stop bar inset fixtures operate as an integral part of the runway safety system with a rugged, low-profile design:

  • Power: Powered from the constant current series circuit via an isolating transformer buried beneath the pavement (typically 45W – 65W).
  • Light Source: Utilizes red LED arrays or red-filtered halogen lamps. The red colour provides a clear stop indication that is universally recognized by pilots.
  • Beam Pattern: Projects a uni-directional beam pattern towards approaching aircraft, ensuring the red light is visible only to pilots facing the stop bar.
  • Mounting: Flush-mounted in a base can or housing set into the pavement, with a heavy-duty lens designed to withstand aircraft wheel loads of up to 100,000 kg.
  • Control: Controlled by the ATC or ALCMS via the stop bar control module, with illumination indicating that the runway is occupied and it is unsafe to proceed.
Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

The fixture itself holds no intelligence — it is a fixed-current series-circuit light like a runway edge or taxiway light, and every switching decision happens upstream of it, in the stop bar control module and, above that, the ALCMS or the tower controller. This separation matters for how faults are read: because the control module is what issues the on/off command and the monitoring unit is what verifies the result, a fixture that fails to illuminate on command surfaces as a discrepancy between commanded state and monitored state, not as a signal from the fixture itself. Because a stop bar's function is to prevent a runway incursion, its control architecture is generally treated as safety-critical rather than convenience-critical: illumination is commanded only when the runway is confirmed occupied or the crossing is otherwise unsafe, and the corresponding runway entrance and centreline lead-on lights are interlocked against the stop bar state so the two indications can't contradict each other at the same holding position.

Construction

Physical Construction

The fixture body is a die-cast aluminium housing, 200–250 mm in diameter and only 50–80 mm tall above its base, anodised or powder-coated for resistance to de-icing fluid, fuel spillage, and UV exposure. A red LED array or filtered 45W–65W halogen lamp sits behind a gasket-sealed optical prism, O-ring sealed to the housing to achieve an IP67/IP68 rating against water and dust ingress. The low-profile inset design seats the fixture into an L-868 base can recessed flush with the pavement, with the top surface load-rated for a 100,000 kg (220,000 lb) aircraft wheel load and the beam pattern angled uni-directionally toward approaching aircraft. Internal wiring terminates at L-823 sealed splice connectors rated for direct burial and continuous immersion, feeding back through a 45W–65W isolating transformer housed in its own nearby base can, with stainless-steel fasteners used throughout to resist corrosion.

Upkeep

Inspection & Maintenance

Lens Cleaning
Quarterly

Wipe the fixture lens free of rubber deposits, de-icing fluid residue, and dirt with approved solvents so light output and beam shape stay within spec.

Photometric Intensity Check
Annual

Verify beam intensity and colour against ICAO / FAA thresholds using a calibrated photometer to catch lens haze or LED degradation early.

Seal & Pressure Vent Inspection
Annual

Check top-cap seals, gaskets, and pressure vents for cracking or compression set, and re-torque fasteners to keep the fixture watertight to its IP68 rating.

Insulation Resistance Test
Annual

Megger-test the isolating transformer and series circuit to confirm insulation resistance stays above the minimum, flagging moisture ingress before it causes a fault.

Cable & Connector Inspection
Annual

Examine primary and secondary cable connections for corrosion or loose crimps, a common source of intermittent series-circuit faults.

Red Filter / LED Degradation Check
Semi-annual

Assess the red source or filter for UV-driven output loss or colour shift, since this fixture depends on a distinctive red indication.

Housing & Load-Bearing Surface Inspection
Annual

Check the fixture body and surrounding pavement for settlement, cracking, or spalling that could compromise the rated 100,000 kg wheel-load capacity.

Drainage Check
Semi-annual

Confirm drainage paths around the inset fixture are clear so standing water doesn't pool against seals or accelerate pavement deterioration.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Lens Contamination and Wear

Inset fixtures are exposed to rubber deposits from aircraft tyres, de-icing fluids, dirt, and abrasion from ground vehicles. Regular cleaning with approved solvents and soft brushes is required to maintain light output. Worn lenses may need replacement to ensure proper photometric performance.

Moisture Ingress

Failed seals or pressure vents can allow moisture to enter the fixture, causing corrosion, short circuits, or optical degradation. Regular inspection of seals, gaskets, and pressure vents is essential to maintain the IP68 rating.

Red Filter/LED Degradation

Red light sources and filters are susceptible to UV degradation and thermal stress. Over time, output can decrease or colour can shift. Regular photometric testing and replacement of LED modules or filters is essential to maintain the distinctive red stop indication.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Reduced Red Light Output or Worn Lens
Likely Cause
Rubber deposits, de-icing fluid, dirt, or abrasion from ground vehicles have contaminated or worn the fixture lens.
Diagnostic Steps
Visually inspect the lens for film, scratching, or wear; take a photometric reading and compare against specification.
Corrective Action
Clean the lens with approved solvents and a soft brush, or replace a worn lens where cleaning does not restore photometric performance.
Fixture Fails or Shows Internal Corrosion
Likely Cause
A failed seal, gasket, or pressure vent has allowed moisture into the inset fixture, compromising its IP68 rating.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect seals, gaskets, and pressure vents for damage; check for internal corrosion or short circuits when the fixture is removed.
Corrective Action
Replace the failed seal/gasket/vent and the fixture if internal corrosion is found, then re-verify the IP rating before reinstating.
Red Indication Appears Dim or Colour-Shifted
Likely Cause
UV degradation and thermal stress have affected the red filter or LED module, reducing output or shifting colour.
Diagnostic Steps
Run a photometric colour and intensity test on the fixture and compare against the required standard for the stop indication.
Corrective Action
Replace the degraded LED module or filter and re-verify photometric compliance before closing the finding.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

LED Array / Isolating Transformer Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Thermal cycling or a manufacturing defect in the LED array, or moisture-driven insulation breakdown in the isolating transformer winding.
Effect
The fixture goes dark, removing the unmistakable red barrier at a holding position that sees continuous heavy traffic.
Detection Method
Test activation of the fixture, and megger testing of the isolating transformer.
Lens Fatigue Under Repeated Wheel or Vehicle Loading
High Severity
Root Cause
Heavy, repeated aircraft wheel and ground vehicle loading at a high-traffic holding position, the exact exposure the inset design accepts in exchange for eliminating a raised obstruction.
Effect
Cracked lens with moisture ingress, or reduced photometric output from surface wear, at exactly the location where reliability matters most.
Detection Method
Lens and top-cap seal inspection at a shorter interval than lower-traffic locations, given the fixture's heavy-use exposure.
Uni-Directional Beam Misalignment
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Settlement or an impact event shifting the fixture's unidirectional optic out of its commissioned aim.
Effect
The red barrier becomes less conspicuous or unevenly visible from the approach direction it's meant to warn, weakening the incursion defence it provides.
Detection Method
Fixture aim check against the holding position layout during scheduled inspection.
L-823 Connector / Base Can Moisture Ingress
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Standing water or gasket failure at the base can given the fixture's flush-mounted, pavement-level installation.
Effect
Intermittent operation or a partial ground fault, undermining reliability at a location that has to work every single time.
Detection Method
Megger testing of the circuit and visual inspection of the base can seal during maintenance.
Snowplough / Ground Vehicle Impact Damage
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Direct contact from a snowplough blade or ground vehicle at a high-traffic location the inset design specifically has to tolerate.
Effect
Cracked lens or housing damage, sometimes not immediately obvious given the fixture sits flush with the surrounding pavement.
Detection Method
Post-winter-operations or post-incident inspection specifically targeting inset stop bar fixtures on plough routes.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Series Circuit Isolation
Electrical Safety

De-energise and lock out the CCR feeding the stop bar circuit and verify zero energy at the fixture before opening the inset base can or disturbing connectors.

ATC Coordination for a Dark Stop Bar
Operational

A stop bar out of service removes a primary runway-incursion barrier at a holding position; notify ATC before starting work, arrange an alternate hold-short procedure, and raise a NOTAM for the outage.

Live-Traffic Exposure at the Holding Position
Personnel Safety

The holding position carries continuous aircraft and ground-vehicle traffic; keep personnel and open base cans clear of the through path and use spotters or a closure when working during active movement.

FOD Control at the Base Can
Operational

Keep tools, fasteners, and gaskets accounted for while the flush-mounted base can is open, and secure or remove loose material before reinstating the fixture to prevent foreign object debris on a high-traffic surface.

Insulation Test Before Re-Energisation
Electrical Safety

Megger test the affected circuit after any cable, connector, or transformer work, since a residual fault at re-energisation can darken the fixture again immediately or trip the CCR.

PPE & Grounding
Personnel Safety

Use insulated tools and appropriate PPE when handling series-circuit connections, and confirm the base can is bonded/grounded per the electrical design before reconnecting the loop.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Confirm correct uni-directional aim, lens condition, and full-intensity operation before closing out the work; do not reopen the holding position to traffic with the stop bar in a degraded state.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Never Simulate 'Off' by Physically Blocking the Lens
Compliance

Manually covering or blacking out a stop bar lens for a quick test is not the same as verifying its actual controlled state through ALCMS; the interlock logic must be exercised through the control system to prove the fixture behaves correctly, not just that it can be made to look dark.

Uni-Directional Aim Is Easy to Get Backwards on Reassembly
Mechanical

Because a stop bar's red lens must face only the approaching direction, a fixture reseated with even a slight rotational offset can present an ambiguous or reduced-intensity signal to the one direction that matters most; confirm orientation against a reference mark, not by eye alone.

Base Can Seal Is the Long-Term Failure Point on Inset Fixtures
Durability

Standing water and de-icing fluid pooling in a recessed base can degrade gaskets and cable glands faster than sun and weather alone, especially at a holding position with heavy traffic; specify seal materials rated for glycol contact and inspect gasket condition at overhaul.

Trend Loop Readings, Don't Just Spot-Check
Troubleshooting

Moisture ingress at an L-823 connector usually shows up first as a slow drift in loop insulation resistance, not a hard fault. Logging megger readings over successive maintenance cycles catches a failing splice months before it takes the loop down.

Size the Transformer to the Load, Not the Legacy Lamp
Electrical

Reusing an isolating transformer sized for the old incandescent wattage on a much lower-draw LED fixture is a common source of premature transformer failure and nuisance loop trips; re-select VA rating against the actual LED load rather than assuming the existing transformer is "close enough."

Balance Loops Sharing One CCR
Design

Where multiple series loops run off a single CCR, unequal loop lengths or lamp counts between loops can produce visibly uneven brightness even though each individual fixture tests within photometric tolerance. Balance loop impedance at the design stage, not after commissioning complaints.

Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about inset stop bar lights: function, colour, control, standards, maintenance and common faults.

Q1What is a stop bar inset fixture?

A stop bar inset fixture is a flush-mounted stop bar light installed in the pavement at a runway holding position. A row of these red lights forms a low-profile barrier telling pilots it is unsafe to proceed onto the runway.

Q2What colour is a stop bar light and who can see it?

Red, in a unidirectional beam aimed at aircraft or vehicles approaching the holding position, so it is visible only to traffic facing the stop bar.

Q3Who controls the stop bar?

Air traffic control or the ALCMS via the stop bar control module. When the bar is lit, it indicates that the runway is occupied or it is unsafe to proceed.

Q4Why use inset stop bar lights instead of elevated ones?

Inset fixtures add no raised obstruction on busy taxiways and survive snowploughs and ground vehicles. They are rated for aircraft wheel loads up to 100,000 kg.

Q5Where are stop bars installed?

In a row across the full taxiway width at the runway holding position, on a line perpendicular to the taxiway centreline. Inset types are common on CAT II/III runways and at busy or snowplough-prone airports.

Q6Which standards apply to stop bar inset fixtures?

The main references are ICAO Annex 14 and FAA AC 150/5345-46, with IEC 61822 for the constant current regulators and IEC 61821 for maintenance of the series circuit. Always verify the current edition.

Q7How often should stop bar inset fixtures be maintained?

Typical intervals are quarterly lens cleaning and annual photometric checks, seal and pressure vent inspection, insulation resistance tests and cable and connector inspection. Your airport's approved maintenance programme takes precedence.

Q8What are the common faults on inset stop bar fixtures?

Common problems are lens contamination and wear from tyre rubber, de-icing fluid and vehicles, moisture ingress through failed seals or vents, and red LED or filter degradation that lowers output or shifts colour.
