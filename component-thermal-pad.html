Thermal Pad

A thermally conductive, electrically insulating pad placed between heat‑generating components (such as LED PCBs or drivers) and heat sinks or fixture housings. Designed to eliminate air gaps and improve thermal transfer, ensuring efficient heat dissipation and reliable long‑term operation of AGL equipment in demanding airfield environments.

Overview

What Is It?

A Thermal Pad (also known as a thermal interface pad or thermal gap pad) is a pre‑formed, soft, thermally conductive material placed between two surfaces to improve heat transfer. In AGL applications, thermal pads are used between heat‑generating components (such as LED PCBs, LED drivers, or power transistors) and heat sinks or the fixture housing. They fill microscopic air gaps and surface irregularities that would otherwise trap air and act as thermal insulators, significantly reducing thermal resistance and ensuring efficient cooling.

Key functions of a thermal pad in AGL applications include:

  • Thermal Conduction: Providing a low‑resistance path for heat to flow from the component to the heat sink or housing.
  • Electrical Insulation: Offering high dielectric strength to prevent electrical short circuits between the component and the heat sink, which is often metallic.
  • Gap Filling: Complying to fill uneven gaps between surfaces, accommodating tolerances and ensuring full contact.
  • Vibration Damping: Absorbing mechanical vibrations and shocks, protecting the component from stress.
  • Pressure Distribution: Evenly distributing mounting pressure to avoid damage to fragile components (e.g., LED PCBs).

Thermal pads are preferred over thermal grease in many AGL applications because they are clean, easy to apply, pre‑formed to the required shape, and maintain consistent performance over time without drying out or migrating.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The thermal pad's purpose is to make sure the heat a component actually generates reaches the heat sink or housing meant to dissipate it, rather than being trapped by the microscopic air gaps that exist at any real-world mechanical interface — air being a poor conductor of heat, even a thin, seemingly negligible gap between an LED PCB and its heat sink can create a surprisingly large thermal bottleneck. By compressing to fill those gaps while still providing electrical insulation between a live component and a metal housing, the pad lets the fixture's heat-dissipation design actually deliver the thermal performance it was engineered for, and its vibration-damping properties protect delicate components from the mechanical stress an airfield's constant vibration would otherwise transmit directly into a rigid joint.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Thermal pads are used in virtually all thermally critical areas of AGL equipment:

  • LED PCBs to Heat Sinks: Placed between the metal‑core PCB (MCPCB) and the fixture's aluminium heat sink to transfer heat from the LEDs to the fixture for dissipation.
  • LED Drivers to Housing: Between the driver's power components (e.g., MOSFETs, transformers) and the metal housing to cool the driver.
  • CCR Power Components: In Constant Current Regulators, thermal pads are used between high‑power transistors or rectifiers and the heatsink.
  • Power Supplies and Rectifiers: In various power conversion stages within AGL systems.
  • Control Electronics: For cooling of voltage regulators and other heat‑generating ICs.
  • ALCMS Server and Networking Equipment: Where processors and power supplies require cooling.
  • PAPI and Approach Light Fixtures: In high‑intensity LED assemblies.

Thermal pads are also used in:

  • Retrofit Upgrades: When replacing older components with LED modules, thermal pads ensure proper thermal interface.
  • Maintenance and Repair: Replacing degraded pads during servicing to restore thermal performance.
  • Prototype and R&D: For testing thermal performance of new designs.
Placement

Installation & Location

The pad is cut or supplied to size and placed directly between the two surfaces it thermally couples, typically the LED PCB and the heat sink, or a driver's power component and the housing wall, with its protective release film peeled away immediately before assembly to keep the contact faces clean. The parts are then clamped or bolted together to the specified compression, since a thermal pad performs correctly only within its designed compression range; too little clamping leaves air gaps, while excessive compression can squeeze the pad thinner than intended and reduce its insulating or gap-filling properties. It's fitted as a one-time consumable and replaced, not reused, whenever the joint is opened for servicing, since a pad that has already taken a compression set won't reseat evenly a second time.

Function

How Does It Work?

A thermal pad functions by providing a highly conductive interface between two surfaces, minimising thermal resistance:

  • Material Composition: Thermal pads are typically made from a silicone, acrylic, or urethane matrix filled with thermally conductive particles (e.g., aluminium oxide, boron nitride, or graphite). The matrix provides compliance and gap‑filling capability, while the fillers provide the thermal conductivity.
  • Thermal Conductivity: The pad has a specified thermal conductivity (typically 1 – 6 W/m·K), which determines how efficiently it transfers heat. Higher conductivity means better heat transfer for a given thickness and area.
  • Compression and Gap Filling: When compressed between the two surfaces (by mounting screws or clamping force), the pad deforms to fill microscopic air gaps and surface irregularities. This ensures that air (which has very low thermal conductivity) is displaced, maximising the contact area for heat transfer.
  • Pressure and Thermal Resistance: The thermal resistance of the pad depends on the applied pressure. Higher pressure reduces the pad's thickness and improves contact, lowering thermal resistance. However, excessive pressure can damage components; the pad is designed to provide optimal performance at the recommended mounting pressure.
  • Electrical Insulation: The matrix material is an electrical insulator, providing dielectric strength typically > 3 kV/mm, preventing electrical shorting between the component and the metallic heat sink.
  • Long‑Term Stability: Thermal pads are designed to maintain their thermal and mechanical properties over a wide temperature range (-40°C to +150°C) and over many thermal cycles, without drying out, pumping out, or degrading.
  • Adhesive or Non‑Adhesive: Some pads have a light adhesive on one or both sides to aid in assembly, while others rely solely on mechanical compression for retention.

In AGL fixtures, the thermal pad is typically placed between the LED PCB and the fixture's heat sink base. When the PCB is screwed down, the pad compresses, ensuring efficient heat flow from the LEDs to the heat sink, which then dissipates heat to the ambient air.

System Design

System Architecture & Control Logic

A thermal pad has no electronics, so its "control logic" is entirely mechanical and set once at assembly — by mounting screw torque and pad thickness — rather than adjusted in service. The heat path runs in one fixed direction: LED die → PCB copper → thermal pad → heat sink base → ambient air, and the pad's only function is to remove the air gap at the PCB-to-heat-sink joint, since air's thermal conductivity is orders of magnitude below any filled polymer. The governing variable is compressive pressure, set by the mounting screw pattern and torque spec: increasing pressure thins the pad and improves contact down to a design-recommended minimum thickness, below which further compression risks damaging the PCB or extruding the pad material rather than improving heat transfer. Because there is no active regulation, the "system" is self-limiting by geometry — the mounting hardware compresses the pad only until the PCB seats against its standoffs, which fixes the final gap and therefore the steady-state thermal resistance for the life of the fixture, unless the pad degrades (drying out, pumping out) or the joint is disturbed during later maintenance.

There is no feedback or adjustment once the fixture is assembled — mounting torque and pad thickness set the thermal resistance for the fixture's service life.

Construction

Physical Construction

A thermal pad is manufactured as a pre-formed, soft, thermally conductive sheet, typically a silicone or polymer matrix loaded with thermally conductive filler particles (such as ceramic, aluminium oxide, or boron nitride) to achieve its rated thermal conductivity while remaining electrically insulating. The pad is calendared or moulded to a controlled thickness and supplied with a low-tack adhesive or liner on one or both faces to hold it in position during assembly between a heat-generating component, such as an LED PCB or driver, and the heat sink or fixture housing it is bonded to. Its compliant, conformable structure is designed to deform under the clamping pressure applied when the fixture is assembled, filling microscopic surface irregularities and air gaps on both mating surfaces so heat transfers by direct contact rather than through an insulating air layer. The material formulation is selected for a stable, self-extinguishing flammability rating and low outgassing, since the pad operates sealed within a fixture housing where trapped gases or degradation products could affect the fixture's optical or electrical components over its service life.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Pad Degradation and Hardening

Over time, thermal pads can harden or degrade due to high operating temperatures, UV exposure (if not shielded), or chemical attack. This increases thermal resistance and reduces cooling efficiency. Regular inspection and replacement of pads (typically every 5–7 years) are recommended, especially in high‑temperature or high‑power applications.

Insufficient Pressure or Uneven Compression

If the mounting screws are not torqued correctly, the pad may not be compressed enough to fill the gaps, leading to poor thermal contact. Uneven pressure can also cause the pad to be thicker in some areas, creating hot spots. Ensure that the correct torque is applied evenly to all mounting points.

Contamination and Debris

Dust, dirt, or grease on the surfaces before pad application can reduce thermal conductivity and prevent proper adhesion/compression. Always clean the surfaces with isopropyl alcohol and a lint‑free cloth before installing a new pad.

Incorrect Pad Thickness

Using a pad that is too thick can reduce thermal conductivity (increasing thermal resistance) and may cause the PCB to sit too high, affecting optical alignment or mechanical fit. Using a pad that is too thin may not fill the gaps, leading to air pockets. Always follow the manufacturer's recommended thickness for the specific application.

Mechanical Damage During Handling

Thermal pads can tear, fold, or become contaminated during installation if handled roughly. This can create voids or uneven surfaces, reducing effectiveness. Handle pads with clean hands or gloves and cut them to the correct size using a sharp knife or die‑cut tool.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

LED Module Running Hotter Than Spec / Premature Failure
Likely Cause
The thermal pad has hardened or degraded over time due to sustained high temperature or chemical exposure, raising thermal resistance across the interface.
Diagnostic Steps
Measure junction or case temperature against the datasheet limit; inspect the pad for hardening, shrinkage, or discolouration when the assembly is opened.
Corrective Action
Replace the degraded pad with a new unit of the specified material and thickness, and re-torque the assembly evenly before returning it to service.
Hot Spots or Uneven Heat Distribution on the Heat Sink
Likely Cause
Mounting screws were not torqued evenly, leaving parts of the pad under-compressed and unable to fill the interface gap fully.
Diagnostic Steps
Use a thermal camera or contact thermometer to map the heat sink surface for localized hot spots; check torque values at each mounting point against specification.
Corrective Action
Re-torque all mounting points evenly to the specified value, replacing the pad if it has already been permanently deformed by uneven compression.
Pad Found Torn, Extruded, or Contaminated on Inspection
Likely Cause
Rough handling during installation, or debris on the mating surfaces before assembly, has damaged the pad or prevented proper adhesion and compression.
Diagnostic Steps
Disassemble and visually inspect the pad and both mating surfaces for tears, extrusion at the edges, or trapped debris.
Corrective Action
Clean both surfaces with isopropyl alcohol and a lint-free cloth, and install a new pad handled per the manufacturer's guidance, cut to size with a sharp tool.
PCB Sits Proud or Optical Alignment Is Off After Assembly
Likely Cause
An incorrect pad thickness for the application has changed the stack height, pushing the PCB out of its intended mechanical or optical position.
Diagnostic Steps
Measure stack height against the design drawing; confirm the pad thickness in use matches the manufacturer's recommendation for this specific fixture.
Corrective Action
Replace with the correct thickness pad specified for the application and re-check optical alignment or mechanical fit before final assembly.

Failure Modes & Root Causes

Pad Compression Set / Thermal Interface Degradation
High Severity
Root Cause
Sustained compressive load and thermal cycling over years of service gradually reducing the pad's ability to fill the interface gap.
Effect
LED module running hotter than spec, accelerating lumen depreciation and shortening the module's service life.
Detection Method
Thermal imaging of the LED module under load, and comparison against the baseline temperature recorded at commissioning.
Uneven Mounting Pressure
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Uneven torque across mounting fasteners, or a warped heat sink surface that prevents uniform contact with the pad.
Effect
Hot spots or uneven heat distribution on the heat sink, concentrating thermal stress at specific points on the module.
Detection Method
Thermal imaging survey across the heat sink surface, and torque-pattern verification during reassembly.
Pad Tearing, Extrusion, or Contamination
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Excessive compression during assembly, handling damage before installation, or debris trapped at the interface during fitting.
Effect
Reduced or compromised thermal transfer at the point of damage, creating a localised hot spot on the module.
Detection Method
Visual inspection of the pad during any planned disassembly, checking for tearing, extrusion beyond the interface edge, or embedded debris.

Low Severity
Root Cause
A pad thickness that does not match the specified gap for the fixture, or a replacement pad substituted from a different product line.
Effect
PCB sits proud or optical alignment is off after assembly, affecting fixture fit or beam pattern.
Detection Method
Dimensional check of pad thickness against the fixture's specified gap before installation.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Circuit Isolation Before Fixture Disassembly
Electrical Safety

De-energise and lock out the associated circuit before opening a fixture to access or replace a thermal pad, and verify zero energy before touching internal components.

Handle by the Edges
Personnel Safety

Handle thermal pads by their edges and avoid touching the working surface with bare fingers where possible, since skin oils can reduce the pad's contact quality and long-term adhesion.

Correct Pad Selection on Replacement
Compliance

Fit a replacement pad of the correct thickness, thermal conductivity, and dielectric rating for the position; a pad that is too thin or too soft can be compressed out of tolerance, while an incorrect dielectric rating can compromise electrical isolation.

Hot Surface Contact
Personnel Safety

Allow the LED PCB, driver, or heat sink to cool before removing or replacing an adjacent thermal pad after the fixture has been active, since these surfaces can retain heat well after power-off.

Correct Mounting Pressure
Mechanical

Apply the manufacturer's specified mounting torque or clamping pressure when reassembling the fixture; under-compression leaves air gaps that degrade heat transfer, while over-compression can extrude or damage the pad.

FOD Control During Reassembly
Operational

Keep fasteners, backing films, and cut-offs from trimming a pad to size accounted for at all times, and remove all loose material from the fixture before closing it, to prevent foreign object debris on the movement area.

Insulation Check Before Re-Energisation
Electrical Safety

Verify insulation resistance between the powered component and the housing after reassembly, particularly where the thermal pad's dielectric function is relied on for electrical isolation from a metallic heat sink.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Do not close up a fixture with a torn, misaligned, or omitted thermal pad; verify correct fit and full surface contact before returning the fixture to service.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Don't Assume "Thicker Is Safer"
Design

A thicker pad feels like a safer bet for gap-filling, but thermal resistance rises with thickness for a given material, so oversizing to "be sure" quietly raises junction temperature. Specify the thinnest pad that reliably bridges the actual gap tolerance stack-up, not a generic one-size default.

Compression Set Is the Silent Failure Mode
Durability

Many pads lose a measurable fraction of their original thickness after repeated thermal cycling, even without visible damage. A fixture that tested fine at commissioning can develop a real air gap years later purely from compression set, so build re-torque or pad-condition checks into the overhaul interval rather than the fault-response cycle.

Dielectric Rating and Thermal Rating Trade Off
Electrical

Pushing for the highest available thermal conductivity can mean accepting a lower breakdown voltage in some pad chemistries. Where the pad is doing double duty as the electrical isolation layer between an LED driver and a metallic heat sink, don't let a thermal-performance upgrade get specified without re-checking the dielectric margin.

Match Pad Hardness to Surface Flatness
Installation

A softer, more conformable pad compensates for surface irregularity on cast or machined heat sinks, while a harder pad needs genuinely flat, well-matched mating surfaces to avoid voids. Selecting purely on conductivity spec while ignoring durometer is a common cause of inconsistent thermal performance across otherwise identical fixtures.

Rising LED Drive Current Is a Clue, Not Just a Symptom
Troubleshooting

Constant-current drivers compensate for a degrading LED by holding output steady even as thermal resistance climbs, which can mask a failing pad until the LED itself shifts colour or drops output. Trend driver case temperature and photometric output together rather than relying on drive current alone to catch pad degradation early.

Reuse Is Rarely a Good Idea
Maintenance

A pad removed intact during disassembly is tempting to reinstall, but the compression and thermal cycling it has already undergone mean its as-new performance is gone. Treat thermal pads as a routine consumable at every fixture opening rather than trying to stretch service life through reuse.

Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about thermal pads: function, specifications, standards, installation and common problems.

Q1What is a thermal pad in an AGL fixture?

A thermal pad, also called a thermal interface pad or gap pad, is a pre-formed, soft, thermally conductive and electrically insulating material placed between a heat-generating component and a heat sink or fixture housing. It fills microscopic air gaps that would otherwise trap air and act as thermal insulators.

Q2What does a thermal pad do?

It provides thermal conduction, electrical insulation between the component and a metallic heat sink, gap filling, vibration damping and even pressure distribution to protect fragile parts such as LED PCBs.

Q3Why use a thermal pad instead of thermal grease?

Thermal pads are clean, easy to apply, pre-formed to the required shape, and maintain consistent performance over time without drying out or migrating, which is why they are preferred in many AGL applications.

Q4Where are thermal pads used in AGL equipment?

Between LED PCBs (MCPCBs) and aluminium heat sinks, between LED driver power components and the metal housing, in CCR power components, power supplies and rectifiers, control electronics, ALCMS servers and networking equipment, and in PAPI and approach light fixtures. They are also used in retrofits and repairs.

Q5How does a thermal pad work?

It is a silicone, acrylic or urethane matrix filled with thermally conductive particles such as aluminium oxide, boron nitride or graphite. When compressed by mounting screws it deforms to displace air and maximise contact area. Thermal resistance depends on applied pressure, so the recommended mounting pressure matters.

Q6What are typical thermal pad specifications?

Thermal conductivity is typically 1.0 to 6.0 W/m·K (up to 12 W/m·K for high-end pads), thickness 0.5 mm to 5.0 mm, and dielectric strength at least 3 kV/mm (typically 5 to 10 kV/mm). Operating range is -40°C to +150°C, and flammability rating is UL 94 V-0.

Q7Which standards apply to thermal pads?

The main references are UL 94 (flammability), ASTM D5470 (thermal conductivity of interface materials), IEC 60243-1 (dielectric breakdown strength) and ASTM E595 (outgassing). Always verify the current edition.

Q8When should a thermal pad be inspected or replaced?

Inspect it at each service or replacement for hardening, cracking or other degradation. At installation, verify even compression and full contact, clean mating surfaces, and confirm the pad thickness matches the specified gap.

Q9What are the common thermal pad problems?

The common issues are degradation and hardening from heat, UV or chemical attack, insufficient or uneven compression from incorrect screw torque, contamination by dust or grease, wrong thickness (a pad that is too thick raises thermal resistance and can affect optical alignment), and tearing or folding during handling.

Q10What precautions apply when replacing a thermal pad?

De-energise and lock out the circuit, let hot parts cool, handle the pad by its edges, and fit the correct thickness, conductivity and dielectric rating. Reassemble at the manufacturer's specified torque, keep backing films and offcuts accounted for, and check insulation resistance between the component and housing before re-energising.
