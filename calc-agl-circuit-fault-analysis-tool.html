AGL Circuit Fault Analysis

Ranks the likely causes of a series circuit fault from the symptoms and readings, and shows where to look first.

About this calculator

Enter what was seen at the CCR and in the field, plus any loop resistance and insulation readings. The tool checks the readings against the expected values, weighs all the evidence, ranks the likely causes, and suggests what to check next. It also estimates the distance to a short and plans a half-split search of the circuit.

Safety first. A series circuit can carry lethal current even when it looks dead. Isolate, lock out and prove the circuit dead before any resistance or insulation test, and follow your site procedures.

Method & Reference

How the analysis works

Each step is shown so the result can be followed and challenged.

1. Loop resistance check

The expected loop resistance is the cable resistance at the conductor temperature, plus any other series resistance you enter. The reading is compared with it.

R_exp = ρ₂₀ × [1 + 0.00393 × (T − 20)] × L ÷ 1000 + R_other

Open circuit means no continuity. Above the tolerance reads as high, below it as low, otherwise normal.

2. Insulation check

The reading is compared with your minimum. Below the minimum is a fail, up to twice the minimum is a warning, and above that is acceptable. A fall from the previous reading beyond your threshold is flagged as a trend.

3. Weighing the evidence

Each answer or reading adds weight to the causes it points to, and some answers take weight away. Negative totals are set to zero. A cause's share is its weight divided by the sum of all weights.

Share = score ÷ Σ scores × 100

Shares show how the evidence divides, not a probability. If the top share is under 30 %, the evidence is mixed and more readings are needed.

4. Distance to a short

For a short between the two conductors of a loop, the resistance seen from the CCR end runs out along one conductor and back along the other.

d = R_meas ÷ (2 × ρ₂₀ × k_T ÷ 1000)

This assumes both conductors are the same size and ignores transformer primaries between the CCR and the fault, so treat it as a starting point.

5. Half-split search

tests = ⌈log₂ n⌉

Each isolation and test halves the suspect range, so 100 fixtures need at most 7 tests.

What this tool does not do

It does not replace fault-finding procedures, manufacturer guidance or the judgement of a qualified AGL technician. It cannot see faults the inputs do not describe, and it does not model transformer secondaries, reactance or cable capacitance. Weights and thresholds are engineering rules of thumb, and the minimum insulation value and tolerances are yours to set.

FAQ

AGL Circuit Fault Analysis Tool: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on how likely series circuit causes are ranked and how the fault is located.

What does the AGL Circuit Fault Analysis Tool do?

It ranks the likely causes of a series circuit fault from the symptoms you observe and the readings you enter, and shows where to look first. It checks the loop resistance and insulation readings against the expected values, estimates the distance to a short, and plans a half-split search of the circuit. It also produces a PDF report.

What safety precautions does the tool state?

A series circuit can carry lethal current even when it looks dead. Isolate, lock out and prove the circuit dead before any resistance or insulation test, and follow your site procedures.

How is the expected loop resistance checked?

Rexp = ρ20 × [1 + 0.00393 × (T − 20)] × L ÷ 1000 + Rother, where Rother covers isolating transformer primaries and joints in the loop. The reading is compared with it. An open circuit means no continuity, above the tolerance reads as high, below it reads as low, and otherwise it is normal. For example, #8 AWG over a 3,000 m loop at 20 °C gives an expected 6.183 Ω.

How is the insulation resistance reading assessed?

The reading is compared with your minimum. Below the minimum is a fail, up to twice the minimum is a warning, and above that is acceptable. A fall from the previous reading beyond your threshold is flagged as a trend. The minimum acceptable value is your own rule, and 50 MΩ is only a placeholder.

How are the likely causes ranked, and is it a probability?

Each answer or reading adds weight to the causes it points to, and some answers take weight away, with negative totals set to zero. A cause's share is Share = score ÷ Σ scores × 100. The shares show how the evidence divides, not a probability. If the top share is under 30 %, the evidence is mixed and more readings are needed.

How is the distance to a short estimated?

For a short between the two conductors of a loop, the resistance seen from the CCR end runs out along one conductor and back along the other, so d = Rmeas ÷ (2 × ρ20 × kT ÷ 1000). It assumes both conductors are the same size and ignores transformer primaries between the CCR and the fault, so treat it as a starting point. In the worked example, a reading of 0.9 Ω gives a short about 218 m away.

What is the half-split planner?

It splits the suspect stretch of the circuit in half at each test, so the faulty section is found in the fewest isolations. The number of tests is ⌈log2 n⌉, so 100 fixtures need at most 7 tests. Number the fixtures along the loop from the CCR. The planner does not depend on the symptoms or readings.

Is the result a diagnosis?

No. The ranking is a rule-based weighing of the evidence you enter, not a diagnosis, and findings must be confirmed by testing on site. The tool does not replace fault-finding procedures, manufacturer guidance or the judgement of a qualified AGL technician. It cannot see faults the inputs do not describe, and it does not model transformer secondaries, reactance or cable capacitance.
