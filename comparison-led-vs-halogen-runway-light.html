Runway Light Source Technology Comparison

LED VS HALOGEN Runway Light

LED runway lights use semiconductor light sources, while halogen runway lights use tungsten-halogen filament lamps. Both can provide the required runway visual guidance, but they differ in energy consumption, service life, maintenance, thermal behaviour, and electrical design.

02 · Basic Operating Principle

How Each Source Generates Light

LED Runway Light

Electrical supply → driver/electronics → LED → optics → runway light beam. Light is produced directly by semiconductor electroluminescence.

Halogen Runway Light

Electrical supply → halogen filament → heat + light → reflector/optics → runway light beam. Light is produced by heating a filament until it glows.

Easy memory: LED = semiconductor → optics → runway beam. Halogen = hot filament → reflector/optics → runway beam.

03 · Energy Consumption

Power Demand

LED

Generally offers lower energy consumption for a comparable runway-lighting function.

Halogen

Generally higher power demand, since electrical energy is used to both heat the filament and produce light.

However, the actual difference depends on the specific fixture design and required photometric performance.

04 · Service Life

Longevity & Degradation

LED

Generally have a longer operating life, with gradual light-output depreciation over time — although electronic or LED-module failures can still occur.

Halogen

Generally require more frequent replacement because the filament has a limited operating life: filament ageing leads to filament failure and lamp replacement.

05 · Maintenance

Typical Maintenance Activities

LED Runway Light

  • Cleaning optical surfaces
  • Checking fixture condition
  • Monitoring light output
  • Inspecting electronics
  • Checking thermal-management components

Halogen Runway Light

  • Cleaning lenses and reflectors
  • Checking fixture condition
  • Replacing lamps
  • Inspecting lamp holders and connections
  • Checking optical alignment

Main difference: LED involves less routine light-source replacement; halogen requires periodic lamp replacement.

06 · Photometric Performance

Technology Doesn't Decide Compliance

Both LED and halogen runway lights must ultimately provide the required optical performance, including luminous intensity, horizontal and vertical distribution, beam coverage, and colour characteristics.

LED technology does not automatically mean better photometric performance than halogen technology. The correct comparison is between the measured performance of the complete runway-light fixture.

07 · Optical Design

Directing the Light

LED

LED → secondary optics → controlled beam. LEDs can be arranged with lenses and optical components to produce the required runway-light beam.

Halogen

Halogen lamp → reflector → lens/optics → controlled beam. Halogen sources emit light more broadly, so reflectors and optics play an important role in collecting and directing the light.

08 · Heat Management

Thermal Behaviour

Halogen

Electrical power → hot filament → heat + light. Halogen runway lights can generate substantial heat.

LED

Electrical power → LED → light + heat → thermal management. LED fixtures are more efficient, but LED does not mean heat-free.

Poor thermal management can reduce LED light output, service life, reliability, and colour stability.

An LED replacement should not automatically be treated as a simple lamp substitution. System compatibility may need consideration for CCR operation, isolation transformer rating, operating current, power characteristics, monitoring systems, open/failed-lamp detection, and mechanical installation.

11 · Retrofit Considerations

A successful conversion requires more than simply installing an LED source. The complete fixture and system should be evaluated for electrical compatibility, optical performance, and mechanical compatibility.

12 · Runway-Light Applications

Where Each Technology Is Used

Both technologies can potentially be used for applications such as runway edge lights, runway centreline lights, runway threshold/end lights, touchdown-zone lights, and other specified runway visual aids.

The technology choice does not change the operational purpose of the light — required runway function → LED or halogen fixture → required optical output.

14 · Engineering Verdict

LED vs Halogen Runway Light

Engineering Verdict

LED and halogen runway lights differ mainly in source technology, not in required outcome. Both must meet the same photometric, colour, electrical, mechanical, and operational requirements for their specific runway-lighting function.

LED runway lights generally offer lower energy use, longer service life, and reduced routine source maintenance, with greater dependence on electronics and thermal management. Halogen runway lights use filament-based technology with higher power consumption, higher heat generation, and more frequent lamp replacement.

LEDSemiconductor source + lower power + longer life + electronics/thermal management dependent.
HalogenFilament source + higher power + shorter life + periodic lamp replacement.

The most important point is that the runway-light technology alone does not determine compliance. Both LED and halogen fixtures must meet the applicable photometric, colour, electrical, mechanical, and operational requirements for their specific runway-lighting function.

15 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the simplest way to remember the difference?

LED = semiconductor → optics → runway beam, with efficient, long life and lower maintenance. Halogen = hot filament → reflector/optics → runway beam, with higher heat, higher power, and lamp replacement.

Does LED always mean better photometric performance?

No. LED technology does not automatically mean better photometric performance than halogen technology. The correct comparison is between the measured performance of the complete runway-light fixture.

Can I simply swap a halogen lamp for an LED module?

Not automatically. An LED replacement should not be treated as a simple lamp substitution — CCR operation, isolation transformer rating, operating current, monitoring systems, and mechanical installation may all need evaluation.

Do LEDs generate no heat at all?

No. LED fixtures are more efficient, but LED does not mean heat-free. Poor thermal management can reduce LED light output, service life, reliability, and colour stability.

Why do halogen runway lights need more frequent maintenance?

Because the filament has a limited operating life. Filament ageing leads to filament failure, requiring periodic lamp replacement, which is generally less frequent with LED sources.

Are both technologies used for the same runway-light applications?

Yes. Both LED and halogen can potentially be used for runway edge lights, centreline lights, threshold/end lights, and touchdown-zone lights. The technology choice does not change the operational purpose of the light.

16 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

FAA AC 150/5340-30

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids — photometric, electrical, and installation requirements applicable to both LED and halogen runway-light fixtures.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — photometric and colour characteristics of aeronautical ground lights, regardless of light-source technology.

FAA AC 150/5345-46

Specification for Runway, Taxiway, Heliport, and Vertiport Light Fixtures — the governing equipment spec for LED and halogen runway-light fixture construction.

FAA AC 150/5345 Series

Specifications for constant current regulators, isolation transformers, and other airfield lighting equipment relevant to LED retrofit compatibility.

Final Engineering Note

Do not select a runway-light source technology based on efficiency claims alone. Verify complete fixture compliance with the applicable photometric, electrical, and mechanical requirements.

LED and halogen are two different ways to produce the same required runway-light function — the fixture, not the source technology, must demonstrate compliance.
