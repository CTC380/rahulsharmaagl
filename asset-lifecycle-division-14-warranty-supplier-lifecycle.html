WAR-01 Warranty Registration
Purpose

Every warranty-bearing AGL asset should be formally registered before it can be considered protected.

Warranty Record Should Identify
  • Asset ID
  • Equipment/system
  • Manufacturer
  • Supplier
  • Model
  • Serial number
  • Purchase reference, purchase date
  • Installation date, commissioning date
  • Warranty start date, duration, expiry date
  • Warranty conditions, warranty documents
  • Contact details
  • Applicable contract/PO
Principle

An asset should not be considered warranty-controlled until its warranty information is captured in the asset-management system.

WAR-02 Warranty Start Date
Purpose

Warranty commencement must be clearly defined. The applicable contractual definition should always govern.

Possible Contractual Triggers
Date of DeliveryDate of InstallationDate of CommissioningDate of AcceptanceDate of Handover
Important

Do not automatically assume Installation Date = Warranty Start Date or Commissioning Date = Warranty Start Date. The actual contract or purchase terms should always be checked.

WAR-03 Warranty Period Tracking
Purpose

Warranty periods should be actively monitored rather than left as a static entry in a register.

Track
  • Start date, duration, expiry date
  • Remaining warranty period
  • Warranty conditions
  • Required maintenance conditions
  • Inspection requirements
  • Claim deadlines where applicable
Suggested Status Flow
Not StartedActiveExpiring SoonExpiredPost-Warranty Support
WAR-04 Warranty Claims
Purpose

A warranty claim should be treated as a controlled lifecycle event, not an informal exchange of emails.

Claim Record
  1. Claim ID
  2. Asset ID
  3. Failure date
  4. Failure description
  5. Failure mode
  6. Initial evidence
  7. Photographs
  8. Test results
  9. Warranty reference
  10. Supplier notification date
  11. Supplier response
  12. Claim decision
  13. Replacement/repair
  14. Cost recovered
  15. Closure date
Principle

Every potentially warranty-covered failure should be screened for warranty eligibility before the airport incurs unnecessary repair or replacement expenditure.

WAR-05 Warranty Failure Analysis
Purpose

Warranty failures should feed into the AGL Failure Database rather than being closed out and forgotten.

Analyze
  • Equipment type, manufacturer, model, serial number
  • Failure mode, failure cause, failure age, failure frequency
  • Location, operating conditions
  • Corrective action
Why It Matters

This allows the airport to identify whether warranty failures are isolated incidents or part of a wider supplier/product trend — the link into Division 11 (Reliability Engineering & Failure Management).

WAR-06 Supplier Response Tracking
Purpose

Supplier responsiveness should be measured, not assumed.

Track
  • Claim notification date, supplier acknowledgement
  • Response time, site visit date
  • Technical response
  • Replacement/repair authorization
  • Final resolution, closure date
KPI Examples
  • Average response time
  • Average resolution time
  • Open claims / overdue claims
  • Repeat claims
  • Supplier SLA compliance

This transforms warranty management from correspondence tracking into supplier-performance management.

WAR-07 Manufacturer Support
Purpose

Manufacturer support should be evaluated across the full asset lifecycle, not only during the warranty period.

Consider
  • Technical support, troubleshooting, engineering assistance
  • Site support, training, documentation
  • Software/firmware support
  • Spare availability, obsolescence support
  • Emergency support
Lifecycle Question

Can the manufacturer continue to support the equipment throughout its expected service life?

WAR-08 Replacement Under Warranty
Purpose

Where an asset/component is accepted as a warranty failure, replacement should be controlled and fully traceable.

Record
  • Failed component, failure reason
  • Warranty decision
  • Replacement part, replacement serial number
  • Replacement date, installation date
  • Testing, commissioning/return-to-service
  • Updated warranty status
Important

The replacement asset should be properly linked to the original asset record — the lifecycle history must not be broken.

WAR-09 Repair Under Warranty
Purpose

Warranty resolution may involve repair rather than replacement, and this must be tracked with equal rigor.

Track
  • Failed component, supplier diagnosis
  • Repair scope, repair date
  • Parts replaced
  • Test results, return date
  • Reinstallation, functional verification

The repair history should remain attached to the asset's lifecycle record.

WAR-10 Warranty Cost Recovery
Purpose

Warranty management should identify the full financial recovery available under the contract, not just a free part.

Potential Recovery
  • Replacement equipment/components
  • Repair cost, labour
  • Freight, shipping
  • Inspection, testing
  • Contractor costs
  • Other contractually recoverable expenses
Principle

A warranty claim that results in a free replacement may still involve significant unrecovered costs. Warranty recovery should be assessed against the full contractually recoverable cost, not only the equipment invoice value.

WAR-11 Warranty Performance Analysis
Purpose

Warranty data should be analyzed periodically rather than treated as a closed transaction after each claim.

Analyze
  • Number of warranty claims
  • Claims by manufacturer, model, component, project
  • Failure rate, repeat failures
  • Average resolution time
  • Cost recovered
  • Open claims, rejected claims
Example

If one product has substantially more warranty failures than comparable products, this should trigger a supplier/product review.

WAR-12 Supplier Reliability
Purpose

Supplier reliability should consider both the performance of the supplied product and the supplier's ability to support it.

Evaluate
  • Product failure rate, warranty claim rate, repeat failures
  • Technical support, response time, resolution time
  • Spare availability, documentation quality
  • Corrective-action effectiveness
  • Delivery reliability
Feeds Back Into

This information should feed back into Division 05 — Vendor Evaluation for future procurement decisions.

WAR-13 Supplier Failure Trends
Purpose

Supplier-level trends should be monitored over time rather than judged from a single incident.

Example — Manufacturer A
Year 1 — Low FailuresYear 2 — StableYear 3 — IncreasingYear 4 — Significant IncreaseSupplier Engineering Review
The Review Should Determine Whether the Trend Is Associated With
  • Product revision
  • Manufacturing issue
  • Installation
  • Environment / operating conditions
  • Maintenance
  • Design
Principle

Supplier trends should be evidence-based rather than automatically attributed to the manufacturer.

WAR-14 Vendor Corrective Action
Purpose

When a supplier-related problem is identified, the supplier may be required to implement corrective action.

Corrective Action May Include
  • Product modification, design change, manufacturing correction
  • Firmware update
  • Installation correction
  • Technical bulletin
  • Replacement programme
  • Additional inspection/testing
  • Training, revised documentation
Track
ProblemSupplier RCACorrective ActionImplementationVerificationClosure
WAR-15 Warranty Closure
Purpose

A warranty claim should only be closed after the required resolution has been verified.

Closure Requirements
  • Corrective action completed
  • Replacement/repair completed
  • Testing completed, asset restored
  • Documentation updated
  • Cost recovery completed
  • Supplier response recorded
  • Root cause recorded where required
  • Lessons learned captured
Principle

Administrative closure is not the same as technical closure.

WAR-16 Post-Warranty Support
Purpose

Warranty expiry does not necessarily mean supplier support ends — this should be assessed deliberately.

Assess Post-Warranty Support For
  • Spare parts
  • Technical support, repairs
  • Software, firmware
  • Calibration, specialist service
  • Training, documentation
  • Obsolescence
Key Question

Can the asset continue to be supported economically and technically after warranty expiry?

WAR-17 Extended Warranty Assessment
Purpose

An extended warranty should not automatically be purchased simply because it is offered by the supplier. It should be evaluated as a lifecycle risk and cost decision.

Evaluate

Cost of extended warranty vs. expected post-warranty failure cost vs. failure consequence vs. supplier reliability vs. asset criticality vs. remaining useful life vs. availability of spare parts vs. post-warranty service capability.

Extended Warranty Decision Model
  1. Asset Criticality — is the asset safety/operationally critical?
  2. Reliability — what is the actual failure history?
  3. Health — what is the current asset condition?
  4. RUL — how much useful life is expected after standard warranty expiry?
  5. Failure Cost — what would a failure cost?
  6. Extended Warranty Cost — what does the additional warranty cost?
  7. Coverage — what exactly does the extended warranty cover?
  8. Exclusions — what is excluded?
  9. Supplier Capability — can the supplier actually provide timely support?
  10. Decision — Purchase / Do Not Purchase / Negotiate Alternative Support
Strategic Principle

An extended warranty becomes more attractive when criticality is high, failure consequence is high, post-warranty support is difficult, spare lead time is long, supplier reliability is acceptable, and the warranty cost is economically justified.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Warranty & Supplier Lifecycle Management in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide.

Q1What is Division 14 — Warranty & Supplier Lifecycle Management in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

Division 14 is the commercial-protection and supplier-performance layer. It covers 17 topics on warranty registration, claims, supplier response tracking, cost recovery, supplier reliability trends and extended warranty assessment.

Q2What is warranty registration?

Every warranty-bearing AGL asset should be formally registered before it can be considered protected. The record links the asset ID, equipment, manufacturer, supplier, model and serial number.

Q3How is the warranty start date defined and tracked?

Warranty commencement must be clearly defined, and the applicable contractual definition should always govern. Warranty periods should be actively monitored, including remaining warranty period and any required maintenance conditions, rather than left as a static register entry.

Q4How should warranty claims be managed?

A warranty claim should be treated as a controlled lifecycle event, not an informal exchange of emails, with a claim ID, asset ID, failure date, description, failure mode and initial evidence. Warranty failures should feed into the AGL Failure Database rather than being closed out and forgotten.

Q5Why track supplier response?

Supplier responsiveness should be measured, not assumed. Tracking covers claim notification date, acknowledgement, response time, site visit date and technical response.

Q6What is involved in repair or replacement under warranty, and cost recovery?

Both replacement and repair under warranty must be controlled and fully traceable, recording the failed component, the warranty decision, and the replacement or repair details and test results. Cost recovery should identify the full financial recovery available under the contract, not just a free part, including labour, freight and testing where covered.

Q7How are supplier reliability and corrective action handled?

Supplier reliability considers both product performance, such as failure and claim rates, and the supplier's ability to support it. Trends are monitored over time rather than judged from a single incident, and the supplier may be required to implement corrective action such as a design change, firmware update or installation correction.

Q8What happens at warranty closure and after warranty expiry?

A warranty claim should be closed only after the required resolution has been verified. Warranty expiry does not necessarily mean supplier support ends, so post-warranty support for spares, service and software should be assessed deliberately. An extended warranty should not be bought automatically but evaluated as a lifecycle risk and cost decision.
