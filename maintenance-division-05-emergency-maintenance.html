Emergency Maintenance

The rapid-response division of the handbook. Emergency maintenance addresses unexpected AGL failures, damage, or hazardous conditions that require immediate engineering intervention — protecting aircraft operations and personnel first, and restoring critical lighting capability as quickly as safely practicable.

This is not "fast corrective maintenance." Division 02 diagnoses a defect on a planned basis and repairs it. Division 05 responds to a critical event, protects operations first, restores capability — temporarily if necessary — and only afterward completes the permanent repair.

Division 02 — Corrective
Defect identified → planned diagnosis → repair/replacement → testing → restoration
Division 05 — Emergency
Critical event/failure → immediate risk assessment → rapid response → operational protection → emergency restoration → testing → return to service → permanent repair

When Emergency Maintenance Is Required

Emergency maintenance may be initiated when an event results in any of the following. Exact operational classification and response priority follow the airport's approved emergency procedures.

Runway circuit loss Taxiway circuit loss PAPI unserviceability CCR breakdown Major cable damage Lightning damage Flooded electrical infrastructure Multiple fixture failures Major sign failure Stop-bar failure Runway guard-light failure Approach-lighting failure Major earthing fault ALCMS / control failure Electrical fire Severe-weather damage Vehicle impact

Emergency Response Levels

A useful structure for classifying the scale of an emergency event before response resources are committed.

LEVEL 1

Local Equipment Emergency

A single critical component or piece of equipment is affected.

  • CCR component failure
  • PAPI unit failure
  • Critical sign failure
LEVEL 2

Circuit / System Emergency

A complete circuit or important AGL subsystem is affected.

  • Runway circuit failure
  • Taxiway circuit failure
  • Stop-bar system failure
LEVEL 3

Major Airfield Emergency

Widespread or infrastructure-level damage.

  • Major cable damage / flooding
  • Lightning event / severe-weather damage
  • Electrical fire / multiple simultaneous failures

Emergency Maintenance Priority

A useful response classification for triaging emergency events. Exact airport response times are defined by approved emergency and operational procedures.

Master Emergency Flow

Every topic in this division ultimately feeds this master sequence — the same twelve-step arc regardless of the triggering event.

1
Emergency Detected

Failure, damage, or hazardous condition identified.

2
Immediate Notification

Responsible operational personnel alerted without delay.

3
Impact Assessment

Operational and personnel safety impact evaluated.

4
Isolation / Protection

Equipment/circuit identified and isolated or protected.

5
Rapid Fault Diagnosis

Fault located and characterised as fast as safely possible.

6
Decision

Can the system be safely restored immediately?

7
Repair or Temporary Restore

Emergency repair, or temporary restoration with operational control.

8
Test

Confirm the restoration is safe and functional.

9
Return to Service

Capability restored under controlled conditions.

10
Post-Emergency Inspection

Check for latent or secondary damage.

11
Permanent Repair Planning

Close-out scope, materials, manpower and target date.

12
Close Event / Trend Review

Root-cause and repeat-emergency review.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about emergency maintenance, based on the topics in this division.

9 QUESTIONS
Q1What is emergency maintenance in AGL?

Emergency maintenance addresses unexpected AGL failures, damage or hazardous conditions that need immediate engineering intervention. It protects aircraft operations and personnel first and restores critical lighting capability as quickly as safely practicable.

Q2What does Division 05 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 05 (Emergency Maintenance) contains 30 topics in three groups: Group A Critical AGL Failures (8 topics), Group B Additional Emergency Scenarios (16 topics) and Group C Emergency Documentation (6 topics).

Q3Is emergency maintenance just fast corrective maintenance?

No. Corrective maintenance diagnoses a defect on a planned basis and then repairs it. Emergency maintenance responds to a critical event, protects operations first, restores capability, temporarily if necessary, and only afterwards completes the permanent repair.

Q4What is the first priority in an AGL emergency?

The first priority is not repair. It is safety and operational risk control: protect aircraft operations, protect personnel and prevent escalation of the failure before any restoration work begins.

Q5What is the emergency maintenance sequence?

Detect, Notify, Assess, Protect, Isolate, Diagnose, Restore, Test, Return to Service, Document and Permanently Repair.

Q6What events can trigger emergency maintenance?

Examples include runway or taxiway circuit loss, PAPI unserviceability, CCR breakdown, major cable damage, lightning damage, flooded electrical infrastructure, multiple fixture failures, major sign failure, stop-bar or runway guard-light failure, approach-lighting failure, a major earthing fault, ALCMS or control failure, electrical fire, severe-weather damage and vehicle impact. Exact classification follows the airport's approved emergency procedures.

Q7What are the three emergency response levels?

Level 1 is a local equipment emergency affecting a single critical component or piece of equipment. Level 2 is a circuit or system emergency affecting a complete circuit or important AGL subsystem. Level 3 is a major airfield emergency involving widespread or infrastructure-level damage.

Q8How are emergency events prioritised?

E1 Critical (immediate safety or operational significance) gets an immediate response, E2 Major (significant AGL degradation) an urgent response, E3 Serious (important but limited impact) a priority response, and E4 Controlled (no immediate operational impact) planned emergency follow-up. Exact response times are defined by the airport's approved emergency and operational procedures.

Q9What happens after service is restored?

After return to service the master emergency flow continues with a post-emergency inspection for latent or secondary damage, permanent repair planning covering scope, materials, manpower and target date, and closing the event with a root-cause and repeat-emergency review.
