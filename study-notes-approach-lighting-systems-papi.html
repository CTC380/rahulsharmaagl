APPROACH LIGHTING SYSTEMS & PRECISION APPROACH AIDS

Approach lighting systems provide visual information to help pilots transition from instrument conditions toward visual references for landing. They extend outward from the runway threshold along the extended runway centreline, on the approach side (the side the aircraft arrives from). FAA describes ALS as providing information on runway alignment, height perception, roll guidance and horizontal references.

Why It Matters

WHY APPROACH LIGHTING IS IMPORTANT

Approach lighting helps the pilot establish visual reference with the runway environment. Important information can include:

  • Runway alignment
  • Longitudinal perspective
  • Roll (bank) reference from the crossbars
  • Threshold identification
  • Lateral reference
  • Transition from instrument to visual conditions
Remember: approach lights give alignment, roll, distance-to-go and horizontal cues. Reliable glide-slope guidance comes from PAPI (or VASI), not from the approach lights.
FAA Terms

MAIN FAA APPROACH LIGHTING TERMS

ALSF-1

Approach Lighting System with Sequenced Flashing Lights, Category I configuration. A high-intensity approach lighting configuration in FAA terminology, associated with Category I approaches. Typical length 2,400–3,000 ft.

ALSF-2

Approach Lighting System with Sequenced Flashing Lights, Category II configuration. FAA describes it as a high-intensity system supporting Category II/III instrument approaches. Typical length 2,400–3,000 ft.

SSALR

Simplified Short Approach Lighting System with Runway Alignment Indicator Lights. A simplified arrangement with runway alignment indication. FAA describes it as medium intensity and associated with Category I approaches; at some runways the ALSF-2 can also operate in an SSALR mode. Typical length 1,400 ft.

MALSR

Medium Intensity Approach Lighting System with Runway Alignment Indicator Lights. Installed in runway approach zones along the extended centreline; combines steady-burning light bars with sequenced flashers (RAIL). Associated with Category I approaches; typical length 1,400 ft.

Important exam point. Do not memorise ALS names without understanding the structure:
ALSF = Approach Lighting System with Sequenced Flashing Lights
MALSR = Medium-intensity system + runway alignment indicator lights
SSALR = Simplified Short approach system + runway alignment indicator lights
RAIL = Runway Alignment Indicator Lights (sequenced flashers on the extended centreline)

Other FAA systems

  • MALS: medium-intensity approach lighting system. MALSF adds sequenced flashers; MALSR adds runway alignment indicator lights (RAIL).
  • SALS: short approach lighting system, a short-length system for runways that do not need a full precision approach lighting system.
  • ODALS: omnidirectional approach lighting system, omnidirectional sequenced flashing lights, typically for non-precision runways.

Indian airport example

Current Indian AIP publications show that aerodromes use different approach lighting configurations depending on the runway and operational characteristics. For example, current AAI eAIP data includes runway entries showing CAT I approach systems, CAT II approach systems, PAPI and touchdown-zone lighting at different aerodromes. Never assume every airport uses the same ALS configuration. In the AIP, look at the aerodrome section AD 2.14 (approach and runway lighting), which gives the type, length and intensity of approach lighting for each runway.

ICAO / India

ICAO ANNEX 14 APPROACH LIGHTING

Indian aerodromes follow ICAO-based provisions, so the FAA names above (ALSF, MALSR and so on) are not normally what you will see in an AAI AIP entry. ICAO describes approach lighting by category rather than by system name. The values below are the standard Annex 14 layouts; always confirm against the applicable AIP or airport specification.

ICAO systemTypical layout
Simple approach lightingCentreline row of lights extending not less than 420 m from the threshold wherever possible, with a crossbar at 300 m; for non-instrument and non-precision runways
Precision approach CAT ICentreline barrettes extending 900 m from the threshold wherever possible, with a crossbar at 300 m; white lights. A shorter system may be unavoidable at some sites but can limit operating minima
Precision approach CAT II/IIICentreline 900 m wherever possible, crossbars at 300 m and 150 m, and red side-row barrettes over the inner 270 m

For CAT II/III the inner 300 m of approach lighting also has the shortest secondary-power switch-over (1 s under Annex 14 Table 8-1, check the current edition); see Note 17.

Related notes: 16 CAT I, II & III AGL · 18 ICAO Annex 14 for AGL · 19 FAA AC 150/5340 Series.

FAA vs ICAO. Do not answer an Indian-aerodrome question with FAA system names, and do not treat FAA lengths in feet as ICAO lengths in metres.
Vertical Guidance

PAPI: PRECISION APPROACH PATH INDICATOR

PAPI provides visual approach slope guidance. FAA describes it as normally consisting of two or four lights installed in a single row, normally to the left of the runway. The two-light version is called APAPI (Abbreviated PAPI). The common four-light interpretation:

Pilot seesMeaning
4 WhiteToo high
3 White + 1 RedSlightly high
2 White + 2 RedOn slope
1 White + 3 RedSlightly low
4 RedToo low

Try it: tap a position

Viewed from the approach: the left light is the unit farthest from the runway and the right light is the unit nearest the runway, so the first light to turn red when you drop below the slope is the one nearest the runway.

Easy memory
  • MORE WHITE = HIGH
  • MORE RED = LOW
  • 2 WHITE + 2 RED = ON PATH

Two-light APAPI

Pilot seesMeaning
2 WhiteAbove slope
1 White + 1 RedOn slope
2 RedBelow slope

APAPI gives only three indications, so it is less precise than a four-light PAPI; it is typically used where the aircraft mix does not justify a full PAPI. See Study Note 11: PAPI & APAPI for siting and aiming.

VASI

VASI: VISUAL APPROACH SLOPE INDICATOR

VASI also provides vertical approach slope guidance. FAA describes a common two-bar interpretation:

Pilot seesMeaning
White/WhiteAbove path
Red/WhiteOn path
Red/RedBelow path

Read the indication as far bar / near bar: red over white means on path. Three-bar VASI arrangements can provide two visual glide paths (a lower one for most aircraft and a higher one for aircraft with a high pilot eye height).

VASI is an FAA legacy system. It is not a standard ICAO visual approach slope indicator: ICAO Annex 14 specifies PAPI and APAPI (Annex 14 recommends that T-VASIS and AT-VASIS be discontinued as standard systems from 1 January 2020; the simple two-bar and three-bar VASIS were deleted from Annex 14 in 1995). For Indian-aerodrome or ICAO questions, answer with PAPI/APAPI.

Exam Questions

APPROACH LIGHTING REVISION QUESTIONS

What is the purpose of approach lighting?

To give pilots runway alignment, height perception and horizontal reference as they move from instrument to visual conditions.

What does ALSF-2 support?

Category II/III instrument approaches in FAA terminology (high-intensity system with sequenced flashers).

What does RAIL stand for?

Runway Alignment Indicator Lights: sequenced flashers along the extended centreline, used in MALSR and SSALR.

What does a pilot see on a four-light PAPI when on the glide slope?

Two white and two red lights.

What does a pilot see when slightly high on a PAPI?

Three white and one red.

What is the ICAO CAT I approach lighting length?

Centreline extending 900 m from the threshold, with a crossbar at 300 m.

What is the ICAO CAT II/III approach lighting layout?

Centreline of 900 m wherever possible, crossbars at 300 m and 150 m, and red side-row barrettes over the inner 270 m.

What does a two-light APAPI show when on the approach slope?

One white and one red light. Two white lights mean above the slope and two red lights mean below it.

Is VASI an ICAO standard visual approach slope indicator?

No. VASI is an FAA legacy system. ICAO Annex 14 specifies PAPI and APAPI; Annex 14 recommends that T-VASIS and AT-VASIS be discontinued as standard systems from 1 January 2020; the simple two-bar and three-bar VASIS were deleted from Annex 14 in 1995.

Why should FAA system names not be assumed for an Indian aerodrome?

Indian aerodromes follow ICAO-based provisions and AIP entries describe approach lighting by category, not by FAA system name.

What is a simple approach lighting system in ICAO terms?

A centreline row of lights extending at least 420 m from the threshold wherever possible, with a crossbar at 300 m, used for non-instrument and non-precision runways.

Key points for exams
  • PAPI = Precision Approach Path Indicator
  • PAPI common 4-light on-path indication = 2 White + 2 Red
  • More White = Above Path
  • More Red = Below Path
  • ALSF-2 = High-intensity approach lighting associated with CAT II/III FAA applications
  • MALSR = Medium-intensity approach system with runway alignment indicator lights
  • APAPI (2 lights): 1 White + 1 Red = on slope
  • VASI = FAA legacy; ICAO uses PAPI/APAPI

Know Which Cue Each Element Gives

Concept

Centreline, crossbars and flashers each give the pilot different information. Understand what is lost when one is missing.

Aim and Level Matter Along a Long Pattern

Alignment

Small errors repeated along a line are visible from the cockpit. Verify aiming at commissioning and after impacts.

Walk or Drive the Pattern at Night

Inspection

Dim, misaligned or missing units show up in darkness when they can be missed by day.

Do Not Mix ICAO and FAA Terms Without Care

Standards
