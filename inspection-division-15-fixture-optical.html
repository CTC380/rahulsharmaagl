Division 15 of 25 · INS-FIX Series

Fixture & Optical Condition Inspection

A standardized methodology for assessing the physical, mechanical, optical, photometric, environmental, and installation condition of AGL light fixtures and their optical assemblies. Covers lenses, prisms, optical surfaces, reflectors/optics where applicable, housings, seals, gaskets, LED modules, lamps, mounting arrangements, orientation, elevation/height, frangible components, corrosion, contamination, and water/moisture ingress.

Rev 0
Initial Issue

Critical control — A fixture can be electrically operational while still being optically or photometrically deficient. "Light ON" shall not by itself be treated as confirmation of acceptable AGL fixture performance. The inspection principle is Physical Condition → Optical Condition → Mechanical Condition → Environmental Condition → Photometric Condition. Particular attention should be given to lens/prism cleanliness, optical damage, water ingress, condensation, fixture orientation, fixture height/elevation, mounting security, frangible components, LED/lamp condition, and photometric performance. Where a visual inspection identifies a potential optical or photometric deficiency, the condition should be escalated for detailed inspection or calibrated photometric measurement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about fixture & optical condition inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook.

Q1What is Division 15 — Fixture & Optical Condition Inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook?

Division 15 contains 104 published topics in 18 sections (A–R). It gives a standardized methodology for assessing the physical, mechanical, optical, photometric, environmental and installation condition of AGL light fixtures and their optical assemblies.

Q2What does the fixture and optical inspection cover?

It covers lenses, prisms, optical cleanliness, reflectors and optical assemblies, housings, gaskets and seals, LED modules, lamps, mounting, orientation and alignment, height and elevation, frangible components, water and moisture, corrosion, photometric and optical condition, and environmental contamination.

Q3What parameters are recorded for fixtures and optics?

The checklist includes fixture identification and type, housing, lens, prism, reflector/optics, optical cleanliness and contamination, gasket, seal, LED module, lamp, illumination, brightness, colour, mounting and bolts, orientation, height/elevation, frangible component, corrosion, moisture, water ingress, internal condensation, environmental contamination and photometric condition.

Q4What is the standard fixture and optical inspection workflow?

The sequence identifies the fixture and verifies type and location, inspects the housing, lens, prism and reflector/optics, checks cleanliness, inspects the LED or lamp, and checks seals, gaskets and moisture. It then inspects mounting and bolts, checks orientation and height/elevation, inspects frangible components, checks corrosion and illumination, and reviews photometric records before classifying, recording, correcting, reinspecting and closing.

Q5How does Division 15 treat water and moisture?

Section N is dedicated to water and moisture. The checklist records moisture as None or Present, water ingress as None, Suspected or Confirmed, and internal condensation as None or Present, alongside seal and gasket condition.

Q6How are fixture defects classified?

Defect status is recorded using the four-level scheme Normal, Defective, Unserviceable and Immediate Action Required, so every observation ends with a clear classification before it is recorded and escalated.

Q7Is a fixture that is switched on necessarily performing acceptably?

No. A fixture can be electrically operational while still being optically or photometrically deficient, so "light ON" is not by itself treated as confirmation of acceptable performance. Where a potential optical or photometric deficiency is found, it is escalated for detailed inspection or calibrated photometric measurement.

Q8Do airport procedures take precedence over this handbook?

Yes. This handbook is an uncontrolled educational reference, not an approved procedure. The airport's approved inspection schedule, SMS procedures, ATC instructions, NOTAM procedures and local aerodrome regulations always take precedence, and the current revision of any official document should be verified before use.

Q9Can I download a printable checklist for each inspection topic?

Yes. Each inspection topic has a Download as Word option that produces an uncontrolled Word checklist template (description, a tick-box inspection table and an inspector sign-off block) for personal reference and internal training, to be reviewed and adopted under the user’s own document control.
