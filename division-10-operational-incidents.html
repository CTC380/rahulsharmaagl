Operational Incidents

The operational-event and service-impact section of the AGL Failure Database. Unlike Divisions 01–09, Division 10 focuses on the operational incident itself and its effect on airport/AGL availability — rather than only the underlying component failure. The same event can therefore carry cross-references to multiple technical divisions.

Initial Issue

Key principle — Division 10 does not replace Divisions 01–09; it sits as the operational-event layer above them. For example: LED driver failure → Division 01, circuit open circuit → Division 02, CCR shutdown → Division 03, vehicle impact damage → Division 06/09 as applicable — but the resulting runway light outage is recorded here as the Division 10 operational incident, retaining every level of the chain from component to airport impact.

Lighting Outage Incidents
3 topics
OPI-01 Runway Light Outage
Failure Definition

Loss of required runway lighting service affecting one or more runway lighting functions.

Potentially Affected Systems
  • Runway edge lights
  • Threshold lights
  • Runway end lights
  • Centreline lights
  • TDZ lights
  • Runway guard lights
Possible Causes
  • Fixture failure
  • Cable failure
  • CCR failure
  • Isolation-transformer failure
  • Power loss
  • Control-system failure
  • Maintenance isolation
  • External damage
Investigation

Determine: what lights are affected → how many → location → circuit → CCR → control status → electrical condition.

Operational Importance

Severity depends on the lighting function affected, number of affected fixtures, location, time of occurrence, operational conditions and redundancy available.

OPI-02 Taxiway Light Outage
Failure Definition

Loss of required taxiway lighting service.

Potentially Affected Systems
  • Taxiway edge lights
  • Taxiway centreline lights
  • Clearance bars
  • Exit lights
  • Rapid-exit taxiway lights
Possible Causes
  • Fixture failure
  • Circuit failure
  • CCR problem
  • Cable/connector failure
  • Control failure
  • Power loss
  • Physical damage
Investigation

Identify the affected taxiway section and determine whether the outage is single fixture, local group, circuit-wide, multiple circuit, or system-wide.

OPI-03 Multiple Circuit Failure
Failure Definition

Failure of two or more AGL lighting circuits or associated circuits during the same incident or related event.

Possible Causes
  • Common power-source failure
  • CCR/control-system problem
  • Distribution failure
  • Lightning
  • Network/control failure
  • Major electrical fault
  • Human/operational error
Investigation

Look specifically for the common cause — for example: common power failure → multiple CCRs unavailable → multiple circuits fail.

Reliability Significance

Multiple-circuit failures deserve higher-level RCA because they may expose a common-mode vulnerability.

Power & Equipment Incidents
3 topics
OPI-04 Power Loss
Failure Definition

Loss of electrical power required for AGL operation.

Potential Causes
  • Incoming power failure
  • Distribution failure
  • Protection trip
  • Generator/UPS problem
  • Switching error
  • Internal electrical fault
Consequences
  • CCR shutdown
  • Circuit outage
  • Control-system failure
  • Lighting outage
  • Airport operational disruption
Investigation

Trace the power path: source → distribution → protection → CCR/control equipment → series circuit → fixture.

OPI-05 Unexpected Equipment Shutdown
Failure Definition

AGL equipment shuts down without an intended operational command or planned maintenance action.

Potential Equipment
  • CCR
  • Control panel
  • PLC
  • SCADA
  • Lighting system
  • Auxiliary equipment
Possible Causes
  • Protection trip
  • Over-temperature
  • Power interruption
  • Internal equipment failure
  • Control logic issue
  • Communication problem
Investigation

Establish whether the shutdown was commanded, automatic, protective, or unexplained. This distinction is essential.

OPI-06 Repeated Alarm Events
Failure Definition

Recurring alarm conditions indicating repeated or unresolved abnormal system behaviour.

Possible Causes
  • Intermittent electrical fault
  • Communication instability
  • Sensor malfunction
  • CCR problem
  • Control-system problem
  • Environmental condition
  • Recurring equipment fault
Investigation

Do not treat each alarm as an independent event initially. Look for frequency, timing, common equipment, common circuit, environmental correlation and maintenance history.

Reliability Significance

Repeated alarm → unresolved condition → eventual operational incident can be an important leading indicator.

External & Accidental Incidents
4 topics
OPI-07 Wildlife Damage
Failure Definition

Damage or disruption to AGL equipment caused by wildlife activity.

Potentially Affected Equipment
  • Lighting fixtures
  • Cables
  • Connectors
  • Signs
  • Control equipment
  • Associated infrastructure
Possible Mechanisms
  • Physical damage
  • Cable damage
  • Nesting/contamination
  • Animal contact
  • Burrowing
  • Impact
Investigation

Record wildlife evidence, location, affected equipment, physical damage, previous occurrences, and environmental conditions.

Important Principle

Wildlife damage should be recorded as the incident cause, while the actual technical damage can be cross-referenced to the relevant failure division.

OPI-08 Vehicle Impact
Failure Definition

An operational incident involving vehicle contact with AGL equipment or associated infrastructure.

Potential Consequences
  • Fixture damage
  • Frangible coupling failure
  • Base-can damage
  • Cable damage
  • Alignment change
  • Structural damage
  • Lighting outage
Investigation

Perform a complete impact assessment: fixture, frangible component, base can, mounting, foundation, cable, connector, alignment, and surrounding pavement/civil condition.

Cross-Reference

Division 06 — Mechanical & Structural Failures and, where applicable, Division 05 — Cable & Connector Failures.

OPI-09 Foreign-Object Damage
Failure Definition

Damage or operational disturbance resulting from a foreign object affecting AGL equipment or the airfield environment.

Potential Sources
  • Loose material
  • Construction debris
  • Maintenance materials
  • Pavement fragments
  • Equipment components
Possible Consequences
  • Fixture damage
  • Optical damage
  • Cable damage
  • Mechanical impact
  • Operational disruption
Investigation

Determine: object → source → impact location → damaged asset → operational consequence.

OPI-10 Construction-Related Damage
Failure Definition

Damage or disruption caused by construction, civil works, excavation, equipment movement or related activity.

Potentially Affected Systems
  • Cables
  • Fixtures
  • Base cans
  • Signs
  • Approach-lighting structures
  • Control systems
Possible Consequences
  • Cable damage
  • Fixture displacement
  • Electrical fault
  • Circuit outage
  • Operational restriction
Investigation

Review construction activity, work permit, work area, drawings, utility/AGL information, and protection/isolation arrangements.

Operational Control Incidents
3 topics
OPI-11 Accidental Equipment Isolation
Failure Definition

AGL equipment or circuit is unintentionally isolated from service.

Possible Causes
  • Incorrect switching
  • Maintenance error
  • Incorrect identification
  • Control-panel operation error
  • Incorrect isolation procedure
Possible Consequences
  • Partial outage
  • Loss of critical lighting
  • Circuit unavailable
  • Operational restriction
Investigation

Verify: equipment intended to remain available → actual isolation → person/system performing isolation → authorization → switching record.

OPI-12 Operational Switching Error
Failure Definition

Incorrect switching action causes an unintended change in AGL system operating condition.

Examples
  • Wrong CCR selected
  • Wrong circuit switched
  • Incorrect intensity selected
  • Incorrect equipment isolated
  • Incorrect control mode selected
Possible Consequences
  • Lighting outage
  • Incorrect lighting configuration
  • Partial system outage
  • Airport operational disruption
Investigation

Review switching instruction, switching sequence, operator action, control indication, equipment response, and switching record.

OPI-13 Emergency Shutdown
Failure Definition

Intentional or automatic shutdown of equipment/system in response to an emergency condition.

Important Distinction

An emergency shutdown is not necessarily a failure. It becomes an operational incident when it results in unexpected loss of service, unplanned operational impact, emergency intervention, or significant AGL unavailability.

Possible Triggers
  • Electrical emergency
  • Fire
  • Equipment fault
  • Safety condition
  • External event
  • Emergency maintenance
Investigation

Determine whether the shutdown was necessary and correct or unnecessary/incorrectly triggered.

System Availability Incidents
4 topics
OPI-14 Loss of Critical Lighting
Failure Definition

Loss of lighting identified as operationally critical to the affected airport movement area or procedure.

Potential Systems
  • Runway lighting
  • Runway centreline
  • Threshold/end lighting
  • Approach lighting
  • PAPI where applicable
  • Taxiway critical lighting
  • Stop bars/guard lights where applicable
Investigation

Record critical lighting function, number of affected units, location, duration, redundancy available, operational effect, and actions taken.

Importance

This category should be linked to operational criticality, not merely the number of failed fixtures.

OPI-15 Partial System Outage
Failure Definition

Loss of part of an AGL system while the remaining system continues operating.

Examples
  • One runway circuit unavailable
  • One taxiway section unavailable
  • Partial approach-lighting failure
  • Some control functions unavailable
Investigation

Determine: affected portion / unaffected portion / redundancy / operational impact.

OPI-16 Complete System Outage
Failure Definition

Complete loss of service of the affected AGL system.

Examples
  • Complete runway lighting outage
  • Complete taxiway lighting outage
  • Complete control-system outage
  • Complete circuit-control failure
Possible Causes
  • Major power loss
  • CCR/control failure
  • Common-mode electrical failure
  • Major infrastructure damage
  • Emergency shutdown
Importance

This should trigger high-priority RCA and incident review.

OPI-17 Intermittent System Outage
Failure Definition

AGL service repeatedly becomes unavailable and subsequently returns without a stable permanent correction.

Possible Causes
  • Intermittent cable fault
  • Loose connection
  • CCR instability
  • Communication problem
  • Control-system issue
  • Thermal/environmental effects
Investigation

Capture start/end time, frequency, duration, conditions, equipment state, alarm history, and environmental correlation.

Reliability Significance

Intermittent failures can be particularly difficult because the equipment may appear normal during inspection.

Airport-Level Impact
1 topic
OPI-18 Airport Operational Disruption
Failure Definition

An AGL incident results in a measurable disruption to normal airport operations.

Potential Effects
  • Runway availability restriction
  • Taxiway restriction
  • Reduced operational capability
  • Operational delay
  • Temporary closure/restriction
  • Increased operational risk
  • Requirement for alternative procedures
Important Principle

Airport operational disruption is an outcome, not necessarily the root failure. Example chain: CCR failure → runway circuit outage → loss of critical lighting → runway operational restriction → airport operational disruption. The database should retain every level of that chain.

B. Operational Severity Classification

Division 10 distinguishes equipment failure severity from operational consequence severity via a five-level scale:

Level 1 — Localized: single fixture or small localized incident (e.g. single taxiway edge light outage)

Level 2 — Limited: multiple fixtures or localized circuit/service impact (e.g. one taxiway lighting circuit unavailable)

Level 3 — Significant: major portion of a lighting system affected (e.g. multiple runway lighting circuits unavailable)

Level 4 — Critical: loss of critical lighting or major AGL capability (e.g. critical runway lighting unavailable)

Level 5 — Airport Operational Disruption: significant airport operational restriction or disruption

E. Component → Airport Impact Escalation Layers

Division 10 should not replace Divisions 01–09 — it acts as the operational-event layer above them. A single failure escalates through seven recognisable levels:

TECHNICAL COMPONENT — LED DRIVER COMPONENT FAILURE — LED DRIVER FAILURE ELECTRICAL CONSEQUENCE — OPEN CIRCUIT LIGHTING CONSEQUENCE — RUNWAY LIGHT OUTAGE SYSTEM CONSEQUENCE — PARTIAL RUNWAY LIGHTING OUTAGE OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE — LOSS OF CRITICAL LIGHTING AIRPORT CONSEQUENCE — OPERATIONAL DISRUPTION

Division 10 vs. Divisions 01–09

Divisions 01–09 answer “what component failed, and why?” Division 10 answers “what did that failure do to lighting service and airport availability?” The recommended database architecture strings both together: Operational Event → Affected AGL Asset → Technical Failure → System Failure → Lighting Service Impact → Operational Severity → Airport Impact → Immediate Response → Root Cause → Corrective Action → Verification → Recurrence/MTTR → Lessons Learned — making Division 10 the bridge between the technical failure divisions and the higher-level maintenance, emergency response, audit, reliability and continuous-improvement divisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about operational incidents and airport-level impact, as covered in Division 10 of the AGL Failure Database.

Q1What is Division 10 — Operational Incidents in the AGL Failure Database?

Division 10 is the operational-event layer above Divisions 01–09. It contains 18 published topics (OPI-01 to OPI-18) covering lighting outages, power and equipment incidents, external and accidental damage, operational control incidents, system availability incidents and airport operational disruption.

Q2Which operational incidents does Division 10 cover?

Runway light outage, taxiway light outage, multiple circuit failure, power loss, unexpected equipment shutdown, repeated alarm events, wildlife damage, vehicle impact, foreign-object damage, construction-related damage, accidental equipment isolation, operational switching error, emergency shutdown, loss of critical lighting, partial, complete and intermittent system outage, and airport operational disruption.

Q3How are the 18 operational incident topics grouped?

They fall into six groups: Lighting Outage Incidents (3 topics), Power & Equipment Incidents (3), External & Accidental Incidents (4), Operational Control Incidents (3), System Availability Incidents (4) and Airport-Level Impact (1).

Q4How does Division 10 classify operational severity?

Division 10 distinguishes equipment failure severity from operational consequence severity using a five-level scale: Level 1 Localized (a single fixture), Level 2 Limited (for example one taxiway lighting circuit unavailable), Level 3 Significant (a major portion of a lighting system affected), Level 4 Critical (loss of critical lighting or major AGL capability) and Level 5 Airport Operational Disruption.

Q5How do Division 10 and Divisions 01–09 differ?

Divisions 01–09 answer what component failed and why. Division 10 answers what that failure did to lighting service and airport availability. Division 10 does not replace the technical divisions; the same operational event can be cross-referenced to any of them depending on the underlying component or cause.

Q6What operational incident chains does Division 10 capture?

Division 10 retains every level of a chain rather than collapsing it into one record. In one runway chain, a CCR failure leads to a runway circuit outage, runway light outage, loss of critical lighting, operational restriction and airport operational disruption. The five representative chains also cover power loss, vehicle impact, operational switching error and intermittent failure.

Q7How does one failure escalate from component to airport impact?

A single failure escalates through seven recognisable levels, from the technical component through component failure, electrical consequence, lighting consequence, system consequence and operational consequence to airport consequence. For example, an LED driver failure can cause an open circuit, then a runway light outage and a partial runway lighting outage.

Q8What should be recorded for an operational incident?

Each incident record captures identification (incident ID, airport/project, date/time, location, affected runway, taxiway or area), the affected system, asset or circuit, incident category and type, failure mode, operational severity and criticality, investigation results, response and reliability information.
