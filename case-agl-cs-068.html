AGL-CS-068 — Average Passes but Individual Points Fail

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A goniophotometric type-test case where the average intensity passed the acceptance criterion but mandatory beam-distribution points failed — from measurement verification through optical inspection, beam-envelope analysis, and corrective retesting.

1Case Study Overview

During a laboratory goniophotometric type test, a runway edge light produced an average intensity that exceeded the required ICAO acceptance value.

The manufacturer initially considered the fixture compliant because the calculated average intensity was satisfactory.

However, detailed analysis of the photometric grid revealed that several mandatory measurement points within the required beam distribution envelope were below the specified minimum intensity.

The engineering question was:

Engineering Question

Can a fixture pass photometric compliance when the average intensity is acceptable but individual beam-distribution points fail the required ICAO values?

This case demonstrates one of the most important principles in AGL photometric certification: compliance depends on required photometric points, not merely on average intensity.

  • Make correct certification decisions.
  • Document photometric non-conformities accurately.

3System Background

A photometric test evaluates:

  • Peak intensity.
  • Vertical beam distribution.
  • Horizontal beam distribution.
  • Chromaticity.
  • Intensity at specified test points.
  • Beam-envelope compliance.

Testing is performed at defined vertical and horizontal angles.

Compliance is generally determined by:

  • Minimum intensity requirements.
  • Maximum intensity limitations.
  • Beam-distribution requirements.
  • Individual measurement-point compliance.

Average intensity is only one statistical indicator.

7Beam Distribution Principle

Correct beam → Required intensity → All mandatory points compliant → Uniform visual guidance → ICAO compliance
Deficient beam → Some points below minimum → Beam distortion → Reduced visual coverage → ICAO non-compliance

Pilots use the entire beam envelope, not only the beam center.

11ICAO Compliance Principle

Photometric compliance requires:

  • Required test points.
  • Required beam distribution.
  • Required intensity values.
  • Required chromaticity.

A fixture may fail because:

  • Edge intensity is insufficient.
  • Vertical coverage is inadequate.
  • Horizontal coverage is incomplete.
  • Mandatory points are below minimum.

The average cannot replace mandatory point compliance.

12Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The fixture failed ICAO photometric compliance because the required beam-distribution envelope contained multiple non-compliant measurement points, even though the average intensity exceeded the acceptance value.

The failure was caused by:

  • Optical-module displacement.
  • Reflector deformation.
  • Beam-axis shift.
  • Reduced beam-edge intensity.

13Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Replaced the optical module.
  2. Corrected reflector alignment.
  3. Restored beam-axis alignment.
  4. Cleaned the optical assembly.
  5. Repeated goniophotometric testing.
  6. Verified all mandatory points.
  7. Confirmed beam-envelope compliance.
  8. Updated photometric certification records.

16Why This Case Was Important

Beam-distribution failures can cause:

  • Reduced visual coverage.

Photometric compliance is a spatial distribution requirement, not merely a brightness requirement.

18Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: Average intensity = 101.9%. Two mandatory beam points below minimum. Measurements verified. What should you do?

A. Certify the fixture because the average passes.
B. Ignore the edge-point failures.
C. Reject the fixture, investigate the beam-distribution defect, correct the optical system, and repeat the photometric test before certification.
D. Increase LED power.
Correct answer: C. Mandatory beam points failing the required intensity is a certification failure even if the average passes. The beam-distribution defect must be investigated and corrected before the fixture can be certified.

21Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about beam-distribution points and why an average intensity can mislead.

Q1What is AGL-CS-068?

AGL-CS-068 is an engineering training case study. It covers a laboratory goniophotometric type test where the average intensity passed but mandatory beam-distribution points failed. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2Can a fixture pass ICAO photometric compliance on average intensity alone?

No. Compliance depends on the required photometric points, beam distribution, intensity values and chromaticity. The average cannot replace mandatory point compliance.

Q3What were the results in AGL-CS-068?

The average intensity was 101.9%, but two mandatory points measured 72% and 76% against a 100% requirement. Repeat measurements confirmed both failures (72–73% and 75–76%).

Q4Why did the average still pass?

Strong readings of 109–118% at the beam centre compensated statistically for the weak beam edges. Operationally, the beam remained deficient.

Q5Why do pilots need the whole beam envelope and not just the centre?

Pilots view the light from different positions, so they use the entire beam envelope. Weak edge intensity, narrow horizontal spread or reduced vertical coverage leaves gaps in visual guidance.

Q6What did the beam distribution analysis show?

Strong central intensity, weak edge intensity, narrow horizontal spread and reduced vertical coverage, so the beam envelope was distorted.

Q7What caused the failure in AGL-CS-068?

Optical-module displacement, reflector deformation, beam-axis shift and reduced beam-edge intensity. Inspection also found lens contamination and partial optical obstruction.

Q8How was compliance restored?

The team replaced the optical module, corrected reflector alignment, restored beam-axis alignment and cleaned the optical assembly. They then repeated goniophotometric testing, verified all mandatory points and updated the certification records.

Q9What is the key lesson from this case?

Check every required test point against its minimum. An acceptable average does not prove the beam envelope is compliant.

22Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

A fixture cannot be considered ICAO compliant when mandatory photometric measurement points fail the required intensity values, even if the average intensity exceeds the acceptance criterion, because beam-distribution compliance depends on the required photometric envelope rather than statistical averaging.

Systematic point-by-point photometric analysis, beam-distribution verification, optical inspection, alignment assessment, and certification retesting can identify beam-envelope defects that average intensity calculations may conceal.

In AGL engineering, pilots do not see an average beam; they see the beam exactly where the photometric points are measured.
