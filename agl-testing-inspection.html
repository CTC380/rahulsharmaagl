AGL Testing & Inspection

A practical testing, inspection and verification guide for Airfield Ground Lighting. AGL testing and inspection is the process of confirming that lighting equipment and systems are physically sound, electrically safe, mechanically correct, operationally functional and optically compliant — from the power source to the final optical output.

Inspect → Test → Measure → Compare → Rectify → Re-Test → Record
Start Learning Review AGL Photometry
Overview

What Is AGL Testing & Inspection?

AGL Inspection is the systematic examination of equipment, installation, components and physical conditions.

AGL Testing is the measurement or functional verification of defined electrical, mechanical, optical or operational parameters.

Together they answer one question: is the AGL system installed correctly, electrically healthy, mechanically sound and capable of providing the required visual performance?

Not Just "Is It ON?"

AGL testing should not be limited to checking whether a light illuminates. A complete program examines the system from the power source to the final optical output.

Four Verification Areas

A practical framework separates verification into electrical, mechanical, optical and operational dimensions — together forming complete AGL verification.

Traceable Evidence

Every inspection and test should be recorded so the system's condition and performance can be demonstrated, trended and defended.

Why It Matters

A System May Look Normal While Having an Underlying Defect

AGL systems operate in demanding environments and may experience a wide range of degrading conditions. Testing helps identify problems before they become operational failures.

Moisture Water ingress Temperature variation Lightning Cable deterioration Mechanical impact Vehicle movement Aircraft blast Vibration Corrosion Connector deterioration Optical contamination Equipment ageing
Verification Framework

Four Dimensions of AGL Verification

A practical AGL inspection framework can be divided into four major areas. Electrical + Mechanical + Optical + Operational = Complete AGL Verification.

1. Electrical

2. Mechanical

3. Optical

4. Operational

Does the complete system operate and respond correctly?

Circuit Health

Continuity & Insulation Resistance Testing

Continuity Test

Verifies that the intended electrical path is complete. For a series circuit, the engineer may verify the circuit path through:

CCR → Cable → Transformer → Connector → Fixture → Transformer → Cable → CCR

Record circuit number, test points, test instrument, measured result, acceptance criterion and PASS/FAIL.

Insulation Resistance Test

Can identify moisture, damaged insulation, cable deterioration, ground leakage, incorrect termination and connector problems.

Testing should be performed using the specified test voltage and procedure. Sensitive electronic equipment should be isolated where required by the manufacturer before applying insulation-test voltage.

Continuity Test Path — Loop Verification

Continuity is verified around the complete series loop — a numbered test point at each stage keeps the result traceable back to a specific segment

Power System

Circuit Monitoring, Voltage & Earthing

Circuit Current Monitoring

Compare actual circuit current against expected system performance. Abnormal current may indicate:

  • Short circuit
  • Incorrect load
  • Transformer problem
  • Fixture failure
  • Cable problem
  • Incorrect connection
  • Circuit configuration error

Voltage Measurement & Earthing

Possible measurement points: CCR input/output, transformer input/output, equipment supply. Consider nominal voltage, operating voltage, variation and drop.

Earthing & bonding: equipment grounding, panel grounding, bonding, metal structures, cable protection, PAPI/sign grounding, surge protection. Test earth continuity and earth resistance where required.

Current Monitoring — Where Abnormal Current Points

The current is the same everywhere on a healthy series loop — where the clamp reading departs from the expected value narrows down which segment to investigate

Do not compare results against arbitrary values — use the applicable design and equipment requirements.

Keyword Reference

AGL Circuit Resistance, Continuity Test & Insulation Resistance Test

The core electrical checks on an airfield lighting series circuit, and what a poor result usually means.

AGL circuit resistance

AGL circuit resistance is the total resistance of the series loop, from the CCR output, through the primary cable and connections, and back. It is compared with the expected value from the cable length and size. A reading that is much higher than expected can point to a poor joint, a damaged conductor or a loose connection. Acceptance limits come from the project specification.

AGL circuit continuity test

An AGL circuit continuity test confirms that the series loop is complete with no open circuit. It is done with the circuit isolated and the CCR disconnected, using a suitable meter. If continuity is lost, the circuit can be split into sections to find where the break is. Continuity alone does not show that insulation is healthy, so it should be followed by insulation testing. See Continuity & Insulation Resistance Testing above.

Airfield lighting insulation resistance test

An airfield lighting insulation resistance test measures how well the cable and connected equipment resist leakage to earth. It is one of the most important checks for finding moisture and insulation damage before they cause faults. Record the result, with conditions such as temperature and weather, so it can be trended over time.

Airfield lighting low insulation resistance

Low insulation resistance in an airfield lighting circuit usually points to water ingress, damaged cable insulation, a poor connector or a failing isolation transformer. Locate the fault by isolating the circuit into sections and testing each, then repair, dry out where needed and re-test. Persistent low readings should be treated as a defect, not a trend to monitor.

Keyword Reference

AGL FAT Testing & Testing Procedures

Testing at the factory and how a testing procedure is written for airfield, airport and ground lighting work.

AGL FAT testing

AGL FAT testing is factory acceptance testing, done at the manufacturer's works before equipment is shipped. It normally covers CCRs, fixtures, transformers and control equipment, and checks electrical, mechanical and, where relevant, photometric performance against the order specification. It is often witnessed by the client or their representative, and passing it is a condition for shipment.

Airfield ground lighting testing procedure

An airfield ground lighting testing procedure sets out what is tested, in what order, with which instruments, and what counts as pass or fail. It normally runs from visual and mechanical inspection through electrical testing to functional and optical checks, with each result recorded. See the AGL Testing Hierarchy section above.

Airfield lighting testing procedure

An airfield lighting testing procedure is the same idea from the aerodrome operator's view: a written, approved method that anyone qualified can follow and get the same result. It should include safety precautions, isolation steps, test equipment and records. Intervals and limits come from your maintenance plan and standards.

Airport lighting testing procedure

An airport lighting testing procedure covers all airside lighting systems, including runway, taxiway, approach, PAPI and signs. A common procedure format keeps results comparable across systems, and each system's specific tests are added as appendices or sub-procedures. Please confirm test criteria with the standard your aerodrome follows.

Avoid These

Common AGL Testing Mistakes

1. Testing only when something fails

Testing should also be preventive.

2. Checking only voltage

Correct voltage does not prove complete system health.

3. Ignoring insulation

A circuit can operate today and still have deteriorating insulation.

4. Skipping photometry

Electrical health does not guarantee optical compliance.

5. Using unverified instruments

Poor measurement equipment can produce unreliable conclusions.

6. Not recording results

Unrecorded testing provides little historical value.

7. Not re-testing after repair

Repair completion does not automatically prove system compliance.

8. Comparing against arbitrary values

Use the applicable technical requirement.

9. Testing components without checking the system

AGL is an integrated system.

10. Declaring PASS because the light is ON

ON does not equal compliant.

Philosophy

The AGL Testing Philosophy

Every AGL test should answer three questions:

What are we measuring?

Electrical / Mechanical / Optical / Operational parameter.

What should it be?

Applicable acceptance criterion.

What did we actually measure?

Recorded result.

Measurement + Requirement + Result = Engineering Verification. A reliable AGL testing program does not ask only "is the light working?" — it asks whether the complete AGL system is physically correct, electrically healthy, mechanically secure, optically compliant and operationally reliable. That is the difference between basic inspection and engineering verification.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is AGL testing and inspection?

AGL inspection is the systematic examination of equipment, installation, components and physical conditions. AGL testing is the measurement or functional verification of defined electrical, mechanical, optical or operational parameters. Together they confirm the system is installed correctly, electrically healthy, mechanically sound and capable of providing the required visual performance.

Q2What are the four dimensions of AGL verification?

Electrical, mechanical, optical and operational. Electrical asks whether the electrical system is healthy, mechanical whether the installation is secure and correctly positioned, optical whether the light produces the required visual performance, and operational whether the complete system operates and responds correctly.

Q3What is the recommended AGL testing sequence?

A useful hierarchy runs from visual inspection and mechanical inspection through electrical testing, functional testing, alignment verification, photometry, system testing and final inspection, and ends with documentation. The sequence helps prevent unnecessary energization of defective equipment.

Q4When should AGL testing be performed?

Exact frequency should follow the applicable airport maintenance program, standards and project requirements. Testing may be required at material receipt, installation, pre-energization, commissioning, acceptance and periodic maintenance, and after equipment replacement, cable repair, major construction, severe weather, suspected lightning damage or abnormal operational performance.

Q5Why inspect equipment before installation?

Testing starts before installation. Incoming fixtures, transformers, CCRs, connectors, cables, PAPI units, signs and control equipment should be checked for correct model, rating, quantity, accessories, physical damage, manufacturing defects, identification, certification, packaging and storage condition.

Q6What does a visual inspection check?

Visual inspection is the first line of AGL quality control and should be documented rather than treated as an informal walk-through. It looks for broken housings, cracks, corrosion, loose hardware, missing covers, damaged lenses, dirty optics, water ingress, cable and connector damage, incorrect installation, missing identification and physical obstruction.

Q7What electrical tests are used in AGL verification?

Electrical testing can include continuity, insulation resistance, voltage, current, circuit resistance, transformer verification, earth continuity, earth resistance where applicable, CCR output and control circuit testing. The exact method and acceptance value must come from the applicable equipment standard, manufacturer instructions and project specification.

Q8What is the difference between a continuity test and an insulation resistance test?

A continuity test verifies that the intended electrical path is complete, for example CCR to cable to transformer to connector to fixture and back. An insulation resistance test can identify moisture, damaged insulation, cable deterioration, ground leakage, incorrect termination and connector problems, and should be performed using the specified test voltage.

Q9Why can an AGL system look normal while having an underlying defect?

AGL systems face moisture, water ingress, temperature variation, lightning, cable deterioration, mechanical impact, vehicle movement, aircraft blast, vibration, corrosion, connector deterioration, optical contamination and equipment ageing. Testing helps identify problems before they become operational failures.

Q10What should be recorded during AGL testing?

Every inspection and test should be recorded so system condition and performance can be demonstrated, trended and defended. For a continuity test, record the circuit number, test points, test instrument, measured result, acceptance criterion and pass or fail.

Q11How to measure insulation resistance in AGL?

Isolate and de-energise the circuit under your site's permit-to-work, lock it out and disconnect the CCR and any equipment the procedure says to disconnect. Use a calibrated insulation resistance tester at the test voltage set by your specification and the cable rating. Connect between the conductor and earth, apply the voltage for the specified time, then read and record the value with the date, circuit and conditions. Discharge the circuit afterwards. Compare the result with the acceptance limit and with earlier readings, and treat a low or falling reading as a defect to investigate.

Visual Inspection Still Finds Many Defects
Visual

Damaged seals, loose fixings and contamination are often visible. Allow time for careful observation before instruments come out.

Insulation Tests Need Proper Preparation
Electrical

Isolate and discharge circuits, choose the correct test voltage for the equipment, and record temperature and conditions.

Compare Readings With History
Trending

A passing number can hide a downward trend. Compare against previous results as well as the acceptance limit.

Verify Optical Performance Where Required
Photometry

Electrical acceptance does not prove light quality. Include photometric and alignment checks in the scope.

Define Pass and Fail Criteria Before Testing
Criteria

Agree acceptance values and responsibilities in advance so results are interpreted consistently.

File Reports Where the Next Team Can Find Them
Records

Test reports are most valuable years later during fault finding. Store them with clear identifiers and revision control.
