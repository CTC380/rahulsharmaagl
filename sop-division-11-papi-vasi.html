PAPI / VASI Installation, Alignment & Photometric SOPs

Controlled procedures for Precision Approach Path Indicator (PAPI) and, where still installed, legacy Visual Approach Slope Indicator (VASI) units — positioning, azimuth and elevation alignment, aiming-angle setting, optical adjustment, lamp/LED module replacement, electrical connection and power supply verification, heater operation, functional testing, photometric testing and periodic inspection.

Rev 0
Initial Issue

Scope Note — The procedures in this division are written around PAPI (and APAPI where applicable), the current ICAO Annex 14 standard visual approach slope system. Legacy VASI / T-VASIS installations follow the same control principles — position, elevation, aiming angle, transition sector, electrical isolation, functional and photometric verification, and flight inspection — but shall use the site-specific values and adjustment procedures of the approved design and manufacturer documentation. Do not apply PAPI unit counts, spacing or angle logic to VASI bars.

Technical Control — PAPI is a precision visual approach-slope system. Exact PAPI location, elevation, azimuth, approach-slope angle, unit separation, aiming angles, photometric requirements, power supply and acceptance criteria shall be taken from the approved aerodrome design, applicable ICAO Annex 14 / national requirements, DGCA requirements where applicable, approved survey data, manufacturer documentation and project specification. Do not use generic values where approved site-specific values exist.

SOP-219 PAPI Installation
Purpose

To establish a controlled procedure for installing PAPI units at the approved location, elevation and orientation.

Pre-Installation Verification

Verify:

  • Approved PAPI layout.
  • Runway designation.
  • Threshold reference.
  • PAPI location.
  • PAPI unit quantity.
  • Unit spacing.
  • Elevation data.
  • Azimuth reference.
  • Approach slope design.
  • Mounting foundation.
  • Power supply.
  • Cable route.
  • Equipment identification.
  • Obstacle/clearance requirements.
  • Obstacle protection surface (OPS) status confirmed against the approved design.
  • Supply type (series-circuit / constant-voltage) confirmed.
Procedure
  1. Review approved PAPI installation drawing.
  2. Establish runway centreline reference.
  3. Verify PAPI installation location.
  4. Verify foundation condition.
  5. Verify unit numbering.
  6. Install PAPI support/base.
  7. Position PAPI units.
  8. Connect electrical supply.
  9. Verify unit orientation.
  10. Perform azimuth alignment.
  11. Perform elevation alignment.
  12. Set approved aiming angles.
  13. Verify optical condition.
  14. Conduct functional testing.
  15. Conduct photometric/alignment verification.
  16. Record final settings.
Acceptance

PAPI shall be accepted only when Location + Elevation + Azimuth + Aiming Angles + Unit Spacing + Optical Performance + Electrical Operation are verified, and flight inspection is satisfactorily completed where required.

SOP-220 PAPI Unit Positioning

Critical Principle — PAPI positioning and PAPI alignment are separate controls. A perfectly aligned unit installed at the wrong location can still provide an incorrect approach indication.

Purpose

To ensure each PAPI unit is positioned at its approved longitudinal, lateral and vertical location.

Reference

Positioning shall be based on:

  • Runway centreline.
  • Threshold reference.
  • Approved PAPI survey coordinates.
  • Approved elevation.
  • Unit-to-unit spacing.
Procedure
  1. Establish survey reference.
  2. Identify PAPI installation line.
  3. Verify lateral distance from runway centreline.
  4. Verify longitudinal position.
  5. Verify individual unit spacing.
  6. Verify unit elevation.
  7. Check foundation stability.
  8. Record measured coordinates/elevations.
  9. Correct deviations before final alignment.

SOP-222 PAPI Azimuth Alignment

Important — Azimuth alignment shall not be based solely on visual judgment from the runway.

Purpose

To ensure PAPI units are correctly oriented in the horizontal plane toward the intended approach path.

Procedure
  1. Establish runway centreline reference.
  2. Establish approved PAPI reference line.
  3. Verify unit physical orientation.
  4. Use approved alignment equipment.
  5. Adjust azimuth.
  6. Verify optical axis.
  7. Check all units for consistency.
  8. Lock adjustment mechanism.
  9. Record final azimuth setting.
SOP-223 PAPI Elevation Alignment

Critical Check — Any unexplained elevation difference between units shall be investigated before final optical alignment.

Purpose

To ensure each PAPI unit has the correct elevation relative to the runway and approved PAPI reference.

Procedure
  1. Verify approved elevation data.
  2. Establish survey datum/reference.
  3. Measure unit elevation.
  4. Verify unit-to-unit elevation relationship.
  5. Check foundation settlement.
  6. Adjust physical mounting where permitted.
  7. Verify final elevation.
  8. Record measurements.
SOP-224 PAPI Angle Setting

Important — Do not insert generic PAPI aiming-angle values into the SOP. The approved project/site-specific values shall control.

Purpose

To establish the approved vertical aiming angles for each PAPI unit.

Procedure
  1. Confirm approved PAPI angle settings.
  2. Verify calibration of alignment equipment.
  3. Establish reference plane.
  4. Set the specified angle for each unit.
  5. Verify each unit independently.
  6. Confirm correct sequence across the PAPI system.
  7. Lock adjustment mechanisms.
  8. Record final settings.

SOP-226 PAPI Lamp Replacement

Critical Rule — Replacement lamp type, rating and optical characteristics must match the approved PAPI configuration.

Purpose

To establish a controlled procedure for replacing conventional PAPI lamps where applicable.

Procedure
  1. Obtain maintenance authorization.
  2. Isolate PAPI power under LOTO (Division 02).
  3. Verify safe condition.
  4. Allow lamp/fixture to cool.
  5. Open unit according to manufacturer instructions.
  6. Remove defective lamp.
  7. Inspect socket and wiring.
  8. Install approved lamp.
  9. Avoid contamination of optical surfaces.
  10. Reassemble unit.
  11. Verify sealing.
  12. Restore power.
  13. Perform functional test.
  14. Perform alignment/photometric verification where required.

SOP-229 PAPI Electrical Connection
Purpose

To ensure reliable and safe electrical connection of PAPI units.

Verify
  • Correct supply circuit.
  • Correct cable.
  • Correct connector/termination.
  • Polarity where applicable.
  • Earthing/protective conductor.
  • Cable identification.
  • Cable sealing.
  • Connection security.
Procedure
  1. Isolate supply under LOTO in accordance with Division 02.
  2. Verify isolation and test for absence of voltage/current (for series circuits, confirm CCR is isolated and locked out — a dark lamp is not proof of isolation).
  3. Identify cable.
  4. Inspect cable.
  5. Prepare termination according to manufacturer instructions.
  6. Complete connection.
  7. Verify mechanical security.
  8. Verify sealing.
  9. Perform electrical tests.
  10. Restore power using controlled energization.
SOP-230 PAPI Power Supply Verification
Purpose

To verify that the PAPI receives the required electrical supply.

Procedure
  1. Identify PAPI supply.
  2. Verify supply rating.
  3. Inspect protective devices.
  4. Verify incoming voltage (constant-voltage supply) or circuit current at the specified brightness step (series-circuit supply) under approved test conditions.
  5. Verify supply stability.
  6. Check control/power interfaces where applicable.
  7. Record readings.
  8. Investigate abnormal values.
Acceptance

Power supply must comply with the manufacturer's specified operating requirements and approved project design.

D. PAPI Common Failure Modes

The following conditions should be specifically monitored during AGL maintenance:

  1. Incorrect PAPI Position — Even a correctly aligned unit can provide incorrect guidance if installed at the wrong location.
  2. Foundation Settlement — Movement can change elevation and therefore the effective approach-slope indication.
  3. Azimuth Misalignment — Can cause the useful beam to be displaced from the intended approach path.
  4. Incorrect Aiming Angle — A small aiming error can materially affect the intended visual indication.
  5. Unequal Unit Elevation — Differences between units can affect the consistency of the PAPI indication.
  6. Optical Contamination — Dirty optical surfaces reduce useful light output.
  7. Moisture/Condensation — Can degrade optical and electrical performance.
  8. Lamp/LED Degradation — A fixture may remain illuminated while its optical output no longer meets requirements.
  9. Power Supply Problem — Incorrect or unstable supply can cause abnormal operation.
  10. Heater Failure — In cold/humid environments, heater failure can result in condensation and optical degradation.
  11. Mechanical Movement — Loose mounting or structural movement can alter alignment.
  12. Incorrect Replacement Module — A visually similar lamp/LED module may not have the required optical characteristics.

Division 11 — PAPI / VASI Golden Rule

"PAPI is not simply a row of four lights. It is a calibrated visual approach-slope guidance system in which position, elevation, azimuth, individual aiming angles, optical performance and unit-to-unit relationship must work together. Any change in the physical installation or optical system can affect the guidance indication and therefore requires controlled verification."

Division 11 — SOP Approval Principle

Final PAPI location, elevation, azimuth, approach-slope angle, unit separation, aiming angles, photometric requirements and power supply arrangements shall be based on the approved aerodrome design, applicable ICAO Annex 14 / national requirements, DGCA requirements where applicable, approved survey data and manufacturer documentation.

Approved site-specific design values and manufacturer documentation shall take precedence wherever they impose more restrictive requirements than a generic value.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about PAPI / VASI SOPs in the AGL SOP Library.

Q1What is Division 11 — PAPI / VASI SOPs?

Division 11 is a set of 17 procedures (SOP-219 to SOP-235, Rev 0) for Precision Approach Path Indicator (PAPI) units and, where still installed, legacy VASI units. It covers positioning, azimuth and elevation alignment, aiming-angle setting, optical adjustment, lamp and LED module replacement, electrical connection and power supply verification, heater operation, functional and photometric testing, and periodic inspection.

Q2Where do PAPI aiming angles and locations come from?

From the approved aerodrome design, applicable ICAO Annex 14 and national or DGCA requirements, approved survey data, manufacturer documentation and the project specification. Generic values must not be used where approved site-specific values apply, and generic aiming-angle values must not be inserted into the SOP (SOP-224).

Q3How is PAPI angle setting done?

Confirm the approved angle settings, verify calibration of the alignment equipment, establish a reference plane, set the specified angle for each unit and verify each unit independently. Confirm the correct sequence across the system, lock the adjustment mechanisms and record final settings (SOP-224).

Q4What if PAPI units show an unexplained elevation difference?

Any unexplained elevation difference between units must be investigated before final optical alignment (SOP-223). Verify approved elevation data, establish a survey datum, measure each unit, verify the unit-to-unit relationship and check for foundation settlement.

Q5What does PAPI photometric testing assess?

Depending on the approved procedure: light intensity, beam distribution, horizontal and vertical coverage, transition characteristics, colour, intensity settings, individual unit performance and system consistency. It uses calibrated test equipment after confirming PAPI alignment (SOP-233).

Q6How is the PAPI heater checked?

Where fitted, SOP-231 verifies the heater specification, connections and control circuit, energizes the heater per the approved procedure and checks for abnormal heating, thermostat function, condensation, moisture, damaged wiring and control malfunction.
