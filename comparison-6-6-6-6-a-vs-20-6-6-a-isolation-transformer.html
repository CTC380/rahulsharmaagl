6.6/6.6 A VS 20/6.6 A

A 6.6/6.6 A isolation transformer has the same rated current on both sides — essentially a 1:1 current-ratio transformer whose main job is electrical isolation. A 20/6.6 A transformer is rated for a 20 A primary and a 6.6 A secondary, so it provides isolation and current transformation together. The notation describes rated currents, not voltage — always confirm actual ratings from the nameplate/datasheet.

02 · 6.6/6.6 A Isolation Transformer

Same Current on Both Sides

The 6.6/6.6 A transformer is rated at 6.6 A on the primary and 6.6 A on the secondary. Because the rated current is the same on both sides, it is essentially a 1:1 current-ratio isolation transformer. Its primary purpose is electrical isolation between the series circuit and the individual light fixture — not current reduction.

03 · 20/6.6 A Isolation Transformer

Higher-Current Primary, 6.6 A Secondary

The 20/6.6 A transformer is rated at 20 A on the primary and 6.6 A on the secondary. It is designed to interface a higher-current primary circuit with a 6.6 A secondary circuit, so it provides both galvanic isolation and current transformation in the same unit.

05 · Why Current Changes

The Underlying Relationship

For an ideal transformer, approximately:

V₁ × I₁ ≈ V₂ × I₂ — so when the secondary current is lower than the primary current, the corresponding voltage relationship differs accordingly. For the rated current ratio, I₁ / I₂ = 20 / 6.6 ≈ 3.03, giving a current ratio of approximately 3.03 : 1. The actual voltage and power ratings must always be checked from the transformer nameplate/datasheet.

06 · 6.6/6.6 A in AGL

A Simplified Series Circuit

In a simplified AGL series circuit, the Constant Current Regulator (CCR) drives 6.6 A through the series loop into a 6.6/6.6 A transformer, which isolates the individual light from the series circuit and passes the same 6.6 A through to the fixture.

Interfacing a Higher-Current Circuit

08 · Important Point

Current Rating Is Not Voltage Rating

Do not read 6.6/6.6 A as 6.6 kV / 6.6 kV. Likewise, 20/6.6 A does not by itself specify the transformer's voltage ratio. This notation describes current ratings only. The nameplate/datasheet must always be checked for primary voltage, secondary voltage, rated current, VA/kVA, frequency, insulation level, temperature rise and the applicable standard.

10 · Myths & Facts

Common Misreadings

Myth: 6.6/6.6 A and 20/6.6 A can be swapped since both have a "6.6 A" side
Fact

The two transformers have different primary current ratings and current ratios. A connected circuit must match the transformer's rated current, voltage, VA rating and intended application — not just one shared number.

Myth: The "6.6" and "20" figures describe voltage
Fact

These figures describe rated current, not voltage. Reading 6.6/6.6 A as 6.6 kV/6.6 kV, or assuming 20/6.6 A sets the voltage ratio, is incorrect — voltage must be confirmed separately from the nameplate/datasheet.

Myth: A 20/6.6 A transformer is simply a "bigger" version of a 6.6/6.6 A unit
Fact

It performs a different function. The 6.6/6.6 A unit provides isolation at a 1:1 current ratio, while the 20/6.6 A unit provides isolation together with current transformation across a ≈3.03:1 ratio.

11 · Engineering Selection

Choosing the Right Rating

When identifying or specifying a 6.6/6.6 A or 20/6.6 A transformer, consider:

  • The rated current on the primary-side circuit the transformer will actually be connected to
  • Whether the secondary-side fixture requires the standard 6.6 A AGL series current
  • Whether isolation alone is sufficient, or current transformation is also required
  • Confirming primary voltage, secondary voltage, VA/kVA, frequency, insulation level and temperature rise from the nameplate/datasheet
  • Applicable ICAO/national AGL requirements and airport-specific technical specifications
  • Never assuming interchangeability based on a shared "6.6 A" figure alone
12 · Engineering Verdict

Which Transformer Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

A 6.6/6.6 A transformer carries the same rated current on both sides, making it a 1:1 current-ratio isolation transformer suited to the standard 6.6 A AGL series lighting circuit. A 20/6.6 A transformer is rated for a 20 A primary and a 6.6 A secondary, so it provides isolation together with current transformation for a higher-current or special circuit arrangement.

The notation describes rated current only — it does not specify voltage. Final selection for an actual project should always be based on the transformer nameplate/datasheet, the approved circuit design and the applicable ICAO/national AGL standard.

13 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the "6.6/6.6 A" or "20/6.6 A" notation mean?

It states the primary and secondary rated currents of the transformer — for example, 20/6.6 A means a 20 A primary and a 6.6 A secondary. It does not describe voltage.

Is a 6.6/6.6 A transformer a current-transforming device?

Not significantly. Since the rated current is the same on both sides, it functions essentially as a 1:1 current-ratio isolation transformer.

What is the current ratio of a 20/6.6 A transformer?

Approximately 3.03:1 (20 A ÷ 6.6 A ≈ 3.03), calculated from the rated primary and secondary currents.

Can a 20/6.6 A transformer replace a 6.6/6.6 A transformer?

Not automatically. The connected circuit's rated current, voltage, VA rating and intended application must match the transformer — the two types should not be treated as interchangeable simply because both have a 6.6 A side.

Does 6.6/6.6 A mean 6.6 kV on both sides?

No. The figures describe rated current, not voltage. Voltage ratings must be confirmed separately from the transformer nameplate/datasheet.

Why would a circuit use a 20 A primary feeding a 6.6 A secondary?

To interface a higher-current primary circuit arrangement with a secondary circuit designed for the standard 6.6 A AGL fixture current, combining isolation with current transformation in one unit.

Do both transformer types provide isolation?

Yes. Both the 6.6/6.6 A and 20/6.6 A units provide electrical isolation; the difference is whether current transformation also occurs across the unit.
