Smart Transformer

An intelligent isolating transformer with embedded sensing and communication capabilities. Smart transformers monitor primary and secondary current, winding temperature, and insulation health in real-time, transmitting diagnostic data to the ALCMS for predictive maintenance and early fault detection in AGL series circuits.

Overview

What Is It?

A Smart Transformer is an advanced isolating transformer used in AGL series circuits that integrates sensing, signal processing, and communication electronics. Unlike conventional isolating transformers, smart transformers provide:

  • Real-time monitoring of primary and secondary current
  • Winding temperature sensing for thermal protection
  • Insulation resistance monitoring to detect degradation
  • Embedded communication via PLC, LoRa, or Zigbee
  • Diagnostic data logging for predictive maintenance
  • Remote fault reporting to the ALCMS

Smart transformers are a key enabler of condition-based maintenance in modern airfield lighting systems, reducing downtime and optimizing maintenance resources.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The smart transformer's purpose is to give a component that has historically failed silently — an isolating transformer whose insulation is degrading or whose winding is overheating — a way to report that decline before it actually causes a fixture to go dark or a circuit fault to develop. Because a failed isolating transformer can take a single fixture out of a series loop with little advance warning under conventional monitoring, the smart transformer's continuous current, temperature, and insulation-resistance sensing is what allows a developing fault to be caught and scheduled for replacement during planned maintenance rather than discovered as an outage. This turns transformer replacement from a reactive response to an unexplained light failure into a planned, condition-based maintenance task.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Smart transformers are deployed in AGL series circuits where intelligent monitoring is required:

  • Runway Lighting Circuits: Monitoring edge, centreline, threshold, and touchdown zone light transformers.
  • Taxiway Lighting Circuits: Edge, centreline, and stop bar transformer monitoring.
  • Approach Lighting Systems: Barrette and crossbar light transformers.
  • Critical or High-Traffic Areas: Where rapid fault detection and response are essential.
  • Remote Airfields: Enabling remote diagnostics and reducing maintenance visits.

They are particularly valuable for airports implementing predictive maintenance strategies and smart airport initiatives.

Placement

Installation & Location

The smart transformer is installed in its own secondary base can exactly as a conventional isolating transformer would be, sited adjacent to or below the fixture it serves, sealed against standing water, and fed by armoured cable from the series circuit and to the fixture. The additional monitoring electronics require no separate foundation or enclosure, but the unit's communication link, whether power-line carrier over the series circuit or a wireless mesh, is verified at installation, and its circuit position and fixture identity are registered on the ALCMS so a reported fault or trend resolves to the correct base can in the field without needing to trace the circuit manually.

Function

How Does It Work?

A smart transformer combines traditional transformer functionality with embedded electronics. The integrated monitoring system performs the following functions:

  • Current Sensing: Hall-effect sensors measure primary and secondary current with high accuracy, detecting deviations from nominal values.
  • Temperature Monitoring: Thermistors embedded in the winding provide continuous temperature data, triggering alerts on overtemperature conditions.
  • Insulation Monitoring: Periodic measurements of insulation resistance between windings and to ground detect early signs of moisture ingress or insulation degradation.
  • Local Processing: An onboard microcontroller analyzes sensor data, compares against thresholds, and generates local alerts.
  • Communication: Transmits status data via Power Line Communication (PLC) over the series circuit, or wirelessly via LoRa/Zigbee to the IoT Gateway.
  • Data Logging: Stores historical performance data for trend analysis and predictive maintenance.

The system operates autonomously, continuously monitoring health parameters and reporting only when thresholds are exceeded or at scheduled intervals to conserve bandwidth.

Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

The transformer's core function — isolating and dropping loop current to its fixture — is unchanged and stays under CCR/loop-level command; the embedded monitoring is an entirely separate, parallel path. Current and temperature sensors feed a local microcontroller that compares readings against thresholds and reports via PLC over the series circuit, or wirelessly, only when a threshold is exceeded or on a scheduled interval. This lets a developing insulation or overtemperature fault be flagged and located to a specific transformer before it degrades into the open-circuit condition that the CCR would otherwise only be able to report as a generic loop-level fault.

Construction

Physical Construction

A smart transformer is built on the same isolating transformer core and winding construction as a conventional unit — a toroidal or shell-type transformer core with primary and secondary windings sized to the fixture's power rating — housed in a sealed, corrosion-resistant enclosure rated to IP67/IP68 for direct burial or base-can installation. Current and temperature sensing elements are integrated around the windings and core, connecting to a potted electronics module that also houses the insulation monitoring circuit, communication interface, and any local data storage. This electronics module is isolated from the high-voltage windings by the same insulation barriers used in the transformer's core design, and is encapsulated in resin or a separate sealed compartment to protect it from moisture and vibration while sitting in the same base can as the transformer itself. The communication hardware, whether a PLC coupling network or wireless radio, is arranged so its antenna or coupling point can operate through the sealed housing, and all external terminals use the same L-823 sealed connector standard as a conventional transformer, keeping the smart transformer a drop-in replacement for existing base can installations.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Energy Harvesting Limitations

At low brightness levels (e.g., 2.8A), the available energy for powering the electronics may be insufficient. Some smart transformers use a small battery backup or supercapacitor to ensure continuous monitoring even at reduced current levels.

Communication Interference

Power Line Communication can be affected by noise from other equipment on the same circuit. Using spread-spectrum modulation and error-correcting codes improves reliability. Wireless communication may be preferred for critical monitoring.

Sensor Drift Over Time

Like all electronic sensors, current and temperature sensors can drift over time. Regular calibration or verification against reference instruments is recommended to maintain accuracy.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Monitoring Data Drops Out at Low Brightness Steps
Likely Cause
At low current settings (e.g., 2.8 A), the energy harvested for powering the onboard electronics is insufficient to maintain continuous operation.
Diagnostic Steps
Check whether data loss correlates with the CCR's low-intensity steps; verify the status of any battery backup or supercapacitor fitted to the unit.
Corrective Action
Confirm the battery backup/supercapacitor is holding charge and replace it if degraded; consult the manufacturer if data loss persists at low steps with a healthy backup.
Communication Errors or Dropouts on the Monitoring Link
Likely Cause
Noise from other equipment sharing the same power circuit is interfering with power line communication.
Diagnostic Steps
Check for other equipment on the same circuit that could be a noise source; review the communication link's error rate against known noisy periods.
Corrective Action
Verify spread-spectrum modulation and error correction settings are enabled, or switch to wireless communication for that unit if PLC interference persists.
Reported Current or Temperature Readings Drift from Reference
Likely Cause
The transformer's internal current or temperature sensor has drifted over time, as is normal for electronic sensors.
Diagnostic Steps
Compare the smart transformer's reported readings against an external reference instrument at the same point in the circuit.
Corrective Action
Recalibrate or verify the sensor against the reference instrument per the manufacturer's procedure, and replace the sensor if it fails to recalibrate within tolerance.

Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Series Circuit Isolation
Electrical Safety

The smart transformer sits on a constant-current series loop that can carry several kV. De-energise and lock out the CCR, then verify zero energy at the unit before opening its housing or handling connectors.

Communications Module Isolation
Electrical Safety

Treat the sensing and communication electronics as part of the isolated circuit, not a separate low-voltage system; disconnect any PLC, LoRa, or Zigbee module power feed along with the main loop before working inside the housing.

Insulation Test Before Re-Energisation
Electrical Safety

After any cable, connector, or transformer work, megger test the affected series loop before restoring power, since a residual fault at re-energisation can trip the CCR or damage healthy transformers on the loop.

Diagnostic Data Is Advisory, Not a Substitute for Testing
Operational

Use the transformer's remote diagnostic readings to prioritise and plan work, but confirm winding resistance, insulation resistance, and turns ratio with calibrated test equipment before returning the unit to service.

PPE & Grounding
Personnel Safety

Use insulated tools and appropriate PPE when working on series-circuit connections, and confirm the transformer case and cable shielding are bonded/grounded per the electrical design before reconnecting the loop.

FOD Control at the Housing
Operational

Keep tools, fasteners, and removed gaskets accounted for at all times when the transformer housing is open, and secure or remove any loose material before reinstating it, to prevent foreign object debris on the movement area.

Hot Surface Contact
Personnel Safety

Allow the transformer case to cool before handling after an extended active period, since sustained loop current and onboard electronics can leave the housing noticeably warm to the touch.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Do not leave a unit reporting a fault, an out-of-range sensor reading, or a lost communications link after maintenance; confirm the transformer reports healthy status to the ALCMS before closing out the work.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Trend the Monitoring Data, Don't Just Alarm on It
Data Analysis

The real value of a smart transformer's current, temperature, and insulation-resistance monitoring is in the trend, not the instantaneous reading; a unit that never trips an alarm can still be showing a slow drift toward failure that only becomes visible when readings are logged and compared across maintenance cycles.

Verify the Communications Link Independently of the Sensing
Integration

A smart transformer can be sensing correctly while its data never reaches the ALCMS due to a communications fault, network configuration change, or addressing conflict; periodically confirm data is actually arriving and updating at the monitoring system, not just that the transformer's local diagnostics report healthy.

Size for the Actual LED Load, Not the Legacy Fixture
Electrical

The same VA-sizing mismatch that affects conventional isolating transformers applies to smart transformers retrofitted onto LED circuits; a unit sized for the old incandescent load running well under capacity can produce monitoring readings that look unusual purely because the transformer itself is oversized for the present load.

Set Winding Temperature Alarm Thresholds Against Ambient, Not a Fixed Number
Calibration

A single fixed winding-temperature alarm threshold can be too conservative in cold climates and too permissive in hot ones; where the monitoring system allows it, reference the alarm threshold against ambient temperature rather than an absolute figure to get consistent early warning across seasons.

A Slow Insulation Resistance Decline Is the Earliest Fault Indicator
Troubleshooting

Moisture ingress or insulation breakdown inside the transformer housing typically shows up first as a gradual decline in monitored insulation resistance, well before any current or thermal anomaly appears; treat a declining IR trend as the trigger for inspection, not just a hard alarm threshold.

Electronics Add a Failure Mode the Base Transformer Doesn't Have
Reliability

The added sensing and communication electronics give a smart transformer far better visibility than a passive unit, but they're also an additional component that can fail independently of the transformer's core function; confirm whether a reported fault is in the transformer itself or in its monitoring electronics before condemning the unit.

Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about smart transformers: monitoring, communication, ratings, standards and maintenance.

Q1What is a smart transformer in AGL?

A smart transformer is an isolating transformer for AGL series circuits with built-in sensing, signal processing and communication electronics. It monitors current, winding temperature and insulation health in real time and reports diagnostic data to the ALCMS for predictive maintenance and early fault detection.

Q2How is a smart transformer different from a conventional isolating transformer?

It performs the same isolating function but adds real-time monitoring of primary and secondary current, winding temperature sensing, insulation resistance monitoring, embedded communication, diagnostic data logging and remote fault reporting. That lets a developing fault be caught before it takes a fixture out of service.

Q3What does a smart transformer measure, and how accurate is it?

Hall-effect sensors measure primary and secondary current over 0 to 10 A AC with ±1% accuracy. Thermistors embedded in the winding cover -40°C to +150°C with ±2°C accuracy. Insulation monitoring covers 1 MΩ to 1000 MΩ using a 500 V DC test.

Q4How does a smart transformer communicate with the ALCMS?

It transmits status through Power Line Communication (PLC) over the series circuit, or wirelessly through LoRa or Zigbee to an IoT gateway. Modbus is also listed as an option. The transmission interval is configurable from 5 minutes to 24 hours, and it reports when thresholds are exceeded or at scheduled intervals.

Q5How is the monitoring electronics powered?

It is typically powered by energy harvested from the series current, with a battery or supercapacitor backup for continuity during zero-current periods. At low brightness steps such as 2.8 A the harvested energy may be insufficient, which is why backup power is used.

Q6What are the typical ratings of a smart transformer?

The rated primary current is 6.6 A AC on the constant-current series circuit, with power ratings of 30, 45, 65, 100, 150 and 200 W. Protection class is IP67/IP68, operating temperature is -40°C to +85°C, and cables are L-824 with L-823 connectors. The transformer casing is bonded to the airfield earth grid.

Q7Where are smart transformers used?

They are deployed in runway and taxiway lighting circuits, approach lighting systems, critical or high-traffic areas where rapid fault detection matters, and remote airfields where remote diagnostics reduce maintenance visits. They suit airports implementing predictive maintenance strategies.

Q8Which standards apply to smart transformers?

The main references are IEC 61823, FAA AC 150/5345-47 (series-to-series isolation transformers), ICAO Doc 9157 Part 5 and IEC 61822, which defines the 6.6 A series loop the primary operates on. Always verify the current edition and your aerodrome's approved requirements.

Q9How often should a smart transformer be checked?

Typical intervals are annual for sensor calibration verification, backup battery or supercapacitor checks, insulation resistance tests, and enclosure and terminal inspection, and semi-annual for communication link tests. Your airport's approved maintenance programme takes precedence.

Q10What are the common smart transformer problems?

The common issues are limited energy harvesting at low brightness levels, Power Line Communication interference from other equipment on the same circuit (improved with spread-spectrum modulation and error-correcting codes), and sensor drift over time, which calls for regular calibration against reference instruments.

Q11Can a smart transformer's diagnostic data replace field testing?

No. The remote readings are advisory and should be used to prioritise and plan work. Winding resistance, insulation and other conditions should still be confirmed by testing, and the affected series loop should be megger tested before re-energising after any cable, connector or transformer work.
