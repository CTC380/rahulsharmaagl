Function of Threshold Lighting

A Critical Visual Reference on Approach and Landing

What the Lights Do

The threshold is a critical visual reference during the approach and landing phase. Threshold lights give a clear visual indication of the threshold location and, depending on the applicable configuration, may form part of a combined threshold/end lighting arrangement.

The optical system is therefore as important as the mechanical fixture itself.

A Properly Selected System Provides

  • Correct light colour
  • Required intensity
  • Correct beam orientation
  • Appropriate vertical and horizontal coverage
  • Adequate visibility from the approach direction
  • Compatibility with the runway lighting circuit
  • Reliable operation under environmental conditions
  • Maintainability throughout the asset lifecycle
Typical Configurations

Elevated and Inset Threshold Lights

1. Elevated Threshold Light

Mounted above the pavement using an appropriate base, mounting arrangement or frangible support system. Useful where the approved airport design calls for above-ground visual aids.

Typical considerations

  • Elevated fixture body
  • Frangible mounting arrangement
  • Directional optical system
  • Series-circuit connection
  • Mechanical protection
  • Alignment with runway threshold
  • Clearance from aircraft movement areas

2. Inset / In-pavement Threshold Light

Installed substantially flush with the pavement surface. Typical where an elevated fixture would interfere with aircraft movement, clearance requirements or the approved pavement configuration.

Key considerations

  • In-pavement housing
  • Light fixture assembly
  • Optical lens
  • Correct orientation
  • Drainage
  • Pavement interface
  • Transformer connection
  • Connector and cable arrangement
  • Sealing against moisture and contamination
  • Serviceability

The complete inset installation should be considered as an assembly, not simply as a light fitting.

Light Colour

Green, Fixed, Unidirectional Toward the Approach

Threshold Colour

Runway threshold lights are green, fixed and unidirectional, visible to aircraft on approach. On precision approach runways, green wing bars may be added on each side of the threshold for extra conspicuity. The number and spacing of lights depend on the runway type and approach category, so confirm them against the edition of Annex 14 or FAA guidance in use. ICAO Annex 14 requires threshold lights on a runway equipped with runway edge lights, except on a non-instrument or non-precision runway with a displaced threshold where wing bar lights are provided.

Combined Threshold/End Fixtures

Many installations use one fixture that shows green toward the approach and red toward the runway. FAA types include L-862E (high-intensity elevated), L-861E (medium-intensity elevated) and L-850D (inset). Displaced thresholds need a different arrangement, so follow the approved layout and confirm the lens orientation on the order.

Compliance Is Not Automatic

Modern threshold lighting may use LED technology for controlled optical output and improved lifecycle characteristics. However, LED technology alone does not establish compliance.

The complete fixture must meet the applicable photometric, electrical, environmental and mechanical requirements.

Why the Whole Chain Matters

Where a constant-current series circuit is used, the threshold light and associated isolating transformer must be evaluated as a coordinated system. A mismatch between fixture, transformer and circuit characteristics can result in:

  • Incorrect light intensity
  • Poor intensity control
  • Circuit instability
  • Excessive current
  • Premature equipment failure
  • Monitoring problems
  • Incorrect photometric performance

Selection should be based on the approved AGL circuit design and manufacturer data.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices, transformers or fixtures
  • Do not disconnect or reconnect a fixture's secondary connector while the series circuit is energised: an open secondary on a series isolating transformer can develop hazardous voltage and damage the transformer
  • Work on the movement area only under the airport's permit-to-work system, with the runway or taxiway closed as required, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures

FAQ

Runway Threshold Light — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a runway threshold light?

Runway threshold lights identify the beginning of the landing portion of the runway and provide an essential visual reference to approaching aircraft. They must deliver the required colour, intensity, beam distribution and directional visibility for the applicable runway configuration. Depending on the configuration, they may form part of a combined threshold/end lighting arrangement.

What colour are runway threshold lights?

Runway threshold lights are green, fixed and unidirectional, visible to aircraft on approach. On precision approach runways, green wing bars may be added on each side of the threshold for extra conspicuity. The number and spacing of lights depend on the runway type and approach category, so confirm them against the edition of Annex 14 or the FAA guidance in use.

When does ICAO require threshold lights?

ICAO Annex 14 requires threshold lights on a runway equipped with runway edge lights, except on a non-instrument or non-precision runway with a displaced threshold where wing bar lights are provided.

What is a combined threshold/end fixture?

It is a single fixture that shows green toward the approach and red toward the runway. FAA types include L-862E (high-intensity elevated), L-861E (medium-intensity elevated) and L-850D (inset). Displaced thresholds need a different arrangement, so follow the approved layout and confirm the lens orientation on the order.

What is the difference between elevated and inset threshold lights?

An elevated light is mounted above the pavement using a base, mounting arrangement or frangible support, and suits designs calling for above-ground visual aids. An inset light is installed substantially flush with the pavement, typically where an elevated fixture would interfere with aircraft movement, clearance requirements or the approved pavement configuration. For inset lights the pavement interface, drainage, sealing and optical alignment are critical, and the complete installation should be considered as an assembly.

Is a threshold light that works electrically automatically compliant?

No. A threshold light that is electrically operational is not automatically photometrically compliant, and for precision approach operations photometric performance should be verified against the applicable requirements rather than judged by visual observation alone. LED technology also does not by itself establish compliance, since the complete fixture must meet the applicable photometric, electrical, environmental and mechanical requirements.

How are threshold lights powered, and why does the whole chain matter?

The arrangement is a constant current regulator, primary cable, isolating transformer, secondary cable and the threshold light. The fixture and its isolating transformer must be evaluated as a coordinated system, because a mismatch between fixture, transformer and circuit characteristics can cause incorrect light intensity, poor intensity control, circuit instability, excessive current, premature equipment failure, monitoring problems or incorrect photometric performance.

What should be verified before and during installation?

Verify the approved threshold coordinates, runway centreline reference, fixture spacing, threshold alignment and approach direction, the correct optical direction, azimuth, elevation, beam alignment and colour direction, the correct isolating transformer, secondary connection, connector, cable length and circuit current, and the mounting arrangement, torque, sealing, gaskets, pavement interface and frangibility arrangement where applicable.

How are threshold lights commissioned and maintained?

Commissioning includes visual inspection, circuit continuity, insulation resistance, transformer and secondary connection verification, circuit current, CCR operation, intensity-step operation and photometric verification where required. Maintenance covers lens cleanliness and damage, light output, housing, fasteners, seals, mounting, transformer, connector and cable condition, circuit current, insulation and moisture. For inset lights, pay special attention to water accumulation, drainage, gaskets, the pavement interface, fixture seating and bolt condition.

What are the common faults with threshold lights?

A light that is off can come from an open circuit, failed light source, transformer failure, connector failure, cable fault, circuit interruption or internal fixture fault. Low output can come from incorrect CCR intensity, optical contamination, LED degradation, incorrect alignment, electrical mismatch or damaged optics. Intermittent operation can come from a loose connector, moisture ingress or a cable or transformer connection problem, and an incorrect colour from incorrect fixture configuration, optical problems, light-source degradation, contamination or an incorrect replacement component. Work along the circuit before replacing a healthy fixture.

What should be confirmed before ordering, and which standards apply?

Confirm the airport and project, runway identification and classification, approach category, threshold configuration, elevated or inset requirement, light colour, directionality, photometric requirement, light-source technology, series-circuit current, isolating transformer, connector, mounting, environmental requirements and the manufacturer’s compliance documentation. References include ICAO Annex 14, FAA AC 150/5340-30J, FAA AC 150/5345-46F (threshold/end types L-862E, L-861E and L-850D), DGCA India requirements and airport-specific specifications. Verify the applicable edition before procurement.

Is it safe to work on a runway threshold light while the series circuit is energised?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage, and opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death. Do not disconnect or reconnect a fixture’s secondary connector while the series circuit is energised, because an open secondary on a series isolating transformer can develop hazardous voltage and damage the transformer. De-energise the circuit at the regulator, apply the site lock-out / tag-out procedure, use the series circuit cut-out switch or an approved isolation device, and work on the movement area only under the airport’s permit-to-work system, with the runway or taxiway closed as required. See the electrical safety warning on this page.
