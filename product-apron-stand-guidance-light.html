Product Overview

Apron Stand Guidance Light

What It Is

Apron Stand Guidance Lights provide visual guidance for aircraft manoeuvring within aircraft stands and apron areas, helping pilots follow the designated stand entry, turning and exit routes and achieve the required parking position.

They are particularly useful on paved aprons and aircraft stands intended for use in poor visibility conditions, where visual guidance from pavement markings alone may not be sufficient. ICAO identifies aircraft stand manoeuvring guidance lights for this purpose unless adequate guidance is provided by other means.

How We Select Them

Precision AGL Solutions supplies apron stand guidance lighting solutions selected according to:

  • Aircraft stand layout and centreline
  • Lead-in, turning and lead-out lines
  • Aircraft stand marking
  • Poor-visibility operating requirements
  • Light colour, optical coverage and spacing
  • Stop-position arrangement
  • Inset or surface-mounted configuration
  • Electrical circuit and switching
  • Environmental conditions
  • Project technical specification
The final configuration should always follow the approved aircraft stand layout, applicable aerodrome standard and project-specific requirements.

Function of Apron Stand Guidance Lighting

A Continuous Visual Indication of the Intended Route

The primary purpose is to provide a continuous visual indication of the intended aircraft route through the stand. The system can support three principal movement segments.

Lead-InGuides the aircraft from the apron / taxiway approach toward the aircraft stand.
TurningProvides visual guidance while the aircraft changes heading to align with the stand centreline.
Lead-OutProvides guidance as the aircraft leaves the stand and returns to the apron / taxiway route.

The lighting works together with the aircraft stand markings to make the intended route clearly identifiable.

Stand Manoeuvring Guidance vs Docking Guidance

Two Different Systems, Two Different Jobs

Apron Stand Manoeuvring Guidance Lights

Provide visual guidance along the stand markings and help the pilot initiate and follow the manoeuvre.

Visual Docking Guidance System (VDGS)

Provides more precise azimuth and stopping information where accurate final aircraft positioning is required.

ICAO material notes that stand manoeuvring lights can provide adequate guidance to initiate the turn and take up the stand centreline, but may not provide the azimuth accuracy or stopping guidance required for nose-in stands equipped with passenger boarding bridges. In those situations, a visual docking guidance system is used.

Apron Stand Guidance Lights are not a substitute for a VDGS where precision docking is required.

Intensity

ICAO specifies that the intensity must be adequate for the visibility and ambient-light conditions under which the aircraft stand is intended to be used.

Omnidirectional vs Directional

For lead-in, turning and lead-out guidance, the lights may need to remain visible across the relevant viewing segments as the aircraft changes direction. Older ICAO design guidance describes omnidirectional low-intensity yellow taxiway-type lights as a normal solution. The exact optical configuration should be selected from the approved stand layout and manufacturer's photometric data.

ICAO Position

ICAO identifies the VDGS as a separate visual aid and calls for one where a visual aid is intended to indicate precise aircraft positioning and alternative methods such as marshallers are not practicable.

The stand manoeuvring lights and VDGS may therefore operate together.

Stop Position Lighting

Treated Differently From the Yellow Route Lights

Stop-Position Lights

ICAO specifies stop-position lights as fixed, unidirectional and red.

The stop indication should correspond with the approved stopping position for the applicable aircraft type.

Multiple Positions / Aircraft Types

For stands with several parking positions or multiple aircraft types, the stopping arrangement may require additional control or VDGS technology.

Switching Arrangement

Switched ON When the Stand Is in Use

ICAO Switching Principle

The lighting circuit should be designed so the lights can be:

  • Switched ON → Stand in use
  • Switched OFF → Stand not in use

This allows stand guidance lighting to be operated according to apron utilization.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures

ICAO

Relevant provisions are contained in ICAO Annex 14 — Aerodromes, Volume I, under aircraft stand manoeuvring guidance lights. ICAO specifies that these lights:

  • Facilitate aircraft positioning on paved stands intended for poor-visibility use unless adequate alternative guidance exists
  • Are co-located with aircraft stand markings
  • Use fixed yellow lights for lead-in, turning and lead-out guidance
  • Use fixed unidirectional red lights to indicate a stop position
  • Use intensity appropriate to visibility and ambient-light conditions
  • Should be switchable to indicate stand use status
  • Use spacing not exceeding 7.5 m on curves and 15 m on straight sections

ICAO design guidance further distinguishes these manoeuvring lights from Visual Docking Guidance Systems, which provide the more precise azimuth and stopping guidance required for certain nose-in aircraft stands.

FAQ

Apron Stand Guidance Light — Frequently Asked Questions

What is an apron stand guidance light?

Apron stand guidance lights provide visual guidance for aircraft manoeuvring within aircraft stands and apron areas. They help pilots follow the designated stand entry, turning and exit routes and achieve the required parking position. They are particularly useful on paved aprons and aircraft stands intended for use in poor visibility, where pavement markings alone may not be sufficient, and ICAO identifies aircraft stand manoeuvring guidance lights for this purpose unless adequate guidance is provided by other means.

What colours do apron stand guidance lights use?

ICAO specifies fixed yellow lights for lead-in, turning and lead-out guidance. Where a light indicates the aircraft stop position, ICAO specifies a fixed unidirectional red light, and the stop indication should correspond with the approved stopping position for the applicable aircraft type.

What is the maximum spacing between stand guidance lights?

The maximum spacing is 15 m on straight lead-in and lead-out sections and 7.5 m on curved turning sections. Spacing may be reduced where required by aircraft type, stand geometry, visibility conditions, lighting performance, the project specification or operational requirements. The fixture layout should be developed from the approved aircraft stand drawing rather than a generic spacing schedule.

Where are the lights located relative to the stand markings?

Under the ICAO provision, aircraft stand manoeuvring guidance lights should be co-located with the aircraft stand markings, so coordination between the stand marking, centreline and light position is essential. The layout should also be coordinated with the lead-in, turning and lead-out lines, aircraft parking and nose position, wingspan, the taxi lane and adjacent stands, the passenger boarding bridge, fixed ground-service equipment and aircraft safety clearances.

Is a stand guidance light a substitute for a VDGS?

No. Stand manoeuvring lights help the pilot initiate and follow the manoeuvre, while a Visual Docking Guidance System provides more precise azimuth and stopping information. ICAO material notes that stand manoeuvring lights can give adequate guidance to initiate the turn and take up the stand centreline, but may not give the azimuth accuracy or stopping guidance required for nose-in stands with passenger boarding bridges, where a VDGS is used. The two can operate together.

How does a stand guidance light differ from a taxiway centreline light?

A stand guidance light is for aircraft stand manoeuvring, is typically yellow, sits on the aircraft stand following lead-in, turning and lead-out geometry, can include a red stop indication and is controlled according to stand use. A taxiway centreline light is for taxiway route guidance, is typically green, sits on the taxiway centreline and is controlled with taxiway AGL control.

How are stand guidance lights switched?

The ICAO switching principle is that the lights can be switched on when the stand is in use and off when it is not. Control may be through local switching, an apron control panel, ALCMS, a stand management system, airport operations control, central lighting control or maintenance control, and the actual switching philosophy is project-specific.

How are stand guidance lights powered?

A possible arrangement is a CCR or AGL power source feeding a primary series circuit, an isolating transformer, a secondary cable and the fixture, although the actual architecture depends on the project design. Before adding or replacing lights, confirm the existing circuit type, operating current, fixture electrical characteristics, transformer rating, number of fixtures, primary and secondary cable, connector type, switching method, monitoring requirements and control-system interface. Incorrect electrical selection may cause incorrect intensity, uneven illumination, circuit instability, transformer stress, premature fixture failure, switching problems or monitoring faults.

Why does fixture surface temperature matter for stand lights?

Stand guidance lights are installed within aircraft movement and parking areas, so the product should be evaluated for its interaction with aircraft movement as well as light output. ICAO design material notes that the surface temperature of the light fittings must be low enough not to adversely affect aircraft tyres that come into contact with them. Consider the LED thermal design, fixture surface temperature, inset installation, aircraft tyre contact, pavement loading and duty cycle.

What are the common faults with stand guidance lights?

A guidance light that is off can come from an open circuit, LED failure, transformer failure, connector failure, a cable fault or a switching problem. Uneven guidance can come from incorrect spacing, mixed fixture types, unequal intensity, optical contamination, fixture degradation or incorrect alignment. A stop light that is off, or an incorrect stop position, can come from a light-source or circuit fault, a stand-marking or aircraft-type change, fixture relocation, or a VDGS or control-system configuration issue.

What should be confirmed before ordering apron stand guidance lights?

Confirm the airport and project, aircraft stand identification and layout, stand marking, lead-in, turning and lead-out lines, stop position, aircraft types served, poor-visibility requirement, whether a VDGS is installed or planned, spacing for straight and curved sections, optical direction, intensity, surface-temperature requirement, series-circuit arrangement, isolating transformer, connector type, switching and monitoring, housing and installation depth, drainage, environmental rating, applicable standard and the photometric and compliance documentation.

Is it safe to work on apron stand guidance lights while the circuit is energised?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage, and opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death. De-energise the circuit at the regulator, apply the site lock-out / tag-out procedure, use the series circuit cut-out switch or an approved isolation device and work under the airport’s permit-to-work system. See the electrical safety warning on this page.
