TCL-241 Taxiway Centreline Light Failure

Symptom — A taxiway centreline light (or a defined group) fails to illuminate when its series circuit is energised, reported via pilot/vehicle report, ALCMS status, or routine night inspection.

Equipment / System

Taxiway Centreline Lights (TCL) — inset, green L-852E in-pavement fixtures embedded along the taxiway centreline at standard spacing, fed via individual isolation transformers off a dedicated taxiway centreline series circuit, in-pavement base cans (L-867), primary series cable loop back to the CCR.

Possible Causes
HighIsolation transformer failure at the fixture (open primary/secondary winding)
HighLamp/LED source failure inside the fixture
MedSeries circuit primary cable fault or bad connection between fixtures on the same run
MedBase can or L-823 connector failure — moisture ingress, corroded pin, or plug not fully seated
LowCCR at reduced/zero output — check whether other TCL fixtures on the same circuit are also affected
LowFixture physically damaged or displaced by tug/aircraft wheel loading, ground vehicle strike, or snow plough contact, given the taxiway's continuous traffic exposure
Required Tools
Clamp-on series circuit ammeterTrue-RMS multimeter (CAT III)1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Series circuit fault locator / TDRTCL as-built circuit and base-can layout drawingBase-can lifting tool and PPE for airside work
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate all airside access through ATC/apron control before approaching a fixture on an active taxiway.
  • Treat the series circuit as energised until confirmed isolated at the CCR and locked out.
  • Use FOD-safe tools and account for every item before leaving the pavement.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCheck the ALCMS/CCR panel to see whether this is a single light or part of a wider dark section, and confirm actual current output of the affected TCL series circuit.
  2. ObservationConfirm with a taxiway inspection whether only the reported fixture is affected or a longer run beyond it is also out — a single dark light with the rest of the run lit points to that fixture; a dark run points to the loop upstream of it.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated and LOTO applied, disconnect the fixture's L-823 plug and megger the isolation transformer primary and secondary windings.
  4. MeasurementInspect the base can for water, debris, or a displaced/damaged connector, and check the lamp/LED module for visible failure.
  5. DecisionTransformer or lamp confirmed faulty at this fixture only → isolated single-fixture fault, proceed under TCL-242. Fault traces onto the primary loop or affects multiple fixtures → route to TCL-254.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the fixture (transformer, lamp, connector) or to the series cable loop feeding it.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific failed component before replacement rather than swapping parts speculatively.
Test Points
TCL fixture isolation transformer (primary/secondary)
L-823 secondary connector and base can
Series cable loop, both directions from the fixture
CCR output current at the panel
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-energise the circuit, confirm the fixture lights at all intensity steps, and inspect the full run at night to confirm no adjacent lights were disturbed during access.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed isolation transformer or lamp/LED module.
  • Reseat or replace a corroded/damaged L-823 connector.
  • Repair the identified series cable fault.
  • Restore CCR output if the root cause was upstream rather than the fixture.
Preventive Action
  • Include TCL fixture and connector inspection in the routine night flight-check and periodic maintenance cycle.
  • Megger the series circuit periodically given continuous ground-traffic loading.
  • Maintain an accurate base-can and circuit layout drawing to speed fault location.
Related References

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30, FAA AC 150/5345-46 (L-852)

TCL-242 Centreline Light Dim

Symptom — A taxiway centreline light (or a group) is noticeably dimmer than neighbouring fixtures at the same commanded intensity step, without being fully dark.

Equipment / System

TCL fixture, isolation transformer, and lens/optic assembly on the taxiway centreline series circuit.

Possible Causes
HighLens contaminated with fuel/oil spillage, tug tyre rubber, or general grime accumulated from continuous ground traffic
HighIsolation transformer secondary output below rated value due to partial internal fault or incorrect tap
MedLamp/LED module degraded or nearing end of life without having fully failed
MedIncorrect or mismatched transformer rating installed at a previous replacement
LowSeries circuit current below the correct value for the commanded step, affecting the whole loop rather than one fixture
LowLens surface abrasion from snow plough blade contact or de-icing chemical exposure
Required Tools
Light output/luminance meterTrue-RMS multimeterClamp-on ammeterLens cleaning kit rated for fuel/rubber deposit removalIsolation transformer test set
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before removing or cleaning a lens or accessing the fixture interior.
  • Allow the fixture to cool before handling if recently energised at a high intensity step.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompare the reported fixture's apparent brightness against neighbouring fixtures at the same commanded step, ideally with a luminance meter.
  2. MeasurementConfirm the series circuit current at the CCR matches the correct value for the commanded intensity step, to rule out a circuit-wide cause.
  3. ObservationInspect the lens for fuel/oil residue, rubber deposit build-up, scratching, or degradation.
  4. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, check the isolation transformer secondary output and confirm its rated wattage/tap matches the design.
  5. DecisionCircuit current correct and lens clean but output still low → transformer or lamp/LED degradation. Lens found contaminated → clean first and re-check before replacing parts.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to lens contamination, transformer output, or lamp/LED degradation.
  7. Root CauseConfirm with a before/after luminance reading once the identified cause is addressed.
Test Points
Fixture luminance output vs. neighbouring fixtures
Series circuit current at CCR
Isolation transformer secondary voltage/wattage
Lens transmission condition
Root Cause Verification

Re-measure luminance output after corrective action and compare against neighbouring fixtures and the manufacturer's rated output for the commanded step.

Corrective Action
  • Clean the lens using a fuel/rubber-deposit-rated method.
  • Replace an isolation transformer with an incorrect or degraded rating.
  • Replace a degraded lamp/LED module.
  • Coordinate with apron/taxiway cleaning teams if fuel or oil spillage is the primary cause.
Preventive Action
  • Include TCL lens cleaning in the taxiway/apron cleaning cycle given fuel-spill and rubber deposit exposure.
  • Verify transformer rating matches the specified lamp/LED module at every replacement.
  • Track luminance readings over time to catch gradual degradation before it becomes a reported fault.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TCL-02 (Lens Cleaning and Inspection Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I (light intensity requirements), FAA AC 150/5340-30

TCL-243 Centreline Light Flickering

Symptom — One or more TCL lights flicker, pulse, or vary in brightness instead of holding a steady output at the commanded intensity step.

Equipment / System

TCL fixture, isolation transformer, connector, and the series circuit supplying it.

Possible Causes
HighLoose or intermittent connection at the L-823 connector, worsened by ground-vehicle and aircraft wheel-loading vibration
HighA partially failed or overheating isolation transformer with an intermittent internal short
MedLamp/LED driver instability or a failing LED module nearing end of life
MedIntermittent cable fault (chafed insulation making and breaking contact) rather than a solid open or short
LowCCR output instability itself rather than a fixture-level fault
LowMoisture inside the base can causing an intermittent leakage path
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeter with min/max holdOscilloscope or power quality logger for waveform captureThermal imaging cameraL-823 connector test adaptor
Safety Precautions
  • Treat intermittent faults as live until proven otherwise.
  • LOTO the circuit before opening any base can even if the light appears steady at the time of access.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm whether the flicker is on a single fixture or several, and whether it correlates with a particular intensity step, ground-traffic movement, or weather condition.
  2. MeasurementLog CCR output current over a period spanning the reported flicker to rule out a source-side instability.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, inspect and re-torque the L-823 connector, and megger the isolation transformer under light mechanical flexing to try to reproduce an intermittent fault.
  4. MeasurementUse thermal imaging on the connector and transformer during a live period to spot a hot connection consistent with intermittent high resistance.
  5. DecisionFlicker follows CCR current logging → CCR/source-side issue, route to Division 01. Flicker isolated to the fixture and reproducible by flexing the connector → connector or transformer fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the connector, transformer, lamp/LED module, or cable.
  7. Root CauseConfirm by replacing the suspect component and re-testing under conditions that previously produced the flicker.
Test Points
L-823 connector contact resistance
Isolation transformer under mechanical flex
CCR output current trend log
Lamp/LED module bench test
Root Cause Verification

Monitor the repaired fixture over several operating cycles, including varying intensity steps, before closing out.

Corrective Action
  • Clean, re-grease, and properly seat the L-823 connector.
  • Replace a transformer confirmed to have an intermittent internal fault.
  • Replace a failing lamp/LED module.
  • Repair the identified intermittent cable fault.
Preventive Action
  • Torque-check connectors as a standard PM step given continuous ground-traffic vibration exposure.
  • Include thermal imaging of connectors and transformers in the periodic PM cycle.
  • Trend CCR output logs to distinguish source-side from fixture-side intermittent issues early.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TCL-01 (TCL Fault Isolation Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-46 (L-852 series), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

TCL-244 Centreline Light Wrong Color

Symptom — A taxiway centreline, lead-on, or lead-off fixture displays a colour that does not match the specified pattern for its location, creating a guidance discrepancy.

Equipment / System

TCL fixture lens/filter assembly and lamp/LED colour source at a specific chainage along the centreline, lead-on, or lead-off zone.

Possible Causes
HighIncorrect coloured lens/filter installed at a previous replacement (e.g. a clear or white lens fitted where green is specified)
HighA fixture installed in the wrong location relative to a lead-on/lead-off alternating-colour zone, or vice versa
MedLED module fitted with the wrong colour source or an incorrect part number
MedLens cracked or missing, letting the underlying white lamp/LED source show through uncoloured
LowUV degradation or fading of the coloured lens shifting its apparent hue over time
LowSurface contamination causing an apparent colour shift without an actual component fault
Required Tools
Colour coordinate/chromaticity meterFixture parts catalogue and specification sheetAs-built lead-on/lead-off zone colour-sequence drawingReplacement lens/filter stock matched to fixture type
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before fixture access.
  • Treat a confirmed wrong-colour fixture as a safety-critical discrepancy pending correction, since it can be misread as an incorrect guidance cue by flight crew or vehicle operators; notify airfield operations.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the reported colour against the as-built drawing for that exact chainage — straight TCL, lead-on, lead-off, or RET zone.
  2. MeasurementInspect the fixture's lens/filter and lamp/LED part number against the maintenance specification for that location.
  3. ObservationCheck lens integrity (cracking, missing lens) and confirm the lamp/LED source colour independent of the lens.
  4. DecisionWrong lens/filter part installed → replace with correct part. Fixture installed at the wrong chainage relative to the zone boundary → relocate/reprogram per the zone drawing.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the discrepancy to the lens/filter, the lamp/LED source, or the fixture's physical position relative to the colour-sequence zone.
  6. Root CauseVerify chromaticity meets the ICAO Annex 14 Vol I colour boundary for the specified colour after correction.
Test Points
Chromaticity coordinates vs. ICAO Annex 14 Vol I colour boundaries
Installed lens/filter part number
Lamp/LED source colour independent of lens
Fixture chainage vs. zone colour-sequence drawing
Root Cause Verification

Re-check chromaticity after correction and confirm visually against neighbouring fixtures in the same colour-sequence zone at night.

Corrective Action
  • Replace with the correct coloured lens/filter or fixture assembly for the specified zone.
  • Relocate or reposition a fixture installed at the wrong chainage.
  • Notify airfield operations/NOTAM if the discrepancy affected published guidance information.
Preventive Action
  • Verify part numbers against the zone-specific specification sheet before any bulk lens/filter replacement.
  • Include colour verification in the periodic night inspection.
  • Maintain accurate lead-on/lead-off zone colour-sequence drawings.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TCL-04 (Colour Verification Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I (colour specifications), FAA AC 150/5345-46

TCL-245 Centreline Light Intermittent

Symptom — A TCL fixture goes fully dark and then comes back on unpredictably, rather than continuously flickering or remaining permanently out.

Equipment / System

TCL fixture and its feeding connector, transformer, base can, and cable segment.

Possible Causes
HighConnector plug not fully seated or working loose under sustained ground-traffic vibration
HighA marginal isolation transformer winding fault that opens intermittently under thermal cycling
MedA cable joint with a partial break making and breaking contact
MedBase can water ingress causing an intermittent short to earth
LowA loose CCR output terminal causing an intermittent circuit-wide symptom
LowCorrosion building an intermittent high-resistance path at the connector
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeter1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Thermal imaging cameraL-823 connector test adaptorCable fault locator / TDR
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before opening any base can, even if the fixture is currently dark and appears de-energised.
  • Treat the circuit as energised until confirmed isolated at the CCR.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationLog the occurrence pattern — time of day, temperature, and any correlation with ground-traffic movement or weather.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, inspect and re-seat the L-823 connector and open the base can to check for moisture.
  3. MeasurementMegger the isolation transformer and cable segment under light mechanical disturbance to attempt to reproduce the fault.
  4. MeasurementCheck the CCR output terminal for the affected circuit is properly torqued.
  5. DecisionFault reproduced by flexing the connector or transformer → component-level intermittent fault. Fault not reproduced but base can shows moisture → treat as a developing water-ingress fault (TCL-247).
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the connector, transformer, cable joint, or CCR terminal.
  7. Root CauseConfirm by monitoring across several day/night and thermal cycles before closing out.
Test Points
Connector seating and contact resistance
Isolation transformer insulation resistance under flex
Cable joint continuity
CCR output terminal torque
Root Cause Verification

Monitor the fixture across several operating and thermal cycles after repair before closing out the fault as resolved.

Corrective Action
  • Reseat or replace the connector.
  • Replace the transformer with the confirmed intermittent fault.
  • Repair the identified cable joint.
  • Dry and reseal the base can; re-torque the CCR output terminal.
Preventive Action
  • Standardise connector torque and seating checks in the PM cycle.
  • Specify moisture-proof base can seals in higher-traffic sections.
  • Include periodic CCR terminal torque checks.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TCL-01 (TCL Fault Isolation Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

TCL-246 Centreline Light Misalignment

Symptom — A TCL fixture is physically displaced or its beam is rotated away from the correct centreline guidance track, reported from a night inspection or a flight-crew/vehicle-operator observation.

Equipment / System

TCL in-pavement fixture and its base can mounting relative to the designed taxiway centreline geometry.

Possible Causes
HighPavement settlement or heave shifting the base can out of its original position
HighFixture struck and physically displaced by an aircraft nose/main gear wheel, tug, or vehicle contact
MedFixture reinstalled off the centreline mark following a pavement repair or resurfacing without a re-survey
MedBase can rotated during installation or previous maintenance, skewing the beam pattern
LowA resurfacing/overlay changing pavement level relative to the fixture lens, altering the effective beam angle
Required Tools
Survey/total station or centreline reference lineBeam alignment gauge or inclinometerFixture removal toolsAs-built taxiway centreline geometry drawing
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate airside access through ATC/apron control before working on an active taxiway.
  • LOTO the circuit before fixture removal.
  • Confirm no aircraft or vehicle movement is scheduled through the work area for the duration of the repair.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationSurvey the fixture position against the design centreline chainage and offset.
  2. ObservationWhere practical, visually confirm the beam direction is off the guidance line by observing from an approach angle representative of an approaching aircraft or vehicle.
  3. MeasurementInspect the base can for rotation, tilt, or settlement relative to its as-installed position.
  4. DecisionBase can position correct but beam rotated within the can → realign fixture in place. Base can itself displaced or settled → coordinate reset with civil works.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the misalignment to the fixture orientation within the base can, or to the base can position itself.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the corrected position matches the design chainage/offset by re-survey.
Test Points
Fixture position vs. design chainage/offset
Base can level and orientation
Beam direction vs. adjacent fixtures
Root Cause Verification

Re-survey the fixture after correction and confirm visual alignment along the taxiway during a night inspection.

Corrective Action
  • Re-set or realign the base can to the design position.
  • Correct fixture orientation within an otherwise correctly positioned base can.
  • Coordinate with civil works if pavement settlement is the underlying cause.
Preventive Action
  • Verify fixture position and orientation as part of post-paving reinstatement checks.
  • Include alignment spot-checks in the periodic inspection cycle.
  • Track settlement-prone sections for ongoing monitoring.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TCL-05 (Fixture Alignment Verification Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

TCL-247 Centreline Light Water Ingress

Symptom — Water is found inside a TCL base can, connector, or transformer housing during inspection or fault investigation, or is suspected from an insulation resistance reading.

Equipment / System

TCL base can, L-823 connector, and isolation transformer housing.

Possible Causes
HighDegraded or missing base can gasket/seal
HighCracked or improperly bedded base can allowing surface or groundwater entry
MedPoor local pavement drainage/grade allowing water to pool over the fixture
MedConnector boot damaged or not fully seated
LowHigh water table combined with a low point in the taxiway drainage design
LowFreeze-thaw cracking of the sealant in colder climates
Required Tools
1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Base can pumping/drying equipmentReplacement gaskets and sealsMoisture detection meter
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before opening the base can.
  • Treat standing water in an electrical enclosure as energised until the circuit is confirmed isolated and verified de-energised.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationIsolate the circuit and open the base can to visually confirm water presence and level.
  2. MeasurementMegger the transformer and fixture to assess whether moisture has compromised insulation.
  3. ObservationInspect the gasket, connector boot, and can body to identify the ingress path.
  4. DecisionInsulation resistance within acceptable limits after draining → dry, reseal, and monitor. Insulation resistance remains low after drying → component-level moisture damage, replace affected parts.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the ingress path to the gasket/seal, the can body, the connector boot, or local drainage.
  6. Root CauseConfirm with a re-megger after drying and reseal, and monitor through the next rainfall event before closing out.
Test Points
Base can gasket/seal condition
Insulation resistance, transformer to earth
Connector boot integrity
Local pavement drainage grade
Root Cause Verification

Re-megger after drying and reseal, and monitor through the next rainfall event before closing out.

Corrective Action
  • Pump out and dry the base can.
  • Replace the gasket or seal.
  • Replace a damaged connector boot.
  • Repair local drainage or grading if pooling is contributing.
Preventive Action
  • Include base can seal condition in the periodic maintenance cycle.
  • Prioritise low-point sections identified from drainage surveys for inspection.
  • Use sealant rated for the local climate and freeze-thaw exposure.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TCL-06 (Base Can Moisture Inspection Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-46, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

TCL-248 Centreline Light Optical Failure

Symptom — A TCL fixture illuminates but the lens, prism, or optical assembly is damaged or degraded, producing an incorrect beam pattern, spread, or reduced transmitted intensity.

Equipment / System

TCL fixture lens, prism, and optical housing.

Possible Causes
HighCracked or fractured lens from vehicle or tug wheel impact
HighPrism displaced or degraded, losing the designed beam spread
MedUV degradation clouding the lens, reducing transmitted light and distorting the beam
MedAn incorrect replacement lens/optic (wrong beam-spread part) installed at a prior repair
LowThermal shock cracking from de-icing fluid contacting a hot lens
LowSurface pitting from sand or grit disturbed by jet or prop wash
Required Tools
Field beam-check kit or portable goniophotometerVisual inspection lightReplacement lens/optic stockFixture part-number reference sheet
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before lens or optic replacement.
  • Treat broken lens fragments as a FOD hazard and account for every piece before leaving the pavement.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationVisually inspect the lens/prism for cracking, cloudiness, or displacement.
  2. MeasurementCompare the observed beam pattern against the design spread using a field beam-check where available.
  3. ObservationConfirm the installed lens/optic part number matches the specification for that fixture type.
  4. DecisionLens/optic damaged or clouded → replace with the correct rated part. Correct part but housing itself deformed/cracked → replace the full fixture.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the defect to the lens, prism, or the optical housing.
  6. Root CauseConfirm with a post-repair beam check that the corrected fixture matches the design spread and intensity.
Test Points
Lens/prism physical condition
Beam spread vs. specification
Installed part number vs. fixture specification
Root Cause Verification

Re-inspect and beam-check after lens/optic replacement, and confirm no fragments remain in the pavement area.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the cracked or degraded lens/prism.
  • Correct a mismatched optic with the correctly rated part.
  • Replace the full fixture if the optical housing itself is damaged.
Preventive Action
  • Verify replacement lens/optic part numbers against the fixture specification before installation.
  • Include lens condition in the periodic visual inspection.
  • Review de-icing fluid application practice near in-pavement fixtures.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TCL-07 (Optical Assembly Inspection Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-46 (L-852), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Appendix 2

TCL-249 Lead-On Light Failure

Symptom — A lead-on light — the taxiway centreline lighting extending from a runway exit onto the taxiway — fails to illuminate or shows an incorrect colour phase, reported from ALCMS, pilot report, or routine inspection.

Equipment / System

Lead-on lights — green (or alternating green/yellow within a runway crossing zone) in-pavement lights extending from the runway exit onto the taxiway, fed off the taxiway centreline series circuit or a dedicated lead-on section, individual isolation transformers, in-pavement base cans.

Possible Causes
HighFixture-level fault (transformer, lamp, or connector) at one or more lead-on fixtures, as for a standard TCL fixture
HighThe lead-on section's isolator/tie point separating it from the main taxiway centreline circuit has opened
MedA fixture positioned within an alternating green/yellow phase showing the wrong colour, masking the runway-exit guidance cue
MedFixture at the runway-holding-position end damaged by higher-frequency aircraft/vehicle crossing traffic
LowCCR at reduced/zero output affecting the wider circuit rather than the lead-on section specifically
Required Tools
Clamp-on series circuit ammeterTrue-RMS multimeter1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Series circuit fault locator / TDRLead-on zone as-built drawingBase-can lifting tool and PPE
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate airside access via ATC given proximity to the active runway exit.
  • LOTO the circuit/section before work.
  • Treat the runway-adjacent end of the lead-on run as within the runway strip safety area, requiring runway protection procedures.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm from ALCMS/CCR and a runway inspection whether the fault is isolated to a single lead-on fixture or the whole lead-on run.
  2. MeasurementCheck whether the lead-on section has its own isolator/tie point and confirm it is closed and passing current.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the affected fixture's transformer and inspect its connector.
  4. ObservationInspect for a colour-phase-specific fixture fault if an alternating green/yellow pattern is specified for that section.
  5. DecisionFault isolated to one fixture → replace the failed component. Fault affects the whole lead-on run → check the section isolator/tie point first.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the fixture (transformer, lamp, connector) or to the lead-on section isolator/tie point.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific failed component before replacement.
Test Points
Lead-on section isolator/tie point continuity
Fixture transformer and connector
Series circuit current at CCR
Colour-phase fixture condition where an alternating pattern is specified
Root Cause Verification

Re-energise and confirm the full lead-on run illuminates correctly in the correct colour sequence, cross-checked against the zone drawing.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed transformer, lamp, or connector.
  • Close or repair a faulted section isolator.
  • Correct a fixture installed with the wrong colour lens for its phase position.
Preventive Action
  • Prioritise the lead-on run in periodic inspection given its role in runway exit guidance.
  • Verify isolator/tie point integrity during PM.
  • Maintain an accurate lead-on zone colour-sequence drawing.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TCL-08 (Lead-On/Lead-Off Light Fault Isolation Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30, FAA AC 150/5345-46

TCL-250 Lead-Off Light Failure

Symptom — A lead-off light — part of the alternating green/yellow sequence guiding an aircraft from the runway centreline onto the taxiway centreline at an exit — fails to illuminate or breaks the required alternating pattern.

Equipment / System

Lead-off lights — alternating green/yellow in-pavement lights guiding an aircraft from the runway centreline onto the taxiway centreline at an exit, continuing the alternating pattern to a defined point before reverting to steady green taxiway centreline lighting.

Possible Causes
HighFixture-level fault (transformer, lamp, or connector) at one or more lead-off fixtures
HighLoss of the alternating pattern where a yellow-phase fixture fails and is misread as an all-green run, masking the runway-exit guidance cue
MedThe lead-off section's isolator/tie point has opened
MedFixture at the runway edge subjected to higher jet blast and FOD exposure
LowIncorrect lens colour installed, reversing the intended green/yellow sequence
Required Tools
Clamp-on series circuit ammeterTrue-RMS multimeter1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Series circuit fault locator / TDRLead-off zone as-built colour-sequence drawingBase-can lifting tool and PPE
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate airside access via ATC — lead-off lights sit within the runway exit path with active aircraft movement.
  • LOTO the circuit/section before work.
  • Use runway protection procedures for any fixture within the runway strip.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm from ALCMS/CCR and inspection whether the fault is isolated to a single lead-off fixture or the alternating pattern is broken across the run.
  2. MeasurementCheck the section isolator/tie point for the lead-off run.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the affected fixture's transformer and inspect its connector.
  4. ObservationConfirm the correct green/yellow colour sequence against the zone drawing.
  5. DecisionFault isolated to one fixture → replace the failed component. Colour sequence reversed or incorrect → correct the lens/filter.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the fixture, the section isolator/tie point, or a lens/colour-sequence error.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific failed component or colour discrepancy before correction.
Test Points

TCL-251 Curve Lighting Failure

Symptom — One or more TCL fixtures fail on a taxiway curve section, where fixtures are installed at reduced spacing to maintain continuous visual guidance through the turn.

Equipment / System

TCL fixtures on a taxiway curve section, installed at reduced spacing per the curve radius to maintain continuous visual guidance through the turn.

Possible Causes
HighFixture-level fault (transformer, lamp, or connector) at one or more curve fixtures — more consequential here since curve spacing already assumes closer intervals for guidance continuity
MedMultiple adjacent fixtures out on the curve creating a guidance gap larger than the design tolerance
MedCurve fixtures subject to higher mechanical wear from tighter turning wheel loads and scrubbing
LowBase can settlement on the curve from concentrated wheel loading during turns
LowIncorrect spacing from a previous repaving not restoring the design curve spacing
Required Tools
Clamp-on series circuit ammeterTrue-RMS multimeter1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Series circuit fault locator / TDRCurve section as-built spacing drawingBase-can lifting tool and PPE
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate airside access; curve sections often coincide with higher ground-traffic density at taxiway junctions or runway exits.
  • LOTO the circuit before work.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm from ALCMS/CCR and inspection how many and which curve fixtures are affected.
  2. MeasurementAssess whether the resulting dark gap exceeds the guidance continuity tolerance for the curve radius.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the affected fixture(s).
  4. ObservationInspect base can condition for wear-related displacement.
  5. DecisionGap within tolerance and isolated to one fixture → standard fixture-level repair. Gap exceeds tolerance or multiple adjacent fixtures affected → treat as priority repair given reduced guidance margin on the curve.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the affected fixture(s) or to base can/spacing issues along the curve.
  7. Root CauseConfirm restoration of continuous guidance by night inspection after repair.
Test Points
Affected fixture transformer/connector
Gap length vs. design spacing tolerance
Base can condition
Series circuit current at CCR
Root Cause Verification

Re-energise and confirm the curve section presents a continuous, evenly spaced light line at night, with no gap exceeding tolerance.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed transformer, lamp, or connector.
  • Reset a settled base can.
  • Restore design spacing if a previous repaving altered fixture positions.
Preventive Action
  • Prioritise curve sections in periodic inspection given the tighter guidance tolerance.
  • Monitor base can condition on high-turning-load curves.
  • Verify spacing is restored to design after any repaving.

High-speed taxiway centreline lighting — the longer connecting taxiway route beyond an RET or standard exit curve, lit to the same guidance continuity standard given the aircraft's elevated speed over a greater distance.

HighFixture-level faults distributed across a longer run, increasing the statistical likelihood of multiple simultaneous outages
HighMultiple adjacent fixtures out creating an extended guidance gap over a route where higher speed reduces the pilot's ability to visually compensate

  • Coordinate with civil works if recent pavement activity caused the damage.

TCL-254 Guidance Lighting Circuit Failure

Symptom — A circuit-level fault affects the shared taxiway guidance series loop, taking out multiple fixtures across the centreline, lead-on/lead-off, or RET sections fed from the same CCR.

Equipment / System

Taxiway guidance series circuit — the CCR-fed series loop supplying the taxiway centreline, lead-on/lead-off, and any RET/high-speed sections sharing a common circuit, including the primary cable route and all in-line isolation transformers.

Possible Causes
HighCCR failure or fault at the source
HighPrimary series cable fault (cut, chafed, or moisture-degraded insulation) somewhere along the shared loop, taking down every fixture downstream of the fault
MedA single shorted isolation transformer collapsing available voltage to the rest of the loop
MedA section isolator or tie point left open after previous maintenance, de-energising an entire branch
LowDamaged or crushed duct from recent excavation or heavy vehicle loading across the taxiway
LowStorm or lightning-related surge damage to circuit components
Required Tools
Series circuit fault locator / TDR1000V/2500V insulation resistance testerClamp-on ammeterFull circuit as-built layout drawingExcavation permit and utility locate request if digging is required
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the CCR before any circuit work.
  • Obtain excavation clearance and utility locates before opening pavement if the fault requires digging.
  • Coordinate extended taxiway closure with ATC/airfield operations given the circuit's shared scope.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm at the CCR/ALCMS whether the fault presents as a complete loss of output (source-side) or a partial dark section with the CCR still delivering rated current (downstream fault).
  2. ObservationCross-reference the extent of the dark section against the circuit layout drawing to identify candidate cable segments and branches.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the primary cable in segments from accessible pull boxes or transformer base cans.
  4. MeasurementUse a TDR or fault locator to estimate fault distance if a direct segment test is inconclusive.
  5. DecisionInsulation resistance fails on a specific segment → cable/joint fault in that segment. All segments megger clear but a specific transformer reads shorted → that transformer is loading down the loop.
  6. Fault LocationPhysically locate and expose the faulted cable segment or confirm the single shorted transformer.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the cause of the cable fault (mechanical damage, water tracking, age-related insulation breakdown) once exposed.
Test Points
CCR output current and voltage
Primary cable insulation resistance by segment
Section isolator/tie point continuity
Suspect transformer primary-to-secondary and to-earth
Root Cause Verification

After repair, megger the full circuit end-to-end, re-energise, and confirm every fixture on the loop — centreline, lead-on/off, and any RET/high-speed sections — illuminates correctly at all intensity steps.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the faulted cable segment.
  • Replace the shorted isolation transformer.
  • Close or repair the open section isolator.
  • Restore duct/cable protection where mechanical damage was the cause.
Preventive Action
  • Schedule periodic full-loop insulation resistance testing and trend results over time.
  • Maintain accurate as-built records of every joint, pull box, and repair splice.
  • Coordinate with civil works teams before any excavation near known circuit routes.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TCL-11 (Guidance Circuit Fault Location Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364 (insulation testing), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

TCL-255 Centreline Circuit Insulation Failure

Symptom — The taxiway centreline series circuit shows a degrading insulation resistance reading or a nuisance CCR earth-fault trip, without necessarily a hard dark section yet present.

Equipment / System

Taxiway centreline series circuit primary cable and its insulation system, including cable jacket, joints, and transformer windings across the loop.

Possible Causes
HighGradual moisture ingress into cable joints or pull boxes degrading insulation resistance over time
HighAged cable jacket cracking from UV/thermal cycling, particularly below pavement expansion joints
MedA single degrading isolation transformer winding developing a partial earth leakage path
MedMechanical damage (a nicked jacket) from a previous excavation that has since begun to fail under moisture exposure
LowCumulative insulation ageing across an older section of the circuit approaching end of service life
LowCCR earth-fault/ground-fault protection tripping on a marginal insulation resistance reading rather than a hard fault
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)CCR earth-fault trend logCable route and joint location drawingTDR / fault locatorMoisture detection meter
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the CCR before any insulation testing or cable work.
  • Treat a marginal insulation reading as a developing hazard rather than a nuisance — do not simply reset the CCR's earth-fault protection without investigating.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the CCR's earth-fault/ground-fault trip history and trend to establish whether the insulation resistance is degrading gradually or a step-change occurred.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the primary cable in segments from accessible pull boxes or transformer base cans, comparing each segment against its baseline/previous test value.
  3. MeasurementIdentify the segment or transformer contributing the greatest drop in insulation resistance.
  4. DecisionDrop isolated to one segment/joint → localised repair. Drop distributed across many segments → treat as cumulative circuit ageing and plan proactive replacement.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific cable segment, joint, or transformer winding contributing most to the reduced reading.
  6. Root CauseConfirm with a full-loop re-test that the reading is restored to, or above, the acceptable threshold.
Test Points
Segment-by-segment cable insulation resistance vs. baseline
Individual transformer winding insulation
CCR earth-fault trip log
Joint/pull box condition in the lowest-reading segment
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-megger the full loop and confirm the reading is restored to (or above) the acceptable threshold, and monitor the CCR earth-fault trend over subsequent operating cycles.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the identified degraded cable segment or joint.
  • Replace the transformer with the failing winding.
  • Reseal an identified moisture entry point at a pull box or base can.
Preventive Action
  • Schedule periodic full-loop insulation resistance testing on a defined interval and trend results year over year rather than waiting for a nuisance trip.
  • Prioritise ageing circuit sections for proactive cable replacement.
  • Seal known moisture entry points identified during PM.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TCL-11 (Guidance Circuit Fault Location Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364 (insulation testing), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30
