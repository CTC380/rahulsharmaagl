PAPI / VASI Troubleshooting

PAPI-291 PAPI Completely Off

Symptom — The entire PAPI wing bar (all units) is dark, with no red or white light visible at night despite the associated lighting circuit reportedly being energised.

Equipment / System

PAPI wing bar assembly (typically four units) and its dedicated series or multi-circuit feed from the CCR, including the local disconnect/isolation point and any photocell brightness control.

Possible Causes
HighCCR feeding the PAPI circuit tripped, off, or commanded to zero intensity
HighPrimary series or feeder cable open circuit or major fault upstream of the first unit
MedMain local isolating switch or circuit breaker at the PAPI enclosure open
MedPhotocell/brightness control system commanding the bar off in error
LowLoss of feeder power to the substation circuit supplying the PAPI panel
LowMain power supply cable to the PAPI panel de-energised upstream
Required Tools
Clamp-on series circuit ammeterTrue-RMS multimeterCCR panel status/logPAPI feed circuit diagram1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before any work, treating it as energised until confirmed isolated at the CCR.
  • Coordinate all airside access through ATC/apron control given the wing bar's position near the runway edge.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCheck the ALCMS/CCR panel for the PAPI circuit's status and commanded intensity step.
  2. ObservationConfirm whether any other runway lighting circuits are also affected, which would point to an upstream feeder or substation fault.
  3. MeasurementMeasure the CCR output current for the PAPI circuit.
  4. MeasurementCheck the local isolator/breaker and photocell control at the PAPI enclosure.
  5. DecisionCCR output present but nothing downstream → local isolator or primary cable fault, proceed to trace the feed. No CCR output → route to CCR/power distribution troubleshooting.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the CCR, local isolator, photocell control, or primary cable.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the root cause before re-energising the full circuit.
Test Points
CCR output current for the PAPI circuit
Local isolator/breaker state at the PAPI enclosure
Photocell/brightness control command signal
Primary cable continuity to the first unit
Root Cause Verification

After repair, confirm all units illuminate at the commanded intensity step and that the correct red/white split is observed from the approach path or with a collimating test device.

Corrective Action
  • Restore or repair the CCR feed to the PAPI circuit.
  • Replace a failed local isolator or breaker.
  • Correct a photocell/brightness control fault.
  • Repair the primary cable fault.
Preventive Action
  • Include PAPI circuit status in the nightly serviceability inspection.
  • Periodically function-test the photocell/brightness control.
  • Trend the CCR log for the PAPI circuit to catch a developing supply issue.
Related References

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-28 (PAPI Systems), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A, ICAO Doc 9157 Part 4

PAPI-292 One PAPI Unit Not Working

Symptom — One of the four units in the PAPI wing bar is dark while the remaining units illuminate normally, altering the red/white ratio seen on approach.

Equipment / System

Individual PAPI unit — lamp/LED module, isolation transformer or local driver, internal wiring, and base connector.

Possible Causes
HighLamp/LED failure within that specific unit
HighIsolation transformer or LED driver failure at that unit
MedL-823 connector or internal wiring fault at the unit
MedBlown internal fuse or local PSU/driver fault
LowMechanical or thermal damage inside the unit housing
LowUnit physically displaced, disconnecting internal wiring
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeter1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Spare lamp/LED module matched to unit typeUnit access toolsPAPI unit technical manual
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before opening the unit.
  • Coordinate airside access with ATC given the unit's position near the runway edge.
  • Re-secure the unit cover carefully to avoid disturbing the elevation/azimuth setting.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm which specific unit in the sequence is dark and note whether the adjacent units are unaffected.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, check voltage/continuity at the unit's isolation transformer or driver output.
  3. MeasurementInspect the lamp/LED module and internal connector for visible failure.
  4. DecisionUnit-level component confirmed faulty → proceed with unit repair. Fault traces to the shared circuit or affects more than one unit → route to PAPI-293.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the lamp/LED, transformer/driver, or connector.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the specific failed component before replacement rather than swapping parts speculatively.
Test Points
Unit isolation transformer/driver output
Lamp/LED module continuity
L-823 connector condition
Internal wiring harness
Root Cause Verification

Re-energise and confirm the repaired unit matches the brightness and colour split of the other three units, verified visually at night or with a collimating test lamp.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed lamp/LED module.
  • Replace the failed isolation transformer or driver.
  • Repair or reseat the connector.
  • Re-level the unit if it was disturbed during repair.
Preventive Action
  • Stock spare lamp/LED modules matched to the installed PAPI type.
  • Include a per-unit visual check in the routine night inspection.
  • Trend individual unit run-hours for proactive lamp/LED replacement.
Related References

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-28, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A

PAPI-293 Multiple PAPI Units Failure

Symptom — Two or more units within the same PAPI wing bar are dark or degraded simultaneously, while at least one unit remains functional, distorting the glide path indication.

Equipment / System

PAPI wing bar shared series circuit, feeder cable, or shared CCR circuit and the multiple individual units fed from it.

Possible Causes
HighShared primary series cable fault between the affected units
HighCCR at reduced output affecting all units on the shared circuit
MedCommon feeder or junction box fault
MedA batch of lamps/LED modules failing near-simultaneously from end-of-life
LowStorm or lightning-induced damage affecting several units together
LowRodent or animal damage to a buried cable run
Required Tools
Clamp-on series circuit ammeter1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)TDR / fault locatorPAPI circuit as-built drawing
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the entire PAPI circuit before work, since multiple affected units point to a shared circuit.
  • Coordinate airside access through ATC/apron control.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationMap which specific units are affected and their position in the wing bar sequence.
  2. MeasurementCheck the CCR output current for the shared circuit.
  3. MeasurementMegger the primary cable in segments between the affected units.
  4. DecisionFault isolated to one cable segment or junction → localised repair. Fault distributed with reduced CCR output → route to Division 01 CCR troubleshooting.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the cable segment, junction box, or shared driver.
  6. Root CauseConfirm with a full circuit re-test that the fault has been fully cleared.
Test Points
CCR output current for the shared circuit
Segment-by-segment insulation resistance
Junction box condition
Affected units' individual driver/transformer readings
Root Cause Verification

Re-energise the circuit, confirm all previously affected units illuminate at the correct intensity and colour split, and monitor over the following nights.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the faulted cable segment or junction.
  • Replace the affected lamp/LED modules.
  • Restore CCR output if the fault is source-side.
Preventive Action
  • Periodically megger the PAPI feeder circuit.
  • Inspect junction boxes for water ingress.
  • Stagger lamp/LED replacement to avoid batch end-of-life clustering.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PAPI-03 (Multi-Unit Circuit Fault Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364 (insulation testing), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

Rev 0 — Initial issue
PAPI-294 PAPI Wrong Indication

Symptom — Pilots report the PAPI indication does not match the aircraft's actual position on the approach path — a red/white ratio inconsistent with the true glide angle as cross-checked against other guidance.

Equipment / System

Complete PAPI wing bar — unit elevation settings, azimuth alignment, and colour-split integrity across all units.

Possible Causes
HighOne or more units mis-set in elevation angle, out of sequence with the schedule
HighUnit(s) rotated or shifted in azimuth so the observed colour split does not correspond to the true vertical angle
MedRed filter degraded or incorrect on one unit
MedGround settlement or frost heave altering the mounting platform level
LowA unit swapped into the wrong sequence position during maintenance
LowAn obstruction partially blocking one or more units' beams
Required Tools
Collimating theodolite / PAPI angle test setPrecision inclinometerAs-built elevation angle scheduleSurvey level
Safety Precautions
  • Notify airfield operations/NOTAM of a suspected wrong indication immediately given the flight-safety implications.
  • Consider a temporary PAPI unserviceable status pending verification.
  • LOTO before any mechanical adjustment.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationGather pilot reports and correlate them with the aircraft's known position using ILS glideslope or track data where available.
  2. MeasurementVerify each unit's elevation angle setting against the as-built schedule using a collimating test set.
  3. MeasurementVerify the wing bar's azimuth alignment against the runway centreline extended.
  4. DecisionOne unit's angle out of tolerance → proceed to PAPI-303 for elevation correction. Whole bar misaligned or rotated → proceed to PAPI-304 for azimuth correction.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the specific out-of-tolerance unit(s) or the whole bar mounting.
  6. Root CauseConfirm with a full angle survey after adjustment.
Test Points
Individual unit elevation angle vs. schedule
Wing bar azimuth vs. runway centreline
Red filter transition angle per unit
Mounting platform level/settlement
Root Cause Verification

Re-survey all units after correction, confirm each is within tolerance, and validate the indication with a ground-based verification check or follow-up flight check before restoring to service.

Corrective Action
  • Re-set the affected unit's elevation angle.
  • Realign the wing bar azimuth.
  • Replace a degraded filter.
  • Re-level the mounting platform.
Preventive Action
  • Include a full angle survey in the periodic calibration cycle.
  • Monitor the platform for ground settlement.
  • Lock elevation adjustment mechanisms after calibration to prevent drift.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PAPI-05 (PAPI Calibration Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A, ICAO Doc 9157 Part 4, FAA AC 150/5340-30

PAPI-295 PAPI Too Many White Indications

Symptom — Pilots consistently report seeing more white units than expected (e.g. three white/one red or four white) while independently confirmed to be on or near the correct glide path.

Equipment / System

Individual unit elevation angle settings, specifically the units expected to show red at the reported approach angle.

Possible Causes
HighOne or more units' elevation angle set too low, shifting the white sector into the correct-path region
HighRed filter faded or degraded, allowing more white-appearing light through the lower sector
MedColour transition mechanism (dichroic filter or red glass) misaligned within the housing
MedAn incorrect unit swapped into a position requiring a different angle step
LowMounting platform tilted forward, reducing the effective angle of all units
Required Tools
Collimating theodolite / PAPI angle test setRed filter transmittance referenceAs-built elevation angle schedule
Safety Precautions
  • Notify airfield operations of the suspected discrepancy.
  • LOTO before adjustment.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the reports are consistent across multiple approaches/aircraft rather than a single anomalous report.
  2. MeasurementVerify each unit's elevation angle against the schedule, focusing on the units expected to show red at the reported path.
  3. MeasurementInspect the red filter condition/transmittance where a unit shows white but its angle checks correct.
  4. DecisionAngle set too low on one or more units → re-set the angle. Filter degraded but angle correct → replace the filter.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the angle setting or the optical filter.
  6. Root CauseConfirm with a post-adjustment survey.
Test Points
Elevation angle per unit vs. schedule
Red filter condition and colour transition sharpness
Mounting platform level
Root Cause Verification

Re-survey the angles, confirm the colour transition occurs at the specified vertical angle for each unit, and validate with a follow-up ground-based or flight check.

Corrective Action
  • Re-set the elevation angle on the affected unit(s).
  • Replace the degraded red filter/lens assembly.
  • Re-level the platform if tilt is the cause.
Preventive Action
  • Include red filter transmittance checks in periodic calibration.
  • Lock angle adjustment mechanisms after calibration.
  • Monitor platform level over time.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PAPI-05 (PAPI Calibration Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A

PAPI-296 PAPI Too Many Red Indications

Symptom — Pilots report an excessive number of red units (e.g. three red/one white or four red) while independently confirmed to be on or near the correct approach path.

Equipment / System

Individual unit elevation angle settings, specifically the units expected to show white at the reported approach angle.

Possible Causes
HighOne or more units' elevation angle set too high, shifting the red sector into the correct-path region
HighWhite lamp/LED source dimming or failing in one or more units, making red appear dominant even at the correct angle
MedMounting platform tilted rearward, increasing the effective angle of all units
MedAn incorrect unit swapped into the wrong angle position
LowVegetation growth or an obstruction partially blocking the white portion of the beam
Required Tools
Collimating theodolite / PAPI angle test setCalibrated luminance meterAs-built elevation angle scheduleSite obstruction/vegetation survey
Safety Precautions
  • Notify airfield operations given the flight-safety relevance.
  • LOTO before adjustment.
  • Assess for obstruction hazards near the PAPI site during the survey.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm consistency of the reports across multiple approaches.
  2. MeasurementVerify each unit's elevation angle against the schedule.
  3. MeasurementCheck the white-source output/luminance where the angle is correct but red is still dominant.
  4. MeasurementInspect the site for vegetation growth or obstructions in the unit's beam path.
  5. DecisionAngle set too high → re-set. White source dim or failing → component repair. Obstruction present → clear the obstruction.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the angle setting, the white source, or an obstruction.
  7. Root CauseConfirm with a post-correction survey.
Test Points
Elevation angle per unit vs. schedule
White sector luminance/intensity
Line-of-sight/obstruction survey from the touchdown zone
Root Cause Verification

Re-survey the angles and luminance, confirm the colour transition occurs at the correct vertical angle, and validate with a follow-up flight check.

Corrective Action
  • Re-set the elevation angle.
  • Replace or repair the white lamp/LED source.
  • Clear the obstruction/vegetation.
  • Re-level the platform.
Preventive Action
  • Schedule vegetation control around the PAPI site.
  • Include a red/white luminance balance check in periodic PM.
  • Lock angle settings after calibration.
Related References

Rev 0 — Initial issue
PAPI-297 PAPI Indication Flickering

Symptom — One or more PAPI units flicker, pulse, or vary in brightness instead of holding a steady output at the commanded intensity step.

Equipment / System

PAPI unit lamp/LED driver, connector, and the series or feeder circuit supplying it.

Possible Causes
HighLoose or intermittent L-823 or internal connector, worsened by wind/vibration loading
HighA partially failed or overheating isolation transformer/LED driver with an intermittent internal fault
MedLED module nearing end of life with driver instability
MedIntermittent cable fault making and breaking contact rather than a solid open or short
LowCCR output instability itself rather than a unit-level fault
LowMoisture inside the unit housing causing an intermittent leakage path
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeter with min/max holdOscilloscope or power quality loggerThermal imaging cameraConnector test adaptor
Safety Precautions
  • Treat intermittent faults as live until proven otherwise.
  • LOTO the circuit before opening any unit even if it appears steady at the time of access.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm whether the flicker is on a single unit or several, and whether it correlates with a particular intensity step, vibration, or weather condition.
  2. MeasurementLog CCR output current over a period spanning the reported flicker to rule out a source-side instability.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, inspect and re-torque the connector, and megger the transformer/driver under light mechanical flex.
  4. MeasurementUse thermal imaging on the connector and transformer/driver during a live period to spot a hot connection.
  5. DecisionFlicker follows the CCR current log → source-side, route to Division 01. Flicker isolated to the unit and reproducible by flexing the connector → connector or transformer/driver fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the connector, transformer/driver, lamp/LED module, or cable.
  7. Root CauseConfirm by replacing the suspect component and re-testing under conditions that previously produced the flicker.
Test Points
Connector contact resistance
Transformer/driver under mechanical flex
CCR output current trend log
Lamp/LED module bench test
Root Cause Verification

Monitor the repaired unit over several operating cycles, including varying intensity steps, before closing out.

Corrective Action
  • Clean, re-grease, and properly seat the connector.
  • Replace a transformer/driver confirmed to have an intermittent internal fault.
  • Replace a failing lamp/LED module.
  • Repair the identified intermittent cable fault.
Preventive Action
  • Torque-check connectors during PM given continuous vibration exposure.
  • Include thermal imaging of connectors and drivers in the periodic PM cycle.
  • Trend CCR output logs to distinguish source-side from unit-side intermittent issues.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PAPI-01 (PAPI Fault Isolation Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-28, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

Rev 0 — Initial issue
PAPI-298 PAPI Unequal Brightness

Symptom — One or more units in the wing bar appear noticeably dimmer or brighter than the others at the same commanded intensity step, without being fully dark.

Equipment / System

Individual unit lamp/LED source, driver, and optical assembly across the wing bar.

Possible Causes
HighLamp/LED degrading gradually rather than failing outright
HighMismatched replacement lamp/LED wattage or part number fitted to one unit
MedLens/filter contamination — dust, oil, or insects — reducing the output of one unit
MedDriver output drifting out of calibration
LowA unit installed with older-generation optics/LED technology than the rest of the bar
LowPartial obstruction reducing the perceived output of one unit
Required Tools
Calibrated luminance meter/photometerLamp/LED part catalogueLens cleaning kitDriver calibration tool
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before unit access.
  • Use appropriate fall protection/lifting equipment for the unit mounting height.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the brightness difference visually at night and/or with a luminance meter across all units at the same step.
  2. MeasurementCheck the lamp/LED part number and rated output against the specification for the dim/bright unit.
  3. MeasurementInspect and clean the lens/filter, then re-measure.
  4. MeasurementCheck the driver output calibration.
  5. DecisionPart mismatch → replace with the correct part. Contamination → clean and re-verify. Driver drift → recalibrate or replace the driver.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the source, optics, or driver.
  7. Root CauseConfirm with a comparative luminance reading across the full bar.
Test Points
Luminance/intensity per unit at each intensity step
Lamp/LED part number verification
Lens/filter cleanliness
Driver output calibration
Root Cause Verification

Re-measure luminance across all units after correction and confirm readings fall within the acceptable balance tolerance between units.

Corrective Action
  • Replace with the correct-specification lamp/LED module.
  • Clean the lens/filter.
  • Recalibrate or replace the driver.
Preventive Action
  • Verify part numbers against specification at every replacement.
  • Include lens cleaning and a comparative luminance check in periodic PM.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PAPI-07 (Luminance Balance Check Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5345-28

Rev 0 — Initial issue
PAPI-299 PAPI Lamp Failure

Symptom — A specific PAPI unit's incandescent/halogen lamp has failed, causing the unit to go dark or noticeably dim, on a lamp-based PAPI installation.

Equipment / System

PAPI unit halogen/tungsten lamp, lamp holder, isolation transformer, and series circuit.

Possible Causes
HighLamp reached normal end of service life (filament failure)
HighVoltage/current spikes on the series circuit shortening lamp life
MedFrequent switching/thermal cycling accelerating filament fatigue
MedVibration from ground traffic or wind loading fatiguing the filament
LowIncorrect wattage/voltage lamp fitted at a previous replacement
LowPoor lamp seating causing arcing at the base
Required Tools
Spare lamps matching the specified type/wattageLamp changing toolTrue-RMS multimeterSeries circuit ammeter
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before lamp replacement.
  • Allow the lamp to cool before handling.
  • Avoid touching halogen lamp glass with bare skin, as oil residue shortens lamp life.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the specific unit and check the lamp for visible filament failure or blackening.
  2. MeasurementCheck circuit voltage/current at the unit's isolation transformer to rule out an overvoltage condition.
  3. MeasurementVerify the lamp holder contacts are not corroded or loose.
  4. DecisionLamp confirmed failed with normal circuit parameters → routine lamp replacement. Circuit parameters abnormal → investigate the transformer/CCR for overvoltage.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to lamp filament failure versus a supply-side overvoltage condition.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the new lamp holds correctly and circuit parameters are normal.
Test Points
Lamp filament continuity
Unit supply voltage/current
Lamp holder contact condition
Root Cause Verification

Re-energise and confirm the replaced lamp operates at the correct brightness and colour split, and monitor for a premature repeat failure.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the lamp with the correct-specification part.
  • Clean or repair the lamp holder contacts.
  • Correct any overvoltage condition at the source.
Preventive Action
  • Track lamp run-hours for proactive group replacement.
  • Verify lamp specification at every replacement.
  • Monitor circuit voltage for anomalies.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PAPI-02 (Unit-Level Fault Isolation Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-28

PAPI-300 PAPI LED Failure

Symptom — A specific unit on an LED-based PAPI system has gone dark, dim, or shows an abnormal colour, with the LED driver reporting a fault or no output.

Equipment / System

LED-PAPI unit LED array/module, integral LED driver, and internal control wiring.

Possible Causes
HighLED array module failure — individual diode or full array
HighLED driver (constant current source) failure
MedDriver overheating due to inadequate heat sinking or blocked ventilation
MedInternal control/communication fault preventing the driver from receiving the intensity command
LowMoisture ingress into the LED module causing a short
LowEnd-of-life LED lumen depreciation beyond the acceptable threshold rather than a hard failure
Required Tools
LED driver diagnostic tool/laptop interfaceTrue-RMS multimeterThermal imaging cameraSpare LED module and driver
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before opening the unit.
  • Discharge any driver capacitors per the manufacturer's instructions before handling.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCheck the unit for complete darkness, dim output, or abnormal colour, and note any driver fault indicator/status light.
  2. MeasurementConnect a diagnostic tool to the driver to read fault codes where supported.
  3. MeasurementCheck the driver output voltage/current to the LED array.
  4. MeasurementThermal image the driver and heat sink for overheating.
  5. DecisionDriver fault code or no output to the array → replace the driver. Driver output normal but array dark → replace the LED module.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the LED array, driver, or control wiring.
  7. Root CauseConfirm with a bench test of the removed component where practical.
Test Points
Driver output voltage/current to the array
Driver fault code/status
LED array continuity
Driver heat sink temperature
Root Cause Verification

Re-energise and confirm correct colour, brightness, and intensity-step response, and monitor driver temperature under normal operation.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed LED module.
  • Replace the failed driver.
  • Clear a ventilation/heat sink obstruction.
  • Reseal the unit against moisture ingress.
Preventive Action
  • Include driver diagnostic readout in periodic PM where supported.
  • Monitor LED lumen output trend for proactive module replacement.
  • Keep heat sinks clear of debris.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PAPI-08 (LED Module and Driver Replacement Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-28 (LED PAPI addenda)

Rev 0 — Initial issue
PAPI-301 PAPI Optical Failure

Symptom — A unit's beam pattern, colour transition, or beam spread is abnormal even though the lamp/LED source itself is confirmed functional, pointing to a fault in the optical assembly.

Equipment / System

PAPI unit optical assembly — primary Fresnel/collimating lens, red filter/dichroic element, internal reflector, and optical housing.

Possible Causes
HighCracked, chipped, or missing primary lens altering the beam spread
HighRed filter/dichroic element cracked, delaminated, or displaced from its mounting
MedInternal reflector misaligned or corroded, reducing beam collimation
MedCondensation or contamination between optical elements scattering the beam
LowOptical assembly loosened by vibration, shifting the colour transition edge
LowUV degradation clouding the lens and reducing overall transmittance
Required Tools
Optical inspection lightCollimating theodolite / PAPI angle test setReplacement lens/filter assemblyTorque driver for optic mounting hardware
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before opening the optical chamber.
  • Handle optical glass/lens components carefully to avoid further damage.
  • Reseal the housing to manufacturer specification after work.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the lamp/LED source is functional and rule out a source-level fault first.
  2. MeasurementInspect the primary lens for cracks, chips, or missing sections.
  3. MeasurementInspect the red filter/dichroic element for cracking, delamination, or displacement.
  4. MeasurementVerify the internal reflector alignment and condition.
  5. DecisionLens/filter physically damaged → replace the optical component. Reflector misaligned → realign and re-secure. Contamination present → clean and reseal.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the lens, filter, reflector, or housing seal.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the beam pattern and colour transition after repair using a collimating test set.
Test Points
Lens integrity and clarity
Red filter/dichroic condition and mounting
Reflector alignment
Beam pattern via collimating test set
Root Cause Verification

Re-survey the unit's elevation angle and colour transition after optical repair, and confirm the beam pattern matches specification.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the damaged lens or filter assembly.
  • Realign and re-secure the reflector.
  • Clean the internal optical surfaces and reseal the housing.
Preventive Action
  • Include optical assembly inspection in periodic PM.
  • Reseal housings with fresh gasket material to prevent condensation ingress.
  • Protect stored spare optics from UV exposure.
Related References

Rev 0 — Initial issue
PAPI-302 PAPI Alignment Error

Symptom — The PAPI wing bar as a whole, or individual units within it, are found out of tolerance during a routine or triggered survey, covering both elevation and azimuth deviations together.

Equipment / System

Complete PAPI wing bar mounting platform, individual unit levelling frames, and site survey reference points.

Possible Causes
HighGround settlement or frost heave shifting the mounting platform
HighA unit disturbed during maintenance and not re-surveyed before returning to service
MedMounting hardware loosened by vibration allowing gradual drift
MedSnow plough, vehicle strike, or wind-storm impact shifting a unit or the platform
LowIncorrect initial commissioning survey
LowSite survey reference monuments themselves disturbed, giving a false alignment reading
Required Tools

PAPI unit housing, door/access seals, cable entry glands, and internal drainage provisions.

  1. ObservationInspect the unit for fogged optics, visible moisture, or corrosion at the next scheduled or triggered access.

Symptom — A photometric survey finds the PAPI wing bar, or one or more units within it, outputting light intensity, colour, or beam spread outside the tolerances specified in the applicable standard, even though the units appear to operate normally.

Equipment / System

Complete PAPI unit optical and electrical output — lamp/LED source, driver/transformer output, and optical assembly — evaluated against the photometric specification.

HighGradual lamp/LED lumen depreciation below the specified minimum intensity
HighDriver/transformer output drifting below its rated current, reducing overall brightness
MedLens/filter contamination or degradation reducing transmittance

LowIncorrect intensity step commanded at the CCR for prevailing conditions rather than a unit fault
LowBeam spread altered by a damaged or incorrectly seated optical assembly
Required Tools

Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before any component-level correction identified by the survey.
  • Coordinate the survey with airfield operations if it requires a ground stop or runway closure.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the photometric survey report to identify which unit(s) and which parameter — intensity, colour, or beam spread — failed.
  2. MeasurementRe-verify the affected unit's output with a calibrated photometer and chromaticity meter under controlled conditions.
  3. MeasurementCheck the CCR intensity step commanded at the time of the original survey.
  4. DecisionIntensity below minimum → investigate the lamp/LED, driver, or lens contamination. Colour outside boundary → investigate the filter condition.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the source, driver, or optical component.

Chromaticity coordinates vs. colour boundary
Beam spread/pattern vs. specification
CCR commanded intensity step at time of survey

  • Replace the lamp/LED source.
  • Replace or recalibrate the driver/transformer.
  • Clean or replace the lens/filter assembly.
  • Realign the optical assembly.
Preventive Action
  • Schedule periodic photometric surveys ahead of the interval required by the applicable standard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about PAPI / VASI troubleshooting in the AGL Troubleshooting Atlas.

Q1What does Division 11 of the AGL Troubleshooting Atlas cover?

Division 11 is the PAPI / VASI Troubleshooting reference. It publishes 20 diagnostic topics, PAPI-291 to PAPI-310, for PAPI / VASI faults on airfield ground lighting systems.

Q2What information does each topic include?

Every topic follows the same diagnostic record: the symptom, equipment/system, possible causes ranked by likelihood (high, medium or low), required tools, safety precautions, diagnostic procedure, test points, root cause verification, corrective action, preventive action and related references (SOPs, standards, diagrams and case studies).

Q3Which PAPI / VASI faults are covered?

The 20 topics are PAPI-291 to PAPI-310: PAPI Completely Off; One PAPI Unit Not Working; Multiple PAPI Units Failure; PAPI Wrong Indication; PAPI Too Many White Indications; PAPI Too Many Red Indications; PAPI Indication Flickering; PAPI Unequal Brightness; PAPI Lamp Failure; PAPI LED Failure; PAPI Optical Failure; PAPI Alignment Error; PAPI Elevation Angle Error; PAPI Azimuth Alignment Error; PAPI Water Ingress; PAPI Heater Failure; PAPI Temperature Alarm; PAPI Power Supply Failure; PAPI Communication Failure; PAPI Photometric Non-Compliance.

Q4How does a diagnostic procedure begin in Division 11?

Each procedure starts with observation before any intervention. For example, PAPI-291 (PAPI Completely Off) begins: Check the ALCMS/CCR panel for the PAPI circuit's status and commanded intensity step.

Q5What safety precautions apply when troubleshooting PAPI / VASI?

Each topic lists its own precautions. For example, PAPI-291 (PAPI Completely Off) starts with: LOTO the circuit before any work, treating it as energised until confirmed isolated at the CCR.

Q6Which SOPs are referenced in Division 11?

Related references in the topics include SOP-PAPI-01 (PAPI Fault Isolation Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure), SOP-PAPI-02 (Unit-Level Fault Isolation Procedure), SOP-PAPI-03 (Multi-Unit Circuit Fault Procedure), SOP-PAPI-05 (PAPI Calibration Procedure), SOP-PAPI-06 (Obstruction Survey Procedure), SOP-PAPI-07 (Luminance Balance Check Procedure) and SOP-PAPI-08 (LED Module and Driver Replacement Procedure), and 7 more.

Where a standard applies, it appears in the topic's Related References. Those cited in this division include FAA AC 150/5345-28, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A, ICAO Doc 9157 Part 4, IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I and FAA AC 150/5340-30.

Q8How can I find a specific fault in Division 11 quickly?
