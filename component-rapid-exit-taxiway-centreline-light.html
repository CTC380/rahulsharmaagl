Rapid Exit Taxiway Centreline Light

A specialized centreline lighting system designed for high-speed taxiway exits that guides aircraft from the runway onto rapid exit taxiways at safe speeds. These lights feature a distinctive alternating pattern (green and yellow or closely spaced green lights) that alerts pilots to an upcoming rapid exit point.

Overview

What Is It?

A Rapid Exit Taxiway Centreline Light is a specialized centreline lighting system designed for high-speed taxiway exits that guides aircraft from the runway onto rapid exit taxiways at safe speeds. These lights feature a distinctive alternating pattern (green and yellow or closely spaced green lights) that alerts pilots to an upcoming rapid exit point. The lights are typically flush-mounted (inset) in the pavement and are arranged along the centreline of the rapid exit taxiway, starting from the runway edge and extending onto the taxiway.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

A rapid exit taxiway lets an aircraft leave the runway at a shallow angle without slowing to the crawl a standard 90° exit would require, which shortens runway occupancy time and increases the airport's landing capacity — but only if the pilot can find and track the exit path confidently at that higher speed. The alternating green/yellow (or closely spaced green) pattern exists specifically to give advance notice that a rapid exit is approaching, so the pilot can begin the turn smoothly rather than reacting late and overrunning or oversteering the exit.

Because this decision happens while the aircraft is still moving at meaningful speed, the lighting has to do more than mark a path — it has to communicate urgency and direction well before the geometry of the exit itself becomes visible, which is why the colour change stands out from ordinary green centreline lighting used elsewhere on the taxiway.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Rapid exit taxiway centreline lights are installed on precision approach runways at busy airports where efficient runway clearance is critical. They are used on:

  • Precision approach runways (CAT II and III) with high traffic volumes
  • Major international airports where runway capacity is a constraint
  • Rapid exit taxiway intersections where high-speed exits are provided
  • Runways with multiple rapid exit points for efficient aircraft flow
  • Low visibility operations where clear guidance is essential
Placement

Installation & Location

Fixtures follow the RET centreline's spiral/curve geometry from the runway centreline, through the turnoff curve, to the taxiway centreline, using the standard inset base can recessed into the pavement at each survey point along the curve. Spacing tightens through the curve compared with straight taxiway centreline spacing, so more fixtures and base cans are required per metre through the turn than on the following straight taxiway. Cabling follows the curved alignment in duct beneath the pavement back to the isolating transformers and on to the RET's own lighting circuit (kept separate from the parent runway and taxiway circuits so exit-light colour changes can be switched independently); setting-out is tied to the RET geometric design survey rather than a simple centreline offset.

Function

How Does It Work?

Rapid exit taxiway centreline lights operate as an integrated guidance system:

  • Identification: A series of lights (typically green and yellow alternating, or closely spaced green lights) are installed on the runway and taxiway to identify the rapid exit point.
  • Indication: The distinctive pattern provides a clear visual cue to the pilot that a rapid exit taxiway is approaching, typically starting approximately 300-500 metres before the exit point.
  • Guidance: The lights guide the pilot along the centreline of the rapid exit taxiway, ensuring a smooth and safe high-speed exit.
  • Integration: The system is integrated with lead-off lights, runway guard lights, and other AGL systems.
Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

An RET's control loop is kept electrically independent of both the parent runway edge/centreline circuit and the general taxiway circuit, fed from its own CCR (or its own switchable section of the ALCMS) so the exit lighting can be turned on or off as a single unit without touching runway or taxiway lighting elsewhere. Within that loop, the alternating green/yellow pattern is not something the control system switches in real time — each fixture position is wired and lensed for its assigned colour when the circuit is built, so illuminating the loop simply lights every fixture at once in its pre-set colour rather than requiring per-fixture colour selection logic. The CCR still regulates brightness across the whole loop in the usual current steps, and because the loop follows the RET's curved geometry rather than a straight run, spacing (and therefore the number of transformers and fixtures per loop) is tighter through the turn than on the straight taxiway sections either side of it.

Construction

Physical Construction

Rapid exit taxiway centreline lights are built as flush (inset) fixtures — FAA Type L-852B (≥1200 m RVR) or L-852D (<1200 m RVR) for curved, wide-beam sections — housed in an L-868 base can set into a cored or cast recess in the pavement, with the load-bearing top surface rated to carry the full wheel loading of the heaviest aircraft using the taxiway. The lens assembly, typically dual-colour to display green and yellow segments from a single fixture body, is a hardened glass or high-strength polycarbonate unit gasket-sealed to the housing to achieve an IP67/IP68 rating against fuel spillage, de-icing fluid, and standing water. Internally, separate LED clusters or filtered lamp segments behind the green and yellow apertures are wired to the isolating transformer in the same base can, with L-823 connectors sealed against moisture at the cable entry. Because these fixtures sit directly in the path of high-speed nose and main gear wheel loads at the exit turn, the base can and surrounding pavement interface are engineered to resist shear and impact fatigue, and the fixture top is kept low-profile and chamfered to minimise tyre scrubbing damage. Stainless-steel fasteners and corrosion-resistant internal wiring are used throughout given the fixture's continuous exposure to aircraft traffic and surface contaminants.

Prerequisites

Installation Requirements

Recess Coring & Base Can Setting
Civil

Core or cast the recess and set the L-868 base can to the RET geometric design survey rather than a simple centreline offset, holding the top flush and level to resist shear and impact fatigue under wheel loading.

Curve Spacing Set-Out
Civil

Set out fixture positions to the tighter spacing required through the turnoff curve compared with the straight taxiway sections either side, since more base cans are needed per metre through the bend.

Independent Circuit Routing
Electrical

Route the RET's cable loop separately from the parent runway edge/centreline and general taxiway circuits so the exit lighting can be switched as a single unit without affecting adjacent lighting.

L-823 Connector Termination
Electrical

Terminate and seal each fixture's connector to a submersible rating before backfill, given continuous exposure to fuel spillage, de-icing fluid, and standing water at this location.

Isolating Transformer Density Check
Electrical

Confirm the correct number of isolating transformers are fitted for the tighter fixture density through the curve, matching each unit's rating to the series circuit's current step.

Colour Segment Verification
Electrical

Confirm each fixture is wired and lensed for its assigned green or yellow position before backfill, since the alternating pattern is built into the fixture layout rather than switched in real time.

Wheel Load Rating Confirmation
Mechanical

Confirm the base can and fixture top surface are rated for the heaviest aircraft wheel loading expected on the taxiway, with the profile kept low and chamfered to minimise tyre scrubbing damage.

Pre-Energisation Sign-off
Documentation

Record base can position, cable route, connector seal, and colour assignment as-built before backfill, since these are not inspectable once the pavement is reinstated.

Upkeep

Inspection & Maintenance

Photometric Intensity Check
Annual

Verify beam intensity and colour against ICAO / FAA thresholds using a calibrated photometer; degraded output usually points to lens haze or an aging LED array.

Lens Cleaning
Quarterly

Clean rubber deposits and de-icing fluid residue from the inset lens so the green/yellow colour cue stays clearly distinguishable to flight crews.

Colour Filter Inspection
Annual

Check green and yellow filters for UV fade or discoloration and replace on schedule to keep chromaticity within the required boundary.

Alignment Verification
Semi-annual

Confirm each fixture's aim along the centreline using a digital level or alignment jig, since pavement settlement can tilt inset lights out of tolerance.

Seal & Load Ring Inspection
Annual

Check the inset fixture's seal and load-bearing ring for cracking or wear, since these units carry full aircraft wheel loads and must stay watertight.

Insulation Resistance Test
Annual

Megger-test the isolation transformer and series circuit to confirm insulation resistance stays above the minimum, flagging moisture ingress before it causes a series-circuit fault.

Cable & Connector Inspection
Annual

Examine primary and secondary cable connections for corrosion or loose crimps, a common source of intermittent series-circuit faults.

Pavement Surround Inspection
Annual

Check the pavement immediately around each fixture for spalling, cracking, or settlement that could affect drainage or create FOD risk near the centreline.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Lens Contamination

Rapid exit centreline lights are exposed to rubber deposits from aircraft tyres and de-icing fluids. Regular cleaning with approved solvents and soft brushes is required to maintain light output and colour visibility.

Colour Filter Degradation

Both green and yellow filters are susceptible to UV degradation and thermal stress. Regular photometric testing and filter replacement is essential to maintain correct colour indication.

Misalignment from Pavement Settlement

Pavement settlement can cause centreline lights to tilt, altering the beam pattern. Regular alignment checks using a digital level or specialized alignment jig are essential.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Single Fixture Dark, Rest of Circuit Normal
Likely Cause
Failed LED module, or an open isolating transformer (blown primary fuse-wire link or failed secondary winding) in the in-pavement fixture.
Diagnostic Steps
Confirm series loop continuity through the fixture's transformer primary; megger the transformer; check secondary voltage at the connector with the CCR energised.
Corrective Action
Replace the LED module if the transformer checks healthy; otherwise swap the isolating transformer and re-seal the L-823 connectors before reinstating the can.
Entire Series Circuit Dark / CCR Will Not Regulate
Likely Cause
Open-circuit fault in the series loop (severed cable, corroded L-823 splice, or a failed transformer acting as an open point).
Diagnostic Steps
Check the CCR fault/open-circuit indication first; loop-resistance test from the CCR output to isolate the faulted segment; sectionalise and megger cable runs to narrow down the break.
Corrective Action
Excavate and repair or replace the faulted cable section and connector; re-test insulation resistance before reinstating the circuit.
CCR Trips on Overcurrent or Ground Fault
Likely Cause
Moisture ingress at a fixture base can or connector has degraded insulation resistance, creating a short or partial ground fault on the series loop.
Diagnostic Steps
Megger the full circuit to identify the low-resistance segment; visually inspect suspect fixtures for cracked lenses, condensation, or damaged secondary cable jackets.
Corrective Action
Dry out or replace the affected fixture/connector, renew O-rings and gaskets, and confirm insulation resistance is back above the acceptable minimum before re-energising.
Wrong Colour Shown, or Green/Yellow Bleed
Likely Cause
Degraded or contaminated colour filter/lens allowing colour bleed between the green and yellow segments, or an incorrectly wired bi-directional fixture.
Diagnostic Steps
Run a photometric colour check against ICAO/FAA chromaticity limits from both directions of travel; inspect the lens and internal baffle for contamination or damage; verify wiring orientation.
Corrective Action
Clean or replace the lens/filter assembly, correct any miswiring, and re-verify chromaticity with a follow-up photometric reading.
Fixture Flickering or Intermittent
Likely Cause
Loose or corroded primary/secondary crimp connections, or a marginal transformer winding under thermal load from repeated high-speed turnoff traffic.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect and re-torque all cable connections at the fixture base can; check crimps for corrosion; monitor secondary voltage for drop under load once the CCR has warmed up.
Corrective Action
Re-terminate corroded connections with new crimps and sealant, or replace the transformer if voltage sag is confirmed under load.
Fixture Cracked, Loose, or Sitting Proud of Pavement
Likely Cause
Repeated tyre loading at the high-speed exit has fatigued the housing or its retaining ring, or the surrounding pavement/subgrade has settled or heaved around the can.
Diagnostic Steps
Visually and physically check the fixture body, lens, and retaining ring for cracking or play; measure fixture height against the surrounding pavement surface.
Corrective Action
Replace the cracked housing or lens, re-seat and re-seal the fixture flush with the pavement, and address any base can or subgrade settlement to prevent recurrence.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

LED Module Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Solder-joint fatigue from thermal cycling, moisture ingress through a compromised board seal, or ordinary end-of-life lumen depreciation in the in-pavement fixture.
Effect
Single fixture goes dark or drops below minimum intensity while the rest of the series circuit continues to operate normally.
Detection Method
Nighttime visual patrol, periodic photometric intensity survey, and continuity/secondary-voltage checks at the fixture connector.
Isolating Transformer Winding Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Moisture ingress leading to insulation breakdown, a manufacturing defect in the winding, or a blown primary fuse-wire link from a transient surge.
Effect
Open-circuit point in the series loop; can darken the single fixture or, if the open extends to the loop itself, take down the entire circuit.
Detection Method
Megger test of the transformer, secondary voltage check with the CCR energised, and loop-resistance testing from the CCR output.
L-823 Connector / Splice Degradation
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Improper seating or crimping during installation, gasket failure allowing groundwater ingress, or corrosion building up on the pins over time.
Effect
Intermittent operation, flickering, or a partial ground fault that degrades circuit insulation resistance.
Detection Method
Insulation resistance (megger) testing of the circuit and visual inspection of connectors at the fixture base can during maintenance.
Series Cable Insulation Breakdown
High Severity
Root Cause
Cable jacket damage from nearby excavation or rodent activity, age-related insulation degradation, or prolonged standing water in the duct run.
Effect
Ground fault or short circuit that trips the CCR or darkens the entire series loop.
Detection Method
Full-circuit megger test to flag the low-resistance segment, followed by sectionalised testing or time-domain reflectometry to pinpoint the fault location.
Colour Filter / Lens Contamination (Green/Yellow Bleed)
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Degraded or contaminated colour filter, internal baffle wear, or incorrect wiring orientation on the bi-directional fixture.
Effect
Colour bleed between the green and yellow segments, or the wrong colour shown from one direction of travel, undermining the go/no-go exit cue.
Detection Method
Photometric colour check against ICAO/FAA chromaticity limits from both directions of travel, and visual inspection of the lens and baffle.
Flush Housing / Retaining Ring Fatigue
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Repeated tyre loading at the high-speed exit fatiguing the housing or its retaining ring, or the surrounding pavement/subgrade settling or heaving around the can.
Effect
Fixture cracks, loosens, or sits proud of or below the pavement surface, creating an FOD and wheel-loading hazard.
Detection Method
Physical and visual check of the fixture body, lens, and retaining ring for cracking or play, and measurement of fixture height against the surrounding pavement.
CCR Regulation Fault
High Severity
Root Cause
Internal component failure (control board or output stage), a load mismatch after circuit modification, or a firmware/setpoint fault.
Effect
Incorrect brightness step, failure to hold regulated current across the loop, or nuisance tripping on healthy circuits.
Detection Method
CCR self-diagnostic/fault indication, output current verification, and a load-bank test to confirm regulation across all intensity steps.
Base Can / Pavement Settlement
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Poor sub-grade compaction at installation, frost heave, or drainage failure allowing water to pool and erode the surrounding pavement fill.
Effect
Fixture tilts or sinks out of flush tolerance, and cable connections come under mechanical stress from the shifted base can.
Detection Method
Visual and level inspection of the fixture against surrounding pavement, and a check of drainage paths during annual maintenance.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Own Circuit Isolation
Electrical Safety

RET centreline lights are wired on their own lighting circuit, kept separate from the parent runway and taxiway circuits so exit colours can switch independently; confirm which CCR feeds the fixture before isolating, then verify zero energy before opening a base can or handling connectors.

High-Speed Exit Traffic
Operational

Aircraft use this exit at higher speed than a standard 90° taxiway turn; work here requires ATC coordination and a NOTAM where the runway or exit is affected, since the exit's geometry leaves less margin than ordinary taxiway centreline work.

Flush Fit & Wheel-Load Tolerance
Mechanical

Each fixture must sit precisely flush with the pavement through the curve; a proud edge or depression becomes a wheel-impact point during a high-speed rollout.

Spacing Tied to Exit Geometry
Compliance

Spacing tightens through the curve to follow the RET's specific design rather than a standard taxiway interval; after any replacement, confirm fixture position against that exit's own survey.

Insulation Test Before Re-Energisation
Electrical Safety

After any cable, connector, or transformer work, megger test the affected loop before restoring power, since a residual fault at re-energisation can trip the CCR or darken the exit guidance line.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Do not leave a fixture dark, out of position, or with the wrong colour after maintenance; verify flushness, colour, and photometric conformance before closing out and cancelling the associated NOTAM.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Colour Transition Point Must Be Precisely Located
Installation

The green-to-yellow (or alternating) transition on a rapid exit centreline is a deliberate cue timed to the pilot's deceleration profile at speed; verify the transition point against the design distance from the exit after any resurfacing or fixture replacement, since even a small shift changes how much warning the pilot gets.

High-Speed Legibility Needs More Than Static Photometrics
Photometric

A fixture that passes a static intensity test can still be hard to distinguish at the closing speed a pilot actually experiences on a rapid exit; where possible, validate the colour-change pattern's legibility from a moving reference, not just individual fixture output at rest.

High-Speed Tire Loading Accelerates Housing Fatigue
Mechanical

Rapid exit centreline fixtures take tire loading at higher speed and more direct alignment than standard inset taxiway lights, and the associated impact and heat load shortens housing and lens life; put these fixtures on a shorter structural inspection interval than standard inset taxiway lighting.

A Single Failed Lamp Is Easy to Miss From the Cockpit
Inspection

A single failed lamp within the alternating colour pattern can be hard for a pilot to notice while decelerating at speed, unlike a dark gap in ordinary centreline lighting; treat the alternating pattern as needing full-pattern verification at each inspection, not spot checks of individual lamps.

Confirm Circuit Segregation From the Main Centreline Loop
Electrical

Where a rapid exit centreline circuit is fed from the same loop as the runway centreline lighting, fault isolation on one can unintentionally affect the other; confirm and document circuit segregation so maintenance work on one system doesn't inadvertently darken the other.

Higher Wear Warrants Glycol-Rated Seals
Durability

Rapid exit points near the runway see concentrated de-icing fluid application in winter operations; specify glycol-rated gaskets for these fixtures specifically and inspect them ahead of the general inset taxiway light rotation.
