01 · Overview

AGL in India at a Glance

Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) systems are critical visual aids used at airports for runway, taxiway, approach and aircraft movement operations.

In India, AGL projects involve multiple engineering disciplines including lighting design, electrical infrastructure, series circuits, Constant Current Regulators (CCRs), isolating transformers, cables, connectors, light bases, airfield signs, PAPI, photometry, testing, commissioning and maintenance.

The Indian airport sector includes a wide range of AGL requirements, from runway and taxiway lighting upgrades to CAT I/CAT II/CAT III approach systems, PAPI installations, photometric testing, control and monitoring systems and long-term maintenance contracts.

Airports Authority of India procurement records show continuing demand for specialist AGL services in India, including photometric testing and annual maintenance of AGL systems. For example, AAI published a 2026 annual maintenance contract for AGL and related E&M installations at Thoothukudi Airport, while other AAI tenders have covered field photometric testing at airports including Rajkot, Kushinagar and Surat.[1]

India's aerodrome regulatory framework is based on DGCA requirements together with applicable ICAO Standards and Recommended Practices. The Ministry of Civil Aviation reported that amendments were made during 2025 to CAR Section 4, Series B, Part I, Aerodrome Design and Operations, incorporating changes proposed by ICAO and requirements arising from airport development projects.[2]

This guide explains the AGL market and engineering scope in India, including AGL products, consultants, contractors, manufacturers, photometry, testing, maintenance and project execution.

03 · Runway, Taxiway & Approach

Runway, Taxiway & Approach Lighting in India

Runway lighting is one of the most important parts of an airport's visual-aid infrastructure. The exact system depends on runway classification and operating requirements.

Runway Lighting

Indian AIP publications show runway lighting configurations varying between airports, including PAPI, CAT II approach lighting, centreline and touchdown-zone lighting.[3]

  • Runway edge lights — define the lateral limits of the runway
  • Threshold lights — identify the threshold for arriving aircraft
  • Runway end lights — required visual indication at the runway end
  • Runway centreline lights — longitudinal guidance where enhanced guidance is needed
  • Touchdown zone lights — guidance in the touchdown area

Taxiway Lighting

Supports safe aircraft movement between runways, aprons, terminals and other manoeuvring areas. Arrangements vary with airport layout and operational requirements.[4]

  • Taxiway edge lights
  • Taxiway centreline lights
  • Stop bars
  • Runway guard lights
  • Turning/curve lighting where applicable
  • Associated signs and guidance systems

Approach Lighting

Gives visual guidance in the final stages of approach. For low-visibility operations the serviceability of specific elements can directly affect whether operations continue.[5]

  • Elevated approach lights
  • Inset approach lights
  • Crossbars
  • Centreline lighting
  • Sequenced lighting
  • PAPI and associated approach guidance equipment

Taxiway lighting may also be integrated into the airport control system so that operators can select the required circuits and intensity levels according to operational conditions.

Constant Current Regulators (CCR)

A CCR regulates the current in many airport series lighting circuits: it controls the series-circuit current while the output voltage varies with the connected load. For FAA-based equipment, AC 150/5345-10H is the current specification for CCRs and regulator monitors. For Indian projects, the equipment requirement should be set by the approved project specification, the applicable DGCA/ICAO framework and purchaser requirements.

  • Multiple intensity steps
  • Local and remote control
  • Monitoring and fault indication
  • Circuit protection
  • Integration with ALCMS

Airport Isolating Transformers

Isolation transformers are used extensively in series-circuit AGL and form the interface between the primary series circuit and each individual light fixture. For FAA projects, L-830/L-831 classifications apply according to frequency; for Indian projects, the approved project and authority requirements govern product selection.

  • 6.6 A and 20 A applications
  • 20/6.6 A configurations
  • Various wattage ratings
  • 50 Hz / 60 Hz requirements according to system
  • Environmental protection and watertight construction
  • Approved connector arrangements

AGL Cables & Connectors

Cable systems form the electrical backbone of airport series lighting. A cable that is electrically continuous but has degraded insulation is not necessarily acceptable — so commissioning should include both continuity and insulation integrity where required.

  • Primary and secondary cable
  • Primary and secondary connectors
  • Cable assemblies and terminations
  • Junction boxes
  • Circuit length and insulation requirements
  • Environmental exposure, mechanical protection and routing
  • Water ingress and connector accessibility

08 · AGL Photometry

AGL Photometry & Photometric Testing in India

AGL photometry gives objective measurement of the optical performance of installed lighting — and it is one of the strongest specialist-service requirements in the Indian airport sector.

What a visual inspection tells you

A visual inspection can tell an engineer: "The light is operating."

What photometry tells you

Photometry can answer: how much light is being produced, where it is being directed, what its optical performance is, and how that compares with the applicable requirement or previous baseline.

How to Select an AGL Consultant, Contractor or Supplier in India

The right partner should understand both airport operations and AGL engineering. The ideal capability covers Airport Operations + Electrical Engineering + Optical Performance + AGL Equipment + Commissioning.

Choosing a consultant

A consultant who understands only electrical engineering may not have sufficient airport visual-aid expertise; an airport specialist without detailed AGL electrical knowledge may not suit a technically complex lighting project.

  • ICAO visual-aid and DGCA aerodrome requirements
  • Runway, taxiway and approach lighting, PAPI
  • CCR systems, series circuits, AGL transformers
  • Photometry, testing and commissioning
  • Airport maintenance and technical specifications
  • Documentation: specifications, BOQs, drawings, test procedures, commissioning reports

Choosing a contractor

The strongest contractor is not necessarily the one offering the lowest initial supply price. AGL is safety-critical infrastructure, so lifecycle reliability matters.

  • Technical capability: can they install the complete system?
  • Product capability: can they supply compliant equipment?
  • Field resources: experienced airport technicians?
  • Testing capability: electrical testing, photometry, PAPI testing, commissioning
  • Maintenance capability: can they support the installation after handover?

Choosing a supplier

  • Product compliance with project and authority requirements
  • Manufacturing quality under a controlled system
  • Field experience at comparable airports
  • Factory and site testing capability
  • Datasheets, test certificates, manuals and drawings
  • Spare parts and technical support throughout equipment life
  • Long-term AMC or field service

18 · Compliance & Standards

Compliance and Standards for AGL in India

An Indian AGL project should be designed and delivered against the requirements actually applicable to that aerodrome.

DGCA

DGCA's CAR Section 4, Series B, Part I — Aerodrome Design and Operations forms an important part of the Indian aerodrome regulatory framework. The Ministry of Civil Aviation reported amendments to this CAR during 2025 to incorporate ICAO changes and address infrastructure-development requirements.[2]

ICAO

Relevant ICAO Standards and Recommended Practices provide the international foundation for aerodrome visual aids.

AAI

AAI's AIP publications provide airport-specific operational and infrastructure information, including runway lighting, taxiway lighting, PAPI, approach lighting and secondary power arrangements.[3]

Project specifications

Individual airport projects may impose additional technical requirements.

Manufacturer requirements

Equipment installation, commissioning and maintenance must also follow the applicable manufacturer's documentation.

The project-specific controlled documents determine the final acceptance requirement.

21 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Which are the best airport lighting companies in India?
There is no single objectively "best" AGL company for every airport. Selection should depend on the specific requirement — manufacturer, consultant, contractor, photometry specialist, commissioning provider or maintenance contractor — and on relevant airport experience, product compliance and technical capability.
How do I choose an airport lighting company in India?
Compare relevant airport experience, AGL technical expertise, product approvals, field resources, testing capability, photometry, commissioning experience, documentation and long-term maintenance support.
What does an airport lighting consultant do?
An airport lighting consultant can support AGL design, specifications, tender documents, technical evaluation, installation inspection, commissioning, photometry and maintenance planning.
What does an airport lighting contractor do?
An airport lighting contractor may undertake supply, installation, civil/electrical works, testing, commissioning and maintenance depending on the contract scope.
What is an AGL contractor?
An AGL contractor is a specialist company or project contractor responsible for installation and/or execution of Airfield Ground Lighting work.
What is an AGL consultant in India?
An AGL consultant provides specialist engineering advice and may support design, specifications, procurement, inspection, testing and commissioning of airport lighting systems.
Who provides AGL photometry in India?
Specialist photometry providers operate in India, and AAI procurement records demonstrate that airports regularly engage specialist organisations for field AGL photometric testing and reporting.[1]
What is runway photometry?
Runway photometry is the measurement of the optical performance of runway lights, including applicable intensity, coverage, colour and alignment characteristics.
What is taxiway photometry?
Taxiway photometry measures the optical performance of taxiway edge, centreline, stop-bar and other applicable taxiway lighting.
Who manufactures PAPI in India?
PAPI should be sourced from a manufacturer or approved supplier whose equipment meets the applicable project and authority requirements. Product availability and approvals should be verified for the specific airport project.
What should I check before buying a PAPI?
Check optical performance, colour, intensity, beam geometry, aiming mechanism, environmental rating, electrical compatibility, monitoring capability, applicable certification and local technical support.
What standards apply to AGL projects in India?
Projects may be governed by the applicable DGCA aerodrome requirements, ICAO provisions, airport-specific specifications, project standards and equipment requirements. DGCA's CAR Section 4, Series B, Part I is a key Indian aerodrome regulatory reference, and the framework was amended during 2025.[2]
Why is photometry important for Indian airports?
Photometry provides measured evidence of optical performance and can identify low intensity, beam misalignment, colour issues and other deficiencies that may not be obvious during visual inspection.
Does AGL require maintenance after commissioning?
Yes. AGL is operational infrastructure and requires an ongoing programme covering inspection, cleaning, electrical testing, photometry, corrective maintenance and documentation.
Can the same company handle AGL design, supply, installation and commissioning?
Yes. Some AGL organisations provide multiple services, while others specialise in one stage such as photometry or equipment manufacturing. The appropriate model depends on the project procurement strategy.
What is SITC in airport lighting?
SITC generally refers to Supply, Installation, Testing and Commissioning. An SITC AGL contract can cover a complete project from equipment supply through final commissioning.
