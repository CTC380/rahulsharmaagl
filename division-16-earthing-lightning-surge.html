Earthing / Lightning / Surge Troubleshooting

Earthing, lightning, and surge protection fault diagnosis across the AGL system — earth resistance, bonding, and grid continuity faults; direct and induced lightning damage; surge protection device failure and repeated/recurring SPD failure; surge-induced damage to CCRs, LED fixtures, and communication links; and earthing-referenced nuisance alarms and ground potential difference problems between separately earthed points. Each topic follows the full engineering diagnostic record: symptom → observation → measurement → decision → fault location → root cause → verification → corrective action → preventive action.

ELS-411 High Earth Resistance

Symptom — A scheduled or fault-investigation earth resistance test on an AGL earth electrode or grid returns a value above the design/regulatory threshold, without any equipment fault yet reported.

Equipment / System

Individual earth electrode, earth rod group, or the CCR/base-can earthing grid, as measured at the earth pit or test link.

Possible Causes
HighSoil drying out seasonally, raising resistivity, particularly in sandy or rocky ground with no moisture retention
HighCorroded or degraded electrode reducing effective contact area with the soil
MedOriginal electrode/grid undersized for the site's actual soil resistivity, marginal since installation
MedA broken or disconnected bonding conductor between electrodes in a multi-rod grid, effectively reducing the grid to fewer active rods
LowTest method error — incorrect probe spacing/placement for the fall-of-potential method, or interference from nearby buried metalwork
LowRecent excavation or construction near the electrode disturbing soil contact
Required Tools
Earth resistance tester (fall-of-potential / clamp-on method)Soil resistivity meterAs-built earthing/grounding drawingEarth pit inspection tools
Safety Precautions
  • De-energise and isolate associated equipment before working in the earth pit where practical.
  • Treat all earth conductors as potentially carrying fault or lightning current — do not disconnect a sole earth path without an alternative in place.
  • Be alert to buried services when opening earth pits.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the test was performed correctly — correct method (fall-of-potential vs. clamp-on), correct probe spacing, and away from interfering buried metalwork or parallel earth paths.
  2. MeasurementRepeat the test and compare against the site's historical readings and the original commissioning value to establish whether this is a genuine rise or a seasonal/test variation.
  3. ObservationInspect the earth pit for electrode corrosion, broken bonding conductors, or physical disturbance.
  4. DecisionReading confirmed genuinely elevated and no test-method explanation found → proceed to grid continuity testing (ELS-416) and consider soil treatment or supplementary electrodes. Reading explained by seasonal dryness and within seasonal historical range → log and re-test after the next wet period.
  5. Root CauseDetermine whether the rise reflects a genuine degradation of the electrode/grid or a normal seasonal/environmental variation.
Test Points
Earth resistance value vs. design threshold and historical trend
Soil resistivity at the electrode location
Electrode/bonding conductor visual condition
Test method and probe placement used
Root Cause Verification

Re-test using the correct method after any corrective action, and confirm the reading is stable and within threshold across at least two independent measurements.

Corrective Action
  • Add supplementary earth electrodes or extend the grid if the design margin is confirmed insufficient.
  • Repair or replace a broken bonding conductor found in the grid.
  • Apply soil treatment (e.g. earth enhancement compound) at electrodes in high-resistivity soil where supplementary electrodes are impractical.
Preventive Action
  • Test earth resistance on a defined seasonal schedule (typically pre- and post-monsoon) and trend results rather than testing once a year at an arbitrary time.
  • Maintain an accurate earthing/grounding as-built drawing showing every electrode and bonding connection.
  • Flag sites with a history of marginal readings for supplementary electrode installation during the next major maintenance cycle.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ELS-01 (Earth Resistance Test Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IEEE 80, IS 3043 (earthing code of practice), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

ELS-412 Poor Equipment Earthing

Symptom — An individual item of AGL equipment (CCR cabinet, transformer housing, base can, or fixture body) is found to have an earth connection that does not meet design resistance or continuity requirements, even though the site's main earth grid tests acceptably.

Equipment / System

Equipment earth bonding conductor from the item in question back to the nearest earth grid connection point or electrode.

Possible Causes
HighEquipment earth bonding conductor never properly connected at installation, or disconnected during a later modification and not reinstated
HighBonding conductor undersized or of an incorrect type for the equipment and fault current expected
MedPaint, corrosion, or a non-conductive coating at the bonding point preventing a low-resistance connection despite the conductor being physically present
MedEquipment earth stud, lug, or terminal damaged or missing
LowEquipment relocated or replaced without re-establishing its earth bond
Required Tools
Continuity/low-resistance ohmmeterEarth bond testerAs-built equipment earthing scheduleWire brush/abrasive pad for bonding surface preparation
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the associated circuit before working on equipment earthing.
  • Do not assume equipment is safely earthed based on appearance alone — verify by test before working on or near it.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationIdentify the specific item of equipment reported or found with a suspect earth connection, and locate its bonding conductor on the as-built earthing schedule.
  2. MeasurementTest continuity/resistance of the bonding conductor from the equipment to the nearest confirmed earth grid connection point.
  3. ObservationInspect the bonding point at both ends for corrosion, paint/coating, looseness, or physical damage.
  4. DecisionBonding conductor confirmed absent, damaged, or high-resistance → repair or reinstate the bond. Bonding conductor tests correctly despite the report → investigate whether the original report was based on a different cause (e.g. a nuisance earth-fault trip unrelated to this equipment's own bond).
  5. Root CauseConfirm whether the poor bond was never correctly made, has degraded over time, or was disturbed during other work.
Test Points
Equipment bonding conductor continuity/resistance
Bonding point surface condition at both ends
Equipment earth stud/lug condition
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-test the bonding conductor's continuity/resistance and confirm it meets the design requirement, and visually confirm a sound mechanical connection at both ends.

Corrective Action
  • Reinstate or replace the missing or damaged bonding conductor.
  • Clean bonding surfaces to bare metal before remaking the connection, and apply an appropriate anti-corrosion treatment after.
  • Replace a damaged or missing earth stud/lug on the equipment.
Preventive Action
  • Include equipment earth bond verification in the periodic maintenance cycle for all major AGL equipment items.
  • Require earth bond verification as a mandatory step whenever equipment is relocated, replaced, or disturbed during other work.
  • Maintain an accurate equipment earthing schedule cross-referenced to the main earthing drawing.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ELS-02 (Equipment Earth Bond Verification Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IS 3043, IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

ELS-413 Broken Earth Connection

Symptom — An earth conductor that was previously intact is found to be physically broken or completely separated, removing that path from the earthing system entirely, whether or not it has yet caused a reported symptom.

Equipment / System

Earth conductor between an electrode, bonding point, or equipment and the rest of the earthing grid.

Possible Causes
HighMechanical damage from excavation, ground vehicle activity, or construction work severing a buried or exposed earth conductor
HighConductor fatigue failure at a flex point or termination from repeated vibration
MedCorrosion progressing to complete separation at a joint or termination (see ELS-415)
MedTheft or unauthorised removal of exposed earth conductor (a known risk for copper earthing material)
LowIncorrect conductor specified or installed originally, failing prematurely under normal conditions
Required Tools
Continuity/low-resistance ohmmeterEarth resistance testerAs-built earthing drawingCable/conductor repair and termination kit
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO associated equipment before working on the earthing system.
  • Confirm an alternative earth path is in place, or equipment is de-energised, before disconnecting or working on the only remaining earth conductor to an item.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationUse the as-built earthing drawing to trace the conductor's intended route between its two end points.
  2. MeasurementTest continuity end-to-end; a break will read as open circuit or very high resistance.
  3. ObservationPhysically trace the route (visually where exposed, or by careful excavation where buried, following correct utility-locate procedure) to find the point of separation.
  4. DecisionBreak located and accessible → repair or replace the conductor. Break suspected but not located along an accessible route → consider installing a new conductor along an accessible path rather than extended excavation.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the exact point and cause of the break.
  6. Root CauseDetermine whether the cause was mechanical damage, fatigue, corrosion, or theft, to inform the repair method and any site security measures needed.
Test Points
End-to-end continuity of the conductor
Visual condition along the accessible route
Overall earth resistance before and after repair
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-test end-to-end continuity and overall earth resistance, and confirm the reading is restored to its expected baseline value.

Corrective Action
  • Repair the break using a correctly rated, mechanically sound joint, or replace the full conductor run if the break location is inaccessible or the conductor is otherwise degraded.
  • Reinstate any excavation per airport engineering standards.
  • Report suspected theft to site security and consider tamper-resistant conductor routing at exposed locations.
Preventive Action
  • Include visual inspection of exposed earth conductor runs in the periodic maintenance cycle.
  • Route or conceal exposed earth conductor to reduce vulnerability to accidental damage or theft where practical.
  • Coordinate with airport works teams to flag earthing conductor routes before any excavation nearby.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ELS-03 (Earth Conductor Fault Location and Repair Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IS 3043, IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

ELS-414 Loose Earth Connection

Symptom — An earth conductor termination — at an electrode, bonding bar, equipment lug, or test link — is mechanically loose, giving an inconsistent or marginal continuity/resistance reading depending on how the connection happens to be seated at the time of test.

Equipment / System

Earth conductor termination point at an electrode, earth bar, equipment earth stud, or test/disconnect link.

Possible Causes
HighTermination bolt/nut not correctly torqued at original installation or a previous repair
HighVibration from ground-traffic loading or equipment operation gradually loosening a mechanical termination over time
MedThermal cycling at the termination causing the connection to loosen incrementally
MedIncorrect lug or termination hardware used, not properly matched to the conductor size
LowA test/disconnect link left partially open after a previous test and not fully reclosed
Required Tools
Torque wrench appropriate to the termination hardwareContinuity/low-resistance ohmmeterThermal imaging camera (for terminations carrying load current)Termination hardware kit
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before working on the connection.
  • Treat a loose high-current earth termination as a potential heating/arcing hazard, particularly if it has been carrying fault or surge current.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationPhysically inspect and attempt to gently move/flex the termination to check for looseness, comparing against a correctly torqued reference connection.
  2. MeasurementTest continuity/resistance both in the as-found (loose) condition and after re-torquing, to confirm looseness was the cause of any marginal reading.
  3. ObservationCheck for signs of arcing, discolouration, or heat damage at the termination, which would indicate the looseness has already caused electrical stress.
  4. DecisionLooseness confirmed as the cause and no thermal damage found → clean and re-torque the connection. Thermal damage or arcing evidence found → replace the termination hardware and inspect the conductor end for damage.
  5. Root CauseConfirm whether the looseness is an isolated installation/torque issue or indicative of a broader vibration exposure affecting nearby terminations too.
Test Points
Termination torque value vs. specification
Continuity/resistance before and after re-torquing
Termination condition for signs of arcing or heat damage
Root Cause Verification

After re-torquing or replacement, re-test continuity/resistance and confirm the reading is stable across repeated gentle flexing of the connection.

Corrective Action
  • Clean the termination surfaces and re-torque to the manufacturer's specification.
  • Replace termination hardware showing signs of arcing, heat damage, or incorrect original specification.
  • Fully reclose and secure any test/disconnect link after testing.
Preventive Action
  • Include termination torque-checking in the periodic maintenance cycle, particularly for terminations subject to vibration.
  • Use anti-loosening hardware (e.g. spring washers, thread-locking compound where appropriate) at terminations known to be vibration-exposed.
  • Document torque values applied at every earthing termination made or remade.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ELS-04 (Earth Termination Inspection and Torque Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IS 3043, IEC 60364

ELS-415 Corroded Earth Connection

Symptom — An earth conductor, electrode, or termination shows visible corrosion, and testing confirms an elevated resistance or continuity reading attributable to the corrosion reducing effective contact area.

Equipment / System

Earth electrode, conductor, or termination point, particularly at below-grade or moisture-exposed locations.

Possible Causes
HighGalvanic corrosion from dissimilar metals in contact at a termination (e.g. copper conductor against a steel or aluminium component without correct transition hardware)
HighProlonged moisture exposure at a below-grade or poorly sealed termination
MedAggressive soil chemistry at the electrode location accelerating corrosion beyond normal expected rates
MedAbsence of, or failed, corrosion-protective coating or compound at the termination
LowStray DC current in the vicinity (e.g. from nearby cathodic protection systems) accelerating corrosion
Required Tools
Continuity/low-resistance ohmmeterEarth resistance testerWire brush/abrasive tools for corrosion removalAnti-corrosion compound/coating rated for earthing connectionsReplacement electrode/termination hardware
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before disturbing a corroded connection that may be a sole earth path.
  • Be aware corroded connections can be structurally weakened — handle with care to avoid unexpected mechanical failure during inspection.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationVisually inspect the electrode, conductor, and termination for corrosion extent and colour/type, which can indicate the corrosion mechanism (galvanic vs. general).
  2. MeasurementTest continuity/resistance across the corroded point and compare against an equivalent uncorroded reference connection elsewhere on the grid.
  3. ObservationCheck for dissimilar-metal contact or missing transition hardware at the termination.
  4. DecisionCorrosion superficial and resistance within acceptable limits → clean and protect, monitor at next cycle. Corrosion has significantly reduced effective cross-section or resistance is elevated → replace the affected electrode/conductor/termination.
  5. Root CauseIdentify the corrosion mechanism — galvanic, soil chemistry, moisture, or stray current — to prevent recurrence at the same or similar locations.
Test Points
Continuity/resistance across the corroded point vs. reference
Visual extent and type of corrosion
Presence/absence of dissimilar-metal transition hardware
Root Cause Verification

After cleaning/protection or replacement, re-test continuity/resistance and confirm the reading is restored to within the acceptable range, and re-inspect at the next scheduled cycle to confirm corrosion has not progressed further.

Corrective Action
  • Clean corrosion back to sound metal and apply an appropriate anti-corrosion compound or coating.
  • Replace an electrode, conductor, or termination where corrosion has significantly reduced effective cross-section.
  • Install correct dissimilar-metal transition hardware where galvanic corrosion is the identified cause.
Preventive Action
  • Include visual corrosion inspection of accessible earth connections in the periodic maintenance cycle.
  • Use correctly rated, corrosion-resistant materials and transition hardware at every new earthing installation or repair.
  • In known aggressive-soil areas, consider corrosion-resistant electrode types or cathodic protection measures at design stage.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ELS-05 (Earth Connection Corrosion Assessment Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IS 3043, IEEE 80, IEC 60364

ELS-416 Earth Grid Continuity Failure

Symptom — Testing of the earthing grid as a whole (multiple electrodes and bonding conductors intended to act as a single low-resistance network) reveals that one or more sections are not electrically continuous with the rest of the grid, even though individual electrodes may test acceptably in isolation.

Equipment / System

Complete earthing grid — all electrodes, bonding conductors, and interconnections intended to form a single equipotential network.

Possible Causes
HighA bonding conductor between two sections of the grid broken, corroded through, or never installed as designed
HighGrid extended or modified during later construction work without properly bonding the new section to the existing grid
MedA test/disconnect link left open, isolating a section of the grid for testing purposes and not reclosed
MedPhysical damage during excavation severing an interconnecting conductor between grid sections
LowDesign ambiguity or an as-built drawing error meaning a conductor believed to interconnect two sections does not actually exist
Required Tools
Continuity/low-resistance ohmmeterEarth resistance tester (whole-grid and section-by-section)As-built earthing grid drawingEarth pit and test link inspection tools
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO associated equipment before grid continuity testing that involves disconnecting links.
  • Ensure at least one confirmed earth path remains connected to critical equipment throughout testing — do not isolate all paths simultaneously.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompare the as-built earthing grid drawing against the actual site, identifying every intended interconnection point and test link.
  2. MeasurementTest continuity between every major grid section pairwise, rather than only testing individual electrodes in isolation, to confirm the grid behaves as a single equipotential network.
  3. ObservationInspect suspect interconnecting conductors and test links for damage, corrosion, or an open link.
  4. DecisionA specific interconnection confirmed open → repair or reinstate that bonding conductor. Grid found to have never been fully interconnected as designed → treat as a design/installation gap requiring a planned remediation.
  5. Fault LocationIdentify precisely which interconnection or section is responsible for the discontinuity.
  6. Root CauseConfirm whether the discontinuity is a recent fault, longstanding installation gap, or a testing artefact from an open link.
Test Points
Section-to-section continuity across the full grid
Overall grid earth resistance vs. individual section values
Test link/disconnect point status
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-test continuity between every grid section and confirm the whole grid behaves as a single low-resistance network, then re-test overall earth resistance.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or install the missing/broken interconnecting bonding conductor.
  • Reclose any test/disconnect link left open after testing.
  • Update the as-built earthing grid drawing to reflect the as-found and as-repaired configuration.
Preventive Action
  • Include whole-grid section-to-section continuity testing, not just individual electrode resistance, in the periodic maintenance cycle.
  • Require earthing continuity verification as a mandatory commissioning step for any construction or modification work near the grid.
  • Maintain a single authoritative, current as-built earthing grid drawing.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ELS-06 (Earth Grid Continuity Test Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IEEE 80, IS 3043, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

ELS-417 Lightning Damage

Symptom — AGL equipment (CCR, transformer, fixture, cabling, or associated control/monitoring equipment) shows physical or electrical damage consistent with a direct or nearby lightning strike, typically following a known thunderstorm event at the site.

Equipment / System

Any AGL equipment within the affected circuit(s) or area, plus the associated earthing and surge protection system.

Possible Causes
HighDirect lightning strike to an exposed fixture, mast, or elevated equipment
HighNearby strike inducing a high-energy transient into buried cable runs or the earthing grid, damaging connected equipment even without a direct hit
MedSurge protection devices present but overwhelmed by the energy of a severe strike, having failed in the process of limiting damage (see ELS-418)
MedEarthing system unable to dissipate the strike energy quickly enough, causing a ground potential rise that stresses connected equipment (see ELS-425)
LowDamage misattributed to lightning when the actual cause is an unrelated fault coinciding with a storm event
Required Tools
Insulation resistance tester (megger)True-RMS multimeterVisual inspection and photographic documentation kitSPD status indicators/test equipmentWeather/lightning strike data for the site and time period
Safety Precautions
  • Do not approach or work on outdoor AGL equipment during active thunderstorm activity — wait until the storm has cleared the area per site lightning safety procedure.
  • LOTO all affected circuits before inspection or repair, treating equipment as potentially damaged and unpredictable until assessed.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCorrelate the reported fault timing against site weather/lightning strike data to confirm a strike event occurred in the relevant timeframe and area.
  2. ObservationConduct a systematic visual inspection of all equipment on the affected circuit(s) for scorching, melted components, cracked housings, or other physical strike damage, not just the first fault found.
  3. MeasurementMegger and function-test each item of equipment on the affected circuit(s) individually, since a strike can damage multiple components with varying severity across a wide area.
  4. DecisionDamage pattern consistent with a single affected item → isolated repair/replacement. Multiple items affected across the circuit → treat as a wider event requiring full-circuit assessment and review of the surge protection/earthing system's performance.
  5. Root CauseConfirm lightning as the cause via physical evidence and weather data, and assess whether the surge protection and earthing system performed as designed or were overwhelmed.
Test Points
Physical condition of all equipment on the affected circuit(s)
Insulation resistance of cable and windings across the affected area
SPD status/indicators on the affected circuit(s)
Site lightning strike data correlated with fault timing
Root Cause Verification

After repair/replacement, re-test all affected equipment individually and the full circuit as a whole, and confirm SPDs on the circuit are in a serviceable, indicating-good condition before returning to service.

Corrective Action
  • Replace equipment confirmed physically damaged beyond repair.
  • Replace any SPDs that operated (sacrificed themselves) in limiting the strike energy, per ELS-418.
  • Repair damaged cable sections identified during inspection.
  • Document the event, damage extent, and repairs for the site's lightning event history.
Preventive Action
  • Review and upgrade surge protection coordination if a strike event reveals inadequate protection for the site's exposure level.
  • Maintain a lightning event log correlating strikes with subsequent equipment failures to identify vulnerable circuits.
  • Ensure lightning protection system (air terminals, down conductors) inspection is current per its own maintenance schedule.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ELS-07 (Post-Lightning-Event Inspection Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IEC 62305 (lightning protection), IEEE 80, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

ELS-418 Surge Protection Device Failure

Symptom — A surge protection device (SPD) installed on an AGL circuit or panel is found to have failed — either in an open/disconnected state (no longer providing protection) or in a shorted/failed-short state (potentially causing a nuisance trip or continuous fault current).

Equipment / System

Individual SPD unit installed at the CCR panel, distribution point, or field equipment location.

Possible Causes
HighSPD has operated (sacrificed itself) in response to a genuine surge event, reaching end of life as designed
MedSPD reaching end of life from cumulative degradation across many smaller surge events over its service life, without one single identifiable event
MedSPD incorrectly rated for the circuit's actual voltage/energy exposure, failing prematurely
LowManufacturing defect or latent fault in the SPD unit
LowSPD status indicator itself faulty, showing a failure that has not actually occurred
Required Tools
SPD status indicator/test equipment appropriate to the installed SPD typeTrue-RMS multimeterManufacturer's SPD test/replacement procedureReplacement SPD of correct rating
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before removing or replacing an SPD.
  • Treat a failed-short SPD as a potential fault-current path — confirm circuit isolation before handling.
  • Follow correct disposal procedure for the failed SPD unit.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCheck the SPD's visual status indicator (where fitted) and any associated remote monitoring/alarm status.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, test the SPD per the manufacturer's procedure to confirm whether it has failed open, failed short, or is a false indication.
  3. ObservationReview the site's lightning/surge event history to determine whether the failure correlates with a specific event or appears to be end-of-life degradation.
  4. DecisionSPD confirmed failed → replace with a correctly rated unit per manufacturer specification. Indicator found faulty but SPD itself tests serviceable → repair/replace the indicator only.
  5. Root CauseDetermine whether the failure was a single significant surge event, cumulative degradation, or an incorrect original rating, to inform whether the replacement SPD should be the same rating or an upgraded one.
Test Points
SPD visual/remote status indication
SPD function test result per manufacturer procedure
Site surge/lightning event history correlated with failure timing
Root Cause Verification

After replacement, confirm the new SPD's status indicator shows serviceable/good, and verify correct installation (polarity, torque, and connection to the earthing system) before returning the circuit to service.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed SPD with a unit of the correct rating for the circuit.
  • Confirm the SPD's earth connection is sound as part of the replacement, since SPD performance depends on a low-impedance earth path.
  • Upgrade the SPD rating if the failure pattern indicates the original specification is inadequate for the site's exposure.
Preventive Action
  • Include SPD status verification in the routine maintenance and inspection cycle.
  • Stock correctly rated replacement SPDs for rapid restoration after a surge event.
  • Log every SPD replacement with date and suspected cause to build a site surge-exposure history.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ELS-08 (SPD Inspection and Replacement Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IEC 61643 (surge protective devices), IEC 62305, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

ELS-419 Repeated SPD Failure

Symptom — The same SPD location fails and is replaced more than once within a period that is short relative to its expected service life, indicating a recurring or ongoing condition rather than a single isolated surge event.

Equipment / System

SPD location with a documented history of repeated failure, plus the associated circuit, earthing, and surge coordination scheme.

Possible Causes
HighSPD earth connection itself is poor, high-resistance, or intermittent, preventing the SPD from dissipating surge energy effectively and causing it to fail under events it should otherwise survive
HighSPD undersized/incorrectly rated for the location's actual surge exposure, failing repeatedly under normal storm activity for the site
MedUpstream surge protection stage missing or itself failed, meaning this SPD is absorbing more energy than the coordinated design intended
MedSite has genuinely unusual lightning exposure (e.g. elevated location, high isokeraunic level) exceeding the original design assumption
LowA repeated internal circuit fault (unrelated to external surges) stressing the SPD from the equipment side
Required Tools
SPD failure history log for the locationEarth resistance tester (for the SPD's specific earth connection)Surge coordination drawing showing all protection stagesSite lightning/surge event history
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before working on the SPD or its earth connection.
  • Treat the recurring failure as a signal of a systemic issue rather than simply replacing the SPD again without investigation.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the failure history for this specific SPD location — dates, replacement frequency, and any correlation with weather events.
  2. MeasurementTest the SPD's specific earth connection resistance/continuity independently, since a poor SPD earth is a common cause of repeated failure.
  3. ObservationReview the surge coordination drawing to confirm whether an upstream protection stage exists and is itself functioning, and whether this SPD is correctly rated for its position in the coordination scheme.
  4. DecisionSPD earth connection confirmed poor → repair the earth connection before replacing the SPD again. Earth connection confirmed good and SPD correctly rated → investigate upstream coordination or consider the site's exposure level for an uprated SPD.
  5. Root CauseIdentify the specific systemic cause — poor earthing, incorrect rating, missing coordination stage, or genuine site exposure — rather than treating each failure as an independent event.
Test Points
SPD-specific earth connection resistance
Upstream protection stage status
SPD rating vs. site exposure/coordination design
Historical failure frequency and correlation with weather
Root Cause Verification

After addressing the identified systemic cause, monitor the SPD's status over subsequent storm events and confirm it survives events of a severity comparable to those that previously caused failure.

Corrective Action
  • Repair the SPD's earth connection if confirmed poor.
  • Install or repair a missing/failed upstream surge protection stage to correctly coordinate energy absorption across stages.
  • Uprate the SPD specification if the site's actual exposure is confirmed to exceed the original design assumption.
Preventive Action
  • Review surge protection coordination design for any location with a repeated-failure history.
  • Include SPD-specific earth testing (not just general grid testing) in the maintenance cycle for known repeat-failure locations.
  • Consider a lightning risk assessment update if site exposure appears to have changed since original design.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ELS-09 (Repeated SPD Failure Investigation Procedure), SOP-ELS-08 (SPD Inspection and Replacement Procedure)

Standards: IEC 61643, IEC 62305, IEEE 80

Rev 0 — Initial issue
ELS-420 Surge-Induced CCR Failure

Symptom — A CCR (or a specific card/module within it) fails in a manner consistent with a surge/transient event — for example a damaged output stage, failed control card, or tripped protection — typically following a lightning event or switching transient on the connected circuit(s).

Equipment / System

CCR power stage, control/regulation card, and any internal SPD or transient protection fitted within the CCR cabinet.

Possible Causes
HighSurge energy entering via the field circuit output, exceeding the CCR's internal/external surge protection capability
HighSurge entering via the incoming mains supply to the CCR, if mains-side surge protection is absent, failed, or inadequate
MedCCR's internal surge protection component (if fitted) has itself failed, having absorbed the event
MedPoor CCR cabinet earthing preventing effective surge energy dissipation, increasing stress on internal components
LowCoincidental internal component failure unrelated to any surge event, misattributed due to timing with a storm
Required Tools
CCR manufacturer diagnostic/fault log toolTrue-RMS multimeterInsulation resistance testerCCR internal SPD status checkSite lightning/surge event history
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the CCR at its supply and field circuit connections before internal inspection.
  • Treat internal CCR components as potentially damaged and holding residual charge — follow manufacturer discharge procedure before handling.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationRetrieve the CCR's internal fault/event log, where available, to identify the specific failure mode and its timing relative to any known surge event.
  2. MeasurementTest the field-circuit-side and mains-side surge protection associated with the CCR independently to determine which path the surge most likely entered through.
  3. ObservationInspect the CCR cabinet earthing connection for adequacy, since poor cabinet earthing increases surge stress on internal components.
  4. DecisionFailure confirmed surge-related via log/pattern and correlated event → replace the damaged card/component and address the entry path (field or mains side) surge protection. Failure pattern inconsistent with a surge event → investigate as a standard component failure under Division 01.
  5. Root CauseDetermine which surge entry path (field circuit or mains supply) was responsible, and confirm the associated protection stage for that path is adequate going forward.
Test Points
CCR internal fault/event log
Field-circuit-side surge protection status
Mains-side surge protection status
CCR cabinet earthing resistance
Root Cause Verification

After repair, confirm the CCR operates correctly across its full range of outputs/intensity steps, and verify both field-circuit and mains-side surge protection are in a confirmed serviceable state before returning to service.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the damaged CCR card/component per manufacturer procedure.
  • Replace or install adequate surge protection on the identified entry path (field circuit or mains supply).
  • Improve CCR cabinet earthing if found inadequate.
Preventive Action
  • Include CCR internal SPD/surge protection status in the routine maintenance cycle.
  • Ensure both field-circuit and mains-side surge protection are specified and maintained for every CCR, not just one side.
  • Review CCR surge protection coordination after any confirmed surge-induced failure.

ELS-421 Surge-Induced LED Failure

Symptom — One or more LED-based fixtures fail in a manner consistent with a transient/surge event — for example a damaged LED driver, failed LED array, or fixture that will not respond to any commanded intensity step — typically following a storm event on the circuit.

Equipment / System

LED fixture driver/power supply module and LED array, plus the isolation transformer and connector serving the fixture.

Possible Causes
HighSurge energy transmitted through the isolation transformer secondary into the LED driver, exceeding its internal transient protection
HighLED driver's internal surge protection component has itself failed, having absorbed the event
MedIsolation transformer itself damaged by the same event, in addition to or instead of the driver (test independently, see Division 01/other light-type divisions for transformer-specific diagnosis)
MedFixture's earth/bonding connection inadequate, increasing surge stress on the internal electronics
LowCoincidental LED driver failure unrelated to any surge event, misattributed due to timing with a storm
Required Tools
LED driver manufacturer diagnostic tool (where available)True-RMS multimeterInsulation resistance testerReplacement LED driver/fixture stockSite lightning/surge event history
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before opening the fixture.
  • Allow the fixture to discharge before handling internal driver components, per manufacturer guidance.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCorrelate the fixture failure timing against site weather/lightning strike data to establish whether a surge event is a plausible cause.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, test the LED driver and LED array independently to identify which component has failed.
  3. MeasurementTest the isolation transformer serving the fixture independently to rule in or out surge damage there as well.
  4. DecisionDriver confirmed failed and correlates with a surge event → replace the driver and verify the transformer is undamaged before reconnecting. Multiple fixtures on the same circuit affected similarly → treat as a wider circuit-level surge event and inspect all fixtures on that circuit.
  5. Root CauseConfirm surge as the cause via correlated timing and driver failure signature, and assess whether the fixture's surge protection/earthing was adequate for the event.
Test Points
LED driver function/output test
LED array continuity/condition
Isolation transformer condition (independent test)
Fixture earth/bonding connection
Root Cause Verification

After replacement, re-energise the circuit and confirm the fixture operates correctly at all commanded intensity steps, and check neighbouring fixtures on the same circuit for any related but less obvious damage.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed LED driver or complete fixture as appropriate.
  • Replace the isolation transformer if also confirmed damaged by the same event.
  • Improve the fixture's earth/bonding connection if found inadequate.
Preventive Action
  • Where fixture-level surge protection components are available from the manufacturer, include them in future fixture specifications for surge-exposed circuits.
  • Include fixture driver condition checks in post-storm inspection procedures.
  • Track LED driver failures by circuit and correlate with weather events to identify surge-vulnerable circuits.
Related References

ELS-422 Surge-Induced Communication Failure

Symptom — A communication/monitoring link associated with the AGL system — for example ALCMS data links, CCR remote monitoring, or field sensor communication — fails or shows corrupted/intermittent data following a storm event, without necessarily any associated lighting circuit fault.

Equipment / System

Communication interface cards, data cabling, and any dedicated data-line surge protection at both ends of the affected link.

Possible Causes
HighSurge energy coupled onto the communication cable itself, particularly if it runs alongside power cabling or lacks dedicated data-line surge protection
HighCommunication interface card at the CCR or monitoring end damaged by a surge entering via the data line or via the equipment's power/earth connection
MedGround potential difference between the two communication endpoints during the surge event stressing the interface (see ELS-425)
MedCommunication cable shield/earth improperly terminated, providing an unintended surge coupling path
LowSoftware/firmware fault coincidentally reported around the same time as a storm event, unrelated to the surge itself
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeterCommunication line surge protection status checkInsulation resistance tester (for cable)Manufacturer's communication interface diagnostic toolSite lightning/surge event history
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO or de-energise per manufacturer procedure before working on communication interface cards.
  • Treat data cabling as potentially carrying induced transient voltage following a storm event — do not assume it is safe simply because it is low-voltage under normal conditions.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCorrelate the communication failure timing against site weather/lightning strike data.
  2. MeasurementTest the communication interface card at each end of the link independently to identify which end (or both) has failed.
  3. MeasurementTest the communication cable's insulation and shield/earth continuity, since surge coupling often occurs via an inadequately earthed shield.
  4. DecisionInterface card confirmed failed at one or both ends → replace the card(s) and verify/install dedicated data-line surge protection if absent. Cable shield/earth found deficient → correct the shield termination as part of the repair.
  5. Root CauseDetermine whether the surge coupled via the data line itself, via the equipment's power/earth system, or via a ground potential difference between the two ends.
Test Points
Communication interface card status at each end
Communication cable insulation resistance and shield continuity
Data-line surge protection status (if fitted)
Ground potential difference between the two communication endpoints
Root Cause Verification

After repair, confirm the communication link operates correctly with stable, uncorrupted data over an extended monitoring period, and verify data-line surge protection status at both ends.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed communication interface card(s).
  • Install or repair dedicated data-line surge protection at both ends of the link if found absent or failed.
  • Correct communication cable shield/earth termination if found deficient.
Preventive Action
  • Specify dedicated data-line surge protection for all communication links between separately earthed AGL system components.
  • Include communication link integrity checks in post-storm inspection procedures.
  • Address any confirmed ground potential difference between communication endpoints per ELS-425.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ELS-12 (Communication Link Surge Damage Investigation Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

ELS-423 Lightning-Induced Cable Failure

Symptom — A section of AGL primary or communication cable is found damaged or with degraded insulation in a pattern consistent with a lightning-induced transient having passed through or near it, distinct from the mechanical or ageing-related cable faults covered elsewhere in the Atlas.

Equipment / System

Series circuit primary cable or communication cable in the vicinity of a known or suspected lightning event.

Possible Causes
HighDirect or near-direct strike energy passing through the cable, causing localised insulation breakdown or conductor damage at one or more points
HighInduced transient voltage from a nearby strike exceeding the cable's insulation rating at a weak point (existing minor damage, splice, or joint)
MedStrike energy travelling along the earthing grid and coupling into the cable at a point where cable and grid run in close proximity
MedCumulative damage from repeated smaller induced transients over multiple storm events, rather than a single severe event
LowCable failure coincidentally timed with a storm event but actually due to an unrelated mechanical or ageing cause
Required Tools
Insulation resistance tester (1000V/2500V megger)Series circuit fault locator / TDRSite lightning/surge event historyCable route drawing
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before any cable testing or repair.
  • Treat a lightning-damaged cable section as potentially having multiple damage points, not just the first one found.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCorrelate the reported cable fault timing against site weather/lightning strike data.
  2. MeasurementMegger the cable in segments to bracket the damaged section(s), being alert to the possibility of more than one damage point given the nature of induced transient events.
  3. MeasurementUse a TDR or fault locator to estimate the physical location of each damage point within the bracketed section(s).
  4. DecisionSingle damage point located and confirmed → repair that section. Multiple damage points suspected from the segment testing pattern → treat as a multi-point fault per SCF-404 and locate each independently.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the specific location(s) and extent of insulation/conductor damage.
  6. Root CauseAssess whether the damage pattern indicates a direct strike, an induced transient, or coupling from the earthing grid, to inform whether additional surge protection or cable rerouting should be considered.
Test Points
Segment-by-segment insulation resistance
TDR fault distance estimate for each damage point
Proximity of the cable route to earthing grid conductors or lightning protection down-conductors
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-megger the full cable run and confirm insulation resistance is restored above the acceptable threshold at every previously identified damage point.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace each identified damaged cable section using correctly rated, sealed splice kits or full replacement as extent of damage requires.
  • Reinstate pavement/trench per airport engineering standards at each excavated location.
Preventive Action
  • Where a cable route runs in close proximity to the earthing grid or lightning protection down-conductors, review separation distance against IEC 62305 guidance and consider rerouting or additional shielding.
  • Include post-storm insulation testing for cable runs in known lightning-exposed areas of the site.
  • Maintain a lightning event log correlated with subsequent cable fault reports to identify vulnerable routes.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ELS-13 (Lightning-Induced Cable Fault Investigation Procedure), SOP-SCF-09 (Cable Fault Location and Repair Procedure)

Standards: IEC 62305, IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

ELS-424 Earthing-Related False Alarm

Symptom — A CCR earth-fault/ground-fault protection trip, or another earthing-referenced alarm, occurs without a genuine insulation or earth fault being present anywhere on the circuit when tested, indicating the alarm itself is being triggered by an earthing system characteristic rather than a real fault.

Equipment / System

CCR earth-fault/ground-fault monitoring circuit and its own reference earth connection, plus the protection setting/threshold configuration.

Possible Causes
HighCCR's own reference earth connection is poor or high-resistance, causing its monitoring circuit to misread normal circuit behaviour as a fault
HighProtection trip threshold set too sensitively for the site's normal, acceptable baseline leakage current
MedA ground potential difference between the CCR's earth reference and the field circuit's earth during normal switching transients, triggering a nuisance trip (see ELS-425)
MedElectromagnetic interference or a nearby high-current switching event coupling a false signal into the monitoring circuit
LowA genuine but very minor and stable leakage current, within safe limits, that has always been present but now trips due to a threshold change or monitoring calibration drift
Required Tools
CCR earth-fault monitoring circuit diagnostic toolInsulation resistance tester (to confirm circuit is genuinely fault-free)Earth resistance tester (for the CCR's own reference earth)CCR protection setting documentation
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before working on the CCR's monitoring circuit or reference earth.
  • Do not simply raise the trip threshold or disable protection to stop nuisance trips without confirming no genuine fault is present.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationPerform a full insulation resistance test on the field circuit to positively confirm no genuine earth fault is present, rather than assuming this from the trip pattern alone.
  2. MeasurementTest the CCR's own reference earth connection resistance and continuity independently of the field circuit's earthing.
  3. ObservationReview the CCR's protection threshold setting against the circuit's normal, historically stable leakage current baseline.
  4. DecisionField circuit confirmed genuinely fault-free and CCR reference earth confirmed poor → repair the CCR's own earth connection. Field circuit fault-free and CCR reference earth good, but threshold appears overly sensitive → review and adjust the threshold only after engineering approval.
  5. Root CauseConfirm whether the nuisance trip originates from the CCR's own earthing, an overly sensitive threshold, or interference, and rule out a genuine minor fault before concluding it is a false alarm.
Test Points
Field circuit insulation resistance (confirmed fault-free)
CCR reference earth resistance and continuity
Protection trip threshold vs. historical baseline leakage current
Trip event timing correlated with switching or interference sources
Root Cause Verification

After corrective action, monitor the circuit over subsequent operating cycles at all intensity steps and confirm no further nuisance trips occur, while re-confirming periodically that the circuit remains genuinely fault-free.

Corrective Action
  • Repair the CCR's own reference earth connection if confirmed poor.
  • Adjust the protection threshold only following engineering review confirming the new setting remains safe and compliant.
  • Address identified electromagnetic interference sources where practical.
Preventive Action
  • Verify CCR reference earth condition as part of the CCR's own periodic maintenance, separate from field circuit earthing checks.
  • Document and review protection threshold settings against actual site baseline data rather than a generic default.
  • Log nuisance trip events with full test results to build a clear record before considering any threshold adjustment.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ELS-14 (Earth-Fault Nuisance Trip Investigation Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-10, IEC 60364, IS 3043

ELS-425 Ground Potential Difference Problem

Symptom — Two points in the AGL system that are expected to be at, or close to, the same earth potential are found to have a measurable voltage difference between them, particularly during fault, switching, or surge events, causing nuisance trips, communication errors, or equipment stress at the interface between the two points.

Equipment / System

Two or more separately earthed points within the AGL system — for example a CCR cabinet and a remote field cabinet, or two buildings/structures with independent earthing systems interconnected by cable.

Possible Causes
HighThe two points are earthed via separate, electrically distant earth electrodes or grids that are not adequately bonded together, allowing them to rise to different potentials during a fault or surge event
HighSoil resistivity or grid layout between the two points is such that a significant potential gradient exists across the intervening ground during fault current flow
MedA bonding conductor intended to equalise potential between the two points is broken, undersized, or was never installed
MedOne of the two points has a significantly higher local earth resistance than the other, causing it to rise to a different potential under the same fault current
LowInstrumentation/measurement error in detecting the potential difference, rather than a genuine physical condition
Required Tools
High-impedance voltmeter capable of transient/peak captureEarth resistance tester at each point independentlyAs-built earthing and bonding drawing showing both points and any interconnectionSite fault/surge event history
Safety Precautions
  • Treat any measured ground potential difference as a genuine shock and equipment-damage hazard until characterised and addressed — do not work across the two points without confirming safe potential equalisation.
  • LOTO both ends before working on any interconnecting cable or bonding conductor.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm and quantify the ground potential difference between the two points, ideally capturing its behaviour during an actual or simulated fault/switching event, since it may not be significant under normal steady-state conditions.
  2. MeasurementTest earth resistance at each point independently to identify whether one point has a substantially higher local resistance than the other.
  3. ObservationReview the as-built earthing and bonding drawing to confirm whether an equalising bonding conductor is intended to exist between the two points, and if so, test its condition.
  4. DecisionA bonding conductor is intended but broken/absent → install or repair it. Bonding conductor present and sound but potential difference persists → investigate soil/grid layout between the points and consider additional electrodes or a dedicated equalising conductor.
  5. Root CauseDetermine whether the root cause is a missing/broken bond, an inherent grid layout/soil resistivity issue, or a significant asymmetry in local earth resistance between the two points.
Test Points
Ground potential difference between the two points, under normal and fault/switching conditions where safely measurable
Independent earth resistance at each point
Bonding conductor condition, where one is intended to exist
Root Cause Verification

After corrective action, re-measure the ground potential difference under representative conditions and confirm it is reduced to within a safe, acceptable margin for the equipment and personnel exposed to it.

Corrective Action
  • Install or repair a bonding conductor to equalise potential between the two points where the design intends one.
  • Add supplementary earth electrodes at the point with higher local resistance to bring it closer in potential to the other.
  • Where full equalisation is impractical, ensure equipment and cabling crossing the interface is rated and protected for the expected potential difference (e.g. isolated interfaces, appropriately rated surge protection).
Preventive Action
  • Assess ground potential difference between all separately earthed AGL system interfaces at design stage, not only after a problem is reported.
  • Include bonding conductor continuity between critical interconnected points in the periodic earth grid continuity testing (ELS-416).
  • Document known ground potential difference risk points on the as-built earthing drawing for future reference.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ELS-15 (Ground Potential Difference Assessment Procedure), SOP-ELS-06 (Earth Grid Continuity Test Procedure)

Standards: IEEE 80, IEC 62305, IS 3043

Q2What information does each topic include?

Every topic follows the same diagnostic record: the symptom, equipment/system, possible causes ranked by likelihood (high, medium or low), required tools, safety precautions, diagnostic procedure, test points, root cause verification, corrective action, preventive action and related references (SOPs, standards, diagrams and case studies).

Each procedure starts with observation before any intervention. For example, ELS-411 (High Earth Resistance) begins: Confirm the test was performed correctly — correct method (fall-of-potential vs. clamp-on), correct probe spacing, and away from interfering buried metalwork or parallel earth paths.

Each topic lists its own precautions. For example, ELS-411 (High Earth Resistance) starts with: De-energise and isolate associated equipment before working in the earth pit where practical.
