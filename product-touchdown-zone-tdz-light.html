Product Overview

Touchdown Zone (TDZ) Light

What It Does

Touchdown Zone (TDZ) lights provide enhanced visual guidance within the touchdown zone of the runway, supporting pilots during the critical landing phase where precise longitudinal and lateral visual information is required.

TDZ lighting forms part of the advanced runway visual-aid system used on precision approach runways and is particularly important for operations requiring enhanced visual cues in reduced-visibility conditions.

The final TDZ arrangement should always be established from the approved aerodrome design, applicable standard and project lighting layout.

Function of TDZ Lighting

A Complete Visual Guidance System

What the Lights Do

The touchdown area is one of the most operationally critical portions of the runway. TDZ lights provide additional visual cues that help pilots assess the runway surface and touchdown area during the landing approach.

TDZ lighting should be considered a complete visual guidance system, not simply a collection of individual light fittings.

TDZ Light Configuration

Paired Barrette Arrangement

ICAO Arrangement

The TDZ system consists of rows of light barrettes arranged symmetrically about the runway centreline.

Under ICAO provisions, the pattern is formed by pairs of barrettes symmetrically located about the runway centreline. The lateral spacing between the innermost lights of the pair corresponds to the selected touchdown-zone marking spacing. Longitudinal spacing between pairs may be 30 m or 60 m, with 30 m spacing noted as advantageous for lower-visibility operations.

A TDZ barrette is composed of at least three lights, with the lights spaced no more than 1.5 m apart, and the barrette length is between 3 m and 4.5 m.

Typical TDZ Layout Concept

The exact number of barrettes, spacing and total longitudinal coverage must follow the applicable runway standard and approved project drawing.

Longitudinal Coverage & FAA Arrangement

Runway Length and Governing Standard Control the Layout

ICAO Longitudinal Coverage

Under ICAO provisions, TDZ lights extend from the threshold for a longitudinal distance of 900 m, except on runways shorter than 1,800 m, where the system is shortened so that it does not extend beyond the runway midpoint.

This makes runway length an important parameter during product selection and layout engineering.

FAA TDZ Arrangement

For FAA-designed systems, AC 150/5340-30J describes touchdown zone lighting as two rows of transverse light bars located symmetrically about the runway centreline. The guidance specifies:

  • Light bars perpendicular to the runway centreline
  • Three unidirectional lights per light bar
  • Lights facing the landing threshold
  • Coverage extending to 3,000 ft, or one-half runway length for runways shorter than 6,000 ft
  • First light bars located 100 ft from the threshold
  • Light beams toed four degrees toward the runway centreline (AC 150/5340-30J); confirm against the approved drawing

Because dimensional and configuration details can vary between governing standards, the project jurisdiction and approved design standard must control final layout selection.

Optical Direction

TDZ Fixtures Are Directional Lighting Equipment

Optical Characteristics

The optical system should provide the intended light output toward the landing aircraft while controlling the beam distribution in the required viewing region.

  • Unidirectional light output
  • Direction toward the landing threshold
  • Controlled beam distribution
  • Horizontal coverage
  • Vertical coverage
  • Optical axis
  • Light intensity
  • Colour performance
  • Toe-in angle where applicable

Why Orientation Matters

FAA installation guidance (AC 150/5340-30J) calls for the TDZ light beam to be toed four degrees toward the runway centreline. The FAA fixture type L-850B is supplied toed left, toed right or straight, so confirm the correct handing for each row against the approved drawing.

Incorrect fixture orientation can significantly affect the effectiveness of the visual guidance system.

Light Colour

Fixed Unidirectional Lights Showing Variable White

ICAO Colour Requirement

ICAO specifies TDZ lights as fixed unidirectional lights showing variable white.

The selected fixture must therefore provide the required colour characteristics over the specified intensity range.

Product selection should consider the fixture together with the isolating transformer, secondary connection and CCR-operated series circuit. Incorrect compatibility can result in:

  • Incorrect light intensity
  • Improper intensity control

The electrical performance of the complete system should be evaluated during design, testing and commissioning.

FAQ

Touchdown Zone (TDZ) Light — Frequently Asked Questions

What are touchdown zone (TDZ) lights?

TDZ lights give enhanced visual guidance within the touchdown zone of a precision approach runway during the critical landing phase, and are particularly important for operations requiring enhanced visual cues in reduced visibility. They should be treated as a complete visual guidance system, not a collection of individual fittings.

Which runways need TDZ lighting?

ICAO provisions identify TDZ lighting for the touchdown zone of precision approach runways Category II or III, and TDZ lights may also be fitted on Category I runways where the approved design calls for them. ICAO also describes a separate, simpler arrangement called simple touchdown zone lights for other runways, which is not what this product covers.

How is an ICAO TDZ light pattern arranged?

The system consists of rows of barrettes arranged in pairs symmetrically about the runway centreline. The lateral spacing between the innermost lights of a pair corresponds to the selected touchdown-zone marking spacing, and longitudinal spacing between pairs may be 30 m or 60 m, with 30 m noted as advantageous for lower-visibility operations. A barrette has at least three lights spaced no more than 1.5 m apart, with a length of 3 m to 4.5 m.

How far along the runway do TDZ lights extend?

Under ICAO provisions they extend from the threshold for 900 m, except on runways shorter than 1,800 m, where the system is shortened so that it does not extend beyond the runway midpoint. Under FAA AC 150/5340-30J, coverage extends to 3,000 ft, or one-half the runway length for runways shorter than 6,000 ft, with the first light bars 100 ft from the threshold.

How does the FAA arrangement differ?

FAA AC 150/5340-30J describes two rows of transverse light bars located symmetrically about the runway centreline, perpendicular to the centreline, with three unidirectional lights per bar facing the landing threshold and beams toed four degrees toward the runway centreline. The governing standard and the approved design control the final layout, so confirm against the approved drawing.

What colour are TDZ lights?

ICAO specifies TDZ lights as fixed unidirectional lights showing variable white. The selected fixture must provide the required colour characteristics over the specified intensity range.

Why does fixture orientation and toe-in matter?

TDZ fixtures are directional, and incorrect orientation can significantly affect the effectiveness of the visual guidance system. The FAA fixture type L-850B is supplied toed left, toed right or straight, so confirm the correct handing for each row against the approved drawing.

Which FAA fixture type is used for TDZ lights?

The in-pavement runway touchdown zone fixture is the L-850B (unidirectional white) under FAA AC 150/5345-46F. The L-850A is the runway centreline fixture, not the TDZ fixture.

What does an inset TDZ installation involve?

A typical installation includes the inset fixture, light housing, LED or light-source assembly, optical lens, mounting hardware, gasket and sealing system, drainage arrangement, secondary cable, isolating transformer and primary circuit connection. Fixture seating, alignment, pavement level, drainage, sealing and cable routing all affect reliability, and installation depth and pavement cut-out must come from the approved manufacturer installation drawing.

Is an electrically working TDZ light automatically compliant?

No. A TDZ fixture that is electrically operational is not necessarily photometrically compliant, and LED technology should not be treated as the sole compliance criterion. The complete optical assembly, electrical characteristics, mechanical design and photometric output must satisfy the applicable requirements.

What should I check when a TDZ light is off or its output is low?

Work through the chain: verify the CCR output, check the primary series circuit, the isolating transformer, the secondary cable and connector, then the fixture, light source, optical assembly and barrette alignment, and finally confirm photometric output. Low output can come from an incorrect CCR intensity, optical contamination, LED degradation or an alignment issue. See the L-830 isolating transformer page.

What should be confirmed before ordering TDZ lighting?

Confirm the runway identification and length, the precision approach category, the TDZ marking and barrette configuration, the number of lights per barrette, longitudinal and lateral spacing, colour, optical direction and toe-in, intensity steps, series-circuit current, isolating transformer and connector type, housing and installation depth, drainage requirements and the governing standard, and request the photometric documentation and type-test reports.
