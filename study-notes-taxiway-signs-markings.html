TAXIWAY VISUAL GUIDANCE

Taxiway signs and markings are a major part of the Surface Movement Guidance and Control System (SMGCS). They help pilots and vehicle operators know their position, follow the route, recognise holding positions, avoid restricted areas and navigate intersections.

The key distinction
  • Marking = information painted on the pavement
  • Sign = information on an elevated or surface-mounted sign
  • Lighting = makes the visual guidance available under the operating conditions

The references are AC 150/5340-18H (sign systems), AC 150/5340-1M (markings) and AC 150/5345-44L (sign specification). For the marking-vs-lighting principle see Note 12.

Taxiway centerline, edge, shoulder and holding positions are yellow, unlike the mostly white runway markings. Memory: solid = not a normal taxi area · dashed = crossing may be intended. Being on the centerline does not by itself guarantee wingtip clearance.

HOLDING POSITION MARKINGS

Holding positionMarkingSign
RunwayPattern A: four yellow lines, two solid and two dashed; solid lines on the holding sideRed background, white legend with black outline (runway designations, e.g. 15-33)
ILS critical area / POFZPattern B: two solid yellow lines 2 ft apart with connecting pairs of lines at 10 ft spacingWhite legend on red: “ILS”
Taxiway / taxiwayPattern C: single dashed yellow line across the taxiway (“hold short of Taxiway XX”); optional, used where aircraft are held short of an intersectionNo mandatory sign; the location/direction array may sit up to 10 ft before the line

The marking says where to stop; the sign says what you are holding for. The runway numerals on the sign show the relative direction of the thresholds: on a “15-33” sign, runway 15 is to the left and runway 33 to the right when facing the runway from the holding position. FAA signs write single-digit runways without a leading zero (9-27); ICAO and Indian practice uses two digits (09-27). Marking is not replaced by the sign: keep both maintained.

ICAO and Indian practice. This note follows the FAA advisory circulars because they publish the most detailed criteria, but Indian aerodromes work to ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I (Chapter 5, visual aids for navigation) and DGCA requirements. Watch these differences: (1) ICAO uses Pattern A at the runway holding position and Pattern B, a “ladder” marking, for the additional holding positions set farther back at CAT II/III runways, whereas FAA Pattern B marks ILS critical areas, the POFZ and approach/departure areas, so the same letter does not mean the same thing; (2) ICAO enhanced taxiway centre line marking runs about 47 m from the holding position marking, while the FAA version runs up to 150 ft (45.7 m); (3) the single broken line across a taxiway is called an intermediate holding position marking in ICAO; (4) sign sizes, character heights and siting distances differ, so use the ICAO tables and the approved aerodrome design, not Table 1-1 of AC 150/5340-18H. Always name the source (FAA or ICAO) when quoting a dimension in an exam or report.

SURFACE-PAINTED SIGNS AND GEOGRAPHIC MARKINGS

Surface-painted signColoursPosition
DirectionYellow background, black inscriptionAdjacent to centerline: left-turn signs on left, right-turn signs on right
LocationBlack background, yellow inscriptionRight side of centerline
Holding positionRed background, white inscriptionLeft of the centerline on the holding side, before the holding-position marking; normally used where the holding-position width exceeds 200 ft (60 m)

Surface-painted signs supplement elevated signs; they do not replace the standard elevated mandatory or direction signs. Geographic-position markings are placed to the left of the centerline, in the direction of taxiing, along low-visibility (RVR below 1,200 ft / 360 m) taxi routes designated in the SMGCS plan: a circular design in black, white and pink with a number or number/letter identifier.

5

TAXIWAY DESIGNATIONS

Designations should be simple and logical. AC 150/5340-18H introduces primary and secondary taxiways: primary taxiways take single letters (A, B), with double same letters (AA, BB) only after single letters run out, and A and B are recommended for the primary taxiways closest to the terminal where practical. Secondary taxiways (entrances, exits, connectors) take alphanumeric designations: the primary taxiway letter followed by a number (B2, C6), never a letter after the number (not B2A).

Avoid: number-only names (confused with runways), the letters I, O and X, descriptive words such as “inner” or “bridge”, and double different letters such as AB, because “Alpha-Bravo” can be heard as two taxiways (the AC allows limited exceptions for non-movement apron entries). Poor naming causes pilot confusion, ATC problems, wrong turns and longer taxi times.

Renaming a taxiway is not a lettering change. It affects elevated and surface signs, markings, airport diagrams, charts, ATC procedures, pilot familiarity and maintenance drawings. Keep sign legend, painted legend, drawings and ATC data consistent.

7

SIGN DETAILS: ARRAYS, DESTINATION, ENDING MARKER, NO ENTRY

Arrays: left-turn designations go left of the location sign, straight-ahead/right-turn designations to the right (with exceptions). Direction signs sit where turns are common and intended and are not collocated with holding position, boundary or destination signs. Mandatory signs generally use no arrows.

Destination signs are optional, supplementary and should not replace standard location/direction signs, nor be placed on runways; they cover outbound routes (towards runways) and inbound routes (to aprons). Outbound signs show the departure runway designation and an arrow (e.g. 27 →); inbound signs use generic terms of at least three characters (APRON, CARGO, TERM). They are not collocated with location, direction or mandatory signs.

Taxiway ending marker: shows that the taxiway does not continue past a T-intersection; a frangible retroreflective barrier on the far side, recommended where excursion risk is high and other cues are inadequate.

No-entry sign: circle with a horizontal bar, placed on both sides of the taxiway before the restricted area (one left-side sign only where a safety assessment justifies it), useful on one-direction paths such as some runway exits; it does not replace the holding-position sign where that is required. Runway exit signs and acute-angle exits have specific criteria in AC 150/5340-18H.

TypeUseColour

L-858CTaxiway ending markerYellow 45° stripes on black
L-858HOne-half distance remainingWhite on black

Signs may be lighted (internal illumination) or unlighted (retroreflective). A lighted sign has a housing, face, legend, light source or LED modules, driver, wiring and frangible mounting. Font and character sizes come from AC 150/5345-44, not the fabricator. Each sign is also specified by Size, Class, Style and Mode. At Part 139 airports AC 150/5340-18H calls for lighted mandatory, location, direction and distance-remaining signs; unlighted retroreflective signs can be an economical option elsewhere, with lighting prioritised for runway holding position signs.

Signs are generally on the left side of the taxiway as seen by the approaching pilot, with explicit exceptions (geometry, conflicts, signs back to back, terrain). The top of the sign needs at least 12 in (30 cm) clearance from any part of the most critical aircraft, measured with its wheels at the pavement edge; if the maximum offset cannot give this, reduce the sign size. Size choice weighs visibility, clearance, space, jet blast, snow and ice removal and geometry, and all signs in one array use the same panel size. The middle of a sign array should be no more than 150 ft (45.7 m) from the taxiway centerline for lighted location/direction arrays, 125 ft (38 m) for lighted runway holding position signs and 60 ft (18.3 m) for unlighted signs.

At a runway holding position the taxiway location sign goes outboard of the holding position sign. Holding position signs go on the left, and also on the right where the holding-position width exceeds 150 ft (45.7 m), the marking does not run straight across, the position is close to a turn, or a risk assessment calls for it. No signs go between the holding position sign and the runway. The sign face is generally perpendicular to the centerline, or canted inward up to 30° where it helps legibility, and consecutive signs need at least 50 ft (15.2 m) between them and enough spacing to be read. Signs go on frangible supports (frangible point no more than 3 in / 7.6 cm above grade) on stable foundations that finish under 1 in / 25 mm above grade, with bonding and grounding per the project design.

Verify sizes, offsets and clearances against AC 150/5340-18H (Table 1-1) and AC 150/5345-44L. The 36 in optimum and 60 in maximum heights belong to vehicle service road STOP signs, not to taxiway guidance signs.

Design order: geometry → designation → route analysis → sign need → location → array. Do not design signs before the route geometry.

10

ELECTRICAL AND CONTROL

Illuminated signs should give consistent illumination at each relevant edge-light brightness setting. Runway-related signs (mandatory, runway-holding location, ILS, CAT II/III, approach/departure, runway exit) should light when runway edge lights light; taxiway location, direction and destination signs when taxiway edge lights light. Simultaneous operation does not require the same circuit, and a dedicated sign circuit lets signs stay available while edge lighting is out for maintenance.

Control roomALCMSSign circuitSigns

Sign power design should consider supply, load, inrush, brightness, driver arrangement, circuit grouping, protection, monitoring, voltage drop and environment. On a series circuit the sign is normally fed through an isolation transformer or series-circuit power adapter unit installed below ground in an L-867 light base, with power passing through breakaway connectors in the frangible coupling (AC 150/5340-18H para 1.17; details in AC 150/5340-30). The sign Style sets its power input, so match it to the circuit type. See Note 09 for the series-circuit side.

Stop bars

A stop bar is a lighting system, not a painted line. A runway holding position can combine marking, mandatory sign and stop bar: marking + sign + lighting. Blue taxiway edge lights are separate from the yellow edge marking.

MAINTENANCE AND FAULT-FINDING

Lighted sign

Electrical (supply, driver, wiring, protection), optical (light source, uniformity, dark spots), mechanical (housing, face, mounting, frangible support).

Unlighted sign

Retroreflective surface, legend, scratches, fading, dirt, night visibility.

Sign face

Dirt, dust and oil reduce contrast and reflectivity; clean with an approved method.

Markings

Centerline, edge, hold lines, shoulder, surface signs: colour, geometry, wear, rubber contamination.

ProblemCheck
Sign physically fine but darkSupply → protection → control → driver → light source → internal wiring
Partly dark sign or dark patchesFailed LED module, driver or current imbalance, wiring, loose connector, moisture, diffuser, uneven ageing
Wrong taxiway identifiedLocation sign, designation, placement, surface-painted location marking
Wrong turnDirection sign, arrow, designation, centerline geometry
Pilot overshoots holding pointMandatory sign, hold marking, enhanced centerline, stop bar
Repeated confusion at an intersectionGeometry, designations, placement, array, centerline, lighting, destination signs, surface supplements

Failure impact: location sign = position uncertainty; direction sign = route uncertainty; mandatory sign = holding uncertainty. A worn hold marking is not made good by the sign. In low visibility pay extra attention to centerline lights, signs, stop bars, hold markings, geographic-position markings and sign illumination.

Safety. A sign on a series circuit is fed through an isolation transformer in the light base. Isolate and lock off the circuit, prove it dead and follow the approved permit-to-work before opening a sign housing or separating the frangible coupling; never disconnect a primary connector on an energised loop (see Notes 07 and 08).

COMMON MISTAKES

Wrong sign colour

Yellow/black where red/white mandatory is required.

Location vs direction confusion

Location signs have no arrow.

Wrong runway designation

Check against the approved design.

Unneeded destination signs

Only where standard signs are not enough.

Side, clearance, jet blast, size

Check exceptions, aircraft clearance and wind loads.

Rigid support

Frangibility is required where specified.

Wrong centerline or edge marking

Continuous edge where crossing is intended; solid side of hold marking on the wrong side.

Missing enhanced centerline; renaming without updating everything

Update signs, markings, drawings and ATC data together.

Exam & Interview

EXAM / INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

What colour is a normal taxiway centerline? Edge? Shoulder?

Yellow for all three.

What is an enhanced taxiway centerline?

A yellow centerline with parallel dashed yellow lines each side before a runway holding position, up to 150 ft (45.7 m).

What is a continuous edge marking? Dashed?

Double continuous yellow, where the outside is not for aircraft; broken double yellow where adjoining pavement may be used by aircraft.

What is the runway holding-position marking?

Four yellow lines, two solid and two dashed, with the solid lines on the holding side.

What does the runway holding sign add to the marking?

The marking shows where to stop; the sign shows what runway or protected area it is for.

What is the ILS holding marking?

Two solid yellow lines 2 ft apart with connecting pairs at 10 ft spacing.

What does a location sign tell the pilot? Its colour?

Where the aircraft is; yellow legend and border on black, no arrow.

What is a direction sign? Its colour?

Shows which taxiway and which way to turn; black on yellow with an arrow.

What colour is a mandatory sign?

White legend with black outline on red.

What is a destination sign?

Supplementary guidance towards a remote destination; yellow with black legend and an arrow.

What is an information sign?

Supplementary aeronautical information, black on yellow, typically unlighted.

What is a taxiway ending marker?

A frangible retroreflective marker showing the taxiway ends at the intersection (L-858C).

What is L-858Y? L-858R? L-858L?

Direction/destination/boundary; mandatory/holding; taxiway and runway location.

What are L-858B and L-858H?

Runway distance remaining and one-half distance remaining.

Which side of the taxiway do signs normally go?

The left side as seen by the approaching pilot, with exceptions.

What minimum clearance is needed above the sign?

12 in (30 cm) from the critical aircraft, per AC 150/5340-18H.

Does a surface-painted sign replace the elevated sign?

No; it supplements it.

Must signs share a circuit with edge lights?

No; they must operate simultaneously but not necessarily on the same circuit.

What do you check on a dark sign?

Supply, protection, control, driver, light source, internal wiring and monitoring.

Is a stop bar a marking?

No; it is a lighting system.

Final Study Takeaway

FINAL STUDY TAKEAWAY

Markings show the surface path, signs give coded navigation information, and lighting makes the guidance visible. Study them as one system: geometry → designation → markings → signs → lighting → control → pilot navigation.

Taxiway guidance = centerline + edge + holding markings + location + direction + mandatory + destination signs + taxiway lighting + stop bars + control + maintenance + correct designation.
