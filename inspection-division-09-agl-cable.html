Division 09 of 25 · INS-CBL Series

AGL Cable Inspection

A standardized methodology for assessing the physical, mechanical, environmental, installation, identification, jointing, termination, and electrical-test condition of AGL primary and associated cables. Covers primary series-circuit cable routes, cable identification, markers, trenches, ducts, cable depth/interface condition, cable separation and protection, insulation, mechanical damage, joints, joint sealing, terminations, connectors, water ingress, and applicable cable test records.

Initial Issue

Critical technical control — AGL cable inspection should not be limited to visible cable damage. A cable can appear physically healthy while having insulation deterioration, moisture ingress, joint degradation, termination problems, or electrical leakage. The complete inspection chain is CCR → Primary Cable → Cable Joint → Isolating Transformer → Secondary Lead → AGL Fixture. Particular attention should be given to cable joints, terminations, connectors, ducts, pits, and water-prone areas, as these are common interfaces where hidden defects develop. Where visual inspection identifies suspected insulation deterioration, water ingress, mechanical damage, abnormal circuit behaviour, or joint/termination defects, escalate for continuity, insulation-resistance, fault-location, or other engineering electrical testing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about agl cable inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook.

Q1What is Division 09 — AGL Cable Inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook?

Division 09 contains 90 published topics in 16 sections (A–P). It gives a standardized methodology for assessing the physical, mechanical, environmental, installation, identification, jointing, termination and electrical-test condition of AGL primary and associated cables.

Q2What does the AGL cable inspection cover?

It covers primary series-circuit cable routes, cable identification and markers, trenches, ducts and conduits, cable depth and interface condition, separation and protection, insulation, mechanical damage, joints and joint sealing, terminations, connectors, water ingress, and applicable cable test records.

Q3What parameters are recorded for AGL cables?

The checklist includes cable identification, circuit association, route, route clearance, installation depth, cable and warning markers, insulation, sheath, mechanical condition, bending and tension, joints, joint sealing, terminations, connectors including L-823, water ingress, duct, trench, drainage, separation, physical protection, continuity and insulation test records, and fault history.

Q4Does the cable inspection review test records?

Yes. Section N covers cable testing and electrical condition, and the workflow includes reviewing electrical test records and fault history. The checklist records continuity and insulation test status as Acceptable or Deficient.

Q5Does Division 09 address construction near cable routes?

Yes. Section O covers cable route modification and construction control, and the checklist records route clearance as Clear or Encroached.

Q6How are cable defects classified?

Defect status is recorded using the four-level scheme Normal, Defective, Unserviceable and Immediate Action Required, so every observation ends with a clear classification before it is recorded and escalated.

Q7Should cable inspection be limited to visible cable damage?

No. A cable can appear physically healthy while having insulation deterioration, moisture ingress, joint degradation, termination problems or electrical leakage. Particular attention goes to joints, terminations, connectors, ducts, pits and water-prone areas, and suspected deterioration is escalated for continuity, insulation-resistance, fault-location or other engineering electrical testing.

Q8Do airport procedures take precedence over this handbook?

Yes. This handbook is an uncontrolled educational reference, not an approved procedure. The airport's approved inspection schedule, SMS procedures, ATC instructions, NOTAM procedures and local aerodrome regulations always take precedence, and the current revision of any official document should be verified before use.

Q9Can I download a printable checklist for each inspection topic?

Yes. Each inspection topic has a Download as Word option that produces an uncontrolled Word checklist template (description, a tick-box inspection table and an inspector sign-off block) for personal reference and internal training, to be reviewed and adopted under the user’s own document control.
