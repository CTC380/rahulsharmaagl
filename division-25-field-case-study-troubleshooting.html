FCS-581 CCR Trip After Rain

Symptom — A CCR serving a runway edge lighting circuit repeatedly tripped on earth-fault protection during and shortly after rainfall events, with the circuit testing normal in dry conditions, prompting a dedicated wet-weather field investigation.

Equipment / System

Primary series circuit cable, joints, and pull boxes along the affected runway edge lighting circuit, and the CCR's earth-fault protection relay.

Possible Causes
HighA degraded joint seal allowing rainwater to bridge a marginal insulation defect only under wet conditions
HighA pull box with a failed lid seal collecting standing water during heavy rain
MedA cable jacket nick from a previous, undocumented repair that only becomes conductive when wet
MedCCR earth-fault relay set at an overly sensitive threshold reacting to a normally acceptable leakage increase
LowA nearby lightning strike coinciding with rain events, causing confusion between two distinct causes
LowRising groundwater at one specific low point along the route
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)CCR earth-fault trend log correlated with weather station dataMoisture detection meterTDR / fault locator
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before all cable/joint work.
  • Treat repeated wet-weather trips as an active hazard rather than a nuisance pattern.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCorrelate the CCR trip log precisely against rainfall timing and intensity from the site weather station over several events.
  2. MeasurementMegger the circuit in segments during a dry period to establish a baseline, then repeat immediately after the next rain event.
  3. MeasurementIdentify the segment showing the largest resistance drop between the dry and wet readings.
  4. DecisionDrop isolated to one segment/joint → excavate and repair that specific point. Drop distributed across many segments → treat as circuit-wide ageing requiring a broader replacement plan.
  5. Fault LocationPinpoint the specific joint or pull box responsible using the wet/dry differential.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the repaired point no longer shows a wet/dry differential over a subsequent rain event.
Test Points
Segment insulation resistance, dry vs. wet
CCR earth-fault trip log vs. weather data
Joint/pull box seal condition at the identified location
Root Cause Verification

Monitor the CCR trip log through at least two subsequent significant rain events after repair and confirm no recurrence.

Corrective Action
  • Reseal or replace the failed joint/pull box seal.
  • Repair the identified cable jacket defect.
Preventive Action
  • Correlate periodic insulation testing with weather timing rather than testing only in dry conditions.
  • Prioritise seal inspection ahead of the wet season on circuits with any trip history.

FCS-582 CCR Trip at Maximum Intensity

Symptom — A CCR operated correctly at low and medium intensity steps but tripped on overcurrent/earth-fault protection specifically when commanded to the maximum intensity step, with the circuit passing a standard insulation resistance test at lower voltage.

Equipment / System

Primary series circuit insulation system, evaluated specifically under the higher voltage stress present at maximum CCR output.

Possible Causes
HighA marginal insulation defect in a joint or cable section with a breakdown voltage low enough to pass at lower steps but fail at maximum output
HighA transformer with a degrading winding that only breaks down under the higher voltage stress of full intensity
MedA connector with a small air gap causing corona/arcing only at higher voltage
MedThe CCR itself producing an output waveform anomaly (harmonic distortion) specifically at maximum step, stressing the circuit
LowA design margin issue where the circuit's total transformer count is near the CCR's rated capacity, causing voltage stress at maximum output
LowA nuisance trip from the CCR's own protection calibration at the top of its range
Required Tools
High-potential (hipot) test setCCR output waveform analyser/oscilloscope1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Transformer test set
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before hipot testing.
  • Hipot testing uses elevated voltages and must only be performed by qualified personnel with appropriate PPE.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the trip is repeatable specifically at maximum intensity and not at any lower step, ruling out a simple overcurrent condition.
  2. MeasurementPerform a hipot test on circuit segments at a voltage representative of maximum CCR output to find the marginal defect that standard megger testing missed.
  3. MeasurementTest individual transformers for a winding that only leaks under higher voltage stress.
  4. DecisionHipot test identifies a specific segment/transformer → targeted repair or replacement. No specific point identified → investigate CCR output quality and circuit loading margin.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific segment, joint, or transformer failing under elevated voltage.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the circuit holds at maximum intensity over a full test cycle after repair.
Test Points
Hipot test result per segment at elevated voltage
Individual transformer winding test under load
CCR output waveform at maximum step
Circuit transformer count vs. CCR rated capacity
Root Cause Verification

Operate the CCR at maximum intensity for an extended period after repair and confirm no trip recurs.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the identified marginal cable/joint section.
  • Replace the failing transformer.
  • Address a CCR output waveform issue with the manufacturer/vendor.
Preventive Action
  • Include a periodic hipot test, not just standard megger, for circuits with any history of intensity-dependent faults.
  • Review circuit loading margin against CCR capacity during any circuit extension.

FCS-583 One Light Failure in Healthy Circuit

Symptom — A single fixture went dark while every circuit-level test (CCR current, insulation resistance, continuity) returned normal readings, suggesting the fault was isolated entirely within the fixture or its immediate connection rather than the circuit.

Equipment / System

The individual fixture, its isolation transformer, and the L-823 connector pair at that specific location, evaluated in isolation from the otherwise healthy circuit.

Possible Causes
HighLamp/LED failure within the fixture itself, unrelated to circuit health
HighIsolation transformer failure at that specific location
MedA corroded or loose L-823 connector at that fixture only
MedA secondary cable break inside the base can specific to that fixture
LowA manufacturing defect isolated to that one fixture unit
LowPhysical damage to that specific fixture, such as impact or vibration, not affecting the shared circuit
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeterSpare fixture/lamp/LED moduleSpare isolation transformerBase can access tools
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before opening the base can even though the primary circuit tests healthy.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm CCR circuit current and overall circuit insulation resistance are both normal, ruling out a shared-circuit cause.
  2. MeasurementTest the isolation transformer output at this specific location.
  3. MeasurementTest the fixture/lamp/LED module directly, bypassing the transformer if needed to isolate the fault further.
  4. DecisionTransformer output normal but fixture dark → fixture/lamp/LED fault. Transformer output abnormal → transformer fault.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the fault is isolated to this single location's transformer, connector, or fixture.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the replaced component restores normal operation without affecting circuit-level readings.
Test Points
Isolation transformer secondary output at the location
Fixture/lamp/LED module continuity
L-823 connector condition
Secondary cable continuity within the base can
Root Cause Verification

Re-energise and confirm the fixture illuminates correctly, and confirm the overall circuit current remains unchanged, verifying no other unit was affected.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed lamp/LED module, transformer, or connector as identified.
Preventive Action
  • Stock spare fixtures/transformers for rapid single-point replacement.
  • Use isolated fixture testing as the default diagnostic starting point whenever circuit-level readings are normal, avoiding unnecessary circuit-wide investigation.
Related References

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-46, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

FCS-584 Multiple Lights Failure After Cable Work

Symptom — Several fixtures along a section of runway or taxiway went dark within hours of nearby, unrelated cable work being completed by another crew, prompting an investigation into possible collateral damage.

Equipment / System

The primary series circuit cable and joints in the vicinity of the recent cable work, and the group of fixtures downstream of that section.

Possible Causes
HighThe other crew's excavation nicked or stressed the AGL cable without a complete break, causing a delayed-onset fault
HighA joint disturbed during the nearby work was not properly reseated or resealed
MedBackfill compaction near the work site placed mechanical stress on the AGL cable
MedThe other crew's work was not properly coordinated via utility locate, and minor incidental contact went unreported
LowThe timing is coincidental and the fault has an unrelated cause
LowVibration from heavy equipment during the work loosened a nearby connector
Required Tools
TDR / fault locator1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)As-built drawing cross-referenced with the other crew's work permit/locate requestVisual inspection at the work site
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before any investigation.
  • Coordinate with the other work crew and their supervisor regarding the exact extent and location of their excavation.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationMap the affected fixtures and confirm their circuit position downstream of the recent work location.
  2. ObservationObtain the other crew's work permit/locate request and excavation extent for cross-reference.
  3. MeasurementMegger and TDR the circuit section adjacent to the work site.
  4. DecisionFault localised to the work site vicinity → proceed with excavation and repair at that point.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the specific point of collateral damage.
  6. Root CauseConfirm repair restores all affected fixtures and document the incident against the works coordination record.
Test Points
TDR distance-to-fault relative to the work site
Insulation resistance in segments near the work location
Visual inspection of backfill and cable condition at the site
Root Cause Verification

After repair, confirm all affected fixtures illuminate correctly and megger the section to confirm insulation resistance is restored.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the damaged cable/joint section identified near the work site.
Preventive Action
  • Require a post-work verification test on any AGL circuit near third-party excavation before the work is signed off as complete.
  • Strengthen utility locate and coordination requirements for any work near AGL cable routes.

FCS-585 Transformer Failure After Flooding

Symptom — A cluster of isolation transformers in a low-lying section of the airfield failed simultaneously following a flooding event, with the affected fixtures dark or flickering once floodwater receded and the circuit was re-energised.

Equipment / System

Isolation transformers and their base can enclosures within the flooded area of the affected circuit.

Possible Causes
HighTransformer windings submerged beyond their rated ingress protection, causing internal short circuits or insulation breakdown
HighContaminated floodwater (silt, chemicals) accelerating insulation degradation inside the transformer housing
MedBase can enclosures not rated for the duration/depth of submersion experienced
MedTransformers re-energised before adequate drying and testing, causing secondary damage
LowPhysical debris carried by floodwater damaging transformer connections
LowA design assumption that the location would not flood, now proven incorrect by the event
Required Tools
1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Moisture detection meterReplacement transformers matched to specificationVisual/physical inspection
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO all affected circuits before any inspection.
  • Treat all submerged transformers as unsafe to energise until fully assessed.
  • Be alert to contamination hazards in floodwater-affected enclosures.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationMap the full extent of the flooded area and identify every transformer within it, not just those confirmed failed.
  2. MeasurementMegger every transformer in the flooded zone individually before considering any re-energisation.
  3. MeasurementInspect for contamination and physical damage in addition to simple moisture.
  4. DecisionTransformer insulation resistance acceptable after drying → return to service with continued monitoring. Insulation resistance remains degraded or contamination present → replace the transformer.
  5. Fault LocationIdentify every individual transformer requiring replacement versus those that can be dried and verified.
  6. Root CauseConfirm each transformer is individually verified before restoring the circuit.
Test Points
Individual transformer insulation resistance
Contamination assessment
Base can enclosure condition and seal rating
Root Cause Verification

After replacement/repair, re-megger all affected transformers, re-energise progressively, and monitor closely over the following days for delayed degradation.

Corrective Action
  • Replace transformers that fail insulation testing or show contamination.
  • Dry and re-test transformers with acceptable readings before returning to service.
Preventive Action
  • Review flood risk for low-lying base can locations and consider elevated or better-sealed enclosures.
  • Develop a flood response procedure prioritising critical circuit assessment ahead of routine work.
Related References

FCS-586 Transformer Overheating Under Load

Symptom — A specific isolation transformer was found running noticeably hotter than adjacent transformers under normal operating current, discovered during a routine thermal imaging survey rather than through a light output complaint.

Equipment / System

The specific isolation transformer, its base can enclosure, and its position within the series circuit loading.

Possible Causes
HighInternal winding degradation increasing the transformer's internal resistance and heat generation under normal current
HighThe transformer connected to a fixture drawing higher-than-rated current, such as an incorrect replacement lamp/LED wattage
MedInadequate heat dissipation due to a poorly ventilated or debris-filled base can
MedThe transformer itself undersized or mismatched for the circuit's actual current rating
LowAmbient ground temperature or a nearby heat source elevating the baseline temperature
LowA normal but previously unnoticed operating characteristic for this transformer type, requiring baseline comparison rather than assuming a fault
Required Tools
Thermal imaging cameraClamp ammeterTransformer specification referenceBase can inspection tools
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before hands-on inspection.
  • Allow the transformer to cool before handling if recently energised.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompare the thermal reading against adjacent transformers of the same type and loading to establish whether this is a genuine anomaly.
  2. MeasurementMeasure the actual current through the transformer and compare against its rated capacity.
  3. MeasurementInspect the connected fixture to confirm the correct lamp/LED wattage is installed.
  4. DecisionCurrent within rating but transformer still hot → investigate internal winding degradation via insulation/resistance testing. Current above rating → correct the fixture load or transformer sizing.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to internal winding degradation, an oversized load, or poor heat dissipation.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the temperature returns to a normal comparative range after correction.
Test Points
Thermal image vs. adjacent transformers
Current through the transformer vs. rated capacity
Fixture lamp/LED wattage verification
Base can ventilation/debris condition
Root Cause Verification

Re-thermal-image the transformer under normal load after correction and confirm its temperature is consistent with adjacent units.

Corrective Action
  • Replace a transformer with degraded internal windings.
  • Correct the fixture to the specified lamp/LED wattage.
  • Clear base can ventilation obstruction.
  • Replace with a correctly sized transformer.
Preventive Action
  • Include thermal imaging surveys in the periodic PM cycle rather than relying solely on light-output complaints.
  • Verify replacement lamp/LED specifications against the original design at every service visit.

FCS-587 Connector Burning in Series Circuit

Symptom — A primary series circuit connector was found visibly charred or burned during a routine inspection, with the associated fixture still functioning but the connector clearly showing signs of localised overheating and arcing.

Equipment / System

The primary series circuit connector at the affected joint, and the transformer/cable interface it serves.

Possible Causes
HighA high-resistance connection at the connector generating sufficient heat to char the surrounding insulation over time
HighMoisture ingress causing intermittent arcing at the connector contact
MedA connector rated for a lower current than the circuit's actual operating current
MedCorrosion at the connector contact surface increasing resistance progressively
LowA manufacturing defect in the specific connector
LowMechanical stress on the connector from cable movement, causing repeated micro-arcing at a loose contact
Required Tools
Thermal imaging cameraMicro-ohmmeterVisual inspectionReplacement connector kit matched to circuit specification
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before any work at the burned connector.
  • Treat a burned connector as an active fire/arc-flash risk and do not delay isolation.
  • Use appropriate PPE given potential residual heat or damaged insulation.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationDocument the extent of the burning and assess whether it has compromised the surrounding cable insulation beyond the connector itself.
  2. MeasurementThermal image adjacent connectors on the same circuit to determine whether this is an isolated defect or a systemic issue.
  3. MeasurementInspect the connector internals for the specific cause: corrosion, moisture, or a loose/under-crimped contact.
  4. DecisionIsolated defective connector → replace and verify. Pattern found across multiple connectors of the same type/batch → escalate to a circuit-wide inspection and potential connector type replacement program.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the extent of damage requiring cable repair versus connector-only replacement.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the new connector shows no thermal signature under normal load after replacement.
Test Points
Thermal signature of the replaced connector under load
Adjacent connector thermal survey
Connector resistance via micro-ohmmeter
Cable insulation condition immediately adjacent to the burned connector
Root Cause Verification

Re-thermal-image the repaired connector after a period of normal operation and confirm no recurring hot spot.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the burned connector and any cable insulation compromised by the heat.
  • Address the root cause identified: moisture sealing, correct connector rating, or proper crimp technique.
Preventive Action
  • Include thermal imaging of accessible connectors in the periodic PM cycle.
  • Standardise on connectors correctly rated for the circuit's operating current.
  • Investigate any connector batch showing a repeat pattern of failure.
Related References

Standards: IEC 60502, FAA AC 150/5345-26

FCS-588 Cable Insulation Failure Underground

Symptom — A buried section of primary series cable was identified as having failed insulation resistance during a routine periodic test, with no visible surface indication and no reported operational fault at the time of discovery.

Equipment / System

The buried cable section identified by the periodic insulation resistance test, prior to any excavation or visual confirmation.

Possible Causes
HighLong-term cumulative ageing of the cable insulation reaching end of service life
HighAn undiscovered installation-era defect (nick, substandard joint) that has progressively worsened over years of burial
MedLocalised soil chemistry or moisture conditions accelerating degradation in that specific section
MedA previously unreported mechanical disturbance, such as root growth or ground settlement, affecting the buried cable
LowRodent damage at a point not yet manifesting as a visible surface issue
LowA testing or measurement error requiring confirmation before committing to excavation
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)TDR / fault locatorAs-built drawingExcavation equipment
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before testing.
  • Coordinate utility locate before any excavation is planned.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the failed reading through a repeat test with calibrated equipment to rule out a measurement error.
  2. MeasurementSegment the circuit to narrow down the specific section responsible for the low reading.
  3. MeasurementUse TDR to estimate the fault location within the identified segment before committing to excavation extent.
  4. DecisionReading confirmed and localised → proceed with targeted excavation and repair. Reading marginal and not clearly localised → continue monitoring with more frequent testing before excavating.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the precise excavation point using TDR and, where needed, a secondary locating method.
  6. Root CauseUpon excavation, visually confirm the actual defect matches the electrical signature predicted.
Test Points
Segment insulation resistance
TDR distance-to-fault
Visual condition of the cable upon excavation
Root Cause Verification

After repair, megger the full loop and confirm the reading is restored to, or above, the acceptable threshold, then continue periodic monitoring.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the identified buried cable section.
Preventive Action
  • Schedule periodic insulation resistance testing on a defined interval for all buried circuits, rather than relying on operational faults to reveal ageing insulation.
  • Prioritise proactive replacement of circuit sections approaching known end-of-life age.

FCS-589 Intermittent Earth Fault

Symptom — A circuit's earth-fault monitoring showed repeated, brief trip events that cleared on their own or on a simple reset, with no fault found during standard testing performed shortly after each event, making the condition difficult to pin down over several weeks.

Equipment / System

The full primary series circuit, evaluated for a fault condition present only intermittently rather than continuously.

Possible Causes
HighA marginal insulation defect that only becomes conductive under specific conditions (temperature, vibration, moisture) not present at the time of routine testing
HighA loose or partially broken conductor making and breaking contact under vibration from aircraft/ground traffic
MedA connector with thermal-cycling-dependent contact, closing the fault path only at a certain temperature
MedA nearby source of electrical interference, or a genuinely marginal CCR earth-fault relay setting, causing some events to be false positives mixed with real ones
LowWildlife activity, such as chewing or nesting, causing an intermittent contact at unpredictable times
LowA fault correlated with a specific, infrequent operational condition, such as only at maximum intensity or during specific weather, not yet identified
Required Tools
CCR earth-fault trend log with timestamps1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Continuous monitoring/data-logging equipmentWeather and operational condition correlation data
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before any hands-on testing.
  • Treat an intermittent fault as an ongoing hazard even between events.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompile a detailed timeline of every trip event with exact timestamp, and cross-reference against weather, temperature, and CCR intensity step at the time.
  2. MeasurementInstall temporary continuous monitoring/data-logging equipment on the circuit to capture conditions at the moment of the next event, since standard spot-testing after the fact has proven insufficient.
  3. MeasurementOnce a correlating condition is identified, attempt to reproduce it under controlled testing, such as testing at maximum intensity or physically flexing suspect connectors.
  4. DecisionA condition is successfully correlated and reproduced → proceed to isolate the specific component responsible under that condition. No clear correlation after extended monitoring → broaden the investigation to a full segment-by-segment stress test.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific component or condition-dependent defect once reproducible.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the fault no longer occurs under the previously identified triggering condition after repair.
Test Points
Timestamped trip log correlated with weather/temperature/intensity
Continuous monitoring data capturing conditions at the moment of fault
Reproducibility of the fault under controlled test conditions
Root Cause Verification

Monitor the circuit over an extended period covering the range of previously implicated conditions and confirm no further trip events occur.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the specific component identified as condition-dependent, such as a loose conductor, a thermally sensitive connector, or a marginal insulation defect.
  • Adjust CCR earth-fault relay sensitivity only if a false-positive component of the pattern is confirmed and remains within acceptable safety limits.
Preventive Action
  • Install permanent trend-logging with sufficient resolution to capture intermittent events on circuits with any history of unexplained trips.
  • Treat repeated 'no fault found' results after a trip event as a signal to escalate to continuous monitoring rather than closing out the investigation.
Related References

SOP: SOP-FCS-09 (Field Case Investigation Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364, FAA AC 150/5340-30

FCS-590 Repeated LED Fixture Failure

Symptom — The same model of LED fixture failed repeatedly across multiple locations on the airfield well before its expected service life, prompting an investigation into whether the cause was fixture design, environmental conditions, or installation practice.

Equipment / System

The specific LED fixture model and its driver, evaluated across multiple failed units to identify a common failure pattern.

Possible Causes
HighA systemic LED driver design weakness, such as inadequate surge protection or thermal management, affecting the whole batch/model
HighA mismatch between the fixture's environmental rating and the airfield's actual operating conditions (temperature extremes, moisture exposure)
MedA common installation practice error repeated across all affected locations, such as incorrect torque or moisture entry at a specific gasket
MedA specific batch/lot of fixtures with a manufacturing defect, distinct from the model as a whole
LowA power quality issue (voltage transients, harmonics) on the circuits feeding these fixtures common to all failures
LowCoincidental unrelated failures being incorrectly grouped as a pattern
Required Tools
Failed unit collection and bench-testing setupLED driver diagnostic toolPower quality loggerManufacturer failure analysis support/RMA process
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before removing any failed unit.
  • Follow manufacturer guidance for handling failed LED drivers, which may retain stored energy in capacitors.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompile failure data across all affected locations: installation date, failure date, location/environment, and circuit.
  2. MeasurementBench-test a sample of failed units to identify the specific internal failure mode: driver component, LED array, or thermal damage.
  3. MeasurementCheck power quality on the circuits feeding the affected fixtures for any common transient or harmonic issue.
  4. DecisionCommon internal failure mode across units → escalate to the manufacturer as a design/batch issue and request failure analysis. Failure mode varies with no common pattern → treat as installation practice at each site individually.
  5. Fault LocationIdentify whether the failure pattern points to the fixture design, a specific batch, installation practice, or circuit power quality.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the corrective action — replacement fixture, corrected installation practice, or power quality correction — eliminates the recurrence.
Test Points
Bench-test failure mode analysis across a sample of failed units
Power quality measurement at affected circuits
Installation records (date, method, technician) for affected locations
Environmental exposure comparison between failed and non-failed units of the same model
Root Cause Verification

Monitor the replacement fixtures, or the corrected installation, over an extended period, at least matching the previous failures' typical time-to-failure, and confirm no recurrence.

Corrective Action
  • Replace affected fixtures with a corrected design/batch per manufacturer guidance.
  • Correct the identified installation practice.
  • Address a power quality issue at its source.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a fixture failure log by model and batch to detect emerging patterns early.
  • Engage the manufacturer proactively when a repeat failure pattern is first suspected rather than after many units have failed.
  • Verify environmental rating suitability before standardising on a fixture model for airfield use.
Related References

FCS-591 Sign Board Partial Illumination

Symptom — An internally illuminated airfield sign was found with only part of its legend or background illuminated, with some segments dark while others remained lit, making the sign's message unclear or misleading.

Equipment / System

Internally illuminated sign panel, its internal light source (LED array or LED panel), and the associated ballast/driver.

Possible Causes
HighPartial failure of an LED array serving only part of the sign panel
HighA driver serving one section of the sign failed while a separate driver for another section remains functional
MedA physical partition or reflector issue within the sign causing uneven light distribution even with a functioning source
MedA wiring fault to one specific portion of the internal lighting
LowA partially failed connector affecting only part of the internal circuit
LowSign panel damage, such as a cracked or delaminated face, reducing apparent illumination in one area without an actual electrical fault
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeterSign panel access toolsSpare LED array/driver matched to sign typeVisual inspection light
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before opening the sign housing.
  • Treat mandatory instruction signs as operationally critical given their role in preventing runway incursions, and prioritise repair accordingly.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationDocument exactly which portion of the sign is dark and correlate with the sign's internal lighting zone layout.
  2. MeasurementWith the housing open and circuit isolated, test each internal lighting zone/driver independently.
  3. MeasurementInspect the sign face and internal reflectors for physical damage separate from the electrical fault.
  4. DecisionSpecific zone's source/driver confirmed faulty → replace that component. Physical panel damage found → assess for panel replacement in addition to any electrical repair.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific internal zone, source, driver, or panel defect.
  6. Root CauseConfirm uniform illumination across the full sign face after repair.
Test Points
Individual internal lighting zone output
Driver function per zone
Wiring continuity to the affected zone
Physical panel condition
Root Cause Verification

Re-energise and visually confirm uniform, full illumination of the sign legend and background at night before closing out.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed LED array or driver for the affected zone.
  • Repair the wiring fault.
  • Replace a damaged sign panel.
Preventive Action
  • Include full-panel illumination verification, not just 'sign is lit', in the periodic PM cycle.

MedA broader infrastructure ageing effect coinciding across many circuits installed in the same era, rather than any single distinct cause
LowA change in fault detection/reporting sensitivity, such as a new monitoring system, revealing faults that previously went unnoticed rather than a genuine increase in the underlying fault rate

  1. ObservationCompile the complete fault history for the affected category over an extended period and establish the actual baseline fault rate for comparison, confirming the increase is statistically real rather than a perception.

  • Address the specific common cause identified, whether through proactive replacement of an at-risk batch, correction of a maintenance practice, or another targeted intervention across all affected locations rather than one-by-one reactive repair.
Preventive Action
  • Establish and maintain a centralised fault history database with sufficient detail (installation date, batch, contractor) to enable future pattern detection at the earliest possible point.
