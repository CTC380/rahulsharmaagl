AGL Installation & Commissioning

AGL installation and commissioning is the stage where the designed airfield lighting system becomes an operational system. A component may be correctly manufactured and a design may be technically correct, but the final system can still perform incorrectly because of installation errors, incorrect connections, poor cable termination, transformer mismatch, inadequate earthing, incorrect fixture positioning, optical misalignment or incomplete testing. A proper commissioning process verifies the complete system — not simply whether the lights turn ON.

Receive → Inspect → Install → Connect → Test → Align → Energize → Measure → Commission → Handover

01 · Definitions

What Is AGL Installation & Commissioning?

AGL Installation is the physical installation and connection of airfield lighting equipment according to approved drawings, specifications and applicable standards.

AGL Commissioning is the systematic process of testing, measuring, adjusting and verifying the installed system before it is accepted for operational use.

Re-Testing After Rectification

A defect is not closed simply because corrective work is completed: Defect → Rectification → Inspection → Re-test → Result → Close. A repaired cable repeats the electrical test; a replaced connector repeats continuity/insulation checks; a replaced fixture repeats functional and optical verification; an adjusted PAPI repeats alignment/photometry; a corrected CCR fault repeats operational testing.

Never hand over drawings that represent the design but not the actual installation — as-built documentation must reflect the actual installed system.
Keyword Reference

Airport Lighting Installation: Overview, Procedure & Method Statement

How an airfield lighting installation is planned, sequenced and documented before work starts on site.

Airport lighting installation

Airport lighting installation is the physical work of placing, connecting and securing airfield lighting equipment to the approved design. It covers civil preparation, cable and duct work, transformers, connectors, fixtures, CCR connection and earthing. Done well, it is the foundation for a commissioning that passes first time.

Airfield lighting installation overview

At overview level, installation follows a chain: engineering review, material receipt, civil and foundation verification, cable installation, transformer and connector installation, fixture installation and CCR connection. Each step has its own checks and hold points before the next one starts. See the AGL Installation Chain and Pre-Installation Engineering Review sections above.

AGL installation procedure

An AGL installation procedure sets out the order of work, who does it, the checks at each stage and the records to be kept. A typical procedure covers setting out, base and foundation work, cable laying, termination, fixture mounting, alignment and pre-energization inspection. It should follow the manufacturer's instructions and the project specification.

Airport lighting installation method statement

A method statement describes how a specific piece of airfield lighting work will be carried out safely and correctly. It normally covers scope, resources, sequence, equipment, quality checks, airside safety, permits, working-hours restrictions and coordination with airport operations. It is usually approved before work begins.

AGL method statement

An AGL method statement is the working document the site team follows. It should be specific to the system involved, such as edge lights, centreline lights or approach lights, and should state the hold points where inspection is required before work continues.

Keyword Reference

Elevated Lights, Base Cans, Shallow Bases & Foundation Details

Mounting arrangements and the civil work that supports the fixture.

Elevated light installation

Elevated light installation covers lights mounted above the surface on frangible supports, such as many edge and approach lights. Key points are correct height and orientation, frangible coupling arrangements, secure foundation, correct connector mating and protection of leads. Always check against the project drawings and the standard being followed.

Airfield lighting base can installation

Base can installation provides the housing in which an in-pavement fixture and its transformer sit. The base must be positioned and levelled to the design, drained and sealed as specified, and protected during paving and construction. Errors in position or level are difficult and costly to correct later.

AGL shallow base installation

Shallow base installation is used where depth is limited, for example in thin pavement or overlay situations. It needs the same attention to position, level, sealing and cable entry as a conventional base can, with extra care over fixture fit and drainage. Follow the manufacturer's instructions for the specific base type.

Airport lighting foundation details

Foundation details show how each fixture, base or support is anchored, including dimensions, materials and the cable and duct entry arrangements. They come from the civil and electrical drawings, and the installer should verify the foundation matches them before fitting equipment. See Civil & Foundation Verification above.

Keyword Reference

AGL Installation Testing, Defects & Pre-Commissioning

Proving the installation is sound before the circuit is energised.

AGL installation testing

Installation testing proves the work is electrically and mechanically sound before commissioning. It normally includes cable and insulation resistance testing, continuity checks, connector inspection and earthing checks, with results recorded against the project's acceptance criteria. Figures come from the project specification, so none are given here. See the Electrical Testing section above.

AGL installation defects

Typical installation defects include poorly made connectors, water ingress, damaged cable insulation, wrong fixture orientation, incorrect transformer rating, poor earthing and base level errors. Defects should be logged, assigned and closed with evidence, and repeat defects fed back into the method statement.

Airfield lighting pre-commissioning

Pre-commissioning is the set of checks done before first energization: verifying the installation matches drawings, completing insulation and continuity tests, confirming labelling and checking that protection and control settings are correct. It reduces the chance of a fault appearing during energization. See Pre-Energization Inspection above.

Keyword Reference

Airfield Lighting Commissioning: Procedure, SAT Testing, Report & Dossier

Bringing the system into service and documenting that it meets requirements.

Airfield lighting commissioning

Airfield lighting commissioning proves that the installed system works as designed and is ready for operational use. It covers energization, functional testing, brightness step checks, control and monitoring checks, alignment and photometric verification, followed by documented acceptance.

Airfield ground lighting commissioning

Airfield ground lighting commissioning is commissioning of the full AGL system, including circuits, CCRs, fixtures and control. It is carried out system by system, such as runway, taxiway and approach lighting, and ends with a commissioning package and handover. See Commissioning by System above.

AGL commissioning procedure

An AGL commissioning procedure defines the order of tests, the conditions for each, acceptance criteria, safety rules and the people who witness or sign off. It is normally agreed with the airport operator before testing begins.

Airport lighting SAT testing

SAT, or site acceptance testing, is the formal demonstration on site that the installed system meets the contract and design requirements. It is witnessed by the client or their representative, and results are recorded and signed off. Test content and acceptance values come from the project specification.

Airfield lighting commissioning report

The commissioning report records what was tested, the results, any deviations and their closure, and the final status of the system. It should be traceable to circuits, equipment and test records, and signed by the responsible parties. See Commissioning Package, Report & Punch-List Management above.

AGL commissioning report template

A commissioning report template gives each project a consistent layout: project and system identification, equipment list, test results, punch list, deviations, photographs and sign-off blocks. Adapt the template to your project specification and the client's required format.

AGL commissioning dossier

The commissioning dossier is the complete handover file. It typically gathers as-built drawings, test records, calibration certificates, commissioning reports, punch-list closure, manuals and warranties, so the operator has a full record of the system as delivered.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is AGL installation and commissioning?

AGL installation is the physical installation and connection of airfield lighting equipment according to approved drawings, specifications and applicable standards. AGL commissioning is the systematic process of testing, measuring, adjusting and verifying the installed system before it is accepted for operational use. Commissioning verifies the complete system, not simply whether the lights turn on.

Q2Why does installation quality matter so much in AGL?

An installation error can create a system-level problem. For example, an incorrect transformer rating can cause abnormal fixture operation, a poor connector installation can cause intermittent light failure, incorrect cable termination can lead to insulation failure, incorrect fixture elevation can cause an optical performance problem, and water ingress can cause repeated electrical failures.

Q3What is the AGL installation and commissioning philosophy?

Do not energize first and inspect later. The installation should progress through controlled stages, never in reverse: verify, install, inspect, test, energize, measure, adjust, verify again, document and hand over.

Q4What are the stages from design to handover?

The process runs through design, installation, inspection, electrical testing, functional testing, alignment, photometry, commissioning and handover.

Q5What should be checked before AGL installation starts?

A pre-installation review covers drawings (approved layout, circuit drawings, cable routing, equipment locations, foundation drawings), equipment (correct items, quantities, ratings, specifications and manufacturer documentation) and the site (work area released, survey references, civil works completed, cable routes, foundations, access and work permits).

Q6Why is equipment traceability important?

Every major AGL component should be inspected on receipt and traceable afterwards. A traceability record can include equipment ID, manufacturer, model, serial number, rating, circuit number, location, installation date and test reference. It is particularly important for CCRs, isolation transformers, light fixtures, PAPI units, signs and control equipment.

Q7Why must civil and foundation work be verified first?

Foundation location, dimensions, elevation, orientation, anchors, drainage, cable entry, mounting interface and frangibility requirements must be verified before equipment is installed. Incorrect civil work should never be compensated for with uncontrolled equipment adjustments.

Q8How can cable damage be avoided during installation?

Avoid excessive pulling force, sharp bends, crushing and abrasion, damaged insulation, improper storage, uncontrolled cable loops and incorrect termination. Cable damage may not be visible externally, so electrical testing is essential, and unnecessary joints should be avoided in critical series circuits.

Q9What is verified when installing the series circuit and isolation transformers?

Verify the circuit sequence, transformer connection, cable continuity, connector compatibility, circuit identification, circuit separation and correct termination. Before installing a transformer, also check its rating, primary and secondary connectors, physical condition, identification, insulation condition, location, water protection and cable compatibility.

Q10How should light fixtures and connectors be installed?

Fixture installation should follow the approved layout strictly, never assumed positions, with correct type, location, orientation, elevation, frangibility arrangement, transformer connection and optical direction. A connector should never be assumed correctly installed simply because it fits, since a poor connector can cause high-resistance connections, intermittent faults or complete circuit failure.

Q11How to commission an AGL system?

Commissioning an AGL system follows a set order: complete installation and pre-commissioning checks, run insulation and continuity tests, carry out a pre-energization inspection, energize each circuit under controlled conditions, then do functional testing at each brightness step. Next check control and monitoring, alignment and photometry, and close out the punch list. The results go into the commissioning report and dossier, and acceptance values come from your project specification.

A circuit can be healthy electrically while optical output or alignment is wrong. Include optical verification in the commissioning scope.

Build the Handover File as the Work Proceeds
Handover

Collecting records at the end invites gaps. Compile test sheets, as-builts and certificates progressively and review them before completion.
