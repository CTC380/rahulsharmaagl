My Experience in Manufacturing CTC Make AGL Isolating Transformers Since 2020

From 360-degree machine winding to injection-molded construction, independent MSME lab testing, and a nationwide vendor-development journey — how CTC Make AGL Isolating Transformers and ESSNA Connectors became AAI-approved products trusted across Indian and international airports.

February 26, 2026 AGL Components Field Experience

In 2020, I entered the manufacturing domain of CTC Make AGL Isolating Transformers with a clear objective: improve the reliability, safety, and lifecycle performance of airfield lighting systems.

Today, CTC Make AGL Isolating Transformers and ESSNA Connectors — Plug, Receptacle & Inline Connectors — are AAI-approved products, widely used in airport projects across India and overseas. This article is a practical account of that journey — the engineering decisions, the testing discipline, and the field lessons that shaped a component that runway and taxiway safety depends on every single night.

Disclosure: I work with Creative Trading Co. (CTC), the manufacturer of the products described here. This is a personal account of our development work, not independent product testing or an endorsement.

Key Takeaway

Reliability in AGL manufacturing is not a marketing claim — it is engineered, batch by batch, through precision winding, moisture-resistant construction, independent lab validation, and disciplined quality control, from prototype to more than 15,000 units in the field.

01

Setting the Context

In a 6.6 A series circuit, the isolating transformer ensures electrical isolation between the primary circuit and the secondary lamp circuit while maintaining continuity across the entire string of fittings. Its reliability directly impacts runway and taxiway safety — a single failed unit (for example, one with an open primary) can affect the whole circuit, not only the fittings near it.

That single fact — one transformer, one point of failure, an entire circuit at stake — is what shaped every manufacturing decision described in this article.

02

Engineering Upgrade — 360-Degree Machine Winding

One of the key technical improvements introduced during development was 360-degree machine winding of transformer coils. Instead of manual or semi-automatic winding, we adopted precision-controlled machine winding to achieve:

  • Uniform turn distribution
  • Better coil compactness
  • Fewer voids between turns
  • Improved magnetic coupling
  • Stable electrical performance

This directly enhanced efficiency, reduced losses, and improved long-term reliability. The objective was not simply to automate manufacturing, but to improve consistency and repeatability in a safety-critical AGL component.

03

Shift to Injection Molding Construction

We developed a fully injection-molded transformer body built to the requirements of FAA AC 150/5345-47, issued by the Federal Aviation Administration (this describes design and test to the specification and is not, by itself, FAA equipment certification under AC 150/5345-53). Injection molding reduced the inconsistencies commonly seen in conventional potting methods, and the advantages achieved included:

Uniform InsulationConsistent thickness across every unit
Mechanical StrengthHigh resistance to handling & installation stress
Moisture ResistanceImproved sealing for underground conditions
Dimensional ConsistencyBetter fit and repeatability batch to batch
Reliability TargetAimed at fewer field failures in underground installs

For underground airfield lighting applications, where equipment can be exposed to moisture and demanding environmental conditions, consistency in construction is particularly important.

04

Development of CTC ESSNA Connectors

Parallel to transformer development, we designed CTC Make ESSNA Connectors — Plug, Receptacle & Inline Connectors — as per FAA AC 150/5345-26. The main focus areas included:

  • Robust connector housing
  • Corrosion-resistant contacts
  • Waterproof sealing system
  • Strong strain relief
  • Reliable series-circuit continuity

The connectors are compatible with FAA-style L-823 configurations used globally in airfield lighting systems. Their development complemented the isolating transformer programme by addressing another critical part of the AGL series circuit.

05

Government MSME Testing — Chennai

To ensure independent validation, we tested all CTC Make AGL Isolating Transformers and ESSNA Connectors at a Government MSME Testing Laboratory in Chennai. Testing covered dielectric withstand, insulation resistance, temperature rise, load testing, and mechanical and sealing checks.

Independent testing was an important part of the development journey — it provided an additional level of technical verification beyond internal manufacturing quality controls.

Manufacturing Scale and Quality Control

From prototype to mass production, we implemented a structured quality-control process:

  • Incoming raw material inspection
  • In-process quality checks

The combination of 360-degree machine winding and injection molding has enabled consistent electrical and mechanical performance across batches. For a critical airport component, manufacturing consistency is essential because reliability must be maintained from one production batch to the next.

What is 360-degree machine winding and why was it adopted?

It is precision-controlled machine winding of transformer coils, used instead of manual or semi-automatic winding. Why use an injection-molded transformer body instead of conventional potting?

The injection-molded body was built to the requirements of FAA AC 150/5345-47 and reduces the inconsistencies commonly seen in conventional potting. The advantages cited are uniform insulation thickness, mechanical strength, improved moisture sealing for underground conditions and dimensional consistency from batch to batch.
