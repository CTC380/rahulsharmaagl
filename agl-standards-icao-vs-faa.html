01 · Understanding the Difference

ICAO vs FAA for AGL in India

ICAO provides international Standards and Recommended Practices for aerodromes, while FAA Advisory Circulars provide U.S. specifications and guidance. For projects in India, the applicable specification depends on the project documentation.

ICAO — commonly relevant for

  • Aerodrome visual aids
  • Runway and taxiway lighting
  • Approach lighting
  • Operational requirements
  • Aerodrome design

FAA — may appear for particular equipment

  • FAA AC 150/5345-46 for airfield lighting equipment
  • FAA Engineering Brief 67D for LED lighting considerations
  • Other FAA equipment specifications where referenced by the project
02 · Both, Together

Both Frameworks in the Same Procurement

AAI tender documents demonstrate that both ICAO and FAA references can occur within the same Indian AGL procurement framework — for example, referencing ICAO Annex 14 Volume I alongside FAA AC 150/5345-46F and FAA Engineering Brief 67D, together with DGCA-CAR compliance.

03 · ICAO in AGL Projects

International Aerodrome Framework

The International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) provides the international framework used for aerodrome design and operation. For AGL engineering, ICAO requirements are particularly relevant to the functional and operational requirements of visual aids. A major reference is ICAO Annex 14, Volume I — Aerodrome Design and Operations.

04 · FAA in AGL Projects

U.S. Airfield Lighting Specifications & Guidance

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) develops specifications, Advisory Circulars and engineering guidance for aviation infrastructure in the United States. In Indian projects, FAA documents may appear when specifically referenced by the tender, airport authority, consultant or equipment specification.

FAA AC 150/5345-46

Used for applicable specifications relating to airfield lighting equipment.

FAA Engineering Brief 67D

May be referenced for LED airfield lighting considerations.

ICAO and FAA should not be treated as interchangeable documents. Their requirements may overlap in subject matter, but their purpose, document structure and technical scope can differ.

06 · Why FAA Can Appear in India

“If the Airport Is in India, Why Does the Tender Mention FAA?”

An Indian AGL tender may reference ICAO + DGCA + AAI + BIS + IEC + FAA within the same project. FAA requirements may be included because of:

Specific equipment requirementsExisting airport infrastructureProcurement specificationsEquipment performance criteriaLED technology requirementsManufacturer requirementsExisting system compatibility

The presence of an FAA reference in an Indian tender does not mean the entire airport project is being designed under the FAA framework. The actual tender wording must be reviewed.

10 · ICAO vs FAA for LED AGL

LED Retrofit Needs More Than an “ICAO or FAA” Answer

An LED retrofit should not be evaluated only by asking whether the fixture is “ICAO” or “FAA” compliant. A complete review considers four areas together.

Fixture

  • Photometric performance & light distribution
  • Colour / chromaticity
  • Beam characteristics
  • Mechanical & environmental performance

Electrical System

  • CCR compatibility
  • Series-circuit characteristics
  • Transformer compatibility
  • Cable condition & connector arrangement

Control System

  • Monitoring & control interface
  • Communication
  • Existing ALCMS compatibility where applicable

Installation

  • Existing mounting arrangement
  • Fixture positioning & orientation
  • Alignment & civil interface

19 · Key Takeaway

No Universal Rule to Follow Only ICAO or Only FAA

The applicable requirements are determined by the project documentation and applicable regulatory and technical framework. A practical approach: identify the project requirement → identify the applicable standard → identify the equipment requirement → verify compliance → verify testing → install correctly → test and commission.

For Indian AGL projects, ICAO, DGCA, AAI / airport specifications, BIS, IEC and FAA references may all form part of the overall compliance framework, but their applicability must be established from the specific project documents.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between ICAO and FAA AGL guidance?
ICAO provides international Standards and Recommended Practices for aerodromes, while FAA Advisory Circulars provide U.S. specifications and guidance. For Indian projects, the applicable specification depends on the project documentation.
Can ICAO and FAA references appear in the same Indian tender?
Yes. AAI tender documents demonstrate that both ICAO and FAA references can occur within the same Indian AGL procurement framework, alongside DGCA-CAR and BIS compliance requirements.
What is the practical approach for choosing the applicable standard?
Identify the project requirement, identify the applicable standard, check equipment compliance, verify testing requirements, then complete installation and commissioning.
Why does an Indian AAI tender reference FAA specifications?
A project may specify particular equipment performance, LED technology, manufacturer or existing-system compatibility requirements. An FAA reference does not mean the whole project is governed under the FAA framework — the complete tender wording must be reviewed.
Is it enough to say a fixture is “ICAO compliant” or “FAA compliant”?
No. A proper compliance statement should identify the specific document, edition/revision, applicable section, equipment, requirement, test and certificate — a general compliance claim doesn't create the traceability a technical submission needs.
Does compliance of one AGL component establish compliance of the whole system?
No. An AGL series circuit runs from the CCR through the primary cable, primary connector, isolating transformer, secondary connector and light fixture to photometric verification — each link needs to be checked, not just the fixture.
