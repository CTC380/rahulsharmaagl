The Guide to AGL Isolating Transformers in the Airfield Lighting Series Circuit

An AGL isolating transformer is a critical interface between the airport series circuit and an individual airfield lighting fixture. It allows individual lighting loads to be connected to a constant-current series circuit while providing electrical isolation between the primary series circuit and the fixture's secondary circuit. Although physically small compared with a CCR or a lighting fixture, it is one of the most important components in the complete AGL circuit.

Overview

What Is an AGL Isolating Transformer?

An AGL isolating transformer is a transformer specifically used within an airfield lighting series circuit to electrically isolate an individual lighting load from the primary series circuit.

It is normally installed between the primary series-circuit cable and the individual fixture, and its fundamental functions are electrical isolation, energy transfer, providing the interface between primary and secondary circuits, matching the lighting fixture to the series-circuit architecture, and supporting reliable operation of each individual AGL fixture.

Electrical Isolation

Provides galvanic separation between the primary series circuit and the secondary fixture circuit.

Energy Transfer

Transfers electrical power from the series circuit to the individual fixture it supplies.

Circuit Architecture

Lets many fixtures form part of one common series circuit without joining their secondary circuits together.

The Series-Circuit Principle

Why Isolating Transformers Are Used

AGL systems commonly use a constant-current series circuit. Instead of connecting every light fixture directly across a common voltage supply, individual fixtures are connected through isolation transformers — allowing multiple lighting units to operate as part of the same series circuit while maintaining electrical isolation at each individual load interface.

The CCR regulates the series-circuit current, each isolation transformer transfers power from the primary circuit to its associated fixture, and the fixture converts that electrical energy into light. The isolation transformer's job is to interface the individual load — not to regulate the circuit itself.

Two Isolated Sides

Primary Side vs Secondary Side

The transformer has two electrically isolated sides, each serving a distinct part of the circuit.

Primary Side

Connected to the CCR through the series-circuit cable. The primary side operates within the high-voltage series-circuit environment.

Secondary Side

Electrical Isolation — Magnetically Coupled, Not Wired Together

There is no direct wire between the primary and secondary windings — energy crosses the core magnetically, which is exactly what protects the fixture side from the series-circuit voltage

Construction

Construction, Insulation & Sealing

An AGL isolating transformer generally contains a magnetic core, primary winding, secondary winding, insulation system, housing/enclosure, leads or connectors, a sealing system and identification markings. The exact construction varies according to manufacturer and transformer design.

Insulation System

Electrical isolation depends on the integrity of the insulation system — winding insulation, inter-winding insulation, lead insulation, external insulation, sealing, moisture resistance and dielectric strength. Deterioration can eventually lead to leakage → earth fault → circuit instability → transformer failure.

Sealing

AGL transformers are installed in harsh outdoor environments — water, moisture, dust, mud, chemicals, temperature variation and mechanical stress. A properly designed and sealed transformer protects its internal electrical system from these conditions.

Water Ingress

One of the most important causes of field problems. Entry points include damaged housing, the cable/lead interface, the connector interface, seal deterioration, mechanical damage and incorrect installation — leading to insulation degradation, corrosion, leakage current, earth fault and reduced life.

Transformer Construction — Common Water Ingress Points

Field Practice

Installation & Locations

Installation should follow the approved design and manufacturer's instructions, verifying correct location, rating, identification, connections, cable routing and mechanical/environmental protection. Depending on the AGL system, transformers may be installed in transformer pits, in light bases, adjacent to fixtures, underground, within approved enclosures, or in other project-approved locations.

Location & Orientation

Correct transformer location, orientation where applicable, rating and identification, verified against the approved design before energizing.

Cable Routing

Routing should prevent excessive mechanical stress, sharp bending, connector damage, water accumulation, abrasion and improper cable tension — the transformer should never carry unnecessary mechanical load on its leads or connectors.

Identification

Manufacturer, model, rating, serial/batch number, primary/secondary rating and certification marking — important for installation, inspection, testing, maintenance, replacement and traceability.

System Thinking

The Transformer's Relationships in the Wider System

Transformer & CCR

The CCR controls the primary series-circuit current; the transformer receives that current and transfers power to the fixture. If the CCR current is incorrect, the fixture may not operate at its intended condition even when the transformer itself is healthy.

Transformer & Photometry

The transformer itself does not determine the complete optical performance of a fixture — photometric performance depends on the complete chain from CCR through the series circuit, transformer, fixture and optical system to light output. Photometric verification may be required after transformer replacement.

Keyword Reference

Series Lighting Transformer: Working Principle, Types, Functions & Components

How the series lighting transformer works inside the airfield lighting series circuit, and what it is made of.

Series lighting transformer

A series lighting transformer is the device that lets one lighting load sit on a constant-current series loop without breaking the loop. The primary winding carries the circuit current, and the secondary feeds a single fixture. Because every transformer in the airfield lighting series circuit shares the same current, the brightness setting is controlled at the CCR rather than at each fixture.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer working principle

The primary winding is connected in series with the circuit, so the circuit current magnetises the core and induces a secondary current in the lamp or LED driver. Isolation means the fixture is not electrically tied to the high-voltage primary loop. If a lamp fails and the secondary opens, the transformer design limits the effect so the rest of the circuit keeps running. Core design and the way a transformer behaves with an open or lightly loaded secondary should be confirmed against the manufacturer's datasheet.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer types

Types are usually separated by rated load, by the fixture they serve and by how they are installed. Common groupings are different wattage ratings, units for elevated and in-pavement fixtures, and units with different lead and connector arrangements. Series-to-series isolating transformers are the standard type in AGL, but the exact classification follows the specification used for the project.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer functions

The main functions are isolating the fixture from the primary circuit, transferring energy to the load, matching circuit current to the fixture's needs, and keeping the series circuit healthy when a load fails. A well-built transformer also protects maintenance staff by keeping dangerous primary voltage away from the fixture side.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer components

The main components are the magnetic core, primary and secondary windings, the insulation system, the housing or moulded body, the primary and secondary leads or connectors, and the sealing that keeps moisture out. The quality of the insulation and sealing largely decides field life. See the Construction, Insulation & Sealing section above for more detail.

Keyword Reference

Specification, Design, Requirements, Standards & Selection Guide

What an airfield lighting isolation transformer must meet, and how to choose and justify one.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer specification

A specification should state the circuit current, rated load, primary and secondary connection type, insulation and sealing requirements, environmental limits, marking and the standard the unit is tested to. Keeping the specification tied to the fixture and CCR it will work with avoids mismatches discovered during commissioning.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer requirements

Requirements come from three directions: the aerodrome regulator, the standard the project follows, and the project's own design basis. In practice they cover electrical performance, insulation integrity, mechanical robustness, connector compatibility, sealing and traceability. Requirements should be checked against the editions of the standards your project uses.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer design

Design covers the core and winding arrangement, how the unit behaves with an open or partly loaded secondary, insulation system, thermal behaviour, lead and connector arrangement and housing. At circuit level, designers also account for the combined effect of all transformers on the series loop and the CCR.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer standards

Commonly referenced documents include FAA advisory circular AC 150/5345-47 for series-to-series isolation transformers, IEC 61823 for AGL series transformers, and ICAO Annex 14 and the ICAO Aerodrome Design Manual for the wider lighting system. Please confirm the exact documents and editions that apply to your project and regulator, including any DGCA requirements.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer selection guide

Select in this order: the fixture and its load, the circuit current and CCR, the connector type, the installation location, and the standard the unit must be approved to. Selecting on wattage alone is a common mistake. See Transformer Ratings & Selection and the Transformer Compatibility Matrix above.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer technical guide

As a technical guide, think of the transformer as the interface between three things: the primary series circuit, the fixture, and the maintenance process. Each section on this page covers one part of that interface, from construction and connectors to testing and replacement.

Keyword Reference

Installation, Commissioning & AGL Transformer Testing

Getting the transformer into service correctly and proving it works.

Airfield lighting transformer installation

Installation means placing the transformer at the right location, making the primary and secondary connections correctly, protecting the leads from mechanical stress and keeping connectors clean, dry and fully mated. Follow the manufacturer's instructions and the project installation specification, and record the transformer identification against the fixture location.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer installation

For an isolation transformer specifically, check before installing that the rating suits the fixture, that the housing and leads are undamaged, and that the connector type matches. After installation, check connector seating and sealing before backfilling or closing the fixture base.

AGL transformer testing

AGL transformer testing normally combines visual inspection, continuity checks and insulation resistance testing, followed by an operational check with the circuit energised under the correct safety procedure. Test values and limits come from the manufacturer and project specification, so I have not stated figures here. Results should always be recorded. See Inspection & Electrical Testing and the Transformer Test Record above.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer testing procedure

A typical procedure runs: isolate and secure the circuit, inspect the transformer and connectors, perform insulation and continuity checks, reconnect, energise under controlled conditions, confirm fixture operation and record the results. Always follow your site's lock-out and permit-to-work procedure.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer commissioning

Commissioning confirms that every installed transformer and its fixture work correctly on the energised circuit. It normally includes verifying identification, connector condition, insulation test records, fixture output at the commissioning brightness steps and circuit behaviour. Any deviation is recorded and closed before handover. See Transformer Replacement & Commissioning above.

Keyword Reference

Maintenance, Preventive Maintenance & Troubleshooting

Keeping the transformer in service, and finding faults efficiently when it is not.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer maintenance

Maintenance covers scheduled inspection, connector checks, insulation testing and replacement of damaged units. Most field problems begin at connectors and seals, so these should be the first things checked.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer preventive maintenance

Preventive maintenance finds problems before they cause outages. Typical activities are periodic insulation resistance trending, visual inspection of housings and leads, checking for water ingress, and keeping records so repeated failures in one area become visible. The inspection interval should follow your maintenance plan and the aerodrome's requirements.

Airfield lighting isolation transformer troubleshooting

Troubleshoot from the symptom: one fixture out, a group out, or a circuit fault. Test before replacing, because continuity alone does not prove a transformer is healthy. Check connectors and leads first, then insulation, then the transformer and fixture individually. See Common Field Failure Modes and Transformer Troubleshooting above.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is an AGL isolating transformer?

An AGL isolating transformer is a transformer used in an airfield lighting series circuit to electrically isolate an individual lighting load from the primary series circuit. It is normally installed between the primary series-circuit cable and the individual fixture, providing electrical isolation, energy transfer and the interface between primary and secondary circuits.

Q2Why are isolating transformers used in AGL series circuits?

AGL systems commonly use a constant-current series circuit. Instead of connecting every fixture directly across a common voltage supply, fixtures are connected through isolation transformers, so many lighting units can operate in the same series circuit while each load stays electrically isolated. The CCR regulates the circuit current; the transformer only interfaces the individual load.

Q3What is the difference between the primary and secondary side of the transformer?

The primary side connects to the CCR through the series-circuit cable and primary connector, within the high-voltage series-circuit environment. The secondary side connects to the AGL fixture through the secondary connector and supplies the individual lighting load. There is no direct wire between the windings; energy crosses the core magnetically.

Q4Can an ordinary transformer replace an AGL isolating transformer?

No. An ordinary electrical transformer should not automatically be substituted. AGL transformers are selected for series-circuit operation, primary and secondary characteristics, power rating, insulation system, environmental exposure, mechanical construction, connector and fixture compatibility, and applicable standards and project requirements.

Q5What power ratings do AGL isolating transformers come in?

Ratings commonly encountered include 15 W, 25 W, 45 W, 65 W, 100 W, 150 W, 200 W, 300 W and 500 W. The required rating depends on the connected fixture or load and the applicable design requirements.

Q6How should an AGL isolating transformer be selected?

Selection should not be made from fixture wattage alone, or by choosing a higher rating for its own sake. The fixture load, required transformer rating and compatible series circuit must all line up, including secondary electrical requirements, primary series-circuit compatibility and CCR compatibility.

Q7Is 6.6 A the series-circuit current for every AGL system?

No. A nominal series-circuit current of 6.6 A is commonly encountered in many AGL applications, but the actual operating current depends on the specific system and design. Selection must be based on the applicable equipment specification and project design rather than one assumed universal current.

Q8Can a transformer's secondary power exceed its primary power?

No. For an ideal transformer primary power is approximately equal to secondary power, and in practice primary power is greater because of copper, core and dielectric losses. Real efficiency and losses must be taken from manufacturer data and verified by the applicable acceptance and test procedures.

Q9What is the main cause of AGL isolating transformer field problems?

Water ingress is one of the most important causes. Entry points include a damaged housing, the cable or lead interface, the connector interface, seal deterioration, mechanical damage and incorrect installation, leading to insulation degradation, corrosion, leakage current, earth fault and reduced life.

Q10What is inside an AGL isolating transformer?

An AGL isolating transformer generally contains a magnetic core, primary winding, secondary winding, insulation system, housing or enclosure, leads or connectors, a sealing system and identification markings. The exact construction varies by manufacturer and transformer design.

Q11How to test an AGL isolation transformer?

Isolate and secure the circuit under your site's permit-to-work, inspect the transformer, leads and connectors, then perform continuity and insulation resistance checks using the limits in the manufacturer's data and your project specification. Reconnect, energise under controlled conditions, confirm the fixture operates and record the results. Continuity alone does not prove a transformer is healthy.

Q12How to select an isolation transformer?

Start with the fixture and its load, then confirm the circuit current and CCR, the primary and secondary connector type, the installation location and the standard the unit must be approved to. Do not select on wattage alone. Check compatibility with the fixture and the CCR, and make sure the unit is approved to the specification your project follows.
