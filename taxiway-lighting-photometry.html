Taxiway Lighting Photometry & Intensity Testing

How photometry verifies the intensity, distribution, colour and alignment of taxiway edge, centreline, stop bar and runway guard lights. A taxiway light can stay illuminated while giving too little output or pointing its beam the wrong way.

Start Learning AGL Photometry Concepts
Overview

What Is Taxiway Lighting Photometry?

Taxiway lighting photometry is the quantitative evaluation of the optical output of taxiway lighting fixtures. It verifies that installed lights give the required intensity, distribution, colour and alignment for their operational function, as visual guidance for aircraft moving between runways, aprons, terminals and other manoeuvring areas.

More than electricity reaching the fixture

The objective is to decide whether the light works as an optical guidance device. A fixture can remain illuminated while giving inadequate intensity or an incorrectly directed beam.

Function drives the method

An elevated edge light and an in-pavement centreline light may share a series circuit, yet their optical geometry and measurement requirements differ substantially. Base the method on the fixture's function, not its appearance.

      Measurement

      Intensity, Distribution and Colour

      Intensity

      Varies with fixture type, optical design, light source, operating step, viewing angle, measurement geometry, electrical condition and fixture condition. Tie every result to its operating condition.

      Distribution

      A fixture can be strong at one angle and weak elsewhere in its required coverage. Peak intensity alone does not describe performance.

      Colour

      A fixture can look bright yet have colour outside requirement. Verify colour separately from intensity where required.

      How Is Taxiway Light Intensity Measured?

      Taxiway light intensity is measured with calibrated photometric equipment in a controlled geometry. The fixture is inspected and, where required, cleaned first. The operating condition is set and recorded, and the sensor position and fixture orientation are established. The measurement is then compared with the applicable requirement or with earlier test data. See the measurement procedure below for the full sequence.

      Taxiway Light Intensity Measurement: Why Operating Step Matters

      Where the system has several brightness settings, a fixture measured at one cannot be compared with a criterion for another. Record the intensity step, control-system condition, fixture type, test position and measurement geometry.

      Colour degradation

      Causes include light-source degradation, filter condition, optical components, lens contamination, incorrect replacement parts and ageing optics.

      Distribution parameters

      ParameterWhat it shows
      Peak intensityHighest measured intensity in the relevant measurement area
      Angular distributionHow output changes with viewing angle
      Beam coverageWhether output is sufficient over the required angular region
      DirectionalityWhether light goes toward the intended operational area
      UniformityWhether adjacent fixtures give a consistent visual pattern
      Take colour and intensity limits from the governing aerodrome standard or approved fixture specification, not from a generic value, including any on this site.
      Equipment

      Taxiway Photometer Setup, Calibration and Positioning

      Equipment

      Photometric sensor, optical sensor, positioning equipment, angular and distance measurement, data acquisition unit, measurement software, location or mapping system and calibration references. The configuration depends on the test system.

      Before testing, verify and record

      • Instrument identification, model and serial number
      • Calibration status and certificate
      • Sensor condition
      • Software configuration and measurement range
      • Data acquisition settings

      Calibration is not enough

      A calibrated instrument does not guarantee a valid measurement. The complete test arrangement must also be correct.

      Positioning

      Accuracy depends on horizontal and vertical position, distance, viewing angle, sensor orientation and fixture orientation. A small error can change measured intensity, especially for directional fixtures.

      Procedure

      Taxiway Photometry Measurement Procedure

      A controlled taxiway test follows twelve steps.

      01

      Review requirements

      Taxiway designation, lighting configuration, fixture types, scope, authority requirements, specifications, equipment requirements and required operating conditions.

      02

      Review drawings

      Lighting layout, fixture schedule and locations, cable circuit information, datasheets, previous test data and as-builts.

      03

      Verify photometric equipment

      Calibration, instrument configuration, sensor condition, data system, positioning system and software.

      04

      Inspect the lighting

      Dirty or damaged lenses, broken housings, water ingress, loose fixtures, wrong orientation, missing components, damaged cables or connectors.

      05

      Clean and prepare fixtures

      Where required, clean optical surfaces before the final acceptance measurement so contamination is not read as permanent degradation.

      06

      Confirm the operating condition

      Set the required intensity or operating mode and record it.

      07

      Establish measurement geometry

      Position the photometer per the test procedure: distance, height, orientation, angle and reference direction.

      08

      Measure the fixture

      Record fixture ID, location, type, operating step and photometric data.

      09

      Analyse the results

      Compare with the applicable acceptance criteria.

      10

      Investigate failures

      Contamination, alignment, optics, light source, electrical supply or measurement error.

      11

      Correct the defect

      Complete the required maintenance or adjustment.

      12

      Retest

      Repeat the measurement and document the final result.

      Alignment

      Taxiway Lighting Alignment

      Why it matters

      A light with good optical output still fails to guide if its beam points the wrong way. Photometry can reveal an error when the measured beam is displaced from its intended measurement region.

      Alignment errors come from

      • Accidental impact
      • Maintenance work or fixture replacement
      • Mounting movement and loose hardware
      • Foundation problems
      • Incorrect initial installation
      • Pavement works

      Troubleshooting sequence

      Abnormal measurement
      Verify photometer set-up
      Verify operating step
      Inspect fixture
      Clean optical surface
      Check physical alignment
      Inspect optics

      This approach reduces unnecessary fixture replacement.

      Staged Approach

      Structured Troubleshooting of an Abnormal Result

      Stage 1: Verify the measurement

      Photometer calibration, sensor position, geometry, fixture ID, operating intensity and repeatability.

      Stage 2: Inspect the fixture

      Lens, optics, housing, orientation, contamination, water ingress and mechanical damage.

      Stage 3: Check the electrical system

      Supply condition, series-circuit operation, isolation transformer, connectors, wiring and control system.

      Stage 4: Correct the defect

      Clean, align, repair or replace as appropriate.

      Stage 5: Retest

      Confirm the final photometric result.

      Cleaning first: use measure, clean, re-measure rather than measure, replace. Record whether output improved after cleaning, which separates contamination from true optical or electrical degradation.

      Maintenance

      Maintenance, Replacement and Pavement Work

      Maintenance testing

      Test periodically, after major maintenance, replacement, accidental impact or resurfacing, when pilots report abnormal lighting, when visual inspection finds inconsistent output, or as part of an asset-condition programme.

      Classify results

      Normal, monitor, maintenance required, failed or retest required. This is more useful than recording only ON or OFF.

      After fixture replacement, verify

      • Correct fixture type and optical assembly
      • Installation orientation and mechanical position
      • Electrical connection and operating condition
      • Photometric output, especially when changing technology or optical design

      After pavement work, ask

      • Is the fixture correctly positioned?
      • Is the optical system undamaged?
      • Is the beam correctly aligned?
      • Is the intensity acceptable, and the colour correct?
      • Does the overall lighting pattern remain consistent?
      Pavement work includes resurfacing, milling, repairs, fixture removal and reinstallation, drainage works, civil construction and taxiway geometry changes. Do not check only whether fixtures illuminate.

      Cleaning and photometric performance

      Cleaning matters most for fixtures at pavement level, where a contaminated optical surface cuts the light transmitted through the lens. Use the sequence below rather than replacing a fixture straight away, so contamination is separated from true optical or electrical degradation.

      Measure
      Clean
      Re-measure
      Instead of Measure, then Replace fixture. In maintenance reports, document whether output improved after cleaning.

      Standards and Acceptance Criteria

      FAA Taxiway Lighting Photometry

      Where FAA requirements apply, taxiway lighting photometry follows FAA fixture specifications and maintenance guidance, for example FAA AC 150/5345-46 for light fixtures and AC 150/5340-26C for maintenance, which includes photometric measurement. Confirm the current edition of each, and see FAA AC 150/5340 series explained.

      No single photometric value applies blindly to every taxiway light. Requirements vary with the following, and the final report should name the specific controlled source used for acceptance.

      ICAO provisions

      International SARPs, including Annex 14 Volume I for aerodrome visual aids.

      National authority

      National civil aviation authority requirements.

      FAA, where applicable

      FAA fixture specifications and maintenance guidance.

      Project and manufacturer

      Approved project specification, fixture design, operational category and manufacturer documentation.

      Avoid copying generic intensity values from another airport or an outdated document. Confirm the current edition of every reference before you rely on it.

      FAQ

      Taxiway Lighting Photometry FAQ

      Q1What is taxiway lighting photometry?

      The measurement and evaluation of the optical performance of taxiway visual-aid systems, including intensity, distribution, colour and alignment where applicable.

      Q2What taxiway lights can be tested photometrically?

      Depending on the airport configuration: taxiway edge lights, centreline lights, stop bars, runway guard lights and other specialized taxiway lighting.

      Q3What does a taxiway photometer measure?

      Light intensity and, depending on configuration, optical distribution, angular performance, colour-related parameters and alignment information.

      Q4Why is photometry better than visual inspection?

      Visual inspection confirms that a fixture appears to operate, while photometry gives quantitative information about its optical performance.

      Q5Can taxiway lights be bright but incorrectly aligned?

      Yes. A fixture can produce substantial output while directing its beam incorrectly, and photometric measurement can help identify this.

      Q6Why do taxiway centreline lights need special attention?

      They are frequently in-pavement and exposed to contamination, aircraft loading, moisture and mechanical stress that can affect optical performance.

      Q7Should taxiway lights be cleaned before testing?

      Where the test procedure requires it, yes. Cleaning separates contamination-related loss from an actual equipment defect.

      Q8What causes low taxiway light intensity?

      Optical contamination, degraded lamp or LED components, damaged lenses, incorrect intensity settings, electrical problems and ageing of the optical system.

      Q9Why should operating intensity be recorded?

      Measured output depends on the operating condition, so recording the intensity step makes a result meaningful and repeatable.

      Q10What should a taxiway photometric report contain?

      Airport, taxiway, fixture, test equipment, calibration status, operating condition, measurement geometry, actual results, applicable acceptance criteria, corrective actions and retest results.

      Q11How often should taxiway photometry be performed?

      It depends on authority requirements, the airport maintenance programme, lighting system, operating environment and historical performance. Do not assume a single interval for every airport.
