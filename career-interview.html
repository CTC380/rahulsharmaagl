05 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about the career and HR interview bank, how the drill and scoring work, and how to use the model answers.

Q1What is the Career & HR Interview question bank?

It is a 100-question reference and drill for AGL and airport-electrical interviews. It covers self-introduction, technical experience, projects and coordination, problem solving, leadership, safety, live-airport working and career goals, and every question has a model answer, guidance on what a strong response includes, key terms and references.

Q2What topics does the question bank cover?

Eight topics: Introduction & Career Profile (15), Technical Experience (15), Projects & Coordination (15), Problem Solving (15), Leadership & Teamwork (10), Safety (10), Live Airport Work (10), Career Goals (10).

Q3What difficulty levels are included?

Four levels: Beginner (3), Intermediate (55), Advanced (37) and Expert (5). Most questions are Intermediate or Advanced because they ask you to describe real experience and decisions, not just recite a definition.

Q4What question formats does the bank use?

Experience-based (41), practical scenario (19), interview response (15), career-based (10) and scenario-based (5), plus a few quality-control, safety, technical-decision, root-cause and behavioural questions.

Q5How does Drill Mode work, and how is my answer scored?

Drill Mode gives a shuffled run through the bank, filtered by level and topic if you choose. Type your own answer and check it, or just reveal the model answer. Typed answers are scored locally in your browser by weighted key-term matching with stemming and typo tolerance, plus overall wording overlap. For interview questions the score weights overall wording and structure more heavily, but it is still an approximation, not true AI grading.

Q6Should I memorise the model answers?

No. The answers are written in the first person as worked examples. Use them to see what a well-structured answer contains, then replace the details with your own real projects, systems and results, because interviewers probe experience claims with follow-up questions.

Q7How should I answer "Tell me about yourself" in an AGL interview?

Give a concise professional summary: your technical identity, the AGL and airport-electrical systems you have worked on, and your emphasis on safety and reliability, without unnecessary personal history. The introduction topic (15 questions) covers this and related openers such as career history, current role and motivation.

Q8What do interviewers look for in problem-solving answers?

A clear method. The Problem Solving topic (15 questions) asks you to describe faults you personally investigated, such as series-circuit, transformer, photometric and installation defects, and how you separated the root cause from the first apparent cause. Name the evidence you used, not just the conclusion.

Q9What safety questions come up?

Ten questions cover safe approach to high-voltage AGL series-circuit work, permit-to-work verification, additional precautions for high-voltage testing and related safety interview topics. Your answer should always show a clear isolate, verify and permit discipline, and it should follow your site's own procedures.

Q10How should I answer questions about working on a live airport?

Show that you coordinate before you work. The Live Airport Work topic (10 questions) covers working-environment differences, ATC coordination, runway and taxiway restrictions, and what to do if airport operations change suddenly while you are mid-task.

Q11Which standards does the question bank refer to?

Answers refer back to FAA AC 150/5340-26C (64 questions), FAA AC 150/5340-30J (43), FAA AC 150/5370-12B (26), FAA AC 150/5370-10H (24), FAA AC 150/5370-2G (12) and ICAO Annex 14 (10), with OSHA 29 CFR 1910.333 cited in a few safety questions.

Q12Does it cover leadership and career-goal questions?

Yes. Leadership & Teamwork (10 questions) covers site planning, team conflict and working to deadlines, and Career Goals (10) covers why you want a role, leadership aspirations and openness to international airport projects.
