Product Overview

WGS Sign Boards

What They Are

WGS sign boards provide structured visual information for aircraft and vehicle operators within airport movement areas. They communicate identification, routing, location, mandatory instructions and other approved operational information at taxiways, aprons, runway entrances and associated airport infrastructure.

Precision AGL Solutions supplies WGS sign board configurations selected according to the required legend, character arrangement, board dimensions, illumination method, mounting arrangement, viewing distance and project specification.

A Typical Assembly May Include

  1. Sign board – main structural and visual body
  2. Legend – approved characters, arrows, numbers, letters or symbols
  3. Sign face – visible surface carrying the information
  4. Illumination system – where illuminated
  5. Support structure – posts, brackets, frames or other approved mounting
  6. Electrical interface – for illuminated configurations
The system can be configured for a new airport development, movement-area extension, apron redevelopment, runway or taxiway rehabilitation, or replacement of existing airfield signage. Final construction depends on the application, airport environment and project requirements.

Illumination Options

Illuminated or Retroreflective, as Operations Require

Configuration

WGS sign boards can be configured as illuminated or retroreflective (non-illuminated) signs according to the operational requirement and applicable standard. Where illumination is required, the system may incorporate LED technology for controlled and consistent sign-face illumination.

The selected illumination method should provide clear information without producing excessive glare.

Under ICAO Annex 14, signs are to be illuminated where intended for use in runway visual range below 800 m, at night on instrument runways, or at night on non-instrument runways of code number 3 or 4; otherwise a retroreflective sign may be acceptable where the authority permits. Confirm the exact wording against your edition. Sign luminance should follow the airfield brightness-step settings so that signs stay balanced with adjacent taxiway lights.

Frangibility

Review Against Obstacle and Frangibility Requirements

Why It Matters

ICAO Annex 14 requires signs on the movement area to be frangible, and those near a runway or taxiway to be low enough to preserve clearance for propellers and engine pods. The support arrangement should therefore be reviewed against the applicable obstacle-limitation and frangibility requirements.

The applicable arrangement depends on the exact installation location and project criteria.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures

FAQ

WGS Sign Boards — Frequently Asked Questions

What are WGS sign boards?

WGS sign boards provide structured visual information for aircraft and vehicle operators within airport movement areas, communicating identification, routing, location, mandatory instructions and other approved operational information at taxiways, aprons, runway entrances and associated infrastructure.

What are the sign categories and their colours?

Mandatory instruction signs (runway holding positions, ILS CAT I/II/III holding positions and no-entry) are white inscription on red. Location signs are yellow inscription on black, with a yellow border where required. Direction and destination signs, and information signs, are black inscription on yellow. Sign colours follow ICAO Annex 14 and the equivalent FAA requirements and are not a free design choice.

When must a sign be illuminated?

Under ICAO Annex 14, signs are to be illuminated where intended for use in runway visual range below 800 m, at night on instrument runways, or at night on non-instrument runways of code number 3 or 4. Otherwise a retroreflective sign may be acceptable where the authority permits. Confirm the exact wording against your edition.

How is the board size decided?

From the legend and viewing requirements: character dimensions and spacing, border dimensions, the number of characters and lines, arrows or symbols, the illumination arrangement and mounting. Character height and sign positions follow ICAO Annex 14 and FAA AC 150/5340-18H according to sign type and runway code or approach category, so take them from the approved signage plan.

Why does viewing distance matter?

Board dimensions and character size should suit the intended viewer, whether aircraft flight crew, a ground vehicle operator, an apron operator or airport operations personnel. The required recognition distance should be established during the signage design process.

What are the advantages of LED illumination?

Long operating life, efficient operation, reduced lamp replacement, consistent illumination, rapid switching, compact construction and reduced maintenance. The LED modules, optical system, power supply and sign face should be engineered as one assembly, and sign luminance should follow the airfield brightness-step settings so signs stay balanced with adjacent taxiway lights.

Do airfield signs have to be frangible?

Yes. ICAO Annex 14 requires signs on the movement area to be frangible, and those near a runway or taxiway to be low enough to preserve clearance for propellers and engine pods. Review the sign enclosure, support posts, base assemblies, fasteners, breakaway components, foundations and sign height against the applicable requirements.

How are illuminated signs powered?

Illuminated signs can be supplied from a series circuit (constant-current, via isolating transformer) or a parallel (constant-voltage) circuit. Coordinate input voltage or current, power consumption, local switching or central control, dimming and brightness steps, surge protection, earthing and bonding with the airport infrastructure, and verify the electrical interface specifically before connecting to an existing airfield lighting system.

What are common sign problems?

Reduced visibility from a dirty face, damaged legend or poor positioning, uneven illumination from LED module or power-supply faults, an outdated or incorrect legend, physical damage from vehicles or ground-support equipment, faded colour or reduced contrast from UV ageing, and corrosion. Check the displayed information against the latest approved airport signage schedule.

Which standards apply to WGS sign boards?

References include ICAO Annex 14 Volume I (signs, Chapter 5, Appendices 1 and 4), ICAO Doc 9157 Part 4, FAA AC 150/5345-44L (runway and taxiway signs) and AC 150/5340-18H (airport sign systems), DGCA India CAR Section 4, and the approved airport signage schedules and project specifications. Verify the applicable edition before manufacture and installation.

Is it safe to work on a sign fed from a series circuit while it is energised?

No. Never open a live series circuit: an open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. De-energise at the regulator, apply lock-out / tag-out, use the series circuit cut-out switch or an approved isolation device and work under the permit-to-work system. See the electrical safety warning on this page.

What should I provide when requesting WGS sign boards?

Provide the airport and project name, sign category, exact legend, quantity, character height, board dimensions, viewing distance, illumination requirement, electrical supply and control requirement, mounting arrangement, foundation information, frangibility requirement, environmental conditions, applicable airport standard and required documentation.
