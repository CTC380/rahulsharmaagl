AGL-CS-077 — PAPI Unit Intensity Imbalance

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A PAPI photometric investigation where unequal unit intensity degraded transition quality — from pilot reports through individual photometry, optical transmission assessment, chromaticity verification, intensity balancing, and operational validation.

Case ID

AGL-CS-077

Category

Level

Expert

Case Type

Engineering Simulation

Primary Skill

Investigation of unequal intensity between PAPI units and restoration of balanced visual guidance

Important: This is an engineering simulation created for training. It is not presented as an actual airport incident. All readings, values, dates and outcomes are illustrative, and no airport, organisation, person or manufacturer is identified or implied.

1Case Study Overview

Flight crews reported that the PAPI appeared uneven during final approach, with one unit noticeably dimmer than the others.

The reported glide-path indication was technically correct, but pilots stated that the red/white transition was difficult to interpret, particularly during night approaches and wet-runway conditions.

A ground inspection showed that all four PAPI units were operating, beam-angle calibration was within tolerance, and no electrical alarms were present.

A detailed investigation was initiated covering unit intensity, photometric balance, chromaticity, optical transmission, power-supply performance, and optical-system condition.

The investigation identified significant photometric intensity imbalance caused by optical degradation and reduced output from one PAPI unit, resulting in degraded visual transition quality.

This case demonstrates that PAPI units may be correctly aligned geometrically while still providing poor operational guidance due to unequal photometric intensity.

3System Background

A PAPI system consists of four optical units.

Correct operation requires:

  • Correct beam angles.
  • Correct unit spacing.
  • Correct elevation.
  • Correct chromaticity.
  • Correct transition sharpness.
  • Balanced intensity between units.

Intensity imbalance may result from:

  • LED aging.
  • Optical contamination.
  • Reflector degradation.
  • Power-supply variation.
  • Driver deterioration.
  • Optical-window contamination.
  • Internal moisture.
  • Incorrect replacement components.

Visual interpretation depends on relative brightness between units.

7Intensity Balance Principle

Equal intensity → Uniform visual pattern → Sharp red/white transition → Reliable glide-path perception → ICAO-compliant guidance
Unequal intensity → Uneven visual pattern → Poor transition perception → Reduced pilot confidence → Operational degradation

PAPI effectiveness depends on photometric balance.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The PAPI intensity imbalance was caused by optical degradation of Unit 3, including:

  • Internal optical contamination.
  • Lens haze.
  • Reflector degradation.
  • Reduced optical transmission.

Beam-angle calibration remained correct.

The primary failure mechanism was unequal photometric output between units.

11Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Replaced the optical assembly of Unit 3.
  2. Restored optical transmission.
  3. Replaced degraded reflector components.
  4. Verified beam angles.
  5. Performed photometric balancing.
  6. Verified chromaticity.
  7. Conducted operational observation.
  8. Updated maintenance procedures.
  9. Established intensity-balance inspections.
  10. Recorded post-repair photometric data.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: All four PAPI units ON. Beam angles correct. One unit visibly dim. Pilots report poor transition visibility. What should you investigate first?

A. Recalibrate beam angles.
B. Increase overall intensity.
C. Measure individual unit intensities, compare photometric balance, inspect optical transmission, and verify chromaticity.
D. Replace the power supply.
Correct answer: C. Uneven appearance with correct beam angles points to a photometric imbalance problem. Individual intensity measurement, optical transmission assessment, and chromaticity verification will identify the cause without unnecessary equipment replacement.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about PAPI unit intensity imbalance.

Q1What is AGL-CS-077?

AGL-CS-077 is an engineering training case study. It covers a PAPI with correct beam angles and a correct glide-path indication, but with one unit noticeably dimmer than the others. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2Can unequal intensity degrade PAPI guidance if the beam angles are correct?

Yes. Uneven intensity gives an uneven visual pattern and a red/white transition that is harder to interpret, especially at night and on wet runways.

Q3What did the intensity measurements show in AGL-CS-077?

Units 1, 2 and 4 measured 24,800, 25,100 and 24,900 cd, while Unit 3 measured 16,200 cd, roughly 35% lower.

Q4Was the electrical supply or calibration at fault?

No. Supply voltage, driver output and current regulation were normal, and all four beam angles were correct.

Q5What was wrong with Unit 3's optics?

Internal lens haze, reflector contamination and optical-window deposits. Optical transmission was 66% against 91–92% on the other units, and chromaticity had shifted slightly toward yellow.

Q6What was the root cause?

Optical degradation of Unit 3: internal contamination, lens haze, reflector degradation and reduced transmission. The primary mechanism was unequal photometric output between units.

Q7How was the cause confirmed?

Replacing Unit 3's optical assembly restored intensity to 24,700 cd, in line with the other units.

Q8What corrective actions were taken?

The team replaced Unit 3's optical assembly and degraded reflector components, verified beam angles, performed photometric balancing, verified chromaticity and conducted operational observation. They also established intensity-balance inspections and recorded post-repair data.

Q9What is the key lesson from this case?

PAPI effectiveness depends on photometric balance as well as geometry. Measure each unit's intensity, don't rely on beam angle alone.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

PAPI unit intensity imbalance is a photometric-performance problem caused by unequal optical output between units, and a PAPI system can have correct beam angles while still providing degraded visual glide-path guidance due to reduced intensity and optical transmission of one unit.

Systematic individual unit photometry, intensity comparison, optical transmission measurement, chromaticity verification, and photometric balancing can identify unequal PAPI output and restore compliant visual approach guidance.

In AGL engineering, a PAPI is not only a system of correct angles; it is also a system of balanced light, where every unit must contribute equally to the pilot's perception of the glide path.
