Earthing & Bonding Maintenance

The protective-electrical-integrity backbone of the handbook. This division maintains a reliable low-impedance path for fault current, equipment bonding, electrical safety, lightning-related currents, and surge protection — keeping AGL equipment and personnel protected.

Core principle: earthing provides the path — bonding connects the assets — surge protection limits transient overvoltage.

Philosophy: a visually intact earth conductor does not automatically mean the earthing system is electrically satisfactory. The maintenance program combines visual, mechanical, and connection inspection with continuity and earth-resistance verification before any corrective action is closed out.

Special Inspection Triggers & Post-Maintenance Verification

Earthing and bonding work does not stand alone — it should be triggered by wider events and verified after other maintenance disturbs it.

Special Inspection Triggers

  • Lightning event
  • Major electrical fault
  • Flooding
  • Civil works
  • Cable, fixture, sign, or transformer replacement
  • CCR maintenance or panel modification
  • Earthing-test failure or repeated equipment failures

Post-Maintenance Verification

  • Connection security
  • Conductor condition
  • Mechanical protection
  • Corrosion treatment
  • Earth continuity and bonding continuity
  • Earth resistance
  • SPD condition and lightning-protection condition

Why it matters: maintenance work elsewhere on the asset can unintentionally disturb an otherwise satisfactory bonding path — the exact testing requirement should correspond to the work performed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about earthing & bonding maintenance, based on the topics in this division.

8 QUESTIONS
Q1What is earthing and bonding maintenance in the AGL Maintenance Handbook?

It is the protective-electrical-integrity division, maintaining a reliable low-impedance path for fault current, equipment bonding, electrical safety and lightning-related currents. In short, earthing provides the path, bonding connects the assets and surge protection limits transient overvoltage.

Q2What does Division 16 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 16 (Earthing & Bonding Maintenance) contains 31 topics in four groups: Group A Earthing System (10 topics), Group B Equipment Bonding (10 topics), Group C Lightning / Surge Protection (6 topics) and Group D Testing (5 topics).

Q3Does a visually intact earth conductor mean the earthing system is satisfactory?

No. A visually intact conductor is not automatically electrically satisfactory, so the maintenance sequence goes beyond inspection to continuity verification and earth-resistance verification.

Q4What is the earthing and bonding maintenance sequence?

Visual Inspection, Mechanical Inspection, Connection Inspection, Continuity Verification, Earth-Resistance Verification, Corrective Action, Retest and Record.

Q5What should be done after a failed or abnormal earth test?

A controlled twelve-step investigation: verify the test setup and the instrument, repeat the test to confirm the result, inspect the connection, conductor, electrode and pit and the bonding path, identify the cause, correct the defect, repeat the test to confirm acceptable values, and record the final measured value.

Q6What events should trigger a special earthing inspection?

A lightning event, a major electrical fault, flooding, civil works, replacement of a cable, fixture, sign or transformer, and CCR maintenance or panel modification. Earthing and bonding should also be verified after other maintenance disturbs it.

Q7How are earthing conditions classified?

Five classes: Good (routine PM), Acceptable (monitor), Deteriorating (planned maintenance), Defective (continuity or physical integrity compromised, corrective maintenance) and Critical (major loss of the protective path or a significant lightning or surge concern, priority or emergency response).

Q8What does the earthing and bonding decision matrix recommend?

A loose earth terminal or bonding connection is tightened, a corroded connection is cleaned, treated or replaced, an earth continuity failure is located and corrected, abnormal earth resistance is investigated and corrected, a failed SPD is replaced, and lightning-event damage is inspected, tested and restored.
