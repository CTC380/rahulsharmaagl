AGL Failure Database

A structured historical and analytical repository of AGL failures, organised into 25 engineering divisions — covering equipment, circuits, installation, environment, control systems, operations, reliability and root-cause investigation. Every failure record answers one question: this failure happened — what happened, why did it happen, what evidence proves it, what was done, and how do we prevent it happening again?

Distinct from the Troubleshooting Atlas — the Atlas answers “I have a fault now, how do I diagnose and fix it?”, while the Failure Database is the historical, evidence-backed record of failures already investigated.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about the AGL Failure Database and how its 25 divisions are organised.

Q1What is the AGL Failure Database?

The AGL Failure Database is a structured historical and analytical repository of Airfield Ground Lighting failures, organised into 25 engineering divisions. It covers equipment, circuits, installation, environment, control systems, operations, reliability and root-cause investigation, and each record documents what happened, root cause, evidence, corrective action and prevention.

Q2How is the AGL Failure Database organised?

It is organised into 25 divisions that can be browsed by keyword or filtered by category: Equipment, Circuits & Electrical, Mechanical & Civil, Control & Technology, Human & Process, Environmental & Ops, Testing & Commissioning, Reliability, RCA & Improvement, and Records & Evidence.

Q3What do Divisions 01–20 cover?

Divisions 01–20 are the failure catalogues, covering lighting fixtures, electrical failures, CCRs, isolation transformers, cables and connectors, mechanical and structural failures, control systems, human and installation failures, environmental failures, operational incidents, PAPI/APAPI, airfield signs, approach lighting, stop bars and runway guard lights, earthing and bonding, base cans and civil interfaces, testing and commissioning, maintenance-induced failures, product reliability and emerging technology.

Q4What do Divisions 21–25 cover?

They are the analytical and improvement layers. Division 21 covers root cause analysis, Division 22 severity and criticality, Division 23 reliability and failure-trend analysis, Division 24 corrective action, preventive action and lessons learned, and Division 25 the master failure record, evidence and intelligence layer.

Q5What does a standard failure record contain?

Every record follows the same 18-field structure: failure ID, equipment/system, airport/project, failure date, operating conditions, symptoms, initial observations, root cause analysis, corrective action, preventive action, applicable standards, related products, related drawings, related SOPs, related case studies, lessons learned, references and revision history.

Q6How does the Failure Database differ from the Troubleshooting Atlas?

The Troubleshooting Atlas answers “I have a fault now — how do I diagnose and fix it?” The Failure Database answers “this failure happened — what happened, why, what evidence proves it, what was done, and how do we stop it recurring?” Kept strictly separate, then linked end to end, the two form an AGL Failure Intelligence Engine rather than a simple fault list.

Q7What question does every failure record answer?

Every record answers one question: this failure happened, so what happened, why did it happen, what evidence proves it, what was done and how do we stop it recurring.

Q8How does the Failure Database connect to other resources?

The database links a failure record to its RCA, troubleshooting procedure, corrective action, maintenance action, inspection point, testing procedure, lessons learned and reliability trend, running end to end from the failure record through to the reliability trend.
