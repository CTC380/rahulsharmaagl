Product Overview

Intermediate Holding Position Light

What It Is

Intermediate holding position lights are used at intermediate holding positions established away from the runway holding position, and are particularly relevant where enhanced visual identification is required during reduced-visibility operations.

Under the ICAO arrangement they consist of three fixed, unidirectional yellow lights arranged symmetrically about, and at right angles to, the taxiway centreline, with one light on the centreline and one on each side.

Applications

Where Intermediate Holding Position Lights Are Used

ICAO requires intermediate holding position lights at an intermediate holding position intended for operation in RVR conditions below 350 m, except where a stop bar has been installed. ICAO also recommends their use where there is no requirement for the stop-and-go function provided by a stop bar.

Identifying Where the Aircraft Must Hold

Holding Point, Not Stop-and-Go

Unlike a stop bar, an intermediate holding position light is generally intended to identify a holding point rather than provide the controlled stop-and-go function associated with an illuminated stop bar.

Location & Spacing

0.3 m Before the Marking, 1.5 m Between Lights

Light Location

ICAO specifies that the lights are located along the intermediate holding-position marking, 0.3 m before the marking.

The relationship that matters is holding-position marking + fixture location + taxiway centreline. Survey and set out the installation from the approved airport drawing.

Fixture Spacing

The standard ICAO configuration specifies 1.5 m between individual lights, creating the characteristic pattern:

● — 1.5 m — ● — 1.5 m — ●

Maintain the spacing accurately so the visual pattern corresponds to the approved design.

Light Colour & Optics

Yellow, Fixed, Unidirectional

Coordinate the holding-light distribution with the visual environment produced by any taxiway centreline lights.

Colour and Distribution

ICAO specifies fixed unidirectional yellow output toward the direction of approach, with a light distribution similar to taxiway centreline lights where such centreline lights are provided.

Review the fixture for yellow colour, chromaticity, directionality, intensity, beam distribution, optical axis and viewing direction.

System Relationships

Centreline, Stop Bar and Runway Guard Lights

Taxiway Centreline Light

Provides continuous longitudinal guidance along the aircraft route. The intermediate holding lights identify where the aircraft must hold, then the centreline continues.

Green Centreline → Yellow Holding Lights → Centreline Continues

Stop Bar vs Holding Lights

The two should not be presented as interchangeable. A stop bar gives a controlled red stop indication associated with movement authorization and interlocking. ICAO recommends intermediate holding position lights where there is no need for stop-and-go signals. The project design determines which visual aid is appropriate.

ICAO

The ICAO Store lists Annex 14, Volume I, 9th Edition, July 2022, including Amendment 18, which took effect on 4 August 2025; its aerodrome design and visual-aids provisions apply from 27 November 2025. ICAO also lists Corrigendum No. 2 (18 March 2026) and No. 3 (8 June 2026) to Volume I; check them for the provisions you rely on.

  • Required at applicable intermediate holding positions for RVR below 350 m, except where a stop bar is installed
  • Recommended where a stop bar's stop-and-go function is not needed
  • 0.3 m before the holding-position marking

FAQ

Intermediate Holding Position Light — Frequently Asked Questions

What is an intermediate holding position light?

It is a light used at an intermediate holding position established away from the runway holding position, particularly where enhanced visual identification is needed during reduced-visibility operations. Under the ICAO arrangement it consists of three fixed, unidirectional yellow lights arranged symmetrically about, and at right angles to, the taxiway centreline, with one light on the centreline and one on each side.

When does ICAO require intermediate holding position lights?

ICAO requires them at an intermediate holding position intended for operation in runway visual range (RVR) conditions below 350 m, except where a stop bar has been installed. ICAO also recommends their use where there is no requirement for the stop-and-go function provided by a stop bar.

How are the lights arranged, spaced and coloured?

The ICAO arrangement is three fixed, unidirectional yellow lights, 1.5 m apart, symmetrical about the taxiway centreline and at right angles to it, located along the intermediate holding-position marking, 0.3 m before the marking. The output is directed toward the direction of approach with a light distribution similar to taxiway centreline lights where such centreline lights are provided. Set out the installation from the approved airport drawing.

How is an intermediate holding position light different from a stop bar?

An intermediate holding position light is generally intended to identify a holding point, whereas a stop bar gives a controlled red stop indication associated with movement authorization and interlocking, which is the stop-and-go function. The two should not be presented as interchangeable, and the project design determines which visual aid is appropriate.

How is it different from a runway guard light?

Both are yellow and unidirectional, but the intermediate holding light gives an intermediate holding indication and is fixed, while the runway guard light is a runway-entry warning that is alternating or flashing where applicable. The holding light is located at an intermediate holding position and coordinated with the taxiway holding marking, whereas the runway guard light is at the runway-holding position of a taxiway/runway intersection.

How does it relate to the FAA clearance bar light?

FAA AIM identifies clearance bar lights at taxiway holding positions as three in-pavement, steady-burning yellow lights that increase holding-position conspicuity in low visibility. FAA terminology does not map one-to-one to the ICAO product name, so for FAA-based projects verify the applicable clearance-bar or holding-position lighting design rather than assuming ICAO designations are interchangeable.

How is the light installed in the pavement?

It is an in-pavement fixture with a housing, treated as a coordinated pavement installation. Verify the housing and fixture dimensions, installation depth and pavement cut-out, fixture seating and fasteners, drainage and sealing, and cable routing and maintenance access. The manufacturer's approved installation drawing should control all dimensional requirements.

What electrical checks are needed before adding or replacing lights?

Fixtures in a series circuit must be electrically compatible with the complete airport lighting system. Confirm the CCR output current, existing circuit configuration, fixture electrical characteristics, transformer rating, number of fixtures, primary and secondary cable, connector system, intensity steps and monitoring. Incorrect coordination may cause low or excessive intensity, uneven illumination, circuit instability, transformer stress, premature failure or monitoring faults.

Does an LED light source make the fixture compliant?

No. LED technology can offer long operating life, lower maintenance, stable yellow output and controlled optical distribution, but it does not establish compliance on its own. The complete product must satisfy the applicable photometric, electrical, environmental and mechanical requirements.

How are intermediate holding position lights tested and commissioned?

Testing is done as part of the complete AGL system. Visual inspection covers fixture and lens condition, yellow colour, fixture position, the three-light arrangement and spacing, alignment, and the pavement interface. Electrical testing covers circuit continuity, insulation resistance, transformer verification, series-circuit current and CCR output, intensity-step operation and monitoring. Photometric verification (colour, intensity, beam distribution, optical direction and alignment) applies where required.

What are the common failure modes and how are they diagnosed?

Typical symptoms are one light off, the complete three-light assembly off, low light output, incorrect yellow colour and pattern misalignment. If other lights on the same series circuit are also dark, treat it as a series-circuit or CCR fault. For an individual failure, work through the sequence CCR, primary series circuit, isolating transformer, secondary cable, connector, holding fixture, light source, optical assembly, alignment and photometric verification.

What should be confirmed before ordering?

Confirm the airport and project, taxiway identification, the intermediate holding position and marking, the RVR requirement, whether a stop bar and taxiway centreline lights are present, the three-light yellow, fixed, unidirectional arrangement with 1.5 m spacing and 0.3 m marking offset, series-circuit current, isolating transformer, connector type, housing, installation depth, pavement cut-out, the applicable standard, and the photometric and compliance documentation.

Which standards apply to intermediate holding position lights?

Select, install and test against the applicable edition of the governing aerodrome standards and the approved project specification. For ICAO-based projects that is Annex 14 Vol. I. For FAA-based projects, verify the clearance-bar or holding-position design. In India, requirements include DGCA requirements, airport-specific AGL specifications, the approved holding-position layout, low-visibility procedures, stop-bar requirements and the taxiway centreline-lighting arrangement.

Engineering Note

Intermediate holding position lighting is a specific visual-aid function, not simply another yellow taxiway light.

For an ICAO-based installation, the key characteristics are the three-light configuration, yellow fixed unidirectional output, 1.5 m spacing, 0.3 m location before the marking and correct relationship to the taxiway centreline. For FAA projects, assess the applicable clearance-bar and holding-position requirements separately, because FAA terminology and system design are not necessarily identical to the ICAO configuration.
