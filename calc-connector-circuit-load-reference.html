Calculator #27

Connector / Circuit Load Reference

Check a series circuit's current and voltage against L-823 cable connector ratings, with contact voltage drop and loss.

About this calculator

Every fixture on a series circuit adds mated connectors to the current path. This tool checks the circuit against the rated current and voltage of the two L-823 connector types, shows the worst-case contact drop and heat the connectors are allowed to produce, and turns a field millivolt reading into a contact resistance you can accept or reject.

FAQ

Connector / Circuit Load Reference Calculator: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on checking a series circuit against L-823 connector ratings.

What does the Connector / Circuit Load Reference calculator do?

It checks a series circuit's current and voltage against the rated current and voltage of the two L-823 connector types. It also shows the worst-case contact drop and heat that the connectors are allowed to produce, and turns a field millivolt reading into a contact resistance you can accept or reject.

What are the two connector types?

Type I is a 1-conductor connector rated 25 A and 5,000 V, and it is the usual choice for a series primary. Type II is a 2-conductor connector rated 20 A and 600 V between contacts, and also 1,500 V to ground, and it suits low-voltage secondary and fixture leads. Class A connectors are factory moulded to the cable and Class B are field attached. A connector must not be moulded to a cable with a higher voltage rating than the connector.

How is the loading checked?

Current loading is the circuit current divided by the rated current, and voltage loading is the maximum voltage divided by the rated voltage. Both must be at or below 100 percent for the connector to be suitable. For a primary, use the highest CCR output voltage, for example from Calculator #14.

What contact resistance is allowed?

The specification limits the drop across a mated contact pair while it carries rated current, so the largest acceptable resistance is the drop limit divided by the rated current. Type I allows 7.5 mV at 25 A and Type II allows 6.0 mV at 20 A, which is 0.30 milliohms for both.

How are the circuit drop and loss calculated?

The drop is the number of mated connectors times k times the current times the maximum contact resistance, and the loss is the number of connectors times k times the current squared times the maximum resistance. Here k is the contact pairs in the current path, 1 for Type I and 2 for Type II because current passes through both pins. This is the worst case a compliant connector may produce, not what a good one will.

How does the field reading check work?

The measured resistance is the measured millivolt drop divided by the test current, and the reading passes if the resistance is at or below the maximum. The specification limit applies at rated current, so at a lower current the check works on contact resistance instead.

Can you give a worked example?

A Type I connector at 6.6 A and 840 V is loaded 26.4 percent on current and 16.8 percent on voltage. The maximum contact resistance is 0.30 milliohms, which is 1.98 mV per connector at 6.6 A. For 200 connectors the circuit drop is 0.40 V and the loss is 2.61 W. A reading of 1.5 mV at 6.6 A is 0.227 milliohms, which passes.

What does the tool not do?

It does not model ageing, corrosion, thermal rise or connector fit, and it holds no ICAO or IEC connector data. Ratings follow FAA AC 150/5345-26E, so confirm the edition in force and the manufacturer's certified data, which may state stricter limits than the specification. Contact resistance also rises with ageing and contamination, so a connector that passes today is not guaranteed to pass later.

Method & Reference

How the connector is checked

Every step is shown so the result can be checked by hand.

1. Loading

Current loading = I ÷ I_rated    Voltage loading = V ÷ V_rated

Both must be at or below 100 % for the connector to be suitable. Type II is checked against its 600 V rating between contacts, and it is also rated 1500 V to ground.

2. Allowed contact resistance

The specification limits the drop across a mated contact pair while it carries rated current, so the largest acceptable resistance follows from that.

R_max = ΔV_limit ÷ I_rated

Type I allows 7.5 mV at 25 A, and Type II allows 6.0 mV at 20 A, which is 0.30 mΩ for both.

3. Drop and loss on the circuit

ΔV = n × k × I × R_max    P = n × k × I² × R_max

Here n is the number of mated connectors, and k is the contact pairs in the current path: 1 for Type I, and 2 for a Type II connector because current passes through both pins. This is the worst case a compliant connector may produce, not what a good one will.

4. Field reading

R_measured = ΔV_measured ÷ I_test

The reading passes if the resistance is at or below R_max.
