Field-ready, tickable checklists for the most common AGL maintenance and fault-response scenarios — from routine daily/weekly/monthly CCR checks, through fixture, cable, connector, and transformer fault response, to PAPI/approach/stop bar checks, earth and insulation fault response, CAT II/III serviceability verification, and time-critical emergency response. Purpose — A quick, daily first-shift verification of CCR health and output status across all series circuits, intended to catch developing faults before they escalate to an operational outage.

Safety Precautions

  • Report any visible smoke, arcing, or unusual odour immediately and do not approach the cabinet.

Pass / Completion Criteria

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about the Troubleshooting Checklist Library in the AGL Troubleshooting Atlas.

Q1What does Division 28 of the AGL Troubleshooting Atlas cover?

Division 28 is the Troubleshooting Checklist Library. It publishes 20 ready-to-use checklists, CHK-641 to CHK-660, for CCRs, lighting circuits, transformers, cables, connectors, signage, PAPI, approach and stop bar lights, earth and insulation faults, photometry, ALCMS, CAT II/III readiness, rain-related faults and emergencies.

Q2Which checklists are in the library?

The 20 checklists are CHK-641 Daily CCR Troubleshooting Checklist; CHK-642 Weekly CCR Troubleshooting Checklist; CHK-643 Monthly CCR Troubleshooting Checklist; CHK-644 Runway Light Fault Checklist; CHK-645 Taxiway Light Fault Checklist; CHK-646 Transformer Fault Checklist; CHK-647 Cable Fault Checklist; CHK-648 Connector Fault Checklist; CHK-649 Signage Fault Checklist; CHK-650 PAPI Fault Checklist; CHK-651 Approach Light Fault Checklist; CHK-652 Stop Bar Fault Checklist; CHK-653 Earth Fault Checklist; CHK-654 Insulation Failure Checklist; CHK-655 Photometry Fault Checklist; CHK-656 ALCMS Fault Checklist; CHK-657 CAT II Fault Checklist; CHK-658 CAT III Fault Checklist; CHK-659 Rain-Related Fault Checklist; CHK-660 Emergency AGL Fault Checklist.

Q3What does each checklist include?

Every checklist has a purpose statement, frequency and scope (how often, how long and how many technicians), safety precautions, the checklist items grouped by activity, pass/completion criteria, what to do if any item fails, and related references.

Q4How many checkable steps are there?

The 20 checklists contain 268 checkable items in total. Each checklist shows a running count of checked items, for example 0 / 14 for the Daily CCR Troubleshooting Checklist (CHK-641).

Q5Which checklists are for routine CCR checks?

CHK-641 Daily CCR Troubleshooting Checklist, CHK-642 Weekly CCR Troubleshooting Checklist and CHK-643 Monthly CCR Troubleshooting Checklist cover routine CCR verification. The Daily checklist is a first-shift check of CCR health and output status intended to catch developing faults before they cause an outage.

Q6Does every checklist require lock-out/tag-out (LOTO)?

No. Each checklist states its own safety precautions. For example, the Daily CCR Troubleshooting Checklist is a visual/reading-only check for which LOTO is not required, but it says not to open any energised cabinet beyond the viewing window or front panel and to report visible smoke, arcing or unusual odour immediately.

Q7What should I do if a checklist item fails?

Each checklist has an 'If Any Item Fails' section that says what to do next and points to the relevant Atlas topics.
