Field Photometry vs Workshop Photometry

FIELD PHOTOMETRY
VS
WORKSHOP PHOTOMETRY

Two different ways of measuring AGL fixture light output — field photometry measures the photometric performance of AGL fixtures in their actual installed condition, while workshop photometry measures an individual fixture under controlled workshop/laboratory conditions. One answers "how is the installed light performing at the airport?" The other answers "how does the fixture perform under controlled test conditions?"

Terminology and scope: "Workshop photometry" here means laboratory photometry of an individual fixture (goniophotometer or photometric test bench); type-test and certification photometry is normally done by a qualified or accredited laboratory. Field photometry is normally done with a mobile measuring unit — Annex 14 calls for this on precision-approach and runway lighting — and mainly assesses the intensity, main-beam and orientation of installed lights. It does not reproduce a full goniophotometric distribution. See also Laboratory Photometry vs Field Photometry.

02 · Basic Difference

Field Photometry

Field photometry measures the light where it is actually installed and operating — as an in-service reading of the complete AGL installation, not just the fixture.

Illustrative percentages only. Maintenance thresholds are set by the applicable standard and the airport maintenance manual.

A declining field result may indicate:

  • LED degradation
  • Lamp aging
  • Dirty lens
  • Optical deterioration
  • Misalignment
  • Electrical problems
  • Mechanical movement

The root cause should be investigated rather than assuming the fixture itself has failed.

Reference point: Annex 14 treats a light as unserviceable when its main-beam average intensity falls below 50% of the value specified for a new light. Airport maintenance programmes for precision-approach and runway lighting therefore include periodic field measurement of intensity, beam spread and orientation. Verify the exact provisions and frequency in the current edition.

Combine both methods: When a field reading is low, removing the fixture for workshop photometry and comparing it with the type-test data separates optical degradation (lens, reflector, LED or lamp) from installation, alignment or circuit causes.

12 · Example: PAPI

Checking a PAPI Both Ways

Workshop Photometry

The unit can be tested under controlled conditions for light intensity, beam characteristics, transition characteristics, color, and optical performance.

Field Verification

After installation, the system must also be correctly positioned, aligned, elevated, oriented, and set to the required visual/approach geometry.

A PAPI can pass workshop photometry but still require field adjustment and verification after installation.

On site, PAPI unit setting angles and alignment are verified by direct angular measurement and confirmed by flight inspection. This is a geometry and alignment check rather than a mobile-unit intensity measurement.

16 · Engineering Verdict

Field Photometry vs Workshop Photometry

Engineering Verdict

Field photometry is the photometric measurement of an AGL fixture/lighting system in its actual installed and operating condition, including the effects of alignment, mounting, circuit conditions, and in-service degradation.

Workshop photometry is the photometric measurement of an individual AGL fixture under controlled test conditions to verify and characterize its optical performance. Neither replaces the other — the practical sequence runs new fixture → workshop photometry → FAT/product verification → installation → field alignment → field photometry → commissioning/in-service verification.

WorkshopControlled fixture performance — goniophotometer, test bench, FAT, certification.
FieldActual installed performance — alignment, CCR current, circuit, contamination included.

Passing workshop photometry does not by itself prove that the installed AGL system will meet field photometric requirements.

17 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fundamental difference between field photometry and workshop photometry?

Field photometry measures an AGL fixture's photometric performance in its actual installed condition at the airport, while workshop photometry measures an individual fixture under controlled workshop or laboratory conditions before or after removal from service.

Can a fixture pass workshop photometry but fail in the field?

Yes. A fixture may be perfectly manufactured and pass every workshop test, but still show poor field photometric performance due to misalignment, mounting condition, CCR current, circuit effects, or lens contamination after installation.

Which method is more repeatable?

Workshop photometry is generally more repeatable because power, position, and environment can be controlled. Field photometry has more variables — weather, installation, alignment, circuit condition, and contamination — so results can vary more.

When is field photometry used instead of workshop photometry?

Field photometry is used for maintenance and compliance verification of installed AGL systems, since it captures the combined effect of the fixture, alignment, mounting, CCR current, circuit condition, and environmental factors together.

When is workshop photometry used instead of field photometry?

Workshop photometry is used for product development, design verification, type testing, production testing, FAT, certification, and comparison between fixtures — anywhere a controlled, repeatable measurement of the fixture itself is required.

Does workshop photometry replace field verification for systems like PAPI?

No. A PAPI unit can pass workshop photometry for intensity, beam characteristics, and color, but still require field adjustment and verification for positioning, alignment, elevation, orientation, and the required visual/approach geometry after installation.

18 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project or maintenance program, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents and the approved test procedure.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I — Appendices & Chapter 10

Aerodrome Design and Operations: Appendix 1 (colours) and Appendix 2 (characteristics of aeronautical ground lights, including intensity and isocandela requirements), and Chapter 10 (maintenance, including field measurement of intensity, beam spread and orientation, and serviceability levels). Verify clause numbers in the current edition.

ICAO Aerodrome Design Manual, Doc 9157 Part 4

Visual Aids: design guidance supporting the Annex 14 light characteristics. Guidance material, not a Standard.

ICAO Airport Services Manual, Doc 9137 Part 9

Airport Maintenance Practices: guidance on visual aid maintenance, including light serviceability.

FAA AC 150/5345-46

Specification for Runway and Taxiway Light Fixtures: photometric requirements for fixtures, the basis for workshop and type-test photometry.

FAA AC 150/5345-53

Airport Lighting Equipment Certification Program: third-party certification of airport lighting equipment.

FAA AC 150/5340-26

Maintenance of Airport Visual Aid Facilities: recommended maintenance practice for airport lighting.

FAA AC 150/5345-28

Specification for PAPI systems: requirements for PAPI units, relevant to the PAPI example above.

DGCA / AAI Requirements (India)

For Indian airport projects, verify applicable DGCA and AAI requirements, airport-specific maintenance manuals and approved photometric test procedures.

Final Engineering Note

Do not substitute one method for the other. The applicable specification and approved test procedure should always govern the scope, sequence, and pass/fail criteria of workshop and field photometry.

Workshop photometry is not a substitute for field photometry, and field photometry is not a substitute for workshop photometry — each answers a different question about fixture and installation performance.
