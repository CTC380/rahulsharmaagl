Approach & Landing Systems Comparison

ELEVATED VS INSET Approach Lights

The basic difference is how the light fixture is physically installed. An elevated approach light is mounted above the ground on a support, while an inset approach light is installed flush with or recessed into the ground/surface.

Elevated

An elevated fixture is mounted on a support above the ground. The light source is physically above the approach surface.

03 · Key Difference

Light Above Surface vs Light at Surface

Elevated

Light above surface

  • Better physical separation from the ground
  • Easy visual inspection
  • Conventional approach-light installation

Inset

Light at surface

  • Minimal obstruction
  • Suitable where elevated structures cannot be used
  • Requires proper pavement/housing/drainage design

In practice: most approach-lighting systems are elevated. The pattern runs hundreds of metres out from the threshold over open and often uneven ground, so lights are carried on frangible masts or poles, raised where needed to keep the pattern close to the required plane through the threshold. Inset approach lights are the exception, used where a light position falls on a paved surface, such as a displaced-threshold area; the approved design and applicable standard decide where.

04 · Why Elevated?

Why Use Elevated Lights?

Elevated fixtures are generally preferred where there is sufficient clearance and no need for a flush installation. Advantages include:

  • Simple installation
  • Easy visual inspection
  • Easy fixture replacement
  • Good heat dissipation
  • Less complicated pavement interface
  • Easier access for maintenance
  • Clear optical path
05 · Why Inset?

Why Use Inset Lights?

Inset lights are useful where an elevated fixture would create an unacceptable obstruction or physical conflict. Typical considerations include:

  • Vehicle movement
  • Aircraft clearance
  • Pavement crossings
  • Restricted approach-area geometry
  • Areas where supports cannot be installed
  • Locations requiring a flush surface

Key advantage: The fixture does not significantly protrude above the surface.

07 · Clearance

Vehicle and Aircraft Clearance

This is one of the biggest differences.

Elevated

Because the fixture projects above the surface, the installation must consider:

  • Frangibility
  • Aircraft clearance
  • Vehicle clearance
  • Equipment clearance
  • Structural loading

Inset

The fixture has a very low profile. It is much better suited to areas where:

Nothing should protrude significantly above the surface.

08 · Optical Function

Mounting Type vs Lighting Function

The two mounting arrangements do not automatically mean different lighting functions. For example, an approach-lighting function can require a particular:

  • Intensity
  • Beam spread
  • Vertical distribution
  • Horizontal distribution
  • Colour
  • Aiming angle

The fixture must be installed so that the required photometric performance is achieved.

Elevated vs inset is primarily a fixture/mounting distinction, not an approach-category distinction.

09 · Maintenance

Maintenance Access

Elevated

Maintenance personnel can normally, without opening a pavement housing:

  • Access fixture
  • Inspect lens
  • Check lamp/LED
  • Check alignment
  • Replace components
  • Inspect cable/transformer

Inset

Maintenance may require:

  • Opening the inset housing
  • Removing cover/lens
  • Checking seals
  • Inspecting drainage
  • Checking transformer/cable connections
  • Cleaning internal components
  • Re-sealing the housing

Inset fixtures generally have more demanding civil/environmental maintenance.

10 · Drainage

Why Drainage Matters for Inset Fixtures

Drainage is particularly important for inset fixtures.

Water must not remain around the fixture. Poor drainage can lead to:

  • Water ingress
  • Corrosion
  • Insulation problems
  • Electrical faults
  • Lens contamination
  • Reduced optical performance

Elevated fixtures generally have less dependence on a recessed drainage system.

11 · Snow & Debris

Environmental Exposure

Elevated

The fixture is above the surrounding surface, so snow and debris accumulation may be less problematic.

Inset

The fixture is close to the surface and may be affected by:

  • Snow
  • Mud
  • Dust
  • Sand
  • Standing water
  • Rubber/debris contamination

Cleaning and drainage become particularly important for inset fixtures.

13 · Frangibility

Frangibility Considerations

Elevated

Frangibility is a major design consideration because the fixture/support projects above the ground. ICAO Annex 14 specifies that elevated approach lights and their supporting structures be frangible, with one exception: beyond 300 m from the threshold, where a structure is taller than 12 m, frangibility applies to the top 12 m only.

Inset

Because the fixture is flush with the surface, the frangibility problem is greatly reduced, although the housing still has to withstand the required loads.

14 · Photometric Alignment

Aiming & Optical Adjustment

Elevated

Adjustment is generally straightforward because the fixture can be physically aimed. Technicians can check:

  • Horizontal orientation
  • Vertical angle
  • Beam direction
  • Fixture elevation

Inset

The fixture's optical alignment is more dependent on:

  • Correct housing installation
  • Correct orientation
  • Pavement elevation
  • Factory-set optical geometry
  • Accurate installation tolerances

Civil installation accuracy is particularly important for inset fixtures.

16 · Selection Logic

Typical Selection Logic

Select Elevated When

  • There is adequate clearance
  • No surface obstruction is created
  • Conventional installation is acceptable
  • Easy maintenance is important
  • Approach-area conditions permit supports

Select Inset When

  • Surface clearance is critical
  • A raised fixture is undesirable
  • Aircraft/vehicle clearance is restricted
  • The fixture must cross a paved or trafficked area
  • A flush installation is specifically required

18 · Important Clarification

Elevated Does Not Mean "Better"

An elevated fixture is not inherently better than an inset fixture. The choice depends on:

  • Optical requirements
  • Aircraft clearance
  • Surface conditions
  • Structural requirements
  • Drainage
  • Maintenance
  • Snow/debris environment
  • Civil works
  • Applicable FAA/ICAO requirements

The correct fixture is the one that satisfies the required photometric and installation criteria.

19 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: Inset lights are lower intensity
Fact

Not necessarily. Fixture mounting type does not by itself determine intensity.

Myth: Elevated lights are only for low-category approaches
Fact

No. Elevated/inset refers to the physical installation arrangement, not approach category.

Myth: Inset means underground and invisible
Fact

The housing is recessed, but the optical portion of the light remains visible to the pilot.

Myth: Inset lights don't require drainage
Fact

The opposite: proper drainage and sealing are especially important.

Myth: All approach lights can simply be changed from elevated to inset
Fact

No. The fixture must be specifically approved/designed for the required optical, structural and environmental conditions.

21 · Engineering Verdict

Elevated vs Inset — The Bottom Line

Engineering Verdict

An elevated approach light is mounted above the ground on a support and is the norm for most of an approach-lighting system, giving simple, accessible installation where clearance permits. An inset approach light is installed flush with or recessed into the surface, chosen where a light position falls on a paved surface or surface obstruction must be minimised.

The mounting arrangement does not by itself define whether the approach lighting is simple, precision, CAT I, CAT II or CAT III.

22 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Are most approach lights elevated or inset?

Most are elevated, mounted on frangible masts or poles. Inset lights are used where a light position falls on a paved surface, so the choice follows the ground the light sits on rather than the approach category.

Are inset approach lights lower intensity than elevated lights?

Not necessarily. Fixture mounting type does not by itself determine intensity.

Are elevated approach lights only used for lower approach categories?

No. Elevated/inset refers to the physical installation arrangement, not approach category.

Is an inset fixture completely hidden underground?

No. The housing is recessed, but the optical portion of the light remains visible to the pilot.

Do inset lights need drainage?

Yes — the opposite of a common assumption. Proper drainage and sealing are especially important for inset fixtures.

Can any approach light simply be swapped from elevated to inset?

No. The fixture must be specifically approved/designed for the required optical, structural and environmental conditions.

Which type is easier to maintain?

Elevated fixtures are generally easier to maintain since technicians can access, inspect and replace components without opening a pavement housing. Inset fixtures require opening the housing, checking seals and inspecting drainage.

23 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual airport project, verify the current applicable editions of the relevant standards before using this comparison for project design.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — approach-lighting layout, fixture mounting, and frangibility requirements for elevated approach lights and their supports. Verify the amendment level adopted by your State (Amendment 18 became applicable on 27 November 2025).

ICAO Aerodrome Design Manual (Doc 9157)

Part 4 (Visual Aids), Part 5 (Electrical Systems) and Part 6 (Frangibility) — design guidance on aerodrome lighting, its electrical supply and frangible mounting.

FAA AC 150/5340-30

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids — installation and siting details for approach-lighting systems.

FAA AC 150/5345 series

Equipment specifications, including AC 150/5345-46 (runway and taxiway light fixtures) and AC 150/5345-42 (light bases). Approach-light fixtures and their support structures are covered by the FAA equipment specifications applicable to them.

DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Fixture selection, frangibility and drainage requirements are configuration- and location-specific. For final design, use the configuration drawings and dimensional criteria from the applicable current standard rather than a generic comparison page.
