Calculator #17

Isolating Transformer Load

Match a series isolating transformer rating to the fixture load it serves, with loading percentage, secondary voltage and losses.

About this calculator

Each fixture on a series circuit is fed through an isolating transformer that isolates the lamp circuit from the high-voltage series circuit and keeps the series circuit closed if the lamp fails. A transformer that is too small overheats, and one that is too large runs lightly loaded, with lower efficiency and a poorer power factor that add to the circuit load. This tool finds the smallest standard size that carries the load and shows how every size compares.

Method & Reference

How the transformer is matched

Every step is shown so the result can be checked by hand.

1. Secondary load

Add the fixtures and any extra load on the secondary, then convert to apparent power with the fixture power factor.

W = n × W_fixture + W_extra    S = W ÷ PF

2. Loading against each size

Each standard size is compared as a percentage of its nominal rating, treated as VA at the rated secondary current (6.6 A). At a lower operating current the usable capacity scales down in proportion.

Loading = S ÷ (rating × I ÷ Irated) × 100

The recommended size is the smallest one at or below your maximum loading. If that size is below your preferred minimum, the tool says so, because a lightly loaded transformer wastes capacity and can run at a poorer power factor.

3. Secondary voltage

The secondary carries the lamp current. It equals the circuit current for a 6.6/6.6 A transformer, while a 20/6.6 A transformer has 20 A in the primary and 6.6 A in the secondary. The voltage across the load follows from the apparent power and the secondary current.

V_secondary = S ÷ I    V_rated = rating ÷ Irated

4. Primary side

Dividing by efficiency gives the real power the primary circuit must supply. This is the figure that feeds the circuit load calculation. It is real power only: for CCR kVA the transformer's magnetising current also counts, and FAA AC 150/5345-47C allows a power factor as low as 0.95 at rated load (see Calculators #13 and #16).

P_primary = W ÷ η    Loss = P_primary − W

What this tool does not do

It does not model transformer efficiency curves, magnetising current, secondary open-circuit voltage or lamp-out behaviour, and the sizes offered are nominal. A fixture with a switching driver can behave differently from a plain lamp, so use the manufacturer's guidance for the pairing. This tool covers sizing and primary load; for efficiency across loading use Calculator #18, and for a multi-fixture order list use Calculator #49.

FAQ

Isolating Transformer Load Calculator: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on matching a series isolating transformer rating to the fixture load it serves.

What does the Isolating Transformer Load calculator do?

It matches a series isolating transformer rating to the fixture load it serves. It finds the smallest standard size that carries the load and shows how every size compares, with loading percentage, secondary voltage and losses. A transformer that is too small overheats, and one that is too large runs lightly loaded, with lower efficiency and a poorer power factor that add to the circuit load.

How is the secondary load calculated?

Add the fixtures and any extra load on the secondary, then convert to apparent power with the fixture power factor. The real load is the number of fixtures times the watts per fixture plus the extra load, and the apparent load is that real load divided by the power factor. Use 1.00 for plain lamps, and the driver's power factor for LED fixtures.

How is the transformer size chosen?

Each standard size is compared as a percentage of its nominal rating, treated as VA at the rated secondary current of 6.6 A. At a lower operating current the usable capacity scales down in proportion. The recommended size is the smallest one at or below your maximum loading. If that size is below your preferred minimum, the tool says so, because a lightly loaded transformer wastes capacity. The loading window is your project's own, not a standard value.

Which transformer sizes are offered?

The sizes are 10/15 W, 20/25 W and 30/45 W (dual-rated, treated as 15, 25 and 45 VA), then 65 W, 100 W, 150 W, 200 W, 300 W and 500 W, the ratings listed in FAA AC 150/5345-47C. They are nominal sizes treated as VA, so confirm them against your transformer data sheet.

What secondary current do I enter for a 20/6.6 A transformer?

Enter 6.6 A, which is the secondary, and not the 20 A primary current. The secondary voltage is worked out from this value. The rated secondary current is the current at which the nominal VA rating applies, normally 6.6 A. Some 300 W and 500 W types have a 20 A secondary, and for those you enter 20 A.

How are the secondary voltage and primary load worked out?

The secondary voltage is the apparent power divided by the secondary (lamp) current, which is 6.6 A for most types. The primary real load is the secondary real load divided by the efficiency, and the transformer loss is the difference. The primary load is real power and feeds the circuit load calculation. For CCR kVA the transformer's magnetising current also counts, and FAA AC 150/5345-47C allows a power factor as low as 0.95 at rated load, so use Calculators #13 and #16 for kVA. Efficiency is a planning assumption, so use manufacturer data; the AC sets minimums of 70 to 90 percent depending on size.

Can you give a worked example?

One 45 W fixture with a power factor of 1.00 gives 45 VA, and at 6.6 A the secondary voltage is 6.82 V. A 30/45 W transformer is loaded 100 percent, at the limit, and a 65 W transformer is loaded 69 percent, so the recommended size is 30/45 W. At 90 percent efficiency the primary load is 50 W with 5 W of loss, and 100 transformers give 5.00 kW of primary load.

What does the tool not do?

It does not model transformer efficiency curves, magnetising current, secondary open-circuit voltage or lamp-out behaviour, and the sizes offered are nominal. A fixture with a switching driver can behave differently from a plain lamp, so use the manufacturer's guidance for the pairing. For efficiency across loading use Calculator #18, and for a multi-fixture order list use Calculator #49. Final selection shall be verified against the isolating transformer and fixture manufacturers' data.
