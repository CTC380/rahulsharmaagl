TUM-01 AGL Technology Assessment
Purpose

Technology assessment establishes the current technological maturity of the AGL system and identifies opportunities for modernization.

Assess
  • Existing technology
  • Equipment age
  • Technology generation
  • Performance
  • Reliability
  • Maintainability
  • Availability
  • Energy consumption
  • Obsolescence
  • Spare availability
  • Manufacturer support
  • Integration capability
  • Future expansion capability
Key Question

Technology assessment should answer: is the existing technology still technically, operationally and economically appropriate for the required asset lifecycle?

TUM-06 Intelligent Sensors
Purpose

Sensors can provide condition and performance information that is difficult to obtain through periodic inspection alone.

Potential Monitoring Parameters
  • Temperature
  • Current
  • Voltage
  • Equipment status
  • Vibration where relevant
  • Environmental conditions
  • Energy consumption
  • Equipment operating condition
Important Principle

Sensors should only be deployed where the information has a defined lifecycle-management purpose. Collecting data without a decision or action linked to that data does not create asset-management value.

Division 17 — Core Principle

Technology modernization should be driven by measurable improvements in safety, reliability, availability, maintainability, operational capability, energy efficiency and lifecycle value — not simply by the availability of newer technology.

The strategic chain is: Existing Technology → Technology Assessment → Condition + Performance → Obsolescence → Operational Need → Technology Options → Risk Assessment → Lifecycle Cost → Business Case → Upgrade Planning → Implementation → Testing & Commissioning → Post-Upgrade Performance Verification → New Technology Baseline → Asset Health & Reliability Monitoring → Future Technology Review.

This makes Division 17 the formal technology transformation and modernization layer of the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide, while Division 16 remains focused on refurbishment and physical life extension.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Technology Upgrade & Modernization in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide.

Q1What is Division 17 — Technology Upgrade & Modernization in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

Division 17 is the technology transformation layer. It covers 18 topics covering AGL technology assessment, LED conversion, Smart AGL, digital control, remote monitoring, intelligent sensors, ALCMS modernization, network modernization, energy-efficiency upgrades, legacy-system modernization, interoperability, new technology evaluation, technology risk, technology lifecycle cost, upgrade business case, planning, implementation and post-upgrade performance verification.

Q2What is an AGL technology assessment?

It establishes the current technological maturity of the AGL system and identifies modernization opportunities, considering existing technology, equipment age, technology generation, performance and reliability.

Q3What is LED conversion?

LED conversion is one of the major AGL modernization opportunities, replacing conventional light sources with LED technology. It requires checking existing fixture condition and optical and electrical compatibility.

Q4What is Smart AGL?

Smart AGL is the transition from conventional equipment-centric operation toward data-driven asset management. It includes asset-level and condition monitoring, remote diagnostics, intelligent alarms and performance monitoring.

Q5Which digital technologies does Division 17 cover?

It covers digital control (digital CCR control, PLC-based control, HMI), remote monitoring, intelligent sensors, ALCMS modernization and network modernization, since modern AGL systems increasingly depend on reliable communication networks.

Q6How does Division 17 differ from Division 16?

Division 16 asks whether an existing asset can be refurbished or its life extended. Division 17 asks the broader question of what technology the airport should adopt to improve the AGL system.

Q7Why do interoperability and technology risk matter?

Modernization should not create isolated technology platforms, so interoperability across CCR, ALCMS, PLC, HMI, sensors and monitoring systems is assessed. Every major modernization should include technology-risk assessment covering maturity, reliability, obsolescence, supplier dependency, proprietary technology and cyber/OT risk.

Q8How should an upgrade be justified and closed out?

Technology decisions should consider lifecycle cost, not initial price alone, and a major upgrade should have a documented business case. Post-upgrade performance verification is the formal closure point, comparing the upgraded system against the pre-upgrade baseline.
