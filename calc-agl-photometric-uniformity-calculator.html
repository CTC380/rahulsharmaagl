Method & Reference

How the uniformity is checked

Every figure comes from the formulas below, so the result can be checked by hand.

Statistics

Average = Σ readings ÷ n

σ = √( Σ(x − avg)² ÷ n )    CV = σ ÷ avg × 100

The standard deviation is the population form, dividing by n.

Uniformity ratios

min ÷ avg    min ÷ max    max ÷ min

The max : min ratio is shown as ∞ when the lowest reading is zero.

Per-fixture checks

Deviation % = (x − avg) ÷ avg × 100

% of nominal = x ÷ nominal × 100

A fixture fails if it is below the minimum % of nominal, or if its deviation from the average is beyond the limit in either direction.

Whole-set checks

The set fails if the average is below the minimum average % of nominal, or if the max : min ratio is above its limit. A blank or zero limit is not applied.

FAQ

AGL Photometric Uniformity Calculator: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on checking a row of intensity readings against your own limits.

What does the AGL Photometric Uniformity Calculator do?

It checks a row of intensity readings against limits you enter, and shows which fixtures to clean or re-lamp first. It returns the minimum, maximum and average, the uniformity ratios, each fixture's deviation from the average and its percentage of design intensity, and it flags the fixtures that fail your limits.

How do I enter the readings?

Paste one fixture per line, in candelas. You can add an ID before the value, for example E-14 3150 or E-14, 3150. A comma inside a line separates the ID from the value, so each fixture must be on its own line. The values shown on the page are sample data, so replace them with your own.

Which statistics and ratios does it calculate?

It calculates the average, the standard deviation in its population form (dividing by n), and the coefficient of variation, which is the standard deviation divided by the average. The uniformity ratios are minimum to average, minimum to maximum, and maximum to minimum. The maximum to minimum ratio is shown as infinity when the lowest reading is zero.

When is a fixture or a row flagged as failing?

A fixture fails if it is below the minimum percentage of nominal intensity, or if its deviation from the average is beyond the limit in either direction. The whole set fails if its average is below the minimum average percentage of nominal, or if the maximum to minimum ratio is above its limit. A blank or zero limit is not applied.

Are the limits built in?

No. All limits are placeholders that you set from your specification, the aerodrome manual or the applicable standard. Limits depend on the fixture type and the circuit category, so confirm them before issuing a compliance result. The design or nominal intensity is optional, and if you leave it empty you only get the statistics.

Can you give a worked example?

With the sample data, ten readings against a nominal of 10,000 cd give an average of 9,275 cd, with a lowest of 5,600 cd and a highest of 10,300 cd. The maximum to minimum ratio is 1.84 against a limit of 3. Fixture E-08 deviates by minus 39.6 percent against a limit of plus or minus 30 percent, so one fixture is flagged.

How should the readings be taken?

Take them with a calibrated meter, under the conditions your specification requires, and at the same brightness step for every fixture. The tool does not correct for meter error, aiming, ambient light or brightness step, so each reading must be taken the same way.

Is a pass a compliance certificate?

No. It is an engineering reference tool, and the tool does not know which standard or specification applies. The limits entered are the user's own and shall be confirmed against the specification, aerodrome manual or applicable standard before a compliance result is issued.
