Photometric Fundamentals Comparison

PHOTOMETRIC INTENSITY VS AVERAGE INTENSITY

Intensity at a Point vs Intensity Over a Range — photometric intensity describes the luminous intensity in one specific direction, while average intensity reduces several directional readings across a defined angular range into a single mean value. Both are expressed in candela, but they answer different engineering questions on an AGL fixture's beam.

02 · Definitions

What Is Photometric Intensity & Average Intensity?

Photometric Intensity

The luminous intensity in a particular direction from a fixture. For an AGL light, each angle around the beam has its own photometric intensity value, and together these values describe the photometric intensity distribution.

Example distribution

DirectionIntensity
0°5,000 cd
1°7,000 cd
2°9,000 cd
3°8,000 cd
4°6,000 cd
5°3,000 cd

Average Intensity

The mean luminous intensity taken over a defined angular range or set of directions. It reduces several directional readings into one representative figure.

Worked example

Using the 2°–4° readings from the distribution at left:

Iavg = (9000 + 8000 + 6000) / 3

Iavg = 7,667 cd for those three directions

03 · Biggest Difference

Point/Direction vs Range/Mean

"What is the intensity HERE?"

Photometric intensity answers this at one specific angle — for example, 8,000 cd at 3°.

"What is the MEAN intensity over this range?"

Average intensity answers this across several directions — for example, (9,000 + 8,000 + 6,000) / 3 = 7,667 cd.

Easy memory: Photometric intensity → POINT/DIRECTION. Average intensity → RANGE/MEAN.

04 · AGL Relevance

Why This Matters on the Beam

AGL lights are directional. An approach or runway light must produce appropriate intensity over a particular angular region as an aircraft moves through the beam. The individual directional intensities show how the light actually performs across that beam.

An average value can summarize part of that performance, but on its own it cannot show the detailed distribution an aircraft will actually encounter.

06 · A Third Quantity

Photometric vs Peak vs Average Intensity

Peak intensity is often confused with average intensity, but the two are distinct.

Term Meaning
Photometric intensityIntensity at a specified direction
Peak intensityHighest intensity found in the relevant distribution
Average intensityMean intensity over a defined range
0°–4° readings: 4,000 / 7,000 / 10,000 / 8,000 / 5,000 cd

Peak intensity (at 2°) = 10,000 cd  |  Average over 0°–4° = 6,800 cd

Peak ≠ Average ≠ Individual directional intensity — each answers a different engineering question.

08 · Important Caveat

"Average" Depends on the Governing Test

The exact meaning of average intensity depends on how the applicable test or specification defines the averaging. It could involve a defined angular interval, specific measurement directions, a specified beam sector, or multiple measurement points.

Do not assume "average intensity" always means a simple average of any arbitrary measurements. For an actual AGL compliance test, the applicable specification/test procedure controls the calculation.

09 · Relationship to Lux

Source-Side Intensity vs Surface Illuminance

Photometric and average intensity are both source-side directional quantities. They describe what the fixture emits, not what a surface downstream actually receives.

10 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: "Two fixtures with the same average intensity perform the same"
Fact

Incorrect. A flat 10,000 cd distribution and a steeply varying 2,000–18,000 cd distribution can share the same 10,000 cd average while producing very different beam performance.

Myth: Average intensity and peak intensity are the same thing
Fact

They are different quantities. Peak intensity is the single highest value in a distribution; average intensity is the mean across a defined range, and the two rarely coincide.

Myth: "Average" always means a simple mean of whatever points you choose
Fact

The applicable specification or test procedure defines exactly which points, intervals, or sectors go into the average — it is not left to arbitrary interpretation.

12 · Engineering Verdict

Photometric Intensity vs Average Intensity

Engineering Verdict

Photometric intensity and average intensity are not interchangeable quantities. Both are expressed in candela, but they answer different questions about the beam.

Photometric intensity preserves the detailed beam distribution at a specific direction; average intensity reduces a defined angular range to a single representative value. A fixture can share the same average with a very different fixture while performing very differently across the actual beam. Which value applies depends on what the governing specification is asking for.

PhotometricIntensity at a specific angle + candela + full beam detail preserved.
AverageMean intensity over a defined range + candela + detail reduced to one figure.

For AGL: use photometric intensity to evaluate how the beam actually performs at each angle, and average intensity when a specification asks for a single representative figure over a defined range.

13 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the simplest way to remember the difference?

Photometric intensity → POINT/DIRECTION (intensity at one angle). Average intensity → RANGE/MEAN (intensity averaged over several angles).

Are photometric intensity and average intensity measured in the same unit?

Yes. Both are expressed in candela (cd), because both are based on luminous intensity. The difference is how the value is represented, not the unit.

Can two fixtures have the same average intensity but different beam shapes?

Yes. A flat distribution and a widely varying distribution can produce the same average, even though their actual photometric performance across the beam is very different.

Is average intensity the same as peak intensity?

No. Peak intensity is the single highest value in a distribution, while average intensity is the mean over a defined range. The two are rarely equal.

Does "average intensity" always mean the same calculation?

No. The applicable test or specification defines which angular interval, directions, or beam sector are used in the averaging — it is not a fixed, universal calculation.

How does this relate to illuminance in lux?

Photometric and average intensity are source-side quantities describing what the fixture emits in candela. Illuminance in lux describes what a surface downstream actually receives once that light travels and lands.

Final Engineering Note

Do not treat an average-intensity figure as equivalent to the full photometric distribution. Confirm which quantity, and which averaging method, the governing standard actually requires.

Photometric intensity is not average intensity — one preserves the beam's detailed distribution, the other summarizes a defined portion of it into a single figure.
