Maintenance Planning & Scheduling

Division 24 establishes the planning and scheduling control system for the entire handbook. It connects the maintenance strategies of Divisions 01–23 with actual field execution — turning asset condition, risk and requirement into a resourced, coordinated, and tracked work window.

Philosophy: maintenance frequency and timing are controlled by the approved maintenance strategy and adjusted where justified by asset condition, risk, operational requirements, and maintenance history — not scheduled on a calendar alone.

What Planning Considers

Planning is not calendar-only — it weighs asset, resource, and operational factors together before a work window is set.

Asset Factors

  • Asset criticality and condition
  • Inspection findings
  • Failure history
  • Previous maintenance
  • Operating conditions and seasonal factors

Resource Factors

  • Spare availability
  • Manpower
  • Test equipment

Operational Factors

  • Airside restrictions
  • Aircraft operations
  • Work permits
  • LOTO requirements
  • Required shutdowns

Maintenance Priority Ranking

A practical priority framework ranks activities by urgency and consequence. Actual priority definitions should follow the airport's approved system.

PRIORITY 1 · CRITICAL

Immediate or urgent action required.

PRIORITY 2 · HIGH

Action required within a controlled short-term window.

PRIORITY 3 · MEDIUM

Planned maintenance required.

PRIORITY 4 · LOW

Can be incorporated into routine planned work.

Planned vs Completed Control

A simple monthly review can classify every activity into one of five statuses.

Green

Completed within the planned window.

Amber

Not completed as originally planned but controlled and rescheduled.

Red

Due date exceeded without acceptable closure.

Deferred

Formally deferred with documented justification and risk control.

Emergency

Unplanned work arising from failure or abnormal condition.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about maintenance planning & scheduling, based on the topics in this division.

9 QUESTIONS
Q1What is Maintenance Planning & Scheduling in the AGL Maintenance Handbook?

It is the planning and scheduling control system for the entire handbook. It connects the maintenance strategies of Divisions 01 to 23 with actual field execution, turning asset condition, risk and operational constraints into planned work.

Q2What does Division 24 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 24 (Maintenance Planning & Scheduling) contains 35 topics in four groups: Group A Maintenance Planning (9 topics), Group B Asset-Based Planning (8 topics), Group C Work Planning (11 topics) and Group D Schedule Control (7 topics).

Q3What four questions does planning answer?

What needs maintenance, why it needs maintenance, when it should be performed, and how it should be prepared and controlled.

Q4What is the maintenance planning cycle?

Every activity moves through nine stages before reaching the field: asset condition, maintenance requirement, priority, resources, work window, schedule, execution, completion and review.

Q5Is maintenance planning just a calendar?

No. Frequency and timing are controlled by the approved maintenance strategy and adjusted where justified by asset condition, risk, operational requirements and maintenance history. Planning weighs asset factors (criticality, condition, findings, failure history), resource factors (spares, manpower, test equipment) and operational factors (airside restrictions, aircraft operations, permits, LOTO and shutdowns) together.

Q6How are maintenance activities prioritised?

Priority 1 Critical needs immediate or urgent action, Priority 2 High needs action within a controlled short-term window, Priority 3 Medium is planned maintenance, and Priority 4 Low can be folded into routine planned work. Actual priority definitions should follow the airport's approved system.

Q7What does the maintenance schedule master register record?

It connects every planned activity to its resources, authorization and status: work order, asset ID, location, maintenance type (PM, CM, PdM, CBM or Emergency), activity, priority, due and planned dates, work window, manpower, spare status, test equipment, permit, LOTO, status, completion date, delay reason and remarks.

Q8How is planned versus completed work controlled?

A monthly review classifies every activity as Green (completed within the planned window), Amber (not completed as planned but controlled and rescheduled), Red (due date exceeded without acceptable closure), Deferred (formally deferred with documented justification and risk control) or Emergency (unplanned work from a failure or abnormal condition).

Q9How should maintenance backlog be managed?

Backlog is not simply a list of unfinished jobs. Each item carries its own risk profile, so it is reviewed by age, priority, risk, asset criticality, reason for delay, required resources, target completion and responsible person, and reduced accordingly.
