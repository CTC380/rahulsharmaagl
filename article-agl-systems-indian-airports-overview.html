Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) Systems in Indian Airports: A Complete Technical Overview

While passengers often notice runway lights during night flights, few realize the level of engineering, standards, and system coordination required behind these lights. Here's a practical technical overview of how AGL is implemented across Indian airports.

RS

Rahul Sharma

Manager – Technical & Commercial · AGL Photometry Specialist

March 16, 2026

Airfield Ground Lighting is one of the most critical safety systems in any airport. In Indian airports, AGL systems are designed and maintained to ensure safe aircraft operations during night conditions, low visibility, and adverse weather.

These systems follow strict regulatory frameworks defined by aviation authorities and international standards. This article provides a practical technical overview of how AGL is implemented across Indian airports and why it is essential for safe and reliable flight operations.

Key Takeaway

Airfield Ground Lighting transforms a dark airfield into a precisely guided pathway for aircraft operations — combining electrical design, optical precision, and strict regulatory compliance at Indian airports.

01

Role of AGL in Airport Operations

AGL systems provide the primary visual guidance for pilots during taxiing, approach, landing, and take-off — and they are especially important when natural visibility is limited. The lighting system helps pilots to:

  • Identify runway and taxiway alignment
  • Maintain correct aircraft positioning
  • Recognize runway thresholds and runway ends
  • Navigate safely between apron, taxiway, and runway

Without reliable AGL systems, safe aircraft movement during night operations would be extremely difficult.

02

Key Components of Airfield Ground Lighting

A modern airport lighting system consists of several integrated lighting elements designed for different operational purposes.

Runway Lighting

Runway edge lights, threshold lights, runway end lights, centreline lights (required on CAT II/III runways), and touchdown zone lights each have a specific colour, beam pattern, and intensity defined by international aviation standards.

Taxiway Lighting

Taxiway lighting ensures safe aircraft movement between runways and aprons, and helps pilots maintain proper taxi routes, while stop bars help prevent runway incursions.

Approach Lighting Systems

Approach lighting systems extend outward from the runway threshold and assist pilots during the final phase of landing — providing visual alignment, distance perception, and guidance in low-visibility conditions. The approach lighting type (simple, precision approach CAT I, or CAT II/III) is selected according to the approach category of the runway.

03

Power Supply and Electrical Infrastructure

Unlike conventional lighting systems, AGL circuits operate on a constant-current series-circuit configuration. The key electrical components include:

01CCRMultiple brightness steps by visibility
02Isolating TransformersPer-fitting electrical isolation
03Primary CablesSeries-circuit current across airfield
04Secondary CablesTransformer to light fitting

This configuration allows the lighting system to continue operating even if a single lamp fails, without interrupting the entire lighting circuit. The supply is backed by standby generators and UPS through automatic changeover, with switch-over times set by Annex 14 according to the runway category.

04

Standards Followed in Indian Airports

AGL systems installed in Indian airports must comply with international aviation standards and national regulatory requirements. The main standards and references include:

  • ICAO Annex 14 — Aerodrome Design and Operations
  • FAA Advisory Circulars (AC 150/5345 equipment specifications, AC 150/5340 design and maintenance)
  • DGCA requirements (CAR Section 4, aerodrome standards)
  • AAI technical specifications

These standards define photometric intensity requirements, beam distribution patterns, colour specifications, installation geometry, and light spacing.

Compliance with these requirements supports uniformity and safety across airports.

05

Photometric Performance and Quality Verification

Photometric performance is one of the most critical aspects of an AGL system. Every AGL fitting must produce:

Light IntensityCorrect output for the fixture type
Beam SpreadProper horizontal/vertical distribution
Colour OutputAccurate chromaticity
UniformityConsistent light distribution

Type photometric testing is performed in specialized laboratories using calibrated equipment to verify that fittings meet ICAO and FAA photometric requirements, and in-service measurement on the airfield confirms that installed lights continue to perform.

Without proper photometric validation, a lighting system may appear functional but fail to provide correct visual guidance to pilots.

06

Control and Monitoring Systems

Modern Indian airports increasingly use Airfield Lighting Control and Monitoring Systems, commonly known as ALCMS. These systems allow operators in the control tower or electrical substation to:

  • Control lighting circuits remotely
  • Adjust brightness levels through CCR steps
  • Monitor circuit health and failures
  • Respond quickly to maintenance issues

Integration with SCADA-based systems further improves operational monitoring and reliability.

07

Maintenance and Operational Reliability

AGL systems operate continuously in harsh environmental conditions, including rain, dust, heat, and aircraft loads. Regular maintenance activities include:

Cleaning light fittings and prisms
Checking lamp or LED performance
Verifying CCR output levels
Inspecting cable insulation and connectors
Conducting periodic photometric verification

Proper maintenance ensures that the lighting system remains reliable and compliant with safety standards.

08

Key Takeaways

  • AGL provides the primary visual guidance pilots rely on for taxiing, approach, landing, and take-off
  • Runway, taxiway, and approach lighting each follow strict colour, beam, and intensity requirements
  • CCRs, isolating transformers, and series circuits let the system keep running even if one light fails
  • ICAO Annex 14, FAA circulars, DGCA guidelines, and AAI specifications together govern Indian AGL installations
  • Photometric testing (lab type-testing plus in-service measurement) confirms a fitting actually delivers correct visual guidance, not just that it lights up
  • ALCMS and SCADA integration give operators remote control, brightness adjustment, and real-time fault monitoring
  • Regular cleaning, CCR verification, cable inspection, and photometric checks keep the network reliable over time

Conclusion

Airfield Ground Lighting systems are far more than simple runway lights. They are highly engineered safety systems that combine electrical design, optical precision, and strict regulatory compliance.

In Indian airports, AGL systems play a vital role in ensuring safe aircraft movement during night operations and low-visibility conditions. From approach lighting to taxiway guidance, every light is designed to deliver precise visual information to pilots.

Understanding the technical structure of these systems highlights the level of planning and engineering required to keep modern airports operating safely and efficiently.

Frequently Asked Questions

What role does AGL play in airport operations?

AGL provides the primary visual guidance for pilots during taxiing, approach, landing and take-off, and is especially important when natural visibility is limited. It helps pilots identify runway and taxiway alignment, maintain correct aircraft positioning, recognise runway thresholds and ends, and navigate between apron, taxiway and runway.

What colours do runway and taxiway lights use?

Runway edge lights are white (yellow in the caution zone at the runway end on instrument runways), threshold lights are green and runway end lights are red. Centreline lights are white, alternating red/white and then red towards the runway end, and touchdown zone lights are white. Taxiway edge lights are blue, taxiway centreline lights are green (alternating green/yellow in the ILS critical area), stop-bar lights are red and runway guard lights flash yellow.

How is AGL powered at Indian airports?

AGL circuits operate on a constant-current series-circuit configuration, using a CCR with multiple brightness steps, isolating transformers for per-fitting isolation, primary cables and secondary cables. The system keeps operating even if a single lamp fails. The supply is backed by standby generators and UPS through automatic changeover, with switch-over times set by Annex 14 according to the runway category.

Which standards apply to AGL at Indian airports?

The article lists ICAO Annex 14, FAA Advisory Circulars (AC 150/5345 equipment specifications and AC 150/5340 design and maintenance), DGCA requirements (CAR Section 4, aerodrome standards) and AAI technical specifications. These define photometric intensity, beam distribution, colour, installation geometry and light spacing.

Why is photometric verification important?

Every fitting must produce the correct intensity, beam spread, colour output and uniformity. Type photometric testing is done in specialised laboratories with calibrated equipment, and in-service measurement on the airfield confirms that installed lights continue to perform. Without proper photometric validation, a lighting system may appear functional but fail to provide correct visual guidance to pilots.

What is ALCMS and what does it do?

ALCMS (Airfield Lighting Control and Monitoring System) lets operators in the control tower or electrical substation control lighting circuits remotely, adjust brightness through CCR steps, monitor circuit health and failures, and respond quickly to maintenance issues. Integration with SCADA-based systems further improves operational monitoring and reliability.

What maintenance does an AGL system need?

Regular activities include cleaning light fittings and prisms, checking lamp or LED performance, verifying CCR output levels, inspecting cable insulation and connectors, and conducting periodic photometric verification. AGL works continuously in rain, dust, heat and under aircraft loads, so proper maintenance keeps it reliable and compliant.
