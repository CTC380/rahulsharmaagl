CBL-341 Primary Cable Open Circuit

Symptom — An entire series circuit, or a defined section of it, goes dark and the CCR cannot maintain rated output current despite no other apparent circuit fault, consistent with a break in the primary series loop.

Equipment / System

Primary series circuit cable (constant-current loop, typically 6.6A/8.3A/20A) connecting the CCR to the isolation transformers around the circuit.

Possible Causes
HighComplete conductor break at a joint or splice
HighCable severed by civil works/excavation without a proper locate
MedCorroded or failed connector at a joint pit
MedConnector pulled apart by ground movement or settlement
LowRodent or animal damage severing the conductor
LowLightning strike vaporising a section of conductor
Required Tools
CCR output current/voltage logTDR / cable fault locator1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Cable route as-built drawingCable locating (tone/trace) equipment
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before any work.
  • Treat the primary series circuit as energised (up to several kV) until confirmed isolated at the CCR.
  • Coordinate all airside access through ATC/apron control.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCheck the CCR panel: an open primary series circuit typically shows the regulator driving toward maximum voltage while current collapses, tripping an open-circuit alarm.
  2. ObservationDetermine which section of the circuit is affected using downstream fixture status.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, perform loop-by-loop continuity/resistance testing from accessible pull boxes.
  4. MeasurementUse a TDR to estimate the distance to the break from the nearest accessible point.
  5. DecisionBreak located at a joint pit → joint repair. Break mid-run in open cable → locate and repair via excavation.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the specific joint or cable section.
  7. Root CauseConfirm full-loop continuity is restored before re-energising.
Test Points
Loop continuity from the CCR
TDR distance-to-fault reading
Joint pit condition at the estimated fault location
Cable route drawing cross-reference
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-energise and confirm the CCR returns to normal voltage/current regulation with full loop continuity, and megger the repaired section.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the broken conductor or joint.
  • Splice using an approved method rated for the cable type.
  • Restore cable route marking if disturbed during repair.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain accurate as-built cable route drawings.
  • Require a utility locate before any excavation near the circuit.
  • Schedule periodic loop continuity and insulation resistance testing.
Related References

CBL-342 Secondary Cable Open Circuit

Symptom — An individual fixture is dark while the primary series circuit itself measures normal current, pointing to a break in the secondary cable between the isolation transformer and the fixture.

Equipment / System

Secondary cable (single-core, low-voltage) between the isolation transformer secondary and the fixture, including its L-823 connectors.

Possible Causes
HighBroken conductor within the L-823 connector plug or socket
HighSecondary cable severed inside the base can or fixture housing
MedConnector not fully seated, allowing strain to break the conductor over time
MedCorrosion at the connector pin causing an eventual open circuit
LowSecondary cable pinched or severed during fixture or transformer replacement
LowManufacturing defect in the secondary cable
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeterContinuity testerSpare secondary cable/connector kitBase can access tools
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before opening the base can.
  • Confirm the isolation transformer secondary is de-energised before handling, even though it is low voltage.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the primary series circuit current is normal, ruling out an upstream fault.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, check continuity of the secondary cable from the transformer secondary to the fixture.
  3. MeasurementInspect the L-823 connector for a broken or corroded pin.
  4. DecisionOpen found within the connector → replace the connector. Open found in the cable run → replace the secondary cable.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the connector or the cable.
  6. Root CauseConfirm restored continuity end-to-end before re-energising.
Test Points
Secondary cable continuity
L-823 connector pin condition
Transformer secondary output voltage
Root Cause Verification

Re-energise and confirm the fixture illuminates at the correct intensity across all steps.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the broken secondary cable.
  • Replace or reseat the L-823 connector.
Preventive Action
  • Inspect secondary cable and connectors during fixture PM.
  • Stock spare secondary cable assemblies.
  • Properly support cable runs to avoid strain at the connector.
Related References

CBL-343 Cable Short Circuit

Symptom — The CCR trips on overcurrent or shows abnormally low output voltage for a given current setting, consistent with a short circuit somewhere on the series circuit loop.

Equipment / System

Primary or secondary cable insulation between conductors, or between adjacent conductors within a joint.

Possible Causes
HighInsulation breakdown at a joint allowing conductor-to-conductor contact
HighCable damage exposing bare conductors that have come into contact
MedWater-filled joint pit bridging two conductors
MedManufacturing or installation defect in a joint kit
LowRodent damage stripping insulation at a contact point
LowA transformer internal short mimicking a cable short
Required Tools
CCR trip logClamp-on ammeter1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)TDR / fault locatorThermal imaging camera
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before any testing.
  • Treat a shorted circuit as a potential arc-flash/thermal hazard at the fault location.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the CCR trip history for an overcurrent/short-circuit trip pattern.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger conductor-to-conductor and conductor-to-earth.
  3. MeasurementUse a TDR to estimate the distance to the low-impedance fault.
  4. DecisionShort isolated to a specific joint → joint repair. Short in an open cable run → excavate and repair.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the specific joint or cable section.
  6. Root CauseConfirm insulation resistance is restored to an acceptable value.
Test Points
Conductor-to-conductor insulation resistance
TDR distance-to-fault
Joint pit condition at the estimated location
Thermal signature at re-energisation
Root Cause Verification

After repair, megger the full loop, re-energise, and confirm the CCR regulates normally without tripping across a full operating cycle.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the shorted joint or cable section.
  • Drain and reseal an affected joint pit.
Preventive Action
  • Schedule periodic insulation resistance testing.
  • Ensure joint pits drain properly.
  • Use approved joint kits rated for the application.
Related References

CBL-344 Cable Earth Fault

Symptom — CCR earth-fault/ground-fault protection trips, or the earth-fault monitor shows a leakage path to ground, without necessarily a complete loss of circuit current.

Equipment / System

Primary series cable insulation relative to earth/ground, including joints, base cans, and any point exposed to moisture.

Possible Causes
HighMoisture ingress at a joint or base can providing a path to earth
HighCable jacket damage allowing conductor contact with wet ground
MedA failing isolation transformer winding leaking to its earthed core/case
MedA deteriorated cable section nearing end of life with cumulative insulation breakdown
LowA nicked cable from previous excavation now failing under moisture exposure
LowCCR earth-fault protection tripping on a marginal reading rather than a hard fault
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)CCR earth-fault trend logCable route and joint location drawingTDR / fault locatorMoisture detection meter
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the CCR before any insulation testing or cable work.
  • Treat a marginal insulation reading as a developing hazard rather than a nuisance trip.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the CCR earth-fault trend to establish gradual degradation versus a step-change.
  2. MeasurementMegger the primary cable in segments from accessible pull boxes, comparing each to its baseline.
  3. MeasurementIdentify the segment or transformer contributing the greatest drop in insulation resistance.
  4. DecisionDrop isolated to one segment/joint → localised repair. Drop distributed across many segments → treat as cumulative circuit ageing.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific segment, joint, or transformer winding.
  6. Root CauseConfirm with a full-loop re-test that the reading is restored to, or above, the acceptable threshold.
Test Points
Segment-by-segment insulation resistance to earth
Individual transformer winding insulation
CCR earth-fault trip log
Joint/pull box condition at the lowest-reading segment
Root Cause Verification

Re-megger the full loop after repair and monitor the CCR earth-fault trend over subsequent operating cycles.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the identified degraded cable segment or joint.
  • Replace a transformer with the failing winding.
  • Reseal an identified moisture entry point.
Preventive Action
  • Schedule periodic full-loop insulation resistance testing and trend results year over year.
  • Prioritise ageing circuit sections for proactive replacement.
  • Seal known moisture entry points identified during PM.
Related References

Standards: IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

CBL-345 Cable Insulation Failure

Symptom — A cable section fails a routine or triggered insulation resistance test, reading below the acceptable threshold even though the circuit may still be operating without an active trip.

Equipment / System

Primary or secondary cable insulation system across its full length, including conductor jacket, joints, and terminations.

Possible Causes
HighCumulative thermal and moisture cycling ageing the insulation over its service life
HighA specific damaged section that has begun to track under voltage stress
MedA substandard joint kit or installation workmanship at a specific splice
MedChemical exposure (fuel, de-icing fluid) degrading the jacket in a localised area
LowUV degradation of exposed cable sections at above-ground transitions
LowManufacturing defect in a specific cable batch
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)High-potential (hipot) test set where applicableCable route and installation date recordsTDR / fault locator
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before testing.
  • Fully de-energise before hipot testing given the higher test voltages involved.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the test result against the previous baseline and the acceptable threshold for the cable type.
  2. MeasurementSegment the circuit and megger each segment to localise the lowest-reading section.
  3. MeasurementInspect the identified segment for visible jacket damage, joint condition, or chemical exposure.
  4. DecisionFailure localised to a specific defect → targeted repair. Failure consistent across an aged section → plan proactive replacement.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific cable section or joint.
  6. Root CauseConfirm restored insulation resistance after repair.
Test Points
Segment insulation resistance vs. baseline
Joint condition in the lowest-reading segment
Visible jacket condition
Installation/service age record
Root Cause Verification

Re-test the full loop after repair and confirm the reading is restored to, or above, the acceptable threshold.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the identified damaged cable section or joint.
  • Replace a substandard joint kit installation.
Preventive Action
  • Trend insulation resistance results year over year rather than relying on a single reading.
  • Prioritise ageing sections for proactive replacement.
  • Protect exposed cable from chemical and UV exposure.
Related References

SOP: SOP-CBL-05 (Insulation Resistance Test Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

CBL-346 Low Insulation Resistance

Symptom — A routine periodic insulation resistance test on a circuit or segment returns a value below the site's acceptable minimum, without any associated operational fault or trip yet occurring.

Equipment / System

Full primary series circuit loop or a defined test segment, evaluated against the site's insulation resistance acceptance criteria.

Possible Causes
HighGradual moisture ingress across multiple joints along the circuit
HighCumulative ageing of an older circuit section approaching end of service life
MedA single degrading joint or transformer winding pulling down the whole-loop reading
MedTest performed in wet ground conditions temporarily depressing the reading
LowTest equipment calibration or technique error
LowA marginal defect not yet severe enough to cause an operational fault
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)Calibrated test recordsCable route and joint scheduleWeather/ground condition log
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before testing.
  • Verify test equipment calibration before relying on a borderline reading.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompare the low reading against the historical trend for this circuit to rule out a test/technique anomaly.
  2. MeasurementRepeat the test under confirmed isolation and calibrated equipment to validate the reading.
  3. MeasurementSegment the circuit and test each segment to localise the contributing section.
  4. DecisionReading confirmed low and localised → proceed to targeted repair. Reading low but distributed and trending gradually → schedule proactive maintenance rather than emergency repair.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the segment, joint, or transformer contributing most to the reduced reading.
  6. Root CauseConfirm with a repeat full-loop test after any corrective action.
Test Points
Full-loop and segment insulation resistance
Historical trend comparison
Ground moisture conditions at time of test
Test equipment calibration status
Root Cause Verification

Retest after any corrective action and confirm the reading is restored to, or trending toward, the acceptable range, then continue periodic monitoring.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the segment/joint identified as the primary contributor.
  • Reseal a moisture entry point.
  • Recalibrate test equipment if found to be the cause.
Preventive Action
  • Schedule periodic full-loop insulation resistance testing on a defined interval and trend results.
  • Note ground conditions alongside the result, avoiding testing immediately after heavy rain where practical.
Related References

SOP: SOP-CBL-05 (Insulation Resistance Test Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

CBL-347 High Leakage Current

Symptom — The CCR or a dedicated leakage monitoring device reports leakage current above the acceptable threshold on a series circuit, without a full earth-fault trip having occurred.

Equipment / System

Full primary series circuit insulation system, evaluated for cumulative leakage current to earth across all joints, transformers, and cable sections.

Possible Causes
HighCumulative small insulation defects across multiple joints adding up to a measurable leakage total
HighA specific degrading isolation transformer with a partial winding-to-core leakage path
MedSurface contamination/moisture film on above-ground terminations providing a leakage path
MedA single significantly degraded cable section dominating the total leakage reading
LowCCR/monitoring equipment measurement drift or calibration error
LowCapacitive leakage on a very long circuit approaching, but not exceeding, normal expected values
Required Tools
CCR/leakage monitoring trend log1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)Clamp-on leakage current meterCable route and transformer schedule
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before segment testing.
  • Treat elevated leakage as a developing hazard warranting investigation rather than a nuisance reading.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the leakage trend to determine whether it is a step-change or a gradual increase.
  2. MeasurementSegment the circuit and megger each section, plus individually test transformer windings, to localise the dominant contributor.
  3. MeasurementInspect above-ground terminations for surface contamination.
  4. DecisionLeakage isolated to one segment/transformer → targeted repair. Leakage distributed across many segments → treat as cumulative ageing and plan proactive maintenance.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific segment, joint, or transformer contributing most to the leakage.
  6. Root CauseConfirm with a full-loop re-test that leakage is restored to within acceptable limits.
Test Points
Segment-by-segment leakage/insulation reading
Individual transformer winding leakage
Above-ground termination cleanliness
CCR/monitoring equipment calibration
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-test the full loop and confirm leakage current is within the acceptable threshold, then monitor the trend over subsequent operating cycles.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the dominant contributing cable segment or joint.
  • Replace the degrading transformer.
  • Clean contaminated terminations.
Preventive Action
  • Trend leakage current over time rather than relying on a single reading.
  • Include termination cleaning in periodic PM.
  • Calibrate monitoring equipment on a defined schedule.

CBL-348 Cable Jacket Damage

Symptom — Visual inspection or excavation reveals the outer jacket of a cable is cracked, abraded, or otherwise compromised, with or without an accompanying electrical fault yet detected.

Equipment / System

Cable outer jacket/sheath along its buried or exposed route.

Possible Causes
HighMechanical abrasion against conduit edges, rocks, or backfill material during or after installation
HighUV degradation at above-ground or shallow-buried transition points
MedChemical attack from fuel, oil, or de-icing fluid exposure
MedRodent or animal gnawing damage
LowThermal cycling embrittlement of an aged jacket
LowImproper handling or bending during a previous repair or installation
Required Tools
Visual inspection lightJacket thickness gauge where applicable1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Jacket repair kit matched to cable type
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before any repair involving cable handling.
  • Confirm the circuit is de-energised even for jacket-only repairs, given the risk of undetected insulation compromise beneath.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationDocument the location, extent, and apparent cause of the jacket damage.
  2. MeasurementMegger the cable at the damaged location to confirm whether the underlying insulation is still intact.
  3. MeasurementAssess whether the damage is isolated or indicates a wider pattern, such as poor backfill practice along a run.
  4. DecisionInsulation intact beneath the damage → jacket-only repair. Insulation compromised → treat as an insulation failure per CBL-345.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the extent of the damaged section requiring repair or replacement.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the repaired section holds insulation resistance and jacket integrity.
Test Points
Insulation resistance at the damaged location
Jacket condition along the adjacent run
Backfill/conduit condition if damage is installation-related
Root Cause Verification

Re-megger after repair and re-inspect the repaired section after backfilling to confirm jacket integrity is maintained.

Corrective Action
  • Repair the jacket using an approved sleeve/wrap method matched to cable type, or replace the damaged section if repair is not viable.
  • Correct the underlying cause, such as adding conduit protection or removing abrasive backfill.
Preventive Action
  • Use proper bedding/backfill material and conduit protection during installation.
  • Include jacket condition in periodic excavation-triggered inspections.
  • Protect above-ground transitions from UV exposure.
Related References

CBL-349 Cable Mechanical Damage

Symptom — A cable is found with visible mechanical damage beyond jacket-only abrasion, such as a deformed, kinked, or partially severed section, typically discovered during excavation, fault-finding, or a reported incident.

Equipment / System

Cable conductor and insulation at the site of mechanical impact or deformation.

Possible Causes
HighImpact from excavation equipment during nearby civil works
HighVehicle or aircraft wheel loading on a shallow-buried or improperly protected cable
MedA heavy object dropped or dragged across an exposed cable during other maintenance
MedGround movement or subsidence placing mechanical stress on a rigid section
LowInstallation-stage damage that has since begun to manifest as a fault
LowFrost heave or freeze-thaw cycling deforming shallow-buried cable
Required Tools
Visual/physical inspection1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)TDR / fault locatorReplacement cable section matched to specification
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before handling.
  • Treat any mechanically damaged section as a potential live hazard until isolation is confirmed.
  • Use appropriate PPE for excavation work.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationDocument the nature and extent of the mechanical damage and the likely cause.
  2. MeasurementMegger the cable to determine whether the damage has compromised insulation or conductor integrity.
  3. MeasurementUse a TDR to confirm no additional damage exists further along the run from the same event.
  4. DecisionLocalised damage with intact insulation → monitor or preemptively repair. Damage compromising insulation/conductor → full section replacement.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the full extent of the affected section.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the repaired/replaced section meets insulation and continuity requirements.
Test Points
Insulation resistance and continuity at the damage site
TDR trace for additional damage along the run
Conduit/bedding condition at the impact location
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-test insulation resistance and continuity and re-inspect after backfilling to confirm no further deformation.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the mechanically damaged cable section.
  • Restore proper bedding/conduit protection.
  • Splice using an approved method rated for the application.
Preventive Action
  • Enforce utility locate and civil works coordination near buried circuits.
  • Add mechanical protection (conduit, warning tape, marker) over vulnerable sections.
  • Investigate ground movement risk in affected areas.
Related References

CBL-350 Cable Crushing

Symptom — A cable is found flattened or crushed along a section of its length, typically from vehicle, equipment, or heavy load contact, with possible conductor deformation even where the jacket remains intact.

Equipment / System

Cable cross-section at the crushed location, including conductor strands and surrounding insulation.

Possible Causes
HighHeavy vehicle or aircraft wheel loading over a shallow-buried or unprotected cable
HighGround support equipment or construction machinery driven over an exposed or shallow cable run
MedInadequate bedding/backfill allowing point loading to transmit directly to the cable
MedA cable installed without conduit protection at a crossing point that later became a vehicle route
LowSnow plough or grader blade contact on a shallow-buried section
LowStacked materials/equipment left on top of an exposed cable during other work
Required Tools
Visual/physical inspection1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Conductor continuity/resistance test setReplacement cable section
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before handling.
  • Assess whether the crushed section poses an immediate short/earth-fault hazard before any access.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationDocument the location and extent of crushing and identify the likely load source.
  2. MeasurementMegger and continuity-test the crushed section to assess conductor and insulation condition.
  3. MeasurementCheck conductor resistance against a known-good section of the same cable to detect strand damage even where insulation appears intact.
  4. DecisionConductor/insulation compromised → replace the affected section. Conductor/insulation intact but crushed → monitor closely and consider preemptive replacement given fatigue risk.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the full extent of the crushed section.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the replaced/repaired section meets specification.
Test Points
Conductor resistance vs. known-good reference
Insulation resistance at the crushed location
Bedding/protection condition at the site
Root Cause Verification

After replacement, re-test insulation resistance and continuity, and confirm proper bedding/protection is restored before backfilling.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the crushed cable section.
  • Install conduit or additional mechanical protection at the location.
  • Restore proper bedding.
Preventive Action
  • Identify and protect known vehicle crossing points over buried circuits.
  • Restrict unauthorised vehicle access over shallow cable routes.
  • Use load-rated conduit at identified crossing points.
Related References

CBL-351 Cable Cutting

Symptom — A cable is found fully or partially severed, typically discovered as a sudden complete circuit failure coinciding with nearby excavation, trenching, or other ground-disturbing work.

Equipment / System

Cable conductor and insulation at the cut location, and the affected series/secondary circuit as a whole.

Possible Causes
HighExcavation or trenching equipment cutting the cable without a prior utility locate
HighDirectional boring/drilling striking the cable
MedHand-digging or landscaping work severing a shallow cable without checking for buried services
MedCable cut during a previous, undocumented repair and re-buried without an as-built update
LowVandalism or unauthorised cable removal
LowA cable cut during demolition of unrelated infrastructure sharing the same trench
Required Tools
TDR / cable fault locatorCable locating (tone/trace) equipment1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Approved splice kit or replacement cable sectionExcavation equipment
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before any work at the cut location.
  • Treat both ends of a cut cable as potentially live until isolation is confirmed at the source.
  • Coordinate with the party responsible for the excavation to halt further work in the area.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the timing and location of the fault against any known nearby excavation or ground-disturbing activity.
  2. MeasurementUse TDR and cable locating equipment to confirm the exact location and extent of the cut.
  3. MeasurementInspect both cut ends for the extent of conductor and insulation damage.
  4. DecisionClean cut with minimal collateral damage → splice repair. Cut with extensive fraying/damage or contamination → replace the affected section.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the full extent of cable requiring repair or replacement.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the splice/replacement restores full continuity and insulation resistance.
Test Points
TDR distance-to-fault
Continuity and insulation resistance across the repaired section
Condition of both cut ends before splicing
Root Cause Verification

After repair, test the full loop for continuity and insulation resistance, re-energise, and confirm normal operation across an operating cycle.

Corrective Action
  • Splice the cut cable using an approved kit rated for the cable type and burial environment, or replace the damaged section entirely.
  • Update as-built drawings to reflect the repair location.
Preventive Action
  • Enforce mandatory utility locate and permit-to-dig procedures for any excavation near AGL cable routes.
  • Mark and maintain accurate cable route records.
  • Consider additional route marking (tape, tracer wire) where not already present.

CBL-352 Cable Water Ingress

Symptom — Standing water or moisture is found inside a cable joint, pull box, or base can, or is suspected due to an intermittent or gradually degrading insulation reading, without the cable itself necessarily being physically damaged.

Equipment / System

Cable joints, pull boxes, base cans, and any point where the cable enters a below-grade enclosure.

Possible Causes
HighDegraded or missing seal at a joint kit or gland allowing water entry
HighA pull box or base can lid seal failed or left improperly seated after previous access
MedRising groundwater table exceeding the enclosure's drainage capacity
MedBlocked or missing drainage provision at the enclosure
LowA cracked enclosure allowing direct water entry
LowCondensation accumulating within a sealed but poorly ventilated enclosure
Required Tools
Moisture detection meter1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Replacement joint kit/sealsDrain-clearing toolEnclosure lid gasket kit
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before opening any enclosure suspected of water ingress.
  • Treat submerged or wet components as live until insulation resistance is verified.
  • Use appropriate PPE for confined space/wet conditions.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationInspect the affected enclosure at the next scheduled or triggered access for standing water or moisture.
  2. MeasurementMegger the cable/joint before any re-energisation.
  3. MeasurementInspect the joint kit, gland, lid seal, and drainage provision to identify the entry point.
  4. DecisionInsulation resistance acceptable → dry out, reseal, and return to service. Insulation resistance degraded → treat as an insulation failure per CBL-345.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the specific seal, gland, lid, or drainage failure providing the entry point.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the enclosure is fully dry and resealed before closing out.
Test Points
Insulation resistance before re-energisation

  4. DecisionTag is simply incorrect/missing with no wider documentation issue → correct the tag. Discrepancy indicates a wider as-built/schedule error → update the drawing and audit related cables in the same area.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the specific cable(s) affected.
  6. Root CauseConfirm corrected identification matches the verified circuit.
Test Points
Tone-and-trace verified circuit identity
Tag/marker legibility and accuracy
As-built drawing and circuit schedule cross-reference
Root Cause Verification

After correction, re-verify the tag against a tone-and-trace check, and confirm the as-built documentation is updated and consistent.

Corrective Action
  • Apply correct, durable identification tags/markers.
  • Update the as-built drawing and circuit schedule.
Preventive Action
  • Use durable, standardised tagging at every joint, pull box, and termination.
  • Verify and update identification as a mandatory step after any rerouting or repair.
  • Conduct periodic identification audits.

CBL-367 Cable Failure After Rain

Symptom — A cable fault — typically an earth fault, short circuit, or reduced insulation resistance — appears or worsens specifically following a rainfall event, with the circuit having tested acceptable prior to the rain.

Equipment / System

Cable insulation system and joints/enclosures at points vulnerable to rainwater ingress along the affected circuit.

Possible Causes
HighAn existing but marginal jacket or joint defect that only becomes a conductive path once wetted by rainfall
HighA pull box or base can lid seal that allows surface water entry during heavy rain
MedSurface water pooling and infiltrating a previously dry crack or joint gap
MedA cable section already near its insulation resistance threshold, pushed over the limit by added moisture
LowLightning-related transient damage coinciding with a rain event, distinct from moisture ingress itself
LowA temporary, self-clearing condition that resolves once the affected area dries out
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)Moisture detection meterCCR earth-fault trend log correlated with weather dataDrainage/seal inspection tools
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before testing.
  • Treat a rain-triggered fault as indicating a real, if intermittent, hazard rather than a nuisance condition.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the fault onset correlates with a specific rainfall event using CCR trip logs and weather records.
  2. MeasurementMegger the circuit in segments to localise the section most affected.
  3. MeasurementInspect the identified segment's joints, pull boxes, and enclosures for a moisture entry point once accessible.
  4. DecisionMoisture entry point identified and localised → repair per CBL-352 (water ingress) or CBL-353 (moisture ingress) as appropriate. Fault clears on its own with no identifiable entry point → monitor closely and re-test after the next rain event.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific segment, joint, or enclosure responsible.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the fault does not recur after a subsequent rainfall event of similar or greater intensity.
Test Points
Segment insulation resistance correlated with weather timing
Joint/enclosure seal condition at the identified location
CCR earth-fault trend vs. rainfall record
Root Cause Verification

After repair, monitor the circuit through at least one subsequent significant rainfall event and confirm the insulation resistance remains stable.

Corrective Action
  • Reseal or repair the identified moisture entry point.
  • Repair or replace a degraded cable/jacket section that only fails under wet conditions.
Preventive Action
  • Correlate periodic insulation resistance testing with weather conditions to catch rain-sensitive defects proactively.
  • Prioritise seal and drainage inspection ahead of the wet season.
  • Trend circuits with a history of rain-related faults for closer monitoring.
Related References

SOP: SOP-CBL-11 (Enclosure Moisture Inspection Procedure), SOP-CBL-04 (Earth Fault Location Procedure)

CBL-368 Cable Failure After Flooding

Symptom — A cable fault occurs, or a previously marginal circuit fails outright, following a flooding event where an area of the airfield or a specific enclosure was submerged well beyond normal wet-weather conditions.

Equipment / System

Cable, joints, pull boxes, base cans, and CCR/transformer enclosures within the flooded area.

Possible Causes
HighProlonged submersion of joints/enclosures exceeding their design ingress protection rating
HighFloodwater carrying contaminants (silt, chemicals, saltwater) that accelerate insulation degradation beyond simple moisture exposure
MedHydrostatic pressure during flooding forcing water past seals that would otherwise resist normal rainfall
MedEquipment (transformers, CCR components) submerged beyond their rated exposure, causing internal damage in addition to cable-level effects
LowDebris carried by floodwater causing physical damage to exposed cable or enclosures
LowA temporary condition where components were submerged but not damaged, requiring drying and verification rather than replacement
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)Moisture detection meterThorough visual inspection of all affected enclosuresWater/contamination sampling where saltwater or chemical contamination is suspected
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO all affected circuits before any inspection.
  • Treat all submerged equipment as unsafe to energise until fully assessed.
  • Be alert to additional hazards — silt, debris, wildlife — in flood-affected enclosures.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationIdentify and map the full extent of the flooded area and all potentially affected enclosures and cable sections.
  2. MeasurementMegger every affected segment and piece of equipment before considering re-energisation, prioritising the most critical circuits.
  3. MeasurementInspect for contamination (silt, chemical residue, saltwater) in addition to simple moisture.
  4. DecisionInsulation resistance acceptable after thorough drying → return to service with continued monitoring. Insulation resistance remains degraded or contamination is present → treat as insulation failure per CBL-345 and plan targeted or wholesale replacement.
  5. Fault LocationMap the full extent of flood-affected cable and equipment requiring assessment.
  6. Root CauseConfirm every affected component is individually verified before restoring the circuit.
Test Points
Insulation resistance at every affected segment and enclosure
Contamination assessment (silt/chemical/saltwater)
Equipment condition (transformers, CCR components) in flood-affected areas
Root Cause Verification

After drying, cleaning, and repair, re-megger all affected sections, re-energise progressively, and monitor closely over the following days for any delayed degradation.

Corrective Action
  • Thoroughly dry and clean all affected joints, enclosures, and equipment.
  • Replace cable sections and components where contamination or submersion has exceeded their rating.
  • Reseal and restore all affected enclosures.
Preventive Action
  • Review drainage and flood risk for low-lying enclosures and circuits.
  • Consider upgrading ingress protection ratings for equipment in known flood-prone areas.
  • Develop a flood response procedure prioritising critical circuit assessment.
Related References

CBL-369 Repeated Cable Fault

Symptom — The same cable section, joint, or circuit experiences a fault, of the same or varying type, on multiple separate occasions despite apparently successful repairs each time, indicating an unresolved underlying cause.

Equipment / System

The specific cable section, joint, or circuit with a documented history of repeat faults, and the repair method(s) previously applied.

Possible Causes
HighA repair method inadequate for the actual root cause, such as repeatedly repairing a joint without addressing an underlying water table or ground movement issue
HighThe true root cause of the original fault never correctly identified or fully corrected
MedThe repaired section subjected to the same damaging condition that caused the original fault, e.g. the same vehicle crossing point
MedA systemic issue affecting the whole circuit (cumulative ageing) manifesting as sequential, seemingly unrelated faults
LowInconsistent repair quality/method used across successive repairs
LowA monitoring or reporting gap meaning some 'repeat' faults are actually separate, unrelated causes coincidentally in the same area
Required Tools
Fault history log/maintenance record for the circuit1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)TDR / fault locatorRoot cause analysis framework
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before any investigation.
  • Treat a location with a repeat-fault history with heightened caution given the demonstrated pattern of failure.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompile the full fault history for the location, including dates, fault types, and repair methods previously used.
  2. MeasurementRe-test the current condition of the repaired section and surrounding area to establish the present state.
  3. MeasurementInvestigate site-specific conditions — traffic loading, groundwater, ground movement — that may be a recurring contributing factor rather than the cable/joint itself.
  4. DecisionPattern points to an inadequate repair method → upgrade to a more robust repair/protection approach. Pattern points to a persistent site condition → address the underlying condition in addition to the cable repair.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm whether the repeat faults are genuinely at the same point or several distinct nearby points.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the underlying cause, not just the immediate fault, has been addressed.
Test Points
Fault history log review
Current insulation resistance/continuity at the location
Site condition assessment (traffic, groundwater, ground movement) at the repeat-fault location
Root Cause Verification

Monitor the location over an extended period, and multiple wet/dry and loading cycles, to confirm the fault does not recur following the upgraded repair.

Corrective Action
  • Upgrade the repair method — a more robust joint kit, additional mechanical protection, or rerouting away from a damaging condition.
  • Address the underlying site condition contributing to repeat failure.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a fault history log by location to identify repeat-fault patterns early.
  • Escalate repeat faults at the same location for root cause analysis rather than routine repair after the second occurrence.
  • Review repair standards where a pattern of inadequate repairs is identified.

CBL-370 Hidden Underground Cable Fault

Symptom — A fault is confirmed to exist somewhere on a buried circuit via electrical testing, but its exact physical location cannot be readily determined from surface indications, requiring a structured fault-location process before any excavation.

Equipment / System

The complete buried cable route for the affected circuit, evaluated using non-invasive fault-location techniques before physical excavation.

Possible Causes
HighA fault located in a section of the route with no accessible pull boxes or joints nearby, requiring surface-based location techniques
HighA fault whose electrical signature, such as a high-resistance rather than a clean short, makes standard TDR distance estimation less precise
MedInconsistent or outdated as-built route information hampering location accuracy
MedEnvironmental factors (soil composition, buried objects, adjacent services) interfering with locating equipment
LowMultiple co-existing faults on the same circuit complicating the location process
LowA fault located under pavement, requiring precise location to minimise excavation extent
Required Tools
TDR / cable fault locatorARC reflection or surge/thumper fault location method where availableAcoustic/electromagnetic ground-tracing equipmentAs-built drawingPinpointing survey markers
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before any fault-location testing.
  • Coordinate airside/pavement access before any excavation is planned.
  • Ensure any surge/thumper testing is performed by qualified personnel given the higher test voltages involved.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the fault type (open, short, earth fault) and general section of the circuit via segment testing from accessible points.
  2. MeasurementUse TDR to obtain an initial distance-to-fault estimate from the nearest accessible point in each direction.
  3. MeasurementApply a secondary locating method, such as surge/thumper with acoustic/electromagnetic tracing or ARC reflection, to pinpoint the fault location on the surface.
  4. DecisionFault location narrowed to a confident pinpoint → proceed with minimal, targeted excavation. Location remains uncertain after available methods → consider a wider excavation window or engage specialist fault-location services.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the pinpointed location before committing to excavation, particularly under pavement.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the fault is fully resolved once excavated and repaired, and that the electrical signature matches expectations.
Test Points
TDR distance-to-fault from both directions
Pinpointed surface location from secondary locating method
As-built route accuracy at the estimated location
Root Cause Verification

After excavation and repair, test the full loop for continuity and insulation resistance, and confirm the excavated location matches the electrical signature that was present before repair.

Corrective Action
  • Excavate at the pinpointed location and repair per the applicable fault type.
  • Minimise excavation extent, particularly under pavement, through accurate pinpointing.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain accurate, updated as-built route drawings to support future fault location.
  • Consider installing tracer wire or route markers on new/replacement cable runs to aid future location.
  • Invest in or retain access to specialist fault-location equipment/services for circuits without ready pull-box access.
Related References

SOP: SOP-CBL-01 (Primary Series Circuit Fault Location Procedure), SOP-CBL-25 (Precision Fault Location Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5340-30, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I
