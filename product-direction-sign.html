Product Overview

Airfield Direction Sign

What It Is

Direction signs give clear visual routing information to pilots and vehicle operators at taxiway intersections and other movement locations, identifying available taxiway routes, runway connections, apron routes and approved destinations.

They are distinct from location signs (current position) and mandatory instruction signs (restrictions or holding requirements). Configuration follows the approved signage plan, taxiway geometry, viewing direction, standard and project electrical requirements.

Configurations

  • Single- or multi-direction
  • Left-turn, right-turn, straight-ahead or combined arrows
  • Internally illuminated (LED or other approved source) or retroreflective where permitted
  • Series-circuit (constant-current) or parallel (constant-voltage) powered
  • Single- or multi-face where required

Final configuration is based on the approved movement-area signage layout.

Applications

Where Direction Signs Are Used

Taxiway intersectionsIndicate the available routes from the intersection.
Runway connectionsRoute toward taxiways, aprons or other areas.
Apron routingTerminal, stand, remote-stand, maintenance and cargo routes.
Complex networksHelp pilots select the intended route.

At runway interfaces, coordinate with runway holding position, mandatory instruction sign, runway designation, taxiway centreline and lighting.

Direction vs Other Signs

Configure a direction sign for the intended route, not the aircraft's current location. Under ICAO Annex 14, direction, location and destination signs are all classed as information signs; mandatory instruction signs are a separate category.

Arrows & Legend

Directional Arrows and Legend Configuration

Directional Arrows

  • Left-turn and right-turn arrows
  • Straight-ahead indication
  • Multiple or combined arrows

Arrow orientation must correspond exactly to the physical route. An incorrect arrow gives misleading operational information; verify every arrow against the approved taxiway layout before manufacture.

Arrow angle should represent the real turn geometry. Where several arrows share one sign, group them by turn direction and, where a location sign is combined with them, separate the panels with black vertical delineators. Confirm panel order against ICAO Annex 14 Chapter 5 and FAA AC 150/5340-18H.

Legend

Normally identifies the taxiway, runway or destination for the route:

  • Taxiway and runway designations
  • Apron, terminal-area and stand destinations
  • Other approved destinations

Take the exact legend from the approved signage schedule.

Multiple-Direction Signs

Several Routes, One Assembly

Possible Designs

  • Two- and three-direction configurations
  • Multiple taxiway destinations
  • Separate arrows for each route

Requirement

The arrangement must remain visually clear and conform to the applicable sign-design standard.

Sign Colour

Black on Yellow

ICAO and FAA Signage

Direction signs use a black inscription on a yellow background under both ICAO Annex 14 and FAA rules (FAA type L-858Y), distinguishing routing information from other signs. Destination and other information signs use the same colours.

Contrast With

  • Location signs: yellow on black
  • Mandatory instruction signs: white on red

Colour must meet the chromaticity and luminance limits of the applicable standard; confirm colour, border and optical characteristics against it.

ICAO & FAA Applications

Standard-Specific Approach

ICAO

Direction signs are part of the movement-area visual guidance system, giving route information at taxiway intersections. Coordinate with:

  • Taxiway designations, location and mandatory instruction signs
  • Pavement markings, taxiway lighting
  • Runway-holding positions

The approved aerodrome signage layout determines legend, arrows and position.

FAA (L-858)

Direction signs are part of the FAA runway and taxiway sign system. The current L-858 specification determines:

  • Classification, size, style
  • Legend, arrow configuration, character height
  • Colour, illumination, electrical interface
  • Mounting and environmental performance

Select as part of the complete airport signage system, not as an isolated product.

Illumination & LED

Contrast and Uniformity

Options

  • Internally illuminated
  • LED illuminated
  • Series-circuit (constant-current) or parallel (constant-voltage) powered
  • Retroreflective where permitted
  • Other approved project-specific configurations

Provide sufficient contrast and uniformity for night and reduced-light operations. Annex 14 requires illuminated signs in runway visual range below 800 m, at night with instrument runways, and at night with non-instrument runways of code number 3 or 4; otherwise a retroreflective sign may be acceptable. Confirm the requirement with the airport authority. Sign luminance should follow the airfield brightness steps.

LED Direction Sign

  • Long life, reduced lamp replacement
  • Lower maintenance, efficient operation
  • Stable, consistent optical output
  • Compatible with modern airfield lighting

LED construction must still meet the complete sign's optical, electrical and environmental requirements.

Electrical Configuration

Series Circuit, Parallel Supply or LED Driver

Series Circuit

Connected to the constant-current circuit through an isolating transformer; brightness follows the CCR steps.

Parallel (Constant-Voltage)

Supplied from a constant-voltage circuit where the airport uses parallel sign circuits.

LED Driver

Dedicated driver or power-conversion arrangement.

CCR Compatibility

  • Circuit current, transformer rating
  • Sign load and number of signs
  • Circuit configuration
  • Brightness steps and sign luminance at each step
  • Monitoring

Principle

Match the sign to the complete airfield lighting circuit and confirm the configuration during technical selection. Never open an energised series circuit: isolate and lock off the CCR first.

Mounting & Frangibility

Support Arrangement

Typical Components

  • Frangible support posts, support channels
  • Mounting brackets, base plates, anchor hardware
  • Frangible couplings, cable-entry provisions

Signs on the movement area must be frangible and low enough to clear propellers and engine pods, while keeping the required position, visibility and edge clearance.

Frangibility

  • Frangible posts, breakaway couplings
  • Lightweight support assemblies
  • Controlled failure interfaces

Evaluate frangibility for the complete sign/support assembly.

Installation Considerations

Position, Orientation and Visibility

Position

Locate at the approved position relative to the intersection, giving pilots sufficient time to identify and interpret the routing information.

Orientation

Face the intended aircraft approach direction. Traffic approaching from multiple directions may need multiple faces or assemblies.

Visibility

  • Reduced visibility, rain, contamination

Consider potential visual obstructions during site installation.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures

FAQ

Airfield Direction Sign — Frequently Asked Questions

What is an airfield direction sign?

A direction sign gives clear visual routing information to pilots and vehicle operators at taxiway intersections and other movement locations. It identifies the available taxiway routes, runway connections, apron routes and approved destinations. Its configuration follows the approved signage plan, taxiway geometry, viewing direction, the applicable standard and the project electrical requirements.

How does a direction sign differ from a location sign and a mandatory instruction sign?

A direction sign indicates a route or destination, normally a taxiway or destination designation with an arrow. A location sign identifies the current position, and a mandatory instruction sign communicates a restriction such as a runway holding position or NO ENTRY. A direction sign is configured for the intended route, not for the aircraft’s current location. Under ICAO Annex 14, direction, location and destination signs are all information signs, while mandatory instruction signs are a separate category.

What colour is a direction sign?

Direction signs use a black inscription on a yellow background under both ICAO Annex 14 and FAA rules, where the FAA type is L-858Y. Location signs are yellow on black and mandatory instruction signs are white on red. The colour must meet the chromaticity and luminance limits of the applicable standard, so confirm colour, border and optical characteristics against it.

Which standards apply to direction signs?

References include ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I (Chapter 5 on signs, with Appendices 1 and 4), ICAO Doc 9157 Part 4, FAA AC 150/5345-44L for runway and taxiway signs, FAA AC 150/5340-18H for sign systems, DGCA India CAR Section 4, and the airport’s own specifications and approved signage plans. For FAA installations the current L-858 specification determines classification, size, style, legend, arrow configuration, character height, colour, illumination, electrical interface, mounting and environmental performance. Verify the applicable edition and any amendments before procurement.

How are arrows and multi-direction signs arranged?

Arrow orientation must correspond exactly to the physical route, and every arrow should be checked against the approved taxiway layout before manufacture. The arrow angle should represent the real turn geometry. Where several arrows share one sign, group them by turn direction and, where a location sign is combined with them, separate the panels with black vertical delineators. Confirm the panel order against ICAO Annex 14 Chapter 5 and FAA AC 150/5340-18H. Two- and three-direction signs are possible as long as the arrangement stays visually clear and conforms to the sign-design standard.

Does a direction sign have to be illuminated?

Annex 14 requires illuminated signs where runway visual range is below 800 m, at night with instrument runways, and at night with non-instrument runways of code number 3 or 4. Otherwise a retroreflective sign may be acceptable, so confirm the requirement with the airport authority. Sign luminance should follow the airfield brightness steps. An LED construction gives long life and lower maintenance, but it must still meet the complete sign’s optical, electrical and environmental requirements.

How is a direction sign powered?

A direction sign is either connected to a series (constant-current) circuit through an isolating transformer, with brightness following the CCR steps, or supplied from a constant-voltage circuit where the airport uses parallel sign circuits. A dedicated LED driver or power-conversion arrangement may also be used. Match the sign to the complete circuit by checking circuit current, transformer rating, the sign load and number of signs, the brightness steps and sign luminance at each step, and any monitoring.

How are character height and sign size decided?

Character height depends on the applicable standard and sign type, the viewing distance, taxiway geometry, cockpit viewing conditions, the operating environment and the approved signage schedule. It should not be selected solely from available sign dimensions. Overall dimensions in turn depend on character height and count, the number of directional panels, the arrow configuration, the legend arrangement, border requirements, sign type and illumination arrangement.

How is a direction sign mounted, and why does it need to be frangible?

Typical components are frangible support posts, support channels, mounting brackets, base plates, anchor hardware, frangible couplings and cable-entry provisions. Signs on the movement area must be frangible and low enough to clear propellers and engine pods, while keeping the required position, visibility and edge clearance. Frangibility should be evaluated for the complete sign and support assembly. The sign should face the intended aircraft approach direction, and traffic approaching from several directions may need multiple faces or assemblies.

What are the common problems with direction signs?

The main problems are a wrong arrow direction (manufacturing error, incorrect installation, changed geometry or an outdated drawing), an incorrect taxiway designation (renaming, airport expansion or an outdated schedule), poor night visibility (LED failure, reduced luminance, a dirty face, optical deterioration or a supply problem) and an obstructed sign (new structures, vegetation, lighting equipment, parked aircraft or vehicles). Verify every arrow against the current approved layout before installation and confirm the legend against the latest approved signage plan.

What should be provided when requesting a quotation for a direction sign?

Provide the airport or project, governing standard, installation location, taxiway intersection, exact legend, arrow directions, character height, quantity and sign dimensions if specified. Also state whether the sign is illuminated or retroreflective, any LED requirement, the electrical supply and series-circuit requirements, the mounting arrangement, frangible requirements, environmental requirements and the documentation needed. An approved airport signage plan or sign-face drawing is strongly recommended for multi-direction signs.

Is it safe to work on a direction sign while the circuit is energised?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage, and opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death. De-energise the circuit at the regulator, apply the site lock-out / tag-out procedure, use the series circuit cut-out switch or an approved isolation device and work under the airport’s permit-to-work system. See the electrical safety warning on this page.
