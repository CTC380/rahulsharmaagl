PHM-451 Low Light Intensity

Symptom — A fixture measures below the minimum specified luminous intensity (candela) at the design aiming angle during scheduled or acceptance photometric testing, even though the fixture illuminates and appears visually normal.

Equipment / System

Runway/taxiway edge, centreline, or approach light fixture undergoing field or bench photometric verification against ICAO Annex 14 Attachment A / FAA AC 150/5340-30 isocandela curves.

Possible Causes
HighLamp/LED module degradation (lumen depreciation) below rated output, especially in aged incandescent or early-generation LED fixtures
HighUndervoltage at the fixture from a low CCR step setting, high circuit resistance, or transformer tap mismatch
MediumLens/prism contamination (dust, dirt, insect residue, oxidation) attenuating output
MediumPartial beam misalignment shifting the peak intensity away from the measurement angle rather than a true output loss
LowInternal reflector degradation, discolouration or delamination
LowLED driver operating in a reduced current/dimming mode due to a configuration error
Required Tools
Calibrated field photometer/luminance meterTrue-RMS clamp ammeterDigital inclinometerLens cleaning kitReference fixture or NIST-traceable calibration source
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate runway/taxiway closure or NOTAM before conducting field photometry that requires close approach to in-pavement fixtures.
  • Use appropriate PPE and reflective/lighted equipment for night runway measurements.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the fixture is otherwise operating normally (no flicker, correct colour) and re-verify the isocandela specification and measurement angle applicable to this fixture type and category.
  2. MeasurementRecord circuit current/voltage at the fixture during the test to rule out an undervoltage condition before attributing the result to the fixture itself.
  3. ObservationInspect and clean the lens/prism, then re-measure to rule out contamination as the cause.
  4. MeasurementRe-check beam alignment (aiming angle) against the design orientation, since a misaimed fixture can show low intensity at the test angle while still producing full total output.
  5. DecisionLow intensity persists after cleaning and with correct circuit voltage and alignment → lamp/LED module or driver degradation. Intensity recovers after cleaning/alignment correction → contamination or misalignment was the root cause.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to lamp/LED module, driver/transformer, alignment, or lens condition.
  7. Root CauseBench-test the removed lamp/LED module against a reference standard to confirm true output degradation.
Test Points
Luminous intensity at 0°/specified test angle
Fixture supply voltage/current
Lens transmittance (visual/cleaned comparison)
Aiming angle vs. design
Root Cause Verification

After correction (module replacement, cleaning, or realignment), re-measure intensity at the full set of specified angles and confirm results meet or exceed the ICAO/FAA minimum curve, then log values into the fixture’s photometric history.

Corrective Action
  • Replace a degraded lamp/LED module.
  • Correct an undervoltage condition at the CCR/transformer.
  • Clean or replace a degraded lens/prism.
Preventive Action
  • Trend photometric readings per fixture over successive maintenance cycles to catch gradual degradation before it drops below minimum.
  • Include lens cleaning in the routine PM schedule.
  • Verify CCR output calibration periodically.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PHM-01 (Field Photometric Test Procedure), SOP-PHM-04 (Lamp/LED Module Replacement Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A, FAA AC 150/5340-30 (Design and Installation of Airport Visual Aids), FAA AC 150/5345-46 (Specification for Runway and Taxiway Light Fixtures)

PHM-452 Excessive Light Intensity

Symptom — A fixture measures luminous intensity meaningfully above the specified maximum (or noticeably above sibling fixtures on the same circuit), producing glare complaints from pilots or unexpectedly high photometric readings during verification.

Equipment / System

Approach, runway edge, or PAPI/VASI-adjacent AGL fixture under photometric test.

Possible Causes
HighFixture set to an incorrect (higher) intensity step in a multi-step CCR or ALCMS configuration
HighIncorrect lamp/LED module wattage or a higher-output replacement fitted in error
MediumOvervoltage at the fixture from CCR calibration drift or a wrong transformer tap
MediumReflector or lens replaced with a non-matching optical assembly increasing peak candela
LowPhotometer calibration drift reading high (cross-reference PHM-460)
Required Tools
Calibrated field photometerTrue-RMS multimeterCCR step/output verification toolFixture nameplate/spec referenceReplacement correctly-rated module
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate runway/taxiway access and NOTAM as required.
  • Treat the circuit as live during observation unless it is isolated for module work, even if the fixture is confirmed over-intensity.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the ALCMS/CCR commanded intensity step matches the step under which the fixture is being tested.
  2. MeasurementRecord fixture supply voltage/current and compare to the nameplate rating for the commanded step.
  3. ObservationVerify the fitted lamp/LED module part number and optical assembly against the fixture’s design specification.
  4. DecisionVoltage/current within spec and correct module fitted → verify photometer calibration (cross-reference PHM-460) before concluding a true fixture fault. Wrong module or overvoltage confirmed → fixture-level fault.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to CCR step/calibration, wrong module, or optical mismatch.
  6. Root CauseConfirm by substituting a known-correct module or retesting after CCR calibration correction.
Test Points
Luminous intensity at test angle
Fixture supply voltage
Module part number vs. spec
CCR commanded step vs. actual output
Root Cause Verification

After correcting the module, CCR step, or calibration, re-measure and confirm intensity falls within the specified range across all commanded steps.

Corrective Action
  • Fit the correct-rated lamp/LED module.
  • Recalibrate CCR output.
  • Correct ALCMS step assignment.
  • Replace a mismatched optical assembly with the design-specified part.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain strict part-number control for lamp/LED modules and optical assemblies during maintenance.
  • Verify ALCMS step-to-fixture mapping after any configuration change.
  • Include CCR output calibration in the periodic PM programme.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PHM-01, SOP-CCR-06 (CCR Output Calibration Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A, FAA AC 150/5340-30

PHM-453 Uneven Light Intensity

Symptom — Fixtures along the same run or circuit show significant variation in measured intensity between adjacent lights, even though each individually may pass or be near the specified minimum, producing a visually inconsistent runway/taxiway edge line.

Equipment / System

A run/series of runway or taxiway edge, centreline, or approach lights on the same circuit or design section.

Possible Causes
HighMixed lamp/LED module ages or part numbers across the run from piecemeal replacement over time
HighVoltage drop variation along a long series or parallel circuit affecting fixtures differently by position
MediumInconsistent lens/prism cleaning or contamination levels across fixtures
MediumIndividual fixtures with minor alignment variance
LowMixed manufacturer batches with slightly different photometric output
Required Tools
Calibrated field photometerTrue-RMS clamp ammeterCircuit layout drawingFixture maintenance/replacement history log
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate extended runway/taxiway occupancy for a multi-fixture survey.
  • Use appropriate lighting and PPE for night measurement runs.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConduct a sequential intensity survey across the full run and plot results by fixture position to identify a pattern (gradual, isolated, or grouped variance).
  2. MeasurementRecord voltage at several points along the circuit to check for position-dependent drop.
  3. ObservationCross-reference each fixture’s maintenance history for module replacement date/part number.
  4. DecisionVariance correlates with circuit position → voltage drop/circuit design issue. Variance correlates with replacement history → mixed module ages/part numbers. Random variance → cleaning/alignment inconsistency.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to circuit voltage profile, module consistency, or fixture-level maintenance quality.
  6. Root CauseConfirm by standardising module part numbers or correcting circuit voltage profile and re-surveying.
Test Points
Intensity per fixture position along the run
Voltage at multiple circuit points
Module part number/age per fixture
Lens condition per fixture
Root Cause Verification

After standardising modules and/or correcting circuit voltage, re-survey the full run and confirm intensity variance falls within an acceptable band across all fixtures.

Corrective Action
  • Standardise lamp/LED module part numbers across the run.
  • Correct circuit design/voltage drop where applicable.
  • Bring cleaning and alignment to a consistent standard across all fixtures.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a single approved part number per fixture type/circuit for replacements.
  • Include full-run photometric surveys (not just spot checks) in the periodic PM programme.
  • Track module installation dates for planned batch replacement.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PHM-02 (Circuit Photometric Survey Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A, FAA AC 150/5340-30

PHM-454 Beam Misalignment

Symptom — Photometric testing shows the peak luminous intensity occurring at an angle offset from the design aiming point, even though total light output may be within specification.

Equipment / System

Any directional AGL fixture (runway/taxiway edge, approach, PAPI) with a defined design aiming angle (elevation and azimuth).

Possible Causes
HighFixture physically bumped or shifted by vehicle contact, snow removal equipment, or mowing operations
HighIncorrect aiming during a prior installation or maintenance visit
MediumMounting base or stake loosened, allowing gradual drift over time from wind/vibration
MediumFrost heave or ground settlement shifting an in-pavement or semi-flush fixture base
LowIncorrect aiming template or reference used during original installation
Required Tools
Digital inclinometer/aiming toolAzimuth reference (compass or runway centreline reference line)Calibrated field photometerTorque wrench for mounting hardwareAiming template per fixture type
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate runway/taxiway closure for aiming correction work requiring access to in-pavement fixtures.
  • Secure the work area from vehicle/aircraft movement during adjustment.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationMeasure luminous intensity across a spread of angles around the design aiming point to map the actual peak location.
  2. MeasurementRecord the fixture’s current elevation and azimuth angle using the aiming tool and compare to the design specification.
  3. ObservationInspect the mounting base/stake for looseness, damage, or signs of impact.
  4. DecisionMeasured peak angle offset matches a physical aiming discrepancy → realign fixture. Base loose/damaged → repair mounting before realigning, since realignment will not hold on an unstable base.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to aiming error alone versus a mounting/foundation issue causing drift.
  6. Root CauseConfirm by re-aiming to design specification and verifying the peak intensity now occurs at the correct angle.
Test Points
Peak intensity angle vs. design aiming angle
Elevation angle
Azimuth angle
Mounting base condition/torque
Root Cause Verification

After realignment (and mounting repair if applicable), re-survey the beam pattern and confirm the peak intensity occurs within the allowable angular tolerance of the design aiming point.

Corrective Action
  • Re-aim the fixture to the design elevation/azimuth using the correct template/tool.
  • Repair or replace a loose or damaged mounting base.
  • Re-torque mounting hardware to specification.
Preventive Action
  • Include aiming verification in the periodic PM programme, particularly after any known vehicle contact or ground disturbance near the fixture.
  • Use witness marks or aiming templates during installation to speed future verification.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PHM-03 (Fixture Aiming and Alignment Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A, FAA AC 150/5340-30

PHM-455 Beam Spread Incorrect

Symptom — The measured beam width (horizontal and/or vertical spread) is narrower or wider than the specification for the fixture type, even when peak intensity and aiming angle are correct.

Equipment / System

Directional AGL fixture with a defined isocandela beam-spread envelope (e.g., elliptical or circular beam optics).

Possible Causes
HighIncorrect lens or reflector/optic assembly fitted, not matching the fixture’s design beam-spread class
HighLens cracked, chipped, or partially obstructed, distorting the beam pattern
MediumLamp/LED module not correctly seated/centred in the optical assembly (off the focal point)
MediumInternal reflector deformed from heat damage or physical impact
LowManufacturing tolerance issue with the specific optical assembly
Required Tools
Calibrated field photometer with angular scan capabilityInspection light/borescopeReplacement optic assembly correctly rated for the fixtureLamp/LED seating gauge
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the fixture before any lens/optic disassembly.
  • Handle glass/plastic lens components carefully to avoid injury from sharp edges.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationPerform an angular intensity scan across both horizontal and vertical planes and compare the resulting envelope to the specified isocandela beam-spread curve.
  2. ObservationVisually inspect the lens/reflector for damage, cracking, or an incorrect part number against the fixture’s design specification.
  3. MeasurementVerify the lamp/LED module is correctly seated at the designed focal point within the optical assembly.
  4. DecisionWrong or damaged optic confirmed → replace with correct-spec assembly. Optic correct but module miscentred → reseat/adjust module.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to optic assembly, lens damage, or module seating.
  6. Root CauseConfirm by re-scanning the beam pattern after correction and comparing against the specified envelope.
Test Points
Horizontal beam spread angle
Vertical beam spread angle
Lens/reflector condition and part number
Module seating position
Root Cause Verification

After replacing the optic or correcting module seating, re-scan the beam pattern and confirm the horizontal and vertical spread fall within the specified isocandela envelope.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the lens/reflector with the correct-spec optical assembly.
  • Reseat or replace the lamp/LED module at the correct focal position.
  • Replace a damaged or cracked lens.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain strict part-number control for optical assemblies by fixture type.
  • Inspect lenses for damage during every routine visit.
  • Verify module seating whenever a module is replaced.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PHM-05 (Optical Assembly Inspection and Replacement Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A, FAA AC 150/5345-46

PHM-456 Incorrect Photometric Distribution

Symptom — The overall isocandela pattern (shape of the intensity distribution across angles) does not match the specified curve for the fixture category, even though peak intensity, aiming, and beam spread individually may appear close to normal.

Equipment / System

Any AGL fixture undergoing a full photometric distribution verification against its ICAO/FAA category curve.

Possible Causes
HighWrong fixture category/model installed for the application (e.g., a taxiway-rated fixture in a runway-rated location)
HighCombination of multiple minor faults (slight misalignment, minor optic damage, module miscentring) compounding into an overall distribution error
MediumNon-approved or counterfeit replacement parts with a different photometric profile
LowFixture design revision mismatch (older fixture generation with a superseded photometric curve)
Required Tools
Calibrated field photometer with full angular scan capabilityFixture category/model reference documentationIsocandela curve overlay tool or software
Safety Precautions
  • Standard fixture-access safety precautions apply; no unique hazard beyond routine photometric survey work.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the installed fixture model/category against the design drawing for that location.
  2. MeasurementPerform a full angular scan and overlay the resulting distribution against the specified isocandela curve to identify where and how it deviates.
  3. ObservationCross-reference the deviation pattern against other single-fault topics (alignment, beam spread, intensity) to determine whether one dominant fault explains the distribution error.
  4. DecisionWrong fixture category installed → replace with correct model. Distribution deviation explained by a combination of minor faults → resolve each contributing fault individually and re-scan.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to fixture category mismatch versus a compounded set of individually minor faults.
  6. Root CauseConfirm by re-scanning after correction and comparing the full distribution to the specified curve.
Test Points
Full angular intensity scan (horizontal and vertical)
Fixture model/category vs. design drawing
Deviation pattern vs. specified isocandela curve
Contributing single-parameter faults
Root Cause Verification

After replacing the fixture or correcting the contributing faults, re-scan the full distribution and confirm it matches the specified isocandela curve within tolerance across all angles.

Corrective Action
  • Replace an incorrect fixture category/model with the design-specified unit.
  • Resolve each contributing minor fault (alignment, optics, module) individually.
Preventive Action
  • Verify fixture model/category against design drawings during installation and at every major maintenance visit.
  • Use only approved, traceable replacement parts.
  • Retain isocandela reference curves for every fixture category in use.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PHM-01, SOP-PHM-06 (Full Distribution Verification Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A, FAA AC 150/5340-30

PHM-457 Incorrect Chromaticity

Symptom — The fixture’s measured chromaticity coordinates fall outside the specified CIE colour box for its designated colour (e.g., white, red, yellow/amber, blue, green), even though the light appears approximately the correct colour to the eye.

Equipment / System

Any colored AGL fixture undergoing chromaticity verification per ICAO Annex 14 Attachment A colour boxes.

Possible Causes
HighLens or filter degradation/fading from long-term UV exposure shifting the transmitted colour
HighIncorrect lens/filter colour fitted (close but not exact match) during a prior replacement
MediumLED module colour shift from thermal ageing or driver current drift (especially phosphor-converted white LEDs)
MediumLens contamination (yellowing film, residue) subtly shifting apparent colour
LowPhotometer/colorimeter calibration drift (cross-reference PHM-460)
Required Tools
Calibrated field colorimeter/spectroradiometerCIE 1931 colour box reference chart for the fixture’s designated colourReplacement lens/filter of correct specification
Safety Precautions
  • Standard fixture-access precautions; no unique hazard.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the fixture’s designated colour per the design drawing and the corresponding ICAO Annex 14 colour box coordinates.
  2. MeasurementRecord the chromaticity coordinates (x, y) using a calibrated colorimeter and plot against the specified colour box.
  3. ObservationInspect the lens/filter for fading, yellowing, or an incorrect part number.
  4. DecisionCoordinates fall outside the box and lens shows visible fading/wrong part → replace lens/filter. Lens appears correct and clean but coordinates still off → suspect LED module colour shift or photometer calibration (cross-reference PHM-460).
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to lens/filter degradation, wrong part, LED module shift, or instrument error.
  6. Root CauseConfirm by re-measuring with a verified-calibrated instrument after lens/filter replacement.
Test Points
Chromaticity coordinates (x, y) vs. specified colour box
Lens/filter condition and part number
LED module colour temperature/drift where applicable
Colorimeter calibration status
Root Cause Verification

After replacing the lens/filter or module, re-measure chromaticity and confirm the coordinates fall within the specified ICAO Annex 14 colour box.

Corrective Action
  • Replace a faded or incorrect lens/filter with the correct-spec part.
  • Replace an LED module exhibiting colour shift.
  • Recalibrate the colorimeter if instrument error is confirmed.
Preventive Action
  • Track lens/filter age and include chromaticity spot checks in the periodic PM programme, particularly for fixtures with high UV exposure.
  • Maintain strict part-number control for coloured lenses/filters.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PHM-07 (Chromaticity Verification Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A (Colours for Aeronautical Ground Lights), FAA AC 150/5345-46

Rev 0 — Initial issue
PHM-458 Wrong Light Color

Symptom — The fixture displays an obviously incorrect colour to visual inspection (e.g., showing white where yellow/amber is required, or red where green is required), distinct from a subtle chromaticity shift.

Equipment / System

Any coloured AGL fixture, typically at a runway/taxiway intersection, threshold, or caution zone where colour coding is safety-critical.

Possible Causes
HighWrong-colour lens/filter fitted during installation or a prior maintenance visit
HighFixture installed in the wrong location relative to the lighting design (e.g., a white edge fixture installed where a yellow caution fixture belongs)
MediumLED module of the wrong colour (e.g., a white module fitted to a fixture requiring in-line colour filtering, without the filter installed)
LowLens completely missing or a clear lens fitted in place of a coloured one
Required Tools
Design lighting layout drawingCorrect-colour replacement lens/filter or LED moduleVisual colour reference standard
Safety Precautions
  • This is a safety-critical, often reportable discrepancy (e.g., at runway/taxiway hold points) — flag and prioritise for immediate correction or fixture de-energisation until fixed.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the fixture’s location against the design lighting layout drawing to verify the colour that should be displayed there.
  2. ObservationInspect the fixture for the fitted lens/filter colour and part number, or confirm whether a lens is missing entirely.
  3. DecisionWrong lens/filter or module fitted → replace with correct colour. Fixture in wrong location per design → relocate or reassign per the design layout.
  4. Fault LocationIsolate to a parts/fitting error versus a location/design-assignment error.
  5. Root CauseConfirm by cross-referencing the as-built layout against the original design and any recent modification records.
Test Points
Fixture location vs. design layout
Lens/filter/module colour vs. design specification
Chromaticity coordinates after correction (cross-reference PHM-457)
Root Cause Verification

After fitting the correct-colour lens/filter/module, visually confirm correct colour display and verify chromaticity coordinates fall within the specified colour box.

Corrective Action
  • Fit the correct-colour lens/filter or LED module.
  • Relocate or reassign the fixture if it was installed at the wrong design position.
  • Update as-built records to reflect the correction.
Preventive Action
  • Verify colour-coding against the design layout during final commissioning and after any fixture replacement.
  • Maintain strict, physically distinct part numbering/packaging for different coloured lenses to prevent mix-ups in the field.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PHM-08 (Fixture Colour Verification Procedure), SOP-PHM-07

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A, FAA AC 150/5340-30

PHM-459 Photometry Measurement Instability

Symptom — Repeated photometric readings on the same fixture, under apparently stable conditions, fluctuate significantly between successive measurements rather than settling to a consistent value.

Equipment / System

Fixture under test together with the field photometer/measurement setup.

Possible Causes
HighLED driver operating in a pulse-width-modulated (PWM) dimming mode not properly synchronised with the photometer’s sampling/integration time
HighUnstable circuit voltage (CCR output fluctuation) during the measurement window
MediumPhotometer battery/power instability or a loose sensor cable connection
MediumAmbient light or reflective interference at the measurement site varying between readings (e.g., passing vehicle headlights, changing cloud cover for daylight-adjacent tests)
LowPhotometer sensor thermal drift during an extended test session
Required Tools
Calibrated field photometerOscilloscope or PWM-capable meter for driver waveform checkTrue-RMS multimeter for circuit voltage stabilityStable/regulated power source for the photometer
Safety Precautions
  • Standard fixture-access precautions; ensure the measurement site is clear of other light sources or reflective interference during testing.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationRepeat the measurement multiple times in immediate succession and record the spread of readings.
  2. MeasurementCheck the LED driver output waveform for PWM dimming and confirm the photometer’s integration time is appropriate for the modulation frequency.
  3. MeasurementMonitor circuit voltage at the fixture throughout the test window for fluctuation.
  4. DecisionPWM/integration mismatch confirmed → adjust photometer integration settings or use an averaging/RMS-capable instrument. Circuit voltage unstable → investigate CCR output stability. Neither → check photometer power/cable/ambient conditions.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to driver modulation/instrument mismatch, circuit voltage instability, or measurement environment/equipment issue.
  6. Root CauseConfirm by re-testing with corrected integration settings, a stabilised circuit, or a controlled measurement environment.
Test Points
Spread of repeated intensity readings
LED driver PWM frequency and duty cycle
Circuit voltage stability during test window
Photometer battery/cable condition
Root Cause Verification

After correcting the identified cause, repeat the measurement series and confirm readings converge to a consistent value within the photometer’s stated accuracy.

Corrective Action
  • Adjust or upgrade the photometer’s integration/averaging settings to suit PWM-dimmed LED drivers.
  • Address CCR output instability.
  • Repair or replace a faulty photometer cable/battery.
  • Relocate the test to minimise ambient interference.
Preventive Action
  • Standardise on photometry equipment and settings appropriate for the fixture types in use (particularly PWM-dimmed LED).
  • Include a stability check (multiple readings) as standard practice in the field test procedure.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PHM-01, SOP-PHM-09 (Photometer Setup and Stability Verification Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A, IES LM-79 (Electrical and Photometric Measurements of LED Products)

Rev 0 — Initial issue
PHM-460 Photometer Calibration Problem

Symptom — A field photometer produces readings that are suspected or confirmed to be inaccurate — consistently high, low, or inconsistent with a reference standard — potentially affecting the validity of fixture acceptance test results.

Equipment / System

Field/portable photometer or luminance meter used for AGL photometric verification.

Possible Causes
HighCalibration interval exceeded — instrument used past its certified calibration due date
HighSensor degradation or damage from physical shock, moisture ingress, or UV exposure over its service life
MediumFirmware/software calibration coefficients corrupted or reset
MediumIncorrect calibration reference or method used at the last calibration cycle
LowEnvironmental factors (temperature, humidity) outside the instrument’s rated operating range affecting the current reading set
Required Tools
NIST-traceable reference light source or calibrated reference fixtureCalibration certificate recordsManufacturer calibration/service procedureSpare calibrated photometer for cross-check
Safety Precautions
  • No unique hazard; standard equipment handling precautions apply.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCheck the instrument’s calibration due date against its current certificate.
  2. MeasurementCross-check the suspect photometer’s reading against a known reference source or a second calibrated instrument.
  3. ObservationInspect the instrument for physical damage, moisture ingress, or sensor discolouration.
  4. DecisionCalibration overdue or reading deviates from reference beyond stated accuracy → send for recalibration/service, do not use for acceptance testing until corrected. Reading within stated accuracy of reference → instrument cleared, investigate other causes for the original suspect result.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to expired calibration, physical/sensor damage, or a valid instrument with a different root cause elsewhere.
  6. Root CauseConfirm via the calibration service report identifying the specific out-of-tolerance parameter.
Test Points
Reading vs. NIST-traceable reference source
Calibration certificate due date
Cross-check against a second calibrated instrument
Physical/sensor condition
Root Cause Verification

After recalibration or repair, re-verify the instrument against the reference source and confirm readings fall within the manufacturer’s stated accuracy before returning it to service.

Corrective Action
  • Send the photometer for manufacturer recalibration or repair.
  • Retire and replace an instrument with confirmed sensor damage.
  • Correct any calibration record/interval tracking error.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a strict calibration interval tracking system with due-date alerts for all field photometry instruments.
  • Store and transport instruments per manufacturer guidance to minimise damage risk.
  • Perform a quick reference-source cross-check before major acceptance test campaigns.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PHM-10 (Photometer Calibration and Verification Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, IES LM-79, ISO/IEC 17025 (calibration laboratory competence, where applicable)

Rev 0 — Initial issue
PHM-461 Photometer Zero Error

Symptom — The photometer reads a non-zero value with no light source present (dark/zero reading check), indicating a baseline offset that will bias every subsequent measurement.

Equipment / System

Field photometer/luminance meter sensor and readout unit.

Possible Causes
HighSensor dark-current drift from thermal effects or long continuous use
HighStray/ambient light leaking into the sensor housing or optical path
MediumZero/dark calibration not performed or performed incorrectly at the start of the session
MediumInternal electronic offset drift from instrument ageing
LowPhysical damage to the sensor housing allowing light leakage
Required Tools
Light-tight cap or dark enclosure for zero-checkManufacturer zero/dark calibration procedureSpare calibrated instrument for cross-check
Safety Precautions
  • No unique hazard.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationPerform the manufacturer’s dark/zero check with the sensor fully covered or in a light-tight enclosure and record the reading.
  2. ObservationInspect the sensor housing and optical path for damage or light leakage.
  3. MeasurementRepeat the zero check after allowing the instrument to thermally stabilise.
  4. DecisionZero offset persists after proper dark check and thermal stabilisation → instrument requires service/recalibration. Zero offset resolves with correct dark-check procedure → prior readings may have used an incorrect zero reference; re-zero and repeat affected measurements.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to a procedural zero-check error versus a genuine instrument fault.
  6. Root CauseConfirm via the calibration service report if the instrument is sent for service.
Test Points
Zero/dark reading value
Sensor housing integrity
Thermal stabilisation time
Repeatability of zero check
Root Cause Verification

After correcting the zero-check procedure or servicing the instrument, confirm a stable near-zero dark reading before proceeding with fixture measurements.

Corrective Action
  • Re-perform the correct zero/dark calibration procedure before each test session.
  • Send the instrument for service if the offset persists after correct procedure.
  • Repeat any fixture measurements taken with an unconfirmed zero reference.
Preventive Action
  • Make a documented dark/zero check a mandatory first step of every field photometry session.
  • Include sensor housing inspection in the routine equipment check.

Equipment / System

Photometer sensor orientation relative to the fixture’s defined horizontal and vertical measurement axes.

Possible Causes

PHM-468 Repeated Photometry Failure

Symptom — A specific fixture or fixture type repeatedly fails photometric acceptance testing across multiple visits or replacement attempts, despite individual corrective actions appearing successful at the time.

Equipment / System

A recurring-failure fixture, its circuit, and its maintenance/test history.

Possible Causes
HighUnderlying circuit-level issue (chronic undervoltage, poor connection) masked as a series of individual fixture-level fixes
HighSystematic use of an incorrect or marginal replacement part across multiple repair attempts
MediumEnvironmental factor specific to the fixture’s location (e.g., persistent debris/vegetation shading, drainage issue causing repeat water ingress) not addressed by prior corrective actions
MediumTest procedure or equipment issue specific to that test station being overlooked as a contributing factor
LowFixture design/category genuinely marginal for its application, requiring an engineering review rather than routine maintenance
Required Tools
Full fixture maintenance and test history logCircuit layout drawingRoot-cause analysis worksheetEngineering escalation contact/process
Safety Precautions
  • Standard fixture-access precautions apply; treat a chronic failure location as a priority for full diagnostic depth rather than repeat quick fixes.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompile the fixture’s full history of photometric failures, corrective actions taken, and subsequent results across all prior visits.
  2. MeasurementRe-verify circuit voltage/current, environmental conditions, and test setup/equipment at this specific location, rather than assuming the fixture itself is again at fault.
  3. ObservationCross-reference the location for any recurring environmental or drainage issue.
  4. DecisionPattern points to a circuit-level or environmental root cause common across all failures → address that systemic issue rather than repeating fixture-level fixes. No common pattern found → escalate for engineering review of the fixture design/application suitability.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to a systemic circuit/environmental cause, a repeated parts/procedure error, or a genuine design/application issue requiring escalation.

Test Points

Circuit voltage/current at this fixture over time
Environmental/drainage condition at the location
Root Cause Verification

After addressing the identified systemic cause, monitor the fixture’s photometric performance over the following one to two maintenance cycles and confirm no recurrence of the failure pattern.

Corrective Action
  • Address the underlying circuit-level or environmental root cause rather than repeating isolated fixture-level fixes.
  • Escalate to engineering review if no systemic cause is found.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain fixture-level failure history logs and review them for repeat-failure patterns as part of the periodic PM programme.
  • Flag chronic-failure locations for a deeper root-cause investigation rather than routine repair.

PHM-470 Fixture Passes Photometry but Field Appearance Abnormal

Symptom — A fixture measures fully within specification on formal photometric testing, yet appears visually abnormal in the field — for example, looking dim, oddly coloured, or unevenly lit compared to neighbouring fixtures under night operating conditions.

Equipment / System

A fixture with a passing photometric test result but a reported or observed field appearance discrepancy.

Possible Causes
HighTest conducted at a different intensity step than the one typically used operationally, or at a different time from the visual observation
HighPerceptual effect from surrounding fixtures, ambient lighting, weather (fog, rain), or pilot/observer viewing angle differing from the fixed test geometry
MediumA parameter within specification but at the edge of its tolerance band, visually distinguishable from neighbouring fixtures comfortably mid-band
MediumPhotometric test measured a single angle/condition that does not capture the specific viewing condition where the abnormality was reported
LowThe photometer itself passed a marginal fixture that a more sensitive or differently-calibrated instrument would have flagged
Required Tools
Calibrated field photometer with full angular scan capabilityALCMS step/log recordsWeather/visibility records for the reported observation timeComparison readings from neighbouring fixtures
Safety Precautions
  • Standard fixture-access precautions apply; if the report originated from a pilot, treat with appropriate priority pending investigation.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the exact intensity step, time, and conditions (weather, viewing angle) under which the abnormality was reported, and compare to the conditions under which the passing photometric test was conducted.
  2. MeasurementRe-test the fixture at the specific intensity step and, where feasible, from a viewing angle closer to the reported condition.
  3. ObservationCompare the fixture’s readings directly against several neighbouring fixtures to check for a within-tolerance but relatively weak position in the run (cross-reference PHM-453).
  4. DecisionRe-test under matched conditions reveals a genuine parameter discrepancy → proceed to the relevant specific-parameter corrective action. Re-test confirms full compliance under matched conditions → likely a perceptual/environmental effect; document and monitor rather than replace a compliant fixture.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to a test-condition mismatch, a marginal-but-compliant fixture, or a genuine parameter fault only detectable under specific conditions.
  6. Root CauseConfirm by correlating multiple reports (if any) against consistent conditions to distinguish a real trend from an isolated perceptual observation.
Test Points
Intensity/colour at the reported operational step
Comparison against neighbouring fixtures
Viewing-angle-specific readings if feasible
Weather/visibility conditions at time of report
Root Cause Verification

If a genuine discrepancy is found and corrected, re-test under the matched condition and confirm the appearance concern is resolved; if no discrepancy is found, document the investigation and monitor for recurrence.

Corrective Action
  • Where a genuine parameter issue is confirmed, apply the relevant specific corrective action (module, alignment, lens).
  • Where the fixture is confirmed compliant, consider tightening its position within an uneven run (cross-reference PHM-453) as a preventive step even without an outright failure.
Preventive Action
  • Include the operational intensity step (not only the acceptance-test step) in routine photometric checks.
  • Log field appearance reports with condition details to help distinguish genuine trends from one-off perceptual effects.

Every topic follows the same diagnostic record: the symptom, equipment/system, possible causes ranked by likelihood (high, medium or low), required tools, safety precautions, diagnostic procedure, test points, root cause verification, corrective action, preventive action and related references (SOPs, standards, diagrams and case studies).
