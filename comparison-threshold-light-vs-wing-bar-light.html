Runway & Taxiway Lighting Comparison

Threshold Light VS Wing Bar Light

Same Colour, Different Job — threshold lights identify the runway threshold across the runway width, while wing bar lights are additional threshold lights that extend outward from the runway sides to make the threshold more conspicuous. Both are part of runway threshold identification, but their position and purpose are different: one marks the threshold, the other makes it stand out.

02 · The Core Idea

One System, One Add-On

Threshold lights identify the beginning of the landing portion of the runway. Wing bar lights are installed as extensions of that same threshold lighting arrangement — additional threshold lights that project outward beyond the runway edges to widen and strengthen the visual signal a landing pilot sees.

03 · Definitions

What Is a Threshold Light & Wing Bar Light?

Threshold Light

Identifies the beginning of the landing portion of the runway. Forms a transverse row across the runway width, green when viewed from the approach direction.

Tells the pilot

  • This is the runway threshold
  • Landing may begin from this point

Wing Bar Light

Additional threshold lights that extend laterally beyond the runway edges, normally supplementary to the standard threshold row.

Tells the pilot

  • The threshold is here
  • Its location is made particularly conspicuous
04 · Why the Name "Wing Bar"

Where the Name Comes From

Think of the threshold lighting line as the body of an aircraft shape, with the wing bars as the wings extending outward from it on either side. The additional lights extend outward like wings from the threshold lighting line — hence "wing bar," a lateral extension of threshold lighting.

05 · Function

Identification vs Conspicuity

Threshold Light

Tells the pilot: "This is the runway threshold." The core identification function.

Wing Bar Light

Tells the pilot: "The threshold is here, and its location/extent is particularly conspicuous." An enhancement, not a replacement.

Threshold light = identification. Wing bar = enhanced conspicuity.

06 · Colour

Both Green on Approach

When viewed from the approach direction, threshold lights are green. Wing bars are also green when forming part of the threshold indication. The exact arrangement and colour requirements depend on the applicable runway lighting standard.

07 · Why It Matters

When Wing Bars Are Useful

Enhanced conspicuity: wing bars can be particularly valuable where the threshold needs to stand out against the surrounding background — during night operations, poor visibility, complex visual backgrounds, or on approaches requiring enhanced threshold conspicuity. They create a wider, more distinctive visual pattern than the threshold row alone.

08 · Not the Same As Edge Lights

Threshold, Wing Bar & Runway Edge — Three Different Roles

These three systems should not be confused. Runway edge lights are installed along the sides of the runway and define runway lateral limits. Threshold lights are installed across the threshold and identify the runway threshold. Wing bars extend outward from the threshold line specifically to enhance threshold conspicuity — a distinct purpose from all-along-the-runway edge marking.

09 · Displaced Threshold

Wing Bars and the Displaced Threshold

This distinction becomes particularly important with a displaced threshold, where the threshold does not coincide with the physical beginning of the runway pavement. Threshold lighting identifies the actual threshold rather than simply the beginning of pavement — and wing bars, where specified, reinforce this visual identification at the correct point.

11 · Myth vs Fact

Common AGL Terminology Confusion

Myth: Wing bars are a completely separate lighting system from threshold lights
Fact

Wing bars are additional threshold lights — a lateral extension of the same threshold indication, not an independent system with its own separate function.

Myth: Wing bars are required on every runway
Fact

Wing bars are normally supplementary and not universally required; whether they are installed depends on the runway type and the applicable lighting standard.

Myth: Wing bars are a different colour from threshold lights
Fact

Wing bars are also green when viewed from the approach direction, matching the threshold indication — the difference is position and conspicuity, not colour.

12 · Engineering Verdict

Threshold Light vs Wing Bar Light

Engineering Verdict

Threshold and wing bar lights are not competing systems — wing bars are a supplementary extension of threshold lighting. The fundamental difference is position and purpose, not colour or basic function.

Threshold lighting identifies where the landing portion of the runway begins; wing bar lighting widens that same indication so the threshold is more conspicuous on approach.

ThresholdGreen + transverse row + defines the threshold itself.
Wing BarGreen + extends beyond runway edges + supplementary conspicuity.

Easy memory: Threshold = "where landing starts." Wing bar = "make the threshold more visible."

13 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between a threshold light and a wing bar light?

Position and purpose — threshold lights form the main transverse row identifying the threshold, while wing bar lights are additional lights that extend outward beyond the runway edges to make that same threshold more conspicuous.

Are wing bar lights a different colour from threshold lights?

No. Both are green when viewed from the approach direction. Wing bars form part of the overall threshold indication rather than presenting a separate colour.

Are wing bars required on every runway?

No. Wing bars are normally supplementary to threshold lights, and whether they are installed depends on the runway type and the applicable lighting standard.

Why are they called "wing bars"?

Because the additional lights extend outward from the threshold lighting line like wings on either side, widening the visual pattern the pilot sees on approach.

Do wing bars matter for a displaced threshold?

Yes. Threshold lighting identifies the actual threshold rather than the physical start of pavement, and wing bars, where specified, reinforce that visual identification at the correct point.

Are wing bar lights the same as runway edge lights?

No. Runway edge lights run the length of the runway and define its lateral limits. Wing bars are localized to the threshold area and extend outward specifically to enhance threshold conspicuity.

14 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — runway threshold and wing bar lighting colour, siting, extent and applicability requirements.

FAA AC 150/5340-30

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids — threshold and wing bar lighting configuration, installation and photometric requirements.

DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Do not design a threshold or wing bar lighting arrangement from a generic web diagram. The final installation drawing should always be developed from the current governing standard, approved airport design and project-specific requirements.
