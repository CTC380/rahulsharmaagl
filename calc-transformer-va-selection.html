Calculator #49

Transformer VA Selection

Pick the standard isolating transformer size for each fixture type on a schedule, with a design margin and a count of transformers by size and type.

About this calculator

Enter each fixture type once, with its real power in watts, its power factor and how many fixtures share a transformer. If you only have the fixture's apparent power in VA (common for LED), enter that value with a power factor of 1.00 so it is not divided twice. The tool finds the smallest standard size that stays within your loading limit, shows the nearest size for comparison, and totals the transformers you need to order by size and type.

Notes on power factor, LED and transformer type

Power factor is 1.00 for plain (halogen) lamps. For LED fixtures, enter the watts and the driver's power factor, or the VA with a power factor of 1.00, and follow the fixture manufacturer's recommended transformer size where one is given. LED loads are often below 22.5 VA, so a "lightly loaded" result on the smallest size is expected. The type must match the current of the series circuit the transformer sits on (6.6 A or 20 A primary). FAA lists no 65 W transformer with a 20 A primary, so the 20/6.6 A type skips 65 VA. Smaller 10/15 W and 20/25 W types also exist for very light loads, but are not on this list. Availability of each size and type depends on the manufacturer.

Selection Rules

Rule note

Extra allowance added to the load before choosing a size, for example for a future lamp change. Your own rule.

Rule note

Used for the secondary voltage of each transformer (S ÷ I). The secondary of a standard airfield series isolating transformer is rated 6.6 A, even when the primary circuit is 20 A. This is the secondary voltage at full rated load.

Rule note

Your project's own loading window, not a standard value. Some specifications set a maximum below 100 % to allow for lamp tolerance and ageing, so set yours to match the project specification. Standard sizes: 30/45, 65, 100, 150, 200, 300 and 500, treated as nominal VA. The 30/45 size is one dual-rated transformer, treated here as 45 VA.

Method & Reference

How the size is selected

Every step is shown so the result can be checked by hand.

1. Load per transformer

Multiply the fixtures per transformer by the watts each, then divide by the power factor to get apparent power. The design margin is added on top.

S = n × W ÷ PF    S_design = S × (1 + margin)

2. Selected size

The selected size is the smallest standard size where the design load is at or below your maximum loading. Loading is then reported for the actual load, without the margin.

S_design ÷ size ≤ max loading

If the loading falls below your preferred minimum, the row is flagged as lightly loaded.

3. Nearest size

The nearest standard size to the actual load is shown beside the selected one. When it is smaller than the load, the row says so, because the nearest size alone would be overloaded.

4. Order count

Transformers are counted per selected size and type across all rows. The secondary voltage is S ÷ the secondary current (6.6 A).

What this tool does not do

The standard sizes follow the series isolating transformer ratings in FAA AC 150/5345-47 (L-830 and L-831). Confirm them against your project specification and the manufacturer's range. It does not model efficiency curves, magnetising current, secondary open-circuit voltage or lamp-out behaviour. The sizes are nominal, and the margin and loading window are your own rules. For primary-side load and losses, use Calculator #17.

FAQ

Transformer VA Selection: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on how the calculator picks isolating transformer sizes.

What does the Transformer VA Selection Calculator do?

It picks the smallest standard series isolating transformer size for each airfield lighting fixture type on your schedule, checks the loading against your own limits, reports the secondary voltage, and totals the transformers to order by size and type.

How is the isolating transformer size selected?

The load per transformer is S = n × W ÷ PF, where n is the fixtures per transformer, W the watts each and PF the power factor. A design margin is added to give the design load. The selected size is the smallest standard size where the design load is at or below your maximum loading. The loading shown is for the actual load, without the margin.

Which standard transformer sizes does the calculator use?

30/45, 65, 100, 150, 200, 300 and 500 VA, treated as nominal sizes. The 30/45 VA size is one dual-rated transformer and is treated as 45 VA. The sizes follow the series isolating transformer ratings in FAA AC 150/5345-47 (L-830 and L-831). Confirm them against your project specification and the manufacturer's range.

How do I enter LED fixtures?

Enter the fixture watts and the driver's power factor, or enter the fixture's apparent power in VA with a power factor of 1.00 so it is not divided twice. Follow the fixture manufacturer's recommended transformer size where one is given. LED loads are often below 22.5 VA, so a lightly loaded result on the smallest size is expected.

What is the difference between the 6.6/6.6 A and 20/6.6 A transformer types?

The type must match the current of the series circuit the transformer sits on, which is 6.6 A or 20 A on the primary. The secondary is rated 6.6 A in both types. Availability of the 20/6.6 A type in every size depends on the manufacturer.

What design margin and loading limits should I use?

These are your project's own rules, not standard values. The calculator starts with a 0 % margin, a 100 % maximum loading and a 50 % preferred minimum. Some specifications set a maximum below 100 % to allow for lamp tolerance and ageing, so set the values to match your project specification.

Can I use the results as the final transformer selection?

No. It is an engineering reference tool for planning and training. It does not model efficiency curves, magnetising current, secondary open-circuit voltage or lamp-out behaviour, and results do not replace the project design, the applicable standards or the manufacturers' data. Verify the final selection against the isolating transformer and fixture manufacturers' data.
