What is the primary connector?

The primary connector is the sealed plug-and-receptacle joint where the transformer’s primary lead meets the primary cable. It carries the full loop current, so the quality of this one joint decides whether the whole series circuit keeps running.

The Fact Circuit practice

The primary connector is the joint between the primary lead of a series isolation transformer and the primary cable of the loop. A pin and socket pass the current, an insulated body holds them, and seals keep water out. Because the transformer’s primary winding is wired into the series loop, the full loop current flows through this connector, the same current that flows through every other joint on the circuit.

That makes it a weak point worth respecting. A joint that opens breaks the loop for every light on the circuit; a loose or corroded contact adds resistance, heats up and can arc; and water in the joint can cause an earth fault. Never separate a primary connector on an energised circuit. Isolate the CCR, apply lock-out and tag-out, prove the circuit dead and make it safe under site procedures before any connector is touched, and treat every lead as live until then.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is the primary connector in AGL?

The sealed plug-and-receptacle joint between the primary lead of a series isolation transformer and the primary cable of the series circuit. It lets the transformer be connected into, and removed from, the loop without cutting the cable, and it carries the full loop current.

Q2What is the difference between the primary connector and the secondary connector?

The primary connector joins the transformer to the series loop and carries the loop current from the CCR. The secondary connector joins the transformer's secondary lead to the light fitting or sign on the low-voltage side. A fault in the primary joint can take out the whole circuit; a fault in the secondary joint mainly affects the one fitting.

Q3Why does a bad primary connector affect the whole circuit?

Because the connector is part of the loop itself. If the joint opens, the series circuit is broken and the current stops for every light on it. A loose or corroded joint can also run hot or arc, and a leaking joint can cause an earth fault.

Q4Can I disconnect a primary connector while the circuit is on?

No. Separating a primary connector on an energised series circuit opens the loop and can expose people to dangerous voltage and arcing. Isolate the CCR, apply lock-out and tag-out, prove the circuit dead and make it safe under site procedures before any connector is touched.

Q5What should I check on a primary connector?

Look for cracks, cuts, swelling or heat marks on the body, a displaced or damaged boot or seal, signs of water in the pit, corrosion and a joint that is not fully mated. Damage is a reason to test and usually re-make or replace the joint under site procedures, not to leave it in service.
