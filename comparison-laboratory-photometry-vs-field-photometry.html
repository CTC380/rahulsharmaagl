Photometric Testing Comparison

LABORATORY PHOTOMETRY VS FIELD PHOTOMETRY

Controlled Fixture Testing vs Installed-System Verification — laboratory photometry measures an AGL fixture under controlled test conditions to characterise its intrinsic optical performance, while field photometry measures the installed lighting system under actual site conditions to verify how it performs in service. Neither replaces the other; a complete performance picture needs both.

02 · Definitions

What Is Laboratory & Field Photometry?

Laboratory Photometry

Tests an AGL fixture in a controlled laboratory environment using a goniophotometer and calibrated instruments. Position, distance, angles and environment are all controlled to determine the fixture's intrinsic photometric performance.

Question answered

What can this fixture do under controlled conditions?

Field Photometry

Evaluates the light after installation at the airport, using field photometric instruments. The measurement can reflect the actual installation geometry and operating conditions of the installed system.

Question answered

How is the installed lighting system performing in practice?

04 · Test Conditions

What Each Environment Controls

A laboratory test attempts to control the variables that could affect the measurement; a field measurement is exposed to the actual site.

Laboratory Variables (Controlled)

Controlled position, controlled distance, controlled angles, controlled environment, and calibrated instruments — enabling detailed and repeatable fixture characterization.

Field Factors (Actual Site)

Mounting alignment, fixture condition, lens contamination, installation geometry, surrounding lighting and environmental conditions — factors laboratory testing alone cannot capture.

05 · Example Result

Laboratory Result vs Field Result

Suppose a new runway light is tested in a laboratory and returns a peak intensity of 10,000 cd. After installation, the field measurement reflects the actual installation and operating conditions — which may differ due to misalignment, dirt or contamination, lens condition, installation orientation, electrical operating conditions, or environmental influences. The exact interpretation depends on the field test method used.

06 · Beam Distribution

Detailed Pattern vs Practical Verification

Laboratory Beam Pattern

A goniophotometric test produces a detailed beam pattern, revealing the fixture's optical design.

Field Verification Points

Field testing focuses more directly on whether the installed light meets required performance at relevant locations or directions.

08 · Installation Effects

One of the Most Important Distinctions

Laboratory photometry does not necessarily verify that a fixture is correctly installed at the airport. Even a small installation or alignment difference can alter the direction in which the light is presented.

Laboratory

Fixture is perfectly positioned; the beam is measured exactly as designed, with no installation geometry involved.

Field

Fixture is measured as actually installed — mounting alignment and orientation can shift the beam away from the designed direction.

09 · Maintenance Effects

Controlled Condition vs Real Condition

Laboratory testing often evaluates a fixture in a controlled, usually new, condition. Field testing can reveal the real maintenance condition of an in-service fixture, including performance changes caused by dirt, lens degradation, mechanical damage, alignment changes, or ageing.

12 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: Laboratory results are accurate and field results are inaccurate
Fact

Laboratory testing is optimized for controlled, repeatable and detailed fixture characterization, while field testing is optimized for assessing the installed system under actual operating conditions. Both require properly calibrated equipment and defined measurement procedures — one is not simply "more accurate" than the other.

Myth: A laboratory test alone confirms a fixture is correctly installed
Fact

Laboratory photometry characterizes the fixture itself, not its installation. Only a field measurement can reflect actual mounting alignment and site conditions.

Myth: Field photometry can replace laboratory testing entirely
Fact

Field testing is often more limited and practical in scope, and is more affected by site conditions. Detailed beam pattern and product qualification data still come from laboratory testing.

14 · Engineering Verdict

Laboratory vs Field Photometry

Engineering Verdict

Laboratory photometry establishes the fixture's detailed optical characteristics; field photometry verifies how the lighting performs after installation and under real operating conditions. They are not direct substitutes.

Use laboratory data to qualify and compare products before procurement. Use field data to verify installation, alignment and in-service condition once the system is on site. Together they provide the complete performance picture — neither alone is sufficient for full AGL assurance.

LaboratoryControlled test + fixture characterization + product qualification.
FieldInstalled system + actual site conditions + operational verification.

For AGL: laboratory photometry proves what a fixture can do under controlled conditions; field photometry proves how it is actually performing once installed. It is generally misleading to call one "accurate" and the other "inaccurate" — each is optimized for a different purpose.

15 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the simplest way to remember the difference?

Laboratory = PRODUCT PERFORMANCE, under controlled conditions. Field = INSTALLED PERFORMANCE, under actual site conditions.

Is laboratory photometry more accurate than field photometry?

Not simply "more accurate" — laboratory testing is optimized for controlled, repeatable, detailed fixture characterization, while field testing is optimized for assessing the installed system under actual operating conditions. Both can require properly calibrated equipment and defined procedures.

Does laboratory testing confirm correct installation?

No. Laboratory photometry does not necessarily verify that a fixture is correctly installed at the airport — it characterizes the fixture itself, not its installed geometry or alignment.

Why might a field measurement differ from the laboratory result?

Possible reasons include misalignment, dirt or contamination, lens condition, installation orientation, electrical operating conditions, and environmental influences. The exact interpretation depends on the field test method used.

Can field photometry replace laboratory testing?

No — the two are complementary rather than substitutes. Laboratory testing provides detailed fixture design characterization; field testing provides installation verification. A complete performance assessment needs both.

What equipment is used for each?

Laboratory photometry typically uses a goniophotometer and other laboratory instruments. Field photometry uses field photometric instruments suited to on-site, installed-system measurement.

16 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

FAA AC 150/5340-30

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids — defines the photometric requirements a fixture must meet, forming the basis for laboratory qualification testing.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — photometric characteristics of aeronautical ground lights, including requirements referenced in both type-test and in-service assessment.

CIE Photometry Standards

International Commission on Illumination definitions and measurement methods, applicable to both goniophotometric laboratory testing and field photometric practice.

Final Engineering Note

Do not treat a laboratory type-test result as confirmation of in-service performance. Field verification is required to confirm the installed system meets the required performance at its actual location and condition.

Laboratory photometry is not field photometry — one characterizes fixture performance under controlled conditions, the other verifies installed-system performance under actual site conditions. For AGL, both together define confidence in the lighting system.
