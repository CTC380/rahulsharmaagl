Airfield Lighting Testing & Commissioning Guide

How to prove an installed AGL system is correctly installed, electrically safe, functionally operational and optically compliant: cable and insulation tests, CCR, transformers, circuits, photometry, PAPI, signs, controls, punch lists and handover records. Commissioning is more than checking that the lights turn on.

Start Learning AGL Maintenance
Overview

What Testing and Commissioning Mean

Airfield lighting testing and commissioning verifies that an installed AGL system is correctly installed, electrically safe, functionally operational and able to provide the intended visual guidance performance. It is not a final check that the lights turn on: it covers the chain from the power supply and CCR through the primary series circuit, isolation transformers and connectors to the fixtures, visual aids, signs and control system.

Testing produces evidence; commissioning uses that evidence to show the system is complete, functional and ready for operation.

Electrical Testing

Cable, Insulation, Continuity and Circuit Impedance

Cable testing

Potential tests depend on cable type, circuit arrangement, voltage, specification and electrical requirements: insulation resistance, continuity, conductor identification, cable integrity, fault location, sheath checks and circuit resistance or impedance. Test before final commissioning where practical so construction defects are fixed before energization.

Insulation resistance

Helps find damaged cable, moisture ingress, defective joints, transformer insulation problems, damaged connectors and termination defects. Test voltage, connections, duration and acceptance criteria come from the approved test procedure.

Do not use a generic insulation-resistance number as a universal AGL acceptance limit.

Continuity is not insulation integrity

Continuity finds open circuits, wrong connections, poor joints, wrong terminations and damaged conductors. A cable can show continuity PASS while insulation FAIL, so both tests are needed where specified.

Circuit resistance / impedance

Depends on cable length, conductors, number and type of transformers, fixture load, temperature, configuration and connections. Compare it with design calculations, manufacturer information, approved criteria, factory or site data and the commissioning baseline. A significant difference should be investigated.

Equipment

Transformers, Connectors and CCR Testing

Isolation transformer

Follow the manufacturer instructions and project specification.

  • Physical and nameplate inspection
  • Primary and secondary checks
  • Insulation, winding continuity, polarity where applicable
  • Damage and moisture ingress

Nameplate verification

Must match the approved equipment schedule.

  • Manufacturer, model, rating, serial number
  • Primary and secondary characteristics
  • Project identification and circuit association
  • A wrong rating causes downstream lighting problems

Connectors

Inspect every connector before energization.

  • Correct type, assembly and termination
  • Secure, no damage, moisture or corrosion
  • Correct identification
  • Defects cause high resistance, intermittent faults or open circuits

CCR commissioning

Consider incoming supply, output current, intensity steps, current regulation, control response, local and remote control, monitoring, alarms, fault indications, protective functions, interlocks, communication and standby functions where applicable. Follow the manufacturer's commissioning procedure for internal adjustments and protective functions. The FAA specification for CCRs and regulator monitors is AC 150/5345-10H.

Current-step testing

Verify each step: the command is received, the CCR moves to the correct condition, output is stable, monitoring reports the state and the lighting responds. Take current values and tolerances from the CCR specification, not a generic table.

Local control

Test ON/OFF, step selection, status and alarm indication, reset and protective trips where specified. Confirm both command and response.

Remote control

Test from the airport's actual control system: ON/OFF, intensity, acknowledgement, feedback, faults, loss of communication, local/remote selection and interlocks.

Functional Testing

Functional and Brightness-Step Testing

Complete-circuit functional test

Show that the whole circuit operates together, not only that each fixture works. The chain should be:

01

Command

02

CCR response

03

Correct current / intensity

04

All connected fixtures respond

05

Correct visual pattern

06

Monitoring confirms status; no unexpected alarms

Brightness-step testing

For each commanded step record: commanded condition, CCR response, circuit current, fixture response, control-system indication, abnormal behaviour and alarms. It can reveal:

  • Incorrect CCR programming or missing intensity levels
  • Unstable current or incorrect control signals
  • Communication problems and monitoring errors
  • Failed or uneven fixtures
  • Wrong local/remote configuration

Treat it as an integrated commissioning test, not simply a CCR test.

Photometry

Photometric Verification

Photometry verifies the optical result: intensity, beam distribution, colour, coverage, orientation, alignment and fixture-to-fixture consistency. It may be required for runway edge, threshold and end, centreline, touchdown zone, approach, taxiway edge and centreline lighting, stop bars, runway guard lights and PAPI. The method must match the lighting function and the acceptance criteria. The FAA specification for runway, taxiway, heliport and vertiport light fixtures is AC 150/5345-46F.

Electrical to optical: electrical input, CCR, series circuit, transformer, light fixture, optical system, measured light output. Electrical commissioning shows the system supplies the expected operating condition; photometry verifies the final optical result.

PAPI

PAPI Testing

PAPI needs specialized commissioning because its function depends on optical geometry as well as electrical operation. The FAA specification for PAPI systems is AC 150/5345-28H.

Functional

Operation first.

  • All units operate, brightness appropriate
  • Control commands received
  • Indications correct and stable, no flicker

Alignment

An engineering measurement.

  • Reference and unit elevation, horizontal position
  • Spacing, azimuth, vertical aiming, runway relationship
  • Do not confirm by judging red/white from the ground

Photometric

Retain data and calibration records.

  • Intensity, beam coverage, colour
  • Alignment-related parameters and transition characteristics where applicable
  • Consistency among units

Signs and Controls

Sign, Control-System and SCADA Testing

Signs

FAA AC 150/5340-18H covers siting, design and installation of airport signs and AC 150/5345-44L covers the specification for runway and taxiway signs; both are listed as active.

  • Installation, location, orientation, legend, identification
  • Mechanical condition, electrical connections
  • Illumination, brightness and control, monitoring, circuit association
  • Emergency/backup behaviour where applicable
  • Functional test: command, sign energized, correct legend, status feedback; verify each independently controlled sign against its control point

Control system

  • Local and remote controls, intensity commands, circuit selection
  • Status feedback, equipment alarms, CCR monitoring
  • Communication links, interlocks, emergency controls
  • Fail-safe behaviour where specified

SCADA / monitoring

Verify equipment, circuit and CCR status, current or intensity information, alarms, communication-loss and fault indication, command acknowledgement, event logging and time/date. Test both directions: control to field, and field to monitoring.

Failure simulations (communication loss, CCR fault, circuit fault, power failure, controller failure, invalid command) belong only in the approved procedure with operational safeguards. Do not introduce uncontrolled failures into an operational airport to demonstrate an alarm.

Workflow

Integrated Commissioning and the End-to-End Sequence

The strongest test follows the operational chain: the operator selects an intensity, the control system sends the command, the CCR changes step, the primary circuit responds, fixtures illuminate, photometric output matches the required condition and the monitoring system reports the correct status.

Test progressively: component, then circuit, then system, then integrated operation. Do not energize and troubleshoot the entire airfield system at once.

Control of the Process

Punch Lists, FAT / SAT, Hold Points and Safety

Punch list

Typical items: wrong fixture orientation, damaged connector, missing label, cable identification, incomplete sealing, incorrect transformer, failed insulation test, low photometric result, PAPI alignment, sign-control mismatch, CCR alarm, SCADA point.

  • Unique reference and location
  • Description and responsible party
  • Corrective action and status
  • Retest requirement and closure date

Factory vs site acceptance

FAT is done before shipment: construction, functionality, electrical performance, control interfaces, alarms, factory configuration and documents. SAT is done after installation: site wiring, power, field interfaces, the actual control system, communications and field performance. For CCRs, control panels and monitoring systems, FAT and SAT complement site commissioning; they do not replace it.

Commissioning baseline

Reference for all future maintenance.

  • CCR output, circuit characteristics, transformer data
  • Fixture identification, photometric performance
  • PAPI alignment, sign operation, control configuration

Commissioning dossier

What the handover package normally holds.

  • Project data and commissioning date, approved, red-line and as-built drawings
  • Equipment records, certificates, calibration certificates, spare-parts lists
  • All test records, punch list, retests, final inspection
  • Commissioning certificate, O&M manuals, training, handover

Commissioning report

Should answer seven questions.

  • Installed correctly? Electrically safe and functional?
  • Lighting operated correctly? Optical system performed?
  • PAPI and signs correct? Control system correct?
  • All defects closed?

Acceptance rests on objective evidence: installation, electrical, functional, optical, control/monitoring evidence, final inspection and complete documentation. Record actual measurements, not PASS/FAIL only, wherever a quantitative result exists. Keep every result traceable: airport, runway/taxiway, circuit, equipment, test, result.

Special Cases

Rehabilitation, LED Conversion and Common Mistakes

Testing during rehabilitation

Commissioning is also required after runway or taxiway lighting replacement, CCR, cable or transformer replacement, control upgrade, LED conversion, PAPI or sign replacement and pavement reconstruction. The scope should reflect the work done: a small replacement does not need the programme of a full reconstruction, but the affected interfaces must be verified.

LED commissioning

Check fixture, LED module, optics, intensity settings, colour, and compatibility with controls, CCR, transformer and monitoring, plus photometric performance. Do not accept an LED fixture just because it saves power or lights up.

Common mistakes

  • Testing only whether lights turn on
  • Skipping insulation testing
  • Using uncalibrated test equipment
  • Not recording the operating intensity
  • Treating all lights as identical
  • Ignoring control-system feedback
  • Closing defects without retesting
  • Not preserving baseline data

FAQ

AGL Testing & Commissioning FAQ

Q1What is airfield lighting commissioning?

Airfield lighting commissioning is the formal process of verifying that an installed AGL system has been correctly installed, electrically sound, operationally functional and capable of meeting its applicable performance requirements.

Q2What tests are required for AGL commissioning?

Typical tests include cable continuity, insulation resistance, transformer checks, connector inspection, CCR testing, circuit measurements, functional testing, brightness-step testing and photometric verification. PAPI, signs and control systems require additional dedicated tests.

Q3What is an AGL testing checklist?

An AGL testing checklist is a structured list used to verify that the electrical, optical, mechanical, functional and control aspects of an airport lighting installation have been tested and documented.

Q4Why is insulation resistance testing required?

It helps identify damaged or degraded insulation, moisture, defective joints and other conditions that can compromise circuit integrity.

Q5Is continuity testing enough?

No. Continuity demonstrates that an intended electrical path exists; it does not by itself demonstrate acceptable insulation condition.

Q6How is a CCR tested during commissioning?

CCR testing can include input power, output current, intensity steps, current regulation, alarms, controls, monitoring, local/remote operation and protective functions, according to the applicable specification and manufacturer procedure.

Q7What is circuit impedance testing?

It is the measurement of the electrical characteristics of the installed circuit and can be compared with design calculations or commissioning baselines to identify abnormal circuit conditions.

Q8Is photometry part of commissioning?

Where required by the project or applicable standard, yes. Photometry verifies the optical performance of the installed lights rather than only their electrical operation.

Q9How is PAPI commissioned?

PAPI commissioning includes functional operation plus verification of position, elevation, alignment, aiming and optical performance as applicable.

Q10Are airport signs included in AGL commissioning?

Where the project includes lighted/sign systems, sign installation, electrical function, control and monitoring should be commissioned as applicable.

Q11What documents are required at handover?

The final package commonly includes test certificates, calibration records, photometric results, equipment records, as-built drawings, PAPI/sign/control-system test records, punch-list closure records, O&M manuals and commissioning certificates.

Q12What happens when an AGL test fails?

The failed result should be investigated, the cause identified, corrective action completed and the affected test repeated. The final record should contain both the original failure and the successful retest.
