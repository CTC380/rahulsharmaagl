PRV-621 Preventing Transformer Water Ingress

Failure Mode Addressed — Water entering the base can and reaching the isolation transformer body, connectors, or leads before it ever causes an insulation failure — addressed here at the prevention stage rather than after the transformer has already failed.

Equipment / System

L-830 series-circuit isolation transformers and the base cans, connectors, and drainage provisions that house and protect them.

Contributing Causes
HighBase can without a functional drain path (weep hole, gravel sump) allowing water to pool around the transformer
HighDegraded or missing lid gasket allowing surface water to enter directly
MediumDuct entries into the can not sealed, letting water track in along the cable route
MediumConnector not properly greased/seated, giving water a path into the can interior over time
LowElevated local water table or poor site grading directing water toward the can regardless of can condition
LowCondensation inside a sealed can from thermal cycling, mistaken for ingress if not distinguished during inspection
Required Tools / Materials
Dielectric grease (correct specification)Base-can lid gasket, correct size/materialDuct entry seals/bootsDrainage rod or sump-clearing toolBase-can moisture inspection logSite drainage/grading reference
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before opening any base can for inspection or preventive work.
  • Treat any base can with standing water as an electrocution hazard until isolation is confirmed.
  • Use appropriate PPE for pit/can entry per site procedure.
Prevention Procedure
  1. BaselineInclude base-can drainage function, not just visual seal condition, in the standard inspection checklist for every PM visit.
  2. PreventionConfirm the weep hole/gravel sump is clear and functioning at every can opened, clearing debris or silt as needed.
  3. PreventionVerify the lid gasket is present, correctly seated, and not perished; replace if any doubt exists rather than reusing a marginal gasket.
  4. PreventionSeal duct entries into the can with the correct boot/sealant so water cannot track in along the cable route.
  5. PreventionApply dielectric grease correctly at every connector reconnection, as a standard step rather than only when a fault is found.
  6. VerificationRe-inspect the can after the next significant rainfall event to confirm the preventive measures are holding before considering the item closed.
Inspection / Monitoring Points
Weep hole/sump function
Lid gasket condition
Duct entry seal condition
Connector grease coverage
Standing water presence after rainfall
Site grading relative to the can
Effectiveness Verification

Re-inspect the can dry and after the next significant rain event, confirming no standing water and no moisture at the connector or transformer body before closing the preventive work order.

If Failure Occurs Despite Prevention
  • If moisture is found despite prevention, isolate and megger the transformer before re-energising.
  • Replace the transformer if insulation resistance is below the acceptable threshold.
  • Re-open and correct the drainage/seal deficiency that allowed the ingress rather than only drying and closing the can.
Preventive Programme Integration
  • Add base-can drainage function as a mandatory PM checklist item, not an optional visual check.
  • Track chronically wet can locations in the CMMS and prioritise them for civil drainage improvement.
  • Standardise the gasket and duct-seal specification across the fleet.
  • Require dielectric grease application to be signed off on every connector work order.
Related References

PRV-622 Preventing Transformer Overheating

Failure Mode Addressed — Isolation transformers or CCR output transformers running at an elevated operating temperature that shortens insulation life, addressed proactively before it progresses to a thermal insulation failure.

Equipment / System

L-830 isolation transformers and CCR internal transformers, including their wattage/tap rating relative to the connected load and their thermal environment.

Contributing Causes
HighWattage/tap mismatch — a transformer supplying a load above its rated capacity, running permanently hot
HighRestricted ventilation around the transformer (undersized base can, blocked CCR house vents) preventing normal heat dissipation
MediumElevated ambient temperature at the installation location beyond the transformer's design assumption
MediumSustained overvoltage or harmonic distortion from the CCR increasing core/winding losses
LowTransformer nearing end of insulation life, so normal heat now produces faster degradation than when new
LowIncorrect transformer type substituted during a past repair, rated for a different duty cycle
Required Tools / Materials
Thermal imaging cameraWattage/tap verification checklistCCR output voltage/THD loggerVentilation inspection checklist for CCR house/pitTransformer nameplate/rating referenceAmbient temperature logger
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before physical inspection; use non-contact thermal imaging where possible on live equipment under proper procedure.
  • Allow adequate cool-down time before handling a transformer suspected of overheating.
Prevention Procedure
  1. BaselineVerify the wattage/tap rating of every transformer at installation and at every replacement against the connected fixture load, not assumed to match the previous unit.
  2. PreventionInclude thermal imaging of a representative sample of transformers/base cans in the periodic PM programme, especially in high-ambient locations.
  3. PreventionConfirm CCR house/pit ventilation paths are clear of debris, vegetation, or blockage at every PM visit.
  4. PreventionVerify CCR output voltage and monitor for harmonic distortion if the CCR is inverter-based, correcting any deviation from design spec.
  5. PreventionFlag transformers approaching design end-of-life for proactive replacement rather than waiting for a thermal failure.
  6. VerificationRe-check thermal imaging results at the next PM cycle to confirm temperatures remain within the normal range for that transformer class.
Inspection / Monitoring Points
Transformer wattage/tap vs. design load
Base-can/CCR house ventilation condition
CCR output voltage and THD
Thermal imaging reading vs. baseline
Ambient temperature at the location
Transformer age vs. design life
Effectiveness Verification

Confirm via thermal imaging at the next scheduled visit that the transformer operates within its normal temperature range under actual load, with no upward trend versus the previous reading.

If Failure Occurs Despite Prevention
  • Replace a transformer confirmed to be overheating with a correctly rated unit.
  • Clear or improve ventilation where restriction is confirmed as a contributing factor.
  • Correct CCR output voltage/harmonic issues at the source.
  • Reduce connected load or upgrade transformer rating if a genuine capacity mismatch is confirmed.
Preventive Programme Integration
  • Make wattage/tap verification a mandatory, signed-off step on every transformer changeout.
  • Include ventilation checks as a standard CCR house/pit PM item.
  • Trend thermal imaging results by location to catch developing hot spots early.
  • Track transformer age by installation cohort to plan proactive replacement before end of life.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PRV-01 (Preventive Maintenance Procedure), SOP-RCL-12 (Transformer Test and Replacement Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-26, IEEE C57.12.91

PRV-623 Preventing Connector Burning

Failure Mode Addressed — L-823 or similar plug/receptacle connectors overheating and showing carbon tracking, melted insulation, or burnt contacts due to progressively rising contact resistance, addressed before the connector reaches thermal failure.

Equipment / System

L-823 primary connectors and secondary lamp/transformer connectors across the series circuit.

Contributing Causes
HighLoose or improperly seated connector pins allowing contact resistance to rise under load current over time
HighCorrosion at the contact surface increasing resistance and generating localised heat under normal operating current
MediumIncorrect or absent dielectric grease allowing oxidation to progress faster than normal
MediumA connector carrying current beyond its rated capacity due to a circuit design or load mismatch
LowRepeated disconnect/reconnect cycles wearing the contact plating faster than its rated cycle life
LowA connector from a batch with a known plating or contact-spring defect
Required Tools / Materials
Contact resistance meterCorrect-torque connector assembly toolDielectric grease (correct specification)Thermal imaging cameraConnector inspection/teardown kitConnector torque specification sheet
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before disconnecting, inspecting, or reassembling any connector.
  • Treat a connector with visible heat damage as potentially still hazardous; do not force a seized or fused connector apart without confirming isolation.
Prevention Procedure
  1. BaselineInclude contact resistance spot-checks on a representative sample of connectors in the periodic PM programme rather than relying on visual inspection alone.
  2. PreventionRe-seat and re-grease every connector opened for any reason, as a standard step, not only when a fault is suspected.
  3. PreventionVerify correct connector torque/seating technique is used at every reconnection, and confirm the crew is trained to the current procedure.
  4. PreventionUse thermal imaging on a sample of connectors during a live period to catch early-stage hot connections before visible damage occurs.
  5. PreventionConfirm the connector's current rating matches the actual circuit load at design/verification stage.
  6. VerificationRe-check contact resistance or thermal signature on any connector flagged as marginal at the next scheduled visit.
Inspection / Monitoring Points
Contact resistance vs. baseline/specification
Visual condition — discolouration, pitting, carbon tracking
Grease coverage and condition
Connector current rating vs. actual circuit load
Thermal imaging signature under live load
Connector age/cycle count where tracked
Effectiveness Verification

Confirm contact resistance is within the accepted range and thermal imaging shows no elevated signature at the next PM visit following any preventive re-termination.

If Failure Occurs Despite Prevention
  • Replace any connector found with visible carbon tracking, melting, or pitting rather than attempting to clean and reuse it.
  • Re-terminate with correct grease and torque where the connector body itself is still serviceable.
  • Investigate circuit loading if a rating mismatch is suspected as a contributing factor.
Preventive Programme Integration
  • Make dielectric grease application and correct torque a mandatory, signed-off step on every connector work order.
  • Add thermal imaging of connectors to the periodic PM programme, prioritising high-load or high-cycle locations.
  • Track connector batch/date-code for early detection of a defective lot.
  • Refresh training on connector assembly technique at defined intervals.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PRV-01 (Preventive Maintenance Procedure), SOP-RCL-03 (Fixture Replacement Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-26, FAA AC 150/5345-46 (L-852)

PRV-624 Preventing Cable Insulation Failure

Failure Mode Addressed — Primary or secondary series cable insulation degrading toward failure — from moisture, mechanical stress, thermal cycling, or age — addressed through proactive testing and protection before an actual insulation breakdown occurs.

Equipment / System

L-824 primary series cable and secondary cable, including duct banks, pull boxes, and field splices along the route.

Contributing Causes
HighChronic moisture at a duct/pull-box location progressively degrading insulation even without a visible fault yet
HighCable approaching end of design life across an installation-date cohort, with insulation resistance trending downward
MediumMechanical stress from ground movement, civil works, or vehicle loading at a specific route section
MediumSustained CCR output voltage or harmonics exceeding the cable insulation's comfortable operating margin
LowSubstandard splice/joint technique used during a past repair, creating a weak point before it fully fails
LowChemical exposure (de-icing fluid, fuel) attacking the cable jacket at a specific location
Required Tools / Materials
1000V/2500V insulation resistance testerInsulation resistance trend log by circuit/segmentAs-built route drawingCivil works/vehicle-loading history for the routeSplice kit specification sheetCCR output voltage/harmonic logger
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the CCR before any insulation testing or cable work.
  • Obtain excavation clearance before any preventive work requiring pavement or verge opening.
  • Coordinate airside access through ATC/airfield operations.
Prevention Procedure
  1. BaselineInclude full-loop insulation resistance testing and trending, not spot checks alone, in the periodic PM programme for every circuit.
  2. PreventionPlot insulation resistance trends by circuit and by installation-date cohort to identify cable approaching end of life before it fails.
  3. PreventionFlag and prioritise chronically wet duct/pull-box locations for drainage remediation before insulation degrades further.
  4. PreventionVerify CCR output voltage and harmonic content remains within the cable's design margin.
  5. PreventionCoordinate with civil works teams on any excavation or resurfacing near known cable routes to avoid mechanical damage.
  6. VerificationConfirm insulation resistance trend has stabilised or improved at the next PM cycle following any preventive remediation.
Inspection / Monitoring Points
Insulation resistance trend by circuit/segment
Installation-date cohort vs. design life
Duct/pull-box moisture condition along the route
CCR output voltage/harmonic content
Civil works activity near the route
Splice/joint condition at known repair points
Effectiveness Verification

Re-test insulation resistance at the next two scheduled PM visits following any preventive action and confirm the trend has stabilised within the acceptable range before closing the item.

If Failure Occurs Despite Prevention
  • Repair or replace cable segments where insulation resistance has fallen below the acceptable threshold, before a full fault occurs.
  • Remediate duct/pull-box drainage at chronically wet locations.
  • Correct any confirmed CCR overvoltage/harmonic issue at the source.
Preventive Programme Integration
  • Trend insulation resistance by installation cohort to plan proactive replacement ahead of expected end of life.
  • Standardise splice kit specification and require photo documentation of every buried splice.
  • Maintain accurate as-built records of every joint, pull box, and repair splice for future reference.
  • Include cable route condition in the risk assessment for any nearby civil works project.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PRV-01 (Preventive Maintenance Procedure), SOP-RCL-02 (Series Circuit Fault Location Procedure)

PRV-625 Preventing Fixture Water Ingress

Failure Mode Addressed — Water entering an inset or elevated fixture housing before it reaches the lamp/LED module, optics, or wiring and causes an operational failure, addressed at the seal and drainage stage.

Equipment / System

Inset L-852 fixtures and elevated fixture housings, including their lens/optic seals, gaskets, cable entries, and base-can drainage.

Contributing Causes
HighDegraded or improperly seated lens/optic gasket allowing water directly into the fixture head
HighBase-can water accumulation reaching the fixture body through the base connection
MediumCable entry into the fixture not properly sealed, allowing water to track in along the secondary cable
MediumPhysical damage (impact, jet blast debris) compromising the housing seal without a full fixture failure yet
LowFixture design/model with a known marginal seal at that specific joint
LowCondensation from thermal cycling inside a sealed fixture, distinct from true ingress
Required Tools / Materials
Lens/optic gasket, correct type and sizeCable entry seal/glandBase-can drainage inspection kitFixture housing inspection checklistTorque specification for lens/housing fastenersMoisture indicator or visual inspection light
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before opening any fixture housing.
  • Coordinate airside access through ATC/airfield operations.
  • Use FOD-safe tools and account for every item before leaving the movement area.
Prevention Procedure
  1. BaselineInclude lens/optic gasket and cable entry seal condition in the standard fixture PM inspection checklist, not only when a fault is reported.
  2. PreventionReplace gaskets showing any sign of perishing, compression set, or improper seating, rather than reusing marginal seals.
  3. PreventionVerify base-can drainage is functional at every fixture serviced, since a wet can will eventually compromise even a well-sealed fixture.
  4. PreventionConfirm lens/housing fasteners are torqued to specification at every reassembly.
  5. PreventionInspect fixtures in high-impact zones (jet blast, snow-plough routes) more frequently for seal-compromising physical damage.
  6. VerificationRe-inspect the fixture after the next significant rainfall event to confirm no moisture has entered before considering the item closed.
Inspection / Monitoring Points
Lens/optic gasket condition
Cable entry seal condition
Base-can drainage function
Housing fastener torque
Physical damage signature (impact, cracking)
Moisture presence after rainfall
Effectiveness Verification

Re-inspect the fixture dry and after the next significant rainfall event, confirming no moisture at the lens, optic, or wiring before closing the preventive work order.

If Failure Occurs Despite Prevention
  • Replace any gasket or seal found compromised rather than attempting a field repair with unsuitable material.
  • Remediate base-can drainage if a wet can is contributing to fixture ingress.
  • Replace the fixture if moisture has already reached the lamp/LED module or wiring with visible corrosion.
Preventive Programme Integration
  • Standardise gasket/seal specification and stock the correct part for the fixture models in use.
  • Add torque verification for lens/housing fasteners as a mandatory PM step.
  • Flag high-impact zones for more frequent fixture seal inspection.
  • Track fixture-level moisture findings in the CMMS to identify a design or batch pattern early.
Related References

SOP: SOP-PRV-01 (Preventive Maintenance Procedure), SOP-RCL-03 (Fixture Replacement Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-46 (L-852), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

PRV-626 Preventing LED Failure

Failure Mode Addressed — LED light-emitting modules degrading or failing before their rated life due to thermal stress, driver mismatch, or moisture, addressed proactively at the driver-matching and thermal-management stage.

Equipment / System

LED light-emitting modules and their drivers within AGL fixtures.

Contributing Causes
HighDriver supplying current outside the LED module's rated range, accelerating thermal degradation of the junction
HighInadequate heat sinking or restricted airflow at the fixture design/location shortening LED life
MediumVoltage transients or harmonic content from the CCR/driver stressing the LED junction over repeated cycles
MediumMoisture ingress at the optic seal degrading the module from behind the lens
LowA LED module batch with a known manufacturing defect
LowIncorrect LED module colour/type installed, drifting toward the edge of the chromaticity box faster than the correct type
Required Tools / Materials
Driver output current/voltage meterLED module datasheet and thermal derating curveThermal imaging cameraPhotometric test equipmentOptic seal inspection kitLED/driver compatibility reference table
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before removing any LED module or driver.
  • Allow LED driver capacitors to discharge per manufacturer procedure before handling.
Prevention Procedure
  1. BaselineVerify driver-to-LED module compatibility at every installation and at every independent replacement of either component, not assumed to match automatically.
  2. PreventionInclude thermal imaging of a sample of LED fixtures in the periodic PM programme, particularly in warmer climates or high-ambient locations.
  3. PreventionConfirm optic seal integrity at every LED fixture serviced to prevent moisture reaching the module from behind.
  4. PreventionTrend photometric survey results by LED batch/installation cohort to catch early lumen depreciation ahead of a hard failure.
  5. PreventionVerify CCR/driver output quality and correct any confirmed overvoltage or harmonic issue.
  6. VerificationRe-check thermal imaging and photometric output at the next PM/survey cycle to confirm the module remains within normal operating parameters.
Inspection / Monitoring Points
Driver output current/voltage vs. LED module rating
Heat-sink/junction temperature (thermal imaging)
Photometric output trend by batch/cohort
Optic seal condition
LED module batch/date-code
CCR/driver output quality
Effectiveness Verification

Confirm at the next scheduled photometric survey and thermal check that the LED module's output and temperature remain within expected parameters for its hours in service.

If Failure Occurs Despite Prevention
  • Replace a mismatched driver with one correctly rated for the LED module.
  • Replace a LED module confirmed to be from a defective batch.
  • Improve thermal management at the fixture if elevated temperature is confirmed as a contributing factor.
Preventive Programme Integration
  • Capture LED/driver batch and installation date in the CMMS to enable hours-to-failure trending against rated life.
  • Make driver-to-module compatibility verification a mandatory step whenever either component is independently replaced.
  • Add thermal imaging of LED fixtures to the periodic PM programme in high-ambient locations.
  • Trend photometric results by batch, not only by division.

PRV-628 Preventing CCR Overheating

Failure Mode Addressed — A Constant Current Regulator running at an elevated internal or house/pit temperature that stresses power electronics and shortens component life, addressed before it triggers a thermal trip or causes component degradation.

Equipment / System

CCR (transformer-type or TSCR/inverter-type) internal components and its house/pit ventilation and cooling provision.

Contributing Causes
HighRestricted CCR house/pit ventilation from blocked louvres, vegetation growth, or debris accumulation
HighA failing or undersized cooling fan/HVAC unit within the CCR house
MediumCCR operating consistently near its rated capacity for the connected load, generating more heat than a properly derated unit would
MediumElevated ambient temperature at the site beyond the CCR's design assumption, especially in peak summer conditions
LowInternal component degradation (capacitors, SCR/IGBT) increasing losses and heat generation as the unit ages
LowDust/dirt accumulation on heat sinks reducing their effective dissipation area
Required Tools / Materials
Thermal imaging cameraCCR house/pit temperature loggerVentilation/louvre inspection checklistCooling fan/HVAC test procedureHeat sink cleaning kitCCR load vs. rated capacity reference
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the CCR before internal inspection or cleaning.
  • Allow discharge time for output capacitors per manufacturer procedure before handling internal components.
Prevention Procedure
  1. BaselineInclude CCR house/pit ventilation and internal temperature checks in the standard PM checklist for every visit.
  2. PreventionClear louvres, vents, and any vegetation or debris blocking airflow at every PM visit.
  3. PreventionTest cooling fan/HVAC operation and confirm correct thermostat setpoint where fitted.
  4. PreventionClean heat sinks and internal surfaces of dust/dirt accumulation on a defined interval.
  5. PreventionCompare actual CCR loading against rated capacity, flagging any unit consistently operating near its limit for capacity review.
  6. VerificationConfirm via thermal imaging at the next PM visit, ideally during peak-temperature season, that internal temperatures remain within the manufacturer's normal range.
Inspection / Monitoring Points
CCR house/pit internal temperature trend
Ventilation/louvre condition
Cooling fan/HVAC function
Heat sink cleanliness
CCR load vs. rated capacity
Ambient temperature at the site
Effectiveness Verification

Confirm via thermal imaging and temperature log review at the next PM cycle, particularly during the warmest season, that the CCR operates within its normal thermal range with no upward trend.

If Failure Occurs Despite Prevention
  • Clear ventilation blockages and repair or replace a failing cooling fan/HVAC unit promptly.
  • Clean heat sinks if dust accumulation is found to be significantly reducing dissipation.
  • Consider load redistribution or capacity upgrade if the CCR is confirmed to be consistently near its rated limit.
Preventive Programme Integration
  • Make ventilation and cooling-system checks a mandatory, signed-off PM item, not a discretionary one.
  • Trend CCR house/pit temperature over time to catch a developing issue before a thermal trip occurs.
  • Schedule heat sink cleaning on a defined interval appropriate to the site's dust/debris exposure.
  • Include CCR loading review in the periodic asset capacity assessment.

PRV-629 Preventing CCR False Alarms

Failure Mode Addressed — A CCR generating nuisance alarms or trips (overcurrent, earth fault, communication) that do not correspond to a genuine fault, addressed at the settings, calibration, and signal-integrity level before they erode operator confidence in the alarm system.

Equipment / System

CCR protection relays, alarm thresholds, ALCMS/SCADA interface, and associated sensors/wiring.

Contributing Causes
HighProtection relay thresholds set tighter than the circuit's genuine operating tolerance, triggering on normal variation
HighSensor or signal wiring picking up electromagnetic interference from nearby equipment, generating a false reading
MediumALCMS/SCADA software or firmware not updated to the current version, carrying a known alarm-logic bug
MediumLoose or corroded sensor wiring connection causing intermittent false signals
LowA commissioning-stage setting never re-validated after a circuit modification changed the genuine tolerance
LowOperator/maintenance staff resetting and dismissing alarms without logging the pattern, masking a developing genuine issue as "just another false alarm"
Required Tools / Materials
Protection relay settings printout vs. design calculationALCMS/SCADA alarm log with timestamp and typeEMI survey equipment, if warrantedSensor wiring continuity/insulation testerFirmware/software version logAlarm pattern trend chart
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the CCR before any internal relay or wiring inspection.
  • Do not disable or widen alarm thresholds without an engineering review confirming the change is safe.
Prevention Procedure
  1. BaselineVerify protection relay thresholds against the design calculation at commissioning and after any circuit modification, not left at generic factory defaults.
  2. PreventionRoute and shield sensor/signal wiring away from known high-power or high-EMI equipment during installation and rework.
  3. PreventionKeep ALCMS/SCADA firmware and software on the current approved version to avoid known alarm-logic bugs.
  4. PreventionInspect and re-torque sensor wiring connections as part of the periodic PM programme.
  5. PreventionRequire every alarm event, including those dismissed as "false," to be logged with a brief cause note rather than silently reset.
  6. VerificationReview the alarm log at the next PM cycle to confirm the nuisance-alarm rate has genuinely reduced, not just been reset more quickly.
Inspection / Monitoring Points
Protection relay threshold vs. design calculation
Alarm log pattern by type and time
EMI presence near sensor wiring
Sensor wiring connection integrity
Firmware/software version vs. approved baseline
Alarm dismissal/logging discipline
Effectiveness Verification

Confirm via alarm log review at the next PM cycle that the nuisance-alarm frequency has dropped and that every alarm event, once genuinely resolved, is documented with its cause.

If Failure Occurs Despite Prevention
  • Re-calculate and adjust protection thresholds where confirmed tighter than the design tolerance.
  • Re-route or shield wiring where EMI is confirmed as the cause.
  • Update firmware/software where a known bug is implicated.
  • Re-terminate any loose or corroded sensor connection found.
Preventive Programme Integration
  • Require alarm threshold re-validation as a mandatory step after any circuit modification.
  • Maintain firmware/software on a defined update schedule aligned to manufacturer releases.
  • Build a non-punitive culture requiring every alarm to be logged with a cause, even if ultimately deemed non-genuine.

PRV-639 Preventing Connector Installation Errors

Failure Mode Addressed — Connectors being installed with incorrect seating, missing grease, wrong torque, or an incompatible mating type, addressed proactively through standardised procedure, tooling, and verification before the installation itself becomes the root cause of a later fault.

Equipment / System

PRV-640 Preventing Recurring AGL Failures

Failure Mode Addressed — A field-wide, system-level pattern of the same fault types resurfacing across multiple divisions and locations despite individual repairs, addressed at the programme level through FRACAS integration, trend review, and cross-division learning rather than one fault at a time.

Equipment / System

The AGL system as a whole — CCR, series circuits, fixtures, signs, earthing, communications — and the overarching maintenance/quality management programme that governs it.

MediumNo repeat-fault escalation trigger in the CMMS, allowing the same fault type to recur many times across the field before it draws management attention
MediumFragmented record-keeping across divisions/teams preventing a field-wide view of failure patterns
LowLessons learned from one division's RCA not communicated to other divisions facing structurally similar equipment or conditions

Prevention Procedure
  1. BaselineEstablish a periodic (e.g. quarterly) cross-division review of all closed RCA findings to identify field-wide patterns rather than treating each RCA as a closed, isolated item.
  2. PreventionRequire every RCA corrective/preventive action to explicitly identify whether it should be applied field-wide, not only at the specific location investigated.
  3. PreventionFeed confirmed RCA findings into PM checklist, SOP, training curriculum, and design standard updates as a formal, tracked step, not an informal follow-up.

  5. PreventionMaintain a consolidated, searchable record of all RCA findings (this division) so future investigations can quickly check for a previously identified pattern.

If Failure Occurs Despite Prevention
  • Re-open programme-level review for any fault type showing a renewed upward trend despite a prior corrective action.
  • Accelerate feedback into PM/SOP/training where a confirmed pattern has not yet been institutionalised.

  • Formalise the cross-division RCA review as a standing item on the maintenance/quality management calendar.
  • Track RCA-to-action-closure (PM/SOP/training/design update) as a KPI in its own right, separate from individual fault closure.
