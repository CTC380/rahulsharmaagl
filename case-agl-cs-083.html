AGL-CS-083 — Approach Lighting Circuit Fault

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. An approach-lighting circuit fault investigation where primary-cable insulation breakdown produced intermittent earth leakage and unstable CCR operation — from CCR analysis through insulation testing, earth-leakage detection, section isolation, TDR verification, cable replacement, and electrical restoration.

1Case Study Overview

During routine night operations, the approach lighting system began exhibiting abnormal circuit behavior.

Several approach lights flickered intermittently, the CCR output voltage fluctuated, and maintenance personnel observed unstable circuit loading.

Unlike a normal segment failure, the affected lights were not confined to one section, and the circuit condition changed during operation.

A detailed investigation was initiated covering:

  • CCR electrical parameters.
  • Circuit current stability.
  • Output voltage behavior.
  • Insulation resistance.
  • Earth leakage.
  • Cable integrity.
  • Isolating transformers.
  • Circuit impedance.

The investigation identified a deteriorated primary cable with moisture-related insulation breakdown, producing intermittent earth leakage and unstable series-circuit operation.

This case demonstrates that approach-lighting circuit faults are electrical-system problems requiring analysis of the complete series circuit rather than individual light fittings.

3System Background

An ALS circuit consists of:

  • CCR.
  • Series primary cable.
  • Isolating transformers.
  • Secondary circuits.
  • Connectors.
  • Cable joints.
  • Light fittings.
  • Grounding system.

Circuit faults may include:

  • Open circuit.
  • Earth fault.
  • Low insulation resistance.
  • Intermittent cable failure.
  • Water ingress.
  • Connector breakdown.
  • Transformer insulation failure.
  • Cable-joint deterioration.

Circuit performance depends on stable series-current operation.

4Problem Statement

The reported condition was:

  • Intermittent light operation.
  • CCR voltage fluctuation.
  • Stable current not maintained.
  • No complete segment failure.
  • Random operational disturbances.

7Circuit Fault Principle

Healthy circuit → Stable impedance → Stable current → Stable voltage → Reliable illumination
Circuit fault → Impedance variation → Voltage fluctuation → Intermittent operation → Unstable illumination → Operational degradation

Circuit faults affect the electrical behavior of the entire series circuit.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The ALS circuit fault was caused by primary-cable insulation breakdown due to long-term moisture ingress, resulting in:

  • Low insulation resistance.
  • Intermittent earth leakage.
  • Circuit impedance variation.
  • CCR voltage fluctuation.

The primary failure mechanism was deterioration of the buried series cable.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: Approach lights flickering. CCR voltage fluctuating. No single failed segment. Circuit behavior changing with humidity. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace all light fittings.
B. Increase CCR current.
C. Analyze CCR electrical parameters, measure insulation resistance, perform earth-leakage testing, isolate circuit sections, and locate cable deterioration.
D. Replace the transformers.
Correct answer: C. Distributed intermittent failures with CCR instability indicate a system-level electrical fault. Systematic analysis of CCR parameters, insulation resistance, earth leakage, section isolation, and cable-fault localization will identify the cause without unnecessary component replacement.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about approach lighting circuit faults, insulation resistance and earth leakage.

Q1What is AGL-CS-083?

AGL-CS-083 is an engineering training case study. It covers an approach lighting circuit with flickering lights and a fluctuating CCR output voltage caused by a deteriorated buried cable. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2Why did the lights flicker and the CCR voltage fluctuate?

A moisture-damaged primary cable caused intermittent earth leakage, which varied the circuit impedance. The CCR responded to the changing load, so voltage oscillated between 2.1 and 3.4 kV and regulation was unstable.

Q3How low was the insulation resistance?

It measured 0.9 MΩ against an expected value above 50 MΩ, showing severe insulation deterioration.

Q4When did earth leakage appear?

It appeared during high humidity, night operation and full-brightness operation, and varied with environmental conditions.

Q5How was the fault localized?

The circuit was divided into four sections. S1, S2 and S4 read above 100 MΩ, while S3 read 1.2 MΩ. TDR then found an abnormal impedance change 312 m from the reference point, on a buried primary-cable section.

Q6What did excavation find?

Water ingress, outer sheath damage, insulation cracking and partial conductor corrosion.

Q7What was the root cause of AGL-CS-083?

Primary-cable insulation breakdown from long-term moisture ingress, leading to low insulation resistance, intermittent earth leakage, impedance variation and CCR voltage fluctuation.

Q8How was the repair verified?

After replacing the damaged section, insulation resistance rose from 0.9 MΩ to above 200 MΩ, and voltage and current became stable. Light operation returned from intermittent to normal.

Q9What is the key lesson from this case?

Intermittent, changing symptoms call for analysis of the whole series circuit. Insulation resistance, earth-leakage tests and section isolation find faults that fitting replacement would miss.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

Approach-lighting circuit faults are system-level electrical problems caused by insulation deterioration, moisture ingress, earth leakage, and circuit impedance instability, and effective diagnosis requires analysis of CCR electrical behavior, insulation resistance, circuit isolation, and cable-fault localization rather than replacement of individual field components.

Systematic CCR analysis, insulation testing, earth-leakage investigation, section isolation, TDR verification, cable replacement, and post-repair electrical validation provide a complete engineering methodology for restoring stable ALS circuit operation and ICAO-compliant approach-lighting performance.

In AGL engineering, the most important fault is often not the light that is dark, but the cable that is changing the behavior of the entire circuit.
