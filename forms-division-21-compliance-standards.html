Initial Issue

Where this fits — every other division in this library creates evidence — a design calculation, a delivery record, an installation checklist, a test report. Division 21 doesn't duplicate any of that. It's the layer that asks, for every requirement in every applicable standard: which clause, does it apply here, how was it designed for, which product satisfies it, how was installation verified, which test proves it, and what's the final evidence — with the Requirement Traceability Matrix (CMP-18) as the master record tying it all together.

The Control Principle

A statement such as "ICAO compliant," "FAA compliant," or "IEC compliant" is not by itself treated as sufficient evidence. The compliance system is built to answer, for every requirement: which clause? does it apply to this project? how was it addressed in the design? which product or material satisfies it? how was installation verified? which test proves compliance? which inspection confirms the installed condition? was any deviation approved? and what is the final evidence? That chain — not the label — is what CMP-18, the Requirement Traceability Matrix, exists to hold together.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about the 25 Division 21 compliance and standards forms, how they are grouped, and how they build a traceable compliance record.

Q1What is Division 21 — Compliance & Standards Forms in the AGL Engineering Forms & Templates library?

Division 21 is a set of 25 compliance and standards forms (CMP-01 to CMP-25) that connect and validate the evidence created across the AGL Engineering Documentation System. They map ICAO, FAA, IEC, BIS/IS, DGCA, project-specification and tender requirements through design, procurement, installation, testing, inspection and maintenance to a documented final acceptance. The forms are grouped into seven categories.

Q2Why is a statement such as “ICAO compliant” not treated as enough evidence?

The division treats a label such as “ICAO compliant”, “FAA compliant” or “IEC compliant” as a claim, not as proof. For every requirement the forms ask which clause applies, whether it applies to this project, how it was addressed in design, which product or material satisfies it, how installation was verified, which test proves it, which inspection confirms the installed condition, whether any deviation was approved, and what the final evidence is. The Requirement Traceability Matrix (CMP-18) holds that chain together.

Q3Which checklists cover ICAO, FAA, IEC, BIS/IS and DGCA requirements?

The Compliance & Requirement Control category has six forms: Technical Compliance Matrix (CMP-01), ICAO Compliance Checklist (CMP-02), FAA Compliance Checklist (CMP-03), IEC Compliance Checklist (CMP-04), BIS/IS Compliance Checklist (CMP-05) and DGCA Requirement Checklist (CMP-06). The ICAO checklist reviews the Annex 14 provisions affecting the project against a specific document and edition rather than a generic “ICAO compliant” claim.

Q4How do the project, tender, product and material compliance forms differ?

Project Specification Compliance (CMP-07) traces each specification clause through the design, equipment, installation and testing response to field evidence. Tender Compliance Matrix (CMP-08) checks compliance against the tender, BOQ and technical specification, flagging deviations and alternative proposals. Product Compliance Certificate (CMP-09) documents that a proposed product satisfies the applicable standards before it is approved for use, and Material Compliance Certificate (CMP-10) verifies that supplied materials conform to approved technical requirements before they are accepted onto site.

Q5What is the Requirement Traceability Matrix (CMP-18) used for?

The Requirement Traceability Matrix is the master link between every significant requirement and the evidence proving compliance, running from the requirement through design, procurement, installation, testing and inspection to final acceptance. Its key fields include the requirement ID, source and clause, design response and equipment, installation, test and inspection evidence, final evidence and compliance status. It is one of the most important forms in the division.

Q6How are technical deviations requested and tracked?

A Deviation Request (CMP-19) formally asks for approval to deviate from an applicable requirement, with technical justification, risk and impact assessed before work proceeds. The Technical Deviation Register (CMP-20) is the master register of approved, rejected and pending deviations across design, material, installation, testing and operational categories, including approval authority, expiry or review date and closure status.

Q7What is the difference between the Standards Reference Register, the Applicable Standards Register and the Standards Revision Tracker?

The Standards Reference Register (CMP-16) is the controlled list of standards referenced by the project, with edition and amendment. The Applicable Standards Register (CMP-17) separates standards that merely exist from those actually applicable to the project, because a project should not claim compliance with every standard in the library. The Standards Revision Tracker (CMP-23) monitors revisions and assesses whether a new edition is applicable to an existing project before it is treated as a requirement.

Q8Which forms handle compliance reviews, gap analysis, audit and final certification?

Stage reviews are covered by the Design, Installation, Testing, Maintenance and Inspection Compliance Reviews (CMP-11 to CMP-15). Compliance Gap Analysis (CMP-21) identifies where current conditions do not satisfy requirements, Compliance Review Record (CMP-22) records periodic or stage reviews, and Compliance Audit Checklist (CMP-24) performs a systematic audit. The Final Compliance Certificate (CMP-25) is the formal declaration that the completed system was reviewed against every applicable requirement, standard, specification and approved deviation, with a final status of fully compliant, compliant with approved deviations, or not compliant.

Q9Can the Division 21 forms be downloaded, and how does this division connect to the others?

Yes. Each entry has a Download as Word button that produces an uncontrolled sample Word template with letterhead, a document-control block, ruled entry fields and a three-tier approval table (Prepared By, Reviewed By, Approved By) as layout only, to be adapted under your own document-control system before use. Division 21 does not duplicate evidence created elsewhere: it draws design evidence from Division 02, product and material evidence from Division 03, installation evidence from Division 04, test evidence from Division 06, photometric evidence from Division 15 and control-system compliance from Division 19, and ties them together in the master compliance record.
