Introduction

AGL Driver / Ballast

What It Does

Drivers and ballasts regulate the electrical operation of compatible airfield light sources and are important replacement components during maintenance. The AGL Driver / Ballast range supports compatible fixtures, LED retrofit applications and legacy lighting systems where the electrical interface requires replacement or refurbishment.

Selection

Select the correct driver or ballast according to the fixture model, light source, input supply and existing electrical configuration. Compatibility is particularly important when upgrading conventional fixtures to LED technology.

LED Driver & Ballast Applications

Match the Electrical Interface to the Light Source

LED Driver Applications

LED light sources need an appropriate electrical interface for controlled operation. The driver supplies the conditions required by the compatible LED module or fixture and must be matched to the LED light source, not selected solely by physical dimensions. For retrofits, evaluate it together with:

  • LED retrofit module
  • Existing fixture
  • Input supply
  • Internal wiring
  • Connector arrangement
  • Protection requirements
  • Monitoring or control system
  • Circuit type: series (constant-current) or parallel (constant-voltage)

On a series circuit, the driver must work from the transformer secondary current at every CCR brightness step, hold stable output across the steps, and present a load the CCR can regulate. LED loads change the circuit power factor and load profile, so confirm the CCR rating and the number of fixtures per circuit.

An unsuitable driver can affect LED operation, reliability and overall fixture performance.

Ballast Applications

Ballasts are associated with discharge or fluorescent sources in legacy equipment. Halogen lamps in series-circuit fixtures normally run directly from the isolating transformer secondary and have no ballast. Replacement should consider the original fixture design and lamp characteristics, and be compatible with:

  • Existing lamp type
  • Lamp rating
  • Fixture electrical arrangement
  • Input supply
  • Internal wiring
  • Connection system
  • Available installation space

For systems converting to LED, the existing ballast may need to be replaced or removed as part of the approved retrofit arrangement.

Protection Features

Airfield equipment operates in demanding outdoor environments. Depending on the specific driver or ballast, protection may include suitable measures for:

  • Electrical overload
  • Short-circuit conditions
  • Thermal conditions
  • Input disturbances and surges
  • Open-circuit or secondary-side faults
  • Abnormal operating conditions

Consider protection as part of the complete fixture electrical design rather than a standalone component specification.

Mechanical Installation & Thermal

Secure Mounting and Heat Dissipation

Mechanical Installation

Install securely using the mounting arrangement specified for the compatible fixture. Considerations:

  • Available mounting space
  • Fixing points
  • Clearance from other components
  • Cable routing
  • Connector access
  • Heat dissipation
  • Service accessibility
  • Environmental protection

The installation should not interfere with optical components, moving parts, covers, seals or other fixture assemblies.

Thermal Characteristics

Thermal performance matters for both LED drivers and conventional ballasts. Install within the specified operating conditions, with adequate clearance for heat dissipation where required.

For retrofits, review the thermal conditions inside the existing fixture, as the original enclosure may have been designed around a different light source and electrical component.

Replacement & Legacy Systems

Lifecycle Maintenance

Replacement and Lifecycle

Drivers and ballasts are replaceable components and may require renewal during a fixture's service life, because of:

  • Component failure
  • Electrical degradation
  • Obsolescence
  • Fixture refurbishment
  • LED conversion
  • Availability of replacement components
  • Planned lifecycle maintenance
  • Damage during maintenance

Where the fixture remains suitable, replacing the driver or ballast can be a controlled maintenance solution without replacing the complete fixture.

Legacy Lighting Systems

Existing installations may contain fixtures based on conventional light sources and older electrical arrangements. Base replacement selection on the existing fixture configuration rather than assuming compatibility with a current-generation component.

Where legacy components are no longer available, the preferred route may be a compatible retrofit module or a complete fixture upgrade.

Installation Considerations

Before Replacing a Driver or Ballast

  • Fixture model
  • Existing component model
  • Light-source type
  • Electrical supply
  • Driver or ballast characteristics
  • Connector arrangement
  • Mounting arrangement
  • Environmental conditions
  • Available replacement documentation

After installation, check the fixture for correct electrical operation at every brightness step, and confirm that the light output still meets the required photometric performance. Isolate and lock off the CCR before opening any series circuit; never open an energised series circuit.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures
Standards & Project Requirements

Fixture and Project Requirements Take Precedence

Reference documents: FAA AC 150/5345-46F (light fixture specification), AC 150/5345-47C (isolating transformers), AC 150/5345-53D (equipment certification), FAA Engineering Brief 67 (non-incandescent light sources), ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I and Doc 9157 Parts 4 and 5. Verify the current edition of each before procurement.

Driver and ballast selection should follow the requirements of the compatible fixture, approved retrofit configuration, airport specifications and applicable project requirements.

Where the airfield authority or project documentation specifies a particular component, electrical interface or replacement procedure, those requirements take precedence. For retrofit projects, evaluate the driver as part of the complete fixture conversion rather than as an independent replacement component.

FAQ

AGL Driver / Ballast — Frequently Asked Questions

What does an AGL driver or ballast do?

Drivers and ballasts regulate the electrical operation of compatible airfield light sources and are important replacement components during maintenance. The range supports compatible fixtures, LED retrofit applications and legacy lighting systems where the electrical interface needs replacement or refurbishment. Selection depends on the fixture model, light source, input supply and existing electrical configuration.

What is the difference between an LED driver and a ballast?

An LED driver supplies the conditions an LED module or fixture needs for controlled operation and must be matched to the LED light source, not selected only by physical dimensions. Ballasts are associated with discharge or fluorescent sources in legacy equipment. Halogen lamps in series-circuit fixtures normally run directly from the isolating transformer secondary and have no ballast. When a system converts to LED, the existing ballast may need to be replaced or removed as part of the approved retrofit arrangement.

How do I select the correct replacement driver or ballast?

Fixture compatibility is the primary consideration. Where available, provide the fixture manufacturer and model, the existing light source, the existing driver or ballast model, input voltage, frequency, output characteristics, connector type, physical dimensions, the internal mounting arrangement and the fixture application. This information helps identify a suitable replacement and reduces the risk of installing an incompatible component.

What matters when a driver is used on a series circuit?

On a series circuit the driver must work from the transformer secondary current at every CCR brightness step, hold a stable output across the steps and present a load the CCR can regulate. LED loads change the circuit power factor and load profile, so confirm the CCR rating and the number of fixtures per circuit. An unsuitable driver can affect LED operation, reliability and overall fixture performance.

Which supply, frequency and protection features should be checked?

The frequency should be compatible with the applicable supply, 50 Hz or 60 Hz, and the circuit type is series (constant-current, through an isolating transformer) or parallel (constant-voltage). Output must follow the CCR or dimmer brightness steps used on the circuit. Depending on the specific unit, protection may cover electrical overload, short-circuit conditions, thermal conditions, input disturbances and surges, open-circuit or secondary-side faults and abnormal operating conditions. Confirm everything against the fixture manufacturer’s requirements or the approved retrofit configuration before installation.

What connector and mounting checks are needed?

Check the connector type, pin arrangement, cable size, polarity where applicable, electrical rating, mechanical fit and environmental protection. If the existing connector is damaged, obsolete or incompatible, a Replacement Connector Kit may be required. Install securely using the mounting arrangement specified for the fixture, allowing for fixing points, clearance, cable routing, connector access, heat dissipation and service accessibility, without interfering with optical components, covers or seals.

How does the driver fit into an LED retrofit?

During an LED retrofit the driver is part of the complete conversion: the existing fixture, the LED retrofit module, the driver and the electrical supply must all be compatible as a system. Replacing only one component without checking the complete electrical interface can result in incorrect operation or an unsuitable installation. Review the existing fixture model and driver information before selecting replacement components, and check the thermal conditions inside the fixture, because the original enclosure may have been designed around a different light source.

When do drivers and ballasts need replacing?

They are replaceable components and may need renewal because of component failure, electrical degradation, obsolescence, fixture refurbishment, LED conversion, availability of replacement parts, planned lifecycle maintenance or damage during maintenance. Where the fixture remains suitable, replacing the driver or ballast can be a controlled maintenance solution without replacing the complete fixture. For legacy systems, base the selection on the existing fixture configuration, and where legacy parts are no longer available the preferred route may be a compatible retrofit module or a complete fixture upgrade.

What should be checked after replacing or inspecting a driver or ballast?

After installation, check the fixture for correct electrical operation at every brightness step and confirm that the light output still meets the required photometric performance. Routine inspection should include the physical condition, mounting security, electrical connections, connector condition, signs of overheating, moisture ingress, abnormal noise or behaviour, damage to wiring or insulation and compatibility with the installed light source. Any component showing damage, overheating or abnormal operation should be assessed before continued service.

Which standards and requirements apply to drivers and ballasts?

Selection should follow the requirements of the compatible fixture, the approved retrofit configuration, airport specifications and applicable project requirements, and where the airfield authority or project documents specify a component, interface or procedure, those take precedence. Reference documents include FAA AC 150/5345-46F (light fixtures), AC 150/5345-47C (isolating transformers), AC 150/5345-53D (equipment certification), FAA Engineering Brief 67 (non-incandescent light sources), ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I and Doc 9157 Parts 4 and 5. Verify the current edition of each before procurement.

Is it safe to replace a driver or ballast while the series circuit is energised?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage, and opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death. Isolate and lock off the CCR first, apply the site lock-out / tag-out procedure, use the series circuit cut-out switch or an approved isolation device and work under the airport’s permit-to-work system. See the electrical safety warning on this page.
