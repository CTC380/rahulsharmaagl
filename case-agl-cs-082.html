AGL-CS-082 — Multiple Approach Lights Not Working

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A multi-fault approach lighting investigation where scattered individual light failures revealed independent field defects — from CCR verification through transformer testing, connector inspection, secondary-circuit analysis, insulation assessment, and systematic field repair.

1Case Study Overview

During a routine night inspection, air traffic control reported that multiple approach lights were not working, but the failure pattern was unusual.

Instead of one complete dark segment, individual approach lights were inoperative at several different locations across the approach lighting system.

Pilots reported that the approach pattern appeared irregular, with dark gaps between operating lights.

The Constant Current Regulator (CCR) remained healthy, circuit current was maintained, and no major circuit trip occurred.

A detailed investigation was initiated covering isolating transformers, secondary cables, connectors, water ingress, insulation resistance, maintenance history, and failure-pattern analysis.

The investigation identified multiple independent field faults involving connectors, transformer secondary circuits, and water-related cable deterioration, rather than a single primary-circuit failure.

This case demonstrates that multiple scattered approach-light failures require systematic individual fault diagnosis and cannot be treated as a single segment failure.

3System Background

An approach-light circuit includes:

  • Primary series cable.
  • Isolating transformers.
  • Secondary cables.
  • L-823 connectors.
  • Light fittings.
  • Cable joints.
  • Junction interfaces.

Multiple scattered failures may result from:

  • Connector failures.
  • Transformer secondary failures.
  • Water ingress.
  • Secondary-cable damage.
  • Optical-module failures.
  • Seal deterioration.
  • Mechanical damage.
  • Aging infrastructure.

Scattered failures usually indicate multiple localized defects.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The multiple approach-light failures were caused by several independent field defects, including:

  • Connector corrosion.
  • Loose connector termination.
  • Transformer secondary failure.
  • Secondary-cable open circuit.
  • Water-ingress-related insulation deterioration.

The CCR and primary circuit remained healthy.

The primary failure mechanism was multiple localized component failures.

11Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Replaced failed connectors.
  2. Repaired secondary cables.
  3. Replaced the defective transformer.
  4. Removed water-damaged cable sections.
  5. Improved connector sealing.
  6. Performed insulation testing.
  7. Verified continuity.
  8. Energized the circuit.
  9. Verified all approach lights.
  10. Updated reliability records.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: Four approach lights OFF. Failures at widely separated locations. CCR healthy. Primary circuit healthy. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace the CCR.
B. Replace the primary cable.
C. Perform individual investigation of each failed location, including transformer testing, connector inspection, secondary-cable continuity, insulation testing, and maintenance-history review.
D. Increase circuit current.
Correct answer: C. Scattered failures with a healthy primary circuit indicate independent field defects. Systematic investigation of each failed location will identify the multiple causes without unnecessary equipment replacement.

Quick Reference: Approach Lighting Working Principle

The approach lighting working principle is to give the pilot a pattern of lights along the extended runway centreline that can be seen in poor visibility. The pilot uses the pattern to line up with the runway and judge the roll and distance. Because the lights work as a pattern, several lights being out at once, as in this case, can change what the pilot sees.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about scattered approach-light failures and independent field faults.

Q1What is AGL-CS-082?

AGL-CS-082 is an engineering training case study. It covers multiple approach lights out at scattered, non-consecutive locations while the CCR and primary circuit stayed healthy. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2How does a scattered failure pattern differ from a segment failure?

A segment failure is one dark run caused by a common circuit interruption. Scattered dark lights with a healthy primary circuit usually mean several independent field defects, each needing its own diagnosis.

Q3What did the checks on the primary circuit show?

The CCR was healthy, the primary circuit was continuous with no major voltage interruption, and no segment was isolated.

Q4What faults were found at each location?

A3 had connector corrosion and moisture. A11 had a loose connector with carbon tracking. A22 had a transformer secondary fault and an open secondary cable. A34 had water ingress and insulation damage.

Q5What insulation resistance values were measured?

A3 measured 18 MΩ, A11 measured 42 MΩ, A22 was open and A34 measured 0.8 MΩ, which showed severe moisture-related deterioration.

Q6Did maintenance history help?

Yes. Records showed connector replacements at A3 and A11, water-ingress history at A34 and repeated transformer maintenance at A22, consistent with known local reliability problems.

Q7What was the root cause of AGL-CS-082?

Several independent defects: connector corrosion, a loose termination, a transformer secondary failure, an open secondary cable and water-related insulation deterioration.

Q8How were the faults corrected?

The team replaced failed connectors and the defective transformer, repaired secondary cables and removed water-damaged cable sections. They also improved connector sealing, tested insulation, verified continuity, verified all approach lights and updated reliability records.

Q9What is the key lesson from this case?

Do not treat scattered failures as one segment fault. Confirm the primary circuit is healthy, then test each failed location individually.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

Multiple approach lights not working at scattered locations usually indicate independent localized field defects involving connectors, transformer secondary circuits, secondary cables, and water-related deterioration, and systematic investigation of each failed location is essential for accurate diagnosis and complete restoration of approach-light reliability.

Systematic failure-pattern analysis, transformer testing, connector inspection, secondary-circuit continuity testing, insulation assessment, and maintenance-history evaluation can efficiently identify multiple independent faults and restore ICAO-compliant approach-light performance.

In AGL engineering, a scattered pattern of dark lights is usually not one failure repeated; it is several failures revealing the reliability of the field system itself.
