AGL Inspection Handbook

A division-wise reference for what to inspect on an installed AGL system — not how to install or repair it. Every division answers one question: for this asset, what must be verified, against what standard, and how is the result classified? Built around a five-level inspection hierarchy, from daily walk-arounds to full airport-wide condition audits.

All 25 divisions are now live, covering 1,173 published inspection topics across the full AGL asset base.

How this differs from the SOP Library: the SOP Library documents procedure — how to install, test, or repair. This handbook documents inspection — what to verify on equipment that is already installed, how to compare it against tolerance, and how to classify and record what's found. Philosophy: observe → verify → compare → classify → record → action.

Five-Level Inspection Hierarchy

Every inspection topic in the handbook sits at one of five levels, from routine daily checks through to a complete airport-wide AGL audit. The level sets the frequency, the trigger, and how deep the inspection goes.

Defect Classification

Every finding is classified consistently across all 25 divisions, so severity means the same thing whether it's a runway light or a CCR cabinet.

Critical

Directly affects flight safety or immediate serviceability. Requires immediate action.

Major

Significant deterioration or non-conformance that must be corrected within a defined short window.

Minor

Does not affect operational serviceability but should be tracked and scheduled for correction.

Observation

Noted for awareness and trend tracking — no immediate corrective action required.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about the AGL Inspection Handbook, its 25 divisions and how inspection topics are structured.

Q1What is the AGL Inspection Handbook?

It is a division-wise inspection reference for installed AGL systems, with 1,173 published inspection topics across 25 divisions. It documents what must be verified on an asset that is already in service, against what standard, and how the result is classified — not how to install or repair it.

Q2How does the Inspection Handbook differ from the SOP Library?

The SOP Library documents procedure: how to install, test or repair. The Inspection Handbook documents inspection: what to verify on equipment that is already installed, how to compare it against tolerance, and how to classify and record what is found.

Q3What is the five-level inspection hierarchy?

Every topic sits at one of five levels: routine inspection (daily, shift or weekly), detailed equipment inspection (monthly, quarterly or annual), special inspection (event-triggered, such as after failure, lightning, flooding, major maintenance, construction, an incident or severe weather), acceptance inspection (milestone-based, for new installation, replacement, modification or commissioning), and AGL system audit (an airport-wide assessment).

Q4How is each inspection topic structured?

Each topic follows the same six-step logic: observe, verify, compare, classify, record and action. A topic sets out the inspection points checked on equipment in service, each with a defined pass condition, such as position, alignment, height, lens, housing, mounting, cable, connector, illumination and surrounding area.

Q5How are inspection findings classified?

The handbook uses four defect categories: Critical, which directly affects flight safety or immediate serviceability and requires immediate action; Major, a significant deterioration or non-conformance to be corrected within a defined short window; Minor, which does not affect operational serviceability but should be tracked and scheduled; and Observation, noted for awareness and trend tracking. Individual division pages also define the status scale they record against.

Q6Which divisions does the Inspection Handbook include?

It has 25 divisions covering the full AGL asset base, from daily airfield inspection (Division 01) through runway, taxiway and approach lighting, PAPI/APAPI, signs, CCRs, transformers, cables, connectors, electrical safety, civil works, earthing, ALCMS and fixtures, to stop bars, obstruction lighting, FOD and environmental conditions, electrical rooms, testing and calibration, preventive maintenance, post-maintenance, pre-commissioning, documentation and compliance, and the airport-wide audit in Division 25.

Q7Can I download printable checklists from the handbook?

Yes. Each inspection topic on the division pages has a Download as Word option that produces an uncontrolled Word checklist template (description, a tick-box inspection table and an inspector sign-off block) for personal reference and internal training, to be reviewed and adopted under the user’s own document control.

Q8Do airport procedures take precedence over the Inspection Handbook?

Yes. The handbook is an uncontrolled educational reference, not an approved procedure. The airport's approved inspection schedule, SMS procedures, ATC instructions, NOTAM procedures and local aerodrome regulations always take precedence, and the current revision of any official document should be verified before use.
