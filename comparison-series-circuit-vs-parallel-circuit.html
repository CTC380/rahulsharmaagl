Electrical & Power Systems Comparison

Series Circuit VS Parallel Circuit

A series circuit connects loads one after another in a single current path, so the same current flows through every element. A parallel circuit connects loads across the same supply through multiple branches, so each branch sees approximately the same voltage. The typical AGL lighting circuit is series, fed by a Constant Current Regulator (CCR); conventional building lighting is typically parallel, fed by a constant-voltage supply.

02 · Definitions

What Is a Series Circuit & a Parallel Circuit?

Series Circuit

A series circuit has one continuous current path, with loads connected one after another. The same current flows through L1, L2 and L3 in turn — I → [L1] → [L2] → [L3] → I.

Main characteristics

  • Single current path through all loads
  • Same current at every point in the circuit
  • Supply voltage is divided among the loads
  • Typical architecture for AGL lighting circuits

Parallel Circuit

A parallel circuit has multiple branches, each connected across the same supply. Each load therefore sees approximately the same supply voltage.

Main characteristics

  • Multiple independent current paths
  • Same voltage across every branch
  • Total current is the sum of the branch currents
  • Typical architecture for conventional building lighting
03 · Key Difference

The Biggest Difference

The easiest way to remember the difference between a series circuit and a parallel circuit:

Series Circuit

One current path → same current

Parallel Circuit

Multiple current paths → same voltage

Easy memory: Series → same current. Parallel → same voltage.

04 · Circuit Topology

Series vs Parallel Architecture

Series — AGL Circuit

CCR (CONSTANT CURRENT)
BACK TO CCR

Fixtures are wired one after another via isolation transformers; the CCR regulates the single series current shared by the whole circuit.

Parallel — Conventional Circuit

LV SUPPLY (CONSTANT V)

Each lamp is connected directly across the supply as an independent branch, each seeing approximately the same supply voltage.

05 · Why AGL Uses a Series Circuit

Series-Connected Runway & Approach Circuits

The AGL series architecture allows a CCR to regulate the lighting circuit current and thereby provide controlled light output across a large number of fixtures distributed along a long route — a runway edge row, a taxiway row, or an approach lighting run. Isolation transformers interface each individual fixture with the series circuit.

Simplified architecture: CCR → Series Circuit → Isolation Transformers → Individual Light Fixtures. The CCR controls the circuit current; the transformers interface the constant-current series circuit with each individual lighting unit.

06 · Conventional Parallel Lighting

How Conventional Lighting Is Typically Wired

A conventional lighting system is typically arranged with each lamp connected across the supply. If the supply is 230 V, for example, each branch is approximately across the 230 V supply, subject to system conditions. Switching or failure of one branch normally does not remove power from the other branches.

08 · What Happens if One Load Fails?

Effect of an Open-Circuit Fault

Condition Series Circuit Parallel Circuit
One load opensA basic series string is interrupted, but an AGL series circuit's isolation transformers are specifically designed to maintain circuit continuity when a lamp/fixture fails openOther branches can normally continue operating
Practical consequenceAn open circuit in the series cable/loop itself (e.g. a cable break) can still interrupt the whole circuit, so monitoring and fault-detection remain importantFailure is usually isolated to that branch

An individual lamp or fixture failing open on an AGL series circuit is not, by itself, expected to interrupt the loop — that continuity is exactly what the isolation transformer is there to preserve (per IEC 61823 and equivalent equipment standards). What can interrupt the whole series circuit is a fault in the series cable or transformer primary itself, which is why circuit monitoring still matters, just not for the reason a simple series-circuit diagram might suggest.

09 · Adding Loads

Effect of Adding an Additional Load

Series

Adding another load increases the total series impedance. In a conventional voltage-source series circuit this affects current and voltage distribution; in AGL, the CCR adjusts/maintains the selected series current, while the required circuit voltage depends on the total circuit characteristics.

Parallel

Adding another branch increases the total current demanded from the constant-voltage supply. Each existing branch continues to see approximately the same supply voltage.

10 · Voltage Behaviour

How Voltage Is Distributed

Series

Supply voltage divides among the series elements (V1, V2, V3…)

Parallel

Branches share the same supply voltage (V1 = V2 = V3)

12 · Advantages

Where Each Architecture Wins

Advantages of Series

  • Same current guaranteed through every fixture on the circuit
  • Well suited to a CCR regulating a large number of distributed loads
  • Consistent light output regardless of circuit length, when correctly regulated
  • Isolation transformers keep an individual lamp/fixture failure from interrupting the rest of the circuit
  • Standard architecture for long, distributed AGL circuits

Advantages of Parallel

  • Each load receives the system voltage independently
  • One branch failing normally does not affect the others
  • Simple to add or remove independent branches
  • Very familiar arrangement for general LV lighting
13 · Do Not Confuse

Series Circuit vs a Simple Chain of Switches

A series lighting circuit is not just loads wired end to end. On AGL circuits, isolation transformers interface each fixture with the series circuit, and a CCR actively regulates the circuit current — the arrangement is a regulated current loop, not simply a chain of unregulated loads.

15 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: If a fixture fails on an AGL series circuit, the whole runway goes dark
Fact

The isolation transformer arrangement, combined with circuit monitoring, is intended to keep the series circuit intact even when an individual fixture develops a fault — an open circuit is still a risk the design guards against, not an inevitable outcome.

Myth: Parallel branches never affect one another
Fact

Adding branches still increases the total current drawn from the supply, and supply/cable sizing must account for this — parallel branches are independent in operation but not entirely independent in their effect on the source.

16 · Engineering Selection

Choosing Between Series and Parallel

When selecting or interpreting a lighting circuit's topology, consider:

  • Whether the application requires guaranteed uniform current (series) or independent branch operation (parallel)
  • Total circuit length and number of light positions
  • Applicable ICAO/national AGL requirements for the primary lighting circuit
  • Isolation transformer ratings and losses, for series AGL circuits
  • Cable resistance and voltage drop over circuit length
  • Consequence of a single load/branch failure for the application
  • Protection, control and monitoring requirements
  • Airport-specific technical specifications
17 · Engineering Verdict

Which Architecture Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

A series circuit gives every connected load the same current and is the typical architecture for AGL lighting, fed by a CCR through isolation transformers. A parallel circuit gives every branch the same supply voltage and is the typical architecture for conventional building lighting, fed by a constant-voltage supply.

The final selection for an actual airport project should always be based on the applicable current ICAO/national AGL standard, the approved circuit design and equipment requirements — not on a generic comparison alone.

SeriesSame current through all loads — suits AGL circuits fed by a CCR.

18 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between a series and a parallel circuit?

In a series circuit, one current path means the same current flows through every load. In a parallel circuit, multiple branches share the same supply voltage while the total current splits between them.

Why is AGL lighting typically wired in series?

A series arrangement allows a single CCR to regulate the current through a large number of distributed fixtures, giving controlled and consistent light output along a long circuit such as a runway edge row.

What happens if one fixture fails on a series AGL circuit?

Normally, nothing beyond that fixture: the isolation transformer is specifically designed to keep the series loop intact when a lamp or fixture fails open, so the rest of the circuit stays lit. What can interrupt the whole series circuit is a fault in the cable or loop itself, not an individual lamp failure — that's the main risk circuit monitoring and fault-detection are there to catch.

What happens if one lamp fails on a parallel circuit?

The other parallel branches can normally continue operating, since each branch is an independent current path across the supply.

Why does conventional building lighting use a parallel arrangement?

Parallel distribution is convenient because each load receives the system voltage independently, and switching or failure of one branch normally does not remove power from the others.

What role do isolation transformers play in an AGL series circuit?

The CCR regulates the series-circuit current, while individual isolation transformers interface that circuit with each individual lighting unit, allowing the series current regulation to be maintained fixture by fixture.

How does adding a load affect each circuit type?

Adding a series load changes the total series impedance, and in AGL the CCR adjusts to maintain the selected current. Adding a parallel branch mainly increases the total current demanded from the supply.
