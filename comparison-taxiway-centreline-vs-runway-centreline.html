Centreline Lighting Comparison

Taxiway Centreline VS Runway Centreline

Taxi Route vs Runway Alignment — the green taxiway centreline light guides an aircraft along its intended taxi path, while the white runway centreline light provides the alignment and directional reference during takeoff and landing. Both sit along a centreline, both are commonly inset, but they operate in different phases of flight and answer different pilot questions.

02 · Definitions

What Is a Taxiway Centreline & Runway Centreline Light?

Taxiway Centreline Light

Provides a continuous visual reference along the intended taxi path. The pilot uses it to answer: "Where should I taxi?"

Provides a reference for

  • The designated taxi route
  • Steering guidance while taxiing
  • Tracking through curves and intersections
  • Route guidance at night and in reduced visibility

Runway Centreline Light

Provides a visual reference along the longitudinal centre of the runway. The pilot uses it to answer: "Am I aligned with the runway?"

Provides a reference for

  • Runway alignment and positional guidance
  • Precise tracking during takeoff roll
  • Precise tracking during landing rollout
  • Runway reference in low-visibility operations
03 · Biggest Difference: Colour & Phase

Green + Taxi vs White + Runway

The fastest way to tell them apart, day or night, is colour combined with the phase of flight they belong to: green centreline → taxi, white centreline → takeoff/landing.

Green

White
Runway Centreline

Runway centreline lighting may incorporate other colours in specific portions of the runway, depending on the applicable standard and runway configuration — the green taxiway centreline does not carry that same colour transition.

04 · Side by Side

How They Sit Between the Edges

Edge Centreline Edge

On a taxiway, the centreline lies between blue edge lights and glows green. On a runway, the centreline lies between the runway edge lights and glows white — in both cases the centreline is the single line the pilot tracks, while the edge lights bound it on either side.

05 · Pilot Perspective

What the Pilot Sees, Phase by Phase

While Taxiing

The green centreline tells the pilot where the aircraft should track along the taxi route — the working instruction is simply "follow the green centreline."

During Takeoff / Landing

The white centreline provides a runway alignment reference — the working instruction is "stay on the runway centre" through the takeoff roll or landing rollout.

The simplest way to remember it: the taxiway centreline answers "where do I go next?" while the runway centreline answers "am I straight?" One is a route to follow; the other is a precise alignment reference.

06 · Installation

Both Are Commonly Inset

Both systems commonly use inset fixtures because aircraft can pass directly over the centreline. The physical installation is similar in principle, but the function and operating environment differ — one guides ground movement, the other guides a high-speed, precision-critical phase of flight.

07 · Curves & Intersections

Where Each System Provides Guidance

On Curves & at Intersections

Taxiway centreline lighting is particularly important on curves and can guide an aircraft through complex taxiway intersections, helping the pilot identify the intended taxi route.

Straight-Line Reference

Runway centrelines are generally straight along the runway's longitudinal axis. Runway centreline lighting is not primarily intended to function as an intersection-navigation system.

08 · Low-Visibility Operations

Which Matters More When Visibility Drops?

Both systems become especially valuable when visual references are reduced, but for different reasons. The taxiway centreline provides visual guidance while taxiing, especially on curves and at intersections. The runway centreline provides an important runway reference during takeoff and landing, when precise alignment matters most.

Easy memory: green centreline = taxi route in low visibility; white centreline = runway alignment in low visibility. Neither substitutes for the other — a pilot moves from one system to the other as the aircraft transitions between taxiway and runway.

09 · Relationship With Edge Lights

Centreline + Edge, Together

Taxiway

Blue lights mark the taxiway edge; green lights mark the centreline — edge defines the limit, centreline defines the path within it.

Runway

Runway edge lights mark the runway limit; the runway centreline marks runway alignment — edge defines the boundary, centreline defines the tracking reference.

12 · Myth vs Fact

Common Colour Confusion

Myth: Both centrelines do the same job because they're both "centreline lights"
Fact

They serve different purposes tied to different phases of flight — the taxiway centreline is a route reference for ground movement, while the runway centreline is a precision alignment reference for takeoff and landing.

Myth: The colour change never matters once you're past the threshold
Fact

Runway centreline lighting may incorporate other colours in specific portions of the runway depending on the applicable standard and runway configuration — the colour scheme itself can carry positional meaning.

Myth: A green light near a runway is always a runway light
Fact

Green is characteristic of taxiway centreline lighting, not runway centreline lighting. Runway centreline is normally white, so a green centreline line indicates a taxiway reference, not a runway one.

13 · Engineering Verdict

Taxiway Centreline vs Runway Centreline

Engineering Verdict

Taxiway and runway centreline lights are complementary systems tied to different phases of flight, not interchangeable variants of the same fixture. One tells the pilot where to taxi; the other tells the pilot whether the aircraft is aligned with the runway.

Together with their respective edge lighting, each centreline system gives the pilot a continuous longitudinal reference — green for the taxi route, white for the runway — so the aircraft is never without a clear line to follow, on the ground or during takeoff and landing.

Taxiway CLRoute guidance + taxi phase + "follow this path."
Runway CLAlignment reference + takeoff/landing phase + "stay on the centre."

Easy memory: Green + centreline → taxi. White + centreline → runway.

14 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between a taxiway centreline and a runway centreline light?

Colour, function and phase of flight — the taxiway centreline is green and guides taxi route navigation, while the runway centreline is white and provides runway alignment during takeoff and landing.

Why is the taxiway centreline green while the runway centreline is white?

The colour distinction lets a pilot instantly identify which system — and which phase of operation — the centreline belongs to, even at night or in reduced visibility.

Does the runway centreline ever change colour?

Runway centreline lighting may incorporate other colours in specific portions of the runway, depending on the applicable standard and runway configuration.

Why are both types of centreline light usually inset rather than elevated?

Because aircraft can pass directly over the centreline in both cases — an inset fixture avoids interfering with wheel contact along the tracked path.

Does the taxiway centreline help at curves and intersections?

Yes. Taxiway centreline lighting is particularly important on curves and can guide an aircraft through complex taxiway intersections, showing the aircraft's intended path.

Is the runway centreline used for intersection navigation the way the taxiway centreline is?

No. Runway centrelines are generally straight along the runway's longitudinal axis, and runway centreline lighting is not primarily intended to function as an intersection-navigation system.

What is the difference between runway centerline and taxiway centerline lights?

Runway centerline lights are white along most of the runway and change colour near the runway end, and they guide the aircraft during take-off and landing. Taxiway centerline lights are normally green and guide the aircraft along the taxi route at lower speed. They differ in colour, intensity, spacing and function.

DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Do not design a centreline lighting arrangement from a generic web diagram. The final installation drawing should always be developed from the current governing standard, approved airport design and project-specific requirements.
