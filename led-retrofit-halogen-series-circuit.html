LED retrofit in a halogen series circuit

Swapping halogen lamps for LED fixtures cuts the load on the circuit far below what the CCR and the transformers were sized for. Load matching brings the whole loop back into range.

halogen, before
LED, unmatched
LED, matched
The Fact FAA AC 150/5345-10 · IEC 61820-3-2 · ICAO Doc 9157 Part 5

A series circuit is sized around its load. The CCR and every isolation transformer were chosen for halogen lamps that draw tens of watts each. An LED fixture draws a fraction of that, so replacing the lamps leaves the same equipment carrying a much lighter load.

Load matching is the check that the lighter load still suits that equipment. It works at three levels: the fixture and its driver, the transformer at each light, and the CCR that feeds the whole loop.

At each light

A transformer carrying far less than its rating still draws magnetising current, so its efficiency and power factor fall, and the loss is repeated at every light on the circuit.

At the CCR

The regulator is made for a range of load. Far below that range its power factor and output waveform can suffer, and some units state a minimum load. Check the CCR data sheet.

What to check on a retrofit

Circuit load

Add up the new fixture loads, the transformer losses and the cable losses, and compare the total with the CCR rating and its stated load range.

Transformer rating

Compare each transformer rating with the new fixture load. Decide whether to keep it or fit a smaller one.

CCR behaviour

Check power factor, output current and waveform at the new load, and confirm the CCR is approved for it.

Brightness steps

Measure the series current at every step and confirm the light output and the step-to-step change still meet the requirement.

Monitoring

Check that lamp-out detection and alarms still respond, because each fixture is now a smaller share of the load.

Records

Record the new load figures and equipment ratings so the next change starts from the right numbers.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is load matching in an LED retrofit?

It means checking that the new, much lighter LED load still suits the equipment that was sized for halogen lamps: each isolation transformer, the CCR and the circuit as a whole. The goal is that every part runs within the range it was designed for, and that the lights still meet their brightness steps.

Q2Why does the circuit load fall when LEDs replace halogen lamps?

A halogen fixture draws tens of watts at the lamp, while an LED fixture draws a fraction of that. Across a whole circuit the total load on the CCR can drop sharply, and the transformer losses and cable losses become a larger share of what is left.

Q3Why is a very light load a problem for the CCR?

A constant current regulator is designed to work over a range of load. At a very light load its power factor can fall and its output waveform and regulation can suffer, and some CCRs have a stated minimum load. The limits depend on the CCR, so check its data sheet.

Q4Can the existing isolation transformers be kept?

Often they can, but a transformer carrying much less than its rating draws a larger share of magnetising current, so its efficiency and power factor fall, and that is added at every light. Whether to keep them or fit smaller ones depends on the design and the fixture manufacturer’s guidance.

Q5How can the load on the CCR be brought back into range?

The usual options are to fit a CCR sized or approved for the lower load, to use transformers matched to the LED load, to add a balancing load, or to combine circuits so that one CCR carries more fixtures. Each has a cost in money or in wasted power, so the choice is made at design level with the CCR and fixture manufacturers.

Q6What should be checked after the retrofit?

Measure the series current at each brightness step, check the circuit load and the CCR behaviour, confirm the lights meet their photometric and brightness requirements, and check that lamp-out and alarm monitoring still work with the lower load. Then record the new load figures.
