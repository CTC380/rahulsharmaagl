What is cable testing in a series circuit?

Proving an AGL cable is one unbroken, well-insulated conductor before it is trusted with the whole circuit’s current — because in a series loop, one weak length affects every light.

The Fact Series circuit design

Cable testing is measuring an AGL primary cable, with the circuit de-energised, to prove it is one complete conductor with sound insulation. In a series circuit the same current passes through every light on the loop, so a single open or leaking length can put out or upset the whole circuit. That is why the cable is tested before it is trusted.

Two readings carry most of the weight. Continuity shows the conductor is unbroken, and a failure means an open circuit. Insulation resistance shows how well the insulation keeps the conductor off earth, and a low reading means leakage and a possible earth fault. Where the specification asks for it, a withstand test adds proof that the insulation holds at a stated voltage. When the whole loop reads wrong, it is split at pits and connectors and each section is tested on its own until one stands out. Treat any cable that could be energised as live until the circuit has been isolated, locked out and proved dead, and discharge it after any DC test.

  1. Isolate. Isolate and lock out the circuit, prove it dead, and disconnect the CCR from the loop.
  2. Continuity. Measure end to end and confirm the loop is one unbroken path.
  3. Insulation resistance. Measure the conductor to earth and check it reads high and steady.
  4. Withstand test, if specified. Apply the specified voltage for the specified time, then discharge the cable.
  5. Split and locate. If a reading is wrong, open the loop at pits or connectors and test each section.
  6. Record. Log every value by cable ID so the next test can be compared with it.
Continuity good, insulation poor

The loop is complete but leaking to earth. The cable carries current today and is an earth fault waiting to happen.

Insulation good, continuity open

The insulation is fine but the conductor is broken. No current can flow, so every light on the loop stays dark.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is cable testing in an AGL series circuit?

Cable testing is measuring a de-energised primary cable to prove it is one complete conductor with sound insulation. The usual tests are continuity, insulation resistance to earth and, where the specification calls for it, a withstand (proof) test.

Q2Why test a series-circuit cable before it is energised?

In a series circuit the same current passes through every light on the loop, so one open or leaking length can affect the whole circuit. Testing finds that weakness on the ground, where it is cheap to fix, rather than in service.

Q3What do continuity and insulation resistance each tell you?

Continuity shows whether the conductor is unbroken from end to end, so a failure points to an open circuit. Insulation resistance shows how well the insulation holds the conductor away from earth, so a low reading points to leakage and a possible earth fault.

Q4Can a cable pass continuity and still be faulty?

Yes. A conductor can be complete while its insulation is damaged or wet, so it passes continuity and fails insulation resistance. The reverse also happens: insulation can be fine while the conductor is broken. Both tests are needed.

Q5How is a fault found after a failed test?

The loop is split at pits and connectors and each section is tested on its own. The section that reads wrong holds the fault. A cable fault locator can then narrow it down along that section.

Q6Why must the cable be isolated and discharged for testing?

A cable that could be energised is treated as live until the circuit has been isolated, locked out and proved dead. After any DC test the cable holds charge, so it is discharged before anyone touches it. Follow the site permit-to-work procedure.
