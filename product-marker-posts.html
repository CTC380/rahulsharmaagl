Safety & Siting

A Marker Post Is Not a Statement That the Cable Is Dead

Treat Every Marked Cable as Live

Primary AGL cables carry series-circuit current at up to 5,000 V insulation class. A post shows the route only. Before any excavation, follow the airport permit-to-work and cable-locate procedure, and never assume a marked cable is de-energised. Never cut or open an energised series circuit.

Siting on the Airfield

Equipment on the operational area must be frangible and mounted as low as its function allows (Annex 14, Vol. I, Chapter 9). Confirm with the airport before placing any post inside a runway or taxiway strip. Posts must not obstruct aircraft, vehicles, signs or lights, and must not be a substitute for accurate as-built records.

Standards & Project Requirements

Established for the Specific Site

References

  • ICAO Annex 14, Vol. I, Chapter 9: siting and frangibility of equipment on operational areas
  • ICAO Doc 9157 Part 5: electrical systems, and Part 6: frangibility
  • FAA AC 150/5340-30J: design and installation details for airport visual aids
  • FAA AC 150/5370-10 (Item L-108): installation of underground airport power cable
  • DGCA India requirements and the airport cable-records system

Project Requirements

Height, material, colour, marking method, foundation and legend follow the site cable-marking system. Record every post in the as-built drawings.

FAQ

AGL Marker Posts — Frequently Asked Questions

What are AGL marker posts used for?

Marker posts give visible identification of underground cable routes, infrastructure and selected equipment locations. They help maintenance personnel find buried AGL services and field connection points without relying solely on underground drawings or records.

Where are marker posts placed?

Typical locations are primary and secondary cable routes, cable crossings, junction points, junction pits and handholes, cable joints, branch routes and equipment interfaces. The positions come from the approved drawings.

What information can a marker post carry?

Possible information includes a cable or circuit reference, an equipment reference, route and junction identification, warning or service identification and project-specific coding. The legend and marking method, such as printed, engraved or labelled, should follow the approved project documentation and stay consistent with the project cable identification system.

What material and construction should be used?

Material selection should consider weather resistance, UV exposure, mechanical durability, visibility, corrosion resistance where applicable and long-term legibility of the markings. Posts may face sunlight, rain, temperature variation, soil contact and routine airfield maintenance activity, so height, material and colour follow the project and identification requirements.

Are there siting restrictions on the airfield?

Equipment on the operational area must be frangible and mounted as low as its function allows, as set out in Annex 14 Volume I Chapter 9. Confirm with the airport before placing any post inside a runway or taxiway strip, and make sure posts do not obstruct aircraft, vehicles, signs or lights.

Does a marker post mean the cable is de-energised?

No. Treat every marked cable as live. Primary AGL cables carry series-circuit current at up to 5,000 V insulation class, and a post shows the route only. Before any excavation, follow the airport permit-to-work and cable-locate procedure, and never cut or open an energised series circuit. See the safety and siting notes on this page.

How are marker posts installed?

Installation follows the approved drawings for route, marker-post location, required height, identification legend, material and colour, and the foundation or fixing arrangement. Confirm visibility from the required approach direction, clearance from aircraft and vehicle movement areas, and the position of existing underground services.

How do marker posts help at cable crossings?

Buried cables are not visible after installation, so a post gives a reference for the approximate location of a crossing and supports future maintenance or civil works. Record the position accurately in the as-built documentation, because a post is not a substitute for accurate as-built records.

What should be inspected and maintained?

Check for damage or displacement, loose foundations, faded or illegible markings, corrosion or material deterioration, obstruction by vegetation or soil, and changes to surrounding infrastructure. Replace damaged or missing posts according to the approved identification scheme.

Can marker posts be added during rehabilitation?

Yes. Markers can be added or replaced during rehabilitation projects where existing underground cable identification is inadequate. Coordinate with available cable drawings, survey information and verified field locations before installing new markers.

Which references apply to marker posts?

References include ICAO Annex 14 Volume I Chapter 9 on siting and frangibility of equipment, ICAO Doc 9157 Parts 5 and 6, FAA AC 150/5340-30J, FAA AC 150/5370-10 (Item L-108) on installation of underground airport power cable, and DGCA India requirements and the airport cable-records system. Height, material, colour, marking method, foundation and legend follow the site cable-marking system, and every post should be recorded in the as-built drawings.
