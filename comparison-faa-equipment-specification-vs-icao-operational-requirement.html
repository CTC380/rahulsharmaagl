FAA EQUIPMENT SPEC VS ICAO OPERATIONAL REQ.

A distinction that trips up a lot of AGL submittals — FAA equipment specifications define what a particular airport-lighting product must be and how it must perform, while ICAO operational requirements define what the aerodrome lighting system needs to achieve for safe aircraft operations. Product compliance and system/operational compliance are not the same thing, and mixing them up is one of the most common technical-submittal mistakes on AGL projects.

05 · Independent Failure Modes

Passing One Does Not Prove the Other

FAA-Qualified Fixture, Installed Incorrectly

Suppose a fixture is FAA-qualified, but it's positioned incorrectly, spaced incorrectly, misaligned, set up with the wrong runway lighting configuration, given an incorrect intensity setting, or fed by an incorrectly configured cable/control system.

Result

The product may be compliant, but the AGL installation may not be operationally compliant.

ICAO-Compliant Performance, Not an FAA-Qualified Product

Suppose an LED fixture meets the applicable ICAO visual characteristics. That does not automatically mean it is an FAA-qualified product — FAA maintains its own equipment specifications and Airport Lighting Equipment Certification Program, under which FAA-approved third-party certifiers evaluate and certify equipment against FAA specifications.

Result

ICAO performance does not equal FAA equipment qualification, unless the applicable FAA requirements have also been separately demonstrated.

06 · Component Examples

Isolation Transformer, L-823 Connector & PAPI

Isolation transformer

FAA equipment requirements specify the type of transformer used in an airport-lighting series circuit, identifying L-830/L-831 isolation transformers and their function isolating the light unit from the high-voltage series circuit. At the system level, ICAO is concerned with the electrical system and operational availability of aerodrome lighting.

L-823 connector

FAA AC 150/5345-26E provides specifications for L-823 plugs and receptacles used with underground power cables, isolation-transformer leads and light-fixture leads — a product/interface requirement. ICAO, by contrast, is primarily concerned with the resulting aerodrome lighting system and its operational performance.

PAPI

FAA has a dedicated equipment specification — AC 150/5345-28H, Precision Approach Path Indicator (PAPI) Systems — covering construction, performance and qualification. ICAO establishes PAPI requirements as part of the aerodrome visual-aid framework, covering installation geometry, visual indication, and operational use.

07 · Photometry

Where the Two Concepts Overlap

Photometry is where FAA and ICAO overlap strongly. FAA can establish what the fixture produces; ICAO establishes how the visual aid functions within the aerodrome. This is a simplified distinction, since FAA also has installation guidance and ICAO contains detailed technical specifications of its own.

08 · FAT vs SAT

How This Maps to Factory and Site Testing

FAT — "Does the equipment meet its specification?"

  • Dimensions
  • Electrical characteristics
  • Insulation / dielectric withstand
  • Functional operation
  • Photometry
  • Manufacturing quality & documentation

SAT — "Does the installed system work correctly?"

  • Installation and alignment
  • Circuit and CCR operation, control
  • Insulation resistance, continuity
  • Functional operation
  • Field photometry
  • System integration

12 · Worked Example

Runway Edge LED Fixture — Five Steps

Suppose you are supplying an LED runway edge light. Getting from requirement to demonstrated compliance runs through five stages:

  • Operational requirement — runway category, required lighting system, location, spacing, colour, intensity, beam characteristics, per ICAO/national/project requirements
  • Equipment requirement — select a fixture satisfying the applicable FAA specification (if required), applicable IEC/IS requirements, and the project specification
  • Product verification — electrical, photometric, environmental and mechanical tests, plus routine tests
  • Installation — location, spacing, elevation, alignment, cable, transformer, connector, CCR compatibility
  • Field verification — electrical and functional tests, photometry/alignment where required, system operation, control verification

Only after all five stages can system/operational compliance actually be demonstrated.

13 · Submittal Language

A Useful Rule for Technical Submittals

Avoid: "FAA-certified fixture = ICAO-compliant runway"
Use instead

"The fixture complies with the applicable FAA equipment specification, and the installed lighting system is designed and verified against the applicable ICAO/national/project operational requirements." That distinction is technically much stronger.

16 · Engineering Verdict

FAA Equipment Specification vs ICAO Operational Requirement

Engineering Verdict

FAA equipment specification proves the equipment is suitable. ICAO/national operational requirement proves the aerodrome lighting system is suitable.

FAT proves the product; SAT/commissioning/field photometry proves the installed system. So the complete chain reads: equipment compliance + correct installation + system performance = operational compliance. FAA itself separates its airport-lighting framework into equipment specifications and airport visual-aid design/installation guidance, while ICAO Annex 14 establishes the broader aerodrome facilities and technical-service requirements.

ICAOSystem/operational compliance — does the aerodrome provide the required visual guidance?

17 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fundamental difference between an FAA equipment specification and an ICAO operational requirement?

FAA equipment specifications define what a particular airport-lighting product must be and how it must perform. ICAO operational requirements define what the aerodrome lighting system needs to achieve for safe aircraft operations. One is product-level, the other is system-level.

Does an FAA-qualified fixture automatically mean the runway is ICAO-compliant?

No. The fixture may be FAA-qualified while incorrectly positioned, spaced, aligned, configured, or connected — the product can be compliant while the installation is not operationally compliant.

Does ICAO-compliant photometric performance mean a fixture is FAA-qualified?

No. FAA maintains its own equipment specifications and a dedicated Airport Lighting Equipment Certification Program. ICAO performance does not automatically establish FAA equipment qualification.

How does this map to FAT and SAT?

FAT verifies whether the supplied equipment meets its specified requirements — closer to the FAA equipment-specification question. SAT verifies whether the installed system works correctly at the aerodrome — closer to the ICAO operational-requirement question.

What's an example where FAA and ICAO both apply to the same component?

An isolation transformer: FAA identifies L-830/L-831 transformer product requirements — electrical, insulation and construction — while ICAO is concerned with the aerodrome electrical system, lighting availability and secondary power at the operational level.

What's the correct wording for a technical submittal covering both?

Avoid saying "FAA-certified fixture = ICAO-compliant runway." Instead state that the fixture complies with the applicable FAA equipment specification, and that the installed lighting system is designed and verified against the applicable ICAO/national/project operational requirements.

What is the correct compliance chain for an AGL project?

Start from the ICAO/national operational requirement, translate it into a project design, select equipment against the applicable FAA/IS/IEC requirements, verify the product at FAT, install it, and confirm system operation at SAT/field test before operational acceptance.

18 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents and the exact project standard hierarchy.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Establishes aerodrome physical characteristics, facilities and technical services, including visual aids, from an aerodrome/system perspective.

FAA AC 150/5345 Series

Equipment specifications for fixtures, PAPI, CCRs, L-823 connectors, cable, bases and transformer housings and other airport-lighting products.

FAA ALECP

The Airport Lighting Equipment Certification Program — FAA-approved third-party certification and product qualification for airport-lighting equipment.

Final Engineering Note

Equipment compliance and operational compliance are not interchangeable claims. Always name the exact FAA document and the exact ICAO/national operational requirement rather than conflating the two.

FAA equipment specifications and ICAO operational requirements are not two versions of the same requirement — one proves the product is suitable, the other proves the aerodrome system is suitable.
