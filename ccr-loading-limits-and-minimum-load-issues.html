What are CCR loading limits and minimum-load issues?

A CCR works properly between a minimum and a maximum load. Too heavy and it cannot hold the current; too light and its power factor, efficiency and regulation can suffer.

The Fact Loading range

CCR loading is the power the series circuit draws, found at the highest brightness step, compared with the CCR’s kW rating. The circuit load is the lamps, the isolation transformer losses and the cable I²R losses added together. The upper limit is the rating: a CCR pushed past it cannot hold the set current on the higher steps, so lamps run dim, and it can overheat or trip on its protection.

There is a lower limit too. A CCR that is far larger than its load is less efficient and can have a poor power factor and less accurate regulation, depending on the design, and may fall below the manufacturer’s minimum loading. The load also moves after commissioning: failed lamps lower it and added fixtures raise it, so a circuit should sit comfortably between the minimum and the rating, with margin, and be checked at both ends.

  1. Calculate the load. At the highest step: lamps, plus isolation transformer losses, plus cable I²R.
  2. Compare with the rating. Add margin for growth, ageing and tolerances, then take the next standard CCR size up.
  3. Check the minimum. Confirm the load is above the manufacturer’s minimum loading and that the CCR regulates well at light load.
  4. Test the lamps-out case. See how far the load falls as lamps fail, and whether the CCR still behaves.
  5. Correct a light load. Options include a smaller CCR, combining circuits, or other measures the manufacturer approves.
  6. Record and recheck. Log the loading, and recalculate after any fixture is added or the circuit is changed.
Too heavy

No spare capacity. The CCR cannot hold the current on the high steps, runs hot and may trip, and any added fixture makes it worse.

Too light

A large CCR on a small load. It still works, but with lower efficiency and power factor, and regulation can be less accurate, depending on the design.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is CCR loading?

CCR loading is the power the series circuit draws, found at the highest brightness step, shown as a percentage of the CCR's kW rating. The circuit load is the lamps, the isolation transformer losses and the cable I²R losses added together.

Q2What happens if a CCR is overloaded?

A CCR loaded past its rating cannot hold the set current on the higher brightness steps, so the lamps run dim. It can also overheat or trip on its protection. Sizing leaves spare capacity for added fixtures, ageing and tolerances.

Q3What is the minimum-load issue?

A CCR that is lightly loaded compared with its rating is less efficient and can have a poor power factor and less accurate regulation, depending on the design. Each manufacturer states a minimum loading, and a CCR far larger than the circuit may fall below it.

Q4Why can the loading change after commissioning?

Failed lamps reduce the load, added fixtures raise it, and cable resistance changes with temperature. A circuit that was comfortably loaded when new can drift towards the light end as lamps fail or towards the limit as fixtures are added, so it is worth checking both ends.

Q5How is the right CCR size chosen?

The load is calculated at the highest step, margin is added for growth, ageing and tolerances, and the next standard CCR size up is chosen. It is then checked at light load against the manufacturer's minimum. The rating, the minimum and the margin come from the data sheet, the standard and the site design basis.
