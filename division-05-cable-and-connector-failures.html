Cable & Connector Failures

The dedicated failure-intelligence section for AGL primary cables, secondary cables, cable joints, terminations, L-823-type connectors and associated connector interfaces. Every record separates the cable/connector failure mode from the physical cause, installation cause and resulting electrical consequence — for example: incorrect connector installation → poor contact → high contact resistance → connector overheating → intermittent/open circuit → fixture outage.

Initial Issue

Key principle — Division 05 contains 14 cable failures and 11 connector/secondary-lead failures, with “Incorrect connector installation” formally numbered as CCF-25 rather than left unnumbered. Every record should answer: what failed → how did it fail → why did it fail → what evidence confirmed the failure → how was it repaired → how can recurrence be prevented?

Relationship to Division 02 — CCF-01, 02, 03, 04, 07, 08 and 15 (cable insulation breakdown, open circuit, short circuit, earth fault, cable joint failure, cable termination failure and connector failure) share a name with topics in Division 02 — Electrical Failures. This division covers the cable- and connector-hardware-specific version — physical construction, installation cause and connector-interface detail. Division 02 covers the same failure modes at the general series-circuit/electrical level.

CCF-01 Cable Insulation Breakdown
Failure Definition

Loss of the required insulation integrity of an AGL cable, resulting in leakage, earth fault, short circuit or circuit failure.

Typical Symptoms
  • Low insulation resistance
  • Earth-fault indication
  • Leakage current
  • Circuit trip
  • Intermittent operation
  • Complete circuit outage
Possible Causes
  • Cable ageing
  • Mechanical damage
  • Water/moisture
  • Improper installation
  • Excessive bending
  • Thermal deterioration
  • Environmental exposure
Investigation
  • Insulation resistance
  • Cable section
  • Cable installation condition
  • Joint condition
  • Termination condition
  • Moisture evidence
  • Historical IR trend
Failure Chain
INSULATION DETERIORATIONINSULATION BREAKDOWNLEAKAGE/EARTH FAULTPROTECTION OPERATIONAGL OUTAGE
CCF-02 Open Circuit
Failure Definition

Complete interruption of the electrical continuity of the cable circuit.

Possible Locations
  • Cable conductor
  • Cable joint
  • Termination
  • Connector
  • Cable damage point
Symptoms
  • Fixture(s) extinguished
  • Circuit continuity failure
  • CCR abnormal response
  • Complete/partial circuit outage
Investigation

Sectionalize the circuit and locate the point at which continuity is lost.

Important Database Distinction

Failure mode: Open circuit

Root cause: Broken conductor / failed joint / damaged termination

CCF-10 Cable Ageing
Failure Definition

Progressive degradation of cable performance due to service age and long-term environmental/electrical exposure.

Possible Effects
  • Reduced insulation resistance
  • Brittleness
  • Cracking
  • Insulation deterioration
  • Increased leakage
  • Increased failure frequency
Investigation — Record
  • Installation date
  • Cable type
  • Service age

  • Failure history

  • Improper locking

B. Four-Level RCA Distinction

Not every item is the same level of failure classification. Division 05 uses four levels:

Case 1

Failure event: Fixture outage

Failure mode: Open circuit

Failure mechanism: High contact resistance → overheating

Root cause: Incorrect connector installation

Case 2

Event: AGL circuit earth fault

Mode: Cable insulation breakdown

Mechanism: Moisture contamination

Root cause: Cable water ingress

Failure Database vs. Troubleshooting Atlas

The Troubleshooting Atlas answers “I have a fault now — how do I diagnose and fix it?” The Failure Database answers “this failure happened — what happened, why, what evidence proves it, what was done, and how do we stop it recurring?” Kept strictly separate, then linked end-to-end, the two form a genuine AGL Failure Intelligence Engine rather than a simple fault list.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about AGL cable and connector failures, as covered in Division 05 of the AGL Failure Database.

Q1What is Division 05 — Cable & Connector Failures in the AGL Failure Database?

Division 05 covers AGL primary and secondary cable failures and L-823-type connector failures. It contains 25 published topics (CCF-01 to CCF-25): 14 cable topics and 11 connector topics.

Q2Which cable failures does Division 05 cover?

Cable insulation breakdown, open circuit, short circuit, earth fault, cable mechanical damage, cable water ingress, cable joint failure, cable termination failure, cable overheating, cable ageing, cable corrosion, improper cable routing, cable identification error and cable installation damage.

Q3Which connector failures does Division 05 cover?

Connector failure, loose connector, poor contact, high contact resistance, connector corrosion, moisture ingress, connector seal failure, connector locking failure, secondary-lead failure, connector overheating and incorrect connector installation.

Q4What are the four levels of RCA distinction in Division 05?

Not every item is the same level of failure classification, so records separate the failure event, failure mode, failure mechanism and root cause. For example, the event is a fixture outage, the mode is an open circuit, the mechanism is high contact resistance leading to overheating, and the root cause is incorrect connector installation.

Q5What connector failure progressions does Division 05 capture?

In an installation chain, incorrect installation leads to incomplete engagement, poor contact, high contact resistance, connector overheating, connector failure and a fixture outage. In a sealing chain, incorrect seal installation leads to seal failure, moisture ingress, connector corrosion, high contact resistance and an open circuit. A mechanical chain runs from cable mechanical damage through connector or lead stress to secondary-lead failure.

Q6Where should the investigation start for a cable or connector failure?

The diagnostic matrices give a primary investigation for each topic. Open circuit starts with continuity and sectionalization, earth fault with IR and earth-fault location, cable water ingress with moisture and sealing, loose connector with engagement and locking, and connector seal failure with seal condition. A generic connector failure begins with a complete connector inspection.

Q7What should be recorded for a cable or connector failure?

Each record is grouped into asset identification (circuit ID, cable ID, connector ID, location, cable and connector type, installation date), failure information, and testing such as continuity, insulation resistance and earth-fault results. Evidence includes cable, joint, termination and connector photographs, moisture and corrosion evidence, thermal or discolouration evidence at contacts, voltage drop, historical IR trend and as-built drawing verification.

Q8How are failure severity and operational impact classified?

Every failure receives a severity classification, plus operational-impact tagging. The classes are Critical / Safety-Critical (immediate operational or safety risk), Major / Operationally Critical (significant availability impact), Moderate (reduced performance, not yet critical), Minor / Non-Critical (limited or no operational effect), and Degradation / Intermittent / Latent (a developing or hidden fault).

Q9How does the Failure Database differ from the Troubleshooting Atlas?

The Troubleshooting Atlas answers “I have a fault now — how do I diagnose and fix it?” The Failure Database answers “this failure happened — what happened, why, what evidence proves it, what was done, and how do we stop it recurring?” Kept strictly separate, then linked end to end, the two form an AGL Failure Intelligence Engine rather than a simple fault list.
