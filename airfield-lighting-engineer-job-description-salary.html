Airfield Lighting Engineer and Technician: Job Description and Salary

What AGL engineers, technicians, site engineers, design engineers and commissioning engineers do, the qualifications and skills employers ask for, and how to read salary information in India without being misled by a single number.

Overview

One Field, Several Very Different Jobs

An Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) engineer works with the electrical equipment and visual aid systems used on airport runways, taxiways, aprons and approach areas.

Depending on the employer and project, the role may involve design, installation, testing, maintenance, troubleshooting, project execution, commissioning, compliance and technical documentation.

An AGL technician typically focuses more directly on equipment inspection, installation, testing support, preventive maintenance, component replacement and fault reporting, within their competence and authorization.

Airfield lighting careers are available with airport operators, engineering consultants, specialist AGL contractors, equipment manufacturers, maintenance service providers and airport infrastructure project companies.

This guide explains common AGL job roles, responsibilities, qualifications, technical skills, career progression and salary considerations in India.

Important: Job titles, qualification requirements and salaries vary by employer, contract, experience and project responsibility. The salary examples in this guide are based on publicly available job listings and should not be interpreted as a guaranteed market rate or offer.

1 · Engineer

What Does an Airfield Lighting Engineer Do?

An Airfield Lighting Engineer helps design, install, test, commission or maintain the lighting systems required for airport operations. The exact responsibilities depend on whether the position is focused on design engineering, site execution, maintenance, commissioning, consultancy, technical sales or project management.

2 · Technician

AGL Technician Job Description

An AGL technician performs hands-on inspection, maintenance and technical support activities for airport lighting equipment. The scope may include routine maintenance, defect identification, component replacement, testing support and reporting. All tasks must remain within the technician's training, authorization and the airport's approved procedures.

Common Technician Duties

Duties

  • Inspect runway, taxiway and other installed lighting fixtures.
  • Identify visible damage, contamination and abnormal operating conditions.
  • Support preventive maintenance activities.
  • Inspect accessible cables, connectors and associated equipment as authorized.
  • Assist with isolating transformer and CCR maintenance under approved procedures.
  • Record lighting failures and maintenance observations.
  • Support prescribed electrical and functional tests.
  • Assist with approved fixture replacement and commissioning activities.
  • Maintain work orders, inspection logs and equipment records.
  • Follow airport access, permit, electrical safety and movement-area procedures.

An AGL technician should not perform electrical work or operational activities for which the person has not been trained and authorized.
3 · Site Engineer

Airfield Lighting Site Engineer Responsibilities

An AGL Site Engineer coordinates day-to-day installation, maintenance or commissioning activities at an airport project or facility. The position combines technical knowledge with site supervision, quality control, planning, records and coordination with other teams.

Typical Site Engineer Duties

Site planning

Review approved drawings, work schedules, materials and daily work requirements.

Installation supervision

Monitor equipment installation, cable routing, fixture positioning and other activities against approved documents.

Quality control

Arrange or support inspections and verify that the required records and approvals are available.

Testing coordination

Coordinate prescribed tests, instrument availability, inspections and witness requirements.

Material management

Track equipment quantities, deliveries, identification and material submittals.

Progress reporting

Record work completed, constraints, defects, resources and planned activities.

Technical problem-solving

Report discrepancies between the approved drawings and actual site conditions and coordinate their resolution.

Document control

Maintain inspection records, work orders, as-built drawings, technical submissions and handover documentation.

Safety coordination

Ensure the team follows the applicable access, permit, isolation and work-zone requirements.

A site engineer may also work with civil, electrical, airside operations, signage and control-system teams to coordinate interfaces.

A recruitment document for an Electrical Site Engineer supporting an Airports Authority of India project at Hisar specifies supervision of electrical works, checking quality-control testing and verifying measurements. The role requires relevant experience and familiarity with electrical drawings and estimation. View the recruitment document.

4 · Design & Commissioning

AGL Design Engineer and Commissioning Engineer

Some AGL roles focus on technical design, while others focus on testing, commissioning and system acceptance.

AGL Design Engineer: may be responsible for

  • Preparing or reviewing runway and taxiway lighting layouts.
  • Developing circuit schedules and equipment lists.
  • Supporting CCR sizing and circuit design.
  • Reviewing cable, connector and transformer requirements.
  • Preparing or reviewing technical specifications and BOQs.
  • Evaluating photometric data and lighting calculations.
  • Coordinating drawings and interfaces with other disciplines.
  • Checking applicable standards and project-specific requirements.
  • Supporting technical submittals and design reviews.

AGL Testing and Commissioning Engineer: may be responsible for

  • Reviewing the approved commissioning plan and ITP.
  • Verifying installed equipment against drawings and equipment schedules.
  • Coordinating inspections and prescribed electrical tests.
  • Checking relevant CCR, circuit and control-system functions.
  • Reviewing photometric results where required.
  • Recording defects and supporting corrective-action closure.
  • Compiling commissioning reports and acceptance documentation.
  • Supporting the approved system handback process.

Useful design skills include CAD software, electrical calculations, technical writing, standards research and the ability to evaluate alternatives against the approved design criteria.

The exact tasks and acceptance authority depend on the contract, employer and airport procedures.

Do You Need an AGL-Specific Qualification?

An AGL-focused course can help develop knowledge of runway and taxiway systems, CCRs, isolating transformers, cables, connectors, installation methods, testing and maintenance.

However, completing a course does not automatically confer an engineering licence, airport access authorization or permission to carry out safety-critical work. The employer and airport determine the applicable competency and authorization requirements.

8 · Salary

There is no single salary for an Airfield Lighting Engineer in India. Compensation depends on experience, specialization, employer, airport project, location, employment arrangement and level of responsibility.

The publicly available salary information for specialist AGL roles is limited, and vacancies do not always disclose compensation. A useful salary guide should therefore distinguish between published job offers, third-party estimates and general salary expectations.

8.3 Technician, Site Engineer and Specialist Engineer: Compare Like with Like

A salary comparison should match the actual duties and experience level.

A maintenance technician responsible mainly for inspections and approved component replacement should not be compared directly with an AGL design specialist responsible for system layouts, CCR sizing, photometry, compliance and multidisciplinary coordination.

Similarly, a contractor's fixed monthly salary should not be compared directly with an employer's annual total compensation without checking the included benefits, bonuses, deductions and work arrangement.

9 · Career

How to Build a Career in AGL

A strong AGL career develops through progressive technical learning, supervised practical experience, reliable documentation and increasing responsibility.

Stage 1: Build the Fundamentals

Learn basic electrical principles, equipment identification, circuit diagrams and the purpose of runway, taxiway and approach lighting. Understand the relationship between a CCR, primary series circuit, isolating transformers, field fixtures and associated control systems.

Stage 2: Gain Practical Exposure

Where properly authorized and supervised, develop practical understanding through inspection, installation, maintenance, testing or commissioning activities. Learn how to identify equipment, read approved drawings, follow safety procedures and record defects accurately.

Stage 3: Improve Technical Documentation Skills

Learn to prepare or review equipment schedules and BOQs, technical submittals and compliance statements, inspection and test records, fault reports and corrective-action records, as-built drawings and handover documents, and maintenance and equipment registers. These skills are useful for technician, site engineering, commissioning and design positions.

Stage 4: Develop a Specialist Skill

Consider deeper capability in AGL photometry, lighting design and engineering, CCR and series-circuit systems, isolating transformers, cables and connectors, ALCMS and control interfaces, testing, inspection and commissioning, standards and technical compliance, or project execution and technical-commercial coordination. Specialization can help distinguish a candidate from someone with only general electrical experience.

Stage 5: Progress to Senior Responsibilities

With relevant experience and demonstrated competence, potential career directions include Senior AGL Technician, AGL Site Engineer, AGL Commissioning Engineer, AGL Design Engineer, AGL Photometry Specialist, AGL Quality or Compliance Engineer, AGL Project Engineer, AGL Project Manager, and AGL Technical Specialist or Consultant.

These are possible career paths rather than guaranteed promotion steps. Advancement depends on qualifications, competence, performance, available opportunities and employer requirements.

How to Improve Your AGL Engineering CV

A useful AGL CV should show the systems you understand, the work you have performed, your level of responsibility and the evidence supporting your experience.

Recommended CV Sections

Professional profile

Summarize your relevant AGL experience, technical strengths and target role.

Technical skills

List specific equipment, lighting systems, standards and tools that you can genuinely use or explain.

Project experience

Describe the type of airport or AGL project, your assigned responsibilities and the stages in which you participated.

Technical responsibilities

Identify your work in design, installation, inspection, testing, commissioning, troubleshooting or documentation.

Standards and compliance

List the applicable requirements with which you have actual working familiarity.

Tools and software

Include CAD, photometric tools, spreadsheets or reporting systems that you have used.

Training and certifications

Provide the exact course or certification name, issuer and completion date, where applicable.

Achievements

Use verifiable outcomes, such as completing a specified project phase, improving documentation quality or closing a defined set of technical issues. Avoid unsupported performance claims.

Example AGL CV Summary

AGL professional with experience in airfield lighting systems, including runway and taxiway lighting, CCRs, isolating transformers, cabling, inspection, testing and project documentation. Familiar with approved drawings, technical specifications, equipment coordination and airport project requirements. Focused on safe work practices, technical accuracy and reliable project execution.

Adapt this summary to reflect your actual experience and responsibilities. Do not claim design authority, testing competence, certification or standards expertise that you cannot substantiate.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What does an airfield lighting engineer do?

The role can cover AGL design, installation, testing, commissioning, maintenance, fault investigation, compliance and technical documentation. The exact scope depends on the employer and project.

Q2What qualifications are required for an AGL technician job?

Requirements vary by employer. A relevant diploma, ITI or other technical qualification may be accepted for certain technician roles, while an engineering degree and aviation-sector experience may be required for specialist design positions.

Q3What is the salary of an airfield lighting engineer in India?

There is no single market-wide salary. Published job examples vary from monthly-paid technician and site-support roles to specialist engineering positions. Compare recent vacancies with similar duties and experience, and verify whether figures are employer-disclosed or estimated by third parties.

Q4What is the difference between an AGL technician and an AGL engineer?

An AGL technician often focuses on practical inspection, maintenance, installation and testing support. An engineer may also be responsible for design, technical review, calculations, project coordination, compliance and engineering decisions. Actual duties depend on the job description and authorization.

Q5Is AGL experience useful for airport project engineering?

Relevant AGL experience can support work on airport lighting design, construction, commissioning and maintenance. Broader project engineering roles may also require civil-interface knowledge, documentation, project planning and coordination with airport stakeholders.

Q6Is knowledge of ICAO and FAA requirements important?

It can be important for design, compliance and technical specialist roles. The relevant standards depend on the airport, jurisdiction, contract and project specification. Candidates should be able to identify the governing requirements for the work they perform.

Q7Does an AGL certificate guarantee a job?

No. Training can demonstrate learning, but employers also evaluate qualifications, practical experience, technical competence, safety awareness, communication and the specific requirements of the position.

Q8How can I move from technician work into AGL engineering?

Build electrical fundamentals, learn to interpret approved drawings and equipment documentation, gain authorized practical exposure, improve technical reporting and develop relevant design, testing or project skills. Check the qualification requirements for the positions you intend to pursue.

Q9What is an airport lighting engineer job description?

An airport lighting engineer job description normally covers design review, installation supervision, testing and commissioning, maintenance planning, fault investigation, compliance records and coordination with airport operations. Exact duties depend on whether the role is with a consultant, contractor, manufacturer or airport operator.

Q10What is an AGL technician job role?

An AGL technician job role is hands-on: inspecting lights, cleaning and replacing fixtures, testing circuits and cables, assisting with CCR maintenance, recording defects and following safety and permit procedures. Technicians usually work under an engineer or maintenance supervisor, and site authorisation depends on the airport.
