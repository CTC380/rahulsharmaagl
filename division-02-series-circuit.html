SC-096 Complete Series Circuit Failure

Symptom — No fixtures on the series circuit loop illuminate when the CCR is running and commanding output, even though the CCR itself may show normal output current/voltage or may be alarming on an open-circuit/output-fail condition.

Equipment / System

AGL series circuit loop (constant-current lighting circuit), isolating transformers, field cable, connectors, and the CCR output feed to the loop.

Possible Causes
HighA complete open circuit somewhere in the loop — broken cable, disconnected connector, or a failed connection at the CCR output terminals
HighThe CCR is not actually producing output despite a healthy-looking display/indication (see Division 01, CCR output topics)
MediumCatastrophic failure (open primary winding) of the first isolating transformer nearest the CCR, which can mask whether the rest of the loop is intact
MediumThe output feeder cable from the CCR to the loop's first pull box has failed
LowerThe wrong circuit has been selected/energized at the CCR or field junction, so the intended loop is simply not receiving output
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterInsulation resistance tester (5kV megger)Time-domain reflectometer (TDR) / cable fault locatorSeries circuit as-built drawingContinuity tester
Safety Precautions
  • Series circuit voltage can reach several kV under load depending on circuit length — never work on loop terminals while the CCR is running or capacitors are charged.
  • Apply lock-out/tag-out (LOTO) on the CCR output isolator before any loop testing.
  • Treat all loop terminals as live until proven de-energized with a tested meter.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the CCR is genuinely producing output (measured, not just displayed) at its output terminals before assuming the fault is in the loop.
  2. DecisionNo genuine output at the CCR terminals → investigate as a CCR fault per Division 01. Output confirmed present at the CCR terminals but absent in the loop → proceed with loop-level open-circuit investigation.
  3. MeasurementWith the CCR isolated, measure loop continuity/resistance at the CCR output terminals with the loop connected.
  4. MeasurementSegment-test the loop at accessible pull boxes/junctions, working outward from the CCR, to localise the point of the open circuit.
  5. ObservationInspect the localised segment/connector for visible damage, disconnection, or a failed isolating transformer.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific cable segment, connector, or transformer responsible for the complete open circuit.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the precise point and cause of the open circuit by physical inspection once localised.
Test Points
CCR output terminals with loop connected
Loop continuity/resistance segment by segment from pull boxes/junctions
First isolating transformer nearest the CCR
CCR-to-loop feeder cable
Root Cause Verification

After repair, confirm loop continuity is restored end to end, then run the CCR through a full load cycle and confirm all fixtures illuminate correctly with no repeat open-circuit indication.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the broken cable, connector, or terminal causing the open circuit.
  • Replace the failed isolating transformer if it is the point of failure.
  • Reconnect or correct a mis-selected circuit at the CCR or field junction.
  • Repair the CCR-to-loop feeder cable if that is the fault location.
Preventive Action
  • Include loop continuity/insulation testing (megger) in the annual PM, trended against baseline.
  • Maintain an accurate, current as-built drawing for each series circuit to speed up segment isolation.
  • Label pull boxes and junctions clearly to support rapid field segmentation during fault-finding.

SC-097 Single Light Failure

Symptom — One specific fixture on the series circuit fails to illuminate while all other fixtures on the same loop operate normally at the correct intensity.

Equipment / System

Individual fixture, its isolating transformer, and the associated field connector(s) at that fixture position.

Possible Causes
HighFailed lamp/LED module within the fixture itself
HighOpen primary or secondary winding in the fixture's isolating transformer
MediumLoose, corroded, or water-damaged connector at that specific fixture
MediumDamaged short cable stub between the connector and the fixture/transformer
LowerFixture lens/housing damage giving the appearance of failure while the light source itself is actually functioning
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterInsulation resistance tester (500V/1000V)MultimeterSpare known-good isolating transformer (for substitution test)
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO on the CCR output isolator before disconnecting or testing at the fixture.
  • Treat the connector and transformer as live until proven de-energized with a tested meter.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm only the one fixture is affected and that adjacent fixtures on the loop are operating at correct intensity, ruling out a broader loop issue.
  2. MeasurementWith the loop isolated, disconnect the fixture's connector and check continuity through the isolating transformer primary and secondary windings.
  3. DecisionTransformer windings show open circuit → transformer fault. Transformer windings test healthy → investigate the fixture/lamp itself or the connector/cable stub.
  4. MeasurementIf the transformer tests healthy, inspect and test the connector for corrosion, looseness, or water ingress.
  5. MeasurementTest or substitute the lamp/LED module to confirm whether it is the failed component.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific transformer, connector, cable stub, or lamp/LED module.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the failed component by isolated testing or substitution before final replacement.
Test Points
Isolating transformer primary and secondary winding continuity
Fixture connector condition
Cable stub between connector and fixture
Lamp/LED module
Root Cause Verification

After replacement, reconnect and confirm the fixture illuminates correctly at all intensity steps, and re-check adjacent fixtures remain unaffected.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed lamp/LED module.
  • Replace the failed isolating transformer.
  • Clean, repair, or replace the damaged connector.
  • Repair or replace the damaged cable stub.
Preventive Action
  • Include individual fixture illumination checks in the routine visual PM.
  • Track isolating transformer failure rates by location to identify systematically weak connector types or installation methods.
  • Verify connector sealing/mating quality after any fixture-level maintenance.

SC-098 Multiple Light Failure

Symptom — More than one, but not all, fixtures on the series circuit fail to illuminate — either scattered across the loop or clustered in one section — while the remaining fixtures operate normally.

Equipment / System

Multiple fixtures and isolating transformers across the affected section(s) of the series circuit loop.

Possible Causes
HighIndependent, coincidental failure of multiple isolating transformers or fixtures (age-related, more likely if the affected units share an installation batch or age)
HighA partial short or leakage path in one section of the loop diverting sufficient current to prevent proper illumination of transformers downstream of that point
MediumA common environmental cause (moisture ingress, corrosion) affecting several connectors in the same physical area or installed at the same time
MediumA single damaged cable section reducing available voltage/current to a cluster of fixtures fed from that section
LowerA batch of fixtures/transformers with a common manufacturing defect failing around the same operating time
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterInsulation resistance tester (5kV megger)Time-domain reflectometer (TDR) / cable fault locatorSeries circuit as-built drawing
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO on the CCR output isolator before loop segment testing.
  • Treat all loop terminals and connectors as live until proven de-energized with a tested meter.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationMap exactly which fixtures are affected against the as-built drawing to determine whether the pattern is scattered or clustered/contiguous.
  2. DecisionAffected fixtures are scattered with no shared cable section → investigate as independent failures at each fixture per SC-097. Affected fixtures are clustered/contiguous → investigate a shared cause in that section of the loop.
  3. MeasurementFor a clustered pattern, megger the loop segment feeding the affected cluster and compare against baseline insulation resistance.
  4. MeasurementWhere a partial short/leakage is suspected, measure loop current at points before and after the affected cluster to check for a diversion of current.
  5. ObservationInspect connectors and cable in the affected section for a common environmental cause (moisture, corrosion, physical damage).
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific cable section, connector cluster, or confirm the failures are genuinely independent.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause for each affected fixture/section by isolated testing.
Test Points
Loop insulation resistance in the affected section
Loop current before and after the affected cluster
Individual transformer/fixture continuity for each affected unit
Cable and connector condition in the affected section
Root Cause Verification

After repair, confirm all previously affected fixtures illuminate correctly at all intensity steps through a full load cycle.

Corrective Action
  • Replace each independently failed transformer/fixture.
  • Repair the cable section or connectors responsible for a shared/clustered cause.
  • Address the underlying environmental cause (moisture sealing, corrosion protection) if confirmed.
Preventive Action
  • Track fixture/transformer failures by location and installation date to identify developing batch or environmental patterns early.
  • Include sectional insulation resistance testing in the PM programme for circuits with a known history of clustered failures.
  • Improve sealing/corrosion protection in areas prone to moisture ingress.

SC-099 Intermittent Series Circuit Failure

Symptom — The series circuit, or a group of fixtures on it, intermittently loses illumination and recovers, rather than failing completely or remaining stable — occurrences may correlate with vibration, temperature swings, or weather.

Equipment / System

Series circuit loop, connectors, joints, and isolating transformers across the affected section.

Possible Causes
HighA marginal/loose connector or joint that makes and breaks contact under thermal expansion/contraction or vibration
HighA partially degraded cable insulation or joint that breaks down intermittently under load or moisture, rather than failing permanently
MediumA developing isolating transformer fault that is temperature- or load-sensitive
MediumVegetation, wildlife, or foreign object contact intermittently bridging or disturbing an exposed connection
LowerOperator repeatedly cycling the circuit without allowing proper investigation between intermittent events
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterData logger for trend/repeated-event analysisInsulation resistance tester (5kV megger)Series circuit as-built drawing
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO before any wiggle test or connector inspection with power isolated.
  • Treat a genuinely intermittent fault as a potential safety hazard — do not repeatedly re-energize without investigation.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationLog the pattern of intermittent failures over a representative period, correlating with weather, temperature, vibration sources, or load/step changes.
  2. MeasurementWith power isolated, perform a wiggle test on suspect connectors/joints along the affected section while monitoring continuity.
  3. MeasurementMegger the affected loop segment and compare against baseline, ideally repeating the test under conditions similar to when failures have occurred (e.g. after rain).
  4. DecisionWiggle test reveals an intermittent connection → connector/joint fault. Insulation resistance is low under specific conditions (e.g. wet) but normal when dry → moisture-related insulation breakdown.
  5. ObservationInspect the affected section for vegetation, wildlife, or foreign object contact near exposed connections.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific connector, joint, cable section, or transformer responsible.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the intermittent fault by reproducing it under the correlated condition where safely possible.
Test Points
Suspect connectors/joints (wiggle test)
Loop segment insulation resistance under representative conditions
Isolating transformers in the affected section
Vegetation/wildlife/foreign object contact points
Root Cause Verification

After repair, monitor the affected section through a sustained multi-cycle period, ideally spanning the correlated condition (e.g. a rain event), and confirm no recurrence.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the marginal connector or joint.
  • Repair or replace the degraded cable insulation section.
  • Replace the developing isolating transformer.
  • Clear vegetation or address wildlife access near exposed connections.
Preventive Action
  • Include connector/joint tightness and sealing checks in the routine PM.
  • Trend insulation resistance over time, including after wet-weather events, to catch developing faults early.
  • Maintain vegetation clearance around exposed series circuit infrastructure.
Related References

SC-100 Circuit Current Too Low

Symptom — Measured series circuit current is below the commanded intensity step value, and fixtures appear dimmer than expected, even though the CCR appears to be regulating and no fault alarm is present.

Equipment / System

Series circuit loop, CCR current feedback path, and loop impedance elements (cable, transformers, connections).

Possible Causes
HighLoop total impedance has increased beyond the design envelope (high-resistance connections, degraded conductors), preventing the CCR from reaching commanded current within its voltage compliance limit
HighCCR current feedback sensor miscalibrated, causing the controller to under-drive the loop while believing the correct current is being delivered (see Division 01, CCR-091-type faults for the sensor itself)
MediumA partial short or leakage path on the loop diverting a portion of current before it reaches downstream fixtures
MediumAdditional isolating transformers added to the loop beyond the original design load, increasing total impedance
LowerField measurement point or method for verifying current does not match the CCR's own sensing point, giving an apparently low but actually normal reading
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meter (calibrated reference)Insulation resistance tester (5kV megger)CCR configuration/parameter toolSeries circuit as-built drawing and load list
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO before loop segment testing.
  • Treat all loop terminals as live until proven de-energized with a tested meter.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationIndependently measure actual loop current with a calibrated clamp meter at the CCR output and compare against the CCR's displayed/regulated value.
  2. DecisionIndependent measurement confirms the CCR's own reading is accurate and genuinely low → investigate loop impedance/leakage. Independent measurement disagrees with the CCR's displayed value → investigate CCR current feedback sensor per Division 01.
  3. MeasurementMegger the loop to check for a partial insulation breakdown or leakage path that could be diverting current.
  4. ObservationCompare the current loop load list (number and type of transformers) against the original design load to check for unauthorised additions.
  5. MeasurementSegment-test loop resistance against the as-built baseline to identify a section with abnormally high resistance.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific high-resistance segment, leakage point, or an overloaded design condition.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the cause by resolving it and re-verifying current reaches the commanded value within the CCR's voltage compliance.
Test Points
Loop current at CCR output (calibrated reference)
Loop insulation resistance
Loop segment resistance vs. as-built baseline
Current loop load list vs. design
Root Cause Verification

After correction, confirm loop current matches the commanded value at all intensity steps, verified independently, through a full load cycle.

Corrective Action
  • Repair the high-resistance connection(s) or replace the degraded cable section.
  • Repair the leakage/partial short path.
  • Remove or re-engineer unauthorised additional loop load if confirmed as the cause.
  • Address CCR current feedback sensor calibration per Division 01 if confirmed as the cause.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a current, accurate loop load list and require change control for any addition/removal of fixtures.
  • Include loop resistance and insulation trending in the PM programme.
  • Cross-check CCR-displayed current against an independent reference meter periodically.
Related References

SC-101 Circuit Current Too High

Symptom — Measured series circuit current exceeds the commanded intensity step value, with fixtures appearing overly bright and at risk of accelerated lamp/transformer wear or damage.

Equipment / System

Series circuit loop and CCR current feedback/regulation path.

Possible Causes
HighCCR current feedback sensor miscalibrated or drifted, causing the controller to over-drive the loop to reach a falsely-low sensed value (a genuine overcurrent condition on the loop, distinct from a display-only fault — see Division 01)
MediumLoop impedance lower than the original design (e.g. following a design change or incorrect cable substitution), allowing more current to flow for a given CCR drive level if regulation is not functioning correctly
MediumCCR control loop configuration/tuning fault causing it to overshoot the commanded setpoint
LowerField measurement point or method does not match the CCR's own sensing point, giving an apparently high but actually normal reading
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meter (calibrated reference)CCR configuration/parameter toolOscilloscopeManufacturer alarm/parameter code reference
Safety Precautions
  • A genuine overcurrent condition can damage lamps, transformers, and cable insulation — do not leave the circuit running at an unconfirmed high current for longer than necessary to measure.
  • Apply LOTO before any internal CCR or loop wiring inspection.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationIndependently measure actual loop current with a calibrated clamp meter at the CCR output and compare against the commanded step value and the CCR's own displayed value.
  2. DecisionIndependent measurement confirms current is genuinely above the commanded step → proceed with CCR-side investigation. Independent measurement shows current is actually correct → the original reading was a measurement/indication issue, not a genuine overcurrent.
  3. MeasurementVerify the CCR current feedback sensor's calibration against a known reference standard.
  4. MeasurementReview the CCR control loop tuning/configuration for evidence of overshoot at step changes, using an oscilloscope if available.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the current feedback sensor, control loop configuration, or confirm no genuine fault exists.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause through recalibration/reconfiguration and re-verification.
Test Points
Loop current at CCR output (calibrated reference)
CCR current feedback sensor calibration
CCR control loop response at step changes
CCR displayed value vs. independent measurement
Root Cause Verification

After correction, confirm loop current matches the commanded value precisely at all intensity steps, verified independently, with no overshoot at step changes.

Corrective Action
  • Recalibrate or replace the CCR current feedback sensor.
  • Correct CCR control loop tuning/configuration per manufacturer guidance.
  • Verify and correct any unauthorised loop impedance change.
Preventive Action
  • Include CCR current feedback sensor calibration in the annual PM.
  • Cross-check CCR-displayed current against an independent reference meter as standard PM practice.
  • Document control loop tuning parameters and require change control for any adjustment.
Related References

SC-102 Circuit Current Unstable

Symptom — Series circuit current fluctuates or oscillates around the commanded value rather than remaining steady, visible as flickering brightness across the loop or on the CCR's current display/trend.

Equipment / System

Series circuit loop, CCR current feedback and control loop, and loop connections.

Possible Causes
HighA marginal/loose connection somewhere on the loop causing a fluctuating impedance under thermal or vibration influence
HighAn intermittent partial short or leakage path on the loop, varying with moisture or temperature
MediumCCR control loop instability — gain/tuning causing the regulator to hunt around the setpoint
MediumUnstable upstream mains supply to the CCR affecting its ability to regulate steadily
LowerElectromagnetic interference affecting the current feedback signal path
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterOscilloscopeData logger for trend/repeated-event analysisPower quality analyzer / voltage logger
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO before any wiggle test or loop connection inspection with power isolated.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationLog loop current over a representative period to characterise the instability — periodic oscillation, random fluctuation, or correlated with load/weather.
  2. MeasurementWith power isolated, perform a wiggle test on accessible loop connections while monitoring continuity.
  3. MeasurementMegger the loop to check for an intermittent leakage/partial short path, ideally under conditions similar to when instability has been observed.
  4. MeasurementUsing an oscilloscope, examine the CCR's current feedback signal and control loop response for evidence of instability/hunting.
  5. MeasurementCheck upstream mains supply quality/stability feeding the CCR.
  6. DecisionWiggle test or megger reveals a loop-side fault → connection or insulation issue. Loop tests stable but CCR feedback/control shows hunting → CCR control loop tuning fault. Loop and CCR both stable but mains supply is unstable → upstream supply issue.
  7. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific loop connection, an intermittent leakage path, CCR control loop tuning, or upstream mains instability.
  8. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause by reproducing and resolving it under monitored conditions.
Test Points
Loop connections (wiggle test)
Loop insulation resistance under representative conditions
CCR current feedback signal and control loop response
Upstream mains supply quality
Root Cause Verification

After repair, monitor loop current continuously through a sustained multi-cycle period and confirm stable regulation with no recurrence of fluctuation.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the marginal loop connection.
  • Repair the intermittent leakage/partial short path.
  • Correct CCR control loop tuning/configuration per manufacturer guidance.
  • Address upstream mains supply instability if confirmed as the cause.
Preventive Action
  • Include connection tightness checks along the loop in the routine PM.
  • Trend CCR current stability over time to catch developing instability early.
  • Monitor upstream mains supply quality periodically.
Related References

SC-103 Circuit Open Circuit

Symptom — The series circuit loop shows very high or infinite resistance/no continuity end to end, resulting in zero current and typically an open-loop/output-fail alarm at the CCR.

Equipment / System

Series circuit loop — cable, connectors, isolating transformer windings in series through the loop path.

Possible Causes
HighA cable break from mechanical damage (excavation, settlement, rodent damage)
HighA disconnected or failed connector/joint
MediumAn isolating transformer with an open primary winding acting as a series break in the loop
MediumA blown in-line fuse or protective device, where fitted in the loop
LowerAn open link or isolation point left open following prior maintenance
Required Tools
Insulation resistance tester (5kV megger)Time-domain reflectometer (TDR) / cable fault locatorContinuity testerSeries circuit as-built drawing
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO on the CCR output isolator before any loop testing.
  • Treat all loop terminals as live until proven de-energized with a tested meter.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the open-circuit condition by measuring loop resistance/continuity at the CCR output with the loop connected.
  2. MeasurementUse a TDR or cable fault locator, where available, to estimate the distance to the break from the CCR.
  3. MeasurementSegment-test the loop at accessible pull boxes/junctions, working outward, to localise the specific section containing the break.
  4. ObservationInspect the localised section for visible cable damage, a disconnected connector, or a left-open isolation link.
  5. DecisionBreak localised to a specific accessible section → proceed to physical repair. Break not resolved by TDR/segment testing → suspect an isolating transformer open winding, test individual transformers in the suspect section.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific cable section, connector, isolation link, or transformer responsible for the open circuit.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the precise point and cause of the break by physical inspection once localised.
Test Points
Loop resistance/continuity at CCR output
TDR distance-to-fault estimate
Segment continuity at pull boxes/junctions
Individual isolating transformer winding continuity in the suspect section
Root Cause Verification

After repair, confirm loop continuity is restored end to end and run the CCR through a full load cycle with no repeat open-circuit alarm.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the broken cable section.
  • Repair or replace the failed/disconnected connector or joint.
  • Replace the isolating transformer with an open winding.
  • Replace a blown in-line fuse/protective device with the correct rating.
  • Close an isolation link left open from prior maintenance.
Preventive Action
  • Include loop continuity testing in the annual PM.
  • Maintain and update the as-built drawing after any cable route change.
  • Mark and record excavation/cable routes to reduce third-party damage risk.
Related References

SC-104 Circuit Short Circuit

Symptom — The series circuit loop shows an abnormally low resistance between conductors, typically causing the CCR to trip on overcurrent, with possible visible damage (scorching, melted insulation) at the fault location.

Equipment / System

Series circuit loop conductors, connectors, and isolating transformer windings.

Possible Causes
HighCable insulation breakdown between the two loop conductors from mechanical damage or age
HighA damaged or water-filled connector bridging the two conductors
MediumInternal short within an isolating transformer's primary winding
MediumA repair splice or joint incorrectly made, bridging conductors that should remain isolated
LowerA foreign conductive object bridging exposed terminals inside a pull box or fixture base
Required Tools
Insulation resistance tester (5kV megger)Time-domain reflectometer (TDR) / cable fault locatorTrue-RMS clamp meterSeries circuit as-built drawing
Safety Precautions
  • A short circuit may indicate a genuine safety hazard — apply LOTO before any physical inspection and treat the location as potentially damaged/hot from prior fault current.
  • Do not repeatedly reset and re-energize an unresolved short circuit — this risks further damage and fire risk.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the CCR event log for the trip current magnitude and timing to characterise the fault.
  2. MeasurementWith the loop isolated, megger between the two loop conductors to confirm and quantify the low-resistance condition.
  3. MeasurementUse a TDR or cable fault locator, where available, to estimate the distance to the short from the CCR.
  4. MeasurementSegment-test the loop at accessible pull boxes/junctions, working outward, to localise the affected section.
  5. ObservationInspect the localised section for visible damage, scorching, or a water-filled connector.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific cable section, connector, splice, or transformer responsible for the short.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the precise point and cause of the short by physical inspection once localised.
Test Points
Insulation resistance between loop conductors
TDR distance-to-fault estimate
Segment resistance at pull boxes/junctions
Suspect connectors/splices for bridging or water ingress
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-megger the loop to confirm resistance between conductors meets or exceeds the design minimum, then run the CCR through a full load cycle with no repeat trip.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the damaged cable section.
  • Replace the damaged or water-filled connector.
  • Replace the isolating transformer with an internal short.
  • Correct or remake a faulty repair splice/joint per manufacturer-approved method.
  • Remove any foreign conductive object found bridging terminals.
Preventive Action
  • Include loop insulation resistance testing (megger) in the annual PM, trended against baseline.
  • Ensure repair splices/joints are made using manufacturer-approved kits and technique.
  • Seal pull boxes and connector housings to industry standard to prevent water ingress.

SC-105 Circuit Earth Fault

Symptom — The CCR raises an earth fault (ground fault) alarm attributable to this series circuit, indicating leakage current from the loop to earth has exceeded the protection threshold.

Equipment / System

Series circuit loop insulation to earth, cable, isolating transformers, and any conductive structures/fixtures the loop passes through or near.

Possible Causes
HighDegraded cable insulation or an isolating transformer allowing leakage current to earth
HighWater ingress at a cable joint, pull box, or fixture allowing a conductive path to earth
MediumDamaged or pinched cable insulation from installation, excavation, or vegetation growth
MediumA fixture or transformer housing not properly bonded/insulated, creating an unintended earth reference
LowerA minor transient switching surge tripping a tightly set threshold rather than a genuine sustained fault
Required Tools
Insulation resistance tester (5kV megger)Earth fault locator/loop testerTrue-RMS clamp meterSeries circuit as-built drawing
Safety Precautions
  • Treat an earth fault trip as a genuine safety event until proven otherwise — do not repeatedly reset without investigation, as it may indicate a live conductor in contact with a person-accessible surface.
  • Apply LOTO before loop testing.
  • Use insulated tools and PPE rated for the loop's potential voltage.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationRecord the earth fault alarm/trip and any fault-current magnitude reported by the CCR controller.
  2. MeasurementWith the loop isolated, megger the full series circuit loop insulation resistance to earth and compare against baseline commissioning values.
  3. MeasurementIf insulation resistance is low, segment-test the loop (pull boxes/junctions) to localise the affected section.
  4. ObservationInspect suspect sections for visible water ingress, cable damage, or fixture/transformer housing bonding issues.
  5. DecisionLow insulation resistance confirmed in a specific segment → genuine earth fault, proceed to repair. Insulation resistance normal across the loop → investigate the CCR's earth fault relay/sensor per Division 01.

MediumA protection threshold set too tight for the loop's normal operating envelope, causing repeated nuisance trips under otherwise healthy conditions
LowerOperator repeatedly resetting and restarting without allowing proper investigation between trips
Required Tools
MultimeterData logger for trend/repeated-event analysisCCR event log export toolInsulation resistance tester (5kV megger)
Safety Precautions
  • Repeated forced resets against an unresolved trip can cause component damage or create a genuine safety hazard — do not simply keep resetting and retrying without investigation.
  • Apply LOTO before any loop wiggle test or inspection.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationExport and review the CCR event/alarm log across all recent trips on this circuit, correlating timing against intensity step, weather, switching events, and any other available data (e.g. maintenance activity, wildlife reports).
  2. DecisionA clear correlation emerges → re-route the investigation to the matching specific topic (SC-112, SC-113, or SC-114). No clear correlation found → proceed with a full intermittent-fault investigation across the loop.
  3. MeasurementWhere an intermittent connection is suspected, perform a wiggle test — with power isolated — on suspect connectors/terminals while monitoring continuity.
  4. MeasurementMegger the loop and, where practical, repeat under varied conditions to try to reproduce the fault.
  5. ObservationInspect exposed sections of the loop for vegetation, wildlife, or foreign object contact.
  6. ObservationReview whether the protection threshold is set appropriately relative to the loop's normal operating envelope, per manufacturer guidance.
  7. Root CauseConfirm root cause with a sustained multi-cycle test under representative conditions rather than a single successful run.
Test Points
CCR event log timestamps vs. all available correlating data
Suspect connectors/terminals (wiggle test)
Loop insulation resistance under varied conditions
Exposed sections for vegetation/wildlife/foreign object contact
Root Cause Verification

Require a minimum of five consecutive successful full-load operating cycles, including under realistic field conditions, before closing out.

Corrective Action
  • Apply the specific corrective action once a correlation is identified and re-routed to the matching topic.
  • Repair the confirmed intermittent connection or insulation point.
  • Clear vegetation or address wildlife access to exposed loop sections.
  • Adjust the protection threshold only within manufacturer-approved limits with documented justification.
Preventive Action
  • Make event log review with correlation analysis a standing part of any repeated-trip investigation.
  • Track recurring trip patterns per circuit in the site failure database.
  • Maintain vegetation clearance and wildlife deterrence around exposed series circuit infrastructure.

SC-116 Circuit Failure After Maintenance

Symptom — A series circuit fault appears immediately or shortly after a general maintenance activity on the circuit, where the circuit was confirmed working correctly before that activity began.

Equipment / System

Series circuit loop and whatever component(s) were the subject of the maintenance activity.

Possible Causes
HighA connector or terminal disturbed during maintenance was not properly reconnected or re-torqued
HighA component was omitted, connected incorrectly, or left disconnected following the work
MediumTools, debris, or packaging material left inside an enclosure, pull box, or fixture, causing a short or obstruction
MediumAn isolation point or link opened for the work was not fully restored afterward
LowerAn unrelated, coincidental fault occurred independently around the same time as the maintenance activity
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterInsulation resistance tester (5kV megger)Maintenance work record/checklist for the activity performedSeries circuit as-built drawing
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO before re-opening any enclosure or connection for post-maintenance fault investigation.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the maintenance work record/checklist to identify exactly which components, connections, or isolation points were disturbed during the activity.
  2. MeasurementBeginning with the specific location(s) worked on, verify continuity, correct connection, and torque of all disturbed terminals/connectors.
  3. ObservationInspect the work area for any left-behind tools, debris, or packaging material.
  4. MeasurementConfirm any isolation point or link opened for the work has been fully restored.
  5. DecisionFault traced to the specific worked-on location → maintenance-related cause confirmed, proceed to correct. Fault not found at the worked-on location → investigate as a coincidental, unrelated fault per the relevant general topic (SC-096 through SC-115).
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific connection, component, or isolation point affected by the maintenance activity.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause and document it as a maintenance-quality finding, distinct from a component or design fault.
Test Points
All connections/terminals disturbed during the maintenance activity
Work area for left-behind tools/debris
Isolation points/links opened for the work
Continuity and insulation resistance at the worked-on location
Root Cause Verification

After correction, confirm the circuit operates correctly through a full load cycle and re-verify the specific item(s) corrected.

Corrective Action
  • Correctly reconnect and re-torque the affected terminal/connector.
  • Correctly install or reconnect an omitted or incorrectly connected component.
  • Remove any left-behind tools, debris, or packaging material.
  • Fully restore the isolation point/link opened for the work.
Preventive Action
  • Use a documented post-maintenance verification checklist (continuity, insulation resistance, visual inspection for debris) before closing out any maintenance activity.
  • Require a second-person check or sign-off on connections disturbed during maintenance.
  • Log all maintenance activities per circuit to support rapid root-cause correlation for any subsequent fault.

SC-117 Circuit Failure After Cable Jointing

Symptom — A series circuit fault (open circuit, short circuit, earth fault, or elevated leakage) appears specifically following a cable joint/splice repair, at or near the location of the new joint.

Equipment / System

The specific cable joint/splice and the cable sections immediately either side of it.

Possible Causes
HighThe joint was not properly sealed, allowing moisture ingress shortly after installation or the next wet-weather event
HighIncorrect crimping/connection technique at the joint, resulting in a poor mechanical and electrical connection
MediumConductors not fully stripped, cleaned, or seated correctly within the joint kit before crimping/connecting
MediumA resin-filled or heat-shrink joint kit improperly prepared or not fully cured before the circuit was re-energized
LowerThe joint is under mechanical stress (tension, bending) beyond its rating due to inadequate slack or support
Required Tools
Insulation resistance tester (5kV megger)True-RMS clamp meterManufacturer joint kit installation instructionsMicro-ohmmeter / low-resistance ohmmeter
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO before opening or inspecting the joint.
  • Treat a recently made joint with a confirmed fault as a potential safety hazard until proven otherwise.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the fault location correlates with the recent joint location using segment testing or a cable fault locator.
  2. MeasurementWith the loop isolated, megger across the joint specifically to assess its insulation integrity relative to the surrounding cable.
  3. MeasurementMeasure resistance across the joint to check for a poor electrical connection.
  4. ObservationIf accessible without further damaging a sealed joint, visually inspect for evidence of improper preparation — inadequate stripping, incomplete cure, or visible moisture.
  5. DecisionJoint insulation resistance is low relative to the surrounding cable → sealing/moisture ingress fault. Joint resistance is high relative to expected → poor crimp/connection fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate the fault specifically to the joint rather than the surrounding cable.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific preparation or installation error by comparing against the manufacturer's joint kit instructions.
Test Points
Insulation resistance across the joint vs. surrounding cable
Joint resistance (electrical connection quality)
Visual condition of the joint (if accessible)
Mechanical support/slack at the joint location
Root Cause Verification

After remaking the joint, re-megger and re-test resistance across it, then run the circuit through a full load cycle, ideally including a subsequent wet-weather check, with no repeat fault.

Corrective Action
  • Remake the joint per the manufacturer's joint kit instructions, ensuring correct stripping, crimping, sealing, and cure time.
  • Replace the joint kit if the original was improperly prepared or damaged.
  • Provide adequate slack/support at the joint location to relieve mechanical stress.
Preventive Action
  • Require joint installation to be performed by trained personnel following the manufacturer's documented procedure exactly.
  • Include a post-installation insulation resistance test across every new joint before closing out the work.
  • Track joint failure rates by installer/crew and joint kit type to identify systematic quality issues.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SC-01 (Series Circuit Loop Testing), SOP-SC-03 (Cable Jointing Procedure)

SC-118 Circuit Failure After Light Replacement

Symptom — A series circuit fault, or the newly replaced fixture itself failing to operate correctly, appears immediately after a fixture (light) was replaced, even though the rest of the loop was unaffected before the replacement.

Equipment / System

The newly replaced fixture, its isolating transformer, and the connector(s) reconnected during the replacement.

Possible Causes
HighThe replacement fixture or isolating transformer is of the wrong rating/type for the circuit's design current
HighThe connector was not fully mated/locked during reinstallation, allowing water ingress or an open/intermittent connection
MediumPrimary or secondary leads were reversed or miswired during the replacement
MediumThe replacement fixture itself is defective out of the box
LowerA pre-existing, unrelated fault elsewhere on the loop coincidentally became apparent at the same time as the replacement work
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterMultimeterInsulation resistance tester (500V/1000V)Manufacturer fixture/transformer specification sheet
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO before disconnecting or re-inspecting the replaced fixture's connector.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the replacement fixture and its isolating transformer match the design specification (current rating, type) for this circuit.
  2. MeasurementWith the loop isolated, verify the connector is fully mated and locked, and check continuity/insulation at the connection.
  3. MeasurementCheck the wiring of the replacement fixture/transformer against the manufacturer's connection diagram to rule out reversed or miswired leads.
  4. DecisionWrong rating/type confirmed → replace with correct component. Connector not fully mated or wiring error found → correct the connection. Component and connection both correct but fixture still faulty → likely a defective replacement unit.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the fixture/transformer rating, the connector, the wiring, or a defective new unit.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause before closing out the replacement work.
Test Points
Replacement fixture/transformer rating vs. design specification
Connector mating/locking and continuity
Wiring configuration vs. manufacturer connection diagram
Isolation resistance at the new connection
Root Cause Verification

After correction, confirm the fixture illuminates correctly at all intensity steps and re-check that adjacent fixtures and overall loop parameters remain unaffected.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the incorrect fixture/transformer with the correct manufacturer-specified unit.
  • Fully mate and lock the connector, replacing it if damaged.
  • Correct reversed or miswired leads per the manufacturer's connection diagram.
  • Replace a confirmed defective replacement unit under warranty where applicable.
Preventive Action
  • Verify replacement component rating/type against the design specification before installation, not just after a fault appears.
  • Include a post-replacement functional and insulation check as a standard step before closing out fixture replacement work.
  • Stock only manufacturer-specified, correctly rated replacement fixtures and transformers.
Related References

The newly replaced isolating transformer and the connectors/leads reconnected during its replacement.

SC-120 Circuit Failure After Connector Replacement

Symptom — A series circuit fault (open circuit, intermittent connection, or earth fault/leakage) appears immediately after a field connector was replaced at a fixture, transformer, or loop joint location.

Equipment / System

The newly replaced connector and its immediate connections at both ends.

Possible Causes
HighThe replacement connector was wired with incorrect polarity/pinning
HighThe connector was not fully mated/locked, or was mated with visible misalignment
MediumThe replacement connector is the wrong type or rating for the application (e.g. voltage/current rating, IP rating mismatch)
MediumPoor waterproofing or potting of the new connector allows moisture ingress shortly after installation
LowerA pre-existing, unrelated fault elsewhere on the loop coincidentally became apparent at the same time as the replacement work
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterMultimeterInsulation resistance tester (500V/1000V)Manufacturer connector specification and pinning diagram
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO before disconnecting or re-inspecting the replaced connector.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the replacement connector's type and rating match the application per the manufacturer's specification.
  2. MeasurementWith the loop isolated, verify wiring polarity/pinning at the connector against the manufacturer's diagram.
  3. MeasurementPhysically inspect the connector for full mating/locking and correct alignment.
  4. MeasurementMegger across the new connection to check for adequate insulation, particularly if the connector has been exposed to weather since installation.
  5. DecisionPolarity/pinning error confirmed → correct the wiring. Connector not fully mated or misaligned → correct the mating. Insulation resistance low → waterproofing/potting fault, remake or replace the connector.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the connector's wiring, its mating/alignment, or its waterproofing/sealing quality.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause before closing out the replacement work.
Test Points
Connector wiring polarity/pinning vs. manufacturer diagram
Connector mating/locking and alignment
Insulation resistance across the new connection
Connector type/rating vs. application requirement
Root Cause Verification

After correction, confirm the circuit operates correctly through a full load cycle, ideally including a subsequent wet-weather check for connectors exposed outdoors, with no repeat fault.

Corrective Action
  • Correct the wiring polarity/pinning at the connector.
  • Fully mate and lock the connector, correcting any misalignment.
  • Replace the connector with the correct manufacturer-specified type/rating.
  • Remake the connector with proper waterproofing/potting per manufacturer instructions.
Preventive Action
  • Verify connector type/rating against the application requirement before installation.
  • Include a post-installation mating check and insulation test as standard practice for every connector replacement.
  • Train personnel on correct connector mating and waterproofing technique per manufacturer instructions.
