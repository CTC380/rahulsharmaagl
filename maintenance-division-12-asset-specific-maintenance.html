Asset-Specific Maintenance

While Divisions 01–11 define maintenance by activity or methodology, Division 12 answers a different question: for this particular AGL asset, what maintenance service should be performed, what should be checked, and how should the asset be returned to service? It is the equipment-level service guide of the handbook.

Purpose: Division 12 provides a standardized service framework for individual AGL assets — asset-specific requirements, service checkpoints, component-level inspection, and functional, electrical, mechanical, and optical verification — so maintenance personnel do not treat every AGL component in exactly the same way.

Core principle: every asset has a service card, has a maintenance history, has a condition status, has a test record, has a return-to-service record. This is the asset-level operating layer of the handbook — the technician identifies the asset, opens its service card, follows the applicable maintenance procedure, records the test results, updates the condition, and closes the service history. That structure also makes the handbook easier to convert into an AGL Asset Maintenance Database / CMMS-style system, because every physical asset has a defined maintenance identity, service procedure, and history.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about asset-specific maintenance, based on the topics in this division.

7 QUESTIONS
Q1What is Asset-Specific Maintenance?

While Divisions 01 to 11 define maintenance by activity or methodology, Division 12 answers what maintenance service should be performed on a particular AGL asset, what should be checked, and how the asset should be verified. It provides a standardized service framework for individual assets.

Q2What does Division 12 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 12 (Asset-Specific Maintenance) contains 35 topics in seven groups: Group A Runway Assets (6 topics), Group B Taxiway Assets (5 topics), Group C Approach / Visual Aids (4 topics), Group D Signage (5 topics), Group E Electrical Infrastructure (7 topics), Group F Control Systems (4 topics) and Group G Maintenance Records (4 topics).

Q3What is the asset service sequence?

Nine steps: Identify, Isolate, Inspect, Clean, Service, Test, Functionally Verify, Restore and Record. The sequence is the same for every asset, but the exact service requirements are adapted to the individual asset.

Q4What is an Asset Service Card?

A common service-documentation format used for every asset in the division. It records the asset name, ID, location, circuit, manufacturer, model and rating, applicable drawings and manuals, safety and isolation requirements, inspection points, cleaning, mechanical, electrical and optical checks, functional test, acceptance criteria, defects identified, components replaced, test results, final condition, technician, date, supervisor or engineer and remarks.

Q5What five questions does every asset service answer?

What is wrong (defect identification), why did it happen (root-cause assessment where necessary), what must be done (repair, replacement, cleaning, adjustment or monitoring), how do we know it is fixed (testing and functional verification), and what happens next (routine maintenance, monitoring, predictive analysis or replacement planning).

Q6How does Division 12 relate to the detailed fixture, CCR, transformer, cable and connector divisions?

It does not duplicate their detailed procedures. It provides the asset-level service card and directs personnel to the detailed technical procedure, for example fixtures to Division 06, CCRs to Division 07, transformers to Division 08, cables to Division 09 and connectors to Division 10.

Q7How does the emphasis change from asset to asset?

The same nine-step sequence applies, but the focus differs. A runway edge light emphasizes optical condition, alignment, mounting, frangibility and secondary connection; a CCR emphasizes electrical performance, cooling, protection, control, and alarms and output regulation; a PAPI emphasizes optical condition, alignment, elevation, functional indication and electrical condition.
