Overview

ICAO PAPI Overview

A Precision Approach Path Indicator (PAPI) gives pilots visual approach-slope guidance on final approach. Its performance depends on correct siting, installation, elevation, unit spacing, aiming, optical performance, colour, intensity, inspection and maintenance.

For international projects, assess PAPI requirements against the applicable edition of ICAO Annex 14, Volume I, the relevant ICAO guidance material and the requirements of the State or national aviation authority.

Configuration

System Configuration and Unit Identification

Standard installation

Four light units in a row generally perpendicular to the runway centreline, with optical systems, light sources, mounting, electrical supply, control equipment, cabling, foundations and alignment and aiming provisions.

Different aiming per unit

Each unit has its own vertical aiming relationship to the others. The difference in aiming angles creates the red/white transition.

Consistent identification

The unit nearest the runway is the inboard unit and the furthest is the outboard unit. Label units A to D (or the project equivalent) consistently across drawings, test sheets and maintenance records.

Location & Elevation

PAPI Location, Distance from Threshold and Elevation

PAPI location is not a convenient distance from the threshold. ICAO-related aerodrome inspection guidance describes it as determined from the approach angle, the difference in level between the threshold and the PAPI units, and the minimum eye height over the threshold. There is no single universal ICAO PAPI distance for every runway.

Calculation should consider

Selected approach slope, PAPI elevation, runway threshold elevation, required minimum eye height, aircraft characteristics, obstacle clearance and runway operational constraints. A change in PAPI elevation can change the nominal location.

Runway profile

Where runway elevation changes significantly between threshold and PAPI, the simple theoretical geometry may not hold. Use threshold elevation, unit elevation, centreline profile, terrain, runway slope and approach geometry from the actual survey.

Elevation survey

Establish survey references for the threshold, runway centreline, PAPI foundation, each unit and relevant reference points, and retain the results with the commissioning documents as the baseline for maintenance. An incorrect elevation affects approach slope, TCH, minimum eye height, location and visual indications.

Aircraft characteristics

Consider cockpit height, eye-to-wheel height, aircraft category, approach speed and operational requirements. The most demanding aircraft expected to use the runway may drive the design; a design for one category is not automatically suitable for all.

Obstacles and ILS

PAPI does not remove the need for obstacle assessment. Where both PAPI and ILS exist, coordinate glide path, nominal slope, threshold crossing geometry, antenna location and eye height. Do not adjust a PAPI because it looks different from the ILS without checking the approved design relationship.

Installation

Unit Spacing, Horizontal Alignment and Aiming

Spacing

Establish spacing from the applicable design requirements and drawings, then measure and record the actual value instead of judging by eye.

Horizontal alignment

Alignment controls beam azimuth. Vertical angles can be perfect and the guidance still wrong if the units are horizontally misaligned. Check with a survey or alignment method and record runway reference, azimuth, design, actual, tolerance and result.

Aiming: the four units are not aimed at identical vertical angles. The setting angles create the 4 White, 3W/1R, 2W/2R, 1W/3R and 4 Red sectors. The exact angles and tolerances come from the current ICAO requirements and the approved project design.

Photometry

PAPI Photometry, Intensity, Colour and Beam Coverage

Photometry verifies optical performance. Depending on the applicable standard and procedure it can address intensity, beam distribution, colour, red/white transition, horizontal and vertical coverage and individual unit performance, using calibrated equipment and an approved procedure. A simple visual check cannot establish photometric compliance.

Intensity

Affected by light-source condition, contamination, filter, optical alignment, electrical supply, internal optics and environment. Produce measured data per unit.

Colour

Relies on distinguishing aviation white and aviation red. Abnormal colour can come from damaged filters, contamination, wrong components, source degradation or internal damage.

Transition and coverage

The transition must be defined enough to give the intended information. A unit can have adequate centre-beam intensity and still fail elsewhere in its distribution. Do not judge colour by looking at the unit from close range.

Equipment

Calibrated photometer, angular measurement system, alignment equipment, colour measurement equipment, data acquisition and reference equipment. Before testing check identification, calibration status, range, resolution, test configuration and environment.

Test report should identify

Instrument manufacturer, model, serial number, calibration date and certificate, measurement range, test date, technician and environmental conditions, so an auditor can see how the result was obtained.

Inspection & Maintenance

PAPI Inspection and Maintenance

Inspection

Visual: output, housing, lens, contamination, damage, obstructions, foundation, mounting. Mechanical: level, alignment, mounting, fasteners, foundation. Electrical: supply, wiring, connectors, protection, controls. Photometric: applicable intensity, colour, distribution, transition.

Maintenance activities

Cleaning, visual, optical, mechanical and electrical inspection, alignment verification, functional testing, photometry where required, fault recording and corrective maintenance.

Maintenance frequency should come from the airport's approved maintenance programme and applicable authority and manufacturer documents, not from a generic web article.

Shared concepts

PAPI gives visual approach guidance; the four-unit PAPI uses different aiming angles; siting, elevation, TCH/MEHT, optical performance, inspection and maintenance all matter.

Where they differ

Numerical requirements, terminology, tolerances, siting procedures and equipment specifications. Use FAA publications for FAA-governed projects, and ICAO provisions plus the State's implementation for ICAO-based projects.

Records

As-Built Documentation, Baselines and Audit File

As-built and baseline

Record coordinates, elevations, spacing, runway offset, aiming angles, equipment model and serial numbers, electrical and control arrangement and photometric results. Baseline data (unit elevation, alignment, aiming angle, intensity, colour, beam, electrical measurements) lets future maintenance compare with the accepted condition. The original design drawing is not enough.

Audit file

Approved design, siting calculation, survey data, installation and as-built drawings, alignment records, photometric reports, calibration certificates, commissioning report, maintenance, fault and corrective-action records, and equipment manuals.

Operations

Operations, Certification and What Engineers Should Verify

PAPI is an operational visual aid, so compliance must support reliability: availability, correct indication, brightness, alignment, maintenance status, fault reporting and aerodrome operating procedures. ICAO contingency material lists visual-aid status, including PAPI calibration, among items for assessing aerodrome readiness, and Annex 14 Vol. I is a core reference for aerodrome certification.

Do not present an unverified number as an ICAO requirement. Explain why each parameter matters, name the governing document, and quote numbers only after verifying them against the current applicable edition.

FAQ

ICAO PAPI Requirements FAQ

Q1What is the ICAO standard for PAPI?

PAPI requirements are primarily in ICAO Annex 14, Volume I (Aerodromes: Design and Operations), together with relevant ICAO guidance and applicable State requirements.

Q2Does ICAO specify PAPI installation requirements?

Yes. ICAO aerodrome provisions address the design and operational requirements for visual aids including PAPI. Check the current applicable edition and the State implementation.

Q3How many lights does an ICAO PAPI have?

A standard PAPI uses four light units. APAPI is the two-unit abbreviated configuration.

Q4What is the PAPI approach angle?

The nominal approach slope comes from the approved aerodrome design. Individual units have different setting angles that create the visual indications.

Q5Does ICAO specify one universal PAPI distance from the threshold?

No. Location comes from the geometry, including approach slope, PAPI elevation, threshold elevation and minimum eye height considerations.

Q6What is PAPI TCH?

Threshold Crossing Height: the height associated with the aircraft's visual approach geometry at the threshold, considered when determining PAPI location and approach-slope geometry.

Q7Does PAPI require photometric testing?

Optical performance should be verified according to the applicable equipment, inspection and regulatory requirements. Photometry gives objective evidence of intensity, colour and beam performance.

Q8What is the difference between PAPI and APAPI?

PAPI normally uses four units and APAPI uses two, so the visual indication and design geometry differ.

Q9Are ICAO and FAA PAPI requirements the same?

No. The principles are similar, but numerical requirements, terminology, tolerances and procedures can differ. Use the governing standard for the project.

Q10Can FAA PAPI values be used for an ICAO project?

Only where the project or competent authority specifically adopts them. Do not present FAA values as ICAO requirements without verification.

Q11What documents should be checked before PAPI installation?

The applicable ICAO provisions, State or national authority requirements, approved aerodrome design, PAPI equipment documentation and project specifications.

Q12How often should PAPI be inspected?

The interval comes from the airport maintenance programme, authority requirements and equipment documentation. Do not use an arbitrary generic interval as a regulatory requirement.

Q13What should be done after PAPI maintenance?

Verify the work, check the physical installation, confirm alignment where affected, run a functional test, perform photometry where required, and record the results.
