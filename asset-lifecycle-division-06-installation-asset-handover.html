Installation & Asset Handover

The transition point between procurement and the operational asset lifecycle. Division 05 establishes what is procured and the supplier's lifecycle obligations; Division 06 establishes the physical asset, verifies that it has actually been installed as intended, captures its final technical identity and condition, establishes baseline performance, and transfers complete asset information into the controlled asset-management system.

Initial Issue

The question this division answers — How is a procured AGL asset physically installed, verified, tested against a baseline, inspected, formally accepted, and handed over — with the physical asset and its lifecycle information transferred together — into operational asset management?

Where Division 06 Sits in the Lifecycle

Division 06 stays focused on the handover gateway — it does not decide procurement or supplier obligations (Division 05), and it does not run day-to-day operational maintenance, inspection or condition monitoring (Division 07 onward). It establishes the verified, baseline-tested, formally accepted record that everything downstream depends on.

05 — Procurement & Supplier Lifecycle
06 — Installation & Asset Handover
07 onward — Operate → Maintain → Inspect → Test → Monitor → Optimize → Replace → Dispose

INST-01 Asset Installation Records
Purpose

Every installed AGL asset should have a permanent installation record — the first permanent field record for the asset.

Minimum Information
  • Asset ID
  • Equipment type
  • Manufacturer
  • Model
  • Serial number
  • Installation date
  • Location
  • Contractor
  • Installation work order/project
  • Drawing reference
  • Installation status
  • Inspection status
  • Acceptance status
What the Record Establishes

What was actually installed, where it was installed, and when it was installed.

INST-12 As-Built Drawings
Purpose

As-built drawings should represent the actual installed system, not simply the original design.

As-Built Information May Include
  • Final equipment locations
  • Cable routes
  • Cable lengths
  • Circuit numbers
  • Transformer locations
  • Base cans
  • Fixtures
  • Signs
  • PAPI
  • CCRs
  • Earthing
  • Junctions
  • Handholes
  • Control interfaces
  • Changes from approved design
Critical Principle

If the field installation differs from the design drawing, the as-built documentation must reflect the actual installation. This becomes a fundamental input for future maintenance, troubleshooting, modification, inspection, testing, replacement and expansion.

INST-18 Asset Acceptance
Purpose

Asset acceptance is the formal decision that the installed equipment satisfies the approved requirements and can enter the operational asset lifecycle.

Acceptance Should Consider
  • Installation complete
  • Testing complete
  • Defects closed
  • Documentation complete
  • As-built drawings complete
  • Asset identification complete
  • Baseline established
  • Initial inspection complete
  • Safety requirements satisfied
  • Contract requirements satisfied
Acceptance Status — Possible Statuses
  • Accepted
  • Accepted with Minor Outstanding Items
  • Conditionally Accepted
  • Not Accepted

The exact acceptance criteria should be defined in the project's approved commissioning/handover procedure.

Most Important Design Principle

Division 06 should remain the handover gateway between project delivery and operational asset management. It defines how the physical asset is verified, tested against a baseline, inspected, formally accepted and transferred as data — not what is procured or the supplier's obligations (Division 05), and not day-to-day operational maintenance, inspection or condition monitoring (Division 07 onward).

Installation is not complete when equipment is physically installed; the asset lifecycle begins only when the physical installation, verified technical information, baseline performance, initial condition, documentation, acceptance and ownership have been formally handed over.

Division 05 — PROCURE
Division 06 — INSTALL, VERIFY & HANDOVER
Division 07 onward — OPERATE → MAINTAIN → INSPECT → TEST → MONITOR → OPTIMIZE → REPLACE → DISPOSE

This is why the framework places Division 06 immediately after Division 05: Division 05 decides what is procured and the supplier's lifecycle obligations; Division 06 establishes the verified, baseline-tested, formally accepted asset that the entire operational lifecycle depends on.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Installation & Asset Handover in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide.

Q1What is Division 06 — Installation & Asset Handover in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

Division 06 is the transition point between procurement and the operational asset lifecycle. It covers 22 topics on installation records, equipment identification, installation verification, photographs, cable/transformer/fixture/sign/PAPI/base-can/earthing records, as-built drawings, installation and commissioning documentation, baseline performance, initial inspection, initial condition, asset acceptance, warranty commencement, asset handover, asset-register update and the handover dossier.

Q2What is an asset installation record?

Every installed AGL asset should have a permanent installation record. It is the first permanent field record for the asset and captures its asset ID, equipment type, manufacturer, model, serial number and installation date.

Q3Why does equipment identification matter at installation?

Equipment identification ensures the physical asset and the asset register refer to exactly the same equipment. It links the asset ID, asset tag, manufacturer, model, serial number and equipment type.

Q4Which records are kept for cables, transformers, fixtures, signs, PAPI, base cans and earthing?

Division 06 has a dedicated record topic for each of these asset types, covering cables, isolating transformers, fixtures, airfield signs, PAPI systems, base cans and earthing arrangements. Each captures the identity and installed configuration of the asset as part of handover.

Q5What is the difference between as-built drawings and design drawings?

As-built drawings should represent the actual installed system, not simply the original design. They record final equipment locations, cable routes and lengths, circuit numbers, transformer locations and base cans.

Q6What must be complete before an asset is accepted?

Asset acceptance is the formal decision that the installed equipment satisfies the approved requirements and can enter the operational lifecycle. Its checks include installation complete, testing complete, defects closed, documentation complete and as-built drawings complete.

Q7What is a handover dossier?

The handover dossier is the complete lifecycle information package transferred to the asset owner or operator. Asset handover moves the asset from the project stage into operational asset management, and the asset register is then updated with the final verified installation information.

Q8When does the warranty start, and what is the initial baseline?

Warranty commencement should be clearly linked to the contractual warranty terms and the relevant project milestone. Baseline performance and initial asset condition establish the verified starting point against which future deterioration can be measured.
