Condition-Based Maintenance

The decision layer of the handbook. Condition-Based Maintenance uses the actual measured and observed condition of an asset — not its age or a fixed calendar — to determine what maintenance action is justified, when, and how urgently.

The key question: what is the actual condition of the asset, and what maintenance does that condition justify?

Distinction: preventive maintenance runs on a schedule, corrective maintenance responds to failure, and predictive maintenance follows a trend. Condition-Based Maintenance sits apart from all three — it evaluates evidence (physical, environmental, and performance condition) and converts that evidence directly into a classification, a threshold check, and a maintenance decision.

CBM Philosophy

Every condition-based decision in this division follows the same underlying sequence — evidence is collected, the asset is classified, and only then is a maintenance action decided.

Preventive — time / schedule Corrective — defect / failure Predictive — trend / anticipation Condition-Based — actual measured condition

Asset A — 10 years old

  • Clean, no corrosion
  • Stable electrical readings
  • No failures, good optical performance
Decision: continue operation / routine PM

Asset B — 4 years old

  • Significant corrosion
  • Repeated moisture ingress
  • Increasing connector failures
Decision: condition-based corrective action despite young age

Principle: asset age alone should not determine maintenance. Asset condition should.

Condition Classification

A generic five-class condition scale — the exact thresholds should be defined in the airport's approved asset-condition matrix.

ConditionMeaningTypical Action
C1 — GoodNormal conditionRoutine PM
C2 — SatisfactoryMinor deteriorationMonitor
C3 — DeterioratingSignificant deteriorationPlanned maintenance
C4 — PoorHigh deterioration / riskPriority corrective action
C5 — CriticalUnacceptable conditionImmediate action / replacement

AGL Asset Condition Index (ACI): physical condition (25%) + electrical condition (25%) + optical/performance condition (20%) + environmental condition (15%) + reliability/maintenance history (15%) can be combined into a weighted score for airport-wide asset ranking. These bands are a proposed management framework, not universal regulatory acceptance limits — final thresholds should be set against the airport's approved standards and historical data.

Repair-or-Replace Decision

The core trade-off behind every condition-based corrective action.

Repair When

  • Asset remains structurally sound
  • Repair is technically feasible
  • Performance can be restored
  • Repair cost is reasonable
  • Spare parts are available
  • Remaining life is acceptable

Replace When

  • Structural integrity is compromised
  • Failure is recurring
  • Repair is no longer economical
  • Spare parts are obsolete / unavailable
  • Performance cannot be reliably restored
  • Remaining useful life is low, or safety / operational risk is high

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about condition-based maintenance, based on the topics in this division.

9 QUESTIONS
Q1What is condition-based maintenance in AGL?

Condition-based maintenance uses the actual measured and observed condition of an asset, not its age or a fixed calendar, to determine what maintenance action is justified, when, and how urgently. The key question is what the actual condition of the asset is and what maintenance that condition justifies.

Q2What does Division 04 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 04 (Condition-Based Maintenance) contains 28 topics in four groups: Group A Physical Condition (11 topics), Group B Environmental Condition (7 topics), Group C Performance Condition (5 topics) and Group D Maintenance Decision (5 topics).

Q3How does condition-based maintenance differ from preventive, corrective and predictive maintenance?

Preventive maintenance runs on time or schedule, corrective maintenance responds to a defect or failure, and predictive maintenance follows a trend. Condition-based maintenance evaluates evidence of physical, environmental and performance condition and converts it directly into a classification, a threshold check and a maintenance decision.

Q4Should asset age decide when maintenance is done?

No. The page contrasts a 10-year-old asset that is clean, electrically stable and optically sound, which continues in operation with routine PM, with a 4-year-old asset showing significant corrosion, repeated moisture ingress and increasing connector failures, which justifies condition-based corrective action despite its young age. Asset condition, not age, should determine maintenance.

Q5What are the condition classes C1 to C5?

C1 Good (normal condition, routine PM), C2 Satisfactory (minor deterioration, monitor), C3 Deteriorating (significant deterioration, planned maintenance), C4 Poor (high deterioration or risk, priority corrective action) and C5 Critical (unacceptable condition, immediate action or replacement). Exact thresholds should be defined in the airport's approved asset-condition matrix.

Q6How do condition, trend and risk combine into a decision?

The decision matrix pairs each class with its trend and risk. C1 stable and low risk gets routine PM; C2 stable gets monitoring; C3 deteriorating at medium risk gets increased monitoring; C4 stable at medium risk gets planned maintenance; C4 deteriorating at high risk gets corrective action; and C5 at very high risk gets immediate intervention.

Q7What is the AGL Asset Condition Index (ACI)?

It is a weighted score for airport-wide asset ranking: physical condition 25%, electrical condition 25%, optical/performance condition 20%, environmental condition 15% and reliability/maintenance history 15%. The bands are a proposed management framework, not universal regulatory acceptance limits, so final thresholds should be set against the airport's approved standards and historical data.

Q8When should an asset be repaired and when replaced?

Repair applies when the asset remains structurally sound, repair is technically feasible, performance can be restored, repair cost is reasonable and spare parts are available. Replace when structural integrity is compromised, failure is recurring, repair is no longer economical, spares are obsolete or unavailable, performance cannot be reliably restored, or remaining useful life is low or safety and operational risk is high.

Q9What is the condition-based maintenance workflow?

Ten steps: identify the asset, collect data, compare history, classify condition, determine risk, check the threshold zone (Normal, Watch, Maintenance or Critical), decide, perform maintenance, verify, and update the record.
