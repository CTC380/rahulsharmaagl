Standards & Compliance Comparison

ICAO VS DGCA

Annex 14, Volume I vs India's regulatory and certification framework — ICAO provides the international Standards and Recommended Practices for aerodrome visual aids, while DGCA converts the applicable international requirements into India's regulatory and certification framework. For an Indian airport project, ICAO is the international baseline and DGCA is the Indian regulatory authority and applicable national requirement.

02 · Definitions

What Is ICAO & DGCA?

ICAO

ICAO = international aerodrome standard. Annex 14, Volume I covers the design and operation of aerodromes, establishing SARPs for physical characteristics, obstacle limitation surfaces, visual aids, and certain facilities and technical services at aerodromes.

Annex 14 covers

  • Aerodrome geometry, runways & taxiways
  • Visual aids, markings, lights & signs
  • PAPI & obstacle lighting
  • Electrical systems & secondary power

DGCA

DGCA = Indian regulatory implementation and oversight. DGCA is India's aviation regulatory authority; its material shows requirements based on Annex 14, including aerodrome data, movement-area facilities and visual aids such as markings, lights and signs. DGCA documents can incorporate or be based on ICAO provisions, but DGCA requirements are the ones that must be followed for regulatory compliance in India where applicable.

DGCA requirements can cover

  • Aerodrome certification & standards
  • Operations, safety & visual aids
  • Inspection, maintenance & compliance monitoring
  • Licensing / certification requirements
03 · Key Difference

International Standard vs Indian Regulatory Implementation

The easiest way to remember the difference between ICAO and DGCA:

ICAO

Establishes what international aerodrome standards should be achieved

DGCA

Establishes what is required for compliance and oversight in India

Engineering point: This is a useful engineering simplification, although the actual relationship depends on the specific provision and the applicable Indian regulatory document.

05 · AGL Example

Runway Edge Lights

ICAO

Annex 14 specifies requirements such as when runway edge lights are required, location, spacing, colour, light characteristics, intensity/distribution and operational application. ICAO emphasizes standardization of visual aids because pilots need consistent visual information internationally.

DGCA

For an Indian airport, the applicable DGCA requirements and approved aerodrome documentation establish how those requirements are applied and verified within India's regulatory system.

ICAO provides the international technical baseline, while DGCA compliance determines whether the Indian aerodrome satisfies the applicable regulatory requirements.

06 · PAPI

Similar Concept, Requirements Applied Separately

PAPI is another good example of a shared international concept applied through two different tracks:

ICAO track

PAPI visual requirements → installation geometry → light characteristics → operational requirements.

DGCA track

Applicable Indian requirements → aerodrome approval/certification → inspection/calibration → operational compliance.

The two should not be treated as completely independent standards. The DGCA framework may adopt or reference ICAO requirements, while also applying Indian regulatory provisions.

07 · Photometry

Why This Distinction Matters for Manufacturers

For AGL manufacturers, this distinction is very important. For a runway light photometric report, you should identify which ICAO requirement was checked, which DGCA/project requirement applies, which test standard was used, and which acceptance limits were applied.

Avoid

"ICAO/DGCA compliant."

Better

"Photometric performance verified against the applicable ICAO Annex 14 requirements and corresponding DGCA/project requirements."

08 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: ICAO and DGCA are two completely separate technical standards
Fact

DGCA documents can be based on ICAO SARPs while incorporating India's regulatory, operational and administrative requirements — the ICAO requirement is adopted or referenced, then implemented through the DGCA framework.

Myth: "ICAO/DGCA compliant" is a sufficient technical statement
Fact

Photometry, geometry, colour, PAPI, approach lighting, CCR, power supply, installation and maintenance requirements should each be compared against the exact applicable reference in an item-by-item compliance matrix.

Myth: An ICAO-compliant fixture automatically satisfies DGCA requirements
Fact

The applicable DGCA requirement and project specification should always be checked first, then mapped back to ICAO Annex 14 and relevant supporting ICAO documents.

09 · Local & National Requirements

Why an Indian Project Has Several Layers

ICAO provides international requirements, but individual States can have national requirements. This is why an Indian airport project may have ICAO requirements, DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific requirements, contract requirements, applicable BIS/IEC standards and manufacturer requirements — all within the same technical submittal.

11 · Equipment Example

Isolation Transformer — Three Levels

Product & system level

Isolation transformer → electrical characteristics → insulation → temperature → routine/type tests. At system level: CCR → primary cable → isolation transformer → secondary → AGL fixture.

Regulatory level

DGCA / ICAO → AGL system requirements → project specification → equipment compliance.

The transformer cannot be declared compliant merely because its individual electrical test passed — the complete AGL system also needs to satisfy the applicable requirements.

14 · ICAO vs DGCA vs AAI

The Third Layer on Indian Projects

For Indian airport projects, there is often another layer beyond ICAO and DGCA: AAI or the airport operator.

ICAO — International Standard
↓
DGCA — Regulatory Authority
↓
AAI / Airport Operator — Project Implementation
↓
Contractor / Manufacturer — Design + Supply + Install

These roles should not be mixed: ICAO handles international standardization, DGCA handles regulatory oversight and compliance, and AAI/airport operator handles project, operational and infrastructure requirements.

16 · Engineering Verdict

Which Framework Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

ICAO establishes what international aerodrome standards should be achieved. DGCA establishes what is required for compliance and oversight in India. For AGL specifically, the safest approach is to start with the applicable DGCA requirement and airport/project specification, then map each requirement back to ICAO Annex 14 and the relevant supporting ICAO documents.

For an Indian AGL project, don't write simply "equipment complies with ICAO" or "equipment complies with DGCA." Use an item-by-item compliance matrix showing: ICAO requirement → applicable DGCA requirement → project specification → test/verification evidence.

ICAOInternational aerodrome SARPs — the "what should be achieved" baseline.
DGCAIndian aviation regulatory requirements — the "what is required for compliance in India" authority.

17 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Are ICAO and DGCA interchangeable standards for AGL?

No. ICAO provides the international Standards and Recommended Practices, while DGCA converts the applicable international requirements into India's regulatory and certification framework — DGCA requirements are the ones that must be followed for compliance in India.

Does an ICAO-compliant fixture automatically satisfy DGCA requirements?

No. The applicable DGCA requirement and project specification should always be checked first, then mapped back to ICAO Annex 14 and the relevant supporting ICAO documents.

Is DGCA a completely separate technical standard from ICAO?

Not necessarily. DGCA documents can be based on ICAO SARPs while incorporating India's regulatory, operational and administrative requirements — the relationship is one of adoption/reference, not independence.

Which authority applies on an Indian airport project?

The applicable DGCA requirements and project/airport specification govern first, with ICAO Annex 14 as the international baseline reference, and AAI/airport operator requirements layered on top for project implementation.

Is it enough to write "ICAO/DGCA compliant" in a technical submittal?

No. An item-by-item compliance matrix identifying the ICAO reference, applicable DGCA requirement, project requirement and verification evidence is much stronger for tender compliance and airport authority approval.

What is the main ICAO document behind DGCA's AGL requirements?

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I — Aerodrome Design and Operations — is the principal reference. DGCA material identifies provisions based on Annex 14 Volumes I and II, including visual aids such as indicators, markings, lights, signs and markers.

18 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual airport project, verify the current applicable editions of the relevant standards and DGCA documents before using this comparison for project design.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — currently the 9th Edition, July 2022, with Amendment 18 dated August 2025 and subsequent corrigenda listed by ICAO in 2026.

ICAO Doc 9157 & Doc 9137

Aerodrome Design Manual and related guidance identified by ICAO as relevant references for visual aids and electrical systems.

Applicable DGCA CARs / Orders

Civil Aviation Requirements and procedures covering aerodrome data, movement-area facilities and visual aids, based on Annex 14 Volumes I and II.

AAI / Airport Project Specification

Project-level implementation requirements layered on top of the DGCA framework for a specific Indian aerodrome.

Equipment-Level Standards

Applicable BIS/IS, IEC, EN or FAA references where specifically called out in the project specification.

Final Engineering Note

The applicable DGCA requirement and project/airport specification determine what is actually mandatory for an Indian aerodrome — check these first, then map back to ICAO.

ICAO = international aerodrome standard. DGCA = Indian regulatory implementation and oversight. Airport/project specification = project-specific contractual requirements. Do not assume "ICAO/DGCA compliant" is sufficient — compare each requirement against the exact applicable reference.
