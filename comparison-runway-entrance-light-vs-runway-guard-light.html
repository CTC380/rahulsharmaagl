Runway & Taxiway Lighting Comparison

Runway Entrance Light VS Runway Guard Light

Automated Red Warning vs Static Yellow Conspicuity — a Runway Entrance Light (REL) is part of the FAA's automated Runway Status Lights (RWSL) system: it is normally unlit and illuminates red only when surface/terminal surveillance detects that it is unsafe to enter or cross the runway. A Runway Guard Light (RGL) is a static yellow fixture, required at essentially every runway holding position under ICAO Annex 14 and FAA AC 150/5340-30, that continuously marks the boundary regardless of real-time runway traffic. Neither light is a stop command or a substitute for an ATC clearance.

02 · The Core Idea

Always-On Warning vs Conditional Automated Warning

Both lights can appear along the same taxi route toward a runway, but they hand the pilot fundamentally different kinds of information. Runway Guard Lights are static: they mark the holding position continuously, regardless of whether the runway is actually occupied — the message is always "caution, runway ahead." Runway Entrance Lights, by contrast, are part of the automated RWSL system: they are normally off and illuminate red only when surface/terminal surveillance detects high-speed traffic on or approaching the runway — the message, when lit, is "stop — do not enter or cross, this specific crossing is unsafe right now."

03 · The Two Fixtures

What Is Each Light?

Runway Entrance Light

Part of the FAA's automated Runway Status Lights (RWSL) system: a series of red, unidirectional, in-pavement lights spaced along the taxiway centreline from the holding line to the runway edge, oriented to be visible only to traffic entering or crossing the runway at that point.

Tells the pilot

  • Illuminated red = the runway ahead is not safe to enter or cross
  • Remain clear of the runway while lit, even with a verbal ATC clearance
  • Unlit conveys no meaning by itself — it is not a clearance to proceed

Runway Guard Light

Uses yellow lighting, sited at or near the runway holding position, ahead of the runway itself. Type A is an elevated pair of lights that flash alternately; Type B is an in-pavement row across the taxiway.

Tells the pilot

  • A runway boundary is immediately ahead
  • Increase awareness of position
  • Verify holding-position markings and required clearance
04 · Biggest Difference: Colour & Purpose

Red = Automated Traffic Warning · Yellow = Static Conspicuity

This is the fastest way to tell the two apart. Red REL illumination is a real-time, surveillance-driven signal that this specific runway crossing is unsafe right now. Yellow RGL lighting is a fixed conspicuity aid that is present at the holding position regardless of actual runway occupancy. Neither light is a stop command in the legal/ATC sense — RWSL indicates status only and does not substitute for a clearance — but an illuminated REL is a direct instruction to remain clear.

Runway Guard Light = be aware, always. Runway Entrance Light = stay clear, but only when lit. The guard light is a constant reminder of where the runway is; the entrance light is a live alert about a specific, current conflict.

05 · Why It Matters

Neither Light Is a Substitute for a Clearance

Status only, not clearance: RWSL indicates runway status only — it does not substitute for an ATC clearance in either direction. If an REL is illuminated, the pilot/vehicle operator should hold short and advise ATC they are holding for red lights, even if a verbal clearance was already received. A runway guard light, meanwhile, warns of the runway boundary ahead and prompts the pilot to verify position and clearance before proceeding — it does not itself command a stop, and that role belongs to a stop bar where one is installed.

06 · Relationship With Stop Bars

Two Warning Systems, Related Colours

Runway Guard Lights, Stop Bars, and Runway Entrance Lights can all be encountered on the same taxi route at RWSL-equipped, low-visibility-capable airports, and their messages are worth keeping distinct. Notably, REL fixtures share the same red, in-pavement fixture family as the stop bar (both are typically the L-852S fixture type) — the REL is, in effect, an automated extension of the stop-bar warning concept along the taxiway centreline rather than only at the hold line.

07 · Low-Visibility Operations

Complementary, Not Duplicate, Layers of Information

Both systems are especially valuable at night, in fog, heavy rain, or complex taxiway operations, but they work differently even then. The runway guard light provides a constant conspicuity cue regardless of traffic; the runway entrance light, where RWSL is installed, adds a real-time, automated layer that reacts specifically to actual runway occupancy — an independent cross-check that operates whether or not the pilot has already received a clearance.

09 · Myth vs Fact

Common Terminology Confusion

Myth: A runway entrance light is a green guidance light showing the route onto the runway
Fact

Incorrect. The REL is a red RWSL warning light. It is normally unlit and illuminates red only when it is unsafe to enter or cross the runway — it never functions as green route guidance.

Myth: If the REL is unlit, the aircraft is cleared to cross
Fact

Not necessarily. An unlit light conveys no meaning by itself and is not a clearance. RWSL indicates runway status only; the pilot must still obtain an ATC clearance before entering or crossing.

Myth: Runway guard lights and runway entrance lights do the same job
Fact

They serve different functions on different systems — the guard light is a fixed ICAO/FAA-required conspicuity fixture present at essentially every holding position, while the entrance light is an automated RWSL warning present only at RWSL-equipped airports.

10 · Engineering Verdict

Runway Entrance Light vs Runway Guard Light

Engineering Verdict

The fundamental difference is automated, real-time warning versus static, always-on conspicuity. Both relate to the same taxi-to-runway transition, but one reacts to live traffic and the other does not.

The Runway Guard Light tells the pilot a runway is ahead and to verify position and clearance, at every holding position, all the time. The Runway Entrance Light, where RWSL is installed, adds an automated red warning that lights up specifically when it is unsafe to enter or cross — and even then, it is a status indication, not a substitute for an ATC clearance.

Runway Guard LightYellow + static conspicuity + sited at the holding position + not traffic-dependent.
Runway Entrance LightRed + automated RWSL warning + sited along the taxiway to the runway edge + lit only when unsafe.

Easy memory: Yellow → guard light → always be aware. Red → entrance light (when lit) → stop, an automated system says this crossing is unsafe right now.

11 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between a runway entrance light and a runway guard light?

System and behaviour — the runway guard light is a static yellow fixture that continuously marks a runway holding position, while the runway entrance light is a red RWSL fixture that is normally unlit and illuminates automatically, only when surveillance detects it is unsafe to enter or cross the runway.

Does an unlit runway entrance light mean the aircraft is cleared to enter the runway?

No. Lights that are off convey no meaning by themselves. RWSL indicates runway status only and is never intended to convey approval or clearance — the pilot must still obtain an ATC clearance to enter or cross.

What should a pilot do if REL is illuminated but ATC has given a clearance?

Hold short of the runway and advise ATC that they are holding for red lights. The illuminated light takes precedence as a safety indication even where it appears to conflict with a verbal clearance.

How do runway guard lights, stop bars, and runway entrance lights relate to each other?

The runway guard light gives a constant "caution, runway ahead" cue at the holding position. The stop bar, where installed, gives an ATC/system-controlled "stop, do not proceed" command at the hold line. The runway entrance light extends a similar red warning automatically along the taxiway to the runway edge, triggered by real-time surveillance rather than manual control.

Where is each light typically installed?

Runway guard lights are sited at or near the runway holding position, ahead of the runway. Runway entrance lights are a series of in-pavement lights spaced along the taxiway centreline from the holding line to the runway edge. The exact configuration depends on the aerodrome lighting system and applicable standards.

Is the runway entrance light available at every airport?

No. Unlike the runway guard light, which is a near-universal ICAO Annex 14 requirement at runway holding positions, the runway entrance light is part of the FAA's Runway Status Lights (RWSL) system and is only present at a limited number of RWSL-equipped airports.

12 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — governs runway guard light colour, siting, configuration and operational requirements. ICAO Annex 14 does not define the FAA's Runway Status Lights (REL) system as a standard aeronautical ground light.

FAA AC 150/5340-30 & AC 150/5345-46

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids, and the Specification for Runway, Taxiway, Heliport, and Vertiport Light Fixtures — cover runway guard light (Type A/L-804, Type B) configuration and the fixture used for stop bars and RELs (L-852S).

FAA Runway Status Lights (RWSL) Program

The RWSL system itself — including REL and Takeoff Hold Light operation and pilot/vehicle-operator procedures — is an FAA program (see FAA JO 7110.65 and related FAA guidance) deployed at a defined set of US airports, not a universal ICAO requirement.

DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Do not design a runway entrance light or runway guard light arrangement from a generic web diagram. The final installation drawing should always be developed from the current governing standard, approved airport design and project-specific requirements.

Runway entrance light = red, automated RWSL warning that illuminates only when unsafe to enter or cross. Runway guard light = yellow, static conspicuity fixture marking a runway holding position at all times.
