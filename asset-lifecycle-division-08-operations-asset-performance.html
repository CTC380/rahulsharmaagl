OAP-01 Asset Utilization
Purpose

Asset utilization measures how and to what extent an AGL asset is actually being used.

Record Where Applicable
  • Operating frequency
  • Operating duration
  • Number of operational cycles
  • Brightness/current levels
  • Seasonal utilization
  • Day/night utilization
  • Normal vs abnormal operation
  • Standby time
  • Actual operating hours
AGL Examples

A CCR may operate continuously during certain periods, at different current stages, according to runway operational requirements, or under varying traffic conditions. Approach, runway and taxiway lighting can each have substantially different utilization profiles.

Lifecycle Importance

Utilization data helps explain energy consumption, component wear, failure frequency, maintenance requirements and remaining service life.

OAP-02 Operational Performance
Purpose

Operational performance measures whether the asset continues to perform its intended operational function.

Consider
  • Functional availability
  • Correct operation
  • Control response
  • Lighting performance
  • Alarm performance
  • Switching performance
  • Dimming performance
  • Monitoring performance
  • Response to operational commands
Example — Runway Lighting System
OPERATIONAL COMMANDCCR RESPONDSSERIES CIRCUIT OPERATESFIXTURES ILLUMINATEALCMS CONFIRMS STATUS

A failure at any stage may affect overall operational performance.

OAP-03 Energy Consumption
Purpose

Energy consumption should be monitored as part of asset performance.

Record Where Applicable
  • kWh
  • Operating hours
  • Power demand
  • CCR input power
  • Circuit power
  • Lighting level
  • Energy per operating hour
  • Energy per operational cycle
Important

Energy data should not be viewed independently. A higher energy value may be caused by increased operating hours, higher brightness settings, increased traffic, equipment deterioration, inefficient equipment or incorrect configuration.

Principle

Energy consumption should therefore be analyzed against utilization, not read in isolation.

OAP-04 Operational Availability
Purpose

Operational availability measures the ability of the asset/system to be available for its intended operational function when required.

Simplified Concept
Operational Availability = Time Available for Operation ÷ Required Operational Time

The exact calculation method should be defined in the airport's approved reliability/availability methodology.

Record
  • Required operating period
  • Available period
  • Unavailable period
  • Planned outage
  • Unplanned outage
  • Operational restrictions
  • Failure-related downtime
Important Distinction

Planned maintenance downtime and unexpected failure downtime should normally be recorded separately.

OAP-05 Equipment Availability
Purpose

Equipment availability focuses on an individual asset or equipment item.

Examples
  • CCR availability
  • PAPI availability
  • Sign availability
  • ALCMS equipment availability
  • Approach-lighting equipment availability
Record
  • Asset ID
  • Required operating period
  • Available time
  • Downtime
  • Failure events
  • Maintenance events
  • Restoration time
Output

This provides equipment-level performance information.

OAP-06 System Availability
Purpose

System availability evaluates the availability of the complete functional AGL system, rather than an individual component.

Example — AGL System Chain
CCRPRIMARY CABLEISOLATION TRANSFORMERSFIXTURESCONTROL/MONITORINGAGL SYSTEM
Key Distinction

An individual component may be available while the overall system remains unavailable. Component availability does not necessarily equal system availability. This distinction is particularly important for runway and approach-lighting systems.

OAP-07 Reliability Monitoring
Purpose

Reliability monitoring tracks whether assets are continuing to perform without unacceptable failures.

Monitor
  • Failure frequency
  • Repeat failures
  • Failure modes
  • Operating hours between failures
  • MTBF where appropriately established
  • MTTR
  • Number of corrective interventions
  • Component replacement frequency
  • Recurring defects
Example — Repeated Fixture Failure
FAILURE 1REPAIRFAILURE 2REPAIRFAILURE 3RELIABILITY INVESTIGATION

The asset should not simply continue through repetitive replacement without investigation.

Trend deterioration can be more informative than a single failed test.

OAP-10 Energy-Performance Trends
Purpose

Energy consumption should be trended against relevant operating variables.

Track
  • Energy consumption
  • Operating hours
  • Lighting level
  • Traffic/usage where relevant
  • Seasonal variation
  • Equipment configuration
Example

If operating hours remain constant but energy consumption progressively increases, investigate equipment efficiency, CCR performance, electrical losses, configuration or measurement accuracy.

Purpose Served

Energy trending should therefore support both operational efficiency and asset-condition assessment.

OAP-11 Asset Utilization Trends
Purpose

Utilization should be monitored over the asset lifecycle.

Track
  • Daily utilization
  • Monthly utilization
  • Annual utilization
  • Seasonal utilization
  • Operating hours
  • Number of operating cycles
  • Brightness-level distribution
Why It Matters

Two identical assets may have different lifecycle behaviour because their actual utilization is different.

Principle

Service-life assumptions should be interpreted alongside actual operating exposure.

OAP-12 Operating-Hours Tracking
Purpose

Operating-hours tracking provides an objective measure of asset exposure.

Record
  • Asset ID
  • Start time
  • Stop time
  • Operating duration
  • Operating mode
  • Current/brightness level where relevant
  • Cumulative operating hours
Particularly Useful For
  • CCRs
  • Lighting systems
  • Rotating/actuated equipment where applicable
  • ALCMS equipment
  • Other continuously monitored assets
Lifecycle Use

Operating hours can support maintenance scheduling, reliability analysis, remaining-life assessment, energy analysis and replacement planning.

OAP-13 Equipment Loading
Purpose

Equipment loading measures the degree to which equipment is being operated relative to its design/approved operating capability.

For CCRs, For Example — Monitor
  • Rated output
  • Actual output
  • Current stage
  • Circuit load
  • Operating condition
  • Thermal behaviour where applicable
For Electrical Systems — Consider
  • Load
  • Voltage
  • Current
  • Power
  • Power factor where relevant
  • Temperature where relevant
Why Loading Matters

Operating conditions influence energy consumption, thermal stress, component degradation, reliability and service life. Loading should therefore be considered when interpreting failures and condition deterioration.

OAP-14 Performance Benchmarking
Purpose

Performance benchmarking compares actual asset performance against an approved reference.

Benchmark Sources
  • Commissioning baseline — the most important internal benchmark
  • Historical performance — how has the same asset performed over time?
  • Similar assets — how does comparable equipment perform?
  • Approved performance requirements — does the asset remain within specified requirements?

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Operations & Asset Performance in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide.

Q1What is Division 08 — Operations & Asset Performance in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

Division 08 is the operational performance-management layer that turns the commissioning baseline into a continuously monitored history of real-world asset performance. It covers 15 topics on utilization, operational performance, energy consumption, availability, reliability monitoring, KPIs, trending, operating hours, loading, benchmarking and performance reporting.

Q2What does Division 08 monitor during AGL operations?

It monitors how the asset is actually performing in day-to-day airport operation, including utilization, operational performance, energy consumption, availability and reliability. It builds on the commissioning baseline from Division 07.

Q3What is the difference between operational, equipment and system availability?

Operational availability measures the ability of the asset or system to be available for its intended operational function when required. Equipment availability focuses on an individual item such as a CCR, PAPI or sign. System availability evaluates the complete functional AGL system rather than a single component.

Q4Why is trend analysis important?

A single measurement provides a snapshot, while a trend provides lifecycle intelligence. Division 08 covers operational trends, energy-performance trends and asset utilization trends over daily, monthly, annual and seasonal periods.

Q5Why track operating hours and equipment loading?

Operating-hours tracking provides an objective measure of asset exposure. Equipment loading measures how far equipment is operated relative to its design or approved capability, comparing rated output with actual output, current stage and circuit load.

Q6How is energy consumption monitored?

Energy consumption is monitored as part of asset performance using kWh, operating hours, power demand, CCR input power, circuit power and lighting level. It is also trended against relevant operating variables.

Q7What is performance benchmarking?

Performance benchmarking compares actual asset performance against an approved reference. The commissioning baseline is the most important internal benchmark, alongside the same asset's historical performance.

Q8How is asset performance reported?

Asset-performance reporting converts operational data into information for engineering and management decisions, covering failures, availability, major alarms, operational restrictions and critical events. Performance indicators turn operational data into measurable management information.
