AGL-CS-042 — Runway Edge Light Single Fixture Failure

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A localized field investigation where one runway edge light failed while the rest of the circuit remained operational — from CCR verification through transformer secondary testing, connector inspection, and secondary-circuit restoration.

1Case Study Overview

During a routine night inspection, one runway edge light fixture was found completely extinguished, while all other runway edge lights in the circuit remained fully operational.

The Constant Current Regulator (CCR) was maintaining the selected 6.6 A current, output voltage was normal, and no CCR alarms were present.

Because the rest of the circuit was operating normally, the investigation focused on localized field components associated with the failed fixture.

A systematic investigation identified a failed isolating transformer secondary connection combined with moisture-contaminated connector contacts, which interrupted power delivery to the individual runway edge light.

This case demonstrates that single fixture failures are usually localized secondary-side faults and require structured field-level diagnosis rather than CCR or primary-circuit troubleshooting.

2Learning Objectives

After completing this case study, the learner should be able to:

  • Diagnose single runway edge light failures.
  • Differentiate primary and secondary circuit faults.
  • Verify isolating transformer operation.
  • Test secondary voltage and continuity.
  • Inspect field connectors and secondary cables.
  • Identify moisture-related connector failures.
  • Restore individual fixture operation safely.

3System Background

A single runway edge light is supplied through:

Primary Series Circuit → Isolating Transformer → Secondary Cable → Connector → Runway Edge Light Fixture

Failure of any secondary component can extinguish the individual fixture while leaving the primary circuit and all other lights operational.

Common causes include:

  • Lamp or LED module failure.
  • Isolating transformer failure.
  • Secondary cable open circuit.
  • Connector corrosion.
  • Moisture ingress.
  • Mechanical cable damage.
  • Fixture terminal failure.

  • No recent excavation work.

7Single Fixture Failure Principle

Primary current present → Transformer energized → Secondary voltage available → Fixture illuminated

If the primary circuit remains healthy, the fault is usually localized to the secondary circuit associated with the failed fixture.

Step 4: Secondary Continuity Testing

Testing showed:

  • Open continuity.
  • Intermittent connection during connector movement.
  • Elevated contact resistance.

The fault was downstream of the transformer.

Step 5: Connector Inspection

Inspection revealed:

  • Moisture contamination.
  • Corroded contacts.
  • Oxidized terminals.
  • Damaged sealing gasket.
  • Reduced contact pressure.

The connector had deteriorated significantly.

Step 6: Transformer Verification

The transformer itself showed:

  • Normal primary continuity.
  • Normal secondary winding continuity.
  • Acceptable insulation resistance.

The transformer was healthy.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The runway edge light failed because corrosion and moisture contamination in the transformer secondary connector interrupted power delivery to the individual fixture.

The primary circuit remained intact, and the CCR continued regulating current normally.

The fault was localized to the connector and secondary connection.

11Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Isolated the circuit.
  2. Removed the damaged connector.
  3. Cleaned the transformer secondary terminals.
  4. Installed a new approved connector.
  5. Restored environmental sealing.
  6. Verified secondary continuity.
  7. Measured secondary voltage.
  8. Tested fixture operation.
  9. Performed thermal verification.
  10. Updated maintenance records.

14Why This Case Was Important

Single fixture failures are the most common field maintenance events in runway edge lighting systems.

Efficient diagnosis avoids unnecessary replacement of:

  • CCRs.
  • Primary cables.
  • Healthy transformers.
  • Multiple field components.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: One runway edge light OFF. Adjacent lights ON. CCR healthy. No circuit alarms. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace the CCR.
B. Increase the current setting.
C. Verify transformer secondary voltage and inspect the secondary connector and cable.
D. Replace all runway edge lights.
Correct answer: C. A single fixture failure with a healthy CCR and operational adjacent lights points to a localized secondary fault. Secondary voltage testing and connector inspection will identify the cause without unnecessary equipment replacement.

Quick Reference: Runway Edge Light Failure, Colour & Fixtures

This case is a runway lighting partial failure: one light is out while the rest of the circuit stays on. That pattern points to a fault at the fixture, its isolating transformer secondary or its connector, not to the CCR or the main cable.

What causes runway light failure? The usual causes are lamp or LED module failure, moisture in the fixture or connector, a failed isolating transformer, cable damage, and impact. If every light on a circuit is out, look at the CCR and the primary cable first.

FAA runway light fixtures such as the L-862 elevated runway edge light are covered by FAA fixture specifications, and the fixture type in use should match the project specification.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about single runway edge light fixture failure, based on this case study.

Q1What is AGL-CS-042?

AGL-CS-042 is an engineering training case study on a single runway edge light fixture that was completely OFF while all other lights in the circuit worked. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2How do you know the primary circuit is intact?

The CCR was maintaining 6.6 A with normal voltage and no alarms, and adjacent lights were operating. The healthy CCR and adjacent lights indicated the primary series circuit was intact.

Q3What was the root cause?

Corrosion and moisture contamination in the transformer secondary connector interrupted power delivery to the individual fixture. The primary circuit stayed intact and the CCR kept regulating normally.

Q4What should you investigate first for a single light OFF with a healthy CCR?

Verify transformer secondary voltage and inspect the secondary connector and cable. Replacing the CCR, increasing the current setting or replacing all runway edge lights is not the right first step.

Q5Which test equipment was used?

The team used a true-RMS multimeter, secondary voltage test equipment, continuity tester, low-resistance ohmmeter, thermal imaging camera and insulation resistance tester, with approved AGL circuit test equipment.

Q6How was the light repaired?

The team isolated the circuit, removed the damaged connector and cleaned the transformer secondary terminals. They installed a new approved connector, restored environmental sealing, verified secondary continuity, measured secondary voltage, tested fixture operation and performed thermal verification.

Q7How was the repair verified?

Secondary voltage and fixture operation were restored, connector temperature was normal and the primary circuit remained normal. The individual runway edge light returned to normal operation.

Q8Why is the fault treated as localized?

Because only one fixture was affected, the investigation focused on the field components of that fixture rather than the whole circuit. Isolating the fault to the secondary connection avoided unnecessary equipment replacement.

Q9What color are runway edge lights?

Runway edge lights are white. On instrument runways the lights in the last part of the runway, normally the last 600 m or the last third of its length if that is less, are yellow, as a caution zone. Confirm the exact arrangement against the applicable standard.

Q10What causes runway light failure?

Runway lights fail from lamp or LED module failure, moisture in the fixture or connector, a failed isolating transformer, damaged cable, and impact. A single light out points to the fixture, transformer secondary or connector. A whole circuit out points to the CCR or the primary cable.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

A single runway edge light failure is usually a localized secondary-circuit fault involving the isolating transformer, secondary connector, cable, or fixture, while the CCR and primary series circuit remain fully operational.

Systematic CCR verification, primary-circuit confirmation, transformer secondary testing, connector inspection, secondary continuity testing, and moisture assessment can rapidly identify the failed component and restore individual fixture operation.

In AGL engineering, when only one runway edge light is dark, the fastest repair usually begins at the transformer secondary connector, not at the CCR.
