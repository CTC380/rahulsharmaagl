Approach & Landing Systems Comparison

MALSR VS MALSF

Medium Intensity Approach Lighting System with Runway Alignment Indicator Lights vs Medium Intensity Approach Lighting System with Sequenced Flashing Lights — both are FAA medium-intensity approach lighting systems that provide visual guidance during the transition from instrument approach to visual runway acquisition. Both are built on the same 1,400 ft MALS base and both use sequenced flashers; the difference is where the flashers sit and how long the system is. MALSR adds five RAIL flashers beyond the MALS portion (2,400 ft overall). MALSF places three flashers within the 1,400 ft MALS.

02 · Definitions

What Is MALSR & MALSF?

MALSR

MALSR stands for Medium Intensity Approach Lighting System with Runway Alignment Indicator Lights. It is an FAA approach lighting system consisting of a series of medium-intensity light bars extending into the approach area, together with Runway Alignment Indicator Lights (RAIL). The RAIL units provide a sequence of flashing lights that helps the pilot identify the runway centreline and approach direction.

Main functions

  • Runway alignment
  • Approach path identification
  • Centreline visual reference
  • Threshold acquisition
  • Transition from instrument to visual flight

MALSF

MALSF stands for Medium Intensity Approach Lighting System with Sequenced Flashing Lights. It uses medium-intensity approach lights together with a sequenced flashing light system. The sequenced flashers create an apparent movement toward the runway threshold.

This helps the pilot

  • Identify the runway direction
  • Establish alignment
  • Acquire the runway environment
  • Transition to visual flight
03 · Key Difference

RAIL vs Sequenced Flashing

The simplest way to remember the difference between MALSR and MALSF:

MALSR

Medium Intensity + RAIL

MALSF

Medium Intensity + Sequenced Flashing

Engineering point: Both systems use the same kind of sequenced flashing visual guidance. What differs is where the flashers are installed and how long the system is: MALSR = MALS + RAIL beyond the steady bars; MALSF = MALS with three flashers at its outer stations.

04 · RAIL Lights

What Are RAIL Lights?

RAIL = Runway Alignment Indicator Lights. In the FAA MALSR, RAIL is five sequenced flashers on the extended runway centreline. The first is 200 ft beyond the last steady-burning bar of the MALS portion (1,400 ft) and the rest follow at 200 ft intervals out to 2,400 ft. They flash in sequence toward the threshold twice per second and are not mixed in with the steady-burning bars. Their purpose is primarily a strong runway alignment cue.

RAIL → Alignment cue.

05 · Sequenced Flashing Lights

What Are Sequenced Flashing Lights?

Sequenced flashing lights are arranged so that the flashes occur progressively toward the runway, twice per second, producing an apparent movement of light toward the threshold. In MALSF the flashers are fitted at three light-bar stations at the outer end of the 1,400 ft array.

Visual sequence: outermost flasher → … → innermost flasher → threshold → runway (three flashers in MALSF, five in the MALSR RAIL). Therefore: Sequenced Flashing → Directional / approach movement cue.

07 · System Length

Typical Installation Extent

The two systems differ in length. MALSR extends 2,400 ft (730 m) from the runway threshold — a 1,400 ft MALS portion plus a 1,000 ft RAIL portion. MALSF extends 1,400 ft (427 m).

Minimum land: MALSF 1,600 ft × 400 ft; MALSR 1,600 ft × 400 ft plus a RAIL corridor 1,000 ft long by 25 ft wide (widenable to 100 ft where visibility, obstruction clearance or access require it).

The exact configuration and installation requirements must be taken from the applicable FAA design criteria and project layout. Do not determine the installation solely from a generic comparison table.

10 · Electrical Architecture

Circuit Design

Simplified FAA-standard arrangement: 120/240 V single-phase supply → power & control equipment (three-step intensity, remote control, flasher timing) → steady-burning light units (through the transformers specified in JO 6850.2C ¶204) and sequenced flasher units. A CCR series circuit applies only where the approved design specifies one.

The electrical design must account for:

  • Fixture wattage
  • Transformer rating
  • Total circuit load
  • Cable length
  • Circuit resistance
  • Supply / CCR capacity (as applicable)
  • Flashing equipment
  • Control wiring
  • Monitoring
11 · Power Supply Sizing

Sizing the Power Supply

The supply (and a CCR, where the approved design uses one) must be selected based on the actual connected load.

Total load

Total load = Number of fixtures × fixture load, subject to the actual circuit configuration.

Other checks

  • Supply / CCR capacity
  • Circuit current
  • Cable resistance
  • Voltage drop
  • Transformer losses
  • Circuit length

The flashing equipment should be considered separately where the approved design uses a dedicated flashing circuit/control arrangement.

12 · Control System

Flashing Function Control

Both systems require control of the flashing function.

MALSR control required for

  • RAIL sequence
  • Flash timing
  • Synchronization
  • Fault indication

MALSF control required for

  • Sequenced flashing
  • Flash timing
  • Sequence synchronization
  • Fault indication

A failure in the flashing sequence can significantly reduce the intended visual guidance even if the steady-burning approach lights remain operational.

Control rules (FAA JO 6850.2C): remote control is required for FAA approach lighting systems (landline or radio). MALSR is controlled independently of the runway edge lights. Sequenced flashers must be on only while the steady-burning lights are on, so RAIL can be selected only after a steady-light intensity has been chosen. With air-to-ground radio control, three, five or seven microphone keyings within five seconds select low, medium or high intensity, and the system times out after 15 minutes.

17 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: MALSR has no flashing lights
Fact

MALSR includes Runway Alignment Indicator Lights, which provide flashing visual guidance.

Myth: MALSR and MALSF are identical
Fact

MALSR is a 2,400 ft system (MALS plus five RAIL flashers beyond it); MALSF is a 1,400 ft MALS with three sequenced flashers. Length, flasher count and flasher locations all differ.

Myth: MALSF is simply a brighter MALSR
Fact

Both are medium-intensity systems with the same steady-light intensity steps. The distinction is system length and the number and location of the flashers, not brightness.

Myth: MALSR is the same as ALSF-1
Fact

No. MALSR and ALSF-1 are different FAA approach-lighting systems with different intensity levels, configurations and applications.

Myth: A flashing-light failure does not matter if the steady lights remain ON
Fact

The flashing component provides an important visual cue. Its failure can reduce the intended system performance.

21 · Engineering Verdict

Which System Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

Both MALSR and MALSF are medium-intensity FAA approach lighting systems built on the same MALS base. MALSR extends that 1,400 ft base to 2,400 ft with five RAIL flashers, strengthening runway alignment at longer range. MALSF keeps the 1,400 ft length and fits three sequenced flashers to help pilots pick out the approach in high ambient lighting.

Final selection should always be based on the applicable FAA design criteria, approach category and approved airport design.

MALSRMALS + five RAIL flashers → 2,400 ft, runway alignment cue.
MALSFMALS + three sequenced flashers → 1,400 ft, moving-light approach cue.

22 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Does MALSR have flashing lights?

Yes. MALSR includes Runway Alignment Indicator Lights (RAIL), which provide flashing visual guidance along the approach centreline.

Are MALSR and MALSF the same system?

No. Both are built on the MALS base, but MALSR adds five RAIL flashers beyond it (2,400 ft overall), while MALSF places three sequenced flashers within the 1,400 ft MALS.

Is MALSR the same as ALSF-1?

No. MALSR and ALSF-1 are different FAA approach-lighting systems with different intensity levels, configurations and applications.

How long are MALSR and MALSF installations?

MALSR is 2,400 ft (730 m) from the runway threshold; MALSF is 1,400 ft (427 m). The exact configuration must follow the applicable FAA design criteria and project layout.

What happens if the flashing lights fail but the steady lights stay on?

The flashing component provides an important visual cue, so its failure can reduce the intended system performance even if the steady-burning lights remain operational.

Is MALSF higher intensity than MALSR?

No. Both are medium-intensity systems with the same steady-light intensity steps. The distinction is length and flasher arrangement, not intensity.

How fast do the flashers flash?

In both systems the flashers fire in sequence toward the threshold twice per second, and they must be on only while the steady-burning lights are on.

23 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual FAA airport project, verify the current applicable requirements before using this comparison for project design.

FAA Order JO 6850.2C — Visual Guidance Lighting Systems

Installation criteria for FAA approach lighting systems: MALSR and MALSF configuration and dimensions, RAIL and sequenced-flasher requirements, obstruction planes for RAIL, brightness steps, remote control and equipment specifications.

FAA AC 150/5340-30 (current edition: J)

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids — installation guidance for airport visual aids including MALSF, frangibility and electrical installation practice. MALSR configuration is set out in JO 6850.2C.

Airport-Specific Technical Specifications

Project drawings and contract specifications control exact light-unit positions, tolerances, system length, flash characteristics, mounting and circuit arrangements. Confirm the current revision of each FAA document before use.

Applicability Note

These are FAA-defined systems. On ICAO/DGCA-regulated aerodromes (including Indian airports), approach lighting is specified as simple or precision (CAT I/II/III) approach lighting under ICAO Annex 14 Volume I, so FAA names such as MALS, MALSF, MALSR and ODALS do not apply directly.

MALSR = MALS (1,400 ft) + five RAIL flashers = 2,400 ft. MALSF = MALS (1,400 ft) + three sequenced flashers.
