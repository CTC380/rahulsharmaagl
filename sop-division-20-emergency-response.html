Initial Issue

Critical Operational Principle — During an AGL emergency, aviation safety and operational coordination take priority over immediate technical restoration. Maintenance personnel shall not independently decide to keep an affected runway/taxiway operational, close it, reopen it, or change its operational status. Such decisions shall follow the airport's approved emergency plan and be coordinated with the responsible ATC, Airport Operations and other designated authorities.

Emergency Response Master Principle — DETECT → ASSESS → NOTIFY → PROTECT → ISOLATE → DIAGNOSE → RESTORE → VERIFY → RELEASE → RECORD.

SOP-418 Complete AGL Circuit Failure
Purpose

To establish the response to complete loss of an AGL series circuit.

Immediate Actions
  1. Confirm failure indication.
  2. Notify Airport Operations/ATC as required.
  3. Identify affected circuit.
  4. Determine operational impact.
  5. Prevent unauthorized access to the affected area.
  6. Check CCR status.
  7. Check alarms.
  8. Determine whether failure is upstream or downstream.
Diagnostic Sequence
CCROUTPUTCIRCUITCABLETRANSFORMERSCONNECTORSFIXTURES
Restoration

After identifying the defect:

  1. Isolate safely under LOTO (Division 02).
  2. Confirm the series loop is complete and free of earth fault before energizing.
  3. Repair.
  4. Test circuit.
  5. Energize under controlled conditions.
  6. Verify field operation.
  7. Confirm restoration to Operations/ATC.

SOP-422 PAPI Failure

Important — A PAPI shall not be returned to operational service merely because the lamps/LEDs illuminate. Required functional and alignment verification shall be completed.

Purpose

To respond to PAPI malfunction or loss of service.

Immediate Actions
  1. Confirm PAPI failure.
  2. Notify ATC/Operations.
  3. Identify affected runway.
  4. Determine whether failure is complete or partial.
  5. Inspect power supply.
  6. Check control/electrical system.
  7. Check individual PAPI units.
  8. Restore if safely possible.
  9. Verify alignment/function.
  10. Report final status.
SOP-423 Stop Bar Failure

Critical Principle — The maintenance team shall not independently establish an alternative operational procedure for a failed stop bar.

Purpose

To manage failure of stop-bar lighting.

Immediate Actions
  1. Notify ATC/Operations.
  2. Identify affected stop bar.
  3. Confirm whether failure is complete, partial, control-related or circuit-related.
  4. Check CCR.
  5. Check circuit.
  6. Check individual fixtures.
  7. Check ALCMS/control interface.
  8. Restore if authorized.
  9. Verify ON/OFF/control function.

SOP-425 Major Power Supply Failure

Do Not — Do not energize large numbers of circuits simultaneously if the supply system has not been verified as stable and capable.

Purpose

To manage loss of electrical power affecting AGL systems.

Response
  1. Confirm incoming power failure.
  2. Notify Operations/ATC.
  3. Check main distribution equipment.
  4. Check AGL supply.
  5. Check UPS/emergency supply where applicable.
  6. Verify secondary power source/change-over operation and switch-over time against the approved requirements (ICAO Annex 14 electrical systems).
  7. Check CCR input.
  8. Determine affected systems.
  9. Coordinate restoration.
  10. Verify electrical stability.
  11. Restore AGL systems in a controlled sequence (stagger CCR start-up; do not start all CCRs simultaneously).

SOP-427 Major Earth Fault

Critical Rule — Never bypass or defeat CCR earth-fault protection simply to restore service.

Purpose

To respond to a significant earth fault in an AGL system.

Immediate Actions
  1. Treat the affected circuit as electrically hazardous.
  2. Keep personnel clear of the suspected fault location until the circuit is isolated (step/touch potential).
  3. Notify responsible personnel.
  4. Isolate circuit under LOTO (Division 02).
  5. Apply LOTO.
  6. Identify fault section.
  7. Test cable/circuit progressively.
  8. Locate defective component.
  9. Repair.
  10. Perform insulation/continuity tests.
  11. Verify earth condition.
  12. Controlled re-energization.
SOP-428 Major Cable Fault
Purpose

To safely respond to a major AGL cable failure.

Procedure
  1. Identify affected circuit.
  2. Notify Operations.
  3. Isolate circuit under LOTO (Division 02).
  4. Prove dead and apply safety earthing at the work location.
  5. Test cable.
  6. Locate fault.
  7. Positively identify the correct cable before cutting or excavating.
  8. Establish safe excavation/access area.
  9. Repair or replace cable/joint.
  10. Test insulation.
  11. Test continuity.
  12. Verify circuit.
  13. Restore cable protection.
  14. Controlled energization.
  15. Operational verification.

SOP-430 Flooded Transformer Pit

Immediate Rule — Do not enter or handle equipment in a flooded electrical pit until the electrical safety condition has been established.

Purpose

To safely manage water accumulation in a transformer pit.

Procedure
  1. Notify Operations.
  2. Identify affected circuit.
  3. Isolate circuit under LOTO (Division 02).
  4. Apply LOTO.
  5. Prevent personnel entry until authorized.
  6. Assess water level.
  7. Remove water using a safe approved method.
  8. Inspect transformer.
  9. Inspect connectors.
  10. Inspect cable entries/seals.
  11. Check drainage.
  12. Perform electrical tests.
  13. Replace damaged components.
  14. Restore pit protection.
  15. Test circuit.
  16. Return to service only after acceptance.

SOP-433 Low Visibility AGL Failure

Important — Maintenance personnel shall not enter an active movement area simply because a failure has occurred without appropriate authorization and coordination.

Purpose

To manage AGL failures occurring during low-visibility conditions.

Priority

During low visibility, operational coordination takes priority over field troubleshooting.

Procedure
  1. Confirm failure.
  2. Immediately notify Operations/ATC.
  3. Determine affected lighting function.
  4. Determine operational impact.
  5. Avoid unnecessary airside entry.
  6. Troubleshoot remotely where possible.
  7. Deploy field team only under approved authorization.
  8. Restore if safely possible.
  9. Conduct operational verification.
  10. Report status.
SOP-434 Emergency Circuit Restoration
Purpose

To restore a failed circuit safely under emergency conditions.

Procedure
  1. Identify circuit.
  2. Determine failure.
  3. Obtain restoration authorization.
  4. Establish safe work conditions.
  5. Repair defect.
  6. Complete minimum required electrical tests (insulation resistance and continuity of the repaired section shall never be omitted).
  7. Inspect installation.
  8. Remove tools/FOD.
  9. Controlled energization.
  10. Verify current.
  11. Verify field operation.
  12. Notify Operations/ATC.
  13. Complete full documentation after immediate restoration.
SOP-435 Temporary AGL Restoration

Critical Rule — A temporary repair shall never become an undocumented permanent installation.

Purpose

To establish controlled temporary restoration when permanent repair cannot immediately be completed.

Temporary Measures May Include
  • Temporary replacement equipment.
  • Temporary cable repair approved for the application.
  • Temporary control arrangement.
  • Temporary lighting equipment where specifically approved.
  • Temporary bypass only where specifically engineered, authorized and safe.
Requirements

Every temporary arrangement shall have:

  • Risk assessment.
  • Technical approval.
  • Identification.
  • Installation record.
  • Inspection.
  • Test.
  • Defined validity period.
  • Permanent repair plan.

Division 20 — AGL Emergency Golden Rule

Related divisions: Division 16 — AGL Inspection, Division 18 — Preventive & Corrective Maintenance, Division 21 — Documentation, Records & Reporting (failure and incident reports) and Division 22 — Training, Competency & Knowledge Management (emergency training and lessons learned).

Emergency Response Hold Points

  • HP-01 — Safety — Personnel and hazard safety confirmed before any intervention.
  • HP-02 — Notification — ATC and airport operations notified of lighting status.
  • HP-03 — Isolation — Faulty circuit isolated before work.
  • HP-04 — Diagnosis — Diagnostic sequence agreed and followed.
  • HP-05 — Temporary Measures — Temporary measures approved by the airport authority after risk assessment.
  • HP-06 — Restoration — Energization controlled and verified.
  • HP-07 — Operational Check — Circuit and intensity steps verified with ATC or operations.
  • HP-08 — Close-Out — Post-emergency inspection completed and records raised before NOTAM cancellation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Emergency Response SOPs in the AGL SOP Library.

Q1What is Division 20 — Emergency Response SOPs?

Division 20 is a set of 21 procedures (SOP-418 to SOP-438, Rev 0) for AGL emergency response. It covers complete circuit failure, runway, taxiway and approach lighting failure, PAPI and stop-bar failure, CCR and major power supply failure, ALCMS failure, earth and cable faults, lightning damage, flooding and water ingress, multiple fixture failure, low-visibility failures, emergency and temporary restoration, urgent airfield repair, stakeholder coordination and post-emergency inspection.

Q2Who decides whether a runway or taxiway stays open during an AGL emergency?

Not maintenance personnel. Aviation safety and operational coordination take priority over immediate technical restoration. Maintenance personnel must not independently decide to keep an affected runway or taxiway operational, close it, reopen it or change its status; such decisions follow the airport's approved emergency plan and are coordinated with ATC, Airport Operations and other designated authorities.

Q3What is the master emergency response flow?

Failure detected and confirmed, protect personnel and aircraft, notify ATC/Operations, identify the affected area and assess operational impact, then remote diagnosis and field response, safe isolation and fault finding. Repair, electrical and functional testing, photometric testing if required, FOD check and controlled energization follow, then operational verification, ATC/Operations notification and service release. Post-emergency inspection, root-cause analysis and the emergency report close it out.

Q4Should a failed runway lighting circuit be restored by repeatedly resetting equipment?

No. Do not restore a runway lighting circuit by repeatedly resetting equipment without identifying the cause of the failure (SOP-419). Notify ATC/Operations, assess operational impact, troubleshoot and restore only if authorized, verify operation and obtain ATC/Operations release.

Q5What is the rule for a flooded transformer pit?

Do not enter or handle equipment in a flooded electrical pit until the electrical safety condition has been established (SOP-430). Notify Operations, isolate the circuit under LOTO, prevent entry until authorized, remove water by a safe approved method, inspect and test equipment, and return to service only after acceptance.

Q6How should lightning damage be handled?

Do not inspect exposed equipment while lightning activity persists (SOP-429). Notify Operations, establish safe conditions, then inspect the CCR, SPDs, control equipment, ALCMS, signs, PAPI, fixtures, transformers, cables and earthing. Check protective devices, alarms and insulation, replace damaged components, test, conduct a post-lightning inspection and document the event.

Q7Can a temporary repair become permanent?

Never as an undocumented installation (SOP-435). Every temporary arrangement needs a risk assessment, technical approval, identification, installation record, inspection, test, a defined validity period and a permanent repair plan. A temporary bypass is allowed only where specifically engineered, authorized and safe.
