Runway/Taxiway Transition Lighting

Lead-On Lighting VS Lead-Off Lighting

Onto vs Off — lead-on lighting guides an aircraft from the taxiway toward the runway, while lead-off lighting guides an aircraft from the runway toward the taxiway. Both are directional transition/continuation systems at the runway/taxiway interface, built to make that hand-off between guidance systems conspicuous rather than to replace the normal centreline lighting.

02 · Definitions

What Is Lead-On & Lead-Off Lighting?

Lead-On Lighting

Provides a visual continuation from the taxiway toward the runway. The pilot's basic message is: "follow this route toward the runway."

Can help an aircraft

  • Identify the runway entry path
  • Maintain visual continuity between taxiway and runway
  • Recognise the intended transition point
  • Improve conspicuity at night or in reduced visibility

Lead-Off Lighting

Provides a visual path from the runway toward a taxiway exit. The pilot's basic message is: "follow this route to vacate the runway."

Can help an aircraft

  • Identify an available runway exit
  • Transition from runway centreline guidance to taxiway guidance
  • Vacate the runway efficiently
  • Maintain visual guidance after landing

Engineering detail — this is implemented as taxiway centreline lighting, not a separate fixture type. Per the FAA AIM/AC 150/5340-30, lead-on and lead-off guidance is provided by alternating green-and-yellow taxiway centreline lights, beginning with green from the runway centreline out to one light position beyond the runway holding position (or the ILS critical area boundary, whichever is more restrictive). Where the same light position must serve both functions, the fixture is bidirectional — one side of the fixture displays the lead-on colour pattern and the other side displays the lead-off pattern, so a fixture showing yellow for lead-off must also show yellow for lead-on.

03 · Biggest Difference: Direction

Lead-On = Onto the Runway · Lead-Off = Off the Runway

This is the fastest way to tell them apart, and the easiest thing to memorise: lead-on leads you onto the runway, lead-off leads you off the runway.

Before takeoff, a pilot follows lead-on lighting from the taxiway toward the runway. After landing, the pilot follows lead-off lighting from the runway toward the intended taxiway exit — same interface, opposite direction of travel.

05 · Why It Matters

Onto vs Off — The Easy Memory

The simplest way to remember it: lead-on leads the aircraft ONTO the runway; lead-off leads the aircraft OFF the runway. Both are transition/continuation lighting that make the runway/taxiway hand-off conspicuous — they are not a replacement for the normal taxiway or runway centreline systems.

06 · Operational Direction

A Two-Way Transition at the Same Interface

Think of the runway/taxiway interface as a two-way transition: lead-on carries traffic inbound to the runway, lead-off carries traffic outbound from it.

07 · Relationship With Centreline Lighting

Continuation, Not Replacement

Lead-On

Sits between the taxiway centreline and the runway, extending visual guidance across the gap so the transition from taxiway lighting to the runway visual environment is conspicuous.

Lead-Off

Sits between the runway and the taxiway centreline, extending guidance the other way so the pilot can transition from runway centreline guidance to taxiway guidance after landing.

Lead-on and lead-off lighting should be understood as transition/continuation lighting at the interface, not as substitutes for the normal taxiway or runway centreline systems they connect.

11 · Myth vs Fact

Common Lead-On / Lead-Off Confusion

Myth: Lead-on and lead-off lighting are just another name for centreline lighting
Fact

They are transition/continuation systems that supplement the normal taxiway and runway centreline lighting at the interface — not a replacement for either.

Myth: The two systems are interchangeable
Fact

They serve opposite directions of travel. Lead-on guides an aircraft toward the runway for entry; lead-off guides an aircraft away from the runway to vacate it — one is inbound guidance, the other outbound.

Myth: Lead-off lighting tells the pilot which exit they must use
Fact

Lead-off lighting improves conspicuity and helps a pilot follow an identified exit route after landing; the operational clearance and intended routing still come from the applicable ATC procedures, markings, signs and other aerodrome controls.

12 · Engineering Verdict

Lead-On Lighting vs Lead-Off Lighting

Engineering Verdict

Lead-on and lead-off lighting are directional mirror images of each other at the same runway/taxiway interface. One carries the aircraft's eye onto the runway; the other carries it off.

Both exist to make the hand-off between the taxiway visual environment and the runway visual environment conspicuous — for departure in one direction, for landing and runway vacation in the other — while the normal centreline systems continue to do the underlying guidance work.

Lead-OnTaxiway → runway + entry guidance + "leads you onto the runway."
Lead-OffRunway → taxiway + exit guidance + "leads you off the runway."

Easy memory: Lead-ON → leads you ONTO the runway. Lead-OFF → leads you OFF the runway.

13 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between lead-on and lead-off lighting?

Direction — lead-on lighting guides an aircraft from the taxiway toward the runway, while lead-off lighting guides an aircraft from the runway toward the taxiway.

When would a pilot use lead-on lighting?

While taxiing toward a departure runway and entering it — lead-on lighting provides visual continuity between the taxiway guidance system and the runway visual environment.

When would a pilot use lead-off lighting?

After landing, while identifying and following the intended exit route to vacate the runway and transition back to taxiway guidance.

Do lead-on and lead-off lighting replace centreline lighting?

No. They are transition/continuation lighting at the runway/taxiway interface, working alongside the normal taxiway and runway centreline systems rather than replacing them.

Is there an easy way to remember which is which?

Yes — lead-ON leads you ONTO the runway, and lead-OFF leads you OFF the runway.

Does lead-off lighting grant clearance to use a particular exit?

No. It improves conspicuity and helps the pilot follow the intended route, but operational clearance still comes from the applicable ATC procedures, markings, signs and other aerodrome controls.

Final Engineering Note

Do not design a lead-on/lead-off lighting arrangement from a generic web diagram. The final installation drawing should always be developed from the current governing standard, approved airport design and project-specific requirements.
