Overview

AGL Inspection, Maintenance and Audit Checklists

An Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) inspection checklist helps airport maintenance teams, project engineers, commissioning personnel and quality auditors conduct systematic inspections and maintain consistent records.

AGL inspections may cover design documentation, runway and taxiway lighting, approach lighting systems, Constant Current Regulators (CCRs), series circuits, isolating transformers, cables, connectors, lighting control systems and project handover documentation.

Using a structured checklist helps teams record observations, identify defects, track corrective actions and verify that inspection activities have been completed.

Important: This page provides general checklist templates for guidance. Before use, adapt each checklist to the airport’s approved maintenance procedures, equipment manuals, project specifications, inspection and test plans (ITPs), applicable regulatory requirements and authorized acceptance criteria. These templates do not replace approved procedures or authorize work on safety-critical airport systems.

10 · Preparing a Checklist

How to Prepare an Effective AGL Checklist

A useful AGL checklist should be specific to the asset, inspection activity and acceptance requirements.

01

Step 1: Identify the inspection objective.

Define whether the checklist is for design review, installation, commissioning, routine maintenance, fault investigation, audit or handover.

02

Step 2: Identify the asset and location.

Include the equipment ID, circuit reference and physical location needed to trace the inspection.

03

Step 3: Reference the governing documents.

Identify the applicable approved drawings, project specification, ITP, maintenance manual, test procedure and relevant standard revision.

04

Step 4: Define the inspection method.

Specify what the inspector must observe, measure, review or functionally verify. Use only authorized methods.

05

Step 5: Establish acceptance criteria.

Record the applicable limits or requirements from the governing document. Avoid vague entries such as "check OK" where specific evidence is required.

06

Step 6: Provide clear result fields.

Use suitable entries such as Pass, Fail and Not Applicable, supplemented by a remarks field. For quantitative tests, include the result, units and criteria reference.

07

Step 7: Assign corrective-action responsibility.

Give every finding a traceable reference, responsible person, due date where applicable and closure status.

08

Step 8: Control checklist revisions.

Maintain a document number, revision, issue date and approval record when required by the organization's document control process.

09

Step 9: Review after use.

Update the checklist through an approved change process when equipment, procedures or governing requirements change.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is an AGL inspection checklist?

An AGL inspection checklist is a structured record used to verify relevant equipment condition, installation quality, electrical-system records, test evidence or maintenance activities against specified requirements.

Q2How do I prepare an AGL maintenance checklist?

Identify the asset, inspection frequency and objective. Refer to the applicable equipment instructions and approved procedure, define the checks and acceptance criteria, and provide fields for observations, test results, corrective actions and verification.

Q3What is an AGL compliance checklist?

An AGL compliance checklist records whether applicable design, material, installation, testing, maintenance or documentation requirements have been met, supported by references and objective evidence.

Q4Can these checklists be used at every airport?

They can serve as a starting point, but each checklist must be adapted to the airport's equipment, operating environment, approved access procedures, applicable requirements and maintenance system.

Q5Should an AGL checklist include measured test values?

Where measurements are required, record actual results with units, the test method or procedure reference, instrument details where required and the applicable acceptance criteria. Do not substitute generic values for approved limits.

Q6Does a completed checklist prove that an AGL system is ready for operation?

No. A checklist is a record of an inspection activity. Operational release or acceptance must follow the airport's authorized procedures, applicable acceptance requirements and required approvals.

Q7What should an airport lighting design checklist include?

An airport lighting design checklist should cover the applicable standards, lighting layouts, circuit arrangement and loading, equipment selection, cable routing, power supply, control and monitoring, redundancy and documentation. It is a design review aid and does not replace the designer's responsibility.

Q8What is an airfield lighting commissioning checklist?

An airfield lighting commissioning checklist records the checks made before and during energisation: equipment installed correctly, insulation and continuity tests, CCR operation, brightness steps, control functions and alarms. Signed results support handover and acceptance.

Q9What does an airport lighting maintenance checklist cover?

An airport lighting maintenance checklist covers routine visual inspection, cleaning, lamp or LED status, connectors and sealing, CCR and circuit readings, defect recording and follow-up actions. It sets the frequency of each task and who signs it off.

Q10What is an airfield lighting compliance checklist and an airfield lighting audit checklist?

An airfield lighting compliance checklist compares the installation and records with the applicable standards and the airport specification. An airfield lighting audit checklist supports a structured review of procedures, records, maintenance and quality systems. Both are used by quality, safety and technical teams.

Q11What belongs on an AGL maintenance safety checklist?

An AGL maintenance safety checklist covers permits, isolation and lockout, test for dead, PPE, movement-area access, communication and emergency arrangements before work starts. Use it together with the airport's own safe-working procedures.

Q12What is an airfield lighting maintenance log template?

An airfield lighting maintenance log template is a standard record of what was inspected or repaired, where, when, by whom, with test readings and follow-up. A consistent log shows trends and supports audits and reliability reviews.

Q13How to prepare an AGL maintenance checklist?

To prepare an AGL maintenance checklist, list the equipment and circuits, take tasks and intervals from the manufacturer's manuals and airport procedures, and add pass criteria and space for readings. Then trial it in the field, review it with the maintenance team and keep it under revision control.

Q14What is an airfield ground lighting inspection checklist and an airfield lighting inspection checklist?

An airfield lighting inspection checklist, also called an airfield ground lighting inspection checklist, lists the checks for each lighting system: condition of fixtures, lenses, alignment, sealing, outages and circuit readings. Each item has a pass or fail result and a defect note.

Q15What do a runway lighting inspection checklist and an approach lighting inspection checklist include?

A runway lighting inspection checklist covers runway edge, threshold, end and centreline lights for outages, damage, dirt, alignment and brightness. An approach lighting inspection checklist covers approach lights, supports and frangibility, aiming and outages. Both record defects for correction.

Q16What do a runway edge lights inspection checklist, runway centreline lights inspection checklist and threshold lights inspection checklist include?

These checklists cover physical condition, lens cleanliness, sealing, colour, alignment, mounting and whether each light operates at all brightness steps. A runway edge lights inspection checklist, runway centreline lights inspection checklist and threshold lights inspection checklist are normally completed along the runway with outages recorded by position.

Q17What is an AGL series circuit inspection checklist and a constant current regulator inspection checklist?

An AGL series circuit inspection checklist records current and voltage readings, insulation resistance, earth-fault indication and visual checks of the circuit. A constant current regulator inspection checklist records the CCR's output current at each step, alarms, cooling, connections and control mode.

Q18What is an airfield lighting isolation transformer inspection checklist and an airfield lighting cable inspection checklist?

An airfield lighting isolation transformer inspection checklist covers housing condition, sealing, connectors, secondary lead and insulation tests. An airfield lighting cable inspection checklist covers visible cable condition, joints, markers, routes and the results of continuity and insulation tests.

Q19What is an airfield lighting control system inspection checklist and an ALCMS inspection checklist?

An airfield lighting control system inspection checklist covers the operator interface, controllers, communications, alarms and backup control. An ALCMS inspection checklist adds checks of status indications, event logs, time synchronisation and interfaces with CCRs. Results are recorded with any alarms found.

A Checklist Supports Judgement, It Does Not Replace It
Use

Inspectors should record observations that are not on the form. Allow space for notes and make sure unusual findings are escalated.

Tailor Generic Forms to the Installation
Customisation

Align item names, tolerances and frequencies with your airfield's equipment and approved procedures before using a template in the field.

Control Revisions of the Checklist Itself
Document Control

Different versions in circulation cause inconsistent inspections. Keep one controlled version and withdraw old copies.

Define Defect Categories Before the Inspection
Classification

Agree in advance which findings need immediate action, which can be scheduled and which are advisory. This keeps responses consistent.

Photograph Defects With a Location Reference
Evidence

A photo with a clear location makes later repair and verification easier. Add the item identifier to the record.

Close the Loop on Every Finding
Follow-Up

Track each defect to completion and re-inspection. Unclosed findings erode trust in the whole inspection process.
