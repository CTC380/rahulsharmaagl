Angular Measurement Equipment

Clinometer / Digital Inclinometer

Why It Matters

Accurate angular measurement is essential when installing, aligning and verifying PAPI systems and other airfield visual-aid equipment. Precision AGL Solutions supplies clinometers and digital inclinometers for field measurement, installation checks and maintenance where a controlled angular reference is required.

They give a measurable indication of inclination during setup and verification, and can check mounting arrangements, reference planes and other equipment whose installation geometry affects operational performance.

Measurement Instrument vs Aiming Tool

A general-purpose inclinometer is an inspection and measurement instrument. An FAA-compliant PAPI system has specific aiming-tool requirements: FAA AC 150/5345-28H specifies a tool that measures the vertical angle of the beam centre from 2° to 8° in 10-minute increments with repeatable accuracy of ±3 arc minutes.

Select the instrument by measurement task, required accuracy and applicable airport procedure.

Clinometer

Gives a practical measure of slope or inclination relative to a defined reference.

Digital Inclinometer

Performs the same function electronically, usually with a digital display so the angle is read directly.

Why Angular Accuracy Matters

PAPI Is an Angular Guidance System

Direct Effect on Indications

PAPI light units project defined red and white optical sectors, so their relationship to the runway reference directly affects pilot indications. FAA guidance makes the standard visual glide path part of the installation design, and Order JO 6850.2C (active) holds the installation criteria.

An angular instrument can help detect:

  • Excessive unit tilt
  • Incorrect mounting angle
  • Uneven support
  • Reference-plane deviation
  • Mechanical movement
  • Incorrect adjustment

Angle Alone Is Not Compliance

Measuring angle does not establish that a PAPI installation is fully compliant. Position, elevation, spacing, optical performance, azimuth and the approved approach geometry must also be considered.

PAPI units also carry a tilt sensor that shuts the system down if one unit is lowered more than ¼° or raised more than ½° from its set angle (AC 150/5345-28H, para 3.2.5). Movement of that size can be detected with an inclinometer, which is one reason periodic angular checks are worthwhile.

Where the PAPI is associated with an ILS, LPV or other published approach path, coordinate it against the approved approach geometry rather than checking it as an isolated mechanical assembly. FAA guidance addresses aligning PAPI visual guidance with the published glide path or vertical descent angle.

Safety: Style B (series-circuit) PAPI units must be isolated and locked out at the regulator before a unit is opened or a connector disturbed. Never open a live series circuit. Work near the runway needs the airport permit and ATC coordination.

PAPI-Specific Aiming Equipment

A General Inclinometer Is Not Automatically a PAPI Aiming Tool

FAA AC 150/5345-28H aiming-tool characteristicRequirement
Measurement range (beam centre)2° to 8°
Increment10 minutes of arc
Repeatable accuracy±3 minutes of arc

General Inclinometer

Suitable for supporting measurements where its accuracy and reference method are adequate.

PAPI Aiming Tool

Purpose-designed for the PAPI optical aiming procedure and subject to the applicable equipment requirement.

For procurement, identify the instrument by its intended role. ±3 arc minutes does not apply automatically to every clinometer; any instrument proposed as a substitute for the specified aiming tool must be evaluated against the applicable requirement and approved procedure. A wider-range inclinometer may still be unsuitable for direct optical aiming without the necessary accuracy, repeatability or mechanical reference arrangement.

AC 150/5345-28H also allows light units to be factory-calibrated to a fixed vertical angle, in which case the manufacturer must give a field procedure to check the calibration to ±3 minutes of arc. The red-to-white transition must itself be within 3 minutes of arc at the beam centre, so an instrument less accurate than this cannot confirm an aiming setting to the tolerance the system is built for.

Maintenance Applications

Use the instrument to investigate after light-unit replacement, mechanical adjustment, vehicle impact, foundation or mounting work, pavement rehabilitation, relocation or reported abnormal indications. Treat it as part of troubleshooting, not the sole basis for returning the system to service.

PAPI Alignment Telescope

A telescope establishes or checks an optical line of sight; an inclinometer gives angular information relative to a physical reference. For fuller commissioning, combine them with survey equipment, the aiming device, a check stick and level/reference equipment, per the PAPI manufacturer's method.

Troubleshooting

Symptoms, Causes and Checks

Inconsistent readings

Poor contact with the reference, dirt beneath the instrument, unstable mounting, low battery, temperature effects, wrong zero, or damage.

Check

  • Clean reference surfaces
  • Re-zero
  • Repeat the measurement
  • Reverse orientation to detect reference bias
  • Compare against a known reference
  • Inspect for damage

Digital display changes while stationary

Vibration, unstable support, low battery, sensor sensitivity, temperature effects or poor seating.

Check

  • Allow the instrument to stabilise before the final reading

Reads an unexpected PAPI angle

Do not assume the PAPI is mis-aimed.

Check

  • Correct measurement point and reference surface
  • Applicable approach angle
  • PAPI unit identification
  • Instrument zero and accuracy
  • Whether the instrument is approved for that method
  • A dedicated aiming tool may be needed for final optical adjustment

Accurate on the bench, inconsistent in the field

Temperature, vibration, uneven mounting, contaminated surfaces, magnetic influence or incorrect procedure.

Check

  • Compare against a verified reference before escalating to the PAPI

PAPI still wrong after angular adjustment

Look beyond angle. FAA guidance requires the system to be sited and aimed as an integrated installation.

Check

  • Light-unit elevation
  • Unit spacing and azimuth
  • Optical condition
  • Published approach geometry
  • Threshold reference
  • Obstacle clearance
  • Electrical operation

Standards & References

Governing Documents

FAA PAPI Equipment

AC 150/5345-28H — Precision Approach Path Indicator (PAPI) Systems, active. Sets the equipment standard, including the supplied aiming tool's angular range and repeatability.

FAA Installation

  • AC 150/5340-30J — Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids; active
  • Order JO 6850.2C — Visual Guidance Lighting Systems; active; installation criteria including PAPI
  • Engineering Brief 95 — siting and survey considerations for PAPI and other visual glide slope indicators
  • AC 150/5340-26C — Maintenance of Airport Visual Aid Facilities

ICAO

Check against Annex 14, Volume I (PAPI, Chapter 5) and Doc 9157 Part 4, and the State's implementation. Confirm the current edition and amendment on the ICAO Store before you cite it. ICAO material also treats PAPI nominal approach-slope information as an installation parameter within aerodrome information.

Important Standards Note

A clinometer or digital inclinometer is not automatically a certified PAPI aiming instrument because it measures angles. The right question is: does the selected instrument, with the approved measurement procedure, provide the required accuracy, repeatability, range and reference method for the task?

For FAA PAPI systems, AC 150/5345-28H sets specific characteristics for the aiming tool supplied with the system. A general-purpose inclinometer may serve as supporting measurement equipment only when appropriate to the project procedure.

FAQ

Clinometer / Digital Inclinometer — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a clinometer or digital inclinometer used for in airfield lighting?

It gives a measurable indication of inclination during setup and verification of PAPI systems and other airfield visual-aid equipment, and can check mounting angles, level and cross-level, support geometry and reference planes, including after impact, foundation work, pavement rehabilitation or hardware replacement.

What is the difference between a clinometer and a digital inclinometer?

A clinometer gives a practical measure of slope relative to a defined reference using an optical or mechanical scale, and often needs no battery. A digital inclinometer does the same electronically with a digital display so the angle is read directly, which typically makes recording and comparison easier and normally needs a battery.

Can an inclinometer be used as a PAPI aiming tool?

Not automatically. FAA AC 150/5345-28H specifies an aiming tool that measures the vertical angle of the beam centre from 2° to 8° in 10-minute increments with repeatable accuracy of ±3 arc minutes. A general inclinometer is a supporting measurement instrument, and any instrument proposed as a substitute must be evaluated against the applicable requirement and approved procedure.

Does measuring the angle prove a PAPI is compliant?

No. Position, elevation, spacing, optical performance, azimuth and the approved approach geometry must also be considered. Where the PAPI is associated with an ILS, LPV or other published approach path, coordinate it against the approved approach geometry rather than checking it as an isolated mechanical assembly.

Why does angular accuracy matter for PAPI?

PAPI light units project defined red and white optical sectors, so their relationship to the runway reference directly affects pilot indications. Units also carry a tilt sensor that shuts the system down if one unit is lowered more than ¼° or raised more than ½° from its set angle (AC 150/5345-28H, para 3.2.5), and movement of that size can be detected with an inclinometer.

What is the difference between resolution, accuracy and repeatability?

Resolution is the smallest displayed increment, accuracy is how close the reported value is to the true value, and repeatability is how consistently the same result is returned. A display may show several decimals while absolute accuracy is materially lower, so judge suitability by the specified accuracy rather than display resolution.

How do I convert between degrees and minutes of arc?

One minute of arc is 1/60 of a degree and one second of arc is 1/60 of a minute. For example, 3°00′ is 3.000°, 3°30′ is 3.500° and 20′ is 0.3333°. PAPI aiming specifications may be given in minutes of arc, so verify the instrument provides an appropriate display and resolution.

How should I take a repeatable measurement?

Use a known reference, clean the contact surfaces, let a digital unit stabilise before recording, repeat the measurement to expose positioning error, reverse orientation to detect reference bias, and document the reference point. A dropped instrument may show plausible values with altered geometry or calibration.

What should be recorded after a verification?

Record the airport and runway, the PAPI unit identification, the instrument model and asset number, the reference point, the required and measured angle, the difference, the condition (pass, investigate or adjust), any corrective action, and the inspector, date and remarks. Take documentation requirements from the airport approved maintenance system. See also the PAPI check stick.

What if the instrument reads an unexpected PAPI angle?

Do not assume the PAPI is mis-aimed. Check the measurement point and reference surface, the applicable approach angle, the unit identification, the instrument zero and accuracy, and whether the instrument is approved for that method. A dedicated aiming tool may be needed for final optical adjustment.

What should I confirm before requesting an instrument?

Confirm the angular range, accuracy, resolution and repeatability, the degree or minute display, relative-angle and zero functions, whether it is for general support or direct PAPI aiming, the PAPI manufacturer and model, the field environment, battery type and calibration or verification capability, and request the datasheet and calibration certificate where required.

Which standards and references apply?

FAA AC 150/5345-28H (PAPI systems), AC 150/5340-30J (design and installation details), Order JO 6850.2C (visual guidance lighting systems), Engineering Brief 95 and AC 150/5340-26C (maintenance), together with ICAO Annex 14 Volume I (PAPI, Chapter 5) and Doc 9157 Part 4. Confirm the current edition and amendment before citing.

Engineering Note

For precision airfield work, measurement capability and measurement procedure must be considered together. A digital inclinometer with high displayed resolution is not necessarily more suitable than a conventional clinometer, and neither should automatically replace a dedicated PAPI aiming tool.

For PAPI installations, use an instrument whose accuracy and reference method are demonstrably suitable for the approved installation procedure, supported by the manufacturer's aiming equipment where specifically required. FAA PAPI equipment requirements illustrate the distinction by defining performance requirements for the dedicated aiming tool rather than for generic inclinometers.

Base final selection on the PAPI equipment, required angular measurement, project acceptance criteria, environmental conditions and applicable aerodrome authority requirements.
