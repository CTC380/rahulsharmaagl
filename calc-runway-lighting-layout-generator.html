Calculator #54

Runway Lighting Layout Generator

Enter the runway, pick ICAO or FAA, and get a plan-view lighting layout — edge, threshold, wing-bar, runway-end, centreline and touchdown-zone lights — with fixture-by-fixture coordinates and a quantity schedule.

About this calculator

Every fixture is generated as an actual position (chainage and lateral offset from the centreline), then drawn, counted and exported from that one list — so the drawing, the schedule and the CSV can never disagree. Colours are shown as the pilot sees them from either approach direction.

Method & Reference

How the Layout Is Built

Chainage x runs from the physical End A (x = 0) to the physical End B (x = L). Lateral offset y is positive to the left and negative to the right of the centreline, looking from End A towards End B.

Edge lights

N = ⌈ L / Smax ⌉ → Sactual = L / N → N + 1 positions per side

Two symmetrical rows at y = ±(W/2 + DE). Maximum spacing: ICAO 60 m (instrument) or 100 m (non-instrument); FAA 200 ft (60.96 m). Edge offset is 3.0 m for jet and 0.6 m for non-jet runways unless you enter your own.

Caution zone & displaced-threshold colour

ICAO: min(600 m, L/3)  |  FAA: min(2,000 ft, L/2)

FAA: edge lights turn yellow in the last stretch of the runway as seen from an instrument approach end, and stay white in the opposite direction; each end is switched independently. ICAO: the yellow section is an edge-light provision that does not depend on an instrument approach, so the generator shows it from both approach directions. Edge lights in a displaced area show red towards the approaching aircraft.

Centreline colours

Positions are generated once, then coloured from each fixture's distance to the runway end ahead of the pilot:

ICAO: white → alternating 900–300 m → red last 300 m
FAA: white → alternating 3,000–1,000 ft → red last 1,000 ft

Because the colours depend on direction, the same fixture is white from one end and red from the other. Use the direction selector to flip the drawing and the colour counts.

Threshold, wing bars & runway end

Non-instrument and non-precision runways get a minimum of 6 threshold lights. CAT I / II / III thresholds use uniform spacing no greater than 3 m across the edge-light row span WE = W + 2·DE. Runway-end lights are a minimum of 6. A 6 m maximum spacing is applied on all precision runways as a conservative planning default, so confirm the requirement for your category in the applicable edition.

At the extremity, threshold fixtures also serve as runway-end lights and are counted once. A displaced threshold gets its own row at the displaced position plus a separate red runway-end row at the physical end. Wing-bar lights start at the edge-light line and run outward at 2.5 m spacing.

Touchdown-zone lights

BasisExtentDefault stations
ICAO900 m (half runway if L < 1,800 m)60 m first, 60 m pitch
FAA3,000 ft (half runway if L < 6,000 ft)100 ft first, 100 ft pitch

Each station is two bars of three lights, one either side of the centreline: 6 fixtures per station. The default station values follow your chosen standard when you switch it; edit them for a project-specific pattern. Stations and extents are measured from each landing threshold, for each approach direction, and the lights are unidirectional.

Lateral bar position and 1.5 m light spacing are drawing defaults. Confirm against ICAO Annex 14 Volume I, Chapter 5 (runway touchdown zone lights) or the touchdown zone layout in FAA AC 150/5340-30J.

ICAO centre line lights are spaced at approximately 15 m. A 30 m spacing is permitted only where the runway is intended for RVR of 350 m or greater and the serviceability objective can be demonstrated, so CAT II and III use 15 m. FAA uses 50 ft (15.24 m).

What this tool does not do

It does not draw approach lighting, taxiway connections, stop bars or signs. It does not reproduce figure-level endpoint conventions, so the end-of-row fixture, alternating-colour start and wing-bar inner-light handling are exposed as options rather than assumed. FAA threshold and runway-end fittings follow the FAA figures; the counts here are nominal. Always verify against the approved aerodrome lighting layout.

FAQ

Runway Lighting Layout Generator: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on what the generator builds, how it counts fixtures and what you can export.

What does the Runway Lighting Layout Generator do?

It builds a calculated runway lighting layout for planning from the design standard, runway category, dimensions and thresholds you enter. It can generate edge, threshold, wing-bar, runway-end, centreline and touchdown-zone lights, draw a plan view with a true-scale detail window, and produce a quantity schedule with colour counts for each approach direction.

Which systems and standards does it cover?

It covers ICAO Annex 14 and FAA AC 150/5340-30J, for non-instrument, non-precision, CAT I, CAT II and CAT III runways. Centreline and touchdown-zone lights switch on automatically for CAT II and CAT III, and you can still override them. It does not draw approach lighting, taxiway connections, stop bars or signs.

How are edge lights generated?

The number of intervals is N = ⌈L ÷ Smax⌉, the actual spacing is Sactual = L ÷ N, and each side has N + 1 positions. The two rows sit at y = ±(W ÷ 2 + DE). The maximum spacing is 60 m for ICAO instrument runways, 100 m for ICAO non-instrument runways and 200 ft (60.96 m) for FAA. The offset is 3.0 m for jet runways and 0.6 m for non-jet runways unless you enter your own.

How are centreline colours and counts handled?

Positions are generated once and then coloured from each fixture's distance to the runway end ahead of the pilot. ICAO is white, then alternating from 900 to 300 m, then red for the last 300 m. FAA is white, then alternating from 3,000 to 1,000 ft, then red for the last 1,000 ft. The same fixture can be white from one end and red from the other, so a direction selector flips the drawing and the colour counts. The first colour of the alternating zone is figure-specific and is exposed as a setting. ICAO centreline spacing defaults to 15 m, with 30 m allowed only where RVR is 350 m or greater and the serviceability objective is demonstrated. FAA uses 50 ft (15.24 m).

How are thresholds, wing bars and runway-end lights counted?

Non-instrument and non-precision runways get a minimum of 6 threshold lights. CAT I, II and III thresholds use uniform spacing of no more than 3 m across the edge-light row span WE = W + 2·DE. Runway-end lights are a minimum of 6. A 6 m maximum spacing is applied on all precision runways as a conservative planning default, so confirm the requirement for your category in the applicable edition. At the runway extremity the threshold fixtures also serve as runway-end lights and are counted once. A displaced threshold gets its own row plus a separate red runway-end row at the physical end, and wing-bar lights start at the edge-light line and run outward at 2.5 m spacing.

How are touchdown-zone lights calculated?

Each station is two bars of three lights, one either side of the centreline, giving 6 fixtures per station. The ICAO extent is 900 m, or half the runway if L is under 1,800 m, with a 60 m first station and 60 m pitch. The FAA extent is 3,000 ft, or half the runway if L is under 6,000 ft, with a 100 ft first station and 100 ft pitch. Stations are measured from each landing threshold, and you can edit the values for a project-specific pattern.

What can I export from the layout generator?

You can export the layout as an SVG or PNG image and the fixture schedule as a CSV file. You can also copy a summary or print the layout. The plan view shows the whole runway with the lateral scale exaggerated, plus a true-scale detail window you can slide along the runway, and hovering over a fixture shows its ID, chainage and colour.

Is the generated layout an issued drawing or final BOQ?

No. It is a calculated nominal layout for design planning, with installed fixtures only, no serviceability allowance and no spares. The generator does not reproduce figure-level endpoint conventions, so the end-of-row fixture, alternating-colour start and wing-bar inner-light handling are exposed as options. FAA threshold and runway-end counts are nominal. Always verify against the approved aerodrome lighting layout.
