Standard Operating Procedures for AGL Photometry Testing

A structured SOP ensures that AGL photometry testing is accurate, repeatable, and aligned with real airfield conditions — a practical procedure based on field execution and testing practice.

RS

Rahul Sharma

Manager – Technical & Commercial · AGL Photometry Specialist

April 7, 2026 Updated October 27, 2026

AGL photometry testing is a controlled and safety-critical activity carried out to verify that runway, taxiway, and approach lighting systems perform as per ICAO, FAA, and DGCA requirements.

A structured Standard Operating Procedure ensures that testing is accurate, repeatable, and aligned with real airfield conditions. This article presents a practical SOP based on field execution and testing practices.

Key Takeaway

Photometry is not just testing. It is operational assurance — from pre-checks to final reporting, every step in the SOP plays a role in ensuring the lighting system delivers accurate visual guidance to pilots under all operating conditions.

01

Scope of Photometry Testing

The objective of photometry testing is to ensure ICAO-compliant performance of AGL systems by verifying:

  • Light intensity — maximum, minimum, and average
  • Beam alignment — elevation and azimuth
  • Chromaticity — as per CIE 1931 / ICAO Annex 14
  • Uniformity across the runway and taxiway

Testing is typically conducted at night, which avoids ambient-light interference with the sensors and minimises disruption to operations.

02

Pre-Test Preparation

Proper preparation directly impacts the accuracy of the results.

a. Physical Inspection
  • Check all fittings for damage
  • Clean lenses using a soft cloth and mild soap solution (or the manufacturer-approved method); avoid abrasive or solvent cleaners that can damage optics. If an as-found assessment is needed, measure before cleaning and again after
  • Ensure there is no dust, moisture, or deposits on optical surfaces
  • Test in dry conditions with no rain, fog or standing water, because a water film on lenses or pavement changes the measured output; record the weather and pavement condition in the report
b. Previous Data Review
  • Analyse earlier photometric reports
  • Identify previously failed or borderline fittings

This helps the testing team focus attention on areas where performance issues may already have been identified.

c. Permissions & Coordination
  • Obtain ATC and ASO clearance
  • Issue NOTAM and obtain runway-closure approval before testing, as required
  • Conduct a briefing between the testing engineer and airport shift in-charge

Proper coordination ensures that testing is carried out within the required operational and safety framework.

03

Safety & Operational Readiness

Safety compliance is mandatory throughout testing. Before starting the activity:

  • All circuits should be operated at full brilliance (the rated maximum current, typically 6.6 A) and allowed to stabilise before measurement
  • An emergency response team should be available at the CCR room
  • The circuits must be capable of being switched OFF, and the runway released to ATC, within the time agreed (for example, 5 minutes)
  • The photometric vehicle driver must avoid damage to elevated fittings
  • Tools and support equipment should be available in the vehicle

Photometry testing takes place within an active airfield environment, making operational readiness an essential part of the SOP.

04

Equipment Setup & Calibration

Correct equipment setup and calibration are fundamental to reliable photometric measurements.

a. CCR Calibration

Verify constant current regulator output with a calibrated ammeter suitable for the CCR waveform, and record the circuit current during testing, because light output varies with current. Confirm that the photometric sensors carry valid calibration certificates.

b. Angle Calibration

Check the measuring system's angle reference and the beam angles before measurement, following the equipment manufacturer's procedure.

c. Equipment Used
Mobile Photometry VehicleFor runway and taxiway measurements
Frame ReaderFor PAPI, elevated approach lights, and RWG lights
Calibrated SensorsSensors and data acquisition systems

The Frame Reader is specifically used where mobile systems cannot perform the required measurements, such as elevated fittings.

05

Measurement Procedure

a. Mobile Photometry Testing

For runway and taxiway measurements:

  • Conduct measurements along the runway/taxiway at a controlled speed of approximately 40 km/h
  • Capture maximum, minimum, and average intensity
  • Record beam angles, including toe-in and elevation
  • Record light position using GPS/D-GPS

This provides the measurement data required for subsequent analysis and compliance evaluation.

b. Static / Frame Reader Testing

Static or Frame Reader testing is used for PAPI units, Runway Guard Lights, and elevated approach fittings. The process generates iso-candela diagrams and intensity distribution data.

c. Chromaticity Verification

Colour performance is measured to verify compliance with the applicable ICAO standards.

06

Real-Time Issue Handling

Any failed or underperforming fitting should be immediately reported. The airport maintenance team can then carry out the required corrective action, such as:

  • Cleaning
  • Alignment correction
  • Rectification of electrical issues

After rectification, retesting is carried out. This approach reduces rework and improves efficiency during the available testing window.

07

Data Recording & Analysis

All measurements should be stored digitally using the available SD card or system database. The recorded data includes:

  • Intensity values
  • Beam angles
  • GPS location
  • Fixture identification

Post-processing generates iso-candela plots, performance curves, and compliance summaries.

Accurate data recording is essential because the final compliance assessment depends on the integrity of the measured and processed information.

08

Acceptance Criteria

Acceptance criteria are evaluated as per ICAO Annex 14 / FAA requirements. The principal requirements include:

  • Minimum intensity levels must be achieved (under Annex 14 maintenance provisions, a light whose main-beam average intensity falls below 50% of the specified value is treated as unserviceable)
  • Beam spread must fall within defined limits
  • The percentage of serviceable lights required for the runway category must be met (Annex 14 maintenance objectives set category-dependent percentages, and limits on adjacent unserviceable lights, for precision approach runways; confirm the figures in the current edition and the CAR)
  • Colour must fall within specified chromaticity boundaries

These periodic checks help maintain continued performance verification of the AGL system, as commonly applied under DGCA CAR (Section 4, Series B, Part I) and aerodrome licence conditions. Always confirm the interval against the current CAR edition and your aerodrome's approved maintenance programme, as other regulators and runway categories differ.

09

Reporting

The final photometry output includes a system-level performance assessment as well as individual fixture information.

a. System Performance Overview

Runway/taxiway-level compliance summary.

b. Individual Fixture Analysis

Detailed report for selected fittings.

c. Key Outputs
  • Intensity — Maximum / Average / Minimum
  • Beam alignment
  • Chromaticity results
  • Compliance status

Reports are generated in PDF format with raw and processed data for audit reference.

10

Post-Test Activities

After completion of testing:

01
Back up all data
02
Share results with the airport authority
03
Highlight non-compliant fittings
04
Recommend corrective actions if required

These activities ensure that the test results are properly preserved, communicated, and converted into actionable maintenance or compliance requirements.

Conclusion

A well-executed SOP ensures that AGL photometry testing is not just a measurement exercise, but a complete validation of airfield lighting performance. From pre-checks to final reporting, every step plays a role in ensuring that the lighting system delivers accurate visual guidance to pilots under all operating conditions.

In real airfield projects, this structured approach helps achieve:

  • Faster approvals
  • Better compliance
  • Safer operations

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the purpose of an AGL photometry testing SOP?

AGL photometry testing is a controlled, safety-critical activity that verifies runway, taxiway and approach lighting performs as per ICAO, FAA and DGCA requirements. A structured SOP ensures testing is accurate, repeatable and aligned with real airfield conditions.

What does AGL photometry testing verify?

It verifies light intensity (maximum, minimum and average), beam alignment in elevation and azimuth, chromaticity as per CIE 1931 and ICAO Annex 14, and uniformity across the runway and taxiway. Testing is typically conducted at night to avoid ambient-light interference with the sensors and to minimise disruption to operations.

How should fittings and conditions be prepared before testing?

Check fittings for damage, clean lenses with a soft cloth and mild soap solution or the manufacturer-approved method, and avoid abrasive or solvent cleaners. Test in dry conditions with no rain, fog or standing water, because a water film changes the measured output. If an as-found assessment is needed, measure before cleaning and again after, and record the weather and pavement condition.

What safety and operational readiness steps are required?

Obtain ATC and ASO clearance, issue a NOTAM and obtain runway-closure approval as required, and brief the airport shift in-charge. Operate all circuits at full brilliance (the rated maximum current, typically 6.6 A) and let them stabilise, keep an emergency response team at the CCR room, and make sure circuits can be switched off and the runway released to ATC within the agreed time, for example 5 minutes.

How is the equipment calibrated and what is used?

Verify CCR output with a calibrated ammeter suitable for the CCR waveform and record the circuit current during testing, because light output varies with current, and confirm that sensors have valid calibration certificates. Check the angle reference before measurement. A mobile photometry vehicle is used for runways and taxiways and a Frame Reader for PAPI, elevated approach lights and RWG lights.

How are the measurements carried out?

Mobile testing runs along the runway or taxiway at a controlled speed of approximately 40 km/h, capturing maximum, minimum and average intensity and beam angles including toe-in and elevation, with light position recorded by GPS or D-GPS. Static or Frame Reader testing is used for PAPI units, Runway Guard Lights and elevated fittings, and chromaticity is also verified.

What are the acceptance criteria and testing frequency?

Minimum intensity levels, beam spread, the category-dependent percentage of serviceable lights and chromaticity boundaries are evaluated against ICAO Annex 14 and FAA requirements, and under Annex 14 maintenance provisions a light whose main-beam average intensity is below 50% of the specified value is treated as unserviceable. The article lists DGCA frequencies of every 6 months for inset lights and every 12 months for elevated lights, which must be confirmed against the current CAR and the aerodrome's approved maintenance programme.

What does the final photometry report contain?

It includes a system-level performance overview, individual fixture analysis and key outputs such as maximum, average and minimum intensity, beam alignment, chromaticity results and compliance status. Reports are generated in PDF format with raw and processed data for audit reference, and after testing the data is backed up, shared with the airport authority and non-compliant fittings are highlighted with recommended corrective actions.
