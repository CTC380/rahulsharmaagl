Each topic follows the full engineering diagnostic record: symptom → observation → measurement → decision → fault location → root cause → verification → corrective action → preventive action.

SCF-391 Unexpected Circuit Resistance

Symptom — A routine or pre-energisation loop resistance measurement on a series circuit reads noticeably different from the recorded as-built or last-known-good baseline, with no fixture reported dark and no CCR alarm active.

Equipment / System

Series circuit primary loop (CCR secondary output through isolation transformers and cable back to CCR), as measured loop-to-loop or loop-to-earth with the circuit isolated.

Possible Causes
MedCircuit records/baseline itself outdated or incorrectly logged after a prior modification, addition, or repair of the run
MedA recent repair, splice, or component replacement altered the loop's total resistance without being reflected in the maintenance record
MedGradual, distributed insulation or connection ageing across the whole loop rather than a single discrete fault
LowTest equipment or test lead condition, calibration drift, or a different meter/technique used than for the baseline reading
LowAmbient temperature at time of test differing significantly from the baseline test temperature, affecting conductor resistance
LowAn early-stage, not-yet-symptomatic fault developing at one point in the loop
Required Tools
Digital loop/insulation resistance tester (megger)Calibrated true-RMS multimeterAs-built circuit drawing with recorded baseline resistanceMaintenance/test history logTemperature reference for the test session
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the CCR before taking any resistance measurement.
  • Confirm the circuit is fully isolated and discharged before connecting test leads.
  • Do not rely on a single anomalous reading to declare a fault — repeat the measurement before acting.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationRe-verify the baseline value in the circuit record and confirm it corresponds to the correct circuit and the correct test method (loop, or loop-to-earth).
  2. MeasurementRepeat the resistance measurement at least twice, with the circuit fully isolated, to rule out a test-technique or connection error.
  3. ObservationCheck the maintenance log for any recent work on this circuit that could explain a genuine change in resistance.
  4. DecisionReading confirmed and no recorded explanation → treat as a genuine deviation and proceed to segment-by-segment testing under SCF-392/393. Reading explained by recorded work or test-method difference → update the baseline record and close out.
  5. Root CauseEstablish whether the deviation reflects an actual physical change in the circuit or a record-keeping/test-condition discrepancy.
Test Points
Current total loop resistance vs. baseline
Individual segment resistance where segmented testing is available
Ambient temperature at time of test
Maintenance log entries for the circuit
Root Cause Verification

Confirm the reading is stable and repeatable across at least two independent measurements, and that the circuit record has been reconciled with either an explanation or an updated baseline.

Corrective Action
  • Update the circuit's baseline resistance record if the change is confirmed genuine and within acceptable limits.
  • Correct the maintenance record if a prior repair was not logged.
  • Escalate to segment-by-segment testing under SCF-392 (excessive) or SCF-393 (low) if the deviation is outside acceptable tolerance.
Preventive Action
  • Log loop resistance at every scheduled test using a consistent method, meter, and reference temperature.
  • Require all splices, repairs, and component changes on series circuits to be logged with an updated resistance reading.
  • Trend resistance readings year over year rather than treating each test in isolation.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SCF-01 (Series Circuit Resistance Test Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364 (insulation/loop testing), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

SCF-392 Excessive Circuit Resistance

Symptom — Total series circuit loop resistance measures significantly above baseline, and the CCR is having to drive a higher output voltage than normal to maintain the commanded constant current, potentially approaching the regulator's voltage limit or triggering a high-voltage alarm.

Equipment / System

Series circuit primary loop — cable, splices, connectors, and isolation transformer primary windings in series around the CCR output.

Possible Causes
HighA developing high-resistance connection at a splice, terminal, or connector somewhere in the loop
HighCorrosion at a cable joint or pull-box connection increasing contact resistance
MedOne or more isolation transformer primary windings degraded, increasing their series impedance
MedA recent repair using an incorrect cable gauge or an unnecessarily long splice run adding resistance
LowCumulative resistance from a large number of ageing connections distributed across a long circuit
LowCCR output voltage sensing or calibration issue reporting a resistance rise that is not physically present
Required Tools
Loop resistance tester (megger)Clamp-on ammeter and true-RMS multimeterCCR output voltage trend logThermal imaging cameraSeries circuit fault locator / TDRAs-built circuit drawing with segment breakpoints
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the CCR before any loop testing or connection work.
  • Treat any connection identified as high-resistance as a potential heat/fire risk — inspect for scorching or insulation damage before re-energising.
  • Allow components to cool before handling if the circuit was recently energised at a high step.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the CCR output voltage trend to confirm the regulator is compensating with higher voltage for a normal commanded current, consistent with a resistance rise rather than a current-sensing fault.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the loop in accessible segments and compare each against baseline to localise the segment contributing the greatest resistance increase.
  3. ObservationUse thermal imaging during a brief live period, where safe to do so, to identify a hot connection consistent with high resistance under load.
  4. DecisionResistance rise isolated to one segment or connection → localised repair. Resistance rise distributed across many segments → treat as cumulative loop ageing and plan proactive maintenance.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific splice, connector, or transformer winding contributing most to the resistance rise.
  6. Root CauseConfirm with a full-loop re-test that resistance and CCR output voltage are restored to expected values.
Test Points
Segment-by-segment loop resistance vs. baseline
CCR output voltage trend for the commanded current
Thermal signature of suspect connections under load
Isolation transformer primary winding resistance
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-test the full loop resistance and confirm the CCR output voltage for the commanded current has returned to its expected baseline range across all intensity steps.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the identified high-resistance splice or connector.
  • Replace an isolation transformer with a degraded primary winding.
  • Correct an incorrectly specified splice or cable run from a prior repair.
Preventive Action
  • Include loop resistance and CCR output voltage trending in the periodic maintenance cycle.
  • Use thermal imaging on accessible connections during scheduled inspections.
  • Prioritise ageing connections for proactive replacement before they progress to a hard fault.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SCF-02 (Loop Resistance Fault Location Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5345-10 (constant current regulators)

Case Study: Division 01 — CCR Troubleshooting (output voltage limit alarms)

Rev 0 — Initial issue
SCF-393 Low Circuit Resistance

Symptom — Total series circuit loop resistance measures significantly below baseline, without a corresponding earth-fault alarm, suggesting a partial short or reduced-impedance path has developed between conductors or across a transformer winding.

Equipment / System

Series circuit primary loop and isolation transformer primary windings, tested loop-to-loop with the circuit isolated.

Possible Causes
HighPartial insulation breakdown between the two primary conductors at a chafed or damaged cable point
HighA shorted or partially shorted isolation transformer primary winding
MedMoisture bridging exposed conductor at a poorly sealed splice or connector, without yet producing a full earth-fault path
MedAn incorrect splice or repair inadvertently reducing loop impedance (e.g. conductors bridged)
LowTest equipment fault or lead condition producing a falsely low reading
LowA parallel unintended path introduced by a temporary bonding or test connection left in place
Required Tools
Loop resistance tester (megger)True-RMS multimeterSeries circuit fault locator / TDRAs-built circuit drawingInsulation resistance tester (1000V/2500V)
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the CCR before any testing.
  • Treat a low-resistance reading as a potential short-circuit hazard — do not re-energise the circuit until the cause is confirmed and cleared.
  • Be alert for signs of prior arcing or heat damage at the suspect location.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the low reading is repeatable and not an artefact of test lead placement or a temporary connection left on the circuit.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the loop in accessible segments and identify the segment showing reduced resistance relative to baseline.
  3. MeasurementTest each isolation transformer primary winding individually within the suspect segment to rule in or out a shorted transformer.
  4. DecisionFault isolated to a cable segment → proceed to cable fault location. Fault isolated to a single transformer → replace that transformer.
  5. Fault LocationNarrow to the specific cable length, splice, or transformer responsible for the reduced resistance.
  6. Root CauseConfirm with a full-loop re-test and an insulation resistance test that the reading is restored above the acceptable threshold.
Test Points
Segment-by-segment loop resistance vs. baseline
Individual isolation transformer primary winding resistance
Suspect splice/connector condition
Full-loop insulation resistance
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-test the full loop resistance and confirm it has returned to within the expected baseline range, with no residual reduced-impedance path detected.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the damaged cable section causing the partial short.
  • Replace the shorted isolation transformer.
  • Correct the faulty splice or remove any inadvertent bonding/test connection.
Preventive Action
  • Include loop resistance testing in the periodic maintenance cycle to catch a developing partial short before it becomes a hard fault.
  • Ensure temporary test connections are logged and removed before returning a circuit to service.
  • Inspect splices for correct sealing and separation during routine maintenance.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SCF-03 (Reduced-Resistance Fault Location Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, IS 12290 (isolating transformers)

Case Study: Division 01 — CCR Troubleshooting (current regulation behaviour under reduced impedance)

Rev 0 — Initial issue
SCF-394 Circuit Voltage Drop Excessive

Symptom — The voltage drop measured across a section of the series circuit — or the CCR's total output voltage for the commanded current — is higher than expected, without a hard open or earth fault present.

Equipment / System

Series circuit primary loop, CCR output stage, and isolation transformer primary windings across the affected section.

Possible Causes
HighA developing high-resistance connection within the measured section (see SCF-392) causing localised voltage drop
MedUndersized conductor used in a prior repair for the length of run installed
MedA long circuit run approaching its design voltage-drop limit even without a discrete fault, particularly at high intensity steps
MedCCR nearing its maximum output voltage capability for the total connected load
LowVoltage measurement taken at an incorrect point or with an incorrect meter category, giving a misleading reading
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeter (CAT III/IV as appropriate)CCR output voltage/current trend logAs-built circuit drawing with conductor gauge and length dataClamp-on ammeter
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before disconnecting any point to insert a meter in series, or use appropriate live-measurement rated equipment and PPE if a live voltage-drop test is required.
  • Confirm meter category rating is appropriate for the circuit voltage before connecting.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the CCR output voltage and current trend to determine whether the total loop is approaching the regulator's rated output voltage for the commanded current.
  2. MeasurementMeasure voltage drop across defined sections of the loop, with the circuit energised at a safe, controlled step if live testing is required and permitted, or isolate and measure resistance per SCF-392 if not.
  3. ObservationCompare the as-built conductor gauge and run length for the section against the expected voltage-drop calculation for that design.
  4. DecisionVoltage drop consistent with a single high-resistance point → treat as SCF-392. Voltage drop consistent with conductor gauge/length across the whole run → treat as a design/loading issue rather than a discrete fault.
  5. Root CauseConfirm whether the excessive drop is due to a discrete fault, an undersized conductor, or the circuit approaching its designed capacity.
Test Points
Section-by-section voltage drop
CCR total output voltage vs. rated maximum for the commanded current
Conductor gauge and run length vs. design calculation
Root Cause Verification

After corrective action, re-measure section voltage drop and confirm CCR output voltage for the commanded current is within its normal operating range across all intensity steps.

Corrective Action
  • Repair the high-resistance point identified within the affected section.
  • Replace an undersized conductor section with correctly rated cable.
  • If the circuit is at or near its design voltage-drop limit without a fault, flag for capacity/load review rather than repeated fault-finding.
Preventive Action
  • Verify conductor sizing against calculated voltage drop for any circuit extension or repair before installation.
  • Trend CCR output voltage for the commanded current over time to catch a gradual rise early.
  • Include voltage-drop verification in commissioning and periodic testing for long circuit runs.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SCF-04 (Voltage Drop Test Procedure), SOP-CCR-02 (CCR Output Verification)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5345-10, IS 12290

Case Study: Division 01 — CCR Troubleshooting (regulator output voltage limits)

Rev 0 — Initial issue
SCF-395 Circuit Current Imbalance

Symptom — The measured series circuit current does not consistently match the value commanded for the selected intensity step, or differs when measured at different points around the loop, when in a true series circuit the current should be identical at every point.

Equipment / System

CCR output, series circuit primary loop, and current measurement instrumentation/CTs used for monitoring.

Possible Causes
HighCCR output current regulation fault or calibration drift — verify against a calibrated reference ammeter
HighAn unintended parallel or leakage current path allowing part of the current to bypass the intended series loop
MedA clamp-on ammeter or monitoring CT reading in error due to incorrect placement, saturation, or calibration
MedA developing earth fault providing a partial alternate current path (see SCF-396 onward)
LowHarmonic or waveform distortion on the circuit affecting a true-RMS reading if a non-true-RMS meter is used
Required Tools
Calibrated true-RMS clamp-on ammeterCCR reference/calibration ammeterEarth-fault/ground-fault monitor readingOscilloscope or power quality logger (for waveform verification)
Safety Precautions
  • Treat the circuit as energised and hazardous during live current measurement — use correctly rated CT/clamp equipment.
  • Do not open a live series circuit to insert a meter in series — always use a clamp-on current measurement.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the discrepancy using a calibrated true-RMS clamp-on ammeter at more than one point on the loop, ruling out a single faulty monitoring instrument.
  2. MeasurementCheck the CCR's earth-fault/ground-fault monitor for any indicated leakage current that would account for a current difference around the loop.
  3. MeasurementIf instrumentation is confirmed accurate and a genuine difference exists, this indicates a parallel or leakage path is present rather than a true series condition.
  4. DecisionDiscrepancy resolves to an instrumentation error → correct or recalibrate the meter. Genuine current difference confirmed → escalate to earth-fault/leakage investigation under SCF-396.
  5. Root CauseConfirm whether the imbalance is instrumentation-related or reflects an actual leakage/parallel path in the circuit.
Test Points
Current at CCR output
Current at one or more points around the loop
Earth-fault/ground-fault monitor reading
Reference/calibration ammeter comparison
Root Cause Verification

After corrective action, confirm current readings match at multiple points around the loop and the earth-fault monitor shows no unexplained leakage, across all commanded intensity steps.

Corrective Action
  • Recalibrate or replace faulty monitoring instrumentation.
  • Investigate and repair a confirmed leakage or parallel path per SCF-396 through SCF-400.
  • Correct CCR current regulation calibration if drift is confirmed at the regulator itself.
Preventive Action
  • Periodically cross-check CCR current readings against an independent calibrated reference ammeter.
  • Include earth-fault/ground-fault monitor verification in the periodic maintenance cycle.
  • Maintain calibration records for all series-circuit current measurement instruments.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SCF-05 (Circuit Current Verification Procedure), SOP-CCR-02 (CCR Output Verification)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5345-10

SCF-396 Current Leakage to Earth

Symptom — The CCR's earth-fault (ground-fault) monitor indicates a measurable leakage current from the series circuit to earth, below the level that trips protection, without any fixture reported dark.

Equipment / System

Series circuit primary loop, isolation transformer insulation systems, and CCR earth-fault/ground-fault monitoring circuit.

Possible Causes
HighGradual moisture ingress at one or more cable joints, pull boxes, or base cans reducing insulation resistance
HighA single degrading isolation transformer developing a partial insulation breakdown to its core/earth
MedAged cable jacket allowing minor moisture or conductive contamination to bridge to earth at one point
MedA connector with a corroded or partially seated pin providing a high-resistance leakage path to earth
LowCCR earth-fault monitor sensitivity or calibration issue reporting leakage current that is not physically significant
LowCumulative distributed insulation ageing across an older circuit rather than one discrete point
Required Tools
CCR earth-fault/ground-fault trend log1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)Segment-by-segment test plan and as-built drawingMoisture detection meter
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before insulation testing.
  • Treat any confirmed leakage current as a developing hazard rather than a nuisance reading — do not disable or desensitise earth-fault protection to suppress the reading.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the CCR earth-fault/ground-fault trend to determine whether leakage is stable, increasing, or correlates with weather (see SCF-409).
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the loop in accessible segments from pull boxes or base cans, comparing each against baseline.
  3. MeasurementIdentify the segment or transformer contributing the greatest reduction in insulation resistance.
  4. DecisionLeakage isolated to one segment/transformer → localised repair per SCF-397 through SCF-400. Leakage distributed across many segments → treat as cumulative circuit ageing.
  5. Fault LocationNarrow to the specific light, transformer, cable section, or connector contributing most to the leakage.
  6. Root CauseConfirm with a full-loop re-test that insulation resistance is restored to, or above, the acceptable threshold.
Test Points
Segment-by-segment insulation resistance vs. baseline
Individual isolation transformer insulation resistance
CCR earth-fault trend log
Joint/connector/base-can condition in the lowest-reading segment
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-megger the full loop and confirm the reading is restored above the acceptable threshold, and monitor the CCR earth-fault trend over subsequent operating cycles.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the identified degraded cable segment, joint, or connector.
  • Replace the transformer with the leaking insulation.
  • Reseal an identified moisture entry point at a pull box, base can, or connector.
Preventive Action
  • Schedule periodic full-loop insulation resistance testing and trend results rather than waiting for a nuisance trip.
  • Prioritise ageing circuit sections for proactive cable or connector replacement.
  • Seal known moisture entry points identified during preventive maintenance.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SCF-06 (Earth Leakage Fault Location Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

Case Study: Division 25 — Field Case Study Troubleshooting

Rev 0 — Initial issue
SCF-397 Earth Fault at Light

Symptom — Segment-by-segment insulation testing has localised a confirmed earth fault to an individual light fixture, distinguishing it from a fault in the cable, transformer, or connector serving that fixture.

Equipment / System

Light fixture body, base can, and the internal wiring between the fixture's secondary connection and its lamp/LED module.

Possible Causes
HighWater ingress into the fixture body through a failed or degraded gasket/seal
HighInternal wiring insulation damage inside the fixture from heat, vibration, or age
MedA conductive foreign body or debris bridging live wiring to the fixture's earthed body inside the base can
MedPhysical damage to the fixture from vehicle/aircraft wheel loading or a ground strike compromising internal insulation
LowManufacturing defect or latent fault in the fixture's internal insulation
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)Fixture-specific service/overhaul documentationReplacement gaskets/sealsBase-can inspection kit
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before opening the fixture.
  • Treat the fixture as a potential shock hazard until confirmed isolated and the fault verified de-energised.
  • Allow the fixture to cool before handling if recently energised.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated and the fixture disconnected at its secondary connector, megger the fixture body and internal wiring independently of the base can and cable.
  2. ObservationOpen the fixture and inspect for water ingress, gasket/seal condition, internal wiring damage, or foreign debris.
  3. DecisionInsulation fault confirmed within the fixture itself, isolated from cable/connector/transformer → proceed to repair or replace the fixture. Fault clears when fixture is disconnected but base can/connector remains suspect → re-route to SCF-400.
  4. Root CauseIdentify the specific ingress point, damaged wiring run, or debris path causing the earth fault.
Test Points
Fixture internal insulation resistance (isolated from cable/base can)
Gasket/seal condition
Internal wiring condition
Base can water/debris presence
Root Cause Verification

After repair or replacement, re-megger the fixture in isolation and confirm the reading is restored above the acceptable threshold before reconnecting to the circuit.

Corrective Action
  • Replace failed gaskets/seals and confirm fixture is watertight before reinstallation.
  • Repair or replace damaged internal wiring.
  • Replace the fixture if internal damage is extensive or the fixture is beyond economical repair.
  • Clear debris and confirm the base can is properly drained.
Preventive Action
  • Include fixture seal/gasket condition in the periodic maintenance and relamping cycle.
  • Confirm base can drainage is functioning during routine inspection.
  • Track fixtures with recurring earth-fault history for proactive replacement.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SCF-07 (Fixture-Level Earth Fault Isolation Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5345-46, IEC 60364

Rev 0 — Initial issue
SCF-398 Earth Fault at Transformer

Symptom — Segment-by-segment insulation testing has localised a confirmed earth fault to an isolation transformer, with the associated fixture, cable, and connector tested clear.

Equipment / System

Individual isolation transformer serving one light fixture on the series circuit, including its primary and secondary windings and core.

Possible Causes
HighMoisture ingress into the transformer housing degrading winding-to-core insulation
HighInternal winding insulation breakdown from thermal ageing or an internal short
MedTransformer physically damaged (impact, cracked housing) allowing moisture or contamination entry
MedIncorrect transformer type/rating installed at a prior replacement, operating outside its designed thermal/insulation margin
LowManufacturing defect or latent insulation fault in the transformer
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)Isolation transformer test setReplacement transformer of correct ratingBase-can inspection kit
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before disconnecting or testing the transformer.
  • Treat a confirmed transformer earth fault as a shock/fire hazard until the unit is removed from service.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, disconnect the transformer from both the primary loop and the fixture, and megger the primary winding, secondary winding, and core independently.
  2. ObservationInspect the transformer housing for cracking, corrosion, or evidence of moisture ingress.
  3. DecisionInsulation fault confirmed within the transformer, isolated from fixture and cable → replace the transformer with a correctly rated unit. Reading remains marginal after removal → re-check the base can and connector per SCF-400.
  4. Root CauseConfirm whether the fault is due to moisture ingress, thermal ageing, physical damage, or an incorrect unit.
Test Points
Transformer primary winding insulation resistance
Transformer secondary winding insulation resistance
Transformer core condition
Base can/housing seal condition
Root Cause Verification

After replacement, re-megger the new transformer in isolation and confirm the reading meets specification before reconnecting to the circuit, then re-test the full loop.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the faulty isolation transformer with a unit of the correct rating.
  • Confirm the replacement transformer is correctly sealed and seated in the base can.
  • Verify the transformer tap/rating matches the fixture's design requirement.
Preventive Action
  • Include isolation transformer insulation testing in the periodic maintenance cycle.
  • Confirm transformer ratings match design specification at every replacement.
  • Track transformers with recurring earth-fault history by manufacturer/batch for possible systemic issues.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SCF-08 (Transformer Earth Fault Isolation Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IS 12290, IEC 60364, FAA AC 150/5345-47 (isolation transformers)

Case Study: Division 25 — Field Case Study Troubleshooting

Rev 0 — Initial issue
SCF-399 Earth Fault at Cable

Symptom — Segment-by-segment insulation testing has localised a confirmed earth fault to a section of the series circuit primary cable itself, with the fixtures, transformers, and connectors serving that section tested clear.

Equipment / System

Series circuit primary cable and its insulation system between two accessible test points (pull boxes, base cans, or transformer connections).

Possible Causes
HighCable jacket damage from a prior excavation, trenching, or ground-vehicle activity allowing moisture ingress
HighAged cable jacket cracking from UV/thermal cycling, particularly at pavement expansion joints or exposed sections
MedRodent or wildlife damage to the cable jacket
MedA poorly made or unsealed splice within the section acting as an insulation weak point
LowManufacturing defect in the original cable
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)Time-domain reflectometer (TDR) or cable fault locatorCable route drawing with pull box/joint locationsExcavation and cable repair kit
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before insulation testing or excavation.
  • Confirm underground service locates are obtained before any excavation near the cable route.
  • Treat the exposed cable end as energisable until confirmed isolated.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the loop in segments between accessible pull boxes/base cans to bracket the faulty section between two known-good test points.
  2. MeasurementUse a TDR or cable fault locator referenced to the cable route drawing to estimate the fault's physical distance within the bracketed section.
  3. ObservationExcavate at the estimated location, following correct utility-locate procedure, and visually inspect the cable jacket.
  4. DecisionFault located and visually confirmed → repair or replace the damaged section. Fault distance estimate uncertain → re-test from the opposite end to cross-reference the location before excavating.
  5. Root CauseConfirm the specific mechanism of jacket damage (impact, ageing, wildlife, splice failure).
Test Points
Segment-by-segment insulation resistance bracketing the fault
TDR fault distance estimate
Cable jacket condition at the excavated point
Splice condition within the section
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-megger the full section and the full loop, and confirm the reading is restored above the acceptable threshold before backfilling and reinstating pavement.

Corrective Action
  • Repair the damaged cable section using a correctly rated, sealed splice kit, or replace the full section if damage is extensive.
  • Reinstate pavement/trench per airport engineering standards.
  • Update the as-built cable route drawing to reflect the repair location.
Preventive Action
  • Include periodic segment insulation testing in the maintenance cycle to catch developing jacket damage before a hard fault.
  • Maintain accurate cable route records to speed future fault location.
  • Coordinate with airport works/excavation teams to avoid future accidental cable damage near known routes.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SCF-09 (Cable Fault Location and Repair Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

Case Study: Division 25 — Field Case Study Troubleshooting

Rev 0 — Initial issue
SCF-400 Earth Fault at Connector

Symptom — Segment-by-segment insulation testing has localised a confirmed earth fault to an L-823 (or equivalent) connector, with the fixture, transformer, and cable on either side of it tested clear.

Equipment / System

L-823 primary or secondary connector and its immediate base can environment.

Possible Causes
HighMoisture ingress into a connector that is not fully seated, has a degraded O-ring/seal, or was not properly greased at installation
HighCorroded connector pins providing a leakage path to earth via base can moisture
MedIncorrect connector type or a damaged connector body used at a prior repair
MedBase can flooding due to blocked or failed drainage, submerging the connector
LowMechanical damage to the connector from base can lid handling or prior maintenance access
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)L-823 connector test adaptorConnector grease/sealant rated for the applicationReplacement connector kitBase-can drainage inspection tool
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before disconnecting or testing the connector.
  • Treat standing water in a base can as potentially energised until the circuit is confirmed isolated.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, disconnect the connector and megger each side (fixture/transformer side and cable side) independently to confirm the fault is within the connector itself rather than either component.
  2. ObservationInspect the connector for a degraded or missing seal, corrosion on the pins, incomplete seating, or physical damage.
  3. ObservationCheck the base can for standing water and confirm whether drainage is functioning.
  4. DecisionConnector confirmed as the fault source, both sides testing clear → clean, reseal, or replace the connector. Fault persists after connector replacement → re-check the adjoining fixture or cable.
  5. Root CauseConfirm whether the root cause is a sealing/installation defect or a base can drainage failure allowing prolonged water exposure.
Test Points
Connector pin insulation resistance (both sides independently)
Seal/O-ring condition
Base can water level and drainage condition
Connector seating/torque
Root Cause Verification

CCR output waveform capture / power quality loggerTrue-RMS multimeter with min/max or peak holdOscilloscope (for detailed transient capture where available)CCR earth-fault trend log correlated with switching events
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before any testing not specifically requiring the circuit to be live.
  • Where live transient capture is required, ensure equipment is correctly rated and connected by a competent person before switching.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the fault correlates specifically with switching events (energisation, de-energisation, or intensity-step changes) rather than occurring at random during steady-state operation.
  2. MeasurementCapture the circuit's current/voltage waveform through several switching events using a power quality logger or oscilloscope to characterise the transient.
  3. MeasurementCompare the transient profile against the CCR's rated soft-start/step-change behaviour to determine whether the transient itself is abnormal or a weak point in the loop is reacting to a normal transient.
  4. DecisionTransient found abnormal at the CCR → route to Division 01 for CCR investigation. Transient normal but a specific loop component reacts to it → isolate and test that component's tolerance to a simulated switching transient.
  5. Fault LocationIdentify the specific component (transformer, connection, or protective device setting) that reacts to the switching transient.
  6. Root CauseConfirm whether the underlying issue is a genuinely marginal component or an over-sensitive protective device setting responding to normal switching behaviour.
Test Points
Waveform capture across several switching events
CCR soft-start/step-change transient profile vs. specification
Component condition/insulation at the point identified as reacting to the transient
Root Cause Verification

Re-test across multiple subsequent switching events, including energisation, de-energisation, and step changes, to confirm the fault no longer recurs under normal switching operation.

Corrective Action
  • Replace or repair the specific component confirmed to fail under normal switching transients.
  • Adjust protective device sensitivity settings if confirmed to be responding to a normal transient rather than a genuine fault, only after engineering review.
  • Escalate to CCR-level investigation and correction if the transient itself is confirmed abnormal.
Preventive Action
  • Capture and retain a reference switching transient profile for each circuit at commissioning for future comparison.
  • Include switching-event behaviour in periodic testing for circuits with a history of switching-dependent faults.
  • Review protective device sensitivity settings against the CCR's actual normal transient profile during commissioning.
