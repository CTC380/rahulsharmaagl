Overview

ICAO Annex 14 and AGL

Airfield Ground Lighting is a fundamental part of aerodrome visual aids. The primary international reference is ICAO Annex 14, Volume I, Aerodrome Design and Operations, which sets Standards and Recommended Practices for the visual aids that support runway operations, taxiing, approach, low-visibility operation, surface movement, runway identification, approach slope guidance and runway-entry awareness.

      Lighting System

      The ICAO Aerodrome Lighting System

      Aerodrome lighting is a coordinated visual system. No single runway lighting configuration applies to every aerodrome: the arrangement depends on runway classification, instrument approach, operational category, runway width and geometry, visibility conditions, aircraft operations and national requirements.

      Runway

      Runway Lighting Requirements

      Edge lights

      Define the lateral runway limits and give longitudinal guidance. Consider location, spacing, orientation, elevation, colour, intensity, beam distribution, condition and visibility from the relevant operating directions.

      Centreline lights

      Used where the runway operation needs centreline guidance, especially precision approach and reduced visibility. In-pavement, so watch rubber contamination, water ingress, lens condition, fixture elevation and impact damage.

      Threshold lights

      Identify the start of the landing area for an arriving aircraft. Verify colour, intensity, alignment, beam distribution, position and orientation, and treat them together with edge, approach and end lighting and markings.

      Runway end lights

      Give the visual indication at the runway end. For bidirectional fixtures, test each viewing direction; inspecting only one optical direction is a common mistake.

      Touchdown zone lights

      Extra guidance in the touchdown area, usually multiple in-pavement fixtures. Check location, spacing, optical direction, intensity, colour, contamination and pavement interface.

      Precision approach runways

      Need more complex lighting for lower-visibility operations: precision approach lighting, edge, centreline and touchdown zone lighting, taxiway centreline lighting, stop bars, PAPI, enhanced control and monitoring, and secondary power.

      Runway configuration for precision approach follows the applicable runway category and operational requirements, not a generic list.

      Taxiway

      Taxiway Lighting, Stop Bars and Runway Guard Lights

      ICAO Taxiway Lighting Requirements

      Taxiway lighting supports movement between runways, aprons, terminals, holding areas and intersections. The configuration depends on taxiway function and aerodrome design.

      Edge and centreline lights

      Edge lights (elevated or in-pavement) mark taxiway limits; centreline lights give longitudinal guidance. Assess centreline lighting as a continuous visual path, not independent fixtures. Check alignment, spacing, colour, intensity, contamination, water ingress and damage.

      Stop bars

      A lighting function and an operational control function: stop-bar lights, control logic, monitoring and runway-entry procedures. Test lamp or fixture operation, intensity, colour, alignment, control and monitoring response, and interlocks where applicable. Availability and control matter especially in low visibility.

      Runway guard lights

      Enhanced visual indication at runway holding positions: elevated or in-pavement, flashing or steady, separately controlled. Verify requirements from the current design and equipment documents, and test operation, colour, intensity, flash characteristics, alignment, control and monitoring.

      ICAO Approach Lighting Requirements

      Visual guidance before the threshold: centreline lights, crossbars, elevated and inset lights, sequenced lighting and associated aids. Evaluate the visual pattern, not one fixture at a time.

      PAPI

      PAPI Requirements

      A conventional four-unit PAPI uses different individual aiming angles to create the approach-slope sequence. ICAO requirements address it within approach-slope guidance, siting and visual-aid design.

      IndicationAircraft position
      4 whiteHigh
      3 white + 1 redSlightly high
      2 white + 2 redOn the designed path
      1 white + 3 redSlightly low
      4 redLow

      Location

      Comes from the approved aerodrome design: threshold position, PAPI elevation, runway longitudinal profile, approach slope, aircraft eye height, threshold-crossing geometry, obstacle clearance and any ILS relationship. Do not publish one "ICAO PAPI distance from threshold" as universal.

      Aiming

      Units are individually aimed to create the visual sectors. Parameters: nominal approach slope, unit setting angles, elevation, azimuth, spacing and mechanical stability. Take values from the applicable controlled standard and approved design, verify independently at commissioning and reassess after disturbance or relevant maintenance.

      Elevation and threshold geometry

      PAPI elevation, position, threshold elevation, selected approach slope and aircraft eye-to-wheel geometry together set the threshold-crossing condition. Tie the installation to survey data and a controlled design calculation.

      Photometry

      Depending on requirements: intensity, colour, beam distribution, red/white transition, horizontal and vertical coverage and alignment-related characteristics, using calibrated equipment. ICAO operational material lists PAPI calibration among visual-aid status considerations.

      Colour and Intensity

      Lighting Colours, Intensity and Photometry

      ICAO Lighting Colour Requirements

      The meaning of a light often depends on its colour and location. Treat colour as an engineering parameter, and use the applicable photometric or chromaticity procedure where objective measurement is required. Do not publish one generic coordinate as an ICAO limit without confirming its source and applicability.

      ICAO Light Intensity Requirements

      Tied to the light's visual function: lighting type, runway or taxiway application, operational category, direction, beam distribution, operating condition and environment. High peak output does not prove compliance.

      Photometry

      Gives quantitative evidence of intensity, distribution, coverage, colour, alignment and degradation, supporting commissioning, acceptance, periodic inspection, maintenance, rehabilitation and troubleshooting.

      The relevant question is: does the fixture provide the required photometric performance over the specified angular region and operating condition? That is why intensity and distribution are considered together.

      Electrical

      Electrical Systems, Secondary Power and Control

      Visual performance depends on reliable power, which is why ICAO separates visual-aid guidance (Doc 9157 Part 4) from electrical-system guidance (Part 5). A visual deficiency can originate in the electrical infrastructure.

      Power supply
      CCR
      Primary series circuit
      Isolation transformer
      Secondary connection
      Light fixture

      Secondary power

      Can be critical for lower-visibility operations: standby generators, UPS, automatic transfer equipment, independent feeders and dedicated AGL supplies. Switching and restoration performance depends on operational category and applicable requirements. ICAO contingency guidance lists power-system status as part of aerodrome readiness.

      Control and monitoring

      ATC or airport operations command the ALCMS, which controls the CCR or circuit controller and the AGL circuit. Status returns from lighting and circuits through monitoring and ALCMS to the operator. Monitoring covers CCR, circuit and lighting status, faults, communications, equipment availability and alarms.

      Frangibility

      Doc 9157 Part 6 covers frangibility. It is relevant to elevated runway, taxiway and approach lights, signs, PAPI and other structures in aircraft operating areas. Assess the complete installation, not the fixture alone.

      Maintenance

      Maintenance and Inspection

      ICAO treats maintenance as essential to visual-aid reliability: installed lighting must keep operating at the required performance. Activities include visual inspection, cleaning, fixture inspection, electrical testing, alignment checking, photometric measurement, corrective maintenance, replacement and documentation. Inspection frequency comes from the airport's approved maintenance programme and regulatory requirements.

      Runway and taxiway inspection

      Look for failed fixtures, damaged lenses, contamination, misalignment, water ingress, loose mounting and mechanical damage. On taxiways add in-pavement contamination, standing water and control faults. Take measurements where severity or cause needs establishing.

      PAPI maintenance covers

      • Optical: lenses, filters, light source, colour, beam
      • Mechanical: foundation, mounting, level, alignment, aiming, damage
      • Electrical: supply, wiring, connectors, control, monitoring
      • Performance: intensity, coverage, colour, alignment, photometry
      A PAPI that is electrically operational can still be unsuitable if its beam geometry has shifted.

      Compliance Evidence

      Documentation, Acceptance Testing and Electrical vs Photometric Tests

      A compliance file should show what was designed, installed and tested, what requirement was applied, what deficiencies were found, what corrective action was completed and the final result.

      Design and equipment records

      • AGL, runway, taxiway and approach layouts
      • PAPI siting and geometry
      • Electrical drawings and control diagrams
      • Equipment schedules, datasheets, certification documents, manuals

      Testing and final records

      • Cable, insulation, electrical and CCR tests
      • Photometric, PAPI, sign and control-system reports
      • As-built drawings and maintenance baseline
      • Corrective-action register, retests, commissioning certificate

      Acceptance testing against the approved design

      Test areaExamples
      ElectricalContinuity, insulation, circuit characteristics, transformer testing, CCR operation
      FunctionalSwitching, intensity steps, control, monitoring, alarms
      OpticalPhotometry, colour, beam distribution, alignment
      Special visual aidsPAPI, signs, stop bars, runway guard lights
      FinalInspection, punch-list closure, retesting, documentation

      Electrical test asks

      Is the equipment receiving the required electrical condition?

      Photometric test asks

      Is the light producing the required optical performance?

      Both can be necessary. A light can have correct electrical operation and poor photometry from contamination, optics, alignment or light-source degradation, and an abnormal photometric result can originate upstream of the fixture.
      ICAO vs FAA vs India

      ICAO and FAA, and ICAO in India

      ICAO

      Centred on Annex 14, the ICAO aerodrome design manuals, ICAO operational guidance and State implementation: international SARPs and supporting design guidance.

      FAA

      Multiple dedicated advisory circulars and equipment specifications covering fixtures, CCRs, isolation transformers, PAPI, light bases, signs, ALCMS and maintenance, for U.S. airport installations.

      The principles are often closely related, but dimensions, tolerances, intensities, colour criteria, equipment classifications and test procedures may differ.

      Do not write

      "ICAO requires this exact PAPI spacing," unless the number has been verified against the applicable ICAO provision.

      Write instead

      "The PAPI spacing shall comply with the applicable current ICAO and State requirements and the approved project design," and cite the exact provision where a numerical statement is needed.

      Indian airports

      ICAO
      DGCA and national requirements
      AAI and airport requirements
      Approved project design
      Equipment specification
      Installation and maintenance procedures

      National regulations may incorporate or modify international requirements for local implementation, so an Indian airport compliance document should not cite Annex 14 in isolation. India's Ministry of Civil Aviation reported 2025 amendments to CAR Section 4, Series B, Part I (Aerodrome Design and Operations).

      Common ICAO Compliance Mistakes

      1

      Using an outdated Annex 14 edition

      Always check amendments and corrigenda.

      2

      Mixing FAA and ICAO requirements

      An FAA value is not automatically an ICAO value.

      3

      Using a generic PAPI distance

      PAPI siting is a geometry problem, not a fixed-distance rule.

      4

      Checking only that lights are ON

      Functional operation does not prove photometric compliance.

      5

      Ignoring the electrical system

      A visual deficiency can start in the CCR, transformer, cable or connector.

      6

      Ignoring national implementation

      ICAO provisions are implemented through the State's regulatory system.

      7

      Publishing unverified numerical values

      Tie exact requirements to the controlled source.

      8

      Ignoring maintenance

      Compliance is not only commissioning; the installation must stay serviceable.

      FAQ

      ICAO Aerodrome Lighting FAQ

      Q1What is ICAO Annex 14?

      The international framework for aerodrome design and operations. Volume I covers aerodromes and includes requirements for visual aids such as aerodrome lighting.

      Q2Which ICAO document covers aerodrome lighting?

      The principal regulatory reference is ICAO Annex 14, Volume I. Detailed visual-aid design information is in the Aerodrome Design Manual, Part 4 — Visual Aids (Doc 9157 Part 4).

      Q3What runway lights are covered by ICAO requirements?

      Depending on the runway configuration: edge lights, threshold lights, runway end lights, centreline lights, touchdown zone lights and approach lighting.

      Q4What taxiway lights are covered by ICAO?

      Taxiway edge lights, centreline lights, stop bars, runway guard lights and associated visual guidance systems.

      Q5Does ICAO specify PAPI requirements?

      Yes. PAPI is part of the aerodrome visual-aid framework, and its siting, approach-slope geometry and performance must meet the applicable provisions.

      Q6Does ICAO specify one universal PAPI angle?

      PAPI geometry follows the applicable design and operational requirements. Nominal approach slope and individual unit settings should not be reduced to an arbitrary universal field setting.

      Q7Does ICAO specify one universal PAPI distance from the runway?

      No. PAPI location comes from the approved geometric design, including approach slope, elevations, threshold geometry and aircraft and operational considerations.

      Q8Does ICAO require runway lighting photometry?

      Photometric performance is part of the technical evaluation of visual aids where applicable, and ICAO guidance addresses measurement and maintenance of visual-aid characteristics. Take the exact procedure and acceptance criteria from the applicable controlled documents.

      Q9What is the difference between ICAO and FAA airport lighting standards?

      ICAO sets international SARPs. The FAA uses its own U.S. regulatory and advisory framework with dedicated equipment specifications. The principles are similar, but numerical requirements and equipment classifications can differ.

      Q10Can FAA requirements be used on an ICAO airport?

      Only when the project or competent authority specifically adopts them. Do not present FAA requirements as ICAO requirements without verification.

      Q11What standards apply to airport lighting in India?

      Consider the applicable DGCA requirements, the ICAO framework, AAI or airport-specific specifications and approved project documentation. DGCA's CAR Section 4, Series B, Part I is a key Indian aerodrome reference.

      Q12What is ICAO's current Annex 14 Volume I edition?

      ICAO's store lists the 9th Edition, July 2022, with Amendment 18 dated 4 August 2025 and corrigenda including No. 2 dated 18 March 2026 and No. 3 dated 8 June 2026. Always check the controlled copy before issuing a compliance statement.

      Q13What is ICAO Doc 9157 Part 4?

      The Aerodrome Design Manual, Part 4 — Visual Aids, which gives design information on airport visual aids, including approach lighting, runway and taxiway lighting and guidance signs.

      Q14What is the role of ICAO Doc 9157 Part 5?

      Part 5 addresses electrical systems and complements the visual-aid guidance in Part 4.

      Q15Does ICAO cover AGL maintenance?

      Yes. The ICAO aerodrome framework addresses maintenance and serviceability of visual aids, with supporting guidance for preventive maintenance and operational readiness.

      Q16Why shouldn't ICAO and FAA numbers be mixed?

      A value from an FAA advisory circular is not automatically an ICAO requirement. Cite the exact verified provision, or state that the design must comply with the applicable current ICAO and State requirements.
