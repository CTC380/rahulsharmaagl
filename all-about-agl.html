01 · Definition

What Is Airfield Ground Lighting?

Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) is the integrated system of visual aids, lighting equipment, electrical infrastructure, control systems and associated equipment used to provide visual guidance to aircraft during approach, landing, takeoff and movement on the aerodrome surface.

AGL is not simply a collection of lights. It is an engineered system involving light fixtures, electrical power, series circuits, Constant Current Regulators, isolating transformers, primary cables, secondary connections, connectors, control systems, monitoring, visual aids, signs, photometry, inspection and maintenance — all working together as one chain.

02 · Purpose

Why AGL Is Important

AGL has one primary purpose: to provide reliable visual guidance to pilots.

Approach Guidance

Helps establish visual reference during approach.

Landing Guidance

Provides runway threshold, edge, centerline and touchdown-related visual information.

Taxi Guidance

Helps pilots identify taxi routes and intersections.

Surface Safety

Provides visual information that supports safe movement on runways, taxiways and other movement areas.

The objective of AGL is not brightness alone. It is correct visual information, in the correct location, with the correct colour, intensity, distribution and reliability.

06 · Engineering

The AGL Electrical System

Unlike a conventional parallel lighting installation, many airport lighting circuits use series-circuit architecture, in which the lighting load is connected through isolating transformers and supplied from a Constant Current Regulator.

CCR
PRIMARY CABLE
ISOLATING TRANSFORMER
SECONDARY CONNECTOR
FIXTURE
EquipmentMain Function
CCRMaintains controlled circuit current
Primary CableCarries the series-circuit supply
Isolating TransformerProvides electrical isolation and transfers energy to the fixture
Secondary CableConnects transformer to fixture
ConnectorProvides field connection
FixtureProduces the required visual signal
Series Circuit Loop — One Loop, Every Fixture Together

The CCR drives a constant current around the whole loop — every isolating transformer on it taps the same current

Because the current is fixed and shared by the whole loop, a fault (open circuit or failed transformer) is detected as a loop-level voltage change, not as a single identified fixture — further sectionalised testing pinpoints the exact location.

09 · Photometry

AGL Photometry

AGL photometry is the measurement and evaluation of the optical performance of airfield lighting equipment or installed lighting systems against applicable requirements.

Intensity

How much light is produced in the required direction.

Distribution

Where the light is directed and how it is distributed.

Colour

Whether the emitted light falls within the applicable chromaticity requirements.

Alignment

Whether the optical system is correctly oriented.

What a Photometric Measurement Looks At

Intensity, colour and alignment are measured against the beam's design axis; distribution is mapped across the full beam pattern

Why Photometry Matters

A light can appear bright and still fail a photometric requirement.

14 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About AGL

Quick answers about airfield ground lighting, from components and series circuits to photometry, standards and maintenance.

Q1What is Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL)?

Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) is the integrated system of visual aids, lighting equipment, electrical infrastructure, control systems and associated equipment that gives aircraft visual guidance during approach, landing, takeoff and movement on the aerodrome surface. It is an engineered system, not just a collection of lights: fixtures, series circuits, Constant Current Regulators, isolating transformers, cables, connectors, control, monitoring, photometry, inspection and maintenance all work together as one chain.

Q2Why is AGL important for airport safety?

AGL exists to give pilots reliable visual guidance for approach, landing, taxiing and surface movement. The goal is not brightness alone but correct visual information in the correct location, with the correct colour, intensity, distribution and reliability.

Q3What are the main components of an AGL system?

The main components are the lighting systems (approach, runway and taxiway lights, stop bars, runway guard lights and PAPI), the electrical system (CCR, series circuit, isolating transformers, primary and secondary cables), connection systems (L-823 plugs, receptacles and connector kits), airfield signs, control and monitoring systems, and the testing regime that keeps everything compliant.

Q4Where is airfield ground lighting used on an airport?

AGL is used across four zones an aircraft passes through on every arrival: the approach area (approach lighting and PAPI), the runway (edge, threshold, end, centreline and touchdown zone lighting), the taxiway (edge and centreline lights, stop bars and runway guard lights) and the apron or manoeuvring areas (stand guidance, signs and related visual aids).

Q5What is a Constant Current Regulator (CCR) and why do AGL circuits use it?

A Constant Current Regulator (CCR) is the power source of an AGL series circuit. It maintains controlled circuit current around the whole loop, so every isolating transformer on that loop receives the same current. Many airport lighting circuits use this series architecture instead of a conventional parallel installation.

Q6What does an isolating transformer do in an AGL circuit?

An isolating transformer provides electrical isolation between the primary series circuit and the fixture, and transfers energy to that fixture through the secondary cable and connector. This is what lets each light run from the shared series-circuit current.

Q7How is AGL brightness controlled?

Brightness is set by selecting an intensity step at the CCR from the control tower or lighting control room. Multiple steps exist to suit night and day operations, reduced visibility, pilot visibility, environmental conditions and operational requirements. The actual number of steps and the current at each step depend on the applicable CCR system, design and specification.

Q8What is AGL photometry and why does it matter?

AGL photometry is the measurement and evaluation of the optical performance of airfield lighting against applicable requirements. It checks intensity, distribution, colour and alignment. It matters because a light can look bright and still fail a photometric requirement.

Q9Which standards govern airfield ground lighting?

Commonly applied references include ICAO Annex 14 (Aerodromes), FAA Advisory Circular 150/5340 and related specifications, relevant Indian Standards, applicable IEC electrical and equipment standards, and DGCA and AAI requirements in India. Always verify against the latest edition of the governing standard, the project specification and the equipment-specific requirements.

Q10What are the most common AGL faults and where do they usually originate?

A single light off usually points to the fixture, secondary cable, transformer or connector. Multiple lights off points to the series circuit, primary cable, CCR or a circuit section. An entire circuit off suggests the CCR, an earth fault, an open or short circuit, or a protection trip. Low intensity, flickering and high leakage each have their own likely areas, such as CCR output, loose connections or moisture in cable insulation.

Q11How is an AGL system tested and maintained?

Testing and maintenance cover four areas: electrical (continuity, insulation resistance, circuit current, voltage, transformer testing and earth-fault investigation), photometric (intensity, distribution, colour and alignment), mechanical (fixture condition, housing, mounting, sealing and connectors) and operational (CCR operation, intensity steps, control, monitoring and fault indications).

Q12How can I find a faulty light on an AGL series circuit?

Because the current is fixed and shared by the whole loop, a fault such as an open circuit or failed transformer shows up as a loop-level voltage change, not as a single identified fixture. Further sectionalised testing is then used to pinpoint the exact location.
