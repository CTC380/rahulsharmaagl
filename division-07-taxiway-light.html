TWY-221 Taxiway Edge Light Not Working

Symptom — A taxiway edge light (or a defined group of them) is not illuminating when its series circuit is energised, reported via pilot/vehicle report, ALCMS status, or routine night inspection.

Equipment / System

Taxiway Edge Lights (TWY) — elevated, blue L-861 (or inset L-852 on some sections) fixtures on frangible couplings, fed via individual isolation transformers off a dedicated taxiway series circuit, base cans (L-867) set back from the taxiway edge, primary series cable loop back to the CCR/TCR.

Possible Causes
HighIsolation transformer failure at the fixture (open primary/secondary winding)
HighLamp/LED source failure inside the fixture
MediumSeries circuit primary cable fault or bad connection between fixtures on the same taxiway edge run
MediumBase can or L-823 connector failure — moisture ingress, corroded pin, or plug not fully seated
LowTaxiway CCR at reduced/zero output — check whether other edge lights on the same circuit are also affected
LowElevated fixture physically knocked off its frangible coupling by a vehicle, aircraft wingtip, or ground-support equipment strike
Required Tools
Clamp-on series circuit ammeterTrue-RMS multimeter (CAT III)1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Series circuit fault locator / TDRTaxiway as-built circuit and base-can layout drawingBase-can lifting tool and PPE for airside work
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate all airside access through ATC/airfield operations and obtain a taxiway closure or inspection slot before approaching the light.
  • Treat the series circuit as energised until confirmed isolated at the CCR/TCR and locked out.
  • Use FOD-safe tools and account for every item before leaving the taxiway edge strip.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCheck the ALCMS/CCR panel to see whether this is a single light or part of a wider dark section, and confirm actual current output of the affected taxiway series circuit.
  2. ObservationConfirm with a taxiway inspection whether only the reported fixture is affected or a run of lights beyond it is also out — a single dark light with the rest of the run lit points to that fixture; a dark run points to the loop upstream of it.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated and LOTO applied, disconnect the fixture's L-823 plug and megger the isolation transformer primary and secondary windings.
  4. MeasurementInspect the base can for water, debris, or a displaced/damaged connector, and check the fixture's frangible coupling and lamp/LED module for visible failure.
  5. DecisionTransformer or lamp confirmed faulty at this fixture only → isolated single-fixture fault, proceed under TWY-222 or TWY-235 if physical damage is found. Fault traces onto the primary loop or affects multiple fixtures → treat as a circuit-level fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the fixture (transformer, lamp, connector, frangible coupling) or to the series cable loop feeding it.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific failed component before replacement rather than swapping parts speculatively.
Test Points
TWY fixture isolation transformer (primary/secondary)
L-823 secondary connector and base can
Series cable loop, both directions from the fixture
CCR/TCR output current at the panel
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-energise the circuit, confirm the fixture lights at all intensity steps, and inspect the full taxiway edge run at night to confirm no adjacent lights were disturbed during access.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed isolation transformer or lamp/LED module.
  • Reseat or replace a corroded/damaged L-823 connector.
  • Repair the identified series cable fault.
  • Restore CCR/TCR output if the root cause was upstream rather than the fixture.
Preventive Action
  • Include taxiway edge light and connector inspection in the routine night flight-check and periodic maintenance cycle.
  • Megger the series circuit periodically to catch developing insulation faults before they cause an outage.
  • Maintain an accurate base-can and circuit layout drawing to speed fault location.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TWY-01 (Taxiway Light Fault Isolation Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30, FAA AC 150/5345-46 (L-861)

TWY-222 Multiple Taxiway Lights Failure

Symptom — A run of two or more adjacent taxiway edge lights is dark or malfunctioning together, while the circuit remains energised and other sections of the same taxiway are lit normally.

Equipment / System

Taxiway series circuit primary cable loop and the group of fixtures fed by the affected section.

Possible Causes
HighPrimary series cable fault (cut, chafed, or moisture-degraded insulation) between the last working fixture and the first dark one
HighA single failed isolation transformer developing a short circuit, collapsing available voltage to downstream fixtures on the same loop
MediumDamaged or crushed duct/cable section from recent excavation, verge works, or heavy vehicle loading along the taxiway shoulder
MediumWater ingress into a cable joint or pull box along the affected section
LowRodent or FOD damage to buried cable in an unpaved verge section
LowA section isolator or field-splice left open after previous maintenance
Required Tools
Series circuit fault locator / TDR1000V/2500V insulation resistance testerClamp-on ammeterCable route and joint location drawingExcavation permit and utility locate request if digging is required
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the CCR/TCR before any cable work.
  • Obtain excavation clearance and utility locates before opening any pavement or verge if the fault requires digging.
  • Coordinate with ATC for any extended taxiway occupancy needed to trace the fault.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the exact extent of the dark section from a taxiway inspection and cross-reference against the circuit layout drawing to identify the affected cable segment(s).
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the primary series cable in segments from accessible pull boxes or transformer base cans, working from each end of the affected section toward the middle.
  3. MeasurementUse a TDR or fault locator referenced to the cable's known length to estimate fault distance if a direct segment test is inconclusive.
  4. DecisionInsulation resistance fails on a specific segment → cable/joint fault in that segment. All segments megger clear but a specific transformer reads shorted → that transformer is loading down the loop.
  5. Fault LocationPhysically locate and expose the faulted cable segment or confirm the single shorted transformer.
  6. Root CauseConfirm cause of the cable fault (mechanical damage, water tracking, age-related insulation breakdown) once exposed.
Test Points
Primary cable insulation resistance, segment by segment
Suspect transformer primary-to-secondary and to-earth
Pull box/joint condition along the affected run
CCR/TCR output current before and during isolation of suspect segment
Root Cause Verification

After repair, megger the full affected loop end-to-end, re-energise, and confirm all fixtures in the previously dark section illuminate correctly across all intensity steps.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the faulted cable segment and joint.
  • Replace the shorted isolation transformer found to be loading the loop.
  • Restore any open section isolator/splice left from prior work.
  • Reinstate duct/cable protection if mechanical damage was the cause.
Preventive Action
  • Include periodic full-loop insulation resistance testing in the PM programme, trending results to catch degrading cable before failure.
  • Maintain accurate as-built records of every joint, pull box, and repair splice.
  • Coordinate with civil/grounds teams before any excavation or verge work near known cable routes.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TWY-02 (Series Circuit Fault Location Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364 (insulation testing), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

TWY-223 Taxiway Light Dim

Symptom — A taxiway edge light illuminates but appears noticeably dimmer than adjacent fixtures at the same commanded intensity step.

Equipment / System

Taxiway fixture optical/lamp assembly, isolation transformer secondary output, and lens condition.

Possible Causes
HighLens heavily contaminated with dirt, dust, or grass/mud splash from adjacent unpaved verge areas
HighIsolation transformer secondary output below rated value due to partial internal fault or incorrect tap
MediumLamp/LED module degraded or nearing end of life without having fully failed
MediumIncorrect or mismatched transformer rating installed at a previous replacement
LowSeries circuit current below the correct value for the commanded step, affecting the whole loop rather than one fixture
LowLens misaligned so peak beam intensity is directed away from the design sightline
Required Tools
Light output/luminance meterTrue-RMS multimeterClamp-on ammeterLens cleaning kitIsolation transformer test set
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before removing or cleaning a lens or accessing the fixture interior.
  • Allow the fixture to cool before handling if recently energised at a high intensity step.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompare the reported fixture's apparent brightness against neighbouring fixtures at the same commanded step, ideally with a luminance meter.
  2. MeasurementConfirm the series circuit current at the CCR/TCR matches the correct value for the commanded intensity step, to rule out a circuit-wide cause.
  3. ObservationInspect the lens for contamination, mud/dust splash, or degradation, and clean if required.
  4. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, check the isolation transformer secondary output and confirm its rated wattage/tap matches the design.
  5. DecisionCircuit current correct and lens clean but output still low → transformer or lamp/LED degradation. Lens found heavily contaminated → cleaning likely resolves it; re-check after cleaning before replacing parts.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to lens contamination, transformer output, or lamp/LED degradation.
  7. Root CauseConfirm with a before/after luminance reading once the identified cause is addressed.
Test Points
Fixture luminance output vs. neighbouring fixtures
Series circuit current at CCR/TCR
Isolation transformer secondary voltage/wattage
Lens transmission condition
Root Cause Verification

Re-measure luminance output after corrective action and compare against neighbouring fixtures and the manufacturer's rated output for the commanded step.

Corrective Action
  • Clean the lens and prism assembly.
  • Replace an isolation transformer with an incorrect or degraded rating.
  • Replace a degraded lamp/LED module.
  • Correct any lens misalignment found during inspection.
Preventive Action
  • Include lens cleaning in the routine taxiway lighting maintenance cycle, especially on sections adjacent to unpaved shoulders or during monsoon/mud season.
  • Verify transformer rating matches the specified lamp/LED module at every replacement.
  • Track luminance readings over time to catch gradual degradation before it becomes a reported fault.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TWY-03 (Lens Cleaning and Inspection Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I (light intensity requirements), FAA AC 150/5340-30

TWY-224 Taxiway Light Flickering

Symptom — One or more taxiway edge lights flicker, pulse, or vary in brightness instead of holding a steady output at the commanded intensity step.

Equipment / System

Taxiway fixture, isolation transformer, connector, and the series circuit supplying it.

Possible Causes
HighLoose or intermittent connection at the L-823 connector, worsened by vibration from adjacent taxiing aircraft or vehicle traffic
HighA partially failed or overheating isolation transformer with an intermittent internal short
MediumLamp/LED driver instability or a failing LED module nearing end of life
MediumIntermittent cable fault (chafed insulation making and breaking contact) rather than a solid open or short
LowCCR/TCR output instability itself rather than a fixture-level fault
LowMoisture inside the base can causing an intermittent leakage path
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeter with min/max holdOscilloscope or power quality logger for waveform captureThermal imaging cameraL-823 connector test adaptor
Safety Precautions
  • Treat intermittent faults as live until proven otherwise.
  • LOTO the circuit before opening any base can even if the light appears steady at the time of access.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm whether the flicker is on a single fixture or several, and whether it correlates with a particular intensity step, aircraft/vehicle traffic pattern, or weather condition.
  2. MeasurementLog CCR/TCR output current over a period spanning the reported flicker to rule out a source-side instability.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, inspect and re-torque the L-823 connector, and megger the isolation transformer under light mechanical flexing to try to reproduce an intermittent fault.
  4. ObservationUse thermal imaging on the connector and transformer during a live period to spot a hot connection consistent with intermittent high resistance.
  5. DecisionFlicker follows CCR/TCR current logging → source-side issue, route to Division 01. Flicker isolated to the fixture and reproducible by flexing the connector → connector or transformer fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the connector, transformer, lamp/LED module, or cable.
  7. Root CauseConfirm by replacing the suspect component and re-testing under conditions that previously produced the flicker.
Test Points
L-823 connector contact resistance
Isolation transformer under mechanical flex
CCR/TCR output current trend log
Lamp/LED module bench test
Root Cause Verification

Monitor the repaired fixture over several operating cycles, including varying intensity steps, before closing out.

Corrective Action
  • Clean, re-grease, and properly seat the L-823 connector.
  • Replace a transformer confirmed to have an intermittent internal fault.
  • Replace a failing lamp/LED module.
  • Repair the identified intermittent cable fault.
Preventive Action
  • Torque-check connectors as a standard step in periodic maintenance.
  • Include thermal imaging of connectors and transformers in the periodic PM cycle.
  • Trend CCR/TCR output logs to distinguish source-side from fixture-side intermittent issues early.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TWY-01 (Taxiway Light Fault Isolation Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-46 (L-861), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

TWY-225 Taxiway Light Intermittent

Symptom — A taxiway edge light or a circuit segment exhibits an intermittent fault — failing and recovering unpredictably — without a fault being reliably reproducible during a single inspection, distinct from a steady flicker.

Equipment / System

Taxiway series circuit as a whole, spanning cable, connectors, transformers, and fixture, where the fault does not present consistently.

Possible Causes
HighA marginal cable insulation fault that only breaks down under specific moisture or thermal conditions
HighA marginal connector connection that opens and closes under vibration or thermal expansion
MediumA transformer with an intermittent internal fault that only manifests under certain load or temperature conditions
MediumA CCR/TCR-side issue (protection relay marginally set, or a control fault) that intermittently affects this circuit — verify against Division 01 before assuming a field-side cause
LowEnvironmental correlation (rain, temperature swing, specific traffic patterns) not yet identified because the pattern has not been systematically logged
Required Tools
Data logger for trend/repeated-event analysis1000V/2500V insulation resistance testerSeries circuit fault locator / TDRCCR/TCR alarm/event log export toolWeather/environmental log for correlation
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before any physical inspection, even though the fault is intermittent and the circuit may appear to be operating normally at the time of access.
  • Coordinate extended monitoring periods with ATC/airfield operations if data logging equipment needs to remain connected across multiple shifts.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationSystematically log every reported occurrence — date, time, weather, and which fixtures/sections were affected — to identify a pattern before attempting physical fault-finding.
  2. ObservationReview the CCR/TCR/ALCMS alarm and event log for any correlation with protection trips, voltage dips, or other circuit-level events coinciding with reported occurrences.
  3. MeasurementInstall a data logger on the circuit to capture current, and if possible individual fixture status, across a period likely to include a recurrence.
  4. DecisionOccurrences correlate with a specific condition (rain, temperature, time of day) → target inspection and testing under that condition. No clear correlation → proceed to a full systematic megger and connector check of the circuit during the next available access window.
  5. Fault LocationNarrow the intermittent fault to a specific cable segment, connector, or transformer using correlation data and, where possible, testing that reproduces the fault condition.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the specific component once identified by isolating and testing it independently, ideally under the condition that previously triggered the fault.
Test Points
CCR/TCR/ALCMS alarm and event log
Circuit current trend log across a monitoring period
Suspect connectors under mechanical flex
Suspect cable segments under simulated wetting, if safe and appropriate
Root Cause Verification

After the suspected component is repaired or replaced, continue monitoring across at least one full cycle of the previously identified correlating condition before closing out.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the specific component identified through correlation-based testing.
  • Escalate to CCR/TCR-level investigation if the intermittency is confirmed to originate at the source rather than in the field.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a structured fault log for all taxiway circuits to make future intermittent-fault correlation faster.
  • Include a periodic full-circuit megger test as standard, since it is often the only way to catch a marginal insulation fault before it becomes a reported outage.
  • Review connector torque and transformer condition preventively on high-traffic taxiway sections.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TWY-01 (Taxiway Light Fault Isolation Procedure), SOP-TWY-02 (Series Circuit Fault Location Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, IEC 60364

TWY-226 Taxiway Light Incorrect Color

Symptom — A taxiway edge light shows a colour other than the specified blue (or, for taxiway centreline/exit lights on the same circuit, other than the specified green/amber) — for example, a discoloured, whitish, or clearly mismatched output compared to neighbouring fixtures.

Equipment / System

Taxiway fixture lens/filter and lamp/LED module colour characteristics.

Possible Causes
HighLens discolouration/fading from UV degradation or heat exposure over the fixture's service life, most noticeable on blue lenses which fade toward white over time
HighWrong lamp/LED module fitted — a module of a different rated colour installed by mistake at a previous replacement
MediumContamination film (dust, dirt) on the lens producing an apparent colour shift
MediumLens cracked or partially melted, altering colour transmission
LowFixture installed in the wrong position relative to a taxiway centreline/exit colour scheme, if this circuit shares infrastructure with centreline lighting
Required Tools
Colour temperature meter (if available)Torch/inspection lightReplacement lens and correct-specification lamp/LED moduleLens cleaning kitTaxiway colour-scheme layout drawing
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before removing the lens or accessing the fixture internals.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the colour discrepancy visually against neighbouring fixtures and against the taxiway colour-scheme layout drawing for that fixture's position.
  2. ObservationInspect the lens for discolouration, contamination, or damage, and clean before further diagnosis if deposits are present.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, confirm the lamp/LED module fitted matches the specified part number and colour for a taxiway edge fixture at this location.
  4. DecisionLens discoloured/contaminated but correct module fitted → clean or replace lens. Lens clear but wrong module or fixture type fitted → replace with correct-specification part.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to lens condition, lamp/LED module colour mismatch, or fixture-position error.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the corrected fixture matches neighbouring fixtures' colour appearance during a night inspection.
Test Points
Lens condition and clarity
Lamp/LED module part number and colour specification
Fixture position vs. taxiway colour-scheme drawing
Comparative colour appearance vs. neighbouring fixtures
Root Cause Verification

Visually confirm matching blue colour appearance against neighbouring fixtures during a night inspection after correction.

Corrective Action
  • Clean or replace the discoloured/contaminated lens.
  • Replace a mismatched lamp/LED module with the correct specification.
  • Relocate the fixture if it is confirmed to be in the wrong position for the colour scheme.
Preventive Action
  • Include lens colour/clarity checks in the periodic maintenance schedule, given the known UV-fade characteristic of blue lenses.
  • Verify lamp/LED module part numbers against specification before stocking or fitting.
  • Maintain the taxiway colour-scheme layout drawing current.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TWY-03 (Lens Cleaning and Inspection Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I (Table 5-2/5-3, taxiway light colour), FAA AC 150/5340-30

TWY-227 Taxiway Light Wrong Intensity

Symptom — One fixture, a group of fixtures, or an entire taxiway circuit displays a brightness level that does not match the intensity step commanded by the CCR/TCR/ALCMS.

Equipment / System

Taxiway fixture output relative to the commanded CCR/TCR intensity step, and the isolation transformer matching that step.

Possible Causes
HighIsolation transformer of the wrong wattage/tap fitted at this fixture relative to the design
HighCCR/TCR itself outputting the wrong current for the commanded step — check whether the discrepancy affects only this fixture or the whole circuit
MediumLamp/LED module rated for a different current than the series circuit design
MediumRegulator/ALCMS step command not reaching this circuit correctly if only part of the taxiway is affected
LowLens degradation making the light appear dimmer than its actual electrical intensity (cross-check against TWY-223)
Required Tools
Clamp-on ammeterTrue-RMS multimeterLight output/luminance meterTransformer rating reference table
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before inspecting or replacing any transformer.
  • Confirm actual circuit current at the CCR/TCR before concluding the fault is fixture-side.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the CCR/TCR is actually outputting the correct current for the commanded step, using the panel/ALCMS reading cross-checked with a clamp meter if available.
  2. DecisionIf the whole circuit is affected, route to Division 01 (CCR intensity/step control) rather than continuing at fixture level. If only one fixture or a small group is affected, continue below.
  3. ObservationInspect the affected fixture(s) and confirm the isolation transformer wattage/tap and lamp/LED module rating against the design specification for that circuit.
  4. MeasurementCompare luminance output of the affected fixture(s) against neighbouring, correctly-performing fixtures at the same commanded step.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to a mismatched transformer, mismatched lamp/LED module, or (if ruled out at fixture level) a source-side step-control issue.
  6. Root CauseConfirm by replacing the mismatched component with the correct rating and re-measuring output.
Test Points
CCR/TCR output current vs. commanded step
Transformer wattage/tap rating
Lamp/LED module current rating
Comparative luminance vs. neighbouring fixtures
Root Cause Verification

After correction, verify the fixture's output matches neighbouring fixtures across all intensity steps.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the transformer with the correctly rated unit for the circuit design.
  • Replace the lamp/LED module with the correct current rating.
  • Escalate to CCR/TCR-level troubleshooting if the fault is confirmed circuit-wide.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a clear parts specification/rating table at the maintenance store to prevent incorrect spares being fitted.
  • Verify transformer and lamp ratings as a standard check whenever a fixture component is replaced.
  • Include a step-by-step intensity comparison in the periodic night inspection.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TWY-01 (Taxiway Light Fault Isolation Procedure), SOP-CCR-08 (Intensity Step Verification)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5345-46

TWY-228 Taxiway Light Broken Lens

Symptom — The fixture lens is found cracked, chipped, shattered, or missing during inspection, typically on an elevated fixture exposed to vehicle, aircraft, or ground-equipment contact.

Equipment / System

Taxiway elevated fixture lens/globe assembly and its gasket interface with the fixture body.

Possible Causes
HighImpact damage from a vehicle, tug, baggage cart, or other ground-support equipment striking the elevated fixture
HighAircraft wingtip or engine/propeller contact with an elevated fixture positioned too close to the taxiway edge or on a tight turn radius
MediumSnow plough or sweeper blade contact during winter operations
MediumThermal shock cracking from rapid temperature change
LowUV degradation and material fatigue leading to cracking under normal vibration loading over the lens service life
Required Tools
Visual inspection kit / torchReplacement lens and gasket, correct part numberTorque wrench for lens retaining fixingsFOD sweep equipment
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before removing a damaged lens.
  • Handle broken glass/polycarbonate lens fragments carefully and ensure a full FOD sweep of the surrounding pavement before returning the area to service.
  • If lens damage indicates the fixture may have been struck by an aircraft, report immediately per the site's aircraft-ground-incident procedure.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationDocument the type and extent of lens damage and photograph for the maintenance record, and conduct an immediate FOD sweep of the surrounding pavement.
  2. ObservationCheck whether the fixture body, frangible coupling, or mounting has also been damaged in the same event (cross-reference TWY-235).
  3. MeasurementConfirm the replacement lens is the correct part number, colour, and specification for this fixture type before fitting.
  4. DecisionLens damage only, fixture body and coupling intact → straightforward lens replacement. Fixture body or coupling also damaged → treat as a combined fixture repair under TWY-235.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the lens itself as the fault, distinct from any underlying fixture body or electrical issue.
  6. Root CauseWhere damage is impact-related, note the likely cause (vehicle, aircraft, snow equipment) for reporting and, if a pattern, for airside operational follow-up on clearance or routing.
Test Points
Lens damage extent and location
Fixture body and frangible coupling condition
Surrounding pavement FOD sweep result
Replacement lens part number/spec match
Root Cause Verification

After lens replacement, confirm the gasket seal is fully seated, re-torque fixings to specification, and re-test insulation resistance to confirm no residual ingress.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the broken lens with the correct specification part.
  • Replace the gasket at the same time as the lens.
  • Repair any underlying fixture damage found during the inspection.
Preventive Action
  • Include lens condition in the routine visual inspection cycle.
  • Report recurring impact damage patterns to airfield operations for possible operational, clearance, or FOD-control follow-up.
  • Review fixture placement/offset on tight-radius taxiway turns if repeated aircraft-contact damage is observed.
Related References

SOP: SOP-TWY-04 (Fixture and Lens Replacement Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-46 (L-861), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

Rev 0 — Initial issue
TWY-229 Taxiway Light Water Ingress

Symptom — Water is found inside the fixture housing or base can during inspection or maintenance, entering from an external source such as rainfall, surface runoff, or a failed seal — distinct from internal condensation (TWY-230).

Equipment / System

Taxiway fixture housing, gasket/seal system, base can, and its interface with the surrounding pavement or verge.

Possible Causes
HighFixture housing gasket degraded, cracked, or incorrectly seated, allowing rainfall or splash to enter
HighBase can not properly sealed to the surrounding duct/pavement or verge surface, allowing groundwater or surface runoff to collect
MediumFixture mounting bolts loose, breaking the seal between the housing and base
MediumPavement or verge settlement/cracking around the fixture allowing surface water to track directly into the base can
LowBase can drainage blocked, causing water to pool and rise above the acceptable level inside the can
LowElevated fixture coupling seal displaced after a minor impact, without full frangible activation
Required Tools
Insulation resistance tester (1000V/2500V)Wet/dry vacuum for base-can pump-outReplacement gaskets and sealsTorque wrench for housing/coupling fixing bolts
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before opening a fixture or base can with standing water.
  • Do not assume a wet fixture is de-energised; test with a rated meter before hand contact.
  • Dispose of pumped-out water per site environmental procedure if contamination is suspected.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the extent of water ingress and note whether any electrical fault (dim, dark, flickering) is already present.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the fixture and transformer to confirm whether insulation has already been compromised.
  3. ObservationInspect the housing gasket, mounting seal, and base-can seal/drainage for the specific external ingress path.
  4. DecisionInsulation resistance already low → treat as an active electrical fault, replace affected components before re-energising. Insulation resistance still acceptable → address the sealing issue and monitor.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the specific ingress path — housing gasket, mounting seal, base-can seal, or drainage blockage.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the fixture and base can are fully dry and re-test insulation resistance before closing out.
Test Points
Insulation resistance, fixture to earth
Insulation resistance, transformer to earth
Housing gasket condition
Base-can drainage function
Mounting/coupling seal integrity
Root Cause Verification

Re-test insulation resistance after drying and resealing, and re-inspect the fixture after the next significant rainfall to confirm the ingress path has been fully resolved.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the degraded housing gasket.
  • Clear or restore base-can drainage.
  • Replace the damaged mounting/coupling seal.
  • Torque the housing fixing bolts to specification.
  • Pump out and dry the base can fully before closing up.
Preventive Action
  • Include gasket and seal condition in the periodic maintenance checklist, replacing proactively rather than only on failure.
  • Check and clear base-can drainage as part of routine maintenance, especially before the wet season.
  • Track fixtures with a history of water ingress for targeted remediation (e.g., verge grading or drainage correction).

  3. ObservationInspect the base can weep hole/drainage outlet for blockage, and assess the surrounding pavement/verge grading for a runoff pattern directing water toward the fixture.
  4. DecisionBlockage found at the drainage outlet → clear and monitor. Grading or sub-surface drainage deficiency identified → escalate to civil/grounds works for a wider correction beyond simple fixture maintenance.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to a localised blockage versus a wider grading/drainage design issue affecting the section.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the base can remains dry after the next significant rainfall following corrective action.
Test Points
Insulation resistance, fixture and transformer to earth
Base-can weep hole/drainage outlet condition
Surrounding pavement/verge grading
Sub-surface drainage connection condition, if fitted
Root Cause Verification

Re-inspect the base can after the next significant rainfall to confirm it drains correctly and does not return to a persistent standing-water condition.

Corrective Action
  • Clear the blocked drainage outlet or weep hole.
  • Coordinate re-grading of the surrounding pavement/verge if runoff is being directed toward the fixture.
  • Repair or reconnect sub-surface drainage if applicable.
  • Pump out and dry the base can as an immediate measure while the underlying cause is addressed.
Preventive Action
  • Include base-can drainage checks in the periodic maintenance schedule, particularly before the wet season.
  • Coordinate with civil/grounds teams on drainage design whenever taxiway resurfacing or verge work is planned near fixture locations.
  • Track fixtures with recurring drainage problems for a section-wide drainage review.

Case Study: Division 25 — Field Case Study Troubleshooting

Rev 0 — Initial issue
TWY-240 Taxiway Light Repeated Failure

Symptom — A specific taxiway fixture or fixture position has failed and been repaired multiple times over a relatively short period, indicating an underlying systemic cause rather than an isolated component fault.

Equipment / System

The full fixture, base can, transformer, cable, and site-condition history at a specific taxiway light position.

Possible Causes
HighAn unresolved base-can drainage or water-ingress issue causing repeated electrical failures at the same location (cross-reference TWY-229/239)
HighA recurring vehicle/aircraft contact pattern at this position causing repeated mechanical damage (cross-reference TWY-235)
MediumA marginal or degraded cable segment feeding this fixture that has not yet been fully replaced, causing repeated downstream symptoms
MediumIncorrect component specification (transformer wattage, connector type) being repeatedly reinstalled without addressing the underlying mismatch
LowA design issue specific to this position — for example, an exposed location, poor drainage grading, or a tight turn radius — that no single repair can resolve without a design-level change
Required Tools
Maintenance history/fault log for this fixture positionFull diagnostic kit as required by the suspected fault type (megger, ammeter, TDR)Site drainage and civil layout drawingsPhotographic record from previous repair visits
Safety Precautions
  • Follow the safety precautions applicable to whichever specific fault type is found to be recurring (electrical isolation, FOD, structural).
  • Treat a repeated-failure investigation as an opportunity to look beyond the immediate symptom — do not simply repeat the same fix without reviewing the history.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationPull the full maintenance history for this fixture position and identify whether the repeated failures share a common fault type (electrical, mechanical, drainage) or vary.
  2. ObservationReview site conditions specific to this position — drainage grading, proximity to vehicle/aircraft paths, pavement condition — that may not be apparent from a single repair visit.
  3. MeasurementConduct a full diagnostic sweep (megger, connector inspection, transformer test, physical inspection) rather than only addressing the most recently reported symptom.
  4. DecisionA common root cause is identified across the failure history (e.g., persistent drainage issue) → address that root cause directly rather than repeating the same component-level fix. No common cause found → treat as coincidental unrelated failures and continue standard fault response.
  5. Fault LocationIdentify the systemic factor — site condition, component specification, or operational pattern — driving the repeated failures.
  6. Root CauseConfirm resolution by tracking this fixture position through at least one full seasonal cycle without recurrence.
Test Points
Full maintenance/fault history review
Site-specific condition assessment (drainage, traffic pattern, pavement)
Component specification verification against design
Post-repair monitoring over an extended period
Root Cause Verification

Track this fixture position specifically over an extended period (at least one seasonal cycle) after the systemic fix, rather than closing out after a single successful repair, to confirm the underlying cause has genuinely been resolved.

Corrective Action
  • Address the identified systemic root cause (drainage correction, specification correction, positional/design review) rather than only replacing the failed component again.
  • Coordinate with civil, design, or airfield operations teams if the root cause falls outside routine electrical maintenance scope.
  • Document the resolution and root cause clearly for future reference.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a structured, searchable fault history by fixture position to make repeated-failure patterns visible early rather than only after several cycles.
  • Review any fixture position with more than two failures in a defined period as a standard trigger for a root-cause investigation rather than routine repair.
  • Feed recurring positional issues back into design/specification review for future taxiway lighting projects.
