Fixture Requirements & Verification Comparison

PHOTOMETRIC REQUIREMENT VS ELECTRICAL REQUIREMENT

Two requirement categories that every AGL fixture must satisfy, but which answer completely different questions — photometric requirements define how much and where an AGL light must emit light, while electrical requirements define how the equipment is powered, controlled, insulated, and electrically protected. In short: photometric = light output, electrical = electrical performance. Passing one says nothing about the other.

03 · Basic Difference

Photometric vs Electrical Requirements

Photometric Requirements

A photometric requirement specifies the optical performance of an AGL fixture. Typical parameters include:

  • Luminous intensity / candela
  • Intensity distribution (horizontal and vertical)
  • Beam spread and angular coverage
  • Colour
  • Minimum/maximum intensity and uniformity
  • Cut-off characteristics

For example: "The fixture shall provide the specified minimum luminous intensity throughout the required angular range." That's a photometric requirement.

Electrical Requirements

Electrical requirements describe the equipment's electrical behavior and safety. Typical parameters include:

  • Rated voltage, current, and frequency
  • Power consumption and power factor
  • Insulation resistance and dielectric withstand
  • Leakage current and earthing
  • Surge and overcurrent protection
  • Short-circuit capability and electrical isolation

For example: "The fixture shall operate correctly at the specified series-circuit current." That's an electrical requirement.

06 · Critical Distinction

Electrical Compliance Does Not Mean Photometric Compliance

A fixture can pass its electrical tests but still fail photometry.

Voltage/current       ✓
Insulation            ✓
Dielectric withstand  ✓
Functional operation  ✓
                       ↓
Photometry             ✗

Possible reasons include:

  • Incorrect LED output
  • Optical degradation
  • Wrong lens
  • Poor optical alignment
  • LED aging
  • Manufacturing variation

Electrical pass ≠ photometric pass.

07 · Critical Distinction

Photometric Compliance Does Not Mean Electrical Compliance

The reverse is also possible — a fixture can produce the correct light distribution while having an electrical problem.

Photometric pass ≠ electrical pass. Both need to be verified independently.

09 · Electrical Verification

Electrical Tests

Insulation Resistance

Checks insulation between specified electrical points. Test → IR meter. Result → MΩ.

Dielectric Withstand

Checks insulation capability under elevated test voltage. Test → HV withstand. Result → no breakdown/flashover.

Continuity

Checks that the intended electrical path is continuous. Test → continuity meter. Result → acceptable resistance/path.

Current/Voltage

Checks operating electrical characteristics. Test → current/voltage measurement. Result → within specified limits.

13 · Requirement → Test → Acceptance

How Each Requirement Gets Verified

Photometric

Requirement: Required minimum intensity at specified angles.

Test: Goniophotometric measurement.

Acceptance: Measured intensity meets or exceeds specified limits.

Electrical

Requirement: Required insulation resistance.

Test: Insulation resistance measurement at specified test conditions.

Acceptance: Measured resistance meets the specified minimum.

18 · Engineering Verdict

Photometric Requirement vs Electrical Requirement

Engineering Verdict

Electrical requirement = input, electrical behavior, insulation, protection and safety.

Photometric requirement = light intensity, distribution, colour and angular performance. Electrical test proves electrical compliance, photometric test proves optical compliance, and functional/system test proves operational behavior — three separate proofs, not one.

PhotometricVerifies the light the fixture puts out — intensity, distribution, colour, coverage.
ElectricalVerifies how the fixture handles electricity — voltage, current, insulation, protection.

A proper AGL FAT/SAT dossier should keep electrical, photometric and functional requirements separately identified, even though they interact during actual operation.

19 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fundamental difference between photometric and electrical requirements?

Photometric requirements define how much and where an AGL light must emit light — intensity, distribution, colour, and coverage. Electrical requirements define how the equipment is powered, controlled, insulated, and electrically protected.

Can a fixture pass electrical tests but still fail photometry?

Yes. Voltage, current, insulation, and dielectric withstand can all pass while the fixture still fails photometry due to incorrect LED output, optical degradation, a wrong lens, poor alignment, LED aging, or manufacturing variation.

Can a fixture pass photometry but fail electrically?

Yes. The reverse is also possible — correct candela distribution, colour, and beam spread do not guarantee acceptable insulation resistance or other electrical parameters. Both must be verified independently.

What instrument is used to verify photometric requirements?

A goniophotometer, or another appropriate calibrated photometric system, is typically used to produce candela tables, polar curves, horizontal and vertical distribution, and colour data.

Does electrical performance affect photometric performance?

Yes, indirectly. Circuit current flows through the fixture input to the LED driver and then to the LED output, so incorrect electrical current can result in incorrect light output. They still remain separate requirements that each need their own verification.

Is there a third category besides photometric and electrical?

Yes — functional requirements, which ask whether the fixture performs its intended function, such as correct ON/OFF behaviour or intensity-step response, distinct from both its optical and electrical performance.

20 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

ICAO Annex 14 / Aerodrome Design Manual

Governs photometric requirements for AGL fixtures, including intensity, distribution, colour, and angular coverage for the applicable light type.

FAA AC 150/5345-46

Specification for Runway, Taxiway, Heliport, and Vertiport Light Fixtures — the single fixture spec whose chapters set out both the photometric qualification requirements and the electrical/dielectric qualification requirements compared on this page.

IEC 60364 / Local Wiring Regulations

Requirements for electrical performance, insulation resistance, and dielectric withstand testing of AGL fixtures and associated circuits.

DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Do not assume that a fixture's electrical test results say anything about its photometric performance, or vice versa. Each requirement type needs its own dedicated test and acceptance criteria.

Photometric and electrical requirements are not two versions of the same check — one verifies the light a fixture produces, the other verifies how it handles electricity to produce it.
