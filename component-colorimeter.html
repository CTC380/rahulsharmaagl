Colorimeter

A precision optical instrument used to measure the chromaticity (colour coordinates) of AGL light sources. Ensures that runway, taxiway, and approach lights conform to the strict colour specifications defined by ICAO, FAA, and IEC, which are critical for correct visual signalling and pilot interpretation.

Overview

What Is It?

A Colorimeter is an optoelectronic instrument that quantifies the colour of light emitted by an AGL fixture. Unlike a photometer, which measures intensity, a colorimeter focuses on chromaticity — the subjective colour perceived by the human eye, expressed in standard CIE (International Commission on Illumination) coordinates. In airfield lighting, each colour (aviation red, green, yellow, blue, white) has a defined chromaticity boundary within the CIE 1931 (x,y) or 1976 (u',v') colour spaces. The colorimeter verifies that the fixture's output falls within the required tolerance box, ensuring unambiguous visual signals for pilots.

Key colour standards for AGL include:

  • Aviation Red: Used for runway end lights, obstruction lights, and stop bars.
  • Aviation Green: Used for threshold lights, taxiway centreline lights, and lead‑on lights.
  • Aviation Yellow: Used for intermediate holding position lights and clearance bars.
  • Aviation Blue: Used for taxiway edge lights.
  • Aviation White: Used for runway edge, centreline, and approach lights.
Purpose

Purpose & Function

The colorimeter's purpose is to confirm that a fixture's colour is not just close to the right shade of red, green, yellow, blue, or white, but falls inside the precise chromaticity boundary that gives that colour its specific operational meaning to a pilot. Because a pilot's decisions — whether they are looking at a threshold, a runway end, or a taxiway edge — depend on correctly identifying colour at a glance, a fixture whose colour has drifted outside its tolerance box, even if it still looks broadly correct to the eye, can create genuine ambiguity about what the light is signalling. The colorimeter's function is to catch that drift objectively, whether during manufacturing quality control, type certification, or a routine field check prompted by a pilot report of an off-colour light.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Colorimeters are deployed in various stages of the AGL lifecycle to ensure colour compliance:

  • Manufacturing Quality Control: Used by AGL fixture manufacturers to verify that each production batch meets the specified chromaticity requirements before shipping.
  • Type Testing and Certification: Independent testing laboratories use colorimeters to certify that new fixture models comply with ICAO, FAA, and IEC colour standards.
  • Airport Commissioning: During new AGL system installation, colorimeters are used to confirm that every fixture emits the correct colour when powered at the required current steps.
  • Periodic Maintenance Inspection: Airports perform regular colour checks (e.g., annually) to detect colour shifts due to ageing of filters, LEDs, or optical components.
  • Troubleshooting: When pilots report colour confusion (e.g., a green light appearing yellowish), a colorimeter is used to pinpoint the cause — often a degraded filter or incorrect LED bin.
  • Calibration of Field Test Equipment: Simpler colour‑checking devices (e.g., colour filters) are calibrated against a reference colorimeter.
Care

Storage & Handling

The colorimeter is stored in its padded carry case between uses, with the sensor head or optical port protected by a lens cap to keep dust and fingerprints off the measurement surface. It is kept in a stable, moderate-temperature environment when not in use and allowed to acclimatise before taking readings if moved between significantly different temperatures, since thermal drift can affect sensor accuracy. Field crews carry it out to the fixture being tested rather than removing the fixture, and the unit is periodically checked against a reference calibration standard to confirm its readings remain within tolerance, with the calibration due date tracked alongside the instrument's storage record.

Function

How Does It Work?

A colorimeter typically uses one of the following methods to measure colour:

  • Tristimulus Filter Method: The instrument uses three or more photodetectors, each fitted with a filter that mimics the CIE colour‑matching functions (x̄, ȳ, z̄). By measuring the response through each filter, the instrument calculates the tristimulus values (X, Y, Z) and derives the chromaticity coordinates (x, y). This is the most common approach for field instruments due to its simplicity and speed.
  • Spectroradiometric Method: A more advanced technique where the instrument measures the full spectral power distribution (SPD) of the light source using a diffraction grating and a linear array detector. From the SPD, the CIE coordinates are computed with high accuracy. This method is used in reference laboratories and for type testing.

For AGL measurements, the colorimeter is positioned at a specific distance and angle relative to the fixture (often the same geometry as intensity measurements). The fixture is operated at the required current step, and the colorimeter records the chromaticity coordinates. The results are compared against the relevant standard’s colour boundary table to determine pass/fail.

Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

The colorimeter operates as a standalone measurement instrument rather than a controlled system component, but it sits within a broader photometric verification architecture. It is typically synchronised with the fixture's power supply (set to the specific current step being tested) and, in a test-bench or production line setup, its digital output feeds into the same data acquisition and pass/fail reporting software used for intensity measurements, so a single test run can log both luminous intensity and chromaticity coordinates against the fixture's serial number. There is no feedback loop in the electrical sense — the colorimeter does not adjust the fixture it is measuring — but the pass/fail verdict it produces is what gates whether that fixture proceeds to shipment, installation, or return for rework, making it a control point in the quality process even though it exerts no control over the electrical circuit itself.

Construction

Physical Construction

The colorimeter is built around a precision measurement head containing either filtered tristimulus photodetectors or a miniature spectrograph, mounted behind an optical aperture that defines the instrument's field of view and fixes its viewing distance and angle relative to the fixture's optical axis. The head and body are housed in a rigid polymer or metal shell that maintains consistent internal baffling to exclude stray light, with the aperture ring precisely machined to hold that fixed, repeatable measurement geometry against the light source under test. Data interface ports (USB, Bluetooth, or RS-232) and the power/battery compartment are positioned away from the optical path to avoid contaminating readings, and the housing carries an IP54-rated seal around seams and connector covers for field use in outdoor AGL environments. A protective lens cap or calibration tile holder is typically supplied to shield the optics and provide an on-site reference check between formal calibrations.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Filter Ageing and Calibration Drift

Tristimulus colorimeters rely on precision optical filters that can degrade over time due to humidity, temperature, and UV exposure. This shifts the spectral response, leading to colour measurement errors. Annual recalibration against a traceable reference lamp is essential. Field checks with a calibrated colour standard (e.g., a stable LED reference) are recommended before each measurement campaign.

Ambient Light Contamination

Outdoor measurements are susceptible to sunlight and other ambient light sources, which can skew chromaticity readings. Using a baffle tube, measuring at night, or employing a modulation/demodulation technique (lock‑in) helps minimise interference. Some instruments have a built‑in ambient light subtraction feature.

Temperature Sensitivity

Both LEDs and photodetectors change their spectral output with temperature. For accurate colour measurements, the fixture should be operated at its normal operating temperature (allow warm‑up time). The colorimeter itself should be stabilised to ambient temperature before use.

Beam Alignment and Viewing Angle

Colour may vary with viewing angle for some fixtures (especially LEDs). The measurement must be taken at the specified angle (usually the beam centre) as defined in the standard. Misalignment can result in erroneous colour readings. Using a precise sighting system or a goniometric setup is recommended.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Chromaticity Reading Falls Outside the Expected Colour Box
Likely Cause
Filter ageing/drift in the tristimulus sensor, a genuine shift in the fixture's LED output, or the reading was taken before the fixture reached thermal stability.
Diagnostic Steps
Verify the colorimeter against a stable, traceable colour reference before condemning the fixture; allow the fixture adequate warm-up time; check the colorimeter's last recalibration date.
Corrective Action
Recalibrate the colorimeter against a traceable reference if it fails the check, or replace the fixture's LED module if a genuine, repeatable colour shift is confirmed after full warm-up.
Reading Varies Significantly With Ambient Conditions
Likely Cause
Sunlight or other ambient light sources contaminating the measurement, especially during daytime outdoor testing.
Diagnostic Steps
Compare readings taken with and without a baffle tube or shading; where available, enable the instrument's ambient light subtraction feature; consider re-testing at night for critical measurements.
Corrective Action
Use a baffle tube, shading, or the ambient-subtraction feature consistently, and schedule critical colour measurements for low-ambient-light periods.
Reading Drifts Over an Extended Test Session
Likely Cause
Both the fixture and the colorimeter's photodetector are temperature-sensitive and may not yet be at thermal equilibrium.
Diagnostic Steps
Allow both the fixture and the colorimeter to fully stabilise to their normal operating temperature before starting the session; monitor a fixed reference reading periodically through the session for drift.
Corrective Action
Extend warm-up/stabilisation time before starting measurements and recheck the reference periodically; recalibrate mid-session if drift exceeds tolerance.
Reading Changes Significantly With Viewing Angle
Likely Cause
The measurement was not taken at the specified beam-centre angle defined in the applicable standard, particularly relevant for LED fixtures.
Diagnostic Steps
Confirm the sighting/aiming setup places the sensor at the correct angle relative to the fixture's optical axis; repeat the measurement using a precise sighting system or goniometric setup.
Corrective Action
Realign the measurement geometry to the standard's specified angle and repeat; invest in a sighting jig or goniometer if angle control is a recurring issue.
Instrument Fails to Calibrate Against Its Reference Standard
Likely Cause
Optical contamination on the colorimeter's input optics, a failed or expired reference standard, or a hardware fault in the instrument.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect and clean the instrument's input optics; confirm the reference standard is within its calibration validity period; attempt calibration with a second known-good reference.
Corrective Action
Clean the optical path and repeat calibration; replace an expired or suspect reference standard; return the colorimeter for service if it still fails to calibrate against a known-good reference.

Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Avoid Looking Directly Into the Light Source
Personnel Safety

Position the colorimeter's sensor head to capture the fixture's output rather than placing your eye at the beam axis, particularly for high-intensity fixtures, to avoid discomfort or glare-induced flash blindness.

Airside Work Coordination
Operational

Any test carried out on an active runway or taxiway fixture requires ATC/airside coordination and a NOTAM where the fixture will be dark, dimmed, or partially obstructed during the reading, with vehicle and personnel movement kept clear of the strip per the aerodrome's safety management procedures.

Instrument Calibration Currency
Compliance

Confirm the colorimeter's calibration certificate is current and traceable before relying on its readings for compliance testing, since chromaticity acceptance is judged against tight CIE coordinate boundaries.

Allow Fixture and Instrument to Stabilise
Operational

Allow a newly energised fixture (and the colorimeter itself, if recently moved between temperature extremes) to reach thermal stability before taking a reading, since output chromaticity can drift slightly during warm-up.

Correct Measurement Distance and Angle
Operational

Position the sensor at the manufacturer's specified distance and angle relative to the fixture's beam axis; an off-axis or too-close reading can misrepresent the colour the fixture actually presents to an approaching pilot.

FOD Control at the Fixture
Operational

Keep tools, tripods, and test equipment accounted for at all times when working around an in-pavement or elevated fixture, and clear the area before reopening it to traffic.

PPE & Ground Awareness
Personnel Safety

Wear high-visibility clothing for airside work and stay aware of surrounding vehicle and aircraft movement while attention is focused on the instrument display.

Record Results Against Compliance Boundary
Compliance

Log the measured chromaticity coordinates against the applicable CIE boundary for that fixture's designated colour, and flag any reading outside tolerance for follow-up before closing out the test.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Let LED Fixtures Thermally Stabilise Before Reading Chromaticity
Technique

LED chromaticity shifts measurably as the die heats up from cold-start to steady-state operation; a colorimeter reading taken in the first minute after power-on can show a fixture passing or failing purely due to warm-up drift rather than its actual settled colour. Always let the fixture reach thermal equilibrium before recording a compliance reading.

Measurement Distance and Angle Affect Chromaticity Readings
Technique

Off-axis colour shift is a real phenomenon on many AGL fixture designs; taking a colorimeter reading at an angle or distance that doesn't match the standard's specified measurement geometry can produce a chromaticity coordinate that doesn't reflect what a pilot actually sees on the intended approach or runway sightline.

A Reading Near the Boundary Deserves a Second Measurement
Verification

A chromaticity coordinate that falls just inside or just outside the required tolerance box is more informative as a trend than a single pass/fail; take a repeat reading before either failing a fixture that's marginally in spec or passing one that's marginally out, since instrument and environmental noise are largest right at the boundary.

Stray Ambient Light Skews Low-Level Readings
Field Practice

Daylight or nearby fixture spill can bias a colorimeter's reading on lower-intensity steps more than it does at full brightness; where possible, take critical chromaticity measurements at night or with the sensor properly shielded from ambient sources rather than in full daylight.

Sensor Calibration Drift Is Invisible Without a Reference Check
Calibration

A colorimeter's optical sensor can drift out of calibration gradually with no obvious symptom on the display; verify against a known reference source at the start of a measurement session rather than trusting the last calibration date alone, especially after the instrument has seen rough field handling.

Lens Contamination Shifts Colour Readings, Not Just Intensity
Maintenance

A dirty or UV-yellowed fixture lens doesn't just reduce measured intensity — it can shift the apparent chromaticity coordinate too, especially toward the yellow end of the spectrum; when a fixture reads marginal on colour, check and clean the lens before concluding the LED source itself has drifted out of tolerance.
