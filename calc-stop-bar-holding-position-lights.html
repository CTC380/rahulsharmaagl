FAQ

Stop Bar & Runway Holding Position Lights Calculator: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on stop bar spacing, positions, configuration and serviceability.

What does the Stop Bar & Runway Holding Position Lights calculator do?

It is a safety-critical intersection calculator. It runs a spacing and position engine, supports in-pavement, elevated and mixed configurations, checks the relationship to the taxiway centreline and holding position, and covers the control, monitoring and circuit framework, serviceability, and an inspection of an existing installation. It keeps the runway holding-position marking, the Stop Bar and the taxiway centreline lights strictly separate, and never counts a marking as a light.

How is the number of stop bar lights calculated?

The number of intervals is the coverage width divided by the design spacing, rounded up, and the number of lights is that plus one. The actual spacing is the coverage width divided by the number of intervals, and it must not exceed the maximum spacing. For example, a 23 m width at 3 m design spacing gives 8 intervals, 9 lights and an actual spacing of 2.875 m, uniformly distributed instead of leaving one oversized final gap.

Where does the spacing limit come from?

It is entered by you from your standard. Stop Bar spacing is never reused from the taxiway-centreline calculator (#09), and no figure is assumed by default for ICAO, FAA or DGCA. For reference, ICAO Annex 14 5.3.20.6 requires lights at uniform intervals of no more than 3 m across the taxiway, but confirm the value in your own copy and in the FAA and DGCA documents.

How do the coverage width, chainages and centreline check work?

The coverage width can be set automatically equal to the taxiway width, or entered as a custom value, and it is reported as a calculated width. You enter the holding-position chainage and the stop bar chainage from the approved layout, and the difference is reported explicitly. If you enter a measured centreline crossing position, it is checked against the expected centred position of half the coverage width, within your tolerance.

What is the difference between in-pavement, elevated and mixed configurations?

They are not treated as interchangeable. In-pavement and elevated configurations each record their own fields, namely circuit grouping and obstruction clearance. For a mixed layout, no standard formula governs the split, so you enter the approved layout's split directly, and the calculator flags a mismatch if the two counts do not sum to the generated total. Control, circuit and monitoring units are BOQ line items and are not counted as light positions.

How is serviceability calculated?

The number of failed fixtures is the total minus the working ones, and the failure rate is failed divided by total, times 100. Serviceability is 100 percent minus the failure rate. No generic limit is hard-coded, so the applicable limit must come from the governing standard or project specification.

How does the existing-installation inspection work?

Enter one measured fixture per line with its x and y position, type (in-pavement or elevated), direction (unidirectional or bidirectional), status (working or failed) and colour. The tool checks spacing, perpendicular alignment to the design Stop Bar line and colour against the expected value, and compiles working and failed counts. This is checked against the design Stop Bar generated on the Calculate tab.

What does the calculator not do?

It does not maintain the exact ICAO, FAA or DGCA Stop Bar spacing and configuration database, does not resolve the detailed CAT II and CAT III configuration, and does not do a true 2D match against a separately run taxiway-centreline calculation. Stop Bar geometry and operational control are safety-critical, so calculated positions and quantities must be verified against the applicable requirements and the approved aerodrome lighting layout.

Methodology

How This Calculator Works

Standard → geometry → spacing engine → position engine → colour/centreline/control → quantity → compliance. A marking, a Stop Bar, and a centreline light are never treated as the same thing.

Marking ≠ Stop Bar ≠ Centreline

The runway holding-position marking is a surface marking. The Stop Bar is a lighting system associated with a controlled holding position. Taxiway centreline lights are a separate guidance system. This calculator never counts a holding-position marking as a light, and never reuses Calculator #09's centreline spacing for the Stop Bar.

Coverage width & quantity formula

N (intervals) = ⌈ LSB / Sdesign ⌉  →  lights = N + 1  →  Sactual = LSB / N  →  Sactual ≤ Smax

Worked example: LSB = 23 m, Sdesign = 3 m → N = ⌈23/3⌉ = 8 → Sactual = 23/8 = 2.875 m, uniformly distributed rather than leaving one oversized final gap. That is 8 intervals and 9 lights, one at each end of the coverage width. This is illustrative only — the real spacing value must come from the selected standard.

Holding-position & centreline relationship

ΔD = DHP − DSB

Both chainages are entered explicitly rather than the Stop Bar location being assumed from taxiway length. Where a measured centreline-crossing position is entered, it is checked against the expected centred position LSB/2 within tolerance T — confirming the Stop Bar is centred on the taxiway and the centreline passes through the intended reference point PCL−SB.

In-pavement vs. elevated vs. mixed

These are not treated as interchangeable. In-pavement and elevated configurations record their own fields (circuit grouping vs. obstruction clearance), and a mixed layout is captured via an explicit manual split of the generated positions — no standard formula predicts how a real mixed layout is arranged, so this calculator asks rather than assumes.

Failure / serviceability calculation

Qfailed = Qtotal − Qworking     Failure Rate = (Qfailed / Qtotal) × 100

Serviceability is reported as 100% − Failure Rate. No generic operational limit is hard-coded — the applicable serviceability threshold must come from the governing standard or project specification, entered explicitly.

Existing-installation inspection

Measured fixtures are checked for spacing, perpendicular alignment to the design Stop Bar line, colour against the standard-derived expected colour, and compiled into working/failed counts — separately from the design-position engine above.

Colour / function framework

Colour is derived from Standard → Function → Colour rather than being a free engineering input. Stop Bar lights are internationally red; this calculator reports that as the standard-derived value and flags it for verification against the project's governing document rather than letting the user pick an arbitrary colour.

Duplicate-fixture control

Generated positions are automatically checked pairwise against tolerance T, consistent with the duplicate-control approach used in Calculators #08 and #09. Any two positions closer than T are flagged rather than silently counted twice.

Physical fixtures vs. functions

The calculator reports the physical fixture count (e.g. 9 individual light units) distinctly from the functional description ("one Stop Bar") — both are shown, since procurement and maintenance need the fixture count, not just the functional name.

What this calculator does not do

It does not maintain the exact ICAO/FAA/DGCA Stop Bar spacing and configuration database, does not resolve the detailed CAT II/III configuration, and does not perform true 2D matching against a separately-run taxiway-centreline calculation — the centreline-crossing check relies on a manually entered measured position. Spacing engine, position generation, and the holding-position/centreline relationship checks are fully calculated; exact figure-level configuration must be confirmed against the approved layout.
