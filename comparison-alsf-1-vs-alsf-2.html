Approach & Landing Systems Comparison

ALSF-1 VS ALSF-2

High-Intensity Approach Lighting Systems with Sequenced Flashing Lights — both share the same outer sequenced-flasher philosophy, but differ significantly in the inner approach configuration nearest the runway threshold.

02 · Definitions

What Is ALSF-1 & ALSF-2?

ALSF-1

A high-intensity approach lighting configuration associated primarily with Category I precision approaches. It combines steady-burning approach lights, centreline lighting, crossbars, red inner approach references, sequenced flashing lights and threshold-related visual references. The sequenced flashers create a moving-light effect toward the runway threshold, helping the pilot visually acquire and align with the runway environment.

Key characteristics

  • High-intensity approach lighting
  • Sequenced flashing lights
  • Centreline approach lighting
  • White crossbar references
  • Red inner terminating / wing-bar arrangement
  • Primarily associated with CAT I approach lighting

ALSF-2

Also a High-Intensity Approach Lighting System with Sequenced Flashing Lights. It is the standard configuration associated with Category II precision approaches and is also used in systems supporting Category III operations. Its most important feature is an enhanced inner approach configuration providing additional visual references close to the runway threshold — particularly important during low-visibility operations.

Key characteristics

  • White centreline light bars
  • Red side-row bars
  • White 500-ft crossbar
  • White 1,000-ft crossbar
  • Sequenced flashing lights
  • High-intensity approach lighting
03 · Key Difference

The Critical Difference: Inner 1,000 ft

The easiest way to understand ALSF-1 vs ALSF-2 is to focus on the inner portion of the approach lighting system.

ALSF-1 inner section

  • Red terminating bar
  • Red wing / pre-threshold bars

ALSF-2 inner section

  • White centreline bars
  • Red side-row bars
  • White 500-ft crossbar
  • White 1,000-ft crossbar

Engineering point: ALSF-2 does not simply mean "more lights." The important difference is the configuration and function of the lights in the inner approach area — creating a denser visual reference pattern for precision approaches with lower visibility minima.

05 · Sequenced Flashers

How Sequenced Flashing Lights Work

Sequenced flashing lights are a defining feature of both ALSF-1 and ALSF-2. The flashers operate sequentially along the approach path, creating an apparent movement of light toward the runway that provides a strong visual cue for alignment and orientation.

Visual sequence: Approach area → sequential flashing → moving-light effect → pilot's visual attention moves toward threshold → runway environment acquired.

06 · Why ALSF-2 Is More Complex

Additional Inner-Zone Visual Information

The additional inner-zone configuration of ALSF-2 gives the pilot more visual information close to the runway threshold — particularly important when breaking out of the instrument approach at relatively low visibility:

  • Centreline reference
  • Lateral reference from red side rows
  • Distance reference from crossbars
  • Sequenced flashing guidance
  • Stronger visual transition from approach to runway environment

08 · Electrical Engineering

Electrical Considerations

From an AGL engineering perspective, ALSF-1 and ALSF-2 require careful coordination of:

Primary series circuit

Approach lighting fixtures are normally supplied through the airport's series circuit architecture, with the applicable circuit configuration determined by the project design.

Isolation transformers

Where series-circuit fixtures require isolation from the primary circuit, the appropriate isolation transformer and secondary connection arrangement must be selected according to the fixture rating and governing requirements.

CCR

The Constant Current Regulator (CCR) controls the series-circuit current and therefore the operating intensity of compatible approach lighting equipment.

Sequenced flasher control

The sequenced flashing system requires dedicated control and synchronization so that the flash sequence operates correctly.

Monitoring

  • Flasher status
  • Circuit faults
  • Fixture failures
  • Control equipment status
  • Power supply faults
09 · Photometry & Alignment

Verification Is Not Just "Lights On"

ALSF systems are not evaluated only by whether the lights are ON. Engineering verification should consider:

  • Light intensity
  • Beam orientation — horizontal and vertical aiming
  • Crossbar and centreline alignment
  • Red-light performance
  • Sequenced flasher operation
  • Uniformity and applicable photometric criteria

A system can be electrically healthy while still having a photometric or alignment problem. Electrical commissioning + visual inspection + alignment verification + photometric verification = complete approach-lighting verification.

10 · Installation

Installation Considerations

Both systems require careful coordination of the approach centreline, crossbar positions, light-unit spacing, fixture orientation, elevated mounting structures, frangible supports, cable routing, isolation transformers, junction boxes, primary series circuits, control circuits, sequenced flasher equipment and obstacle/terrain conditions.

For ALSF-2, particular attention should be given to the inner approach section because of the additional centreline and red side-row configuration.

11 · Maintenance

Maintenance Considerations

Visual Inspection

  • Damaged fixtures
  • Broken frangible supports
  • Lens/prism damage, water ingress, corrosion
  • Cable damage, loose connections

Electrical Inspection

  • Series-circuit continuity
  • Isolation transformer condition, insulation resistance
  • Connector condition, CCR operation
  • Flasher controller, control wiring

Photometric Inspection

  • Intensity, beam alignment, colour
  • Light output
  • Crossbar and centreline performance

Sequenced Flasher Inspection

  • Correct flash sequence and timing
  • No missing flash positions
  • Correct synchronization and control equipment operation

13 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: ALSF-2 is just a longer ALSF-1
Fact

The important difference is the inner configuration, not simply system length.

Myth: ALSF-2 only has more lights
Fact

The arrangement and function of the lights are more important than the total number of fixtures.

Myth: Sequenced flashers are unique to ALSF-2
Fact

Both ALSF-1 and ALSF-2 use sequenced flashing lights.

Myth: ALSF-2 automatically means CAT III
Fact

ALSF-2 is the standard Category II approach lighting configuration and is also used in Category III environments. The actual operational category depends on the complete approach system and applicable approval requirements.

Myth: If every fixture is ON, the ALS is compliant
Fact

Correct operation also requires appropriate photometric performance, alignment, colour, configuration and control-system performance.

14 · Engineering Verdict

Which System Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

ALSF-1 and ALSF-2 are closely related high-intensity sequenced-flasher approach lighting systems, but their inner approach configurations are significantly different. ALSF-1 uses the red terminating/wing-bar arrangement, while ALSF-2 provides an enhanced inner visual pattern using white centreline bars, red side-row bars and a 500-ft white crossbar.

For airport engineering, selection should not be based only on fixture quantity. The correct system must be established from the required approach category, runway operational minima, aerodrome design basis, applicable FAA/ICAO requirements, national aviation authority requirements, airport-specific specifications, approved approach lighting configuration, electrical/control architecture and photometric requirements.

ALSF-1CAT I-oriented high-intensity sequenced approach lighting.
ALSF-2Enhanced inner approach configuration for CAT II and CAT III environments.

15 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ALSF-2 only for CAT III?

No. ALSF-2 is the standard Category II approach lighting configuration and is also used for Category III operations.

What is the biggest difference between ALSF-1 and ALSF-2?

The major difference is the inner approach configuration nearest the runway threshold.

Do both ALS systems use sequenced flashers?

Yes.

Does ALSF-2 have a 500-ft crossbar?

Yes. The 500-ft white crossbar is one of the important distinguishing features of the ALSF-2 inner configuration.

Does ALSF-1 have red lights?

Yes. The ALSF-1 inner configuration includes red terminating/wing-bar references.

Does ALSF-2 have red lights?

Yes. ALSF-2 uses red side-row bars in its inner approach section.

Is ALSF-2 always better than ALSF-1?

Not as a general engineering statement. The correct system depends on the required approach category, operational minima, applicable standards and approved airport design.
