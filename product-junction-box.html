Product Overview

AGL Junction Box

What It Is

Junction boxes provide protected access points for field cable connection and distribution within an Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) installation.

They provide a controlled enclosure for cable terminations, connections and equipment interfaces while helping protect electrical components from environmental and mechanical conditions.

Precision AGL Solutions supplies junction-box configurations selected according to cable type, connection requirements, enclosure conditions, mounting arrangement and project-specific installation requirements.

Circuit Applications

Primary and Secondary Cable Applications

Primary Cable Applications

Where used within an AGL primary circuit, select the junction box for compatibility with the circuit arrangement and cable system. Considerations:

  • Series-circuit configuration
  • Cable construction
  • Operating circuit current
  • Connector system
  • Insulation requirements
  • Environmental exposure
  • Required clearances and terminations

For high-voltage AGL primary circuits, review the complete connection arrangement as a system rather than selecting the enclosure independently. Primary series-circuit connections are normally made with approved splice or L-823 connector assemblies rather than general-purpose terminal blocks, and the enclosure must never be opened while the series circuit is energised.

Secondary Cable Applications

Junction boxes may also be used for selected secondary cable and equipment-interface applications. Selection should consider:

  • Fixture or equipment connection
  • Secondary cable type
  • Connector arrangement
  • Cable-entry requirements
  • Environmental exposure
  • Maintenance access

The arrangement should be compatible with the associated isolating transformer, fixture lead or field equipment.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures

FAQ

AGL Junction Box — Frequently Asked Questions

What is an AGL junction box?

An AGL junction box is a protected enclosure that gives an access point for field cable connection and distribution within an Airfield Ground Lighting installation. It provides a controlled enclosure for cable terminations, connections and equipment interfaces while protecting electrical components from environmental and mechanical conditions.

Where are junction boxes used?

They are used for primary and secondary field cable connections, field cable distribution, equipment interfaces, connection points within lighting systems, cable transition points, maintenance and inspection installations, new AGL infrastructure and rehabilitation projects.

How does a junction box differ from a junction pit?

A junction box provides protected above-ground or equipment-level field cable connections, whereas a junction pit provides underground access to cable joints, connectors and field electrical equipment. See the AGL junction pit page for the underground option.

Can a junction box be used on a primary series circuit?

Where used within a primary circuit, select it for compatibility with the series-circuit arrangement, cable construction, operating current, connector system and insulation requirements, and review the complete connection arrangement as a system. Primary series-circuit connections are normally made with approved splice or L-823 connector assemblies rather than general-purpose terminal blocks, and the enclosure must never be opened while the series circuit is energised.

What should be coordinated for cable entries and terminals?

Coordinate the cable diameter, the number of incoming and outgoing cables, glands or other entry arrangements, connector dimensions, bend radius, cable support and sealing around the entries. Entry positions should leave clearance for installation, termination and future maintenance. The internal terminal arrangement should match the electrical function, with earthing and bonding of metallic enclosures per the approved design, and be identified in coordination with the approved electrical drawings.

What ingress protection does a junction box need?

The enclosure and cable entries should protect against rain and water ingress, dust and contaminants, moisture and condensation, corrosive conditions, temperature variation and mechanical exposure. Determine the required ingress-protection level from the installation location and project specification, with IEC 60529 as the reference for IP ratings.

How can a junction box be mounted?

Options include surface mounting, equipment mounting structures, service-area mounting, junction-pit interfaces and other approved field mounting arrangements. The method should give adequate mechanical support while keeping access for cable installation and maintenance. Within the movement area or runway strip, boxes must not become an obstacle: use below-grade installation, for example in a pit, or a frangible arrangement, as the aerodrome design requires.

What maintenance access should be provided?

The box should let technicians inspect cable entries, check terminal connections, identify circuits, inspect sealing, check for moisture or contamination, verify cable condition and perform approved electrical tests. Consider access during the initial installation rather than after the box has been permanently positioned.

What is checked at installation and commissioning?

Before installation, verify the dimensions, cable types and quantities, cable-entry positions, terminal arrangement, enclosure material, ingress-protection requirement, mounting method, accessibility and identification requirements. At commissioning, inspect cable routing, terminations, circuit identification, cable-entry sealing, enclosure integrity, mounting, visible damage and electrical continuity where applicable.

Which standards apply to junction boxes?

Principal references are FAA AC 150/5345-42K (airport light bases, transformer housings, junction boxes and accessories), AC 150/5340-30J, ICAO Doc 9157 Part 5, IEC 60529, and DGCA India CAR Section 4 together with airport authority specifications. Use the edition current at the time of your project.

Is it safe to open a junction box on a live series circuit?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage, and opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death. De-energise the circuit at the regulator, apply the site lock-out / tag-out procedure, verify the circuit is de-energised and work under the permit-to-work system. See the electrical safety warning on this page.

What information is needed to select a replacement junction box?

Provide the existing junction-box dimensions, cable types and sizes, number of cable entries, existing terminal arrangement, mounting arrangement, environmental conditions, available installation space and photographs or drawings. This helps determine whether the replacement can use the existing installation arrangement or needs modification.
