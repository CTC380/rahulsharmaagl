Approach & Landing Systems Comparison

Traditional PAPI VS LED PAPI

Traditional PAPI uses conventional lamps — typically halogen/incandescent-type sources — while LED PAPI uses solid-state LEDs. Both must provide the required red/white visual indication and precise angular performance; the main differences are source technology, maintenance, energy use, thermal behaviour and electronics.

02 · Definitions

What Is PAPI?

PAPI — Precision Approach Path Indicator — provides visual guidance to an approaching aircraft using a combination of white and red light sectors. A typical PAPI installation has four light units.

Function chain: Aircraft approach → PAPI units → white/red indication → pilot determines approach path.

PAPI is not simply a bright lamp — the angular boundary between white and red light is fundamental to its function, regardless of the light source used to achieve it.

03 · Traditional PAPI

Conventional Lamp-Based PAPI

A conventional PAPI generally uses a lamp-based optical arrangement: power → lamp → reflector/optical system → filter/lens → white + red sectors → aircraft. The lamp produces the optical energy, while the optical system creates the required angular distribution and colour separation.

Advantages

  • Mature technology
  • Familiar maintenance procedures
  • Relatively simple source technology
  • Existing maintenance knowledge
  • Often lower initial equipment cost

Disadvantages

  • Shorter lamp life
  • More frequent lamp replacement
  • Higher energy consumption
  • High operating temperature
  • Lamp aging affects optical output
  • Filament vulnerable to vibration/shock
04 · LED PAPI

Solid-State LED PAPI

LED PAPI replaces the conventional lamp with LEDs: power → LED driver → LED array → optical system → white + red sectors → aircraft. The optical design is therefore closely integrated with the LED source.

Advantages

  • Long operating life
  • Lower energy consumption
  • Reduced lamp-replacement workload
  • Better vibration resistance
  • Rapid switching
  • More stable light output over useful life
  • Potentially lower lifecycle cost

Disadvantages

  • Higher initial cost
  • Driver/electronic components introduce complexity
  • Thermal management is important
  • Surge protection becomes important
  • Optical design must be carefully engineered
  • Retrofit requires electrical/photometric verification
05 · Key Difference

The Most Important PAPI Difference: Angular Accuracy

For PAPI, the critical performance question isn't merely "is the light bright enough?" It is: does the light transition between white and red at the correct elevation angles? The optical system must create the required transition with appropriate sharpness and stability.

Engineering point: PAPI photometry and angular alignment are critical regardless of whether the source is halogen or LED.

06 · Photometric Behaviour

Traditional vs LED Photometric Behaviour

Traditional lamp

As the lamp ages, filament deterioration causes optical output to change, and photometric performance may drift over the lamp's life.

LED

LED output is generally more stable, but is still affected by LED aging, junction temperature, driver current and optical degradation. LED does not mean "no photometric degradation."

07 · Colour Performance

Achieving Red & White Indication

PAPI depends on clearly distinguishable red and white indications. Traditional systems may use a lamp with optical filters to create the required colour separation; LED systems can use appropriately selected LEDs and optical arrangements. In both cases the engineering objective is the same: required colour + required intensity + required angular distribution = correct PAPI visual indication. The exact colour and photometric limits should always be taken from the applicable aerodrome/equipment specification.

08 · Energy Consumption

Power Use Comparison

Traditional: electrical energy → filament heating → light + substantial heat. LED: electrical energy → LED driver → LED → light + heat. LED generally provides a significant reduction in power consumption for equivalent lighting performance, and for an installation with four PAPI units operating for long periods this can provide meaningful energy savings.

09 · Maintenance

Maintenance Comparison

Traditional PAPI

  • Lamp inspection
  • Lamp replacement
  • Lens/optical cleaning
  • Alignment checks
  • Photometric verification
  • Wiring inspection

LED PAPI

  • Optical/lens inspection
  • Alignment checks
  • Driver/electronic inspection
  • Surge/protection checks
  • Functional verification
  • Photometric verification as required

LED reduces lamp-maintenance frequency but does not eliminate PAPI maintenance.

10 · Failure Modes

How Each System Fails

Traditional

The classic failure: filament failure → lamp OFF.

LED

  • LED degradation
  • Individual LED failure
  • LED-array failure
  • Driver failure
  • Surge damage
  • Electronic component failure
  • Thermal degradation

The actual failure behaviour depends on the specific PAPI architecture.

11 · Thermal Management

Heat Behaviour of Each Source

Traditional PAPI: high lamp temperature is an inherent characteristic of the light source. LED PAPI: LEDs still generate heat, and the driver/electronics also generate heat. A poorly designed LED thermal system can lead to LED degradation, reduced output, photometric deviation and reduced useful life.

LED thermal design is a key engineering consideration — it is not a solved problem simply by switching to LEDs.

12 · Electrical Architecture

LED PAPI requires evaluation of input voltage/current, driver characteristics, surge protection, power consumption, electrical isolation where applicable, failure behaviour and control compatibility.

13 · Alignment

Alignment Is Critical for Both

Even a perfectly functioning LED PAPI can give incorrect guidance if it is incorrectly aligned. Correct PAPI guidance depends on photometric performance plus mechanical alignment together.

LED technology does not remove the need for accurate PAPI alignment.

15 · Site Commissioning

SAT / Site Commissioning

For either type, the process runs: installation → physical inspection → electrical verification → mechanical alignment → optical/photometric verification → operational check → commissioning.

For LED PAPI, additional attention may be required to driver operation, control electronics, surge protection, LED fault indication, and interface with the electrical supply/control system.

16 · Retrofit

Traditional PAPI → LED PAPI Retrofit

A retrofit should not be treated simply as replacing a lamp. A proper process runs: existing PAPI → existing electrical arrangement → existing optical geometry → LED PAPI compatibility assessment → new fixture/source → photometric verification → alignment → site commissioning. The replacement must maintain the required PAPI performance throughout.

17 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: LED PAPI is automatically a better PAPI
Fact

LED provides major advantages in source technology, but compliance still depends on the complete PAPI system — optical, mechanical, electrical and operational performance together.

Myth: LED sources never degrade photometrically
Fact

LED output is generally more stable, but it is still affected by aging, junction temperature, driver current and optical degradation.

Myth: An LED retrofit is just swapping the lamp
Fact

A retrofit requires a full compatibility assessment of the existing electrical arrangement and optical geometry, followed by photometric verification, alignment and site commissioning.

Myth: LED removes the need for precise alignment
Fact

Alignment and photometric objectives remain the same regardless of source technology — LED does not remove the need for accurate PAPI alignment.

20 · Engineering Verdict

Which One Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

Traditional PAPI depends on a conventional lamp and optical system, whereas LED PAPI uses LEDs and electronic drivers to achieve the same precision visual-guidance function with longer source life, lower energy consumption and reduced maintenance.

For PAPI, the decisive issue is not simply LED vs halogen — it is whether the complete unit maintains the required intensity, colour, red/white transition, angular distribution and mechanical alignment throughout its service life.

TraditionalMature lamp-based technology with higher running cost.
LEDSolid-state source with lower running cost but added electronics complexity.

21 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is LED PAPI always more accurate than traditional PAPI?

Not automatically. Photometric and angular alignment requirements are critical for both technologies — accuracy depends on the complete engineered system, not the light source alone.

Does LED PAPI eliminate maintenance?

No. LED reduces lamp-replacement frequency but maintenance still includes optical inspection, alignment checks, driver/electronic inspection and photometric verification.

Can I simply swap a traditional PAPI lamp for an LED unit?

No. A retrofit requires a full compatibility assessment of the existing electrical arrangement and optical geometry, followed by photometric verification, alignment and commissioning.

Does LED PAPI generate no heat?

LEDs generate lower source heat than a lamp, but the driver and electronics still generate heat, so thermal management remains an important design consideration.

Is LED PAPI cheaper overall?

Initial cost is generally higher for LED PAPI, but lifecycle cost is often lower due to reduced energy use and maintenance — the actual economics depend on operating hours, electricity cost and labour.

Do both PAPI types need the same alignment accuracy?

Yes. Alignment and the red/white angular transition are critical for both traditional and LED PAPI — the source technology does not change this requirement.

National CAA / DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

The decisive issue for PAPI is not LED vs halogen, but whether the complete unit maintains required intensity, colour, red/white transition, angular distribution and mechanical alignment throughout its service life.

Traditional PAPI and LED PAPI both deliver the same visual-guidance function; the difference lies in source technology, maintenance, energy use, thermal behaviour and electronics — not in the fundamental photometric objective.
