Cable Maintenance

The dedicated maintenance chapter for the AGL primary cable network — cable routes, joints, terminations and associated physical protection. This division explains how the cable system itself is inspected, maintained, repaired, tested and restored, kept deliberately separate from transformer, CCR and predictive-analysis chapters.

Not just circuit-operating: a cable should not be considered healthy merely because the associated AGL circuit is operating. Physical, electrical, environmental, joint, termination and route condition must each be assessed independently.

Cable Moisture Management

Moisture is treated as a chain to resolve, not a symptom to keep repairing — the underlying ingress mechanism must be identified and corrected.

Principle: do not repeatedly repair the symptom while leaving the water-ingress mechanism unresolved.

Cable Joint Quality Principle

A cable joint should be considered satisfactory only when every element of joint integrity has been addressed — not just continuity.

Electrical integrity Insulation integrity Mechanical integrity Environmental sealing Identification

Rule: a joint that passes continuity but has poor sealing is not a fully satisfactory joint.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about cable maintenance, based on the topics in this division.

9 QUESTIONS
Q1What is cable maintenance in the AGL Maintenance Handbook?

It is the dedicated chapter for the AGL primary cable network, covering cable routes, joints, terminations and associated physical protection, and how the cable system is inspected, maintained, repaired, tested and restored.

Q2What does Division 09 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 09 (Cable Maintenance) contains 33 topics in five groups: Group A Cable Route (9 topics), Group B Cable Condition (5 topics), Group C Cable Joints (8 topics), Group D Termination (5 topics) and Group E Testing & Restoration (6 topics).

Q3Is a cable healthy if the AGL circuit is operating?

No. Physical, electrical, environmental, joint, termination and route condition must each be assessed independently.

Q4What is the cable maintenance sequence?

Eight steps: Identify, Inspect, Protect, Test, Repair, Retest, Restore and Record.

Q5How should a cable circuit fault be diagnosed?

The fault-diagnosis logic stops technicians opening a cable route before narrowing down the fault. After a circuit failure, confirm the CCR output and the circuit, identify the cable section, then check the transformer, primary cable, joints and terminations, carry out electrical tests, locate or confirm the defect, repair or replace, retest, verify function, restore the route and record.

Q6How are cable conditions classified?

Six categories: Good (continue routine PM), Acceptable (monitor), Deteriorating (correct the condition), Defective (repair), Critical (isolate and restore urgently) and High Risk (repeated failures or a worsening trend, condition-based replacement assessment).

Q7How is cable moisture managed?

Moisture is treated as a chain to resolve. Locate it in the cable, joint, termination, duct, trench or pit, identify and correct the source, assess condition, test, restore and trend recurring moisture. The principle is not to repeatedly repair the symptom while leaving the ingress mechanism unresolved.

Q8When is a cable joint satisfactory?

Only when electrical integrity, insulation integrity, mechanical integrity, environmental sealing and identification have all been addressed. A joint that passes continuity but has poor sealing is not fully satisfactory.

Q9What data does cable maintenance feed into predictive maintenance?

It generates insulation resistance and continuity results, test deviations, moisture and flooding events, duct and trench problems, cable, joint and termination failures, repeat repairs and replacement history. Rising fault frequency, declining insulation resistance and increasing moisture events, joint failures or repair frequency feed Division 03.
