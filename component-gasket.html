Gasket

A resilient sealing component that prevents moisture, dust, and contaminants from entering the AGL fixture housing. Made from rubber, silicone, or other elastomeric materials, the gasket ensures the fixture maintains its IP67/IP68 ingress protection rating, safeguarding internal electronics and optics from environmental damage.

Overview

What Is It?

A Gasket is a mechanical seal that fills the space between two or more mating surfaces in an AGL fixture, preventing the ingress of moisture, dust, and other environmental contaminants. Typically made from elastomeric materials such as silicone, EPDM (ethylene propylene diene monomer), nitrile rubber, or fluorosilicone, the gasket is compressed between the fixture's housing and its cover, lens, or cable entry to create a watertight and airtight seal. The gasket is a critical component for maintaining the fixture's IP67 (dust-tight and protected against temporary immersion) or IP68 (dust-tight and protected against continuous immersion) rating, ensuring reliable operation in the harsh outdoor airfield environment.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The gasket's purpose is the same protective function as the O-ring's, applied to flat or irregular mating surfaces rather than round bores: keeping moisture, dust, and contaminants out of an AGL fixture so its electrical and optical components keep working as intended. Because a lighting circuit's outage can take an entire string of runway or taxiway lights out of service, the gasket's seal at a cover joint, cable entry, or cabinet door is a direct line of defence against water ingress that would otherwise corrode terminals or cause electrical faults. Its function depends on being compressed to the correct percentage of its thickness — enough to seal reliably against the groove, but not so much that it damages the housing or loses its elasticity — so that the seal continues to perform through years of temperature cycling, vibration, and exposure to fuels and de-icing chemicals.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Gaskets are used in virtually every AGL fixture and enclosure where environmental sealing is required:

  • Runway Edge & Centreline Lights: Gaskets between the housing and lens/cover to prevent moisture ingress.
  • Threshold & End Lights: Seals for the optical window and cable entry points.
  • Taxiway Lights: Gaskets for the housing and lens assembly.
  • Approach Lighting: Seals for barrette housings and cable glands.
  • PAPI & VASI: Gaskets for the optical units and cable entries.
  • Obstruction Lights: Seals for beacon housings and lens assemblies.
  • Connectors & Cable Entries: Gaskets for cable glands and junction boxes.
  • CCRs & Control Panels: Gaskets for cabinet doors and cable entries.
Placement

Installation & Location

The gasket is seated in a machined groove or against a flat mating face between the two housing halves, lens, or cover it seals, and is compressed evenly by the fastening bolts or retaining ring so it forms a continuous, unbroken seal around the full perimeter. It's fitted dry or with a thin film of compatible silicone grease (never a solvent-based adhesive, which can attack the gasket material) and is replaced, rather than reused, whenever the joint is opened for lamp changes, cleaning, or servicing, since a compressed gasket can take a permanent set and no longer seal reliably once removed. Bolt torque is applied evenly and in sequence across the joint so the gasket compresses uniformly rather than being pinched unevenly on one side and left loose on the other.

Function

How Does It Work?

A gasket works by being compressed between two surfaces, filling the microscopic gaps and irregularities that would otherwise allow water, dust, or air to pass through. The gasket's elastomeric properties allow it to deform under pressure, creating a tight seal. The sealing effectiveness depends on several factors:

  • Material: The elastomer must be resistant to the environmental conditions (UV, ozone, temperature extremes, chemicals).
  • Compression: The gasket must be compressed to the correct percentage (typically 15–30% of its original thickness) to achieve an effective seal without causing stress damage to the housing.
  • Groove Design: The housing must have a properly designed groove or channel that retains the gasket and provides the correct compression.
  • Surface Finish: Mating surfaces must be smooth and free of burrs or damage to ensure uniform compression.

In AGL fixtures, gaskets are often combined with O-rings for rotating or threaded joints, and are designed to withstand high-pressure water jets (IP69K) in some applications.

Construction

Physical Construction

Gaskets are die-cut or moulded to match the exact joint profile they seal — a flat ring for a top-cover-to-housing face, a flanged section for a cable gland, or a custom cut-out for an irregular mating surface. Cross-section and thickness are specified so that, once the joint is torqued to spec, the material compresses to its target percentage (typically 20–30% of free height) without over-compressing into permanent set or under-compressing and leaving a leak path. Most fixture gaskets are retained in a shallow moulded or machined groove in one of the mating faces, which keeps the gasket correctly positioned during assembly and prevents it from extruding sideways under compression. Material selection (silicone, EPDM, nitrile, or fluorosilicone) is matched to the joint's exposure — UV and ozone resistance for an externally visible seal, fuel resistance for an apron-area fixture — since the wrong elastomer can harden or swell well before its rated service life.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Compression Set (Permanent Deformation)

Over time, gaskets can lose their elasticity due to thermal cycling and compression set, resulting in reduced sealing force and moisture ingress. This is a common failure mode. Regular inspection and replacement of gaskets (typically every 3-5 years) is recommended.

UV & Ozone Degradation

Gaskets exposed to direct sunlight and ozone can become brittle, crack, and lose their sealing properties. Use of UV-stabilised materials (silicone, EPDM) is essential for long-term durability in airfield environments.

Chemical Attack from De-icing Fluids

De-icing fluids (ethylene glycol, propylene glycol) and jet fuel can cause swelling or degradation of some elastomers. Always use gasket materials that are compatible with the chemicals used at the airfield.

Improper Installation (Twisting, Pinching)

If the gasket is twisted, pinched, or not seated correctly during installation, it will not seal properly. Follow the manufacturer's installation instructions carefully, and ensure the gasket is clean and free of damage before installation.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Moisture Fogging or Condensation Inside the Fixture
Likely Cause
Gasket has taken a permanent compression set and lost its sealing force, or was twisted/pinched during a previous reassembly and never sealed correctly.
Diagnostic Steps
Remove the cover and inspect the gasket for flattening, permanent deformation, or visible gaps in the seal line; check for twist marks or pinch points against the mating surface.
Corrective Action
Replace the gasket with a new one of the correct material and size, seat it carefully and evenly around the full perimeter, and torque the cover fasteners to the specified sequence and value.
Gasket Brittle, Cracked, or Crumbling
Likely Cause
UV and ozone exposure has degraded a non-UV-stabilised elastomer over time, common with materials not rated for outdoor airfield use.
Diagnostic Steps
Visually inspect for surface cracking, chalking, or crumbling when flexed; confirm the gasket material against the fixture's specified part (silicone, EPDM, etc.).
Corrective Action
Replace with a UV-stabilised material as originally specified; if the wrong material was fitted previously, correct the part number for future replacements.
Gasket Swollen or Softened
Likely Cause
Chemical attack from de-icing fluids or fuel exposure on an elastomer not compatible with those chemicals.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect for swelling, tackiness, or softening compared to an unused reference gasket; identify likely chemical exposure sources near the fixture location.
Corrective Action
Replace with a chemically compatible gasket material for the site's de-icing/fuel exposure profile, and review nearby fixtures for the same risk.
Seal Fails Shortly After a Maintenance Visit
Likely Cause
Gasket twisted, pinched, or reused when it should have been replaced during the last cover removal.
Diagnostic Steps
Remove the cover and check the gasket seating line for pinch marks or twist; confirm whether the gasket was reused past its recommended replacement interval.
Corrective Action
Fit a new, clean, correctly seated gasket rather than reusing the old one, and follow the manufacturer's installation sequence to avoid twisting during cover fitment.

Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Electrical Isolation
Electrical Safety

De-energise and lock out the fixture circuit before opening the housing or cover to replace a gasket, since the seal sits directly against live-adjacent internal components.

Correct Seating & Compression
Mechanical

Seat the replacement gasket fully in its groove without twisting or stretching, and tighten fasteners evenly to the specified pattern and torque; an unevenly compressed gasket is a common source of a re-opened leak path.

Material Compatibility
Compliance

Use only the gasket material specified for the fixture (silicone, EPDM, nitrile, or fluorosilicone as applicable); substituting an incompatible material can fail early under UV, fuel exposure, or temperature extremes.

Verify IP Rating After Reassembly
Compliance

Confirm the housing is fully closed and the seal is intact before re-energising, since a compromised gasket undermines the fixture's IP67/IP68 rating and can lead to a downstream electrical fault.

FOD Control
Operational

Account for old gasket material, fasteners, and packaging when working in the movement area, and remove all debris before leaving the site.

PPE
Personnel Safety

Use insulated tools for any electrical connection work and standard airside PPE when working on or near an active movement area.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Confirm no visible gaps, pinching, or damage to the new gasket before closing out the work and returning the fixture to service.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
A Slow Ingress Fault Often Traces Back Months
Troubleshooting

Moisture ingress from a degraded gasket rarely causes an immediate fault; it more commonly shows up as a gradually worsening insulation resistance reading over several maintenance cycles, so when investigating a developing electrical fault, check gasket condition even if the fixture was serviced without issue at its last visit.

Chemical Exposure Ages Seals Faster Than UV Alone
Durability

Fixtures exposed to repeated glycol-based de-icing fluid or fuel residue see accelerated gasket degradation compared to sun and weather exposure alone; specify chemical-rated material for positions near taxiway/runway intersections or apron stands, and inspect these positions on a shorter interval than fixtures in less exposed locations.

Uneven Fastener Torque Undoes a Correct Gasket
Mechanical

A gasket that's the right material and correctly seated can still fail to seal if fasteners are tightened out of sequence or to uneven torque, since this can pinch or distort the seal at one point while leaving it under-compressed elsewhere; follow the specified tightening pattern rather than simply working around the housing in one pass.

Cold-Weather Installation Needs Extra Care
Installation

Some gasket materials stiffen noticeably in cold conditions, making it easier to crease or pinch the seal during installation without it being obvious at the time; take extra care seating a gasket in cold weather and consider warming the material slightly if the manufacturer permits it, rather than forcing a stiff gasket into place.

Don't Assume Generic Aftermarket Gaskets Are Equivalent
Procurement

A generically-sized aftermarket gasket can match dimensions while using a different compound than the original specification, changing its chemical resistance or temperature range without any visible difference; source replacement gaskets against the fixture manufacturer's material specification rather than dimension alone.

Check the Groove, Not Just the Gasket
Maintenance

A perfectly good new gasket can still fail to seal if the housing groove itself has developed corrosion pitting or debris buildup from the previous seal's failure; clean and inspect the groove surface whenever replacing a gasket, rather than assuming a fresh seal alone resolves a recurring leak.
