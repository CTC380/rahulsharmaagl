Approach Lighting Systems

The visual transition between the approach path and the runway. An Approach Lighting System (ALS) is an arrangement of aerodrome lights installed in the area leading toward a runway threshold, giving pilots visual information about approach alignment, runway direction, threshold location and the transition from instrument approach to visual reference. It's more than a row of lights — it's a coordinated system of light units, supporting structures, electrical circuits, CCRs, isolation transformers, connectors, control, alignment and photometric performance.

Overview

What Is an Approach Lighting System?

An Approach Lighting System provides visual guidance to an aircraft approaching a runway. It extends outward from the runway threshold into the approach area and creates a visual reference that helps the pilot establish the relationship between the aircraft's approach path, the approach lighting, the runway threshold and the runway itself.

The exact configuration depends on the runway, approach procedure, visibility requirements, aerodrome category and applicable standards.

Not Just a Row of Lights

An ALS is a carefully arranged system of light units, structures, circuits, CCRs, transformers, connectors, control and photometric performance.

Progressive Visual Reference

During an instrument approach the pilot may initially have little or no visual reference — the ALS builds progressively stronger visual information as the runway nears.

Alignment Is Everything

Even a correctly functioning light can provide incorrect visual information if it is installed in the wrong position.

Purpose

Why Approach Lighting Is Important

The approach lighting system helps provide progressively stronger visual information as the aircraft approaches the runway, establishing visual continuity between the approach and the runway.

Runway alignment

Centreline and crossbar arrangement establish the visual axis toward the runway.

Runway direction

Approach lighting confirms the correct heading and orientation on final approach.

Approach centreline

Provides the primary reference line the pilot tracks toward the threshold.

Threshold location

Marks precisely where the runway begins.

Distance from the runway

Progressive lighting elements give the pilot a sense of remaining distance.

Transition to the runway

Creates continuity from the approach environment into the runway lighting system.

Core Elements

Main Elements of an Approach Lighting System

An approach lighting system can include combinations of the following elements. The exact arrangement is determined by the applicable ALS configuration.

Approach Centreline Lighting

Establishes the visual axis leading toward the runway and provides an important reference for aircraft alignment. Can incorporate individual light units, light bars, supporting structures and sequenced flashing elements where applicable. Correct positioning and alignment are critical.

Approach Crossbars

Provide lateral visual references across the approach centreline, helping the pilot recognize the approach lighting geometry and maintain visual orientation relative to the runway. Arrangement, spacing and dimensions depend on the applicable ALS configuration.

Sequenced Flashing Lights

Create a visual sequence that leads the pilot's attention toward the runway threshold. Must operate with the correct timing, order and visual relationship to the rest of the approach lighting system.

High-Intensity Approach Lights

Used where enhanced conspicuity is required for the operational category and visibility conditions applicable to the runway.

Supporting Structures

Elevated structures or other approved mounting arrangements that carry approach light fixtures, cabling and, where applicable, frangible elements.

Control & Monitoring Equipment

Coordinates circuit switching, intensity, sequencing and fault monitoring across the complete ALS installation.

Configurations

Approach Lighting Configurations

Approach lighting is not one universal system — different configurations exist for different operational requirements, including simple approach lighting systems, precision approach lighting systems, systems incorporating sequenced flashing lights, high-intensity approach lighting systems, and category-specific approach lighting configurations.

The terminology and detailed configuration should always be checked against the applicable ICAO, FAA, national and project requirements.

Approach Lighting Working Principle & Approach Lighting Pilot Guidance

The approach lighting working principle is pattern recognition. The centreline row shows the runway axis, crossbars give a roll and distance reference, and sequenced flashing, where fitted, draws the eye along the approach. This is approach lighting pilot guidance: it lets the pilot transition from instruments to visual references.

Approach Lighting Visibility

Approach lighting visibility depends on intensity, colour, correct aiming, clean optics and the prevailing weather. A light that is on but misaimed or dirty can still fail the pilot.

ICAO Annex 14 requirements

CAT II ALS & CAT III ALS

CAT II ALS adds side-row lighting near the threshold for lower-visibility operations. CAT III ALS is the most complete pattern, with the highest intensity, for the lowest visibility. Pattern and intensity come from the applicable standard, so confirm them against the approved design.

Circuit Components

CCR, Isolation Transformers & Connectors

Constant Current Regulator

Supplies controlled current to the approach lighting circuit. Design considers number of lights, fixture ratings, transformer ratings, circuit length, total load, required intensity levels, circuit configuration, and control & monitoring requirements. Different ALS circuits may use separate CCRs depending on the system design.

Isolation Transformers

Provide the electrical interface between the primary series circuit and individual approach lights (Series Circuit → Transformer Primary → Transformer Secondary → Approach Light). Selection must be coordinated with the fixture rating and complete circuit design.

Connectors

Provide the electrical connection between the series circuit, isolation transformers and fixtures — primary connectors, secondary connectors, plugs, receptacles, connector kits. Connector condition can directly affect system reliability: loose connections, high resistance, water ingress, insulation deterioration and intermittent contact are common problems.

Verification

Photometry & Alignment

Approach lighting requires careful photometric verification because the lights are viewed from a specific approach direction. A light can be electrically operational while still having low intensity, incorrect beam direction, incorrect colour or abnormal distribution.

Photometry

Evaluates intensity, distribution, colour, alignment, individual fixture performance and system-level performance — helping identify optical faults objectively.

Alignment

Considers horizontal direction, vertical direction, centreline relationship, crossbar relationship, fixture orientation and structure position. An alignment error can create a visual error even when the electrical system is operating normally.

Electrical Testing

Cable continuity, insulation resistance, transformer testing, connector inspection, series-circuit testing, CCR testing, current verification, control verification, flashing sequence verification, monitoring/fault verification and earthing checks where applicable.

Is the circuit energized?

2

3

Check continuity and insulation resistance along the circuit.

4

Test the isolation transformer's primary and secondary performance.

Assess beam direction, distribution and colour.

Quick Reference

Approach Lighting LED, Halogen, Flashers, Wiring & Acceptance Testing

Technology, flashing equipment and wiring decisions affect both performance and maintenance. The acceptance test then proves the finished system.

Approach Lighting Halogen vs LED

Approach lighting halogen fixtures are proven and easy to source; approach lighting LED fixtures typically offer longer life and lower power. Whichever is chosen, it must still meet the photometric requirement.

Approach Lighting Flashers & Flash Sequence

Approach lighting flashers produce the sequenced flash that runs along the approach. The approach lighting flash sequence depends on timing and synchronisation equipment, which needs its own commissioning checks.

Approach Lighting Wiring

Approach lighting wiring follows the series circuit from the CCR through isolation transformers and connectors to each fixture. Correct connector installation and earthing are essential.

System Relationships

Approach Lighting, Runway Lighting & PAPI

One Visual Guidance Chain

Approach lighting and runway lighting should be treated as one visual guidance chain: Approach → Approach Lighting → Threshold → Runway → Runway Lighting. The transition between these systems is particularly important for the pilot's visual acquisition and alignment.

Approach Lighting vs PAPI

Approach lighting and PAPI perform different functions. Approach lighting provides visual references for approach alignment, runway direction, threshold identification and transition toward the runway. PAPI provides visual information about the vertical approach path / glide path. They complement one another rather than perform the same function.

Keyword Reference

Approach Lighting for CAT I, CAT II & CAT III

How the approach lighting pattern changes with the category of operation.

Approach lighting for CAT I

Approach lighting for CAT I supports precision approaches with the lowest category of visibility minima. It gives the pilot a clear centreline and distance cues leading to the threshold. Pattern, length and intensity must follow ICAO Annex 14 or the national standard the aerodrome uses.

Approach lighting for CAT II

Approach lighting for CAT II adds to the CAT I pattern, notably with side-row lighting near the threshold, to give better roll and lateral guidance in lower visibility. Intensity, serviceability and power supply requirements are tighter than for CAT I.

Approach lighting for CAT III

Approach lighting for CAT III supports operations in the lowest visibility. It builds on the CAT II pattern and carries the most demanding requirements for intensity, serviceability and secondary power. Confirm the exact pattern and performance limits against the standard you follow. See the Approach Lighting System Types section above.

Keyword Reference

Approach Lighting Types, Functions & LED Approach Lights

What the main kinds of approach lighting do and how LED fits in.

Approach lighting types

Approach lighting types are grouped by the kind of approach they serve, such as non-precision, CAT I and CAT II/III patterns, and by the layout and light units used, including centreline barrettes, crossbars, side rows and sequenced flashing lights. Naming varies between ICAO, FAA and national systems.

Approach lighting functions

Approach lighting gives the pilot visual guidance in the final approach: alignment with the runway centreline, a sense of roll, and distance cues to the threshold. It helps the pilot transition from instruments to visual references and decide whether to continue the landing.

LED approach lights

LED approach lights replace halogen light sources with LED units. Typical attractions are lower power use, long life and fewer lamp changes. They must still meet the required photometric performance and work correctly on the series circuit and with the CCR, so compatibility and approval need to be checked. See the Approach Lighting LED, Halogen, Flashers, Wiring & Acceptance Testing section above.

Keyword Reference

Power Supply, ICAO Standards, Selection Guide & Technical Guide

The supply, the rules and the practical way to choose and understand the system.

Approach lighting power supply

Approach lighting is normally fed from a CCR on a constant-current series circuit, with isolation transformers at each light. Higher categories of operation also require secondary power and defined changeover arrangements, so the supply design should be checked against the category served and the standard followed.

Approach lighting ICAO standards

The main ICAO references are Annex 14 Volume I for the approach lighting specifications and the ICAO Aerodrome Design Manual for guidance on visual aids and electrical systems. FAA advisory circulars are often used alongside, and DGCA requirements apply in India. Confirm the editions that apply to your project.

Approach lighting selection guide

To select an approach lighting system, start with the runway and approach category, then check the standard, the available space and structures, the power supply, the light technology and the maintenance plan. Compare the LED and halogen options on photometry, compatibility and total life cost.

Approach lighting technical guide

As a technical guide, an approach lighting system is a set of light units on structures, fed by series circuits, controlled from the CCR and control system and checked by alignment and photometry. Each section on this page covers one link in that chain.

Keyword Reference

Approach Lighting Testing Procedure, Troubleshooting & Preventive Maintenance

Proving the system, finding faults and keeping it serviceable.

Approach lighting testing procedure

A testing procedure normally covers visual inspection, insulation and continuity checks, circuit current and brightness-step checks, flasher operation, control checks, and alignment and photometric verification. Test values and acceptance criteria come from the project specification and standard, so none are given here. See Photometry & Alignment above.

Approach lighting troubleshooting

Troubleshoot by symptom: one light out, a section out, flashers not sequencing or a whole circuit tripped. Check connectors, lamps or LED units, transformers and circuit insulation before replacing equipment. See Common Approach Lighting Faults and A Practical Troubleshooting Approach above.

Approach lighting preventive maintenance

Preventive maintenance covers regular inspection of lights and structures, cleaning, alignment checks, insulation trending, connector checks and records of faults, so repeated problems become visible. The interval should follow your maintenance plan and the aerodrome's requirements.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is an Approach Lighting System (ALS)?

An Approach Lighting System is an arrangement of aerodrome lights installed in the area leading toward a runway threshold. It gives pilots visual information about approach alignment, runway direction, threshold location and the transition from instrument approach to visual reference. The exact configuration depends on the runway, approach procedure, visibility requirements, aerodrome category and applicable standards.

Q2Why is approach lighting important?

It provides progressively stronger visual information as the aircraft nears the runway, establishing visual continuity between the approach and the runway. It helps the pilot judge runway alignment, runway direction, the approach centreline, threshold location, distance from the runway and the transition into the runway lighting system.

Q3What are the main elements of an approach lighting system?

Depending on the configuration, an ALS can include approach centreline lighting, approach crossbars, sequenced flashing lights, high-intensity approach lights, supporting structures, and control and monitoring equipment. The exact arrangement is determined by the applicable ALS configuration.

Q4What are sequenced flashing lights in an ALS?

Sequenced flashing lights create a visual sequence that leads the pilot's attention toward the runway threshold, flashing in order from the outer approach toward the threshold. They must operate with the correct timing, order and visual relationship to the rest of the approach lighting system.

Q5Is there one universal approach lighting configuration?

No. Different configurations exist for different operational requirements, including simple approach lighting systems, precision approach lighting systems, systems with sequenced flashing lights, high-intensity systems and category-specific configurations. Terminology and detailed configuration should always be checked against the applicable ICAO, FAA, national and project requirements.

Q6What colour is approach lighting?

Approach lighting commonly uses white light for the principal approach-light elements, while specific associated lighting functions can have different colour requirements. Colour should always be evaluated against the particular ALS configuration and applicable standard.

Q7Why does geometry and alignment matter so much in approach lighting?

Even a correctly functioning light can give incorrect visual information if it is installed in the wrong position. Approach lighting depends on the runway centreline, threshold location, approach centreline, longitudinal spacing, crossbar position, lateral positioning, approach direction, supporting structure location, terrain, obstacles and the aircraft's visual perspective.

Q8What do the supporting structures for approach lights do?

Supporting structures are elevated structures or other approved mounting arrangements that carry approach light fixtures, cabling and, where applicable, frangible elements. Structural design may need to consider fixture mounting, stability, height, alignment and wind loading.

Q9What is the electrical chain behind an approach lighting system?

A simplified chain runs from the airport electrical supply and AGL control system to the CCR, series circuit and isolation transformers, then to the approach light fixtures, their optical output and finally the pilot's visual reference. More complex installations can add sequenced flashing, dedicated control equipment, monitoring and multiple circuits.

Q10How does an ALS relate to the rest of runway lighting?

The approach lighting system creates the transition from the approach environment into the runway lighting system, so it should be considered as part of the overall runway visual guidance rather than in isolation.

Q11What are the approach lighting system types?

The main approach lighting system types are non precision approach lighting and the precision CAT I ALS, CAT II ALS and CAT III ALS patterns, with the choice set by runway type and approach category.

Q12What is the approach lighting working principle?

Approach lighting gives pilots a recognisable pattern of lights leading to the threshold, so they can align with the runway and judge distance when they transition from instruments to visual flight.

Q13What does an approach lighting acceptance test include?

Visual, electrical, alignment, photometric and functional checks, followed by an approach lighting test report recording measured against required values.
