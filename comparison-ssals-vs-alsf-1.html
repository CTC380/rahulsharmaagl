Approach & Landing Systems Comparison

SSALS VS ALSF-1

Simplified Short Approach Lighting System vs Approach Lighting System with Sequenced Flashing Lights — both are approach lighting systems, but they are designed around different levels of visual guidance and different regulatory design philosophies. SSALS is a simplified, shorter configuration; ALSF-1 is a high-intensity system with sequenced flashing lights, typically associated with CAT I precision approaches in FAA practice.

02 · Definitions

What Is SSALS & ALSF-1?

SSALS

SSALS stands for Simplified Short Approach Lighting System. It is intended to provide basic visual guidance to an aircraft approaching the runway. The system extends visual references beyond the runway threshold into the approach area. The defining characteristic is its short and simplified configuration.

Main functions

  • Runway alignment
  • Centreline identification
  • Threshold acquisition
  • Visual transition during the final approach

ALSF-1

ALSF-1 stands for Approach Lighting System with Sequenced Flashing Lights. It is a high-intensity approach lighting system designed to provide substantially more visual information to the pilot during a precision approach. In FAA practice, ALSF-1 is generally associated with CAT I precision approach lighting.

Key characteristics

  • High-intensity approach lights
  • Centreline lighting
  • Crossbars
  • Sequenced flashing lights
  • Red inner approach references
03 · Key Difference

Short + Simplified vs High Intensity + Sequenced Flashing

The simplest way to remember the difference between SSALS and ALSF-1:

SSALS

Short + Simplified + Basic Visual Guidance

ALSF-1

High Intensity + Extended Configuration + Sequenced Flashing

Engineering point: The difference is not simply the number of lamps. It is the overall configuration and level of visual information provided to the pilot.

05 · Sequenced Flashers

Why Does ALSF-1 Need Sequenced Flashers?

The sequenced flashers create an apparent moving-light effect toward the runway threshold. This helps the pilot identify runway direction, establish alignment, acquire the runway environment, and transition from instrument references to visual references.

Visual sequence: Outer approach → flash → flash → flash → threshold. SSALS does not provide this same sequenced-flashing function.

06 · Visual Guidance

How Each System Guides the Pilot

SSALS provides

  • Approach centreline
  • Basic alignment reference
  • Threshold acquisition
  • Runway

ALSF-1 provides

  • Approach centreline
  • Crossbar references
  • Sequenced flashing
  • Red inner references
  • Threshold and runway

Therefore, ALSF-1 provides a considerably richer visual pattern.

07 · Approach Coverage

System Length & When Each Applies

One of the major practical differences is the length of the approach lighting installation.

SSALS

The system is intentionally short and simplified. Useful where:

  • Approach area is restricted
  • A simplified lighting system is appropriate
  • Terrain limits installation
  • Land availability is limited
  • Full ALS installation is not required

ALSF-1

The system provides a much more extensive approach-lighting arrangement intended to support precision approach operations.

Simplified ALSF-1 electrical arrangement: CCR → Primary Series Circuit → Isolation Transformers → Approach Light Fixtures, with a parallel Sequenced Flasher Control path. SSALS generally has a simpler electrical arrangement because the sequenced flasher system is not the defining part of the system.

09 · CCR Considerations

Sizing the Constant Current Regulator

For either system, the CCR must be selected based on the actual circuit design, considering:

  • Number of fixtures
  • Fixture wattage
  • Transformer ratings
  • Circuit length
  • Cable resistance
  • Voltage drop
  • Total circuit load
  • Required intensity steps
  • Control architecture

SSALS — generally fewer fixtures mean a lower connected load, subject to the actual equipment used. ALSF-1 — the larger number of fixtures and additional flashing equipment result in a more involved electrical and control design.

10 · Photometry

Photometric Considerations

Both systems require verification against the applicable photometric requirements.

SSALS checks

  • Light intensity
  • Centreline alignment
  • Light distribution
  • Colour
  • Fixture orientation

ALSF-1 additionally checks

  • Sequenced flasher performance
  • Red-light performance
  • Crossbar performance
  • Inner approach alignment
  • Flashing sequence

A fixture being electrically operational does not automatically mean it is correctly aimed or photometrically compliant.

11 · Installation

Installation Considerations

SSALS installation involves

  • Approach centreline
  • Light units
  • Applicable crossbar
  • Supports
  • Cable network
  • Isolation transformers
  • Frangible mounting arrangements

ALSF-1 additionally requires

  • Sequenced flashers
  • Flasher control
  • More extensive light positions
  • Red inner lighting
  • Crossbars and centreline
  • Control and monitoring

The civil and electrical coordination is therefore more demanding for ALSF-1.

13 · Practical Selection

SSALS vs ALSF-1: When to Choose Each

Select SSALS when the approved design requires

  • Simplified approach guidance
  • Shorter approach lighting
  • Lower installation complexity
  • Limited approach-area availability
  • A simplified visual reference system

Select ALSF-1 when the approved design requires

  • High-intensity approach lighting
  • Precision approach visual guidance
  • Sequenced flashing
  • Extended approach visual references
  • CAT I-oriented FAA approach lighting configuration

The final selection must always be based on the applicable aerodrome standard and approved airport design.

14 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: SSALS is just a low-intensity ALSF-1
Fact

No. They are different approach-lighting configurations. The distinction is primarily configuration, extent and system function.

Myth: SSALS has sequenced flashers
Fact

Sequenced flashing lights are a defining feature of ALSF-1, not SSALS.

Myth: ALSF-1 is simply a longer SSALS
Fact

ALSF-1 adds significant visual and functional features, particularly sequenced flashing and the more extensive inner approach configuration.

Myth: SSALS automatically means CAT I
Fact

Approach category and approach-lighting configuration are separate concepts.

Myth: More lights automatically mean a higher approach category
Fact

The approach category is determined by the complete operational system and applicable regulatory criteria.

16 · Engineering Workflow

From Requirement to Commissioning

Determine approach requirement → identify applicable standard → select SSALS/ALSF-1 → prepare approach layout → determine light positions → design series circuit → select CCR → select isolation transformers → design primary & secondary cables → (ALSF-1: design sequenced flasher system) → install fixtures → alignment → electrical testing → photometric testing → functional testing → commissioning.

17 · Engineering Verdict

Which System Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

SSALS is a shorter, simpler approach visual guidance system with a lower infrastructure requirement. ALSF-1 is a high-intensity, more extensive approach lighting system with sequenced flashers and enhanced inner approach visual references.

The final selection must always be based on the applicable aerodrome standard, approach category and approved airport design.

SSALSShort + Simplified — lower infrastructure requirement.
ALSF-1High Intensity + Sequenced Flashing — enhanced visual guidance.
18 · Important Standards Note

Don't Directly Equate ICAO and FAA Terminology

Do not directly equate SSALS with an FAA ALSF classification. SALS/SSALS terminology is commonly encountered in ICAO-based aerodrome lighting classifications, while ALSF-1 is an FAA approach-lighting configuration.

Therefore, for an international airport project, first identify whether the project is being designed to ICAO requirements, FAA requirements, national CAA requirements, or airport-specific requirements — then use the applicable configuration and dimensional criteria.

19 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Does SSALS have sequenced flashing lights?

No. Sequenced flashing lights are a defining feature of ALSF-1, not SSALS.

Is SSALS equivalent to ALSF-1?

No. They are different approach-lighting configurations from different regulatory design philosophies and should not be treated as direct equivalents.

Is ALSF-1 always CAT I?

In FAA practice, ALSF-1 is typically associated with CAT I precision approach lighting, but the applicable operational category depends on the complete approach system and regulatory approval.

Why does ALSF-1 need more maintenance than SSALS?

ALSF-1 has additional components — sequenced flashers, flasher synchronization and red inner lighting — that require dedicated inspection and testing beyond what SSALS needs.

Can SSALS be used on the same runway design basis as ALSF-1?

Only if the applicable aerodrome standard and approved airport design permit it. The systems come from different classification frameworks (ICAO vs FAA) and should not be substituted without verifying the governing standard.

Is a shorter system always cheaper?

Generally SSALS has lower installation cost, fewer components and lower maintenance requirements, but final selection must always follow the governing standard rather than cost alone.

National CAA / DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

SSALS and ALSF-1 should not be treated as direct equivalents. For final design, use the configuration drawings and dimensional criteria from the applicable current standard rather than a generic comparison page.

SSALS → Short + Simplified + Basic visual guidance. ALSF-1 → High-intensity + Extended + Sequenced flashing + Enhanced visual guidance.
