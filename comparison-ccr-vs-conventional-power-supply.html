CCR VS Conventional Power Supply

Constant Current Regulator vs Conventional Power Supply — a CCR supplies a controlled, constant current to series-connected airfield lighting circuits, while a conventional power supply typically maintains a fixed voltage and lets current vary with the connected load. The distinction between "current-controlled" and "voltage-controlled" is the foundation of how airfield series circuits are designed, protected and maintained.

02 · Definitions

What Is a CCR & a Conventional Power Supply?

CCR

CCR stands for Constant Current Regulator. It supplies a controlled, constant current to the series-connected airfield lighting circuit it feeds, regardless of moderate changes in the circuit's electrical characteristics. Rather than fixing the output voltage, the CCR adjusts its output voltage as needed so that the current flowing through the series loop stays at the selected regulated value.

Main functions

  • Regulate current through the series lighting loop
  • Provide selectable brightness/intensity steps
  • Sustain stable output over long cable runs
  • Interface with isolation transformers and fixtures

Conventional Power Supply

A conventional power supply typically maintains a fixed output voltage at its terminals. Loads are commonly connected in parallel across this voltage, and the current drawn by each branch depends on that branch's own load characteristics rather than on a shared regulated current.

Main functions

  • Maintain a stable output voltage
  • Feed parallel-connected general loads
  • Allow branches to operate independently
  • Support standard electrical distribution
03 · Key Difference

Current-Controlled vs Voltage-Controlled

The easiest way to remember the difference between a CCR and a conventional power supply:

CCR

Controls current → series lighting circuit

Conventional Supply

Controls voltage → load current varies

Engineering point: Easy memory — CCR → "I" (current) is the controlled quantity; conventional supply → "V" (voltage) is the controlled quantity. Everything else about series vs parallel circuit behaviour follows from this single distinction.

04 · Circuit Architecture

Series vs Parallel Circuit Arrangement

This is one of the most important practical distinctions between the two approaches.

CCR-based series circuit

The CCR feeds a single series loop. Every fixture on that loop is connected in the same current path, so the same regulated current flows through each lighting unit in turn before returning to the regulator.

Conventional parallel distribution

The supply feeds multiple branches connected across the same voltage. Each branch draws its own current independently, so the loads are not electrically dependent on one another in the same way series-connected fixtures are.

Circuit Element CCR / Series Conventional / Parallel

Shared voltageVoltage divides across fixturesSame voltage across all branches
Branch independenceFixtures share one current pathBranches operate independently
Typical isolation methodSeries isolation transformers per fixtureDirect or locally protected connection
05 · Why Series Lighting Uses a CCR

Why Airfield Circuits Rely on Constant Current

Airfield lighting installations can contain many fixtures distributed over long distances. A CCR-fed series circuit lets the regulator establish the desired circuit current despite changes in the circuit's electrical characteristics within its operating range — which is difficult to achieve reliably with a fixed-voltage, parallel arrangement stretched over similar distances. Reasons airfield lighting favours a current-controlled series approach include:

  • Large number of fixtures spread over long cable runs
  • Need for uniform light intensity across every fixture on the circuit
  • Ability to change intensity for the whole circuit from one regulator
  • Reduced sensitivity to cable voltage drop over distance
  • Compatibility with isolation transformers at each fixture

A conventional voltage supply remains well suited to general parallel loads, but is less commonly used for long, uniform-intensity series airfield lighting circuits.

06 · Electrical Behaviour

How Each System Responds to Changing Load

CCR behaviour

The CCR holds the circuit current at the selected regulated level. If the load's electrical characteristics change within the regulator's operating range, the CCR adjusts its output voltage to keep the current constant.

  • Current held constant
  • Output voltage adjusts as needed
  • Operates within a defined regulation range

Conventional supply behaviour

The supply holds its output voltage constant. For a simplified resistive load, current follows I = V / R — so if the load resistance changes while voltage stays fixed, the current drawn changes accordingly.

  • Voltage held constant
  • Current varies with load
  • No shared current regulation across branches

08 · Intensity Control

CCR Brightness / Intensity Step Control

One major benefit of a CCR is the ability to select different regulated current levels for the same series circuit, giving the whole circuit a uniform brightness step without touching individual fixtures.

CCR Current Setting Resulting Light Output
Low currentLower light output
Medium currentMedium light output
High currentHigher light output

The exact current values and number of steps depend on the particular airfield lighting system and regulator equipment. A conventional voltage supply generally requires separate control equipment at each load to achieve the same effect.

09 · Isolation & Long Circuits

Transformer-Isolated Series Lighting

Airfield series lighting fed by a CCR commonly uses an isolation transformer at each individual light fixture. The series circuit carries the regulated current, while the isolation transformers provide the appropriate interface — matched primary/secondary current rating — to each lighting unit without breaking the series loop if a fixture is removed.

Simplified circuit architecture: CCR → Series Circuit → Isolation Transformers → Lighting Fixtures. In a conventional parallel arrangement, this transformer-per-fixture pattern is not typically required, since branches connect more directly to the distribution voltage.

10 · Fault & Failure Behaviour

Open-Circuit and Load-Change Considerations

In a series circuit, an open circuit at any single point interrupts current through the entire loop — every fixture downstream of the fault can be affected, not just the failed unit. Because of this, CCR-fed airfield lighting systems incorporate appropriate monitoring, protection and circuit design to detect and manage faults, such as open-circuit detection and load-matching between the CCR and the connected fixtures.

A conventional parallel system behaves differently: individual branches can remain electrically independent, so a fault on one branch does not necessarily interrupt the others in the same way.

Design implication: Series-circuit fault behaviour is a key reason CCR systems are engineered with dedicated monitoring and protection, distinct from the branch-level protection typically used on conventional parallel distribution.

11 · Installation Considerations

Installation Considerations

Before installation, the following should be coordinated for a CCR-fed series circuit: CCR sizing and current rating, number and rating of isolation transformers, series-loop cable route and insulation class, earthing/bonding, CCR room location, and control/monitoring interfacing.

For a conventional parallel supply, the corresponding considerations are supply voltage and capacity, branch circuit protection, cable sizing for voltage drop over distance, and standard distribution panel design.

15 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: A CCR is just a transformer with extra steps
Fact

A CCR regulates current to a selected value across changing load conditions; an isolation transformer simply interfaces individual fixtures to the series circuit without regulating current itself.

Myth: A conventional power supply could replace a CCR for airfield lighting without design changes
Fact

A voltage-controlled parallel supply does not provide the shared regulated current that series-connected airfield fixtures and isolation transformers are designed around, so replacing a CCR requires a full circuit redesign, not a like-for-like swap.

Myth: Higher CCR current always means higher voltage output
Fact

The CCR's output voltage depends on the connected circuit's electrical characteristics at that current setting, not on the current setting alone.

Myth: An open circuit only affects the failed fixture
Fact

In a series circuit, an open circuit interrupts current through the whole loop, which is why CCR systems require dedicated fault monitoring and protection.

16 · Engineering Selection

Choosing the Right Supply for a Circuit

When deciding whether a load should be fed from a CCR-based series circuit or a conventional parallel supply, consider:

  • Whether the load is airfield series lighting or a general electrical load
  • Number of fixtures and required intensity uniformity
  • Cable run length and voltage-drop sensitivity
  • Need for centralised brightness-step control
  • Applicable ICAO Annex 14 / national CAA requirements
  • Fault monitoring and protection requirements
  • Compatibility with isolation transformers
  • Maintenance and testing regime
17 · Engineering Workflow

From Requirement to Commissioning

Determine circuit type and intensity requirement → select CCR rating and step configuration (or conventional supply capacity for parallel loads) → design series circuit / distribution branches → select isolation transformers where applicable → design primary and secondary cabling → install fixtures/loads → electrical testing → insulation resistance and protection testing → photometric or functional testing → commissioning.

18 · Engineering Verdict

Which Supply Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

A CCR and a conventional power supply solve different problems. The CCR regulates current for series-connected airfield lighting circuits, giving uniform, adjustable intensity over long cable runs. A conventional power supply maintains voltage for parallel-connected general loads, where each branch draws its own current.

The final selection should always follow the applicable ICAO/national standard, the circuit's electrical design, and the approved airport electrical design — not a generic comparison alone.

CCRConstant, regulated current for series airfield lighting circuits.

19 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a CCR just a special type of transformer?

No. A CCR is a regulator that controls the current supplied to a series circuit; isolation transformers are separate devices used to interface individual fixtures to that series circuit.

What is the biggest difference between a CCR and a conventional power supply?

A CCR regulates and holds current constant for a series circuit, while a conventional power supply holds voltage constant and lets current vary with the connected parallel load.

Why does airfield lighting use CCRs instead of conventional supplies?

Series circuits fed by a CCR are well suited to long cable runs with many fixtures, since the regulator maintains uniform current — and therefore uniform intensity — across the whole circuit despite distance and load changes within its operating range.

Can a conventional power supply be used for airfield series lighting?

Not directly for a series circuit designed around a CCR. Series-connected fixtures and isolation transformers are engineered around a shared regulated current, so using a conventional voltage supply would require a different circuit design entirely.

How does a CCR control light intensity?

By stepping its regulated output current up or down between selectable levels, which raises or lowers the light output of every fixture on that series circuit together.

What happens if there's an open circuit in a CCR-fed series loop?

An open circuit interrupts current through the whole series loop, which is why CCR-fed systems incorporate dedicated open-circuit detection, monitoring and protection as part of the circuit design.

20 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual airport project, verify the current applicable editions of the relevant standards before using this comparison for project design.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — electrical power supply and series-circuit requirements for airfield ground lighting systems.

ICAO Aerodrome Design Manual

Applicable material covering CCR selection, series circuit design and electrical configuration guidance for airfield lighting.

IEC 61820-3-2 — CCR Specification

Electrical installations for lighting and beaconing of aerodromes — Constant Current Regulators. Traditionally specifies CCR requirements for a nominal 6.6A output on an aeronautical ground lighting series circuit. Note: IEC 61822:2009 was withdrawn in 2023 and superseded by IEC 61820-3-2:2023 — confirm the edition your project references.

FAA AC 150/5345-10

Specification for Constant Current Regulators and Regulator Monitors — the FAA equipment spec covering L-828/L-829 CCRs used in place of the ICAO/IEC framework on FAA-governed projects.

Final Engineering Note

CCR rating, step configuration and exact circuit design requirements can vary with the governing standard and specific airport electrical design. For final design, use the equipment schedules and approved circuit drawings rather than a generic comparison page.

A CCR regulates current for series-connected airfield lighting circuits; a conventional power supply maintains voltage for parallel-connected general loads. The applicable current setting or load rating should always come from the approved project design.
