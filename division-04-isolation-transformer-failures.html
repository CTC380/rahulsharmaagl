Isolation Transformer Failures

The dedicated failure-intelligence section for AGL isolating transformers — covering electrical, thermal, moisture, winding, mechanical, connection, insulation, installation and reliability-related failures. Do not record an isolating transformer simply as “failed.” Identify the failure mode, physical evidence, electrical condition, probable cause, root cause, corrective action and recurrence risk.

Initial Issue

Key principle — The first seven categories — insulation breakdown, overheating, water ingress, winding failure, core damage, connector failure and potting-compound degradation — are the specifically established starting categories for isolation transformer failures. Every record should answer: what failed → how did it fail → why did it fail → what evidence confirmed it → how was it corrected → how can recurrence be prevented?

ITF-01 Insulation Breakdown
Failure Definition

Failure of the transformer insulation system resulting in reduced electrical isolation between windings, conductors, core or earth.

Typical Symptoms
  • Low insulation resistance
  • Earth fault
  • Leakage current
  • Abnormal electrical output
  • Transformer overheating
  • Circuit protection operation
  • Complete transformer failure
Possible Causes
  • Ageing
  • Thermal deterioration
  • Moisture/water ingress
  • Electrical overstress
  • Manufacturing defect
  • Mechanical damage
  • Potting degradation
  • Environmental deterioration
Investigation
  • Insulation resistance
  • Primary-to-secondary insulation
  • Secondary-to-earth condition
  • Physical transformer condition
  • Moisture evidence
  • Potting condition
  • Temperature history
  • Previous failure history
Evidence
  • IR test readings
  • Transformer photographs
  • Damaged insulation photographs
  • Test report
  • Environmental condition
  • Operating history
ITF-02 Overheating
Failure Definition

Transformer temperature exceeds its normal operating condition.

Symptoms
  • Excessive temperature
  • Discolouration
  • Burning smell
  • Insulation deterioration
  • Potting degradation
  • Reduced transformer life
  • Abnormal output
  • Premature failure
Possible Causes
  • Excessive load
  • Abnormal current
  • Poor thermal dissipation
  • Installation condition
  • High ambient temperature
  • Internal winding problem
  • Electrical fault
  • Repeated overload condition
Investigation

Record transformer operating temperature, load/current, ambient temperature, installation environment, physical condition and previous temperature-related events.

Failure Chain
ABNORMAL LOADINGHEATINGINSULATION DEGRADATIONWINDING DAMAGETRANSFORMER FAILURE

Symptoms

  • Low insulation resistance

  • Abnormal output
  • Premature failure
Investigation

  • Moisture

ITF-07 Potting-Compound Degradation
Note

Potting-compound degradation is specifically identified as a primary transformer failure category.

Failure Definition

Deterioration of the encapsulating/potting material that protects internal transformer components.

Failure Modes
  • Cracking
  • Shrinkage
  • Separation
  • Softening
  • Voids
  • Loss of sealing
  • Thermal degradation
Consequences

Potting deterioration may expose internal components to moisture, contamination, mechanical stress and thermal cycling.

ITF-09 Secondary-Lead Failure
Failure Definition

Failure of the transformer secondary lead or connection to the associated fixture/load.

Failure Modes
  • Open secondary lead
  • Broken conductor
  • Insulation damage
  • Loose termination
  • Connector failure
  • High resistance
  • Moisture damage
Symptoms

  • Intermittent fixture operation

  • Circuit continuity problem
Important Distinction

If the transformer itself is healthy but its secondary lead has failed, the failure record should identify Secondary-lead failure rather than incorrectly classifying the entire transformer as defective.

ITF-14 Moisture Contamination
Important Distinction

Water ingress is the mechanism by which water enters. Moisture contamination is the resulting condition affecting the transformer. This distinction improves the accuracy of the RCA database.

Possible Effects
  • Insulation deterioration
  • Corrosion
  • Leakage current
  • Reduced insulation resistance
  • Electrical failure
  • Potting deterioration
Recommended Failure Chain
FAILED SEALING / WATER INGRESSMOISTURE CONTAMINATIONINSULATION DETERIORATIONELECTRICAL FAILURE

Failure Definition

Complete interruption of the intended transformer electrical path.

Possible Locations
  • Primary lead
  • Primary winding
  • Secondary winding
  • Secondary lead
  • Terminal
  • Connector
Symptoms

  • No secondary output

ITF-16 Short Circuit
Failure Definition

Unintended low-resistance electrical path within or associated with the transformer.

Possible Causes
  • Winding insulation failure
  • Lead insulation damage
  • Moisture
  • Connector damage
  • Internal electrical fault
Symptoms
  • Excessive current
  • CCR protection trip
  • Earth fault
  • Circuit outage
  • Transformer overheating
Investigation

Check the transformer separately from the rest of the series circuit to avoid misdiagnosis.

ITF-17 High-Temperature Operation
Failure Definition

Transformer repeatedly operates at an abnormally high temperature, even if immediate failure has not yet occurred.

Possible Causes
  • Excessive load
  • Poor installation condition
  • High ambient temperature
  • Internal winding problem
  • Electrical abnormality
  • Thermal deterioration
Why This Deserves a Separate Category

Overheating can describe an observed failure event. High-temperature operation can describe a persistent operating condition that may eventually cause failure.

Failure Progression
HIGH-TEMPERATURE OPERATIONINSULATION AGEINGWINDING DEGRADATIONPREMATURE TRANSFORMER FAILURE

Possible Causes

  • Poor material
  • Design weakness

  • Excessive electrical stress

  • Environmental exposure
  • Incorrect application

  • Operating hours

Moderate — reduced performance, not yet critical

Minor / Non-Critical — limited or no operational effect

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about AGL isolating transformer failures, as covered in Division 04 of the AGL Failure Database.

Q1What is Division 04 — Isolation Transformer Failures in the AGL Failure Database?

Division 04 covers failures of AGL isolating transformers. It contains 20 published topics (ITF-01 to ITF-20) covering insulation, winding, core, connector, lead, potting, housing, moisture, thermal and installation-related failures.

Q2Which isolation transformer failures does Division 04 cover?

Insulation breakdown, overheating, water ingress, winding failure, core damage, connector failure, potting-compound degradation, primary-lead failure, secondary-lead failure, terminal failure, mechanical damage, housing damage, corrosion, moisture contamination, open circuit, short circuit, high-temperature operation, abnormal electrical output, installation-related failure and premature transformer failure.

Q3How are the 20 transformer topics grouped?

They fall into six groups: Insulation, Winding & Core Failures (5 topics), Connection & Lead Failures (5), Mechanical & Environmental Failures (4), Electrical Circuit Failures (2), Thermal & Performance Failures (2) and Installation & Reliability Failures (2).

Q4What failure chains does Division 04 capture?

Division 04 records relationships between failure modes rather than treating each as isolated. In the moisture chain, water ingress leads to moisture contamination, corrosion, insulation deterioration, insulation breakdown, an earth fault or short circuit and finally transformer failure. In the thermal chain, high-temperature operation leads to overheating, insulation degradation, winding deterioration, winding failure and abnormal electrical output.

Q5Where should the investigation start for each transformer failure?

The diagnostic matrix gives a primary investigation for each failure. Insulation breakdown (low IR) starts with insulation testing, overheating with load and thermal condition, water ingress with housing, sealing and environment, winding failure with winding condition, core damage with physical and core condition, connector failure with connector and contact, and potting degradation with potting condition.

Q6What should be recorded for an isolation transformer failure?

Each record should cover transformer identification (location, manufacturer, model, rating, serial/batch number, installation date, operating age or hours), failure information, and testing such as primary and secondary continuity, insulation resistance and electrical output. Visual inspection, temperature condition, connector and lead condition, and photographs of the transformer, housing, leads, potting and corrosion are also recorded.

Q7How does a transformer failure become engineering intelligence?

Every failure follows a loop: failure event, failure mode, failure mechanism, root cause, corrective action, preventive action and post-repair test. For example, a fixture outage is traced to a transformer open circuit, then to secondary winding failure and thermal degradation, leading to transformer replacement, a review of thermal and loading condition, and a post-repair electrical test.

Q8What evidence supports a transformer failure record?

Visual evidence includes photographs of the transformer, housing, nameplate, leads, connectors, terminals, potting, corrosion, water ingress and damaged components. Technical evidence includes insulation resistance, primary and secondary continuity, electrical output, temperature history, test reports, operating history and maintenance records.

Q9How are failure severity and operational impact classified?

Every failure receives a severity classification, plus operational-impact tagging. The classes are Critical / Safety-Critical (immediate operational or safety risk), Major / Operationally Critical (significant availability impact), Moderate (reduced performance, not yet critical), Minor / Non-Critical (limited or no operational effect), and Degradation / Intermittent / Latent (a developing or hidden fault).

Q10How does the Failure Database differ from the Troubleshooting Atlas?

The Troubleshooting Atlas answers “I have a fault now — how do I diagnose and fix it?” The Failure Database answers “this failure happened — what happened, why, what evidence proves it, what was done, and how do we stop it recurring?” Kept strictly separate, then linked end to end, the two form an AGL Failure Intelligence Engine rather than a simple fault list.

Winding failures, moisture ingress, thermal breakdown and mismatched loading all look similar from the outside — real troubleshooting means understanding isolating transformer construction and loading limits well enough to prevent the next one from failing the same way.
