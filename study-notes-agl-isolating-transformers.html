WHAT IS AN AGL ISOLATING TRANSFORMER?

The AGL isolating transformer is one of the most important field components in a conventional series-circuit lighting system. It is the electrical interface between the primary AGL series circuit and an individual light fixture or other field load.

In simple terms
  • The CCR controls the series-circuit current
  • The isolating transformer transfers power from the primary circuit to the individual fitting and provides electrical isolation

For FAA-based systems, series-to-series isolation transformers are covered by FAA AC 150/5345-47C. It identifies L-830 transformers for 60 Hz and L-831 transformers for 50 Hz.

Main purposes

  1. Isolate the individual fitting from the primary series circuit.
  2. Transfer power from the primary winding to the secondary winding.
  3. Provide the secondary characteristics the fitting needs.
  4. Allow individual fixtures to be connected into a common series circuit.
  5. Maintain reliable operation underground, wet and outdoors.
CCRPrimary cableIsolation transformerSecondary cableAGL fixture
2

PRIMARY SIDE VS SECONDARY SIDE

Primary side

The primary winding is connected into the airport series circuit. Typical primary currents are 6.6 A or 20 A, and the actual circuit current must match the transformer design.

Secondary side

The secondary winding supplies the individual fitting. Depending on type it is designed for 6.6 A, 20 A or other equipment-specific requirements.

PRIMARY    CCR → Series circuit → Transformer
SECONDARY  Transformer → Individual fitting

Why a single failed light does not open the loop

The primary winding stays in the series circuit even when the secondary-side fitting has a fault, so an individual lamp or fixture failure does not automatically open the primary series circuit. This is one of the main practical advantages of the isolation-transformer arrangement.

The reverse is not true: the primary winding and its leads are part of the series loop, so an open primary winding, lead or connector takes the whole circuit out, not just one fitting. Many open secondaries (for example a run of failed lamps) also lightly load the CCR, which some CCR designs tolerate poorly (see Note 07).

3

HOW IT WORKS

The transformer works by electromagnetic induction. Alternating current in the primary winding creates changing magnetic flux in the core, which induces voltage in the secondary winding. The windings are electrically separate but magnetically coupled.

Primary AC currentPrimary coilMagnetic flux in coreSecondary coilSecondary outputAGL fixture
Ideal transformer:  Vp / Vs = Np / Ns     Vp × Ip ≈ Vs × Is
AGL point. Do not select an AGL transformer only from a conventional voltage ratio. AGL isolating transformers are specified by wattage, primary current, secondary current and other performance characteristics. Real transformers also have losses.

5

READING THE COMPLETE RATING

Typical ratings are 10/15 W, 20/25 W, 30/45 W, 65 W, 100 W, 150 W, 200 W, 300 W and 500 W. A slash rating such as 10/15 W is a single specified rating category, not two different transformer types; interpret it from the standard and datasheet.

6.6 A / 6.6 A

Example 200 W, 6.6 A / 6.6 A: rated 200 W, 6.6 A primary, 6.6 A secondary. For a 6.6 A primary series circuit.

20 A / 6.6 A

Example 200 W, 20 A / 6.6 A: 20 A primary, 6.6 A secondary. It cannot simply replace a 6.6 / 6.6 A unit because the primary current differs.

20 A / 20 A

Some types, such as specified 300 W and 500 W units, have both windings at 20 A. They differ from 20 A / 6.6 A types. Types with a 6.6 A primary and a 20 A secondary (L-830/831-8 and -12) also exist, so never assume primary and secondary currents are equal. Always read the complete rating.

Low wattage

10/15 W and 20/25 W exist because not every fitting needs 100 W or 200 W. Do not use a 100 W unit just because it is on the shelf.

Rule
  • Primary current must match the actual AGL series circuit
  • Transformer = Application + Wattage + Primary current + Secondary current + Frequency + Standard
6

ELECTRICAL CHARACTERISTICS

AC 150/5345-47C specifies minimum power factor, minimum efficiency, secondary full-load and short-circuit current, load resistance and maximum secondary open-circuit RMS voltage.

Power factor

Power factor = Real power ÷ Apparent power. A high value reduces unnecessary apparent-power demand. The FAA table gives a minimum of 0.95 for the listed types.

Efficiency

Efficiency (%) = Output ÷ Input × 100. Minimums of 70, 80, 85 or 90% depending on type: for example 90% for several 200 W, 300 W and 500 W types and 70% for the 10/15 W and 20/25 W types.

Capacity margin

The standard requires enough capacity for lamp ageing, secondary lead losses and contact losses, so nominal lamp wattage alone is not always enough.

Open and short secondary

The transformer is designed for indefinite operation with short- or open-circuit secondary under the specified primary conditions. This is a reason AGL units differ from ordinary transformers. Never short an energised transformer intentionally. An open secondary can still carry a specified maximum open-circuit voltage (tens of volts, up to roughly 70 V RMS on the larger types), so treat the secondary leads of an energised circuit as live.

Losses

Copper loss (Pcu = I²R, winding resistance), core loss (magnetic operation) and further losses from leakage flux and connections all become heat. Excessive temperature accelerates insulation ageing.

7

CONSTRUCTION

An AGL isolating transformer is not an ordinary indoor transformer. AC 150/5345-47C requires:

  • Electrically separate primary and secondary coils, insulated from each other and from the core
  • A core providing the magnetic path
  • A permanently sealed, watertight case
  • Molded connectors on primary and secondary leads
  • Tightly wound, secured windings before molding
  • No internal air pockets in the encapsulated assembly

Case and encapsulation

  • Minimum black cross-linked polychloroprene, or equivalent or better materials such as certain TPE or TPV
  • Withstands sunlight, oil, gasoline, water, runway deicing fluids and acidic/alkaline soils
  • Protects core, windings and internal connections from moisture, dirt, chemicals and mechanical damage

Environment

  • Continuous outdoor operation approximately −55 °C to +65 °C
  • Operates buried in the ground and submerged in water
  • Meets the specification in any orientation (upright or on any side)
Sealed construction does not excuse poor installation. Cable joints, connectors, pits and drainage must still be installed correctly, and installation follows manufacturer instructions and approved drawings.

50 Hz VS 60 Hz

50 Hz

Use the applicable L-831.

60 Hz

Use the applicable L-830.

For projects on a 50 Hz electrical system, verify frequency from the airport electrical design, project specification, CCR documentation, transformer datasheet and approved drawings. Do not assume frequency from the country alone.

Wrong frequency. A transformer must run at its rated frequency. An incorrectly rated unit can give incorrect performance, excessive heating, incorrect output, reduced efficiency and possible damage. Always verify against the actual standard rather than memory alone.

SELECTION METHOD

  1. Identify the fitting. Runway edge, centreline, taxiway, approach, PAPI, sign or other equipment.
  2. Determine fitting power. For example 15, 45, 100 or 200 W.
  3. Identify primary circuit current. 6.6 A or 20 A.
  4. Determine secondary requirement. Secondary current, voltage and fixture requirements.
  5. Check frequency. 50 Hz or 60 Hz.
  6. Select the applicable type. L-831 for 50 Hz, L-830 for 60 Hz.
  7. Verify the standard. FAA, ICAO/project requirement, national authority, airport specification, manufacturer documentation.

REPLACEMENT RULE AND LED FIXTURES

When replacing a failed transformer, do not ask only "what wattage was installed?" Ask what type, frequency, primary current, secondary current, wattage, connector, fitting, standard, project specification and manufacturer requirements apply.

LED fittings. LED fixtures have different electrical characteristics from traditional lamps. When an LED fitting replaces a conventional lamp, do not assume the existing transformer is suitable. Verify the LED input requirement, transformer rating, secondary characteristics, current compatibility, manufacturer approval, CCR compatibility and photometric performance.
CCR currentTransformerSecondary supplyFixtureOptical outputPhotometry

An incorrect transformer can give incorrect fixture current, reduced or abnormal intensity and poor photometric performance. Replacing 45 W lamps with low-power LED fixtures can also leave transformers and the CCR lightly loaded, so re-check circuit loading (see Note 07).

Case inspection before installation

  • Cracks, blisters, holes, cuts, deep abrasions
  • Damaged leads or connectors, loose connector parts
  • Deformation, poor molding, contamination

Connector inspection

  • Plug body, receptacle body, contacts, seals
  • Cable sheath, mechanical condition, connector fit
  • Keep mating surfaces clean and protected

Protective caps. The specification requires water-resistant caps on the lead connectors. Keep them on during transport, storage, handling and installation to protect mating parts from dirt, moisture and contamination.

Handling. Do not throw transformers, drag them by cables, force connectors, cut connector boots, damage the case, pull leads unnecessarily or let conductive contamination reach connectors. The FAA shock and lead-rigidity tests show that the design is durable, but they are qualification tests, not handling procedures.

Storage. Keep connectors capped, avoid mechanical damage, do not cut or bend leads, keep identification visible and follow manufacturer recommendations.

WATER INGRESS AND INSULATION

Moisture is one of the main enemies of an AGL electrical system. It causes reduced insulation resistance, leakage current, corrosion, tracking, connector deterioration, transformer failure and circuit faults, which is why sealed construction and insulation testing are critical.

Insulation must separate primary from secondary, primary from core/ground, and secondary from core/ground. AC 150/5345-47C contains specific insulation-resistance and leakage-current requirements. The primary winding is specified for 5,000 V and the secondary for 600 V; this is part of the insulation design, not the normal operating voltage.

15

QUALIFICATION TESTING

Qualification establishes that the design complies. AC 150/5345-47C specifies tests on three samples of each unit submitted. Do not confuse this with routine production testing of individual units.

The 20-cycle insulation test

Each cycle involves connector installation, heating operation, water immersion, water soaking and insulation-resistance measurement, with connectors remaining mated throughout.

StageRequirement
HeatingMinimum 6 hours at room temperature with rated primary current, secondary open-circuited, mating connectors installed
ImmersionTransformer, leads and connectors fully submerged, then insulation resistance measured (tests the whole transformer + lead + connector system wet)
SoakMinimum 12-hour water soak within the cycle
Insulation resistanceDC test voltage applied for one minute under water conditions; minimum resistance or maximum leakage-current criteria

FAA insulation values, transformers up to 150 W

WindingTest voltageMinimum insulation resistanceOr maximum leakage
Primary15 kV DC7,500 MΩ2 µA
Secondary5 kV DC2,500 MΩ2 µA
These are FAA qualification-test requirements; limits differ for transformers above 150 W. Quote cycle counts, durations and voltages only after checking them in the current AC. Field test voltages and acceptance criteria must follow the approved project procedure, manufacturer instructions and applicable standard.

Other qualification tests

  • Temperature rise at rated load, short circuit and open circuit
  • Shock: two drops from about 6 ft (about 1.8 m) onto smooth hardwood, in orientations that challenge case, core and windings
  • Lead rigidity: lead attachment secure after shock and mechanical loading

Production tests (each unit)

  • Current-ratio test at rated frequency, rated primary current and rated secondary load
  • Insulation testing
  • Void inspection: voids are grounds for rejection (they can create moisture paths, stress concentrations and thermal problems)

The current-ratio test matters because AGL series circuits are current-controlled: the transformer must deliver the intended secondary current, which affects light output, loading, reliability and photometry.

FIELD TESTS: CONTINUITY VS INSULATION

TestWhat it showsLimit of the test
ContinuityWhether a conductive path exists; finds open winding, broken lead, open connector, broken cableDoes not prove health: a unit can pass continuity and still have low insulation resistance, wrong output, turn-to-turn faults, thermal problems or moisture ingress
Insulation resistanceWhether insulation prevents unwanted leakage (primary → ground, secondary → ground, primary/secondary relationship)Method and voltage must follow the approved procedure
Dielectric withstandWhether insulation withstands an applied test voltage without breakdownTest voltage, duration and method come from the standard and approved procedure
Current / ratioWhether secondary current meets the specified characteristicsNeeds rated frequency, primary current and secondary load
Beginner mistake
  • Continuity checks whether a conductive path exists
  • Insulation resistance checks whether unwanted leakage is adequately prevented
  • A good transformer must satisfy both

Insulation failure

Symptoms: low insulation resistance, high leakage current, CCR alarm or trip, circuit instability, intermittent operation, water-related fault indications. Likely causes: moisture ingress, damaged case or lead, connector contamination, internal breakdown.

High-resistance connector

This can end in connector damage, secondary voltage drop and fixture failure. A connector that physically fits is not necessarily a good electrical connection; the same applies to secondary cable loss (I²R), where longer or higher-resistance cable increases loss.

COMMON MISTAKES

1. Selecting only by wattage

"Need 200 W, buy any 200 W transformer" is wrong. Check wattage, primary and secondary current, frequency and standard.

2. Ignoring frequency

50 Hz is not 60 Hz.

3. Mixing 6.6 A and 20 A

A 20 A-primary unit is not automatically interchangeable with a 6.6 A-primary unit.

4. Ignoring connector compatibility

A correct electrical rating with an incompatible connector is still an incorrect installation.

5. Ignoring environmental protection

Underground installation still needs proper sealing of the complete connection system.

6. Treating continuity as complete testing

Continuity does not confirm insulation or performance.

7. Installing a damaged transformer

Investigate serious cracks, damaged leads or damaged connectors first.

Exam & Interview

EXAM / INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

What is an AGL isolation transformer?

A specialised series-to-series transformer that electrically isolates and supplies an individual airport lighting fitting from the primary series circuit.

What is the difference between L-830 and L-831?

L-830 is 60 Hz; L-831 is 50 Hz.

Why is an isolation transformer used in AGL?

To isolate the individual fixture from the primary series circuit while transferring the required power.

What is a 200 W 6.6/6.6 A transformer?

One rated 200 W with 6.6 A primary and 6.6 A secondary characteristics.

What is a 200 W 20/6.6 A transformer?

One rated 200 W with 20 A primary and 6.6 A secondary characteristics.

Can a 6.6/6.6 A transformer replace a 20/6.6 A transformer?

Not simply. The primary current must match the actual series-circuit design.

Does wattage alone identify a transformer?

No. Also verify current, frequency, secondary characteristics, application and standard.

Which type is used for 50 Hz? For 60 Hz?

L-831 for 50 Hz; L-830 for 60 Hz.

Why is the transformer case sealed?

To protect against water and the environmental exposure of airport field installation.

Why are AGL transformers suitable for underground installation?

Their construction and sealing are designed for outdoor, buried and water-exposure conditions under the applicable specification.

What is the purpose of the primary winding?

To connect the transformer into the series circuit and receive the controlled primary current.

What is the purpose of the secondary winding?

To supply the individual AGL fixture with the specified secondary characteristics.

What is the difference between continuity and insulation-resistance testing?

Continuity verifies a conductive path; insulation resistance verifies that insulation prevents unwanted leakage.

Why is connector condition important?

A poor connector causes resistance, heating, voltage drop, intermittent operation and eventual failure.

What is the basic AGL chain?

CCR → Primary Cable → Isolation Transformer → Secondary Cable → AGL Fixture.

Final Study Takeaway

FINAL STUDY TAKEAWAY

The isolating transformer is the critical interface between the high-voltage primary series circuit and the individual lighting fitting. Remember: primary on the CCR-controlled loop; secondary to the fitting; isolation between them; wattage matched to the load; primary and secondary current ratings; frequency (L-830 / L-831); environment; compatible connectors; testing; and the whole system of CCR, cables, transformer, connector, fixture and photometry.

Transformer knowledge = Wattage + Primary current + Secondary current + Frequency + Insulation + Connectors + Environment + Testing + Compliance.

Suspect the Leads and Connectors First

Fault Finding

In practice, many apparent transformer failures sit in the leads or moulded connectors. Check and test them before condemning the unit.

Rating Follows the Fixture Load

Selection

Choose the rating from the connected load with sensible margin, and recheck it whenever the fixture type changes, especially in LED conversions.

Insulation Decline Usually Starts With Moisture

Water Ingress

A falling insulation trend in a wet pit points to the environment as well as the transformer. Fix the entry path or the replacement will age the same way.

Records

Fitting the wrong rating or type during a night repair is an avoidable error. Keep nameplate details on the register and in stores.
