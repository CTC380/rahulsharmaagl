THE AC 150/5340 SERIES

The FAA AC 150/5340 family covers airport visual aids at system level: design, installation, markings, signs and maintenance. The FAA listing shows five active ACs.

ACTitleMemory
150/5340-1MStandards for Airport MarkingsMarkings
150/5340-5DSegmented Circle Airport Marker SystemMarker
150/5340-18HStandards for Airport Sign SystemsSigns
150/5340-26CMaintenance of Airport Visual Aid FacilitiesMaintenance
150/5340-30JDesign and Installation Details for Airport Visual AidsDesign + install
Airport designVisual aidsLayoutInstallationMarkings and signsMaintenancePhotometryServiceability

An AC is not automatically law. It applies through FAA requirements, airport certification, grant conditions or contract documents; for example, 30J and 26C are mandatory for projects funded through the Airport Improvement Program (AIP) or Passenger Facility Charge (PFC) revenue. Work out why an AC applies before treating every paragraph as a universal requirement.

Issue dates in these notes (30J Feb 2018, 26C Jun 2014, 18H Sep 2024, 1M May 2019, 5D Sep 2013) and the 5345 editions below were checked against the FAA advisory circular list (all Active) on 22 October 2026. Check for later changes or errata (1M especially) and confirm status in the FAA advisory circular database before use.

AC 150/5340-30J: DESIGN AND INSTALLATION

What is FAA AC 150/5340-30?

FAA AC 150/5340-30 is the advisory circular "Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids". It is the 5340-series reference for how runway, taxiway, approach and PAPI lighting is designed and installed. These notes use edition 30J (2018); confirm the current edition in the FAA advisory circular database. An AC is not automatically law, so check why it applies to your project.

Start with the visual function required, not the product: function, location, spacing, aiming, installation, wiring, control, testing. 30J covers runway, threshold, end, edge, centreline and TDZ lights, taxiway lights, approach lights, PAPI, REIL and land-and-hold-short lighting, plus bases, saw kerfs and wiring.

ItemAC 150/5340-30J value
Runway edge spacingGenerally up to 200 ft (61 m) longitudinal; opposite lights aligned across the runway
Runway edge offsetAbout 2 ft to 10 ft from the full-strength pavement edge
RCL spacing50 ft (15 m), about ±2 ft longitudinal; uniform offset up to about 2.5 ft (0.8 m)
RCL/TDZ requiredCAT II/III, and CAT I landing below 2,400 ft (750 m) RVR; RCL for takeoff below 1,600 ft (480 m) RVR unless otherwise approved
RCL base to pavement jointOn a new runway, no closer than 2 ft (0.6 m), measured to the edge of the fixture base
TDZTwo rows of bars, three unidirectional lights per bar, from about 100 ft (30 m) to 3,000 ft (900 m) or half the runway, whichever is less, toed in toward the centreline (angle from the 30J aiming figure)
LAHSORow of pulsing white in-pavement lights; 6-light bar standard for new installations, 7 for some existing ones

Threshold groups on opposite sides should use the same style (both elevated or both in-pavement), and displaced thresholds need their own arrangement. Pick approach systems (ALSF, MALSR, MALS, SSALR, SALS, ODALS) from approach type and airport design, not from the word "precision".

The 2,400 ft and 1,600 ft RVR criteria are FAA values, not ICAO ones. Do not copy them into an ICAO-governed project.
4

RUNWAY CENTRELINE, TDZ AND PAPI

FAA RCL colour (AIM, viewed on landing)
  • White until 3,000 ft of runway remain
  • 3,000 ft to 1,000 ft remaining: alternating white and red
  • Last 1,000 ft: all red

RCL supports landing, rollout and takeoff; TDZ lights mainly support landing. TDZ is steady white, so compare it with the RCL colour change when reading the pattern.

PAPI unit (3° path)Nominal aim
D, nearest the runway3°30′
C3°10′
B2°50′
A, farthest2°30′

PAPI units sit 20 to 30 ft (6 to 9 m) apart (for another glide-path angle, set A to B to C to D at the angle minus 30′, minus 10′, plus 10′ and plus 30′), aimed parallel to the runway centreline within about ±0.5°, with beam centres in one horizontal plane (about ±1 in). REIL marks the runway end, with its own location and aiming rules in 30J.

5

TAXIWAY LIGHTING AND STOP BARS

Taxiway edge spacing depends on section length, so lay it out from the actual geometry: straight sections, curves (spaced by radius), intersections, fillets, tapers and single-edge areas. Taxiway end indicators sit about 50 ft (15 m) from the last light on a straight section, and about 50 ft before the point of tangency on curves and intersections.

Centreline lights give continuous guidance in low visibility. Stop-bar equipment sits mainly in the 5345 family, with installation and control integration through 30J and airport procedures. A LAHSO bar is not a stop bar.

Taxiway edge-light spacing varies with section length and geometry. Take exact spacing from the AC 150/5340-30J table for the section being designed.

SIGNS AND MARKINGS: 18H AND 1M

Sign type (18H)ColoursPurpose
Mandatory instructionWhite on redRunway entry, ILS/CAT II/III critical areas, no entry
LocationYellow on black, yellow borderWhere you are
DirectionBlack on yellow, arrowsTurns leaving an intersection
InformationBlack on yellowOther information
Runway distance remainingWhite numerals on blackDistance during takeoff/landing

18H also covers destination, vehicle roadway and taxiway ending marker signs. Under 1M, runway markings are white and taxiway and holding-position markings are yellow. Runway marking types are visual, nonprecision instrument and precision instrument. A runway holding position marking is two solid and two dashed yellow lines; the solid lines are on the side where the aircraft holds, and it does not cross unless ATC authorizes it. Marking, sign and light work together: yellow hold marking, red and white mandatory sign, stop bar where installed, then centreline lights. 5D, the segmented circle, matters least for core runway lighting.

AC 150/5340-26C: MAINTENANCE AND PHOTOMETRY

26C treats maintenance as a cycle of inspection, measurement, maintenance, retest and trending, not "fail, replace, forget". Regular photometric measurement checks output and finds misalignment.

26C pointDetail
Maintenance targetClean or service when output falls below about 70% of the minimum required output at full intensity (reference from the fixture specification)
Before/afterMeasure before and after cleaning, relamping or realignment
FrequencyPrecision approach runway: at least twice per year, based on traffic, pollution and reliability
RCLRubber builds up; weekly checks may be needed on CAT II/III runways
Mobile systemsTest individual fixtures quickly, with isocandela data and GPS positions

The 70% basis and the inspection frequencies follow 26C. Confirm the exact table in the current text; many airports set tighter intervals in their own maintenance programme.

70% is not
  • A universal project acceptance limit
  • The same thing as system serviceability
  • The same as the ICAO 50% individual-light criterion

Judge serviceability by how many failed, where, whether adjacent, and whether the pattern holds. Compare Note 18 for ICAO and Note 15 for photometry.

8

26C: SAFETY, FIXTURES AND FAULT-FINDING

Electrical safety

Series circuits, stored energy, energized circuits, arc flash, isolation. Check grounding periodically, not only at installation: poor grounding can leave dangerous voltage on equipment frames. 26C treats 25 Ω as the maximum acceptable ground resistance and prefers 10 Ω or less.

Fixtures

Check lens, gasket, mounting, bolts, water ingress, optics and alignment. Use a calibrated torque wrench, not an impact wrench; loose bolts in TDZ areas cause movement and misalignment.

Water

Water in the base leads to corrosion, connector and wiring problems, then failure. Drainage and sealing are part of performance.

Fault methods

Ground, open-circuit, load-to-load, primary cable and transformer faults, using intentional-ground and grounded-output tests, VOM, megger and midpoint testing.

CCRCircuit behaviourFault patternGround/open analysisLocalizeRepairRetest

Read the circuit: which lights are on, dim or off, and where the transition is, before opening pits.

9

INSTALLATION DETAILS

In-pavement work needs attention to pavement, fixture, base, wireway, sealant, cable, transformer, connector and drainage. 30J has saw-kerf and wireway details for RCL and TDZ; the kerf must be clean, dry, sealed with a pavement-compatible sealer and mechanically protected. Bases must allow for wheel loading, pavement type, joints, elevation and drainage (5345-42K). Elevated lights need a sound foundation, frangible point, verticality, height and aiming; frangible connections are also covered by AC 150/5220-23A.

Control chain: ATC or control, L-821 panel, CCR, series circuit, lights, with monitoring through PLC, SCADA or ALCMS as the approved design requires.

REVIEW CHECKLISTS AND MISTAKES

Drawing review

Runway geometry, centreline, edge line, threshold and displacement, RCL and TDZ spacing, approach layout, taxiway spacing, stop bars, signs, circuits, transformers, cables, joint and pit conflicts.

Submittal

Datasheet, FAA class, photometric and isocandela data, drawings, electrical data, connector, base, qualification and routine tests, FAT procedure, manuals.

Photometric report

Airport, runway, fixture, instrument and calibration, weather, results, required vs measured, percentage, orientation, beam, corrective action, retest.

Database

Fixture ID and type, circuit, location, installation, required and measured values, status, last clean and photometry, next action.

Exam & Interview

EXAM / INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

What is AC 150/5340-30J?

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids. It is a system document, not a fixture specification.

What is AC 150/5340-26C?

Maintenance of Airport Visual Aid Facilities: maintenance, inspection, troubleshooting and photometric guidance.

What is the FAA 70% concept?

About 70% of minimum required output at maximum intensity is the maintenance and cleaning target in 26C.

18H, 1M and 5D?

Airport sign systems, airport markings and the segmented circle marker system.

5340 vs 5345?

5340 covers system design, installation, signs, markings and maintenance; 5345 holds the equipment specifications.

FAA RCL spacing and colours?

50 ft (15 m); white until 3,000 ft remain, alternating white/red for the next 2,000 ft, then all red over the last 1,000 ft.

How far do FAA TDZ lights extend?

About 100 ft beyond the threshold to 3,000 ft, or to the runway midpoint on shorter runways.

PAPI aiming for a 3° path?

D 3°30′, C 3°10′, B 2°50′, A 2°30′; units 20 to 30 ft apart.

Which ACs cover L-823, fixtures, CCR and PAPI?

5345-26E, 5345-46F, 5345-10H and 5345-28H.

Why not mix FAA and ICAO RVR numbers?

FAA 30J uses 2,400 ft (750 m) for CAT I RCL/TDZ; ICAO Annex 14 uses different triggers.

Final Study Takeaway

FINAL STUDY TAKEAWAY

AC 150/5340 tells you how the visual-aid system is designed, installed, marked, signed and maintained; the 150/5345 series tells you what the equipment must be.

30J: design it right. 5345: buy the right equipment. 26C: keep it working. Photometry: prove it.
