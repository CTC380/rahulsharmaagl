How is an airport lighting transformer tested?

An isolating transformer is tested against the project specification and its reference standard, FAA AC 150/5345-47C for L-830 (60 Hz) and L-831 (50 Hz) series isolating transformers. Testing covers electrical checks such as continuity and insulation resistance, with manufacturer test certificates as compliance evidence, and field verification once the transformer is installed in the series circuit. We support this as part of our AGL testing and inspection services. See L-830 Isolating Transformer, L-831 Isolating Transformer and the Technical Guide.

FAQ

AGL Products — Frequently Asked Questions

What AGL products does Precision AGL Solutions offer?

The catalogue is organised by application into ten groups: runway lighting, taxiway and apron lighting, approach lighting, PAPI and accessories, airfield guidance signs and markers, AGL series circuit power and connectivity, wind and meteorological aids, obstruction and identification lighting, civil and field infrastructure, and spares, retrofit and replacement equipment.

Are the products mapped to ICAO Annex 14 and FAA designations?

Yes, wherever a recognised equivalent exists. The cross-reference table on the page links each lighting function to its ICAO Annex 14 designation and FAA fixture type, for example L-850C inset or L-862 and L-861 elevated for runway edge lights. It is an orientation aid only: the applicable project specification governs the exact type, colour and intensity, and the current ICAO Annex 14 and FAA AC 150/5340 editions should be confirmed.

What is the difference between L-830 and L-831 isolating transformers?

L-830 is the FAA designation for 60 Hz series isolating transformers and L-831 is the 50 Hz version. Both are referenced to FAA AC 150/5345-47C. Selection depends on circuit current, fixture load, frequency and the project specification.

What do the primary and secondary connector kits do?

Primary connector kits are for the high-voltage primary series cable and are used for circuit connections at transformers and cable joints, with reference to FAA L-823 (AC 150/5345-26E). Secondary connector kits connect the isolating transformer secondary to the fixture lead. Transformer, lead cable and fixture interface should be checked for compatibility together.

How is an airport lighting transformer tested?

An isolating transformer is tested against the project specification and its reference standard, FAA AC 150/5345-47C for L-830 and L-831. Testing covers electrical checks such as continuity and insulation resistance, with manufacturer test certificates as compliance evidence, and field verification once the transformer is installed in the series circuit. Precision AGL Solutions supports this as part of its AGL testing and inspection services.

Do you supply civil and field infrastructure as well as lighting?

Yes. The infrastructure group covers deep, shallow and inset bases, junction boxes and pits, handholes, frangible couplings, mounting and extension stems, base plates, cable ducts, cable protection, drainage and earthing accessories, marker posts and cable identification tags, so contractors can source the complete installation package.

Are spares, retrofit modules and replacement equipment available?

These items are listed as available on request. They include LED retrofit modules, drivers and ballasts, replacement isolating transformers and connector kits, CCR cards and spares kits, sign components and LED boards, halogen-to-LED conversion kits and obsolete or legacy replacement units. The support route is to identify the installed equipment, verify photometric and circuit compatibility, and propose the replacement or upgrade.

What should I send to get help choosing a product?

Share the airport, the application, existing equipment, circuit details or the tender specification. That lets the required product family, technical parameters, accessories and testing requirements be identified.

Can I rely on the product information on this page for procurement?

No. Availability, exact configuration, certification or approval status and compliance depend on the selected manufacturer, product variant and project specification, so verify everything against the latest approved datasheet and project requirements before procurement.
