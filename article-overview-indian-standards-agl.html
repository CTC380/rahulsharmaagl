Overview of Indian Standards for Airfield Ground Lighting

Airfield Ground Lighting is one of the most critical safety systems installed at an airport. In India, it is governed not by a single rulebook but by a combined framework of ICAO, DGCA, AAI, and BIS requirements working together.

RS

Rahul Sharma

Manager – Technical & Commercial · AGL Photometry Specialist

May 27, 2026

Every runway light, taxiway light, approach fitting, illuminated sign board, and associated electrical component at an Indian airport must operate reliably under demanding environmental and operational conditions. No single standard covers all of this on its own.

In India, AGL systems are designed and operated using a combination of ICAO standards, DGCA regulations, AAI technical specifications, Indian Standards (IS Standards), international electrical and photometric standards, and selected FAA equipment specifications — together maintaining globally accepted levels of safety, reliability, and operational performance.

Key Takeaway

AGL compliance in India is not about picking one rulebook. It's a layered framework — ICAO sets the operational baseline, DGCA regulates and certifies, AAI specifies procurement and installation detail, and BIS governs the electrical and material quality underneath it all.

01

Why Standards Matter in AGL Systems

Airport lighting is not simply an electrical installation — it is a precision visual guidance system that pilots depend on during night operations, low visibility, heavy rain, fog, taxiing, and critical landing and takeoff phases. Any error in lighting intensity, beam alignment, spacing, or colour can directly affect flight safety.

Standardization is what makes the system trustworthy across different airports and different crews:

  • Uniform lighting practices that pilots can interpret consistently, airport to airport
  • Reliable aircraft guidance under demanding conditions
  • Safer, more predictable airport operations
  • Proper maintenance procedures and longer equipment life
  • Compliance with aviation regulations at every stage of a project

A properly standardized AGL system lets a pilot read runway, taxiway, and approach lighting the same way, no matter which airport they're landing at.

02

Key Organizations Behind the Standards

Four organizations shape the framework Indian AGL projects are built on — each covering a different layer, from international operational rules down to material-level electrical quality.

OrganizationPrimary Role in AGL
ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I is the primary international reference — runway, taxiway, and approach lighting, PAPI systems, signage, light characteristics (Appendix 2), colours (Appendix 1), spacing, secondary power and maintenance.
DGCA India's regulatory authority for aerodrome licensing, safety inspections, AGL compliance verification, and operational readiness approval. Requirements are set through the DGCA CARs (Section 4, aerodrome standards) and are broadly aligned with ICAO Annex 14.
AAI Publishes the technical specifications most Indian airport tenders are based on — product approval, installation guidelines, testing procedures, and approved makes.
BIS Develops Indian Standards for cables, transformers, earthing, insulation, and material quality — the electrical foundation everything else sits on.

ICAO sets what "correct" looks like operationally. DGCA certifies that an airport actually meets it. AAI tells contractors how to build it. BIS defines what the materials must be capable of.

03

Core Indian Standards Used in AGL Systems

Underneath the regulatory framework sit the specific technical standards that govern individual components and installation practices.

Isolating TransformersIS 12290 covers insulation, temperature rise, dielectric strength, and efficiency of isolating transformers for series circuits (typically 6.6 A at maximum brightness step)
CablesXLPE insulation (IS 7098), conductor quality, flame-retardant properties, voltage rating, and insulation resistance for underground, high-stress conditions, as called up in the AAI or project specification
Earthing & Lightning ProtectionEarthing (IS 3043) and lightning protection (IS/IEC 62305): resistance limits, bonding practices, and protection for CCRs, circuits, and personnel in open outdoor areas
Environmental ProtectionIP ratings (IEC 60529) and related IEC requirements for water ingress, dust resistance, and mechanical impact under continuous exposure

Because the isolating transformer sits directly between the primary series circuit and each lighting fitting, its electrical integrity has an outsized effect on overall system reliability.

04

Photometric Compliance Requirements

Photometric performance is one of the most important parts of AGL compliance — it verifies that a fitting isn't just electrically functional, but is actually producing the optical output pilots rely on. Indian airport authorities normally require photometric compliance aligned with ICAO standards, and photometric reports are essential during inspection and commissioning.

Beam AngleAiming and orientation checked against the required beam direction
IntensityMeasured output against the required isocandela values
ChromaticityColour accuracy checked against the standard
UniformityConsistency of output between fittings in the same pattern or circuit
Beam SpreadVertical and horizontal coverage of the beam

Correct intensity, colour, beam direction, and distribution are what turn an electrically working light into reliable visual guidance for the cockpit.

05

Role of FAA Standards in Indian Airports

Although Indian airports primarily follow ICAO and DGCA requirements, many projects also reference FAA equipment specifications (the AC 150/5345 series) alongside them — largely because manufacturers design products compliant with both frameworks to improve international acceptance.

  • FAA L-823 plug and receptacle connectors
  • FAA L-830 isolation transformers (FAA’s term; ICAO and IEC say “isolating”)
  • FAA L-861 (low-intensity) and L-862 (high-intensity) elevated runway edge lights, with L-861T for taxiways
  • FAA photometric classifications and approvals under the AC 150/5345 equipment series

For engineers on international airport projects, familiarity with FAA equipment specifications is a useful complement to ICAO and Indian requirements — not a replacement for them.

06

Testing, Commissioning & Documentation

Before commissioning, AGL systems in India go through a defined sequence of inspections and tests (the exact sequence follows the AAI specification and the DGCA-approved commissioning plan):

01Electrical TestingInsulation resistance, earthing, CCR, transformer & cable continuity
02Photometric VerificationType-test reports plus in-situ intensity, aiming & colour checks
03Functional & Field TestingCircuit energisation, ALCMS control, secondary-power switch-over, PAPI aiming and field alignment
04DocumentationCertificates, calibration records, as-built & GA drawings, O&M manuals

Complete project documentation — test certificates, compliance declarations, calibration records, GA drawings, O&M manuals, and inspection reports — is critical during DGCA and airport authority inspections, and provides the technical record that supports future maintenance.

Testing confirms the installed system performs to the required technical and operational criteria; documentation is what proves it, long after commissioning day.

07

Modernization of Indian AGL Systems

Indian airports are rapidly upgrading to advanced lighting technologies — LED airfield lighting, smart monitoring systems, remote CCR control, energy-efficient infrastructure, and advanced approach lighting. This shift raises the bar rather than lowering it:

  • Higher reliability expectations from newer LED and electronic systems
  • Reduced maintenance, but greater precision required when maintenance does happen
  • Better energy efficiency without compromising photometric performance
  • Improved operational monitoring and faster fault detection

The move toward LED and smart monitoring technologies comes with an increasing — not decreasing — need for reliable testing, monitoring, documentation, and standards compliance.

08

Key Takeaways

  • Indian AGL compliance rests on four layers working together: ICAO (operational baseline), DGCA (regulation and certification), AAI (procurement and installation specification), and BIS (electrical and material quality)
  • IS 12290 and related Indian Standards govern the electrical integrity of transformers, cables, earthing, and environmental protection
  • Photometric compliance verifies optical performance, not just electrical function — beam angle, intensity, chromaticity, uniformity, and beam spread all matter
  • FAA specifications like L-823, L-830, and L-861/L-862 (AC 150/5345 series) frequently appear alongside ICAO and Indian requirements on international-facing projects
  • Testing, commissioning, and documentation aren't a final step — they're the evidence that everything upstream was actually done correctly
  • Modernization to LED and smart monitoring systems increases, rather than reduces, the importance of standards compliance

How the Indian AGL Standards Framework Fits Together

A layered framework, not a sequence: ICAO baseline · DGCA regulation · AAI specification · BIS material quality → Compliant, Photometrically Verified AGL

Conclusion

Airfield Ground Lighting systems are precision-engineered safety systems where even a small compliance or installation error can affect aircraft operations. Indian AGL projects depend on a combined framework of ICAO operational standards, DGCA regulatory requirements, AAI technical specifications, BIS electrical standards, and international photometric practices.

AGL compliance is not limited to selecting the correct lighting fitting — it extends across design, electrical infrastructure, photometric performance, installation, testing, commissioning, documentation, and long-term maintenance.

Understanding these standards is essential for engineers, contractors, manufacturers, consultants, and airport authorities involved in airport infrastructure projects in India.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which standards govern airfield ground lighting in India?

No single standard covers everything. AGL systems are designed and operated using a combination of ICAO standards, DGCA regulations, AAI technical specifications, Indian Standards (IS Standards), international electrical and photometric standards and selected FAA equipment specifications.

What roles do ICAO, DGCA, AAI and BIS play?

ICAO Annex 14 Volume I is the primary international reference, covering runway, taxiway and approach lighting, light characteristics, colours, spacing, secondary power and maintenance. DGCA is India's regulatory authority for aerodrome licensing, safety inspections and AGL compliance verification, with requirements set through the DGCA CARs (Section 4, aerodrome standards). AAI publishes the technical specifications most Indian airport tenders are based on, and BIS develops Indian Standards for cables, transformers, earthing, insulation and material quality.

Which Indian Standards apply to AGL components?

The article cites IS 12290 for isolating transformers for series circuits, XLPE cable insulation (IS 7098) as called up in the AAI or project specification, earthing (IS 3043), lightning protection (IS/IEC 62305) and IP ratings under IEC 60529. IS 12290 covers insulation, temperature rise, dielectric strength and efficiency.

What photometric compliance is required at Indian airports?

Indian airport authorities normally require photometric compliance aligned with ICAO standards, and photometric reports are essential during inspection and commissioning. Verification covers beam angle, intensity against the required isocandela values, chromaticity, uniformity and beam spread.

Why are FAA specifications used on Indian airport projects?

Although Indian airports primarily follow ICAO and DGCA requirements, many projects also reference FAA equipment specifications from the AC 150/5345 series, largely because manufacturers design products compliant with both frameworks to improve international acceptance. Examples include L-823 connectors, L-830 isolation transformers and L-861/L-862 elevated runway edge lights, as a complement to ICAO and Indian requirements, not a replacement.

What testing is done before commissioning an AGL system in India?

The sequence follows the AAI specification and the DGCA-approved commissioning plan, and typically includes electrical testing (insulation resistance, earthing, CCR, transformer and cable continuity), photometric verification, functional and field testing (circuit energisation, ALCMS control, secondary-power switch-over, PAPI aiming and field alignment) and documentation.

What documentation is needed for DGCA and airport authority inspections?

Complete project documentation includes test certificates, compliance declarations, calibration records, GA drawings, O&M manuals and inspection reports. It is the technical record that proves the testing was done and supports future maintenance.

Does moving to LED and smart monitoring reduce the need for standards compliance?

No. Modernisation raises the bar, with higher reliability expectations from LED and electronic systems, reduced maintenance but greater precision when maintenance happens, and improved monitoring and faster fault detection. The need for reliable testing, monitoring, documentation and standards compliance increases.
