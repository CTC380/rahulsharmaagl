L823-371 Loose Connector

Symptom — An L-823 plug and receptacle at a fixture or transformer secondary lead is found only partially seated or easily separated by hand, with no confirmed light-output complaint yet reported.

Equipment / System

L-823 secondary connector (plug and receptacle pair) joining the isolation transformer secondary leads to the fixture leads, found in base cans, hand holes, and light fixture housings across every circuit type in the AGL system.

Possible Causes
HighConnector not pushed fully home during the last installation or maintenance visit, leaving the locking feature disengaged
HighRepeated thermal cycling and vibration from ground or aircraft traffic gradually backing the plug out of the receptacle
MedWorn or undersized locking lugs/pins that no longer hold full engagement
MedSilicone grease has hardened or washed out, letting the halves separate under their own cable weight
LowIncorrect connector pairing (mismatched manufacturer parts) that mates but does not lock properly
LowBase can too small for the cable slack, placing continuous outward tension on the connector
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeter (CAT III)Torque/pull-test reference per manufacturer dataL-823 connector gauge/go-no-go toolSilicone dielectric greaseBase-can lifting tool and PPE for airside work
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before opening the connector for inspection or reseating.
  • Coordinate airside access through ATC/apron control before approaching a fixture on an active surface.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationOpen the base can and visually confirm how far the plug is seated into the receptacle, noting any visible gap or exposed pins.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, separate the connector fully, inspect the pins/sockets for wear, and dry-fit to confirm the locking feature engages positively.
  3. MeasurementCheck for adequate cable slack in the base can; a taut cable pulling on a properly seated connector will re-loosen it over time.
  4. DecisionLocking feature engages positively on dry-fit → reseat and dress cable. Locking feature will not hold → treat as connector lock failure and replace the pair.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific plug/receptacle pair rather than assuming the whole circuit run is affected.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the reseated connector holds under a gentle pull test before closing the base can.
Test Points
Seating depth of plug in receptacle
Locking lug/pin engagement
Cable slack and strain relief in base can
Continuity across the reseated connector
Root Cause Verification

After reseating, verify continuity end-to-end, confirm the fixture lights at all intensity steps, and re-check the connector on the next scheduled inspection to confirm it has not backed out again.

Corrective Action
  • Fully reseat the connector until the locking feature engages positively.
  • Apply fresh silicone dielectric grease to both halves before mating.
  • Add or redress cable slack in the base can to remove tension on the connector.
  • Replace a connector pair with worn locking lugs that will not hold engagement.
Preventive Action
  • Include a positive-seating check and gentle pull test for L-823 connectors in the routine PM cycle.
  • Standardise on a single matched connector manufacturer/part combination per circuit to avoid mismatched pairings.
  • Re-grease connectors at each scheduled base-can service interval.
Related References

L823-372 Connector Open Circuit

Symptom — A fixture or transformer fails to pass current even though the upstream series circuit is confirmed energised, with the fault traced specifically to the L-823 connector rather than the transformer or lamp.

Equipment / System

L-823 plug and receptacle pair and its internal pin/socket contacts, forming the electrical joint between the isolation transformer secondary and the fixture leads.

Possible Causes
HighOne or both contact pins have corroded through or broken internally, presenting an open circuit despite the connector appearing mated
HighA crimp connection inside the plug or receptacle has failed completely, separating the wire from the contact
MedA pin has been pushed back out of its cavity during a previous mating, leaving no electrical contact despite mechanical mating
MedContact surfaces are heavily oxidised or contaminated, presenting effectively infinite resistance
LowWrong-gauge wire crimped into the contact barrel, giving a mechanically intact but electrically open joint
LowThe receptacle or plug housing itself is cracked, allowing the pin to float free of its socket
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeter (CAT III) with continuity/resistance function1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)L-823 connector disassembly/pin-extraction tool kitReplacement crimp contacts matched to connector typeSeries circuit fault locator / TDR
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before disconnecting or disassembling the connector.
  • Treat the connector as energised until isolation is confirmed at the CCR, even if the fixture is already dark.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the series circuit is energised and current is flowing up to the point immediately upstream of the suspect connector.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, separate the connector and check continuity through each half individually — pin to conductor on one side, socket to conductor on the other.
  3. MeasurementIf continuity fails within one half, open the housing and inspect the crimp joint and pin seating.
  4. DecisionOpen traced to a failed crimp or pushed-back pin → repair or replace that contact. Open traced to a cracked housing → replace the full connector body.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the open to the specific pin/socket and conductor rather than replacing the whole assembly speculatively.
  6. Root CauseConfirm continuity is restored through the full connector before re-mating and re-energising.
Test Points
Continuity through each connector half independently
Crimp joint integrity at the failed contact
Pin seating depth in the housing cavity
Full-circuit continuity after repair
Root Cause Verification

Re-mate the connector, restore power, and confirm current flow and fixture output at all intensity steps before closing out.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed crimp contact and re-terminate with a correctly sized ferrule and tooling.
  • Re-seat a pushed-back pin fully into its housing cavity, confirming a positive click.
  • Replace a cracked connector housing.
  • Replace both halves as a matched pair if contact damage is extensive.
Preventive Action
  • Verify crimp tool calibration and die selection matches the specified wire gauge before any field termination.
  • Include contact continuity spot-checks in the periodic PM cycle for connectors in high-traffic circuits.
  • Retire and replace connector bodies showing housing cracks during any inspection.
Related References

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-26, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

L823-373 Burnt Contact

Symptom — On disassembly, one or both L-823 connector contacts show visible scorching, blackening, or melted plastic around the pin/socket, sometimes accompanied by an intermittent or fully failed fixture.

Equipment / System

L-823 plug and receptacle contacts and the immediately surrounding housing material at the point of electrical connection.

Possible Causes
HighHigh resistance at a loosely mated or corroded contact caused localised arcing and heat build-up under normal circuit current
HighUndersized or damaged crimp joint increased contact resistance to the point of overheating under sustained load
MedRepeated hot-mating (connecting/disconnecting a live circuit) caused arcing damage at the contact face
MedA partial short between adjacent pins from moisture or debris caused sustained arcing before the fault fully developed
LowIncorrect connector current rating for the circuit it was installed on
LowA manufacturing defect in the original crimp or contact plating
Required Tools
Thermal imaging cameraTrue-RMS multimeter (CAT III)L-823 connector gauge/go-no-go toolReplacement connector pair matched to circuit ratingContact cleaning and inspection kit
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before disassembling a burnt connector; do not assume it is safe simply because the fixture is dark.
  • Treat a burnt contact as a potential fire and arc-flash hazard and inspect the surrounding cable insulation for heat damage before re-energising anything nearby.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationNote the extent and pattern of scorching — confined to the contact face versus spreading into the housing or cable insulation.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the adjacent cable to rule out heat damage extending beyond the connector.
  3. MeasurementInspect the crimp joint on the burnt contact for looseness, undersizing, or poor plating adhesion.
  4. DecisionDamage confined to the connector → replace the connector pair and re-test. Damage extends into cable insulation → also replace the affected cable segment.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm whether only one contact or both are affected, and whether the mating half shows corresponding damage.
  6. Root CauseIdentify why the contact overheated — loose mating, bad crimp, or rating mismatch — rather than simply swapping the part.
Test Points
Cable insulation resistance adjacent to the burnt contact
Crimp joint condition on the removed contact
Connector current rating vs. circuit design current
Thermal signature of the replacement connector under load
Root Cause Verification

After replacement, thermally image the connector under a normal operating cycle to confirm no abnormal heating, and re-check at the next scheduled inspection.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the burnt connector pair in full, including both plug and receptacle.
  • Replace any cable segment showing heat-damaged insulation.
  • Re-terminate with a correctly sized crimp and verified tooling.
Preventive Action
  • Include thermal imaging of connectors in the periodic PM cycle, particularly on circuits with a history of loose connections.
  • Enforce a strict no-hot-mating policy for L-823 connectors.
  • Confirm connector current rating matches circuit design current at every replacement.
Related References

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-26, NFPA 70E (arc-flash precautions)

L823-374 Overheated Contact

Symptom — Thermal imaging or a routine PM check identifies an L-823 connector running significantly hotter than comparable connectors on the same circuit, without yet showing visible burning or a reported light failure.

Equipment / System

L-823 plug and receptacle contact pair on a live series circuit, assessed while carrying normal operating current.

Possible Causes
HighElevated contact resistance from a marginally loose or partially corroded mating, generating heat under sustained current without yet failing
MedA crimp joint that is mechanically intact but marginally undersized for the conductor, increasing localised resistance
MedContact plating wear exposing base metal, increasing resistance at the mating surface
LowAmbient heat from a nearby source (transformer, sun-exposed base can lid) being mistaken for a connector fault
LowA connector rated below the circuit's actual operating current
Required Tools
Thermal imaging cameraTrue-RMS multimeter (CAT III) with resistance/millivolt-drop functionL-823 connector gauge/go-no-go toolTorque/pull-test reference per manufacturer data
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before disassembling or re-terminating an overheating connector even though it has not yet failed.
  • Treat a confirmed hot spot as a developing fault and prioritise investigation rather than deferring to the next routine cycle.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompare the thermal reading of the flagged connector against others on the same circuit under the same load to confirm it is a genuine outlier.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, take a millivolt-drop reading across the mated connector and compare against the manufacturer's acceptable threshold.
  3. MeasurementDisassemble and inspect the contact plating and crimp joint for the elevated-resistance connector.
  4. DecisionMillivolt-drop within tolerance and thermal reading explained by ambient exposure → no fault, log and monitor. Millivolt-drop elevated or contact plating degraded → proceed to replacement.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific contact pair rather than the connector body as a whole.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the replacement connector's thermal signature is in line with the rest of the circuit under the same load.
Test Points
Millivolt drop across the mated connector
Comparative thermal reading vs. neighbouring connectors on the same circuit
Contact plating condition on disassembly
Post-replacement thermal signature under load
Root Cause Verification

Re-thermally-image the repaired connector during a subsequent normal operating cycle and confirm the reading is consistent with unaffected connectors on the same circuit.

Corrective Action
  • Replace a connector pair confirmed to have elevated millivolt drop or degraded plating.
  • Re-terminate with a correctly sized crimp if the joint is marginal.
  • Apply fresh dielectric grease and confirm full seating on reassembly.
Preventive Action
  • Include thermal imaging as a standard PM step across all base cans, not only on reported-fault circuits.
  • Trend millivolt-drop readings where feasible to catch degradation before it becomes visible heat damage.
  • Confirm connector rating matches circuit design current during any bulk connector replacement programme.
Related References

SOP: SOP-CON-04 (Thermal Screening and Millivolt-Drop Testing Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-26, IEEE guidance on contact resistance testing

L823-375 Water Ingress

Symptom — Standing water or visible moisture is found inside an L-823 connector housing during a base-can inspection, sometimes accompanied by a nuisance earth-fault trip, flicker, or reduced insulation resistance reading on that circuit.

Equipment / System

L-823 connector housing, its seal/gasket interface, and the base can or hand hole it sits in.

Possible Causes
HighBase can flooded from a failed or missing lid gasket, poor drainage, or a high water table, submerging the connector
HighThe connector's own watertight seal (O-ring or boot) is degraded, split, or missing, allowing ingress even in a dry base can
MedCondensation forming inside a sealed but poorly vented base can under temperature cycling
MedCable jacket damage upstream allowing water to track down the conductor into the connector
LowConnector not fully mated, leaving a gap for water to enter around the joint
LowBase can drainage gravel/weep hole blocked with silt or debris
Required Tools
1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Moisture detection meterBase-can lid gasket and drainage inspection kitReplacement L-823 connector with intact seal/bootWet/dry vacuum for base-can pump-out
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before handling a wet connector — water inside an energised connector is a serious shock and arc hazard.
  • Do not assume a connector is safe to handle simply because the fixture appears to be working normally.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationNote the water level in the base can and whether the connector itself is submerged or just sitting in a damp environment.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the fixture/transformer secondary through the connector before disturbing it, then again after drying and reassembly.
  3. MeasurementInspect the connector's seal/boot for splits, gaps, or missing components, and check the base-can lid gasket and drainage.
  4. DecisionInsulation resistance recovers fully after drying and the seal is intact → dry out, re-grease, and re-seal. Insulation resistance remains low or the seal is damaged → replace the connector.
  5. Fault LocationDetermine whether the water source is the connector seal, the base-can lid, or an upstream cable jacket defect.
  6. Root CauseFix the entry point identified, not just the symptom at the connector.
Test Points
Insulation resistance before and after drying
Connector seal/boot condition
Base-can lid gasket and drainage path
Cable jacket condition upstream of the connector
Root Cause Verification

After drying and resealing, re-megger and confirm the reading meets the acceptable threshold, then re-check the base can after the next significant rainfall or irrigation cycle.

Corrective Action
  • Pump out and dry the base can before reassembling.
  • Replace a degraded or missing connector seal/boot.
  • Replace the base-can lid gasket if it has failed.
  • Clear and restore the base-can drainage weep hole or gravel bed.
Preventive Action
  • Include base-can drainage and lid-gasket condition in the routine PM checklist.
  • Verify connector seal integrity at every reconnection, not only during fault investigation.
  • Prioritise base cans in low-lying or high-water-table areas for more frequent inspection.
Related References

SOP: SOP-CON-05 (Water Ingress Inspection and Remediation Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-26, IEC 60364 (insulation testing)

L823-376 Moisture Ingress

Symptom — An insulation resistance reading on a circuit is found to be gradually trending down over successive tests, or a fixture exhibits an intermittent fault under damp conditions, without standing water being found at the connector.

Equipment / System

L-823 connector interior and the isolation transformer secondary connection, evaluated for early-stage or low-level moisture presence short of visible water ingress.

Possible Causes
HighGradual moisture penetration through a seal that is degraded but not yet fully failed, showing as slowly declining insulation resistance
HighCondensation cycling inside the connector housing from repeated temperature swings without adequate sealing
MedDielectric grease has thinned, migrated, or washed out over time, reducing its moisture-exclusion function
MedHigh humidity combined with a connector that is mated but not fully torqued/seated, allowing slow vapour ingress
LowA pinhole or micro-crack in the connector housing not visible without close inspection
LowMoisture wicking along the conductor strands from a distant cable defect rather than entering at the connector itself
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger) with trend logMoisture detection meterDielectric greaseMagnifying inspection light for housing crack detectionCCR earth-fault trend log
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before opening or servicing the connector.
  • Treat a declining insulation trend as a developing hazard and investigate before it reaches a nuisance-trip or hard-fault threshold.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the insulation resistance trend log for the circuit to establish the rate and pattern of decline.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, open the connector and check for dampness, condensation film, or grease degradation without waiting for standing water.
  3. MeasurementInspect the housing under magnification for hairline cracks and check the seal/boot for early-stage degradation.
  4. DecisionReading recovers after drying, re-greasing, and reseating → close out and continue trending. Reading does not recover or a housing defect is found → replace the connector.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate whether the moisture source is condensation within an otherwise sealed connector, or slow ingress through a degrading seal.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the corrective action addresses the actual entry mechanism rather than only re-greasing a failing seal.
Test Points
Insulation resistance trend before and after service
Housing condition under magnified inspection
Seal/boot early-stage degradation
Grease condition and coverage
Root Cause Verification

Re-megger immediately after service and again after the next several operating/weather cycles to confirm the declining trend has been arrested.

Corrective Action
  • Dry, re-grease, and reseat the connector where the seal is still serviceable.
  • Replace the connector where the housing or seal shows any degradation.
  • Improve venting or sealing at the base-can lid if condensation cycling is the source.
Preventive Action
  • Track insulation resistance trends over time rather than relying on a single pass/fail reading.
  • Re-grease connectors on a defined interval rather than only at fault investigation.
  • Flag circuits with a declining trend for closer-interval monitoring until stabilised.
Related References

SOP: SOP-CON-06 (Insulation Resistance Trending Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-26, IEC 60364

L823-377 Improper Crimping

Symptom — A connector contact shows a mechanically weak or electrically marginal joint on inspection — the conductor can be pulled from the contact by hand, or resistance across the crimp is elevated, despite the connector appearing outwardly normal.

Equipment / System

L-823 connector crimp contacts and the conductor termination inside the plug or receptacle housing.

Possible Causes
HighWrong crimp die or tool used for the contact/wire combination, producing an under- or over-compressed joint
HighCrimp tool out of calibration or worn, no longer achieving the specified compression
MedInsufficient conductor strands inserted into the contact barrel, or strands trimmed too short before crimping
MedInsulation crimped instead of, or in addition to, bare conductor, preventing proper contact
LowField termination performed without the manufacturer-specified crimp tool at all (e.g. pliers)
LowCorrect tool and die used but a single crimp cycle skipped or incomplete
Required Tools
Manufacturer-specified crimp tool and die set for the connector typePull-test gauge for crimp verificationTrue-RMS multimeter (CAT III)Wire strippers with gauge-matched stopsReplacement crimp contacts
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before disassembling or re-terminating a connector.
  • Do not re-use a contact that has already been crimped once with the wrong tool — replace it rather than re-crimping over a compromised joint.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationNote whether the fault presents as intermittent (marginal crimp still passing current sometimes) or a hard open (crimp has fully failed).
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, disassemble the connector and perform a gentle pull test on the suspect conductor-to-contact joint.
  3. MeasurementInspect the crimp visually for correct compression pattern, correct number of strands captured, and no insulation caught in the barrel.
  4. DecisionCrimp fails pull test or shows incorrect compression → cut back and re-terminate with the correct tool and die. Crimp passes and resistance is within tolerance → reassemble and monitor.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific contact rather than assuming the whole connector needs replacement.
  6. Root CauseConfirm which tool and die were actually used for the original termination to prevent repeating the error on the re-crimp.
Test Points
Pull-test result on the crimp joint
Millivolt drop across the reterminated crimp
Visual compression pattern vs. manufacturer specification
Strand count and insulation position in the barrel
Root Cause Verification

After re-terminating, pull-test the new crimp, verify millivolt drop is within tolerance, and confirm fixture operation before closing the base can.

Corrective Action
  • Cut back the conductor and re-terminate with the correct crimp tool, die, and technique.
  • Replace the contact if the barrel was damaged by an incorrect prior crimp.
  • Verify torque/seating of the reassembled connector before closing out.
Preventive Action
  • Verify crimp tool calibration on a defined schedule and retire worn dies.
  • Standardise field crews on a single manufacturer-specified crimp tool and die set per connector type.
  • Include a pull-test step in the acceptance procedure for any newly terminated connector before it is placed in service.
Related References

SOP: SOP-CON-07 (Connector Crimp Termination and Acceptance Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-26 (crimp specification), manufacturer crimp tool instructions

L823-378 Incorrect Crimp Position

Symptom — A crimp is found compressed on the wrong section of the contact barrel — too far forward, too far back, or straddling the insulation/conductor transition — producing a joint that may pass an initial continuity check but is mechanically unreliable.

Equipment / System

L-823 connector crimp contact barrel and the conductor strip length/insertion depth used during termination.

Possible Causes
HighConductor stripped to the wrong length, leaving the crimp compressed partly on insulation or with excess bare wire beyond the contact
HighContact not fully bottomed in the crimp tool's locating groove before the crimp cycle, offsetting the compression point
MedWrong contact size used for the wire gauge, so even a correctly positioned crimp does not seat properly
MedField termination performed without a strip-length gauge, relying on estimation
LowContact barrel visually similar between two different part numbers, leading to a mismatched selection
Required Tools
Wire strippers with gauge-matched stopsStrip-length gauge specific to the connector typeManufacturer-specified crimp tool with locating grooveMagnifying inspection lightReplacement crimp contacts
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before disassembling or re-terminating a connector.
  • Do not attempt to re-crimp over an incorrectly positioned crimp — cut back and start with a fresh contact and conductor end.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationVisually inspect the crimp location relative to the conductor/insulation transition and the contact barrel's designed crimp zone.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, pull-test the joint and check resistance across it even if it initially passes continuity.
  3. MeasurementCompare the strip length used against the manufacturer's specified dimension for that connector.
  4. DecisionCrimp positioned outside the specified zone → cut back and re-terminate correctly. Crimp within the specified zone and passes pull test → no fault, reassemble.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm which specific contact is affected before disturbing the rest of the connector.
  6. Root CauseIdentify whether the root cause is a missing strip-length gauge in the field kit or a training gap, and address accordingly.
Test Points
Crimp position relative to manufacturer's specified zone
Pull-test result
Resistance across the reterminated joint
Strip length used vs. specification
Root Cause Verification

After re-terminating with the correct strip length and crimp position, pull-test and verify continuity/resistance before reassembly and re-energising.

Corrective Action
  • Cut back the conductor and re-terminate at the correct strip length and crimp position.
  • Replace the contact if the barrel has been deformed by the incorrect crimp.
  • Reassemble and re-seat the connector fully.
Preventive Action
  • Issue strip-length gauges as standard kit for any crew performing L-823 terminations.
  • Include crimp-position spot checks in the acceptance procedure for newly terminated connectors.
  • Reinforce correct strip-length and crimp-zone training during periodic crew refreshers.
Related References

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-26, manufacturer crimp tool instructions

L823-379 Damaged Contact

Symptom — A connector pin or socket is found deformed, cracked, chipped, or otherwise physically damaged on inspection, independent of any burning or overheating, typically discovered during a fault investigation or scheduled base-can service.

Equipment / System

L-823 connector pin and socket contacts, assessed for mechanical condition independent of thermal or crimp-related faults.

Possible Causes
HighRepeated mis-alignment during mating over time has deformed the pin tip or socket opening
HighA previous forced mating attempt (connector jammed together at an angle) cracked or chipped the contact
MedCorrosion has pitted or thinned the contact surface to the point of mechanical weakness
MedA foreign object or debris in the base can was driven into the contact during a previous mating
LowManufacturing defect in the contact plating or base metal
LowContact damaged during a prior disassembly using an incorrect extraction tool
Required Tools
L-823 connector gauge/go-no-go toolMagnifying inspection lightReplacement crimp contacts and extraction toolTrue-RMS multimeter (CAT III)
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before disassembling the connector for inspection.
  • Handle removed contacts carefully and account for all small parts to prevent FOD on the pavement.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationNote whether the damage is isolated to one contact or present on both halves of the mating pair, which may indicate a chronic mis-alignment issue rather than a one-off event.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, use a go-no-go gauge to confirm whether the damaged contact still meets dimensional tolerance.
  3. MeasurementInspect the mating half for corresponding wear or damage that would explain a repeated mis-alignment.
  4. DecisionContact fails the go-no-go gauge or shows visible cracking/chipping → replace it. Contact passes gauge and shows only minor cosmetic wear → clean and monitor.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific pin or socket rather than replacing the full connector body unnecessarily.
  6. Root CauseDetermine whether the damage is a one-off installation error or a recurring pattern pointing to a base-can or cable-slack issue causing repeated mis-alignment.
Test Points
Go-no-go gauge result on the damaged contact
Condition of the mating half
Alignment of connector housings when mated
Continuity and resistance after contact replacement
Root Cause Verification

After replacing the damaged contact, confirm alignment on remating, pull-test, and verify continuity before closing out.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the damaged contact using the correct extraction and insertion tooling.
  • Replace the mating half as well if it shows corresponding damage.
  • Correct any base-can or cable-routing issue that is causing repeated mis-alignment.
Preventive Action
  • Include a go-no-go gauge check in the periodic PM cycle for connectors in high-traffic or previously damaged locations.
  • Train crews on correct extraction tooling to avoid damaging contacts during disassembly.
  • Address base-can layout or cable-slack issues that force connectors to mate at an angle.
Related References

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-26

Rev 0 — Initial issue
L823-380 Bent Contact

Symptom — A connector pin is visibly bent out of its normal axial alignment, either preventing full mating or causing a marginal/intermittent connection once forced together.

Equipment / System

L-823 plug contact pins, assessed specifically for bending or misalignment relative to the receptacle socket axis.

Possible Causes
HighThe connector was mated at an angle or forced together without proper alignment, bending the pin on insertion
HighA dropped or mishandled plug during storage or transport bent the exposed pin before installation
MedRepeated mating/unmating cycles without care have gradually walked the pin out of true alignment
MedExcessive cable tension pulling the plug sideways while mated has stressed the pin over time
LowA manufacturing defect left the pin slightly out of tolerance from the outset
Required Tools
L-823 connector gauge/go-no-go toolFine needle-nose pliers or manufacturer-approved pin-straightening tool (for minor correction only)Magnifying inspection lightReplacement crimp contacts
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before handling the connector.

  5. Fault LocationDistinguish between a location-specific systemic cause and a component-batch-specific cause before deciding the scope of corrective action.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the corrective action addresses the systemic driver identified, not merely the most recent symptom.
Test Points
Historical failure mode pattern from work order records
Base-can/cable slack and routing condition
Comparative bench test of suspect batch connectors
Post-correction monitoring interval results
Root Cause Verification

After corrective action, place the location on a shortened monitoring interval for several cycles and confirm the failure pattern does not recur before returning it to the standard PM schedule.

Corrective Action
  • Correct the identified underlying mechanical or environmental condition at the location (cable slack, base-can drainage, routing).
  • Replace the connector with an upgraded or better-suited type if the standard part is inadequate for that location's conditions.
  • Proactively replace other connectors from a confirmed defective batch.
Preventive Action
  • Flag locations with two or more repeat connector failures for root-cause review rather than routine like-for-like replacement.
  • Track connector batch/lot numbers to enable early detection of systemic quality issues.
  • Feed recurring-failure locations into the design/engineering review process for a permanent fix where a like-for-like repair keeps failing.
