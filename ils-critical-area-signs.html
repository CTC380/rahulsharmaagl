What are ILS critical area signs?

A pair of signs back to back at the edge of the area that protects the ILS signal. Red says stop short before you enter. Yellow says you are clear after you leave.

holding position sign (red / white)
boundary sign (yellow / black)
ILS critical area
The Fact FAA AIM & AC 150/5340-18

An aircraft or vehicle standing inside the ILS critical area can disturb the signal that a landing aircraft is flying. Two signs manage that edge. One tells a pilot where to stop, the other tells a pilot when the area has been left behind, so neither needs to guess from the pavement alone.

1The red sign: stop short

The ILS critical area holding position sign is white on red, like every mandatory instruction sign, and reads ILS. When air traffic control instructs, pilots and vehicle drivers stop short of it, keeping every part of the aircraft or vehicle outside the area.

2The yellow sign: you are clear

The ILS critical area boundary sign is black on yellow and carries a graphic of the ladder-style marking. It faces a pilot leaving the area, and is another visual cue that the aircraft is clear of it, as a runway boundary sign is for the runway.

3One line on the pavement

Both signs stand next to the ILS holding position marking, a ladder-style pair of lines across the taxiway. Sign and marking are read together, so the limit of the area is the same whether it is seen on the ground or on the sign.

ElementRule

These two signs are FAA signs, described in the AIM and AC 150/5340-18. ICAO practice protects the ILS area with CAT II/III holding positions (Sign Type 12). Which applies, and its exact format, depends on your State, so confirm against the edition in force and your regulator.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What are ILS critical area signs?

Two signs at the edge of an ILS critical area. The ILS critical area holding position sign is white on red and reads ILS. The ILS critical area boundary sign is black on yellow with a ladder graphic.

Q2What is an ILS critical area?

The area around the localizer and glide slope antennas where an aircraft or vehicle can disturb the ILS signal. When the ILS is in use in low visibility, air traffic control may ask pilots to hold short of it.

Q3What is the difference between the two signs?

The red ILS sign faces aircraft approaching the area and means stop short when instructed. The yellow boundary sign faces aircraft leaving the area and tells them they are clear of it.

Q4What marking goes with these signs?

The ladder-style ILS holding position marking on the pavement. Both signs stand next to it, so the sign and the marking are read together.
