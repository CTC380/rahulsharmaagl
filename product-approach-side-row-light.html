Product Overview

Approach Side Row Light

What It Is

Approach side-row lights form part of configured approach lighting systems, providing lateral visual references on either side of the extended runway centreline.

With centreline lights and crossbars they create a recognizable geometric pattern. They are particularly important in precision approach Category II and III systems, supporting operations in reduced visibility.

Helps Pilots Assess

  • Runway alignment
  • Lateral position
  • Roll attitude
  • Approach orientation
  • Aircraft relationship to the runway centreline

Selected According To

  • Approach category and lighting system type
  • Runway geometry and threshold location
  • Centreline and crossbar arrangement
  • Touchdown-zone lighting gauge
  • Side-row length and fixture spacing
  • Light colour, optical direction, photometric performance
  • Mounting arrangement and support structure
  • Electrical circuit and control requirements
  • Project technical specification

The final arrangement should follow the approved approach-lighting layout and applicable aerodrome standard.

Function

A Lateral Frame Around the Centreline

Role

The approach centreline gives the longitudinal reference; the side rows give a lateral frame around it. They are an integral element of the complete approach-lighting geometry.

The Combined Arrangement Helps Interpret

  • Runway approach alignment
  • Lateral deviation and roll attitude
  • Approach orientation
  • Distance and perspective
  • Relationship between centreline and threshold
Operational Role

Why a Failed Side Row Matters

Under US rules (14 CFR 91.175), a pilot may not descend below 100 ft above touchdown zone elevation using the approach lights as the visual reference unless the red side-row bars or red terminating bars are distinctly visible. A failed or misaligned side row can therefore affect whether the approach can continue.

Annex 14 also sets serviceability levels for approach lighting, higher for Category II/III, and limits adjacent failed lights. Plan maintenance and spares for side-row fixtures against those levels.

Side-Row Position

  • Not more than 22.5 m

The relationship with TDZ lights maintains a consistent visual geometry between the approach area and the runway touchdown zone.

Side-Row Longitudinal Spacing

Side-row lights normally share the longitudinal spacing of the centreline lights; the innermost side-row lights are located 30 m from the threshold. Where the serviceability level can be demonstrated, a 60 m option is permitted.

Side Rows & Crossbars

Centreline + Side Rows + Crossbars as One System

ICAO Category II/III

  • Crossbar at 150 m — fills visual gaps between centreline and side-row elements
  • Crossbar at 300 m — strong transverse reference within the approach system
  • Crossbars connect or visually relate centreline and side-row elements

Geometry

Side Row → Crossbar → Centreline → Crossbar → Side Row creates a three-dimensional visual reference: longitudinal + lateral + transverse. Select side rows together with the corresponding centreline and crossbar assemblies.

Light Colour

Colour

Verify Against

FAA ALSF-2

FAA ALSF-2 Side-Row Configuration

ALSF-2

A high-intensity approach lighting system for Category II/III instrument approaches, providing runway alignment, height perception, roll guidance and horizontal references. Typical arrangement: green threshold lights, red side-row bars, high-intensity white lights and sequenced flashers.

Side-Row Bars

Nine side-row bars on each side of the ALS centreline (18 bars, 54 red lamps in total), 100 ft longitudinal spacing, three red lamps per bar.

This is a system-specific FAA arrangement and should not be assumed identical to an ICAO Category II/III layout.

FAQ

Approach Side Row Light — Frequently Asked Questions

What are approach side-row lights?

Approach side-row lights form part of configured approach lighting systems, providing lateral visual references on either side of the extended runway centreline. With centreline lights and crossbars they create a recognizable geometric pattern, and they are particularly important in precision approach Category II and III systems, helping pilots assess runway alignment, lateral position, roll attitude and approach orientation.

What does the ICAO Category II/III approach lighting system comprise?

It comprises one centreline row wherever possible 900 m from the threshold, two side rows extending 270 m (240 m where the serviceability level can be demonstrated), and two crossbars at 150 m and 300 m. The approved project drawing controls the actual stationing, spacing and geometry.

What is the side-row gauge and why must it match the TDZ gauge?

ICAO specifies the gauge between the innermost side-row lights as not less than 18 m and not more than 22.5 m, preferably 18 m, and in any case equal to the touchdown-zone-light gauge. That relationship maintains a consistent visual geometry between the approach area and the runway touchdown zone, so check the side-row layout against the TDZ layout rather than reviewing it independently.

How long are the side rows and what is the longitudinal spacing?

Side rows normally extend 270 m from the threshold, or 240 m where the serviceability level can be demonstrated. Side-row lights normally share the longitudinal spacing of the centreline lights, with the innermost side-row lights located 30 m from the threshold; a 60 m option is permitted where the serviceability level can be demonstrated. The reduction is not a general product option but a system-design decision linked to demonstrated serviceability and the approved arrangement.

What colour are approach side-row lights?

ICAO Category II/III side rows provide red visual references, and FAA ALSF-2 uses steady-burning red side-row-bar lamps. Red side-row lighting should not be applied generically to every approach lighting system, so verify against the approach category and authority, the approach lighting system and project drawing, and the fixture's photometric specification.

What is the FAA ALSF-2 side-row configuration?

ALSF-2 is a high-intensity approach lighting system for Category II/III instrument approaches with green threshold lights, red side-row bars, white lights and sequenced flashers. It has nine side-row bars on each side of the centreline (18 bars, 54 red lamps in total) at 100 ft longitudinal spacing, three red lamps per bar. This is system-specific and should not be assumed identical to an ICAO Category II/III layout; ALSF-1 uses red terminating bars instead of side rows.

Why does a failed side row matter operationally?

Under US rules (14 CFR 91.175), a pilot may not descend below 100 ft above touchdown zone elevation using the approach lights as the visual reference unless the red side-row bars or red terminating bars are distinctly visible, so a failed or misaligned side row can affect whether the approach can continue. Annex 14 also sets serviceability levels for approach lighting, higher for Category II/III, and limits adjacent failed lights, so plan maintenance and spares against those levels.

How is a side row different from a crossbar or a sequenced flasher?

A side row is a continuous lateral reference parallel to the approach centreline, typically red in Category II/III, giving lateral and roll guidance. A crossbar is a transverse reference across the centreline, typically white, giving a horizontal reference. Sequenced flashers are a separate dynamic element; FAA ALSF-2 includes 15 sequenced flashers operating from the outer approach area toward the threshold, and the different light functions should remain visually distinguishable.

How are approach side-row lights powered and controlled?

A simplified series-circuit arrangement is CCR, primary circuit, isolating transformer, secondary cable and the side-row fixture; for other architectures the approved system design governs. Verify operating current and circuit type, power consumption and CCR requirements, secondary connection, and the control interface and monitoring. The system may provide high, medium and low intensity or multiple intensity steps, and without considering those settings a fixture may not deliver the required visual balance with the centreline and crossbar elements.

What are the common faults in approach side-row lighting?

Typical symptoms are a side-row light that is off, low red intensity, an incorrect side-row position, an uneven appearance and a poor lateral reference. Troubleshoot in sequence: approach lighting control, CCR or power, primary circuit, transformer or local supply, secondary connection, side-row fixture, light source, optical assembly, alignment and photometry. For system-level visual problems also verify centreline, side row, crossbar, threshold and TDZ geometry.

What should be confirmed before ordering approach side-row lights?

Confirm the runway designation and threshold position, approach category, approach lighting system type and ICAO, FAA or authority basis, side-row requirement and length, centreline and crossbar arrangement, TDZ gauge and side-row gauge, longitudinal spacing and first-light station, light colour, optical direction, intensity steps, support structure and frangibility, and electrical and control details. Request the side-row layout drawing, photometric report, red chromaticity data, mounting drawing and compliance certificates.

Engineering Note

Approach side-row lights should not be specified independently from the rest of the approach-lighting system.

For an ICAO Category II/III installation, the side-row gauge, longitudinal spacing, side-row length, centreline spacing, crossbar positions and TDZ-light relationship must be coordinated as one geometric system.

For FAA projects, identify the approach system first: ALSF-2, ALSF-1, MALSR, MALSF or other FAA configurations can have materially different side-row and centreline arrangements. FAA identifies ALSF-2 specifically with red side-row bars and sequenced flashers; ALSF-1 uses red terminating bars instead of side rows.
