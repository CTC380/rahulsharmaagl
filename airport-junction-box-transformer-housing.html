Airport Junction Box & Transformer Housing

The physical infrastructure of AGL: L-867 and L-868 light bases, cable entry, drainage, connectors, loading, frangibility, installation, inspection and maintenance.

Start Learning L-867 vs L-868
Overview

Junction Boxes, Transformer Housings & Light Bases

The physical infrastructure that supports and protects AGL equipment.

These components provide mounting, cable entry, transformer accommodation, electrical connections and mechanical protection for airport lighting equipment. Design must account for the lighting application, aircraft and vehicle loading, installation depth, pavement construction, cable routing, drainage, corrosion, connector access, maintenance, alignment, environmental exposure and frangibility where applicable.

In FAA terminology, Type L-867 bases suit airport light fixtures, transformer housings and junction boxes subject to occasional light-vehicle loading. Type L-868 bases suit in-pavement fixtures, series-circuit transformer housings and junction boxes subject to aircraft and other heavy-vehicle loading. The governing specification is FAA AC 150/5345-42K (30 September 2024), which the FAA lists as Active.

Light base

The mounting interface for a fixture, and often also transformer housing and junction box.

Transformer housing

Physically accommodates the isolation transformer. The transformer does the electrical work.

Junction box

A protected location for cable connections and maintenance access.

Definitions

Junction Box, Transformer Housing & Light Base

The three functions often overlap in one component.

Airport junction box

A protected enclosure or base for electrical cable connections. May route primary and secondary cables, hold connection points, accommodate isolation transformers and give maintenance access.

Transformer housing

The enclosure or base accommodating an AGL isolation transformer: mechanical and environmental protection, cable entry, transformer positioning, connector and maintenance access.

Airport light base

The physical mounting interface for a fixture. Depending on type it also serves as transformer housing, junction box and cable connection point.

FAA Type L-867 is specified for use as a light-fixture mounting base, transformer housing and electrical junction box. A junction box in an AGL circuit may sit in the path CCR → primary cable → junction / connection point → isolation transformer → secondary cable → fixture, and may contain cable terminations, transformer, connectors, cable loops, grounding connections, fixture connections and spare cable.

Installation Depth

Shallow and Deep Bases

Engineering terms, not FAA type designations.

"Shallow base" and "deep base" describe physical installation arrangements and should not be confused with L-867 / L-868. Depth and configuration depend on fixture type, pavement thickness, cable routing, transformer size, installation depth, drainage, structural requirements, maintenance access and project drawings. A deeper base gives more volume for transformers and cable routing; a shallower one suits restricted depth. Selection follows the approved installation design.

Applications

In-Pavement and Elevated Light Bases

Different exposure, different base.

In-pavement base (L-868)

Supports fixtures flush or substantially flush with the pavement, exposed to aircraft wheel loads, heavy vehicles, pavement movement, water, deicing chemicals and temperature cycling. Must give a stable, accurately aligned interface.

Elevated base (L-867 + L-894)

Mounts an elevated fixture via an L-867 base and an L-894 cover/baseplate, providing fixture support, bolt interface, transformer receptacle positioning, drainage and a frangible connection interface.

Loads & Testing

Load Environment and Certification Testing

Selection is by loading, not only electrical function.

L-867: 250 psi

Certification load test applied uniformly through a rubber block, three loadings, no permanent deformation, cracking or bolt-torque loss.

L-868: 450 psi

Same method at 450 psi for the aircraft and heavy-vehicle environment; also fatigue, impact and flange bolt torque tests where non-A36 materials are used.

L-894: 2,500 lb

Static compressive load, with a bending moment of 2,500 ft-lb for L-804 and 700 ft-lb for other applications.

Certification includes weld-integrity (air-pressure leakage), dimensional, coating-thickness and visual tests, a 21-day potassium acetate test at 194 °F (90 °C) for Class IB/IIB, a temperature-shock test for non-metallic Class II, and torque tests for adjustable L-868 bases. Production lots are sampled to ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 at AQL 2.5. The 2024 revision added corrosion-prevention considerations and new load-test requirements.

"Fits the fixture" does not mean "is an approved replacement". A visually similar base can differ in wall thickness, flange geometry, material, strength, corrosion protection, connector interface and load performance.

Cable Entry

Cable Entry, Conduit and Routing

Entry must avoid bending, abrasion, water traps and connector strain.

Entry options

Bottom, side or conduit entry, or direct-buried cable, for primary series-circuit and secondary fixture cable. Standard bases have two conduit entrances near the bottom; number, type, size and location can be varied to project needs.

Conduit connections

Coordinate with wall thickness, cable size, bend radius, sealing, drainage and corrosion protection. Sharp edges inside the body must be removed to prevent cable chafing.

Routing inside the base

Keep cable from preventing fixture seating, damaging connectors, interfering with the transformer, making sharp bends, being trapped under a cover or retaining water. Provide slack where the approved procedure requires it.

Do not drill or modify a certified light base casually. Modifying a standard certified component makes it non-standard and voids certification. The AC's only exceptions are cutting additional conduit holes during installation and installing threaded inserts in the upper flange, because they do not affect structural integrity. Even then, cutting must be supervised by the Construction Manager and corrosion protection applied as directed. Coatings only work while the surface is unbroken.

Connector interfaces follow the L-823 plug and receptacle specification (AC 150/5345-26). For elevated mounting, the transformer receptacle (typically L-823 Class A, Type II, Class 8) must be positioned so its mating face is at the yield point and will not be dislodged by separation from the plug, with drainage below the yield point so no water builds above the mating surface.

The base needs working space for connector installation, transformer replacement, cable inspection, electrical testing, cleaning and fault diagnosis. Poor access can turn a simple transformer swap into a major maintenance operation.

Drainage

Drainage in Airport Light Bases

Good base design = electrical protection + mechanical protection + drainage.

The conduit/base system should have drains at low points so water and deicing fluids drain away. If a drain is specified it is provided in the bottom of the base; if not specified, it should be 3/4 in (19 mm) in diameter. If water and deicing fluids pool for long periods, corrosion of the conduit and base system accelerates.

What standing water causes

  • Corrosion and accelerated metal degradation
  • Connector deterioration
  • Cable insulation damage
  • Transformer problems and ground faults
  • Intermittent lighting

Surface water protection

  • Grooved spacer ring with O-ring gasket seals the fixture/base interface on L-868
  • Protective dam stops grout or sealant sealing the fixture in place
  • Elevated covers are flat or sloped to drain

Installation

  • Plan drainage before concrete placement
  • Light base → low point → drain → drainage path
  • Keep water from the transformer, connectors, cable, flange and fixture interface

If water is found in a base, determine why before just removing it. Persistent water usually indicates a drainage, sealing or environmental problem.

Alignment

Spacer Rings, Extensions, Level and Azimuth

The base sets the physical reference for the fixture.

AccessoryFunction
Flat spacer ringHeight adjustment for L-867 or L-868
Grooved spacer ringWith an O-ring gasket, seals the in-pavement fixture to the base against surface water (L-868)
Beveled spacer ringLevel and/or height correction for out-of-level bases
Azimuth correction spacer ringCorrects the alignment of fixtures on misaligned L-868 bases
Adapter ringConverts an existing base bolt circle to a different fixture bolt circle, with at least 1/2 in thread engagement
Base extensionHeight adjustment when spacer rings cannot reach it; minimum length 1-3/4 in (L-867) or 2-1/4 in (L-868)

A maximum of three rings may be stacked, and stacked height should not exceed 2.25 in (57 mm); beyond that, use an extension. A base that is tilted, rotated, mislocated or at the wrong elevation causes problems with aiming, fixture alignment, photometric performance, pavement interface, drainage and mechanical fit — especially for centreline, directional, approach and PAPI-related fixtures.

Frangibility

Frangibility Considerations

Evaluate the base together with the complete fixture mounting assembly.

Frangible device
Elevated fixture

L-894 baseplates mate with a male-threaded frangible device. The height to the frangible-device breakpoint, once threaded into the baseplate, must be no higher than 3 inches above the surrounding grade (per AC 150/5300-13), and the hub height must comply with AC 150/5345-46 and AC 150/5220-23. Standard thread sizes are 1-1/2 in × 12 UNF and 2 in × 11.5 NPT/NPS. A neoprene gasket (nominal 1/8 in) seals cover to base, and the cover needs a ground lug or bolt for at least #6 AWG.

The L-895 stake alternative uses 2 × 2 × 3/16 in steel angle, 30 in long as standard; L-804 fixtures must not be stake mounted.

Materials

Materials, Corrosion Protection & Grounding

Corrosion protection

Ferrous parts must be treated. Class I bases, extensions and rings thicker than 1/4 in are hot-dip galvanized (ASTM A123); thinner plates and rings are zinc plated (ASTM B633) or galvanized. Zinc-dust primer touch-up must not exceed 10% of the treated area.

Bolts

Per EB 83A: 18-8 stainless steel typical, coated steel permitted. Anti-seize is recommended for stainless bolts and is not supplied by the base manufacturer.

Grounding

Each base has an internal and external ground strap connected to a ground lug. Never use an exothermic weld on a galvanized base: heat damages the zinc coating and field repair is usually ineffective.

Appropriate separation material is required between dissimilar metals to prevent galvanic action. Coating damage from drilling, welding or cutting accelerates corrosion of the exposed steel, so field modifications must be controlled and corrosion protection restored.

Standards

Standards and Applicability

FAA AC 150/5345-42K — Specification for Airport Light Bases, Transformer Housings, Junction Boxes, and Accessories (30 September 2024; FAA status Active; it cancels 42J). It covers light bases, transformer housings, junction boxes, accessories, elevated light covers and stakes, and sets type, class, size, dimensions, materials, protective coatings, fabrication, load testing, qualification and production testing. Only equipment meeting it is listed in AC 150/5345-53 six months after the issue date.

Use is mandatory for projects funded under federal grant programs including AIP, and for PFC-funded projects, and recommended for other airport operators. L-867 and L-868 are FAA designations: the applicable standard at an airport depends on the governing aviation authority and project requirements. Related references: AC 150/5345-26 (L-823 connectors), AC 150/5345-46 (fixtures), AC 150/5340-30 (visual aid installation details), AC 150/5220-23 (frangible connections), EB 83A (in-pavement fixture bolts).

Pitfalls

8 Common Installation Mistakes

1. Wrong base type

An L-867 where an L-868 heavy-load base is required.

2. Poor drainage

Water trapped around the transformer and connectors.

3. Incorrect elevation

Fixture too high or too low relative to the pavement.

4. Poor alignment

Base installed at the wrong azimuth.

5. Damaging the protective coating

Cutting or drilling without restoring corrosion protection.

6. Connectors under tension

Incorrect connector routing.

7. Ignoring access

Transformer or connectors where maintenance is needlessly hard.

8. Treating the base as generic hardware

Using an unapproved enclosure chosen only on dimensions.

Why It Matters

More Than an Enclosure

A junction box or transformer housing is not a generic outdoor box. AGL components may need to satisfy aircraft and vehicle loading, frangibility, pavement and buried installation, water exposure, deicing chemicals, connector systems, standardized dimensions and fixture mounting. They form the physical interface between civil infrastructure, electrical infrastructure, AGL equipment, maintenance access and environmental protection.

Select the correct base. Protect the circuit. Control water. Maintain alignment. Preserve the approved installation.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is an airport junction box?

A protected enclosure for electrical connections that, depending on application, may also serve as a transformer housing or light-fixture mounting base.

Q2What is an AGL junction box?

A junction enclosure specifically intended for Airfield Ground Lighting electrical connections and associated components.

Q3What is an airport transformer housing?

A protected physical location for an AGL isolation transformer and its associated cable connections.

Q4What is an airport light base?

A structural mounting component that supports airport lighting fixtures and may also function as a transformer housing or junction box.

Q5What is an L-867 light base?

An FAA base type for airport light fixtures, transformer housings and electrical junction boxes subject to occasional light-vehicle loading but no aircraft or heavy vehicle loading.

Q6What is an L-868 light base?

An FAA base type for in-pavement airport light fixtures, series-circuit transformer housings and junction boxes subject to aircraft and other heavy-vehicle loading.

Q7What is the difference between L-867 and L-868?

The loading environment. L-867 is for occasional light-vehicle loading; L-868 is for aircraft and heavy-vehicle loading. Certification load tests are 250 psi and 450 psi respectively.

Q8Can an L-867 be used for an in-pavement runway light?

Not automatically. The type must follow the approved design and loading requirements. FAA identifies L-868 for in-pavement fixtures subject to aircraft or heavy-vehicle loading.

Q9What is an airport light base drain?

A drainage provision that lets water and other liquids leave the base and conduit system instead of pooling around cables, transformers and connectors.

Q10Why is drainage important in AGL bases?

Standing water can accelerate corrosion and contribute to electrical and connector problems. The FAA specification calls for drains at low points in the base and conduit system.

Q11Can an airport light base be drilled on site?

Only per the approved installation procedure. Modifying certified components makes them non-standard and voids certification, except for additional conduit holes and threaded inserts in the upper flange, with supervision and corrosion protection.

Q12What is an L-894?

An FAA designation for a non-load-bearing elevated light cover (baseplate) that mounts on an L-867 light base.

Q13What is an L-895?

An FAA designation for an elevated light stake mounting, used when an elevated fixture is not installed on a light base.

Q14What do Class IA, IB, IIA and IIB mean?

IA and IB are metal; IIA and IIB are non-metallic. The B classes have also passed corrosion testing against deicing fluids containing potassium acetate.

Q15Why are airport light bases different from ordinary electrical junction boxes?

They may have to handle aircraft or vehicle loads, environmental exposure, drainage, fixture alignment, frangibility and standardized dimensions.

Q16Can an airport transformer housing be used as a junction box?

Yes. The FAA specifies L-867 and L-868 bases to serve as both transformer housing and electrical junction box.

Q17What causes water inside an airport light base?

Blocked drainage, poor drainage design, damaged seals, incorrect installation or surface-water entry.

Q18What causes corrosion in airport light bases?

Water accumulation, damaged protective coatings, deicing chemicals, soil exposure and dissimilar-metal contact.

Q19How do I select an airport light base?

By fixture type, loading environment, base size, installation method, electrical arrangement, drainage, material class and applicable aviation authority requirements.

Q20What standards apply to airport light bases?

For FAA projects, FAA AC 150/5345-42K. Other projects follow their governing aviation authority and project specification.

Q21Does every airport have to use FAA L-867 or L-868 bases?

No. L-867 and L-868 are FAA designations; the applicable standard depends on the governing aviation authority and project requirements.

Q22Why is light-base alignment important?

The base fixes the physical position and orientation of the fixture, so a misaligned base makes the required fixture orientation and photometric performance hard to achieve.
