AGL Photometric Testing Guide

The practical field guide to verifying that runway, taxiway, approach and PAPI lighting delivers the required intensity, distribution, colour and alignment — equipment, field measurement, evaluation, reporting and troubleshooting.

Start Learning Jump to the Procedure
Overview

What Is AGL Photometry?

The measurement and evaluation of the photometric performance of Airfield Ground Lighting systems.

AGL photometry verifies whether installed runway, taxiway, approach and PAPI lighting provides the required light intensity, distribution, colour and alignment for its operational function. It is carried out during commissioning, periodic inspection, maintenance, rehabilitation, troubleshooting and performance verification. A properly planned inspection helps identify degraded light output, incorrect beam alignment, colour deviations, optical contamination and installation problems that can affect the visual aids pilots rely on.

The objective is not simply to determine whether a light is ON or OFF. A fixture can operate electrically and still fail photometrically — through optical degradation, incorrect aiming, contamination, damaged lenses or filters, incorrect installation, lamp or LED degradation, mechanical movement, incorrect transformer or circuit conditions, or measurement setup errors.

Electrical Serviceability

Does the fixture light up and carry current correctly?

Photometric Serviceability

Does the light deliver the required intensity, distribution, colour and alignment?

Two Different Checks

Electrical serviceability and photometric serviceability are different aspects of AGL inspection.

Purpose

Why Is Photometric Testing Required?

Objective evidence that the installed system continues to perform to the applicable design and acceptance requirements.

New Installation

Verify performance of newly installed lighting before commissioning and operational acceptance.

Commissioning

Establish that the completed installation meets the specified photometric requirements.

Periodic Inspection

Identify deterioration in lighting performance over time.

Preventive Maintenance

Verify performance after maintenance activities and component replacement.

Rehabilitation

Establish the condition of existing lighting before and after rehabilitation work.

Troubleshooting

Investigate abnormal performance, uneven brightness, incorrect beam coverage or alignment problems.

Airport audits and acceptance. Photometric measurements can form part of technical inspection, acceptance documentation and airport lighting performance records.

Equipment

AGL Photometric Testing Equipment

A professional survey needs suitable, calibrated equipment configured for the lighting facility under test.

Airport Photometer

Measures photometric performance of airfield lighting — intensity, angular distribution, colour, beam characteristics and values at defined positions. It should suit the type of AGL facility under test.

Runway Photometer

Used for runway edge, threshold, end, centreline, touchdown zone and approach lighting. The arrangement must correspond to the applicable test geometry and requirements.

PAPI Photometer

Evaluates PAPI optical characteristics: intensity, beam coverage and alignment, colour transition, vertical angular characteristics and horizontal coverage. Highly sensitive to geometry and alignment.

Setup

Measurement Frame & Sensor Positioning

The physical position and orientation of the sensor are critical.

Errors in sensor height, distance, horizontal position, vertical angle, azimuth, fixture orientation or reference point can produce incorrect photometric results.

Establish the measurement setup before testing and keep it consistent throughout the survey.

Visual Approach Slope

PAPI Photometry

Its photometric performance is closely tied to the accuracy of the visual glide-path indication given to the pilot.

Intensity

Verify the PAPI light units produce the required light output.

Beam Coverage

The beam should provide the required horizontal and vertical coverage.

Beam Alignment

Each unit's angular position is critical — guidance relies on the relationship between the red and white portions of the beam.

Colour

The white-to-red transition must be clearly defined and within applicable requirements.

Vertical Angle

Vertical angular characteristics of each unit are a key part of performance verification.

Geometry

Measurement geometry matters as much as the readings themselves.

PAPI photometry is not simply an intensity test. Measurement geometry, beam alignment and colour transition are equally important.

Procedure

The AGL Photometry Procedure

A structured procedure gives repeatable, traceable results.

1. Preparation

Review the test requirement; identify the lighting facilities to be tested; review drawings, runway/taxiway configuration and previous photometric reports; prepare test forms; verify equipment and calibration status; coordinate with airport operations and ATC as required.

2. Calibration verification

Verify photometer ID, certificate and validity, sensor condition, power supply, software and settings. Record equipment details in the report.

3. System inspection

Visually check for damaged fixtures, dirty lenses, broken components, incorrect orientation, loose fittings, obstructions, water ingress, cable or connector issues, transformer issues and abnormal light output — separating physical problems from true photometric deficiencies.

4. Measurement setup

Confirm reference point, distance, sensor height and orientation, horizontal and vertical position, angle and fixture identification. Keep the method consistent.

5. Field measurement

Operate at the required condition and record at selected units, defined points, CCR intensity steps and angular positions. Each measurement must be traceable to its location.

6. Data evaluation

Compare against applicable requirements: intensity, distribution, colour, alignment, coverage, uniformity. Do not fail a fixture merely because it looks dim.

7. Failure identification

Identify the probable cause: degradation, contamination, aiming, optical damage, LED/lamp, transformer, connector, cable, CCR output, installation error or measurement error.

8. Rectification

Clean, realign, replace fixture or optical parts, replace transformer, repair connector or cable, investigate the CCR, correct mechanical faults.

9. Retest

Repeat the relevant measurement to confirm the problem is resolved and the requirement is now met.

10. Final report

Issue a complete, traceable report: project and airport/runway details, test date, weather/visibility where relevant, equipment and calibration information, methodology, measurement locations, raw and evaluated results, pass/fail status, deficiencies, corrective actions, retest results, photographs, test personnel and approval/sign-off.

Photometry and the AGL Electrical Chain

Photometry should never be considered independently from the electrical system.

A photometric deficiency may require investigation across the complete chain. For low light intensity, work from the fixture back: fixture condition → optical system → secondary connection → isolating transformer → primary circuit → CCR output. This avoids replacing a fixture when the real problem is elsewhere in the series circuit.

Operating Condition

Photometric Testing at Different CCR Intensity Steps

Verification should consider the applicable test condition for the facility and measurement requirement.

AGL systems operate at different intensity steps through the Constant Current Regulator (CCR). The purpose is to establish whether the system delivers the required performance under the specified operating condition.

When investigating abnormal results, comparing performance across CCR steps can reveal fixture response problems, circuit abnormalities, transformer issues, CCR-related issues and non-linear or abnormal light output.

Troubleshooting

Photometry Troubleshooting Approach

When a measurement fails, avoid immediately replacing the fixture.

1. Confirm the measurement

Equipment, calibration, sensor position, measurement geometry and test settings.

2. Inspect the fixture

Lens, optical components, cleanliness, alignment, mechanical condition.

3. Check the secondary circuit

Secondary connector, transformer, fixture connection.

4. Check the primary circuit

Primary connector, cable, series circuit.

5. Check the CCR

Output current, intensity step, circuit condition, monitoring indications.

6. Repeat the measurement

After corrective action, perform a controlled retest.

Key Principles

8 Key Principles of AGL Photometry

01 — A light being ON does not prove photometric compliance.

Electrical operation and photometric performance are different checks.

02 — Measurement geometry matters.

Incorrect sensor position can produce incorrect results.

03 — Calibration matters.

Measurements need controlled, traceable equipment.

04 — Alignment matters.

A working fixture can still give incorrect visual guidance if misaligned.

05 — Colour matters.

Intensity alone does not establish complete photometric performance.

06 — Troubleshoot the complete circuit.

The cause may be upstream of the fixture.

07 — Always retest after rectification.

A repair should be verified by measurement.

08 — Record everything.

Data is valuable when traceable and comparable over time.

Measure correctly. Evaluate objectively.

Rectify systematically. Retest and record. Photometry is a complete engineering verification process — equipment, geometry, configuration, electrical systems, optical performance, alignment, colour, evaluation and corrective action.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is AGL photometry?

AGL photometry is the measurement and evaluation of the photometric performance of Airfield Ground Lighting systems, including intensity, distribution, colour and alignment as applicable.

Q2What is an airport photometer?

An airport photometer is a measurement instrument designed or configured to measure the photometric performance of airport visual aids and lighting systems.

Q3What is a runway photometer?

A runway photometer measures the photometric characteristics of runway lighting facilities such as edge, threshold, centreline and other applicable runway lights.

Q4What is PAPI photometry?

PAPI photometry is the measurement and evaluation of the optical performance of a PAPI system, including intensity, beam coverage, colour and angular characteristics.

Q5How is runway lighting tested?

Using a defined measurement procedure and suitable photometric equipment. The measurement geometry, test points and acceptance criteria depend on the particular runway lighting facility and applicable requirements.

Q6How is taxiway lighting tested?

By measuring the applicable photometric characteristics of taxiway edge, centreline, stop bar, runway guard and other installed lighting facilities.

Q7Why is runway light intensity testing important?

It provides objective evidence that the lighting system produces the required visual guidance performance under the specified test conditions.

Q8Can a light pass visual inspection but fail photometry?

Yes. A fixture may appear operational while producing inadequate intensity, incorrect beam distribution, incorrect colour or incorrect alignment.

Q9How often should AGL photometry be carried out?

The required frequency depends on the applicable aviation authority requirements, airport procedures, project specifications, operational requirements and the maintenance programme.

Q10Is photometry required after AGL maintenance?

Where specified by the maintenance procedure or applicable requirements, photometric verification can confirm that the lighting system still meets its required performance after maintenance or adjustment.

Q11What causes low AGL light intensity?

Potential causes include optical contamination, lamp or LED degradation, damaged optical components, incorrect CCR output, transformer problems, connector or cable issues, fixture degradation or measurement errors.

Q12What causes PAPI photometric failure?

Possible causes include incorrect unit alignment, optical degradation, contamination, damaged components, incorrect measurement geometry or other system configuration issues.

Q13What information should an AGL photometric report contain?

Project details, scope, equipment and calibration information, measurement methodology, test geometry, measured results, acceptance criteria, deficiencies, corrective actions, retest results and supporting records.

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Electrical Health Is Not Optical Performance
Principle

Fixtures can draw correct current while delivering poor intensity or colour. Photometric checks confirm what the pilot actually sees.

Calibrate and Document the Measurement Setup
Equipment

Record instrument identity, calibration status and sensor position. Results are only credible when the setup can be reproduced.

Control Ambient Light and Surface Conditions
Conditions

Stray light and contamination affect readings. Choose suitable conditions and clean optics before measurement, noting what was done.

Alignment Errors Can Look Like Low Intensity
Alignment

Check aiming before concluding that a unit is under-performing. A correctly aimed unit may measure very differently from a misaligned one.

Trend Results Against a Baseline
Trending

Compare each measurement with commissioning data. Gradual change is easier to see and act on than a single reading.

Report What Was Measured and How
Reporting

Include method, location, conditions and instrument details so another engineer could understand and repeat the test.
