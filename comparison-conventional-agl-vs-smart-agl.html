Systems & Technology Comparison

Conventional AGL vs Smart AGL

Conventional
VS
Smart

Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) with conventional control and periodic inspection versus AGL with an added digital layer. Conventional AGL is primarily designed around physical lighting equipment, series circuits, CCRs and manual inspection; Smart AGL adds sensing, digital control, communication, monitoring and diagnostics to the same core airfield-lighting function.

Conventional does not mean unmonitored. Annex 14 contains monitoring provisions for aerodrome lighting, particularly for runways used in low-visibility (CAT II/III) operations, and Chapter 10 sets lighting serviceability levels. Conventional AGL therefore commonly already includes CCR status/monitoring, an airfield lighting control and monitoring system (ALCMS) and, where fitted, individual lamp monitoring. The practical difference on this page is the depth of sensing, diagnostics, data and integration, not monitoring versus no monitoring. Verify exact provisions in the current edition.

Operational Data

Conventional AGL monitoring is typically performed by operator + technician + periodic inspection/testing. Smart AGL adds an information layer that can combine power regulation, digital control, equipment monitoring, fault detection, alarms, event logging, remote diagnostics and operational data.

03 · The Biggest Difference

Operate vs Operate + Know + Control + Communicate

Conventional AGL

"Make the lights operate." Lighting → Control → Inspection.

Smart AGL

"Make the lights operate + know their condition + control them + communicate their status." Lighting → Control → Sensing → Monitoring → Communication → Diagnostics → Data.

  • Centralized control, interface with airport systems
  • Fault alarms, equipment health info, historical events
05 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: Smart AGL means wireless AGL
Fact

A Smart AGL system may use fiber optic, Ethernet, copper, fieldbus, serial or industrial network communication — wireless is only one option. "Smart" refers to intelligence, sensing, control and information, not the physical communication medium.

Myth: LED AGL means Smart AGL
Fact

You can have LED + conventional CCR, or LED + smart CCR + remote monitoring. LED describes the light-source technology; "Smart" describes the integrated intelligent AGL system — they're independent layers.

Myth: Remote monitoring makes maintenance predictive automatically
Fact

Simply having remote monitoring does not automatically make maintenance predictive. Predictive maintenance requires meaningful condition data and appropriate analysis.

Myth: A circuit alarm identifies the exact failed fixture
Fact

A Smart CCR reporting a circuit abnormality does not automatically mean a specific fixture has failed, unless the AGL architecture includes suitable individual-fixture monitoring or fault-localization technology.

09 · Maintenance Philosophy

Periodic vs Condition-Based

Conventional

Periodic + preventive + corrective. Scheduled inspection → fault discovered → repair.

Smart

Can support condition-based + predictive. Continuous data → trend/abnormal condition → early warning → planned maintenance.

Caution: Simply having remote monitoring does not automatically make maintenance predictive — that requires meaningful condition data and appropriate analysis.

10 · Photometry & Electrical Compliance

Smart AGL Doesn't Replace Verification

Smart AGL does not eliminate photometric requirements. The light still needs to produce the required candela, beam distribution, horizontal/vertical distribution, colour and angular coverage — Smart AGL simply may provide better operational information around the equipment. Field photometry remains an independent verification activity where required.

Similarly, Smart AGL does not replace electrical testing: continuity, insulation resistance, dielectric withstand where applicable, earthing, CCR performance, transformer performance, cable condition and protection all still need appropriate verification. A remote system can report that a CCR is operating normally, but that does not automatically prove every electrical requirement.

Remote monitoring ≠ electrical testing, and monitoring ≠ photometric compliance. Both remain independent verification activities.

13 · Example — Runway Circuit

100 Fixtures, Two Different Experiences

Conventional

An operator can control the circuit while technicians perform inspections and tests. If a problem occurs — "Runway lighting appears abnormal" — technicians investigate the field equipment directly.

Smart

The system may report: circuit abnormality detected → CCR-03 alarm → abnormal output condition → event timestamp recorded. The technician can use this to troubleshoot more efficiently — but may still need to go to the field.

14 · Important Limitation

Smart AGL Doesn't Always Pinpoint the Failed Fixture

Smart AGL does not necessarily identify the exact failed fixture. A Smart CCR reporting "circuit abnormal → alarm" doesn't automatically mean "Fixture No. 47 failed" — unless the AGL architecture includes suitable individual-fixture monitoring or fault-localization technology. This distinction is critical when writing a technical specification.

16 · What Makes an AGL "Smart"?

A Useful Engineering Definition

An AGL system becomes "smart" when it can sense relevant operating conditions, process that information, communicate it, and support automated or informed control/maintenance decisions.

Not every Smart AGL system necessarily contains all these functions at the same level. This is a working engineering definition used on this page: "smart" and "conventional" are industry terms, not formal designations in ICAO, FAA or IEC documents, so capability should always be specified functionally.

Conventional

That is the practical value of Smart AGL.

18 · Engineering Verdict

Same Core Function, Different Layer on Top

Engineering Verdict

Conventional AGL focuses primarily on providing and controlling the required airfield lighting. Smart AGL provides the same fundamental lighting function but adds an intelligent digital layer for monitoring, control, communication, diagnostics and data management.

Smart AGL does not replace design requirements, photometric requirements, electrical testing, field inspection or commissioning — it adds better visibility and control around them.

19 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Smart AGL the same as LED AGL?

No. LED describes the light-source technology; Smart AGL describes the integration of intelligent monitoring, control, communication and diagnostic functions into the system. You can have LED with a conventional CCR, or LED with a smart CCR and remote monitoring.

Does Smart AGL have to be wireless?

No. A Smart AGL system may use fiber optic, Ethernet, copper, fieldbus, serial or industrial network communication — wireless is only one possible option among several.

Does remote monitoring replace photometric or electrical testing?

No. Remote monitoring is not equivalent to electrical testing, and monitoring is not equivalent to photometric compliance. Field photometry and electrical verification remain independent activities even on a Smart AGL system.

Can Smart AGL tell you exactly which fixture failed?

Not necessarily. A circuit-level alarm does not automatically identify the exact failed fixture unless the architecture includes suitable individual-fixture monitoring or fault-localization technology.

Does adding remote monitoring automatically enable predictive maintenance?

No. Predictive maintenance requires meaningful condition data and appropriate analysis — simply having remote monitoring does not automatically make maintenance predictive.

What does Smart AGL add during testing and commissioning?

Beyond the conventional FAT/SAT sequence, Smart AGL adds a significant integration and communication testing layer — CCR monitoring, remote control, communication, alarm testing and system integration.

21 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual airport project, verify the current applicable editions of the relevant standards and the manufacturer's documentation before using this comparison for project design.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I — Chapters 8 & 10

Aerodrome Design and Operations: electrical systems for visual aids (power supply, system design and monitoring of lighting systems, particularly for low-visibility operations) and maintenance, including lighting serviceability levels. Verify exact provisions in the current edition.

ICAO Aerodrome Design Manual, Doc 9157 Part 5

Electrical Systems: guidance on electrical practices and installations for aerodromes. Guidance material, not a Standard.

FAA AC 150/5345-10

Specification for Constant Current Regulators and Regulator Monitors: defines CCR types (L-828 regulator without monitoring, L-829 regulator with monitoring, L-827 monitor), classes (6.6 A and 20 A) and 3- or 5-step brightness styles.

FAA AC 150/5345-56

Specification for L-890 Airport Lighting Control and Monitoring System (ALCMS): minimum requirements for control and monitoring of airport lighting.

FAA AC 150/5340-26

Maintenance of Airport Visual Aid Facilities: recommended maintenance practice for lighting circuits, CCRs and ALCMS.

IEC 61820 series & IEC 61821

Electrical installations for AGL at aerodromes (including requirements for series circuits), and maintenance of AGL constant current series circuits.

IEC 62443 / Applicable Cybersecurity Frameworks

Security of industrial automation and control systems: relevant wherever gateways, networks or platforms are added around AGL control and monitoring.

DGCA / AAI Requirements (India)

For Indian airport projects, verify applicable DGCA aerodrome requirements, AAI technical requirements, airport-specific specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Smart AGL adds a digital layer around the same series-circuit lighting system. It does not change the photometric, electrical, mechanical or maintenance requirements the lighting must still meet.
