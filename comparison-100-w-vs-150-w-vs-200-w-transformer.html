Electrical & Power Systems Comparison

100 W VS 150 W VS 200 W

100 W, 150 W and 200 W indicate different transformer power capacities. A higher rating means the transformer can supply a higher rated load, subject to its specified voltage, current, VA rating and operating conditions. A higher wattage rating does not by itself mean brighter light output — actual output depends on the connected fixture and the manufacturer's specified transformer rating.

02 · Definitions

What Is a 100 W, 150 W & 200 W Transformer?

100 W Transformer

A 100 W transformer is designed for a load within its specified 100 W class: SUPPLY → [100 W] → LOAD.

It is suitable where the connected equipment requires a relatively lower power capacity.

150 W Transformer

A 150 W transformer provides 50% more rated power capacity than a 100 W transformer: SUPPLY → [150 W] → LOAD.

150 / 100 = 1.5, so a 150 W unit is 1.5 times the 100 W rating.

200 W Transformer

A 200 W transformer provides the highest capacity among the three: SUPPLY → [200 W] → HIGHER LOAD.

200 / 100 = 2, so a 200 W unit is twice the 100 W rating.

03 · Biggest Difference

Power Capacity Progression

100 W

Lower capacity — smallest of the three ratings.

150 W

Medium capacity — the middle rating, 1.5× the 100 W reference.

200 W

Higher capacity — the largest rating, 2× the 100 W reference.

Easy memory: 100 W → smallest · 150 W → middle · 200 W → largest.

04 · Do Not Assume

Higher Wattage Does Not Mean Higher Light Output

A 200 W transformer does not automatically make the connected light brighter. The transformer rating represents its power-handling capacity — 200 W transformer ≠ 200 W of light output. Actual equipment output depends on the connected load and its operating requirements, not on the transformer rating alone.

07 · W vs VA

Important: W vs VA

For transformers, the rated capacity is commonly specified in VA rather than W — for example 100 VA, 150 VA or 200 VA.

VA ratings should not automatically be treated as exactly equivalent to the same figure in W, because power factor and transformer losses affect the relationship between real power (W) and apparent power (VA). Always follow the nameplate/datasheet rating rather than assuming W and VA are interchangeable.

08 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: A 200 W transformer makes the connected light twice as bright as a 100 W transformer
Fact

The wattage rating describes the transformer's power-handling capacity, not the light output. Brightness depends on the connected fixture and its own rated output, not on the transformer rating alone.

Myth: Choosing the largest available transformer is always the safer choice
Fact

Selection should follow the connected load, required voltage/current/VA and the fixture manufacturer's specified rating — not simply the largest transformer on the shelf.

Myth: A transformer rated in VA can be read directly as the same number in W
Fact

Power factor and transformer losses mean VA and W are not automatically equal. The nameplate/datasheet rating should always be followed.

09 · Engineering Verdict

Which Rating Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

100 W, 150 W and 200 W are different transformer power capacities: 150 W is 1.5 times the 100 W rating, and 200 W is 2 times the 100 W rating. Higher rating means higher load-handling capability, but higher rating does not automatically mean higher light intensity.

For an AGL application, the transformer should be selected according to the specified fixture load, voltage/current requirements and the manufacturer's approved transformer rating — not on a generic comparison alone.

Size From the Actual Fixture Load Plus Margin
Selection

Work from the connected fixture's real VA at its operating setting, then choose the next suitable rating. Picking the largest unit by default wastes cost and changes circuit behaviour.

A Bigger Transformer Is Not Automatically Safer
Loading

A larger rating changes magnetising and no-load behaviour on the series circuit. Include it in the circuit loading calculation rather than treating it as a free upgrade.

Keep Ratings Distinct in Stores and Registers
Spares

Units of different ratings can look alike. Label them clearly and record the fitted rating at each location so the wrong size is not installed on a replacement.

Check Temperature After Any Rating Change
Verification

After changing transformer size or fixture type, compare running temperatures with similar units. A hot transformer is an early sign of a mismatch.

10 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between a 100 W, 150 W and 200 W transformer?

They are different rated power capacities. A 150 W transformer supplies 1.5 times the 100 W rating, and a 200 W transformer supplies twice the 100 W rating, subject to the specified voltage, current and operating conditions.

Does a 200 W transformer make a light fixture brighter than a 100 W transformer?

Not automatically. The transformer rating is its power-handling capacity, not the light output. Actual brightness depends on the connected fixture and its own specified output.

Should I always choose the highest available transformer rating?

No. Selection should follow the connected load's required voltage, current and VA/power, and the fixture manufacturer's specified transformer rating — not simply the largest rating available.

Is a transformer's VA rating the same as its W rating?

Not necessarily. Power factor and transformer losses affect the relationship between real power (W) and apparent power (VA), so the nameplate/datasheet rating should always be followed rather than assuming the two are equal.

Are 150 W and 200 W transformers typically larger and costlier than 100 W units?

Generally, yes — higher-capacity transformers tend to be physically larger and higher in cost, though the exact size and price depend on the specific manufacturer and design.

How should the correct transformer rating be selected for an AGL fixture?

By working from the connected load's required voltage, current and VA/power, and using the fixture manufacturer's specified transformer rating rather than a generic comparison table.
