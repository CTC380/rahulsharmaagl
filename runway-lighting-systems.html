Runway Lighting Systems

The visual guidance system that defines the runway environment. Runway lighting gives pilots the visual references needed for approach, identification, landing, takeoff and movement along the runway — built from fixtures, series circuits, Constant Current Regulators, isolation transformers, cables, connectors, control, monitoring and photometric performance working together as one integrated system.

Overview

What Is a Runway Lighting System?

A runway lighting system is the coordinated arrangement of visual lighting aids installed along and around a runway to provide pilots with the visual information required for safe runway operations.

Depending on the runway configuration and applicable operational requirements, the system may include runway edge lights, centreline lights, threshold lights, end lights, touchdown zone lights, approach lighting, lead-on/lead-off lighting, runway guard lights, stop bars at associated taxiway intersections, illuminated signs and PAPI or other visual approach slope indicators. The exact combination depends on the runway type, operational category, airport configuration and applicable standards.

Not a Collection of Lights

Runway lighting is an integrated system of fixtures, series circuits, CCRs, isolation transformers, cables, connectors, control and monitoring, electrical infrastructure and photometric performance.

The Primary Visual Reference

The runway is the primary visual reference during critical phases of flight — approach, landing, takeoff and ground movement, especially during darkness or reduced visibility.

Serves the Pilot

Every element exists to answer the pilot's questions: where is the runway, where does it begin and end, where is the centreline, the edges and the touchdown area?

Why It Matters

Why Runway Lighting Is Important

The runway is the primary visual reference for aircraft during critical phases of flight. During darkness or reduced visibility, these visual references become especially important for a pilot to answer:

Where is the runway? Where does it begin? Where does it end? What is the centreline? What are the edges? Where is the touchdown area? What is my position relative to the runway?

Quick Answers

Runway centerline color: whiteRunway threshold color: greenRunway end light color: redRunway touchdown zone light color: whiteRunway edge: white (yellow near the end of instrument runways)

What Color Are Runway Edge Lights?

Runway edge lights are white. On an instrument runway they change to yellow over the final portion of the runway, which tells the pilot that the remaining runway is limited.

What Color Are Runway Centerline Lights?

Runway centerline lights are white for most of the runway, then alternate red and white, and finally become red over the last section before the runway end.

What Color Are Runway Threshold Lights?

Runway threshold lights are green when viewed from the approach side. The green threshold lights, wing bars and threshold marking together provide runway threshold identification.

Runway Distance Markers

Runway distance markers are normally signs that show the distance remaining. They complement the lighting colour changes and are separate from the runway lights themselves.

Runway Threshold Color

Runway threshold color is green. Threshold lights, and the wing bars at their ends, show green toward an approaching aircraft, while runway end lights are red. Together they let the pilot tell the start of the usable runway from its end.

Runway Threshold Light Spacing

Runway threshold lights are placed in a line across the runway at the threshold, spaced evenly between the runway edge light rows, with wing bars at the sides. The number of lights and their spacing depend on runway width and approach category, so take the values from Annex 14 or the applicable standard and the approved design.

Weather & Visibility

Runway Lighting Visibility: Fog, Snow, Rain & Wet Weather

Runway lighting visibility depends on more than the lamp. Intensity setting, colour, alignment, cleanliness and weather all decide how well a pilot sees the runway.

Runway Lighting Pilot Visibility

Pilot visibility of the runway lights depends on the intensity step, the beam direction and the condition of the optics. A fixture that is lit but dirty or misaligned gives the pilot less than the design intends.

Runway Lighting Fog

In fog and low visibility the pilot relies on the lights for guidance, so the correct intensity step, consistent output between fixtures and correct alignment matter most.

Runway Lighting Snow

Runway lighting in snow faces obscured fixtures from snow, ice and slush, and elevated fixtures can be damaged by snow-clearing operations. Clearing procedures protect both visibility and equipment.

Runway Lighting Wet Weather

In wet weather, water can enter fixtures, connectors and bases and lower insulation resistance. Good sealing and drainage are what keep runway lights working through heavy rain.

Runway Lighting Rain Testing

Runway lighting rain testing uses water spray or wet-condition checks, followed by insulation measurements, to show that seals and connectors keep water out after installation or repair.

Runway Lighting Visual Guidance

The visual guidance that runway lighting gives comes from colour, pattern, position and brightness working together. Weather reduces how much of that the pilot can see, which is why consistency matters.

Electrical Core

CCR & Isolation Transformers

Constant Current Regulator

The CCR provides controlled current to the AGL series circuit and, depending on system design, may provide multiple intensity levels. A problem anywhere in the chain — CCR current, series-circuit performance, transformer operation or fixture output — can affect the final photometric performance.

Isolation Transformers

Isolation transformers electrically separate individual runway fixtures from the primary series circuit — series circuit → transformer primary → transformer secondary → runway fixture. Transformer selection must be coordinated with the associated fixture and the overall series-circuit design.

The Electrical Interface

Runway Lighting Connectors

AGL connectors provide the electrical interface between the series circuit, transformers and fixtures — typically a primary connector, secondary connector, plug, receptacle and connector kits. Connector integrity matters because poor connections can result in:

Operations

Runway Lighting Intensity Steps, Remote Control, Monitoring & Redundancy

How runway lighting is operated, watched and protected against failure.

Runway Lighting Intensity Steps

The CCR output is set to discrete brightness steps, commonly three or five, chosen to match visibility and ambient light. Each step corresponds to a defined output current.

Runway Lighting Remote Control

Runway lighting remote control lets the tower or a control room select circuits and intensity steps. Runway lighting ALCMS is the control and monitoring system that normally provides this.

Runway Lighting Monitoring

Runway lighting monitoring reports circuit status, current and faults to the operator, so a failing circuit can be found early. See the ALCMS page.

Runway Lighting Redundancy & Reliability

Runway lighting redundancy comes from measures such as interleaved circuits, duplicate equipment and standby arrangements. Runway lighting reliability depends on the design, the maintenance and how quickly faults are found.

Runway Lighting Backup & Emergency

Runway lighting backup and runway lighting emergency supply arrangements, such as a standby generator or secondary source with automatic changeover, keep the lights running when the main supply fails.

Energy Saving, LED Conversion & Solar

Runway lighting energy saving comes from runway lighting LED conversion, lower load and using lower intensity steps when conditions allow. Runway lighting solar is generally used for temporary or remote strips rather than main instrument runways.

Runway Lighting ALCMS

The airfield lighting control and monitoring system (ALCMS) lets the tower or a control room switch runway lighting circuits, select intensity steps and see circuit status and faults. See the ALCMS page for how it works.

Runway Lighting Reliability

Runway lighting reliability depends on circuit redundancy, maintenance quality, available spares and how quickly faults are found through monitoring. It is judged against the aerodrome's serviceability requirements.

Runway Lighting Emergency

Runway lighting emergency arrangements keep the lights available when the main supply fails, usually through a standby generator or secondary source with automatic changeover. The changeover time required depends on the runway type and operation, so confirm it in the applicable standard.

Runway Lighting Energy Saving & Runway Lighting Solar

Runway lighting energy saving comes from LED conversion, lower connected load and using lower intensity steps when conditions allow. Runway lighting solar is generally used for temporary or remote strips rather than main instrument runways.

Installation & Alignment

Runway Light Installation, Aiming & Calibration

Correct installation and aiming decide whether a runway light delivers its design performance.

How Are Runway Lights Installed?

Runway lights are installed on a runway light base set into the pavement or ground, with cables routed to the base and the fixture mounted on it. Elevated lights typically use frangible couplings; inset lights sit flush in the pavement.

Inset Installation & Installation Depth

Runway inset light installation and runway light installation depth follow the manufacturer's drawings. Wrong depth can affect drainage, loading and how far the fixture sits above the surface.

What Is Runway Light Aiming?

Runway light aiming is setting the direction in which a fixture emits its beam, so that the main beam points where the pilot will be looking. Aiming is checked with alignment tools and then confirmed by measurement.

Beam, Vertical & Horizontal Angle

Runway light beam angle is the extent of the useful beam. The runway light vertical angle is the elevation of the beam axis, and the runway light horizontal angle is its aiming relative to the runway axis.

Runway Light Calibration

Runway light calibration covers the calibration of the aiming tools and photometric instruments, and resetting fixtures to the calibrated aim. See AGL Photometry for the measurement side.

Runway Lighting Drawing

A runway lighting drawing shows the layout, circuits, cable routes and base positions. Keeping as-built drawings up to date is essential for maintenance and fault finding.

Runway Light Beam Angle

Runway light beam angle is the angular spread of a fixture's useful beam. It is defined by the fixture's isocandela diagram, and different light types have different beam spreads, so install only the type specified for the position.

Runway Light Vertical Angle & Runway Light Horizontal Angle

The runway light vertical angle is the elevation of the beam axis above horizontal. The runway light horizontal angle is the aiming of the beam relative to the runway axis. Both are set at installation, re-checked after fixture replacement, and confirmed by measurement.

Runway Lighting Acceptance Test

A runway lighting acceptance test is the witnessed, formal check against the specification before the owner accepts the system. It brings together the electrical, photometric and functional results.

Runway Lighting Electrical Test

The runway lighting electrical test confirms the condition of circuits and equipment: insulation, continuity, circuit resistance and current. Run it on isolated, locked-out circuits and compare results with the specification.

Runway Lighting Insulation Test

The runway lighting insulation test checks the insulation of cables and equipment, typically with a megger, with the circuit isolated and the regulator disconnected. Test voltage and minimum values come from the specification.

Runway Lighting Continuity Test

The runway lighting continuity test confirms the series circuit is unbroken and correctly connected, and it is run before the circuit is energised.

Runway Lighting Test Report

A runway lighting test report records every result, the instruments used and their calibration, the date and the pass or fail outcome. Sign it and keep it with the as-built record.

Safety & Failures

Runway Lighting Safety, NOTAM, Outages & Failures

Working on runway lighting and handling failures both need controlled procedures.

Runway Lighting Electrical Safety

Runway lighting electrical safety starts from the fact that series circuits carry constant current at potentially dangerous voltage. Circuits must be isolated, locked out, tagged and earthed per procedure before work, and an energised series circuit must never be opened.

Runway Lighting Work Permit

A runway lighting work permit controls access to the airside, isolation of circuits and coordination with air traffic control before any work begins, and confirms the circuit is safe to work on.

What Is Runway Lighting NOTAM?

A runway lighting NOTAM is a notice to airmen announcing that runway lighting is unserviceable, restricted or being worked on. It is issued through the aerodrome's NOTAM process so pilots know what lighting is available.

What Happens When Runway Lighting Fails?

When runway lighting fails, the effect depends on how much is out. Aerodrome serviceability criteria limit how many lights may be unserviceable. Beyond those limits, operations may be restricted, a NOTAM is issued, and night or low-visibility operations may be suspended until it is restored.

Runway Light Not Working & Runway Lighting Outage

A runway light not working may be a lamp, transformer, connector, cable or CCR problem, and a runway lighting outage affects a whole circuit. Fault finding starts from the circuit status and current. See the troubleshooting engine.

AAI Runway Lighting

The Airports Authority of India (AAI) operates a large share of Indian airports. AAI runway lighting is specified and maintained in line with ICAO Annex 14 as implemented through DGCA regulation and AAI's own technical specifications.

Fundamentals

Runway Lights Meaning, Types, Functions & Working Principle

Runway Lights Meaning

Runway lights meaning comes from colour and pattern: white edge and centreline lights show the usable runway, green marks the threshold, red marks the runway end, and yellow or alternating red-white lights warn how much runway remains.

Runway Lights At Night

Runway lights at night are the pilot's main reference for the runway's edges, centre, start and end. Intensity is set to suit visibility so the lights are clear without dazzling the pilot.

Runway Lighting System Components

Runway lighting system components are the light fixtures, isolation transformers, series cable and connectors, constant current regulators, and the control and monitoring system, supported by power supply and earthing.

Runway Lighting Types

Runway lighting types include edge, centreline, threshold, runway end and touchdown zone lights, with approach lights leading in. Fixtures are elevated (frangible) or inset in the pavement, in white, green, red or yellow by position.

Runway Lighting Functions

Runway lighting functions are to show the runway edges, give alignment and distance guidance along the centreline, mark the threshold and end, and show the touchdown zone, so the pilot can land, roll out and take off safely.

Runway Lighting Working Principle

Runway lighting working principle: a constant current regulator drives one fixed current round a series loop, and each light is fed by an isolation transformer. Changing the regulator's current step changes the brightness of every light on the circuit together.

Operations

Runway Lighting Intensity Levels, Runway Visual Range & Operations

Runway Lighting Intensity Levels

Runway lighting intensity levels are the brightness steps selected on the constant current regulator, commonly three or five. Higher steps suit poor visibility or bright daylight and lower steps suit clear nights; confirm the step table in the applicable standard.

Runway Visual Range Lighting

Runway visual range lighting links the measured RVR, the distance over which a pilot can see runway lights or markings, to the intensity step selected. Lower RVR calls for higher intensity, and RVR limits also decide which lighting systems low-visibility operations need.

Runway Lighting Operations

Runway lighting operations cover switching circuits on and off, selecting intensity, monitoring status and faults, and coordinating outages with air traffic control, normally through the ALCMS at the tower or control room.

Portable Runway Lighting

Portable runway lighting is a temporary, usually battery or solar powered set of lights used at temporary strips, during works or after damage. It supplements rather than replaces permanent AGL, and use needs regulator approval.

Edge Lights

Runway Edge Lights: Working Principle, Types, Design & Standards

Runway Edge Lights Working Principle

Runway edge lights working principle: each fixture is powered through its own isolation transformer on the runway series circuit, so a failed lamp does not break the loop. The CCR sets the current, which sets the brightness.

Runway Edge Lights Types

Runway edge lights types are elevated fixtures on frangible couplings, and inset fixtures where elevated lights are impractical. They are white, with yellow over the last part of an instrument runway, and use halogen or LED sources.

Runway Edge Lights Functions

Runway edge lights functions: define the lateral limits of the usable runway so the pilot can judge alignment and width, and through the yellow zone, the remaining runway length.

Runway Edge Lights Components

Runway edge lights components are the lamp or LED module, optical assembly and lens, fixture body, frangible coupling or inset housing, isolation transformer, connectors and base can.

Runway Edge Lights Specification

Runway edge lights specification covers photometric output, colour, mounting height, frangibility and ingress and impact protection, taken from the applicable standard (for example ICAO Annex 14 or FAA AC 150/5345-46) and the project specification.

Runway Edge Lights Design

Runway edge lights design fixes the spacing, which ICAO limits to 60 m on an instrument runway and 100 m on a non-instrument runway, the lateral position, the yellow zone and the circuit split.

Runway Edge Lights Maintenance

Runway edge lights maintenance includes cleaning lenses, checking aim and level, replacing failed lamps, inspecting frangible couplings and connectors, and recording serviceability.

Runway Edge Lights Testing Procedure

Runway edge lights testing procedure combines insulation resistance and continuity tests, current checks at each brightness step, a visual night check and, where required, photometric measurement.

Runway Edge Lights Selection Guide

Runway edge lights selection guide: choose elevated or inset, halogen or LED, and the colour and beam for the runway category, then match the fixture to the transformer and circuit load.

Runway Edge Lights Technical Guide

Runway edge lights technical guide: read it with the manufacturer datasheet, the aerodrome's standards and the circuit design to verify beam, colour, spacing and electrical data before installation.

Runway Edge Lights Standards

Runway edge lights standards include ICAO Annex 14 Volume I for location and characteristics, FAA AC 150/5345-46 for fixtures, AC 150/5340-30 for installation, and national rules such as DGCA requirements in India.

Runway Edge Lights Commissioning

Runway edge lights commissioning confirms position, aim, colour, circuit current at every step and tower control, with results recorded in the commissioning report.

Centreline Lights

Runway Centreline Lights: Working Principle, Types, Installation & Standards

Runway Centreline Lights Working Principle

Runway centreline lights working principle is that of the runway series circuit: inset fixtures each fed by an isolation transformer from the CCR, often split across alternate circuits so one circuit failure leaves every other light on.

Runway Centreline Lights Types

Runway centreline lights types are inset, in-pavement fixtures, unidirectional or bidirectional, white and red where the colour code calls for it, built to take aircraft wheel loads.

Runway Centreline Lights Functions

Runway centreline lights functions: give directional guidance during landing roll and take-off and, through the white, alternating red-white and red zones, show distance remaining.

Runway Centreline Lights Components

Runway centreline lights components are the inset fixture with lens and colour filter, light source, base can or shallow base, isolation transformer, cable and connectors.

Runway Centreline Lights Specification

Runway centreline lights specification covers photometry, colour, load rating, surface temperature limits and sealing against water, with values taken from the standard and the manufacturer's certification.

Runway Centreline Lights Design

Runway centreline lights design sets the spacing, colour zones along the runway, circuit arrangement and alignment with the runway axis, and depends on runway category and take-off visibility limits.

Runway Centreline Lights Installation

Runway centreline lights installation needs an accurate survey of the axis, correct base depth, flush fitting, sealed cable entries and correct fixing torque. Check aim after fitting, because inset lights are hard to adjust once the pavement is surfaced.

Runway Centreline Lights Maintenance

Runway centreline lights maintenance focuses on lens cleaning, removing rubber and tyre deposits, checking seals and bases, and replacing damaged fixtures, since landing traffic wears these lights fastest.

Runway Centreline Lights Testing Procedure

Runway centreline lights testing procedure includes circuit insulation and continuity tests, current at each step, light output and aim checks, and a night inspection for dark or misaligned lights.

Runway Centreline Lights Selection Guide

Runway centreline lights selection guide: choose by runway category, unidirectional or bidirectional pattern, colour filters, source (LED or halogen) and load rating for the aircraft using the runway.

Runway Centreline Lights Technical Guide

Runway centreline lights technical guide: check spacing, colours and photometric data against the design and standard, then confirm installation and wiring details on the manufacturer drawing.

Runway Centreline Lights Standards

Runway centreline lights standards sit in ICAO Annex 14 Volume I, which requires them on CAT II and III precision runways, and in FAA AC 150/5340-30 and AC 150/5345-46 for design and fixtures.

Runway Centreline Lights Commissioning

Runway centreline lights commissioning proves position along the axis, colour sequence, aim, circuit current at all steps and tower control before the runway is released for use.

Threshold, End & Touchdown

Threshold Lights, Runway End & Touchdown Zone Lighting Design

Threshold Lights Working Principle

Threshold lights working principle: green fixtures, on their own series circuit or shared with other runway lights, are driven by the CCR through isolation transformers and show arriving aircraft the start of the usable runway.

Threshold Lights Types

Threshold lights types are elevated or inset, green toward the approach, including combined threshold and end fixtures that show green to arriving aircraft and red to aircraft on the runway.

Threshold Lights Functions

Threshold lights functions: identify the beginning of the runway for landing, outline the runway width with wing bars where provided, and give a clear reference for where the runway can be used.

Threshold Lights Components

Threshold lights components include the green lens or filter, light source, fixture body or inset housing, isolation transformer, base and connectors, with wing bar lights added where required.

Threshold Lights Specification

Threshold lights specification covers green chromaticity, intensity toward the approach, mounting and frangibility. Use the applicable standard and fixture datasheet to confirm each value.

Threshold Lights Requirements

Threshold lights requirements depend on runway type and category. ICAO Annex 14 sets where they are needed, their colour, and the number and arrangement of lights across the threshold; check the current edition and national rules.

Threshold Lights Design

Threshold lights design sets the line of fixtures across the threshold, symmetry about the centreline, any wing bars, and how the circuits are arranged and shared.

Threshold Lights Installation

Threshold lights installation needs the line set square to the runway axis at the correct threshold position, with green fixtures aimed toward the approach and matching bases and cables.

Threshold Lights Maintenance

Threshold lights maintenance covers lens cleaning, aim and colour checks, lamp or LED replacement and connector inspection, with fast repair because a dark threshold weakens the pilot's reference.

Threshold Lights Troubleshooting

Threshold lights troubleshooting starts with the symptom: one light out points to the lamp, transformer or connector, while a group out suggests a cable fault, the circuit or the CCR. Measure current and insulation resistance to narrow it down.

Threshold Lights Testing Procedure

Threshold lights testing procedure checks insulation resistance, continuity, current at each intensity step, colour and aim, then confirms from the tower that the threshold circuit switches correctly.

Threshold Lights Selection Guide

Threshold lights selection guide: pick elevated or inset fixtures, plain or combined threshold/end units, and LED or halogen sources, matching colour, intensity and load to the circuit.

Threshold Lights Technical Guide

Threshold lights technical guide: use it with manufacturer drawings and the standard to verify position, count, colour and wiring of the threshold line before installation and testing.

Threshold Lights Standards

Threshold lights standards include ICAO Annex 14 Volume I for position and colour, FAA AC 150/5340-30 and AC 150/5345-46 for design and fixtures, and national requirements such as DGCA rules.

Threshold Lights Commissioning

Threshold lights commissioning confirms position, green colour, aim, circuit current and tower control, and records the results in the commissioning report.

Runway Threshold Lighting Design

Runway threshold lighting design sets the threshold line, wing bars and their relation to approach lights and markings. For a displaced threshold the lights must be adapted to match, so follow the standard for displaced thresholds.

Runway End Lighting Design

Runway end lighting design places red fixtures across the runway end, facing the runway, so they show red to aircraft rolling out and, with the threshold lights, mark both ends of the runway.

Runway Touchdown Zone Lighting Design

Runway touchdown zone lighting design uses rows of white barrettes either side of the centreline in the first part of the runway. ICAO requires them on CAT II and III precision runways; spacing and length follow the standard.

Runway Distance Remaining Signs

Runway distance remaining signs show the distance left to the runway end, in thousands of feet in FAA practice. They are lit signs beside the runway, and whether they are provided depends on the aerodrome's rules.

Runway Designation Markings And Lights

Runway designation markings are painted numbers at the threshold, not lights. At night they are seen in aircraft landing lights, so AGL design should coordinate threshold lights, markings and any lit signs.

AGL design for a new runway starts with the runway category, then selects light types and layout from the standard, designs series circuits, sizes CCRs and cables, plans control and standby power, and ends with photometric checks and drawings.

Runway lighting ICAO requirements are in Annex 14 Volume I, Chapter 5, which sets which lights each runway type needs with their colour, intensity and spacing, plus electrical (Chapter 8) and maintenance (Chapter 10) provisions. Check the current edition.

Runway lighting selection guide: match fixture type, light source, colour and intensity to the runway category, pavement, climate and maintenance capability, then check compatibility with transformers and regulators.

Runway lighting technical guide: this page links design, circuits, photometry, testing and maintenance, and should be read with the aerodrome's standards, manufacturer data and the project specification.

Runway lighting testing procedure covers insulation resistance, continuity, current at every step, earthing, aim and photometric checks, and control and monitoring tests, as set out in the Runway Lighting Tests section.

Runway Lighting Preventive Maintenance

Runway lighting preventive maintenance is the scheduled programme of inspection, cleaning, electrical tests, aim checks and replacement of worn parts, which keeps lights within serviceability limits and cuts unplanned outages.

Keyword Reference

Runway Lighting Installation, Approach Lights, LED Runway Lights & Threshold Lights Spacing

Runway lighting installation

Runway lighting installation is the physical work of placing the fixtures, transformers, cables and connectors for a runway and connecting them to the CCRs. It follows the approved drawings and the manufacturer's instructions, with checks at each stage before the circuit is energised. See Runway Light Installation, Aiming & Calibration above.

Approach lights runway

Approach lights runway is the common search phrase for the lighting that leads the pilot to the runway threshold. The approach lighting system sits on the extended centreline before the runway and works together with the threshold and runway lights. See Runway Approach Lighting above, and the Approach Lighting Systems page for the full detail.

LED runway lights

LED runway lights use LED light sources in edge, centreline, threshold and other runway fixtures. They offer lower power use and long life, but they must still meet the required photometric performance and work correctly with the series circuit and CCR. Compatibility and approval should be confirmed before replacing existing fixtures. See LED Runway Lighting above.

Threshold lights spacing

Threshold lights spacing is the distance between lights across the runway threshold. It is set by the standard the aerodrome follows, so no figures are given here. Correct spacing gives a clear green line that marks the start of the runway, and changes should be checked against the design drawings and the standard.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is a runway lighting system?

It is the coordinated arrangement of visual lighting aids installed along and around a runway to give pilots the visual information needed for safe runway operations. Depending on runway type, operational category and standards, it may include edge, centreline, threshold, end and touchdown zone lights, approach lighting, lead-on and lead-off lighting, runway guard lights, stop bars, illuminated signs and PAPI.

Q2Why is runway lighting important?

The runway is the primary visual reference during approach, landing, takeoff and ground movement, especially in darkness or reduced visibility. Runway lighting lets pilots see where the runway is, where it begins and ends, where the centreline and edges are, where the touchdown area is and their position relative to the runway.

Q3What are the main runway lighting elements?

Edge lights define the lateral limits, centreline lights give longitudinal guidance, threshold lights identify the beginning of the usable runway, end lights identify the end, and touchdown zone lights add guidance in the touchdown area. Stop bars and illuminated signs support runway-protection and guidance around the runway.

Q4How does approach lighting relate to runway lighting?

Approach lighting provides visual guidance before the aircraft reaches the runway, creating a transition from the approach environment toward the threshold, and should be considered part of the overall runway visual guidance system. Elements include light bars, centreline and side elements, sequenced lighting and flashing elements.

Q5What do runway lighting colours mean?

Colour communicates function and location, for example at the edge, threshold, runway end, centreline, touchdown zone and approach systems. Exact colours, transition points and applicability depend on runway category and the applicable ICAO, FAA or project requirements.

Q6Does correct power guarantee correct runway guidance?

No. A fixture that is correctly powered but incorrectly positioned can still compromise the intended visual guidance, since position is a spatial system covering longitudinal spacing, transverse position, centreline location, threshold and runway end, touchdown zone and taxiway intersections.

Q7What electrical architecture do runway lights use?

Many runway lighting systems use series circuits, so multiple lights operate as part of a controlled constant-current circuit: CCR, series cable, isolation transformers and runway lights, with the return path to the CCR. The isolation transformers electrically separate each fixture from the primary series circuit.

Q8What does the CCR do in a runway lighting system?

It provides controlled current to the AGL series circuit and, depending on the system design, may provide multiple intensity levels. A problem anywhere in the chain, including CCR current, series-circuit performance, transformer operation or fixture output, can affect the final photometric performance.

Q9What determines which runway lights are installed?

The exact combination depends on the runway type, operational category, airport configuration and applicable standards.

Q10Is runway lighting just a collection of lights?

No. It is an integrated system of fixtures, series circuits, CCRs, isolation transformers, cables, connectors, control and monitoring, electrical infrastructure and photometric performance. The runway light is only the final visible element of a much larger electrical and control system.

Q11How does runway lighting work?

Runway lighting works as a series circuit: a constant current regulator (CCR) drives one current through a loop of cable, and each light is fed through an isolation transformer. The CCR sets the brightness step, the control system switches circuits and steps, and the colour and position of the lights give the pilot information about the runway.

Q12What color are runway edge lights?

Runway edge lights are white. On an instrument runway they turn yellow over the final portion of the runway to warn the pilot that the remaining runway is limited.

Q13What color are runway centerline lights?

Runway centerline lights are white for most of the runway, then alternate red and white, and finally become red over the last section before the runway end.

Q14What color are runway threshold lights?

Runway threshold lights are green when viewed from the approach direction. They mark the start of the usable landing runway, and runway end lights are red.

Q15What is runway light aiming?

Runway light aiming is setting the direction in which a runway light emits its beam, in both the horizontal and vertical planes, so that the main beam points where the pilot will see it. Aiming is checked with alignment tools and confirmed by measurement.

Q16How are runway lights installed?

Runway lights are installed on a light base set into the pavement or ground, with cables routed to the base and the fixture mounted on it. Inset lights sit flush in the pavement, while elevated lights use frangible couplings. Installation depth and orientation follow the manufacturer's drawings and the project specification.

Q17What is runway lighting NOTAM?

A runway lighting NOTAM is a notice to airmen announcing that part or all of the runway lighting is unserviceable, restricted or being worked on, so pilots know what lighting is available.

Q18What happens when runway lighting fails?

The effect depends on how much of the lighting is out. If the unserviceable lights exceed the aerodrome's serviceability limits, operations may be restricted, a NOTAM is issued, and night or low-visibility operations may be suspended until the lighting is restored. A standby supply and interleaved circuits reduce the chance of a total loss.
