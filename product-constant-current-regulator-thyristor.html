Product Overview

Constant Current Regulator

A CCR maintains the required circuit current despite changes in the connected series-circuit load within its specified operating range. In an AGL series circuit, fixtures are connected through isolating transformers and associated primary circuit components; the CCR is the controlled source for that circuit and plays an important role in consistent lighting performance.

A thyristor-based regulator uses controlled semiconductor switching to regulate output power, supporting controlled current levels and, depending on configuration, multiple brilliance settings. Typical CCR systems may include:

  • Thyristor power-control assembly
  • Input power section
  • Output series-circuit section
  • Control electronics
  • Current regulation and sensing
  • Protection functions
  • Alarm and monitoring interfaces
  • Local controls and indications
  • Remote control interface where specified
  • Enclosure and internal switching/protection equipment

The exact configuration depends on the selected CCR rating and project requirements.

Thyristor Control and Current Regulation

Thyristor-Controlled Operation

  • Multiple selectable brilliance steps
  • Local and remote control
  • Current monitoring
  • Fault, alarm and status indication

Constant Current Regulation

  • Regulates to the specified current rather than a fixed voltage
  • Accommodates changes in series-circuit characteristics within its designed range
  • Sizing considers total load, fixture count and type, isolating transformer ratings
  • Also considers circuit resistance and impedance, cable characteristics, maximum circuit voltage
  • Starting and operating conditions must be included

CCR sizing should be completed as part of the overall series-circuit design, not as an isolated equipment selection. The control arrangement should follow the airport's AGL control philosophy and existing infrastructure.

Brilliance Control

Multi-Step Brilliance Control

Airfield lighting commonly requires different intensities depending on operational conditions, visibility and circuit type. Where applicable, the CCR provides multiple predefined current settings so the connected circuit can operate at different brilliance levels. The number and value of the steps should be established from the project specification.

  • Number of required intensity steps
  • Rated series-circuit current
  • Minimum and maximum operating current
  • Lighting fixture characteristics
  • Circuit load
  • Control system interface
  • Local/remote operation requirements
  • Airport operational procedures

The CCR should be coordinated with the connected lighting equipment so that each selected current level produces the intended system performance.

Sizing & Selection

CCR Sizing and Selection

Correct sizing is one of the most important steps in designing an AGL series circuit. Precision AGL Solutions supports selection from the available project information:

Series-circuit typeRunway, taxiway, approach or another lighting application.
Connected loadNumber and type of fixtures and associated isolating transformers.
Required circuit currentNominal operating current and required brilliance settings.
Circuit voltageCalculate or confirm the expected voltage at the required current.
Control requirementsLocal, remote or integrated control.
Monitoring requirementsRequired alarms, status indications and interfaces.
Environmental conditionsInstallation environment and enclosure requirements.
Existing infrastructureFor replacements: existing CCR rating, circuit configuration and control system.

This avoids selecting a regulator on nominal current alone while overlooking the complete circuit load and operating conditions. Allow for isolating-transformer and cable losses and for planned future expansion. Do not oversize either: a regulator that is lightly loaded gives a poorer power factor and waveform, and this matters after halogen-to-LED conversion, where the connected load drops sharply. Re-check the regulator against the new load.

System Integration

Integration with AGL Series Circuits

CCR → Primary Cable → Primary Connectors → Isolating Transformers → Airfield Light Fixtures

Each component must be electrically compatible with the selected regulator. The design should consider CCR output current, cable length and impedance, transformer ratings, fixture load, circuit insulation and continuity, expected operating voltage and required brilliance settings.

Incorrect matching between the CCR and series circuit can result in unsuitable current regulation, excessive circuit voltage, nuisance alarms or reduced system performance.

Activities may include visual inspection, cleaning of equipment and ventilation areas, inspection of connections, cooling and protection systems, verification of indications, alarms, output current and brilliance-step operation, functional testing of control interfaces, checking for overheating or abnormal noise, and review of recorded faults. Intervals should follow the manufacturer's recommendations and the airport's maintenance program.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Open-circuit protection in the regulator is a protective function, not a method of isolation — always isolate and lock out first
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures
Standards

CCR Specification and Ratings

FAA Specification

Constant current regulators and regulator monitors for airport lighting series circuits are covered by FAA AC 150/5345-10H — Specification for Constant Current Regulators and Regulator Monitors. The specification defines the L-827 regulator monitor, the L-828 regulator (without monitoring) and the L-829 regulator (with monitoring). Class 1 is a 6.6 A output and Class 2 a 20 A output. Style 1 has three brightness steps and Style 2 has five. The AC covers other regulator designs as well as thyristor types. Confirm the current edition and the applicable class/style designation with the manufacturer datasheet.

Also review IEC 61822 (constant current regulators for aerodrome lighting), ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I Chapter 8, the ICAO Aerodrome Design Manual (Doc 9157) Part 5 — Electrical Systems, and applicable national electrical requirements.

Ratings to Specify

  • Regulator rating (kVA) matched to the connected circuit load
  • Rated output current and available current steps (for example 3-step or 5-step brightness control; on a 6.6 A circuit the FAA steps are typically 4.8 / 5.5 / 6.6 A for 3-step and 2.8 / 3.4 / 4.1 / 5.2 / 6.6 A for 5-step; on a 20 A circuit the 5-step values are typically 8.5 / 10.3 / 12.4 / 15.8 / 20 A — confirm against the project specification)
  • Input supply voltage and frequency
  • Regulator class/style per the applicable specification
  • Monitoring and remote-control interface

Exact kVA and current ranges offered are supplied on the manufacturer datasheet for the project.

Power Quality

Harmonics, Power Factor and EMI

Thyristor (phase-controlled) regulators draw non-sinusoidal current and can introduce harmonic distortion, a reduced input power factor at low brightness steps, and electromagnetic interference. These matter when sizing standby generators and UPS supplies and when routing sensitive control or communication cables.

  • Request total harmonic distortion (THD) and power-factor figures at each brightness step from the datasheet
  • Size generators and UPS with harmonic loading and power factor in mind
  • Check EMI/EMC compliance and filtering requirements with the manufacturer
  • Separate regulator output cabling from low-level signal and communication cables
  • ICAO Annex 14 Chapter 8 sets maximum switch-over times to the secondary power supply by runway type, so check how quickly the regulator restarts and restores output after a supply change

FAQ

Thyristor Constant Current Regulator — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a thyristor constant current regulator?

A thyristor CCR uses controlled semiconductor switching to regulate output power, giving a controlled constant-current supply to runway, taxiway, approach and other series-connected lighting circuits, with multiple brilliance settings depending on configuration.

What does a CCR do in an AGL series circuit?

It maintains the required circuit current despite changes in the connected series-circuit load within its specified operating range. Fixtures connect through isolating transformers and primary circuit components, and the CCR is the controlled source for the circuit.

Which designation applies for 50 Hz systems such as India?

For India (50 Hz), specify the 50 Hz regulator, designated L-829, and IEC 61822 where the project follows IEC. The 60 Hz designation is L-828 under FAA AC 150/5345-10H. Confirm the input frequency and series-circuit current on the datasheet. See also the ferroresonant CCR page.

How is a thyristor CCR sized?

Sizing considers the series-circuit type, the number and type of fixtures and isolating transformers, the required circuit current and brilliance settings, circuit resistance and impedance, cable characteristics, maximum circuit voltage, control and monitoring requirements and environmental conditions. Allow for transformer and cable losses and planned future expansion, but do not oversize, because a lightly loaded regulator gives a poorer power factor and waveform.

Does LED conversion affect the regulator?

Yes. After halogen-to-LED conversion the connected load drops sharply, and a lightly loaded regulator gives a poorer power factor and waveform, so re-check the regulator against the new load.

What brilliance steps are provided?

Where applicable the CCR provides multiple predefined current settings so the circuit can operate at different brilliance levels. The number and values of steps should be established from the project specification, the rated series-circuit current, the fixture characteristics and the control system interface.

What protection and monitoring functions does it include?

Depending on configuration: input protection, output overcurrent protection, open-circuit protection (output de-energised if the series circuit opens), overvoltage and surge protection, thermal and semiconductor protection, internal fault and control circuit protection, earth or insulation fault monitoring where specified, and alarm and status indication, with local and remote control interfaces.

How is the output current verified at commissioning?

Use a true-RMS instrument, because phase-controlled output is not a pure sine wave. Commissioning also covers input supply and connections, earthing, series-circuit continuity and insulation resistance, brilliance steps, control, alarm and remote-control testing, circuit operation at the required intensity levels and documentation of the measurements.

What should I check when the current is unstable or a step does not work?

For incorrect or unstable current, check the configuration, whether the circuit load is outside range, series-circuit faults, control-system issues or an internal regulator fault. For a brilliance step not operating, check the control input, step-selection configuration, CCR settings, control-system command and regulator fault status. Do not simply reset repeated alarms; assess the regulator, series circuit, transformers and control system together.

What should be reviewed before replacing an ageing regulator?

Review the existing CCR rating, series-circuit current, circuit load, input supply, control interface, remote-control arrangement, alarm requirements, cabinet dimensions, cable termination arrangement and available spare capacity. A replacement should not be selected solely from the rating of the existing unit.

Is it safe to open a series circuit while the regulator is running?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. De-energise the circuit at the regulator, apply lock-out / tag-out, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device and work under the permit-to-work system. See the electrical safety warning on this page.

What information should I provide for a CCR quotation?

Provide the AGL application and circuit, required rated current, input supply voltage and frequency, existing CCR rating for replacements, number of brilliance steps, series-circuit load, existing control system, remote interface and alarm requirements, installation environment, existing cabinet dimensions, project standards, quantity and existing equipment make and model.
