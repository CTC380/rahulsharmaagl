PAPI Check Stick

What It Is

The PAPI Check Stick is a field inspection and verification accessory used during PAPI alignment, maintenance and performance checks. It is a purpose-designed mechanical reference that helps check the angular or alignment condition of an installation.

It provides a repeatable physical reference between the PAPI light unit and the established aiming reference, helping maintenance personnel identify units that have moved, been disturbed or need re-alignment.

Used Together With

  • A PAPI light unit
  • A defined runway or survey reference
  • An aiming/alignment device
  • Clinometer or digital inclinometer where applicable
  • Installation reference points
  • The manufacturer's approved adjustment procedure

It is an inspection aid, not a substitute for the complete commissioning process. Match geometry and procedure to the PAPI type, aiming device and approved airport maintenance procedure.

Why Alignment Verification Matters

Small Movement, Meaningful Change in Indication

Angular Relationships

A PAPI projects sharply defined optical sectors; the apparent indication depends on the angular relationship between the light units, runway reference and aircraft. FAA PAPI installation criteria include a dedicated aiming section with angles relative to a preselected glide path, and FAA Order JO 6850.2C sets siting dimensions, alignment considerations and assembly requirements.

A small mechanical movement can therefore change the system indication.

Sources of Alignment Change

  • Mounting hardware movement
  • Vehicle or maintenance-equipment impact
  • Foundation settlement
  • Pavement rehabilitation
  • Accidental disturbance during grass cutting
  • Replacement or adjustment of optical assemblies
  • Incorrect previous maintenance adjustment
  • Longitudinal or transverse reference changes

A check stick is a practical means of identifying a unit that no longer corresponds to the established field reference.

Inspection Concept

Field Verification Against a Known Reference

Depending on the check stick and aiming system design, the process may involve:

  1. Confirm the PAPI is in the correct maintenance condition
  2. Establish the approved runway or survey reference
  3. Position the checking accessory per the applicable procedure
  4. Observe the relationship between reference and optical unit
  5. Check for angular or physical displacement
  6. Compare against the specified acceptance criteria
  7. Adjust the PAPI unit when necessary
  8. Repeat verification after adjustment
  9. Record final condition and settings

The exact sequence must follow the PAPI manufacturer's maintenance instructions and the airport's approved inspection procedure.

Operational note: a unit found out of alignment gives pilots a wrong slope indication. Treat the PAPI as unserviceable and report it under the airport procedure until it is corrected and re-verified. After realignment or unit replacement, the authority may require a flight inspection or a notified unserviceability before the PAPI returns to service. Follow the airport procedure. The excessive-tilt cut-out in FAA-qualified units operates only beyond a fraction of a degree, so a PAPI that is still lit is not proof that it is aligned.

Commissioning, Inspection & Maintenance Use

Three Moments in the Life of a PAPI

Commissioning

  • Correct PAPI location and unit sequence
  • Unit spacing and common reference elevation
  • Vertical aiming and horizontal alignment
  • Optical transition performance
  • Relationship to approved approach path
  • Mechanical stability

Periodic Inspection

  • Change in alignment reference
  • Incorrect unit elevation or tilt
  • Damaged mounting components
  • Optical contamination, uneven output
  • Changed red/white transition
  • Evidence of impact, corrosion or deterioration

Post-Maintenance

  • Light-unit replacement
  • Mounting adjustment
  • Impact damage from mower or vehicle
  • Pavement or runway work near the threshold
  • Electrical maintenance that required moving the units

Routine checks need not re-survey the PAPI each time; the stick helps identify a unit that has moved and needs detailed re-alignment. Manufacturer documentation can prescribe a check stick for elevation/alignment inspection and recommends it where a unit is suspected of being misaligned. FAA criteria for siting, aiming and spacing show verification is a defined part of PAPI engineering.

  • Damaged threads or fittings
  • Marking deterioration

During Use

  • Do not use it as a lever
  • Avoid excessive force
  • Avoid dragging reference surfaces over abrasive contamination
  • Do not use visibly damaged equipment
  • Do not modify reference dimensions in the field

After Use

  • Clean as specified for the material
  • Store protected, where geometry cannot distort

Troubleshooting

Symptoms, Causes and Checks

Non-repeatable check-stick result

Incorrect positioning, damaged reference surfaces, loose hardware, wrong PAPI reference, contamination between surfaces, or a bent accessory.

Check

  • Stick condition
  • Reference positioning
  • Contact points
  • PAPI mounting condition
  • Associated aiming equipment

Aligned, but indications inconsistent

The issue may not be mechanical alignment alone. A check stick finds physical misalignment; it cannot verify every photometric or optical parameter.

Check

  • Optical contamination
  • Damaged lens or filter
  • Light-source condition
  • Unit aiming and elevation
  • Electrical output
  • Installation reference and external obstructions

Check stick indicates a discrepancy

Do not force the unit into position. First confirm:

Check

  • Check-stick reference is correct
  • PAPI installation reference unchanged
  • Inspection equipment undamaged
  • Approved aiming angle is correct
  • Mounting hardware secure
  • Survey reference still valid
  • Then perform controlled re-alignment

PAPI disturbed by maintenance equipment

Treat as a potential alignment change even with little visible damage. Return the unit to its approved condition before normal service.

Check

  • Housing position
  • Mounting legs and foundation
  • Tilt condition
  • Vertical aiming and horizontal alignment
  • Optical presentation

ICAO

The project specification, aerodrome authority requirements and approved PAPI maintenance procedure take precedence over a generic accessory description.

Important Standards Note

There is a distinction between the PAPI system standard and the inspection accessory used to maintain it. FAA's PAPI equipment specification addresses the system; FAA installation guidance addresses siting and aiming. A check stick is best specified as compatible inspection equipment for the selected PAPI and aiming method, not as a universal FAA-certified measuring instrument.

Some manufacturers' L-880/L-881 maintenance manuals list a check stick as part of the bubble-level aiming kit and recommend it for routine alignment checks. Follow the manual for the installed model.

FAQ

PAPI Check Stick — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a PAPI check stick?

A PAPI check stick is a field inspection and verification accessory, a purpose-designed mechanical reference that gives a repeatable physical reference between the PAPI light unit and the established aiming reference. It helps maintenance personnel identify units that have moved, been disturbed or need re-alignment. It is an inspection aid, not a substitute for the complete commissioning process.

When is a PAPI check stick used?

It is used for new PAPI commissioning, periodic inspection, post-maintenance checks after light-unit replacement or adjustment, fault investigation when red/white indications are inconsistent, checks after accidental disturbance or impact, verification after foundation or mounting work, and after severe weather. It is particularly useful where repeatable field checks are needed without relying solely on pilot observation.

Why does PAPI alignment verification matter?

A PAPI projects sharply defined optical sectors, so a small mechanical movement can change the indication. Sources of alignment change include mounting hardware movement, vehicle or maintenance-equipment impact, foundation settlement, pavement rehabilitation, accidental disturbance during grass cutting, replacement of optical assemblies and incorrect previous maintenance adjustment.

Does a check stick replace a clinometer, aiming device or alignment telescope?

No, they are complementary, not interchangeable. Use the check stick for a rapid alignment check, a digital inclinometer for numerical angular measurement, a clinometer for manual angle verification, an alignment telescope for optical line-of-sight alignment, and a manufacturer-approved aiming device for the full PAPI aiming procedure. For higher-accuracy commissioning, combine them: survey reference, PAPI aiming device, alignment telescope, check stick, then final verification.

Is a PAPI check stick an FAA-certified instrument?

A check stick is not an independently standardised airport lighting device. It is best specified as compatible inspection equipment for the selected PAPI and aiming method, not as a universal FAA-certified measuring instrument. Some manufacturers' L-880/L-881 maintenance manuals list a check stick as part of the bubble-level aiming kit and recommend it for routine alignment checks, so follow the manual for the installed model.

What is the field verification workflow with a check stick?

The workflow is reference, check, measure, adjust, re-check and record: use the approved reference point, runway datum and PAPI settings, check whether the unit is still in its established alignment, quantify any discrepancy with a clinometer, inclinometer, aiming device, telescope or survey, make a controlled mechanical adjustment per the manufacturer's procedure, repeat the verification and record the final settings, date, equipment ID and corrective work.

What should be done if a PAPI unit is found out of alignment?

A unit out of alignment gives pilots a wrong slope indication, so treat the PAPI as unserviceable and report it under the airport procedure until it is corrected and re-verified. After realignment or unit replacement, the authority may require a flight inspection or a notified unserviceability before the PAPI returns to service. A PAPI that is still lit is not proof that it is aligned, because the excessive-tilt cut-out in FAA-qualified units operates only beyond a fraction of a degree.

What if the check stick indicates a discrepancy?

Do not force the unit into position. First confirm that the check-stick reference is correct, the PAPI installation reference is unchanged, the inspection equipment is undamaged, the approved aiming angle is correct, the mounting hardware is secure and the survey reference is still valid, then perform a controlled re-alignment.

How should a PAPI check stick be cared for?

Inspect it before use for bending, cracks, loose components, worn reference surfaces, corrosion and damaged fittings. Do not use it as a lever, avoid excessive force, do not use visibly damaged equipment and do not modify reference dimensions in the field. Clean it as specified, store it where its geometry cannot distort, and keep equipment identification and dimensional verification records, with the verification interval set by the airport's maintenance system or the manufacturer.

Why might a check-stick result not repeat, or the indications still be inconsistent?

A non-repeatable result can come from incorrect positioning, damaged reference surfaces, loose hardware, the wrong PAPI reference, contamination between surfaces or a bent accessory. If the unit is aligned but the indications are inconsistent, the issue may not be mechanical alone: a check stick finds physical misalignment but cannot verify every photometric or optical parameter, so also check optical contamination, lens or filter damage, light-source condition, unit aiming and elevation, and electrical output.

What information is needed to select a PAPI check stick?

Provide the PAPI manufacturer and model, installed equipment configuration, existing aiming-device type, existing check-stick type if available, the PAPI installation drawing, alignment procedure, latest maintenance manual, runway reference information, the required inspection procedure and existing survey or reference-point details.

Engineering Note

A PAPI check stick is a reference and inspection tool, not a replacement for the PAPI system's complete alignment, photometric and operational verification process.

For reliable field use, treat it as part of a controlled inspection system: an established reference, compatible PAPI aiming equipment, appropriate measurement instruments and documented acceptance criteria. Select it for compatibility and repeatability, and verify its dimensions and operating method against the applicable PAPI equipment documentation and airport maintenance procedure.

FAA installation criteria place specific emphasis on PAPI aiming, siting tolerances and assembly condition, while manufacturer maintenance instructions can prescribe a check stick for routine alignment verification.
