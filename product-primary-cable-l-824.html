Product Overview

AGL Primary Cable (L-824)

What It Is

AGL Primary Cable (L-824) carries high-voltage series-circuit power between the Constant Current Regulator (CCR), field isolating transformers and associated airfield ground lighting components.

It forms the electrical backbone of an airport series-circuit lighting system, providing suitable insulation, mechanical protection and environmental performance while remaining compatible with the selected primary connectors, transformers and jointing systems.

What We Supply

Precision AGL Solutions supplies primary cable configurations selected according to system voltage, conductor size, insulation construction, installation method and project requirements.

A continuous primary circuit supplies multiple fixtures. The cable may also be routed between field connection points and through approved cable joints or splices, so selection should be performed as part of the complete AGL circuit design.

The primary cable forms a closed loop from the CCR out to the field transformers and back to the CCR. A single open-circuit fault (cable break, failed connector or joint) stops current in the whole loop, which is why cable, joint and connector quality govern circuit reliability.

Why It Matters

The primary cable carries the controlled current supplied by the AGL CCR through the series circuit.

A cable selection that does not match the complete circuit design can affect system reliability, insulation performance and maintenance requirements. Series circuits are normally regulated at up to 6.6 A (FAA 3-step regulators: 4.8, 5.5 and 6.6 A; 5-step: 2.8, 3.4, 4.1, 5.2 and 6.6 A), although some legacy installations use 20 A, so conductor size and insulation are chosen with the circuit length, regulator output voltage and transformer load, not the current alone.

Lightning Protection

Counterpoise and Earthing

In FAA practice a bare counterpoise conductor is laid in the cable trench above the primary cable and bonded as designed, for lightning protection (AC 150/5340-30J and AC 150/5370-10, Item L-108). Indian and other projects follow the airport design and national earthing standards. Confirm the counterpoise size, bonding points and test requirements with the approved design before the cable is installed.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • After any insulation-resistance or high-voltage test, discharge the cable and apply temporary earths before handling conductors, as cable capacitance can hold a dangerous charge
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures
Specification

Voltage Classes and Cable Specification

L-824 underground electrical cable for airport lighting series circuits is specified in the FAA AC 150/5345-7F series (Specification for L-824 Underground Electrical Cable for Airport Lighting Circuits). The standard defines 5,000 V and 600 V insulation classes. The 5,000 V class is the normal choice for primary series circuits; the 600 V class is typically used for secondary and other low-voltage runs. Select the class to suit the circuit design, and confirm the current AC edition and conductor/insulation construction with the manufacturer datasheet.

FAQ

AGL Primary Cable (L-824) — Frequently Asked Questions

What is L-824 primary cable used for?

L-824 primary cable carries high-voltage series-circuit power between the constant current regulator (CCR), the field isolating transformers and associated airfield ground lighting components. It is the electrical backbone of the series circuit and is used on runway, taxiway, approach, apron and other underground airfield lighting circuits.

Why can one cable fault stop a whole lighting circuit?

The primary cable forms a closed loop from the CCR out to the field transformers and back to the CCR. A single open-circuit fault, such as a cable break, failed connector or failed joint, stops current in the whole loop. This is why cable, joint and connector quality govern circuit reliability.

What does the FAA specify for L-824 cable?

L-824 underground electrical cable for airport lighting circuits is specified in the FAA AC 150/5345-7F series. It defines 5,000 V and 600 V insulation classes. The 5,000 V class is the normal choice for primary series circuits, while the 600 V class is typically used for secondary and other low-voltage runs. Confirm the current edition and the conductor and insulation construction on the manufacturer datasheet.

What series-circuit current does the cable carry, and how does it affect selection?

Series circuits are normally regulated at up to 6.6 A. FAA 3-step regulators use 4.8, 5.5 and 6.6 A, and 5-step regulators use 2.8, 3.4, 4.1, 5.2 and 6.6 A, although some legacy installations use 20 A. Conductor size and insulation are chosen with the circuit length, regulator output voltage and transformer load, not the current alone.

How must L-824 cable match L-823 connectors, L-830 transformers and joint kits?

Select the cable and connector as a matched connection system. Check conductor size, conductor construction, cable insulation, outside diameter and voltage class against the L-823 primary connector, and confirm the transformer primary lead and connector configuration for the L-830 isolating transformer. A joint or splice kit must suit the cable construction and should not be selected by conductor size alone.

How is primary cable installed on an airfield?

Most airport primary cable networks are buried, either direct-buried or in ducts. Installation should follow the approved airport civil and electrical design, with attention to route, burial depth, soil, drainage, crossings and future maintenance access. Respect the manufacturer minimum bending radius and pulling tension limits, because damage during installation may not be visible immediately and can later cause insulation or circuit faults.

Is a counterpoise needed with primary cable?

In FAA practice a bare counterpoise conductor is laid in the cable trench above the primary cable and bonded as designed, for lightning protection, under AC 150/5340-30J and AC 150/5370-10 (Item L-108). Indian and other projects follow the airport design and national earthing standards. Confirm the counterpoise size, bonding points and test requirements with the approved design before the cable is installed.

How is installed primary cable tested?

Depending on the project, testing may include visual inspection, conductor continuity, insulation resistance, high-voltage or dielectric testing where specified, cable identification, joint and connector inspection, series-circuit verification and transformer connection verification. Base the test voltage, duration and acceptance criteria on the applicable project procedure and the cable manufacturer requirements.

What are the common faults in primary cable circuits?

An open series circuit can come from a cable break, failed primary connector, failed joint, transformer connection fault, damaged conductor or incorrect termination. An insulation fault can come from damaged insulation, moisture ingress, mechanical damage, a failed joint or connector sealing problem. Underground faults may need sectional testing to locate the affected portion, and repeated failures should be investigated at system level rather than by replacing only the visible failed component.

Is it safe to work on primary cable while the circuit is energised?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage, and opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death. De-energise the circuit at the regulator, apply the site lock-out / tag-out procedure, use the series circuit cut-out switch or an approved isolation device rather than an improvised bypass, and work under the permit-to-work system. After any insulation-resistance or high-voltage test, discharge the cable and apply temporary earths, as cable capacitance can hold a dangerous charge. See the electrical safety warning on this page.

What should be confirmed before ordering L-824 primary cable?

Confirm the cable designation, conductor size, voltage class, insulation, screen or shield and sheath requirements, approximate length and drum lengths, installation method, operating current and frequency, primary connector type, joint or splice requirements, the airport or project specification and the quantity. For an existing installation, a cable datasheet, cable marking or a photograph of the cable identification helps confirm the required construction.
