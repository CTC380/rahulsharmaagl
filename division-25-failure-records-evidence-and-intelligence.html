Failure Records, Evidence & Intelligence

The master database layer and final integration division of the AGL Failure Database. Division 25 is not another failure category — it is the controlled information architecture that connects every failure, investigation, evidence item, corrective action, reliability result and engineering lesson captured across Divisions 01–24. Its architecture: Failure Record → Evidence → Classification → RCA → Severity → Reliability → Corrective/Preventive Action → Knowledge → Related Records.

Key principle — Division 25 should not be viewed as merely an Excel register. It is the master information layer connecting the entire AGL Failure Management System: Divisions 01–20 supply the failure classification, Division 21 the root cause, Division 22 the severity & criticality, Division 23 the reliability & trends, and Division 24 the corrective action, preventive action & lessons learned. Division 25 binds all of it into one traceable Master Failure Record.

Division 25 · Master Failure Record Field Architecture

48 of 48 fields published
A
Standard Failure Record — 18 Fields
18 fields
MFR-01 Failure ID
Definition

A unique identifier assigned to every failure, e.g. AGL-F-2026-0001. The exact numbering convention can be standardised separately, but every failure receives exactly one master record.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Unique, non-reusable identifier per failure
  • Becomes the primary key for the entire record
  • Format may encode year and sequence (AGL-F-YYYY-NNNN)
Purpose & Cross-Links

The Failure ID is the primary key linking RCA, evidence, maintenance, testing, corrective action, preventive action, reliability analysis, lessons learned and related failures into one traceable chain.

MFR-02 Equipment/System
Definition

Records the equipment or system involved in the failure, plus identifying detail where appropriate.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Runway edge light, taxiway centreline light, CCR, isolation transformer
  • PAPI, airfield sign, stop bar, RGL, primary/secondary cable, connector
  • ALCMS, control system
  • Equipment ID, Asset ID, Manufacturer, Model, Part number, Serial number where appropriate
Purpose & Cross-Links

Anchors the failure to a specific asset class and, where identified, a specific physical unit — enabling equipment-level and fleet-level reliability analysis.

MFR-03 Airport/Project, if permitted
Definition

Records the applicable airport/project information, subject to required access and confidentiality controls. The source framework explicitly qualifies this field with "if permitted."

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Airport, Project, Area
  • Runway, Taxiway, Circuit
  • Equipment location
Purpose & Cross-Links

Access restrictions and information-sharing permissions must be respected before airport/project identity is recorded or disclosed in any record, report or case study.

MFR-04 Failure Date
Definition

Records the date of failure, together with the surrounding time markers that establish the failure's timeline.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Failure date and failure time
  • Detection time, response time
  • Repair start, restoration time, closure date
Purpose & Cross-Links

Provides the temporal foundation for Division 23 reliability analysis — MTBF, MTTR, downtime and repeat-failure interval all depend on this field being captured accurately.

MFR-05 Operating Conditions
Definition

Records the operating conditions at or around the time of failure — essential context for any failure that is intermittent, thermal, environmental or electrical in nature.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Equipment/circuit/CCR operating condition, load
  • Weather, temperature, rainfall, humidity
  • Maintenance status, operational condition
  • Recent switching, recent repair, recent installation
Purpose & Cross-Links

Particularly important when investigating intermittent failures, thermal failures, environmental failures, electrical failures and operational incidents — conditions that leave no physical trace.

MFR-06 Symptoms
Definition

Records what was observed when the failure was detected — kept strictly separate from root cause to prevent premature conclusions.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Light not illuminated, flickering, low intensity
  • Incorrect PAPI indication, CCR alarm, circuit trip
  • Communication loss, sign partially illuminated
  • Intermittent operation, abnormal temperature
Purpose & Cross-Links

Symptom is the starting point of the investigation, not its conclusion. Example: "Runway light not operating" is a symptom — not yet an observation or a cause.

MFR-07 Initial Observations
Definition

Records the first technical observations made during inspection or troubleshooting — the step between symptom and root cause.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Visible moisture, burn mark, loose connector
  • Damaged cable, corroded terminal, broken frangible component
  • Abnormal CCR indication, physical impact, optical contamination
Purpose & Cross-Links

Distinguishes three stages explicitly: Symptom ("Runway light not operating") → Initial observation ("Moisture observed inside fixture") → Root cause (to be established through RCA).

MFR-08 Root Cause Analysis
Definition

Links the failure record directly to Division 21. Captures the full causal chain from immediate cause through to root cause, with the evidence supporting the conclusion.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Immediate cause, failure mechanism, root cause
  • Contributing factors, RCA method
  • Evidence supporting the conclusion, RCA status
Purpose & Cross-Links

Example chain — Failure: fixture outage → Immediate cause: LED driver failure → Failure mechanism: thermal deterioration → Contributing factor: elevated operating temperature → Root cause: design/thermal issue, if supported by evidence.

MFR-09 Corrective Action
Definition

Links directly to Division 24. Records what was actually done to restore the equipment or system to service.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Immediate corrective action, temporary corrective action
  • Permanent corrective action, component replacement
  • Repair, system restoration, retesting, closure
Purpose & Cross-Links

Corrective action closes the immediate failure; it is distinct from, and a prerequisite to, preventive action, which addresses recurrence.

MFR-10 Preventive Action
Definition

Records the action taken to prevent recurrence of the failure — the mechanism through which RCA findings feed back into standards, design and practice.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • PM modification, inspection modification
  • Design change, installation improvement, component improvement
  • SOP update, training, testing improvement
  • Environmental protection, spare-parts strategy
Purpose & Cross-Links

Completes the connection Failure → RCA → Preventive Action — without this link, RCA findings never change future outcomes.

MFR-11 Applicable Standards
Definition

Records the applicable technical/regulatory standards or requirements associated with the failure. The database records the specific applicable reference, not merely a body name.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Applicable ICAO requirements, FAA requirements where applicable
  • DGCA requirements, Indian Standards
  • Project specifications, approved technical specifications
  • Manufacturer requirements, airport standards/procedures
Purpose & Cross-Links

A reference such as "ICAO Annex 14, Vol I" is required — a bare "ICAO" or "FAA" entry does not meet the intent of this field.

MFR-12 Related Products
Definition

Links the failure to the relevant product/component record — LED fixture model, LED driver, isolation transformer, connector, cable, CCR, PAPI unit, sign.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • LED fixture model, LED driver
  • Isolation transformer, connector, cable
  • CCR, PAPI unit, sign
Purpose & Cross-Links

Creates the connection to the AGL Product Encyclopedia / Product & Component Reliability system, enabling product-level failure-rate analysis.

MFR-13 Related Drawings
Definition

Links relevant engineering drawings, with the drawing revision that was applicable at the time of failure explicitly identified.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • AGL layout, circuit diagram, wiring diagram
  • Installation detail, base-can detail
  • PAPI layout, sign drawing, CCR schematic
Purpose & Cross-Links

Revision control matters: the record should identify the drawing revision used or applicable at the time of failure, not simply the current drawing.

MFR-14 Related SOPs
Definition

Links applicable procedures — inspection, maintenance, testing, installation, alignment and emergency-response SOPs.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Inspection SOP, maintenance SOP, testing SOP
  • Connector installation SOP, CCR maintenance SOP
  • PAPI alignment procedure, emergency response procedure
Purpose & Cross-Links

Allows the database to determine whether the failure exposed a procedure weakness or a procedural non-compliance — a key preventive-action trigger.

MFR-15 Related Case Studies
Definition

Links similar historical failures or engineering cases, so an engineer facing a new failure can immediately ask: have we seen this failure before?

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Prior case studies with matching failure mode
  • Prior case studies at the same location/equipment type
  • Prior case studies with the same root cause
Purpose & Cross-Links

The database should surface related records automatically rather than relying on an engineer's memory of past incidents.

MFR-16 Lessons Learned
Definition

Links the lessons captured in Division 24 — the mechanism through which knowledge from this specific failure is retained for future projects.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Installation lesson, testing lesson
  • Maintenance lesson, design lesson
  • Reliability lesson, safety lesson
Purpose & Cross-Links

A failure record without a lessons-learned link is closed but not yet converted into reusable engineering knowledge.

MFR-17 References
Definition

Records supporting references used during the investigation — the source documents behind every conclusion in the record.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Test report, inspection report, manufacturer analysis
  • Maintenance record, photometric report, drawing
  • Technical specification, standard
  • Previous failure record, RCA report
Purpose & Cross-Links

Every reference cited in the RCA or corrective/preventive action should trace back to a document listed here.

MFR-18 Revision History
Definition

Every significant change to the failure record should be traceable — particularly important for technically controlled failure information.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Revision, date, change
  • Changed by, reviewed by, approved by
Purpose & Cross-Links

Typical progression: Rev 0 — failure created → Rev 1 — RCA added → Rev 2 — corrective action added → Rev 3 — closure/effectiveness verification.

B
Evidence Database — 12 Fields
12 fields
EVD-19 Failure Photographs
Definition

Stores photographs showing the failure condition, with metadata sufficient to establish provenance and context.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Failed fixture, damaged cable, burnt component
  • Corrosion, water ingress, physical impact
  • Damaged connector, structural damage
Purpose & Cross-Links

Recommended metadata: Failure ID, date, location, photographer, description, before/after status — without this, a photograph cannot be reliably linked back to its record.

EVD-20 Test Readings
Definition

Records measured technical values taken during investigation, retaining the original test record rather than only a summarized value where possible.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Insulation resistance, earth resistance
  • Voltage, current, contact resistance
  • CCR output, transformer measurements, photometric values
Purpose & Cross-Links

Where possible the original test record — not just a transcribed number — should be retained, preserving traceability back to the instrument and test conditions.

EVD-21 Measurement Records
Definition

Stores detailed measurement information, always paired with the context needed to interpret the value correctly.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Electrical measurements, photometric measurements
  • Temperature, alignment measurements
  • Physical dimensions, resistance, voltage/current
Purpose & Cross-Links

Every measurement should record value + unit + instrument + date + condition where applicable — a bare number is not sufficient evidence.

EVD-22 Inspection Records
Definition

Links inspection records associated with the failure, helping establish whether the defect was already present before the failure occurred.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Daily inspection, routine inspection
  • Detailed inspection, post-maintenance inspection
  • Pre-commissioning inspection, condition inspection
Purpose & Cross-Links

Answers a critical investigative question: was the defect already present before the failure, and if so, was it detectable at the time?

EVD-23 Maintenance Records
Definition

Links relevant maintenance history to the failure — essential for identifying repeat failures on the same asset.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Previous repair, component replacement
  • PM activity, corrective maintenance
  • Emergency maintenance, inspection findings
Purpose & Cross-Links

Maintenance history is essential reliability evidence — it is the primary source for identifying repeat failures against Division 23's repeat-failure analysis.

EVD-24 Alarm/Event Logs
Definition

Captures system-generated information, establishing sequence and timing — particularly important for intermittent or control-system failures.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • CCR alarms, ALCMS alarms
  • Communication alarms, protection trips
  • Control-system events, system shutdown events
Purpose & Cross-Links

Event logs can establish sequence and timing objectively, independent of what an operator recalls or reports — critical for control-system RCA.

EVD-25 Photometry Records
Definition

Links relevant photometric information, particularly valuable for detecting degradation before complete failure.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Intensity measurements, photometric survey
  • Beam-angle results, uniformity, colour measurements
  • PAPI photometric performance, fixture performance trends
Purpose & Cross-Links

Photometric trend data can reveal degradation well before a fixture fails outright — a leading indicator that a bare pass/fail test would miss.

EVD-26 Test Reports
Definition

Links formal test reports relevant to the failure, from factory acceptance through to functional and integration testing.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Factory Acceptance Test, Site Acceptance Test
  • Insulation test, CCR test, transformer test, cable test
  • Functional test, integration test, photometric test
Purpose & Cross-Links

Formal test reports provide the documented, signed-off evidence trail that a bare reading in the field cannot substitute for.

EVD-27 Damaged-Component Photographs
Definition

Distinguished from general failure photographs — provides close-up evidence of the specific damage mechanism at the component level.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Burnout, cracking, corrosion
  • Thermal damage, seal deterioration
  • Mechanical damage, connector damage, internal component failure
Purpose & Cross-Links

These close-up images become valuable evidence for future RCA and for product-level reliability analysis across similar components.

EVD-28 RCA Evidence
Definition

Stores the evidence specifically used to establish the root cause — the objective basis on which the RCA conclusion rests.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Test results, teardown photographs, laboratory analysis
  • Manufacturer report, historical trend, environmental data
  • Comparison with healthy equipment, failure reproduction
Purpose & Cross-Links

Critical principle: the RCA conclusion should be traceable to its supporting evidence — a root cause without linked evidence is an unsupported opinion.

EVD-29 Repair Evidence
Definition

Records evidence demonstrating what was actually repaired, distinct from what corrective action was merely planned or reported.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Before/after photographs, replaced component
  • Repair record, wiring correction
  • Connector replacement, seal replacement, equipment modification
Purpose & Cross-Links

Repair evidence closes the gap between the corrective action recorded in the failure record and what was physically done in the field.

EVD-30 Post-Repair Test Evidence
Definition

Confirms that the corrective action actually worked, completing the technical evidence chain from failure through to verified restoration.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Insulation resistance, continuity, earth resistance
  • Functional test, CCR test, photometry
  • PAPI verification, ALCMS communication test
Purpose & Cross-Links

Completes the chain: Failure Evidence → RCA Evidence → Repair Evidence → Post-Repair Evidence — a failure is not truly closed until this evidence exists.

C
Intelligence Layer — 18 Fields
18 fields
INT-31 Failure Classification
Definition

Every failure receives a standardised classification, aligned with the category divisions established earlier in the Failure Database (01–20).

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Lighting fixture, electrical, CCR, transformer failure
  • Cable/connector, mechanical, control-system failure
  • Human/installation, environmental failure, operational incident
  • PAPI, sign, approach-lighting failure
Purpose & Cross-Links

Standardized classification is the entry point for every statistical query — it must be applied consistently to be useful.

INT-32 Failure-Mode Tag
Definition

Assigns a standardised failure-mode tag describing how the equipment failed, enabling rapid searching and statistical analysis.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Open circuit, short circuit, moisture ingress
  • LED failure, driver failure, insulation breakdown
  • Misalignment, corrosion, overheating, communication loss
Purpose & Cross-Links

Failure-mode tags are what make it possible to answer "how many LED driver failures did we have this year" in seconds rather than by manual review.

INT-33 Cause Tag
Definition

Links to the RCA cause classification from Division 21, categorizing why the failure occurred at the systemic level.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Electrical, mechanical, optical, thermal
  • Environmental, installation, maintenance, design
  • Manufacturing, material, human, procedural, software, communication, operational
Purpose & Cross-Links

Directly connects Division 25 records to Division 21's root-cause taxonomy, keeping cause classification consistent across the whole database.

INT-34 Severity Tag
Definition

Links to Division 22's severity classification, indicating how serious the failure was from an engineering standpoint.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Critical, Major, Moderate
  • Minor, Degradation
Purpose & Cross-Links

Severity tagging supports prioritisation of both immediate response and longer-term reliability investment.

INT-35 Safety-Impact Tag
Definition

Records the safety impact of the failure, enabling rapid identification of safety-related failures across the whole database.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • None, Low, Moderate
  • High, Critical
Purpose & Cross-Links

Safety-impact tagging must be applied independently of operational-impact and severity tagging — a low-severity failure can still carry meaningful safety risk.

INT-36 Operational-Impact Tag
Definition

Records the operational impact of the failure, supporting operational prioritisation alongside safety-impact tagging.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • None, Low, Moderate
  • High, Critical
Purpose & Cross-Links

Feeds directly into downtime and repair-time prioritisation decisions made at the operational level.

INT-37 Environmental-Condition Tag
Definition

Tags the environmental condition associated with the failure, allowing correlation between environmental conditions and failure occurrence.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Rain, flooding, high temperature, humidity
  • Sand, dust, salt, lightning
  • Storm, UV, extreme temperature
Purpose & Cross-Links

Environmental correlation is one of the most powerful outputs of the intelligence layer — it can reveal seasonal or climate-driven failure patterns invisible in a single record.

INT-38 Repair-Time Tag
Definition

Classifies repair duration into standardised bands, with exact time thresholds to be standardised separately.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Very short, Short
  • Moderate, Long, Extended
Purpose & Cross-Links

Standardized repair-time bands make it possible to compare repair performance across equipment types and technicians without exact-duration noise.

INT-39 Downtime Tag
Definition

Classifies system/equipment downtime resulting from the failure, always backed by the actual downtime value recorded elsewhere in the record.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • No downtime, Short downtime
  • Moderate downtime
  • Long downtime, Critical downtime
Purpose & Cross-Links

The downtime tag must be backed by an actual downtime value — it is a classification of a measured quantity, not a standalone judgement.

INT-40 Repeat-Occurrence Tag
Definition

Identifies whether the failure has occurred previously on the same or comparable equipment — directly linked to Division 23's repeat-failure analysis.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • First occurrence
  • Repeat occurrence
  • Recurring failure, Chronic failure
Purpose & Cross-Links

A failure tagged as chronic should automatically trigger a preventive-action review rather than another routine corrective action.

INT-41 Detectability Tag
Definition

Classifies how easily the failure could have been detected before it became a full failure — particularly useful for identifying hidden reliability risks.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Immediately detectable, Inspection detectable
  • Functional-test detectable, Specialized-test detectable
  • Intermittently detectable, Latent
Purpose & Cross-Links

Failures tagged latent or intermittently detectable are the strongest candidates for inspection-checklist or testing-procedure revision.

INT-42 Reliability Category
Definition

Assigns a reliability classification to the failure, based on defined criteria rather than subjective judgement.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • High reliability, Normal reliability
  • Degraded reliability, Low reliability
  • Recurring reliability issue, Critical reliability concern
Purpose & Cross-Links

Defined criteria — not ad hoc judgement — keep this tag consistent and statistically meaningful across the whole failure population.

INT-43 Related Failure Records
Definition

Links similar or connected failures, allowing engineers to identify patterns immediately rather than rediscovering them independently.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Same equipment model
  • Same airport or location
  • Same batch, same root cause
Purpose & Cross-Links

Example: Failure F-001 (LED driver failure) links to F-014 (same model), F-027 (same airport), F-041 (same batch) and F-063 (same root cause).

INT-44 Related Troubleshooting Procedure
Definition

Links the appropriate troubleshooting guide from the Troubleshooting Atlas, converting the failure record into a practical engineering tool.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • CCR troubleshooting, series-circuit troubleshooting
  • Fixture troubleshooting, PAPI troubleshooting
  • Connector troubleshooting, ALCMS troubleshooting
Purpose & Cross-Links

This is the link that turns a historical record into something a technician can actually use in the field the next time a similar fault appears.

INT-45 Related Maintenance Procedure
Definition

Links the relevant maintenance SOP from the Maintenance Handbook, connecting the failure back to the procedure meant to prevent or catch it.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Runway fixture maintenance, CCR maintenance
  • Transformer maintenance, connector maintenance
  • Cable maintenance, PAPI maintenance
Purpose & Cross-Links

Allows an engineer reviewing a maintenance procedure to ask: what failures caused this procedure to be revised?

INT-46 Related Inspection Checklist
Definition

Links the inspection checklist that should detect the failure, creating a feedback mechanism between failures and inspection scope.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Daily airfield inspection, runway inspection
  • Taxiway inspection, PAPI inspection
  • CCR inspection, connector inspection, earthing inspection
Purpose & Cross-Links

Creates the key feedback question: was the defect detectable through the existing inspection checklist — and if not, should the checklist change?

INT-47 Related Testing Procedure
Definition

Links the test procedure capable of detecting or confirming the failure, from the Testing & Commissioning Manual.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Insulation-resistance testing, continuity testing
  • Earth-resistance testing, CCR testing, transformer testing
  • Cable testing, photometric testing, PAPI alignment testing, ALCMS integration testing
Purpose & Cross-Links

Ensures every failure mode has at least one identified test capable of catching it before it reaches full failure.

INT-48 Related Case Study
Definition

Links the failure to a detailed engineering case study, transforming an individual failure record into reusable engineering knowledge.

Recorded Information / Examples
  • Failure background, symptoms, investigation
  • Evidence, RCA, corrective action
  • Preventive action, lessons learned, reliability outcome
Purpose & Cross-Links

The case study is the final output of the traceability chain — a complete, standalone narrative built entirely from linked record fields.

Division-Wide Architecture

How the 48 fields work together as a single Master Failure Record, and how Division 25 binds Divisions 01–24 into one traceable system.

A. Complete Information Flow

A mature Division 25 record moves through ten stages, each drawing on a specific group of fields:

1. IDENTIFY 2. DESCRIBE 3. INVESTIGATE 4. DIAGNOSE 5. ASSESS 6. CORRECT 7. PREVENT 8. LEARN 9. ANALYSE 10. CLOSE

Identify (Failure ID + Equipment + Location + Date) → Describe (Operating Conditions + Symptoms + Initial Observations) → Investigate (Evidence + Testing + Inspection + Maintenance History) → Diagnose (Failure Classification + Failure Mode + RCA) → Assess (Severity + Safety Impact + Operational Impact + Detectability) → Correct (Corrective Action + Repair + Retesting) → Prevent (Preventive Action + SOP/Design/Training/Product Improvement) → Learn (Lessons Learned + Case Study) → Analyse (Reliability Category + Repeat Occurrence + Related Failures) → Close (Post-Repair Evidence + Effectiveness + Revision History).

B. Traceability — Forward

Division 25 establishes complete bidirectional traceability. Read forward, a single failure resolves into a full engineering chain:

FAILURE RECORD EVIDENCE RCA ROOT CAUSE SEVERITY CORRECTIVE ACTION TEST REPORT PREVENTIVE ACTION SOP REVISION LESSONS LEARNED RELIABILITY TREND

C. Traceability — Reverse

The same links must also work in reverse, so that any node in the system can answer a practical engineering question:

From an SOP: Which failures caused this SOP to be revised?

From a product: What failures have occurred with this product?

From a component: What is its failure rate, repeat-failure history and RCA?

From a failure: What procedure should I use to troubleshoot and repair it?

D. Master Linking Model

The overall AGL Failure Knowledge System, expressed as one continuous chain:

FAILURE CLASSIFICATION FAILURE MODE EVIDENCE RCA ROOT CAUSE SEVERITY & CRITICALITY RELIABILITY ANALYSIS CORRECTIVE ACTION PREVENTIVE ACTION LESSONS LEARNED SOP/INSPECTION/TESTING/DESIGN/PRODUCT UPDATE RELATED FAILURE RECORDS FUTURE ENGINEERING KNOWLEDGE

Division 25 Purpose

The fundamental purpose of Division 25 is to ensure that every AGL failure becomes a controlled, traceable, evidence-supported and reusable piece of engineering knowledge. It functions as the master information layer connecting the entire AGL Failure Management System, giving a complete architecture: what failed → why it failed → how serious it was → what the trend shows → what we did → what we learned → what we must do differently next time. That is a Failure Knowledge Base — an intelligence system, not simply a failure log.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about the master failure record, evidence database and intelligence layer, as covered in Division 25 of the AGL Failure Database.

Q1What is Division 25 — Failure Records, Evidence & Intelligence in the AGL Failure Database?

Division 25 is the master database layer and final integration division. It defines the 18-field Standard Failure Record, the 12-part Evidence Database and the 18-tag Intelligence Layer, 48 fields in total, connecting every failure, RCA, corrective action and reliability result across Divisions 01–24.

Q2What are the 18 fields of the Standard Failure Record?

Failure ID, equipment/system, airport/project (if permitted), failure date, operating conditions, symptoms, initial observations, root cause analysis, corrective action, preventive action, applicable standards, related products, related drawings, related SOPs, related case studies, lessons learned, references and revision history.

Q3What does the Evidence Database contain?

Twelve evidence types: failure photographs, test readings, measurement records, inspection records, maintenance records, alarm and event logs, photometry records, test reports, damaged-component photographs, RCA evidence, repair evidence and post-repair test evidence.

Q4What is the Intelligence Layer?

It consists of 18 standardised tags (INT-31 to INT-48) that convert individual records into searchable engineering knowledge. They cover classification, failure mode, cause, severity, safety impact, operational impact, environmental condition, repair time, downtime, repeat occurrence, detectability and reliability category, plus links to related failures, troubleshooting, maintenance, inspection, testing and case studies.

Q5What stages does a mature failure record move through?

Ten stages: identify, describe, investigate, diagnose, assess, correct, prevent, learn, analyse and close. Each draws on a specific group of fields, from failure ID, equipment, location and date at the start to the reliability analysis and closure at the end.

Q6What does bidirectional traceability mean in Division 25?

Read forward, a failure record resolves into a full chain of evidence, RCA, root cause, severity, corrective action, test report, preventive action, SOP revision, lessons learned and reliability trend. Read in reverse, an SOP can show which failures caused its revision, a product can show its failures, a component can show its failure rate and RCA, and a failure can point to the procedure for troubleshooting and repair.

Q7How do Divisions 01–24 feed Division 25?

Divisions 01–20 supply failure classification, Division 21 the root cause, Division 22 severity and criticality, Division 23 reliability and trends, and Division 24 corrective action, preventive action and lessons learned.

Q8What is the purpose of Division 25?

It ensures every AGL failure becomes a controlled, traceable, evidence-supported and reusable piece of engineering knowledge, answering what failed, why, how serious it was, what the trend shows, what was done, what was learned and what must be done differently next time.
