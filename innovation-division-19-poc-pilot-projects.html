Proof-of-Concept & Pilot Projects

Division 19 is the field-validation and technology-demonstration division of the AGL Innovation Framework. Division 18 builds and technically validates a prototype in the lab; Division 19 takes that prototype and answers the next question — does it actually work, deliver measurable value and remain technically, safely and economically viable under realistic AGL operating conditions? With a framework target of 100+ Proof-of-Concept projects, this division functions as a standardized PoC pipeline, evaluation system, evidence database and scale-up decision mechanism, not merely a collection of pilots.

POC-01 PoC Methodology
Purpose

Establish a common methodology for taking an innovation from an engineering hypothesis to a controlled real-world demonstration, so results from different PoCs can later be compared on a like-for-like basis.

Standard PoC Cycle
Innovation IdeaPoC ProposalTechnical FeasibilityPoC SelectionPoC PlanningPilot-Site Selection
ImplementationTesting & MonitoringPerformance ValidationSafety ValidationCost ValidationUser Feedback
PoC EvaluationScale-Up / Modify / Stop
Why Standardization Matters

With a target of 100+ PoCs across the innovation framework, an inconsistent methodology would make results incomparable across divisions, airports and technology categories. The standard cycle keeps every PoC — however different the underlying technology — evaluated the same way.

POC-04 Technical Feasibility
Purpose

Before implementation, determine whether the proposed innovation can realistically operate in the intended AGL environment.

Assessment Areas
  • Technical: Electrical compatibility, mechanical compatibility, optical compatibility, control compatibility, communication compatibility, environmental suitability
  • Operational: Installation requirements, maintenance requirements, accessibility, operational constraints
  • Reliability: Expected failure modes, reliability potential, spare requirements, recovery requirements
  • Integration: Existing AGL infrastructure, CCR, ALCMS, monitoring systems, existing fixtures, existing circuits
Technical Feasibility Decision
Proceed / Modify / Hold / Reject
POC-05 Pilot-Site Selection
Purpose

The pilot site is critical because a poor site can produce misleading results — good site selection is a precondition for valid PoC evidence.

Site Selection Criteria
  • Technical suitability
  • Operational relevance
  • Asset criticality
  • Environmental conditions
  • Accessibility
  • Monitoring capability
  • Safety requirements
  • Installation feasibility
  • Data availability
  • Maintenance support
  • Representative operating conditions
  • Ability to compare baseline vs pilot performance
Important Principle

The pilot site should be representative enough to test the intended application, while the risk of experimentation remains appropriately controlled.

POC-10 Safety Validation
Mandatory PoC Gate

Safety validation should be a mandatory PoC gate where the innovation affects operationally or electrically significant AGL functions.

Areas
  • Electrical safety
  • Operational safety
  • Maintenance safety
  • Control-system safety
  • Failure behavior
  • Fault response
  • Emergency behavior
  • Human interaction
  • Environmental safety considerations
Key Question
Does the innovation introduce any new hazard or change the existing risk profile?

The PoC should not proceed to broader deployment until applicable safety requirements have been satisfactorily addressed.

POC-14 PoC Success Criteria
Purpose

Every PoC should have measurable success criteria before implementation. This prevents the evaluation criteria from being changed after seeing the results.

Categories
  • Technical: Functional performance, electrical performance, optical performance, reliability
  • Operational: Ease of operation, maintenance performance, availability
  • Safety: Defined safety requirements satisfied, no unacceptable new risks
  • Economic: Target cost, lifecycle benefit, maintenance savings
  • User: User acceptance, training requirements, maintainability

POC-15 PoC Report
Purpose

Every completed PoC should produce a formal PoC Final Report.

Recommended Structure
  • A. Executive Summary — Objective, technology, site, result, decision
  • B. Background — Existing problem, existing technology, innovation proposed
  • C. PoC Scope — Included, excluded, duration
  • D. Technical Description — Architecture, equipment, configuration
  • E. Implementation — Installation, commissioning, baseline
  • F. Testing — Test methods, test results
  • G. Monitoring — Performance data, trends, events
  • H. Safety — Safety assessment, risk observations
  • I. Cost — PoC cost, operating cost, lifecycle assessment
  • J. User Feedback — Benefits, issues, recommendations
  • K. Failures / Lessons Learned — Failure modes, root causes, improvements
  • L. Final Assessment — Successful / Partially Successful / Unsuccessful / Requires Further Development
  • M. Recommendation — Scale / Modify / Repeat / Hold / Stop
POC-16 Scale-Up Decision
Most Important Function

This is one of the most important functions of Division 19. A successful PoC should not automatically become an airport-wide deployment.

Scale-Up Assessment
  • Technical: Requirements achieved? Reliability demonstrated? Integration proven?
  • Safety: Safety requirements satisfied? Risks controlled?
  • Operational: Maintenance practical? Workforce capable?
  • Economic: Lifecycle economics acceptable?
  • Manufacturing: Product available at scale? Supply chain established?
  • Support: Spare parts available? Documentation available? Training available?
Decision
SCALE / MODIFY & REPEAT / HOLD / STOP

Ultimate Vision for Division 19

The objective is not simply to achieve 100+ PoCs — it is to create 100+ evidence-based AGL innovation experiments, where every project produces one of three valuable outcomes.

1

Validated Innovation

→ Scale up

2

Improvement Required

→ Modify and retest

3

Technology Not Viable

→ Stop, document and capture the lesson

Most Important Design Principle

No PoC should be considered wasted. A successful PoC produces a validated technology. An unsuccessful PoC produces engineering knowledge. A modified PoC produces design improvement. And a scaled PoC produces operational innovation.

Division 19 should function not merely as a collection of pilot projects, but as a standardized PoC pipeline, evaluation system, evidence database and scale-up decision mechanism — the field laboratory for the entire AGL Innovation Strategy, feeding validated technologies forward into Division 20 (Technology Validation, Qualification & Deployment) and preserving every outcome, successful or not, in the AGL Knowledge Hub.

The complete Division 19 philosophy:

SELECT → PLAN → DEMONSTRATE → MEASURE → VALIDATE → LEARN → SCALE
