02 · Standards & Compliance

AGL Standards & Compliance in India

AGL projects in India should be developed against the requirements applicable to the particular aerodrome and project. Indian airport tender and technical documentation can reference a combination of ICAO standards and guidance, DGCA requirements, AAI / airport-specific specifications, BIS / Indian Standards, IEC standards, and FAA specifications where specifically required by the project.

For example, an AAI tender for airfield ground lights references ICAO Annex 14 Volume I, ICAO Airport Design Manual Parts 4 and 5, IEC TS 61827, FAA AC 150/5345-46F and FAA Engineering Brief 67D. It also refers to DGCA-CAR compliance and relevant BIS requirements for acceptance testing of indigenous products.

IS 12290:1987 — isolating transformers for airport lighting systems. IS 12291:1987 — constant-current regulators for airport lighting systems. IS 11071 — inset-type aerodrome lighting fittings. IS 12309 Parts 1 & 2 — installation and maintenance of aerodrome lighting fittings.

There is no single generic specification set that applies to every Indian AGL project. The applicable requirements should always be checked against the airport, project tender, approved design, applicable DGCA requirements, AAI or airport specifications and equipment-specific standards.

03 · ICAO vs FAA

ICAO vs FAA for AGL in India

ICAO provides international Standards and Recommended Practices for aerodromes, while FAA Advisory Circulars provide U.S. specifications and guidance. For projects in India, the applicable specification depends on the project documentation — and AAI tenders show both references can occur within the same procurement framework.

ICAO — commonly relevant for

  • Aerodrome visual aids
  • Runway and taxiway lighting
  • Approach lighting
  • Operational requirements
  • Aerodrome design

FAA — may appear for specific equipment

  • FAA AC 150/5345-46 for airfield lighting equipment
  • FAA Engineering Brief 67D for LED lighting considerations
  • Other FAA equipment specifications where referenced by the project

Practical approach: identify the project requirement → identify the applicable standard → check equipment compliance → verify testing requirements → complete installation and commissioning.

11 · Photometry

Airport, Runway & AGL Photometry in India

AGL photometry in India verifies that installed airfield lights deliver the intensity, beam pattern and colour the specification requires. Airport photometry is used for new installations, acceptance, periodic checks and before and after LED retrofit.

Runway photometry India

Runway photometry covers edge, threshold, end, centreline and touchdown-zone lights, measured against the intensity and beam requirements that apply to each light type.

  • Intensity and beam spread
  • Aiming and alignment check
  • Colour of emitted light

Airport photometry India

Airport photometry extends to taxiway, approach and PAPI lighting, so the whole visual-aid system can be assessed rather than individual fixtures only.

  • Taxiway centreline and edge lights
  • Approach lighting
  • PAPI beam and transition setting

AGL photometry India

AGL photometry results support acceptance decisions, maintenance planning and replacement of lights that no longer meet their required output.

  • Acceptance and commissioning records
  • Periodic performance checks
  • LED retrofit validation

Photometry complements electrical testing: a fixture can be energised and still fall short of its required light output, which is why photometric measurement is commonly part of acceptance and periodic checks on Indian airfields.

17 · FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

What standards apply to AGL projects in India?
Indian AGL projects can reference a combination of ICAO Annex 14 and Airport Design Manual guidance, DGCA requirements and CARs, AAI or airport-specific specifications, BIS/Indian Standards, IEC standards and FAA specifications where specifically required by the project. The applicable set should be checked against the airport, tender and approved design.
Do Indian AGL tenders use ICAO or FAA specifications?
Both can appear within the same procurement framework. AAI tender documentation has referenced ICAO Annex 14 Volume I and the Airport Design Manual alongside FAA AC 150/5345-46F and FAA Engineering Brief 67D, together with DGCA-CAR and BIS compliance requirements.
Which Indian Standards (BIS) apply to AGL equipment?
BIS documentation identifies IS 12290 for isolating transformers, IS 12291 for constant-current regulators, IS 11071 for inset-type aerodrome lighting fittings, and IS 12309 Parts 1 and 2 for installation and maintenance of aerodrome lighting fittings.
Is LED AGL retrofit suitable for Indian airports?
LED technology is increasingly relevant to Indian airport lighting projects. Before converting an existing system, CCR compatibility, series-circuit characteristics, transformer compatibility, cable condition, connectors, control interfaces and fixture photometric performance should be evaluated.
What AGL requirements apply to CAT II/III operations in India?
CAT II/III operations require specialised facilities and carefully maintained visual aids, including precision approach lighting, runway and taxiway centreline lighting, stop bars, runway guard lights and reliable power-supply arrangements, with serviceability requirements linked to DGCA CAR and AIP procedures.
What are the PAPI requirements for airports in India?
PAPI requirements for an Indian airport depend on the runway, the approach and the approved aerodrome design. Provision at each runway end, unit count and setting angles should be confirmed against DGCA requirements, ICAO Annex 14 Volume I and the airport tender documents.
Which standards apply to PAPI in India?
Indian PAPI projects commonly reference ICAO Annex 14 Volume I and Airport Design Manual guidance, with DGCA and AAI requirements. FAA AC 150/5345-28 may appear where a tender specifies FAA-type PAPI equipment, and BIS or IEC requirements apply as stated in the project documents.
How do I choose a PAPI manufacturer in India?
Compare compliance with the specified standard, type-test and photometric reports, beam and colour-transition accuracy, service life, spares and local support, and compatibility with the existing series circuit and CCR.
What should a PAPI BOQ and tender specification include?
A PAPI BOQ usually lists PAPI units, mounting stands and foundations, isolating transformers, cable and connectors, earthing, aiming tools, testing and commissioning. The tender specification should state the governing standard, photometric acceptance criteria, installation tolerances and testing requirements.
Why is runway and airport photometry needed in India?
Photometry confirms that installed lights deliver the required intensity, beam pattern and colour. It supports acceptance, periodic checks and LED retrofit validation, because an energised fixture can still fall short of its required light output.
What should an AGL tender specification in India include?
It should name the governing standards for each item, equipment ratings, photometric and electrical acceptance criteria, installation tolerances, documentation to be submitted, and the tests to be witnessed before handover. Where ICAO and FAA references both appear, it should state which governs each item.
How is AGL commissioned at an Indian airport?
AGL commissioning covers circuit and insulation testing, equipment and control checks, photometry and documented acceptance before handover. The exact tests follow the project specification and the applicable DGCA, AAI and ICAO requirements.
Where can I find AGL training, courses and jobs in India?
