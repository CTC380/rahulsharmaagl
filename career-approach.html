05 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about the approach and visual guidance question bank, how the drill and scoring work, and the core systems it covers.

Q1What is the Approach & Visual Guidance question bank?

It is a 300-question reference and drill on approach lighting systems, PAPI, APAPI, VASI, REIL, visual approach guidance and runway alignment. Every question has a model answer, an explanation and tagged key terms, and the whole bank is searchable, filterable and drillable on this page.

Q2What topics does the question bank cover?

Seven topics: Approach Lighting Systems (80), PAPI (50), APAPI (30), VASI (30), REIL (25), Visual Approach Guidance (40) and Runway Alignment (45). Each topic can be opened pre-filtered from the topic chips or the filter pills in the explorer.

Q3What difficulty levels are included?

Three levels: Beginner (43), Intermediate (161) and Advanced (96). About 86% of the bank is Intermediate or above, so it leans toward working-engineer depth. If terminology is still new, start with the Beginner filter, or with the AGL Fundamentals bank.

Q4What question formats does the bank use?

Technical explanation (206), scenario-based (35), multiple choice (26), troubleshooting (21), interview-style (9), short answer (2) and maintenance (1). Fault-finding, scenario and interview-style questions probe how you diagnose, inspect and justify decisions on approach and visual guidance systems, not just what you can define.

Q5How does Drill Mode work, and how is my answer scored?

Drill Mode gives a shuffled run through the bank, filtered by level and topic if you choose. Type your own answer and check it, or just reveal the model answer. Multiple-choice cards are answered by picking an option. Typed answers are marked with weighted key-term matching (with stemming and typo tolerance) plus overall wording overlap. It is a close local approximation of a marker, not true AI grading, so use it to spot gaps rather than as a formal assessment.

Q6What does an approach lighting system do?

An approach lighting system extends outward from the runway threshold and gives the pilot visual references for alignment, height perception and roll cues during the transition from instrument to visual flight. The bank covers how those systems are configured, how they relate to the runway lighting, and how they are inspected, maintained and troubleshot.

Q7What is the difference between PAPI, APAPI and VASI?

PAPI uses a single wing bar of four sharp-transition light units, and the pilot reads how many appear red or white to judge position against the approach slope. APAPI is the abbreviated form with two units. VASI uses two or three light bars and the pilot compares the colours of the bars. All three are covered in their own topics, including installation, alignment, photometry and fault-finding.

Q8What are Runway End Identifier Lights (REIL)?

REIL are a pair of synchronised flashing lights, one on each side of the runway threshold, that give quick, positive identification of the approach end of a runway, especially where it is hard to pick out against surrounding lights. The bank has 25 REIL questions, from purpose and configuration to synchronisation faults and acceptance.

Q9What does the Runway Alignment topic cover?

The 45 Runway Alignment questions cover how approach and visual guidance systems relate to the runway and the approach path: the purpose of alignment, how obstacles affect visual alignment, and the survey, commissioning and photometric checks that confirm the installation does what the design intends.

Q10Which standards does the question bank refer to?

Answers are framed against ICAO Annex 14 (referenced in 131 questions) and FAA publications, most often AC 150/5340-30J (124), AC 150/5345-28H (40) and AC 150/5340-26C (56), plus the FAA AIM Chapter 2 (56). Many answers are deliberately worded as subject to the applicable standard, so always check the current edition that governs your project.

Q11Is this useful for AGL job interviews?

Yes. Alongside 9 interview-style questions, the 21 troubleshooting and 35 scenario questions mirror how technical interviewers probe fault-finding, alignment and compliance judgement on approach and visual guidance systems.
