PAPI Lamp/LED Module

The light source at the core of every PAPI unit. Available as a traditional halogen lamp or a modern LED module, this component provides the raw luminous output that the optical assembly shapes into the precise red/white glide slope beam. The choice between halogen and LED affects intensity, lifespan, power consumption, and maintenance intervals.

Overview

What Is It?

The PAPI Lamp or LED Module is the replaceable light‑generating component within a PAPI light unit. It is the primary source of the high‑intensity light that the optical assembly (reflector, prism, filters) shapes into the characteristic glide slope beam. Two main technologies are used:

  • Halogen Lamp: A tungsten‑halogen incandescent lamp, standardised at 200W (6.6A, PK30d base), with a colour temperature of 2800K–3200K. Halogen lamps provide high luminous flux and excellent colour rendering but have a relatively short lifespan (~1,000 hours at full intensity) and degrade over time.
  • LED Module: A solid‑state assembly containing multiple high‑power LEDs with an integrated driver and heat sink. LED modules offer 50,000+ hours of life, instant‑on capability, lower power consumption, and consistent output over their lifetime, but require careful thermal management and have higher upfront cost.

Both types are designed to be field‑replaceable, with the lamp or module fitting into a standardized socket or holder within the PAPI unit. The choice between halogen and LED affects the photometric performance, maintenance schedule, and total cost of ownership of the PAPI system.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The lamp or LED module’s purpose is to sustain, over its entire service life, the exact colour and intensity that the pilot depends on to read a sharp red/white split. PAPI works by contrast: a pilot judges glide slope by the crisp boundary between red and white, not by either colour in isolation, so if one unit's light source degrades or shifts in colour temperature relative to the other three, the transition point stops being consistent across the array even though the optics and angle haven't changed at all. This is precisely why halogen lamps, which dim and yellow measurably as they age, need comparatively frequent replacement to keep every unit matched, while LED modules hold their output and colour far more consistently across a much longer service life. Whichever technology is used, the module's job is the same: keep the light source itself from becoming the thing that quietly undermines an otherwise correctly aligned and powered PAPI unit.

Comparison

Halogen vs. LED

Each light source technology has distinct characteristics that influence selection for new installations or retrofit projects:

Halogen Lamp

Pros: High luminous intensity (up to 25,000 cd), excellent colour rendering (CRI > 95), low initial cost, simple dimming via current control.
Cons: Short life (1,000–2,000 hours), declining output over time, high heat generation, frequent replacements, higher running cost (energy + labour).

LED Module

Pros: 50,000+ hour lifespan, consistent intensity, instant‑on, low energy consumption, reduced maintenance, no UV/IR output, environmentally friendly.
Cons: Higher initial cost, requires precise thermal management, potential for colour shift over time, complex dimming (PWM or current‑controlled), driver failures possible.

Many airfields are transitioning to LED PAPI systems to reduce maintenance costs and improve reliability. However, halogen remains a proven, cost‑effective option for low‑traffic or budget‑constrained operations.

Application

Where Is It Used?

The PAPI lamp or LED module is a universal component used in every PAPI system, regardless of the installation type:

  • Standard 4‑unit PAPI – on precision runways (CAT I, II, III) worldwide.
  • APAPI (2‑unit) systems – using a single lamp or LED per unit.
  • Helicopter PAPI – with steeper angle optics, often using LED for rapid start and reliability.
  • Portable PAPI – typically LED for low power consumption and battery operation.
  • Retrofit projects – replacing halogen lamps with LED modules in existing PAPI fixtures to extend life and reduce maintenance.
  • As a spare part – stocked by airfield maintenance teams for scheduled or unscheduled replacements.
Placement

Installation & Location

The lamp or LED module fits into the optical housing at the rear of each PAPI unit on the wing bar, secured with a bayonet, threaded, or bracket mount depending on the fixture design, and positioned so its light source sits precisely at the optical system's focal point. Correct seating is critical, since even a small offset from focus will blur the sharp red/white transition the whole PAPI depends on, so the module is pushed fully home and locked before the housing is resealed against moisture. Replacement is done in the field at the fixture itself, with the wing bar's alignment re-checked afterward to confirm the module hasn't shifted the beam's aim, and spare modules are kept on hand at the airfield precisely because this is the component most likely to need swapping between full unit replacements.

Function

How Does It Work?

The PAPI lamp or LED module converts electrical energy into visible light, providing the raw output for the optical system:

  • Halogen Operation: An electric current passes through a tungsten filament sealed in a quartz envelope with halogen gas. The filament heats to ~3000°C, emitting a continuous spectrum of light. The halogen cycle redeposits evaporated tungsten back onto the filament, extending life compared to standard incandescent lamps.
  • LED Operation: A semiconductor diode emits light when forward‑biased. Multiple LEDs (often in a chip‑on‑board array) are mounted on a metal‑core PCB with a phosphor coating to produce white light. An integrated driver regulates the current, and a heat sink dissipates thermal energy. The module may include secondary optics to tailor the beam shape before it enters the PAPI’s main optical assembly.
  • Intensity Control: Both types are controlled by the CCR via the series circuit. Halogen lamps dim smoothly by reducing current. LED modules require either current reduction (if the driver is linear) or pulse‑width modulation (PWM) – the module’s driver translates the CCR current into the appropriate LED drive signal, providing the required brightness steps (day, twilight, night).
  • Mounting & Electrical Interface: The lamp or module fits into a socket or connector within the PAPI unit. Halogen lamps use a PK30d prefocus base, while LED modules have a proprietary connector or screw terminals for the driver. Both must be handled carefully to avoid contamination or damage.
Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

The lamp or LED module sits at the end of the control chain, not inside it — it has no control logic of its own and simply converts whatever current or drive signal it receives into light output. A halogen lamp dims smoothly because reducing the series current directly reduces filament heat; an LED module needs an integrated driver to translate the same series current (held constant) into the PWM or regulated-current signal its diodes actually require, since LEDs don't dim proportionally to current the way a halogen filament does.

This is why LED retrofits into legacy CCR circuits need a driver stage the halogen lamp never required: the CCR's control logic (step selection, loop monitoring) is unchanged either way, but the module itself has to do more translation work when the light source is a semiconductor rather than a filament.

Construction

Physical Construction

A halogen lamp is built as a sealed quartz or hard-glass envelope containing a tungsten filament and a halogen gas fill, mounted on a standardised base or bi-pin holder that plugs directly into the socket inside the PAPI unit's optical assembly; a dichroic or reflective coating on some versions helps direct heat away from the beam path. An LED module, by contrast, is a small circuit assembly: an array of high-power LED die mounted on a metal-core or ceramic circuit board for heat spreading, bonded to a finned aluminium heat sink that draws heat away from the junctions, with an integrated driver circuit regulating current to the array and a connector or flying leads bringing in primary power. Both formats are built to the same mechanical footprint and mounting interface as the socket or holder they replace, so a technician can swap a failed lamp or module for a new one of the same type without modifying the optical assembly around it, though moving between halogen and LED typically requires a compatible holder or driver change.

Prerequisites

Installation Requirements

Lamp/Module Inspection
Pre-Installation

Inspect the halogen envelope or LED module for shipping or handling damage before insertion, avoiding bare-finger contact with quartz-envelope lamps.

Holder/Driver Compatibility Check
Pre-Installation

Confirm the socket or holder in the optical assembly matches the PK30d prefocus base or proprietary LED connector, since moving between halogen and LED typically requires a compatible holder or driver change.

Seating & Fit Confirmation
Mechanical

Seat the lamp or module fully into the socket or holder, confirming positive contact, since a poor connection causes flicker or premature failure.

Heat Sink Contact Check (LED Variant)
Mechanical

Confirm the LED module's metal-core board is properly bonded to the finned aluminium heat sink, since poor thermal contact shortens junction life.

Pre-Energisation Sign-off
Documentation

Record lamp/module type, batch or lot number, and seating confirmation as-built before the circuit is energised for luminous intensity testing.

Verification

Testing & Commissioning

Luminous Intensity Verification
Final Acceptance

Measure peak white and red luminous intensity with a calibrated photometer against the rated cd values, confirming the lamp or LED module meets specification at rated current.

Operating Current Verification
Energisation

Confirm the halogen lamp or LED driver draws the correct 6.6A series current (or driver-regulated equivalent) at each intensity step without exceeding rated limits.

Colour Temperature / Chromaticity Check
Final Acceptance

For halogen, confirm colour temperature falls within 2800–3200K; for LED modules, confirm the selected 4000–6500K bin matches the design colour point.

Base / Connector Fit Check
Installation

Confirm the PK30d prefocus base or proprietary LED connector seats fully and makes positive contact, since a poor connection causes flicker or premature failure.

Warm-up / Turn-on Time Check
Energisation

Confirm the lamp or module reaches rated output within the specified time (under 1 second for halogen, instant-on for LED), important for approach-light visibility.

Thermal Performance Check
Final Acceptance

Verify the module's operating temperature stays within its rated envelope/junction limit under sustained operation, especially for LED modules in the fixture housing.

Dimming Response Check
Energisation

Step the module through the CCR's current or PWM range and confirm smooth, correctly proportioned light output at each step with no flicker.

Documentation & Lot Sign-off
Final Acceptance

Record luminous intensity, colour, and operating current readings alongside lamp/module batch or lot number in the commissioning dossier for handover.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Halogen Lamp Burnout

The most frequent failure mode. Lamps should be replaced in pairs or sets to maintain uniform intensity across the PAPI array. Always use the correct wattage and base type. Handle lamps with gloves to avoid skin oil contamination, which can cause premature failure.

LED Module Driver Failure

LED modules themselves are robust, but the integrated driver or power supply can fail due to surges or overheating. Check driver voltages and current outputs when troubleshooting; replace the entire module if the driver is non‑repairable.

Lumen Depreciation

Both halogen and LED sources lose output over time. Halogen can drop 20–30% by end of life; LED modules typically maintain 70% at 50,000 hours. Regular photometric checks ensure intensity remains within ICAO tolerances; schedule replacements accordingly.

Contact Corrosion

Lamp bases and LED connectors can corrode in marine or industrial environments, leading to intermittent operation or voltage drop. Apply dielectric grease to contacts during installation and inspect regularly.

Thermal Management (LED)

LED modules require effective heat sinking. If the heatsink is clogged with dust or the thermal interface material degrades, the LED may overheat, reducing life and shifting colour. Ensure heatsink fins are clean and thermal pads are intact.

Incorrect Lamp Wattage

Using a lamp with a different wattage than the fixture is rated for can affect the beam intensity and transition angle. Always use the standard 200W PK30d halogen lamp, and ensure the LED module is compatible with the CCR settings.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Fixture Dark, No Output
Likely Cause
Halogen lamp burnout — the most frequent failure mode.
Diagnostic Steps
Check lamp continuity, inspect for a visibly broken filament, and verify the correct wattage and base type were fitted.
Corrective Action
Replace lamps in pairs or sets to maintain uniform intensity across the array, using the correct wattage and base type and handling with gloves.
LED Module Dark or Erratic Despite LEDs Appearing Intact
Likely Cause
Integrated LED driver/power supply failure from a surge or overheating.
Diagnostic Steps
Check driver input/output voltages and current, and inspect for signs of thermal stress on the driver housing.
Corrective Action
Replace the entire module if the driver is non-repairable, and check upstream surge protection.
Photometric Check Shows Output Below Tolerance
Likely Cause
Lumen depreciation — normal ageing of halogen or LED sources.
Diagnostic Steps
Run a photometric intensity check against ICAO tolerances and compare against the source's rated life and hours in service.
Corrective Action
Replace the lamp or LED module per the scheduled replacement interval, or immediately if output falls outside tolerance.
Intermittent Operation or Voltage Drop at the Lamp/Module
Likely Cause
Contact corrosion at the lamp base or LED connector.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect lamp base/connector contacts for corrosion and check for dielectric grease application.
Corrective Action
Clean or replace corroded contacts, apply dielectric grease, and inspect regularly in aggressive environments.
LED Module Overheating or Colour Shifting
Likely Cause
Poor thermal management — a clogged heatsink or degraded thermal interface material.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect heatsink fins for dust/debris and check the thermal pad condition.
Corrective Action
Clean the heatsink, replace degraded thermal interface material, and confirm temperature returns to normal operating range.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

Complete Lamp / LED Module Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Solder-joint fatigue from repeated thermal cycling, moisture ingress through a compromised board seal, filament failure on halogen sources, or ordinary end-of-life lumen depreciation.
Effect
Fixture goes dark with no output, removing one of the four PAPI units from the glide path indication.
Detection Method
Nighttime visual patrol, periodic photometric intensity survey, and continuity/secondary-voltage checks at the fixture connector.
LED Driver Fault
High Severity
Root Cause
A degraded driver component, or a firmware fault in a driver with onboard control logic.
Effect
LED module dark or erratic despite the LEDs themselves appearing intact on visual inspection.
Detection Method
Driver output voltage/current verification, and driver fault indicator check where fitted.
Gradual Lumen Depreciation
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Ordinary LED or lamp ageing over its rated service life, accelerated by sustained operation at elevated temperature.
Effect
Photometric check shows output below tolerance, weakening the visual cue presented to pilots even though the fixture still lights.
Detection Method
Scheduled photometric intensity testing against the ICAO/FAA threshold for the unit's colour and step.
Connector / Wiring Degradation
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Corrosion at the lamp/module connector, or a wiring termination that loosened from vibration or thermal cycling.
Effect
Intermittent operation or a voltage drop at the lamp/module, causing flickering or reduced output.
Detection Method
Voltage drop measurement across the connection under load, and visual inspection of the connector during maintenance.
Thermal Management Failure
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Degraded thermal paste or a heat sink fouled with debris, reducing the module's ability to shed heat during operation.
Effect
LED module overheating or colour shifting, and accelerated ageing of the LEDs from sustained elevated junction temperature.
Detection Method
Thermal imaging of the module under load, and inspection/cleaning of the heat sink during scheduled maintenance.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Series Circuit Isolation
Electrical Safety

De-energise and lock out the CCR feeding the series circuit and verify zero energy before removing or replacing the lamp or LED module.

Hot Module Handling
Personnel Safety

Allow the module to cool before handling after an active period; halogen sources in particular retain significant heat and can cause burns during replacement.

Halogen Bulb Handling
Mechanical

Avoid touching a halogen bulb's glass envelope with bare skin; skin oils can create localised hot spots that shorten lamp life or cause premature failure.

Optical Alignment Preservation
Compliance

Seat the replacement module fully and squarely so it does not disturb the unit's precision optics; a shifted light source changes the beam's red/white transition point independent of the unit's own aim angle.

Thermal Contact on Reinstallation
Mechanical

Ensure proper thermal paste and heat sink contact when fitting an LED module; poor thermal coupling accelerates colour shift and shortens service life even if the module initially looks fine.

Outage Coordination
Operational

Raise a NOTAM if the replacement will leave the unit dark or out of tolerance during active approach operations, since PAPI is relied on as a glide-slope guidance aid.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Verify intensity, colour, and beam pattern meet specification before closing out the work and cancelling any associated NOTAM.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
A Module Swap Can Silently Shift the Transition Point
Retrofit

Even a module rated identical to the original can sit a fraction of a millimetre differently in the optic due to manufacturing tolerance, and that small shift is enough to move the red/white transition angle. Always verify the transition point after any lamp or LED module replacement, not just intensity and colour, since a healthy-looking replacement can still be optically mis-set.

Colour Shift Precedes Total LED Failure
Reliability

An LED module nearing end of life more often drifts in colour temperature or red/white balance before it fails outright, especially if thermal coupling has degraded. Track colour measurements over successive visits rather than waiting for a hard failure to trigger replacement.

Halogen-to-LED Retrofit Needs a Beam Pattern Recheck
Retrofit

An LED replacement module can match a halogen source's intensity and colour point on paper while still producing a subtly different beam spread, since the light-emitting geometry differs. Re-verify the full beam pattern against the optic's design spec after a technology retrofit, not just at the single reference angle used for halogen units.

Thermal Paste Degrades Even Without Visible Damage
Maintenance

Thermal interface material can dry out and lose conductivity over years of thermal cycling well before any visible sign appears at the module or heat sink. Where a module's colour or intensity is drifting faster than expected for its service age, check thermal contact condition before assuming the LED die itself is the root cause.

Match Modules Across the Array, Not Just Within Spec
Design

Two modules can each individually pass tolerance checks yet still look visibly different in colour or intensity when viewed side by side across the four-unit array. Where practical, replace modules from the same production batch across the array, or verify cross-unit consistency specifically, rather than only checking each unit in isolation.

Contamination During Handling Shows Up Later, Not Immediately
Installation

Fingerprints or dust introduced onto a lens or reflector surface during a module swap often aren't visible at close-out but become noticeable as a subtle intensity or uniformity drop weeks later, once dirt bonds to the residue. Clean the optical path as a standing step in every module replacement, not just when contamination is visibly obvious.

Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about the PAPI lamp or LED module: halogen versus LED, photometrics, standards, maintenance and faults.

Q1What is the PAPI lamp or LED module?

It is the replaceable light-generating component inside a PAPI light unit, either a halogen lamp or an LED module. It produces the light that the optical assembly (reflector, prism and filters) shapes into the glide slope beam.

Q2Why does the light source matter so much?

PAPI works by contrast: a pilot judges the glide slope from the crisp boundary between red and white. The source must sustain the exact colour and intensity needed for that split over its entire service life.

Q3Where is the lamp or LED module used?

In every PAPI system: standard 4-unit PAPI on CAT I, II and III precision runways, APAPI (2-unit) systems and helicopter installations.

Q4How does a halogen lamp compare with an LED module?

A 200 W halogen lamp gives about 3,800 lm and up to 25,000 cd with a 1,000 hour rated life. An LED module gives 3,000-5,000 lm and up to 20,000 cd at system level, with a rated life of 50,000 hours (L70) and much higher efficacy (80-120 lm/W versus about 19 lm/W).

Q5How is the module powered?

Both variants run on the 6.6 A series circuit, with LED modules using a PWM or current-controlled driver matched to the module's rated current and dimming method.

Q6How is intensity dimmed?

Halogen lamps dim by current reduction. LED modules dim through their PWM or current-controlled driver.

Q7Which standards apply?

ICAO Annex 14, FAA AC 150/5345-28, IEC TS 61827, SAE AS25050 (chromaticity and transmission for aeronautical lights) and IEC 60598-1 (luminaires, general requirements).

Q8How often should the lamp or LED module be checked?

Semi-annually for LED thermal performance, and annually for lumen output, LED driver function and contact corrosion. When replacing a lamp, verify the wattage matches the fixture specification.

Q9What if the fixture is completely dark?

On halogen units, lamp burnout is the most frequent cause. Check lamp continuity, look for a broken filament, and verify the correct wattage and base type were fitted.
