AGL-CS-025 — Water Ingress and Transformer Failure

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A moisture-related investigation where repeated transformer failures at the same pit location were traced to standing water and inadequate drainage — from pit inspection through insulation testing to environmental correction.

AGL-CS-025

Category

Level

1Case Study Overview

During the monsoon season, one runway edge light circuit experienced repeated transformer failures at the same field location.

The Constant Current Regulator (CCR) remained healthy, and the primary series circuit showed normal current regulation. However, the transformer associated with one light fitting had been replaced twice within eight months.

Following another period of heavy rainfall, the light became inoperative again.

A detailed investigation identified water ingress into the transformer pit, which had caused moisture contamination of the transformer, progressive insulation deterioration, corrosion of the winding terminations, and eventual transformer failure.

This case demonstrates that repeated transformer replacement will not solve a moisture-related environmental failure unless the source of water ingress is eliminated.

2Learning Objectives

After completing this case study, the learner should be able to:

  • Understand moisture-related transformer failure mechanisms.
  • Recognize symptoms of water-ingress-related transformer deterioration.
  • Investigate transformer pit drainage conditions.
  • Differentiate transformer failure from environmental failure.
  • Perform insulation and moisture-related transformer testing.
  • Assess corrosion damage caused by water ingress.
  • Develop long-term preventive maintenance solutions.

3System Background

An AGL isolating transformer is normally installed in a pit adjacent to the runway or taxiway lighting fixture.

The transformer depends on:

  • Proper sealing.
  • Drainage.
  • Environmental protection.
  • Moisture control.
  • Connector integrity.

Water ingress may occur through:

  • Flooded pits.
  • Damaged pit covers.
  • Poor drainage.
  • Defective cable entries.
  • Damaged transformer seals.
  • Condensation accumulation.

Long-term moisture exposure can affect:

  • Winding insulation.
  • Core insulation.
  • Terminations.
  • Connectors.
  • Secondary cables.

The reported condition was:

  • Same pit location affected.

  • CCR healthy.
  • Adjacent transformers operating normally.

Is the transformer itself defective, or is water ingress causing repeated environmental failure?

7Water-Ingress Failure Mechanism

A typical sequence is:

Pit flooding → Moisture exposure → Seal deterioration → Water penetration → Insulation contamination → Corrosion → Leakage current → Overheating → Transformer failure

The process is usually progressive.

The connector showed:

  • Moisture contamination.
  • Corrosion.
  • Seal deterioration.
  • Contact oxidation.

The connector environment supported continued moisture ingress.

Step 5: Internal Transformer Examination

The removed transformer showed:

  • Moisture inside the transformer enclosure.
  • Corroded winding terminations.
  • Insulation discoloration.
  • Resin cracking.
  • Evidence of long-term water exposure.

The transformer had suffered environmental moisture damage.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The transformer failures were caused by repeated water ingress into the transformer pit due to inadequate drainage and deteriorated environmental sealing.

The moisture caused:

  • Insulation deterioration.
  • Corrosion.
  • Leakage current.
  • Thermal degradation.
  • Progressive transformer failure.

The replacement transformers failed because the environmental root cause remained uncorrected.

11Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Isolated the circuit.
  2. Removed the failed transformer.
  3. Installed a new approved transformer.
  4. Replaced damaged connectors.
  5. Improved pit drainage.
  6. Cleared drainage blockages.
  7. Repaired cable-entry sealing.
  8. Verified pit water removal.
  9. Performed insulation resistance testing.
  10. Established monsoon inspection procedures.

14Why This Case Was Important

A common maintenance mistake is repeatedly replacing:

  • Transformers.
  • Connectors.
  • Secondary cables.

without investigating:

  • Drainage.
  • Water accumulation.
  • Pit sealing.
  • Environmental conditions.

The environment can be the actual failed component.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: Third transformer failure at the same pit. Standing water in the pit. CCR operating normally. Adjacent transformers healthy. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace the transformer again.
B. Replace the CCR.
C. Investigate pit drainage, moisture ingress, and environmental sealing.
D. Increase CCR current.
Correct answer: C. Repeated failures at the same location with standing water indicate an environmental root cause. Drainage and sealing must be addressed before transformer replacement.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about water ingress and transformer failure, based on this case study.

Q1What is AGL-CS-025?

AGL-CS-025 is an engineering training case study on repeated transformer failures at one runway edge light pit, traced to water ingress. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2Why did the same transformer location keep failing?

The transformer at that light had been replaced twice within eight months, while adjacent transformers operated normally. Repeated failures at one location strongly suggested an environmental root cause.

Q3What was the root cause?

Repeated water ingress into the transformer pit due to inadequate drainage and deteriorated environmental sealing. The replacement transformers failed because that environmental cause remained uncorrected.

Q4What did the moisture do to the transformer?

It caused insulation deterioration, corrosion of the winding terminations, leakage current, thermal degradation and progressive transformer failure.

Q5What should you investigate first if a third transformer fails at the same pit with standing water?

Investigate pit drainage, moisture ingress and environmental sealing. Drainage and sealing must be addressed before transformer replacement, so replacing the transformer again is not the right first step.

Q6Which test equipment was used?

The investigation used a 5 kV insulation resistance tester, winding resistance meter, moisture inspection tools, thermal imaging camera, approved AGL transformer test equipment and pit drainage inspection equipment.

Q7How was the problem corrected?

The team isolated the circuit, removed the failed transformer, installed a new approved transformer and replaced damaged connectors. They improved pit drainage, cleared blockages, repaired cable-entry sealing, verified pit water removal, tested insulation resistance and established monsoon inspection procedures.

Q8How was the repair verified?

Pit drainage was functional, insulation resistance was greater than 1000 MΩ and the light was restored. No further transformer failures occurred after the drainage correction.

Pattern

Repeated failures in one pit identify the environment as the cause. Map them.

Fix Drainage Before Replacing Again

Drainage

Check outlets and gradients. Without drainage improvement replacements will fail the same way.

Sealing

Consider improved seals or a better pit arrangement in chronically wet locations.

Inspect Pits After Heavy Rain

Inspection

Check for standing water and silt after storms. Early action prevents later failures.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

Water ingress is often an environmental root cause that can repeatedly damage isolating transformers through insulation deterioration, corrosion, and thermal degradation unless drainage and sealing deficiencies are corrected.

Systematic pit inspection, drainage assessment, insulation testing, moisture investigation, and environmental correction can prevent repeated transformer failures and significantly improve the long-term reliability of the AGL field equipment network.

In AGL engineering, replacing the transformer without eliminating the water source usually means replacing the next transformer in the same flooded pit.
