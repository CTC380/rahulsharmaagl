SGN-311 Sign Not Illuminated

Symptom — An airfield guidance sign shows no internal illumination at all when its circuit is energised, appearing completely dark to a pilot or ground vehicle at night or in low visibility, though the sign panel/legend itself is physically intact.

Equipment / System

Airfield Guidance Signs — L-858 mandatory instruction and L-853/L-857 informational/direction signs, internally illuminated by an LED module or light guide plate (LGP) assembly, driven by an LED driver/ballast and fed via an isolating transformer on a series circuit or a dedicated constant-voltage branch circuit, in a weatherproof frangible-mounted aluminium housing.

Possible Causes
HighIsolating transformer failure at the sign (open primary or secondary winding) — the most common single cause of a completely dark sign on a series circuit
HighLED driver/ballast failure, cutting output to the entire LED module or LGP illumination source
MediumComplete LED module failure — all strings/rows failed together, distinct from a partial module failure
MediumInternal wiring fault or a disconnected/corroded connector between the transformer, driver, and module
LowCCR or branch circuit at zero output — rules out a sign-specific fault; check whether other signs on the same circuit are also dark
LowSign physically damaged or displaced by jet blast, snow plough, or vehicle strike, severing internal wiring
Required Tools
Clamp-on series circuit ammeterTrue-RMS multimeter (CAT III)1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Sign as-built circuit and base-can layout drawingBase-can lifting tool and PPE for airside workReplacement transformer/driver of correct rating
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate all airside access through ATC/airfield operations before approaching the sign.
  • Treat the series circuit or branch feeder as energised until confirmed isolated and locked out at the CCR or panel.
  • Use FOD-safe tools and account for every item before leaving the movement area.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCheck the CCR/ALCMS panel or branch circuit breaker status to confirm whether this is an isolated sign fault or a wider circuit outage affecting other signs.
  2. ObservationConduct a night inspection to confirm only the reported sign is dark, and that the legend/panel itself is undamaged.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated and LOTO applied, disconnect the sign at the base can and megger the isolating transformer primary and secondary windings, or check branch circuit voltage at the sign feed.
  4. MeasurementIf the transformer/feed tests good, open the sign housing and check LED driver output voltage/current against nameplate rating.
  5. DecisionTransformer or feed fails → transformer/feed fault, replace. Feed good but driver shows no output → driver fault, route to SGN-318. Driver good but module dark → module fault, route to SGN-317.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the transformer/feed, the driver, or the LED module/LGP assembly before ordering parts.
  7. Root CauseBench-test the removed component where practical to confirm the specific failure mode for the maintenance record.
Test Points
Isolating transformer / branch feed voltage and insulation resistance
LED driver input and output voltage/current
Sign internal wiring continuity
Base can and connector condition
Root Cause Verification

After replacement, re-energise and confirm the sign illuminates evenly across its full legend at the commanded intensity, and log the repair against the sign's maintenance history.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed isolating transformer, driver, or LED module as isolated by testing.
  • Repair or reseat a faulted internal wiring connection.
  • Address any base can water ingress found during access to prevent recurrence.
Preventive Action
  • Include sign transformer/driver output checks in the periodic PM programme.
  • Maintain correctly rated transformer and driver spares for the sign types in service.
  • Trend individual sign failure history to identify chronically weak locations.
Related References

SGN-312 Sign Partially Illuminated

Symptom — An airfield guidance sign is illuminated but only part of the legend or panel area is lit — one row of a two-line sign, part of a character string, or a single module segment — while the rest of the sign remains dark.

Equipment / System

Airfield Guidance Signs — L-858 mandatory instruction and L-853/L-857 informational/direction signs, internally illuminated by an LED module or light guide plate (LGP) assembly, driven by an LED driver/ballast and fed via an isolating transformer on a series circuit or a dedicated constant-voltage branch circuit, in a weatherproof frangible-mounted aluminium housing.

Possible Causes
HighPartial failure of the LED module — one string, row, or segment of LEDs failed while the rest of the module remains functional
HighA partially delaminated or cracked LGP section no longer transmitting light from the edge-lit LED source to that area of the panel
MediumA loose or partially disconnected internal harness connector feeding one section of a multi-module sign
MediumLED driver output degraded on one channel of a multi-channel driver
LowLocalised moisture ingress causing a corroded connection at one module only
LowPhysical impact damage isolated to one section of the sign panel
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeterLED module test harness / bench power supplyDigital camera for illumination pattern documentationReplacement LED module/LGP section of correct type
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before opening the sign housing.
  • Allow driver components to cool before handling if the sign has been recently energised.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationPhotograph the illuminated/dark pattern from the normal viewing angle to document exactly which section is affected.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, open the housing and visually inspect the affected module/LGP section and its harness connector.
  3. MeasurementCheck driver output voltage/current at the affected channel or module string versus a known-good channel.
  4. DecisionDark section correlates with a specific module/string with normal driver output at its input → module/LGP fault. Driver output itself low or absent at that channel → driver fault, route to SGN-318.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm whether the fault is in the LED module, the LGP, the internal harness, or the driver channel.
  6. Root CauseDetermine whether the failure is age/end-of-life related or points to a manufacturing or installation defect common to that module batch.
Test Points
Per-channel driver output voltage/current
Module/LGP section continuity
Internal harness connector condition
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-energise and confirm uniform illumination across the entire sign legend, viewed from the design viewing angle at night.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed LED module or LGP section.
  • Reseat or replace a faulted internal harness connector.
  • Correct any water ingress point found at the affected module.
Preventive Action
  • Include full-legend illumination checks (not just 'is the sign lit') in the routine night inspection.
  • Stock module/LGP spares matching the sign types and legends in service.
Related References

SGN-313 Sign Flickering

Symptom — An illuminated sign flickers, pulses, or visibly varies in brightness instead of holding a steady output, reported either from a pilot/vehicle observation or a routine night inspection.

Equipment / System

Airfield Guidance Signs — L-858 mandatory instruction and L-853/L-857 informational/direction signs, internally illuminated by an LED module or light guide plate (LGP) assembly, driven by an LED driver/ballast and fed via an isolating transformer on a series circuit or a dedicated constant-voltage branch circuit, in a weatherproof frangible-mounted aluminium housing.

Possible Causes
HighA failing LED driver with unstable output current, common as electrolytic capacitors age
HighA loose or intermittent connection at the internal harness or isolating transformer connector, varying contact resistance under vibration
MediumA partially failed isolating transformer with an intermittent internal short
MediumSource-side (CCR/branch circuit) voltage instability rather than a sign-level fault
LowMoisture inside the housing causing an intermittent leakage path across driver or module terminals
LowAn LED module nearing end of life with individual diodes intermittently dropping out
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeter with min/max holdOscilloscope or power quality loggerThermal imaging cameraSign connector test adaptor
Safety Precautions
  • Treat intermittent faults as live until proven otherwise — a fault that comes and goes can still be present during handling.
  • LOTO the circuit before opening the housing even if the sign appears steady at the time of access.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm whether the flicker is on a single sign or several, and whether it correlates with a particular intensity step, time of day, or weather condition.
  2. MeasurementLog CCR/branch circuit output over a period spanning the reported flicker to rule out a source-side instability.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, inspect and re-torque all internal connectors, and megger the isolating transformer under light mechanical flexing to try to reproduce an intermittent fault.
  4. MeasurementUse thermal imaging on connectors and the driver during a live period to spot a hot connection consistent with intermittent high resistance.
  5. DecisionFlicker follows source-side current logging → route to Division 01 (CCR) or branch circuit. Flicker isolated to the sign and reproducible by flexing a connector → connector, transformer, or driver fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the connector, transformer, driver, or LED module based on where the flicker could be reproduced or measured.
  7. Root CauseConfirm by replacing the suspect component and re-testing under conditions that previously produced the flicker.
Test Points
Internal connector contact resistance
Isolating transformer under mechanical flex
Driver output current stability
Source circuit voltage trend log
Root Cause Verification

Monitor the repaired sign over several operating cycles, including varying intensity steps and at least one period of the weather condition originally associated with the flicker, before closing out.

Corrective Action
  • Clean, re-grease, and properly seat all internal connectors.
  • Replace a driver or transformer confirmed to have an intermittent fault.
  • Replace a failing LED module.
Preventive Action
  • Torque-check internal connectors as a standard step in periodic maintenance.
  • Include thermal imaging of sign connectors and drivers in the periodic PM cycle.

SGN-314 Sign Too Dim

Symptom — A sign is illuminated and steady but its overall brightness is visibly below the level of adjacent signs or below the level expected for the commanded intensity step, without being fully dark or flickering.

Equipment / System

Airfield Guidance Signs — L-858 mandatory instruction and L-853/L-857 informational/direction signs, internally illuminated by an LED module or light guide plate (LGP) assembly, driven by an LED driver/ballast and fed via an isolating transformer on a series circuit or a dedicated constant-voltage branch circuit, in a weatherproof frangible-mounted aluminium housing.

Possible Causes
HighLED driver output current set or drifted below the correct level for the module, whether by a mis-set dip switch/potentiometer or age-related drift
HighGeneral LED degradation (lumen depreciation) across an ageing module approaching end of service life
MediumAn undervoltage condition at the sign feed — voltage drop over a long series circuit or branch run
MediumA dirty or fogged sign lens/panel face reducing effective light output
LowWrong-wattage or wrong-spec replacement driver fitted at a previous repair
LowLGP surface contamination scattering rather than transmitting light — cross-reference SGN-327
Required Tools
Calibrated luminance meterTrue-RMS multimeterClamp-on ammeterDriver configuration reference sheet
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before adjusting or replacing driver settings/components.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompare the sign's apparent brightness against an adjacent known-good sign of the same type at the same intensity step.
  2. MeasurementMeasure luminance at the sign face with a calibrated meter and compare to the manufacturer's specified output for the commanded step.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, check driver output current against nameplate and confirm any dip-switch/configuration setting matches the design intensity step.
  4. MeasurementMeasure supply voltage at the sign feed and compare to source voltage to check for excessive drop.
  5. DecisionDriver output below spec with correct configuration → driver drift, replace. Configuration incorrect → correct the setting. Voltage drop confirmed at feed → route to circuit-level investigation.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate whether the shortfall originates at the driver, the LED module, the lens, or the supply voltage.
  7. Root CauseConfirm whether the cause is component ageing, misconfiguration, or a persistent voltage-drop condition on the feeder.
Test Points
Sign face luminance vs. spec at each intensity step
Driver output current and configuration setting
Supply voltage at the sign feed
Root Cause Verification

After correction, re-measure luminance at the sign face across all intensity steps and confirm it meets the manufacturer's minimum specified output.

Corrective Action
  • Replace a drifted or misconfigured driver, or correct its configuration setting.
  • Clean the lens/panel face if contamination was contributing.
  • Replace an ageing LED module found to be below acceptable lumen output.
Preventive Action
  • Include luminance measurement, not just a visual check, in the periodic PM programme.
  • Trend luminance readings over time to plan proactive module replacement before signs fall below minimum.

SGN-315 Sign Too Bright

Symptom — A sign is illuminated well above the expected brightness for its commanded intensity step, appearing glaring or noticeably brighter than adjacent signs, which can be as operationally significant as a sign being too dim.

Equipment / System

Airfield Guidance Signs — L-858 mandatory instruction and L-853/L-857 informational/direction signs, internally illuminated by an LED module or light guide plate (LGP) assembly, driven by an LED driver/ballast and fed via an isolating transformer on a series circuit or a dedicated constant-voltage branch circuit, in a weatherproof frangible-mounted aluminium housing.

Possible Causes
HighLED driver output current set above the correct level for the module, whether by a mis-set configuration or an incorrect replacement driver
HighThe sign's intensity step command from the CCR/ALCMS is not being followed — driver stuck at a fixed high output
MediumWrong-spec driver or module fitted at a previous repair, rated for a higher output than the original design
MediumA control/dimming interface fault preventing the driver from stepping down with the rest of the circuit
LowA firmware or configuration fault in a digitally addressable driver reverting to a default high-output state
LowIncorrect commissioning setting never corrected since installation
Required Tools
Calibrated luminance meterTrue-RMS multimeterDriver configuration reference sheetManufacturer configuration/programming tool if applicable
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before adjusting or replacing driver settings/components.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompare the sign's apparent brightness against adjacent signs at the same commanded intensity step, and confirm the reported step from the CCR/ALCMS.
  2. MeasurementMeasure luminance at the sign face with a calibrated meter and compare to the manufacturer's specified output for the commanded step.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, check driver configuration/dip-switch settings and, for addressable drivers, confirm the received intensity command.
  4. DecisionDriver configured for a higher step than commanded → correct the configuration. Driver correctly configured but not responding to step-down commands → control/dimming interface fault. Wrong-spec driver/module fitted → replace with correct-spec part.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate whether the excess brightness originates at driver configuration, a control interface fault, or an incorrectly specified component.
  6. Root CauseConfirm whether the root cause is a commissioning error, a previous incorrect repair, or a genuine control fault requiring driver/interface replacement.
Test Points
Sign face luminance vs. spec at each intensity step
Driver configuration setting
Intensity command signal received at the sign
Root Cause Verification

After correction, re-measure luminance at the sign face across all intensity steps and confirm output tracks the commanded step correctly, including at the lowest step.

Corrective Action
  • Correct the driver configuration to match the design intensity step.
  • Replace a wrong-spec driver or module with the correct-rated part.
  • Repair or replace a faulted control/dimming interface.
Preventive Action
  • Verify driver configuration against design intent at every driver replacement, not just at commissioning.
  • Include a full intensity-step sweep check in periodic PM.

SGN-316 Uneven Sign Illumination

Symptom — The sign is illuminated across its full legend but with visibly uneven brightness — bright and dim patches, banding, or hot spots — rather than a uniform, evenly diffused output across the panel face.

Equipment / System

Airfield Guidance Signs — L-858 mandatory instruction and L-853/L-857 informational/direction signs, internally illuminated by an LED module or light guide plate (LGP) assembly, driven by an LED driver/ballast and fed via an isolating transformer on a series circuit or a dedicated constant-voltage branch circuit, in a weatherproof frangible-mounted aluminium housing.

Possible Causes
HighDegraded or partially delaminated LGP causing uneven light distribution across the panel, common as LGPs age or after thermal cycling stress
HighUneven LED spacing or a mix of old and newly replaced LEDs of differing output within the same module
MediumContamination or scratching on the LGP surface scattering light unevenly — cross-reference SGN-327
MediumA partially failed driver channel feeding only part of a multi-channel module at reduced output
LowManufacturing defect in the LGP diffusion pattern
LowIncorrect LED module orientation or spacing from a previous non-OEM repair
Required Tools
Calibrated luminance meter (multi-point)Digital camera for illumination pattern documentationInspection light / borescope for LGP surface check
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before opening the housing for internal inspection.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationPhotograph the illuminated sign face at night to document the specific pattern of unevenness (banding, hot spots, patchiness).
  2. MeasurementTake multi-point luminance readings across the panel face and compare the spread against the manufacturer's uniformity specification.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, inspect the LGP surface and edges for delamination, scratching, or contamination, and check LED module for mixed/mismatched components.
  4. DecisionUniformity failure traced to LGP condition → LGP fault, route to SGN-326. Traced to a partial driver channel → driver fault, route to SGN-318. Traced to mismatched LEDs → module replacement needed.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the uneven illumination to the LGP, the LED module, or a partial driver channel.
  6. Root CauseDetermine whether the cause is component ageing, contamination, or an inconsistent prior repair using non-matched parts.
Test Points
Multi-point sign face luminance uniformity
LGP surface and edge condition
LED module consistency across the panel
Root Cause Verification

After correction, re-measure multi-point luminance and confirm the uniformity ratio meets the manufacturer's specification, with no visible banding or hot spots to the eye.

Corrective Action
  • Replace a degraded or delaminated LGP.
  • Clean a contaminated LGP surface.
  • Replace a mismatched LED module with a full matched set rather than mixing old and new LEDs.
Preventive Action
  • Always replace LED modules as a complete matched set rather than individual LEDs.
  • Include LGP surface inspection in the periodic PM programme.

SGN-317 LED Module Failure

Symptom — The LED module inside a sign has failed — either entirely dark or with a significant portion of its diodes non-functional — confirmed as the isolated cause after ruling out the driver, transformer, and wiring.

Equipment / System

Airfield Guidance Signs — L-858 mandatory instruction and L-853/L-857 informational/direction signs, internally illuminated by an LED module or light guide plate (LGP) assembly, driven by an LED driver/ballast and fed via an isolating transformer on a series circuit or a dedicated constant-voltage branch circuit, in a weatherproof frangible-mounted aluminium housing.

Possible Causes
HighEnd-of-life LED degradation or catastrophic diode failure within the module, more likely in older installations or modules exposed to sustained thermal stress
HighThermal damage to the module from inadequate heat dissipation, often linked to a housing ventilation or thermal-pad defect
MediumOverdriven LEDs from an incorrectly configured or wrong-spec driver supplying excess current
MediumMoisture ingress reaching the module PCB, causing corrosion of solder joints or trace damage
LowManufacturing defect in the module batch
LowPhysical/impact damage to the module during a previous repair or from panel flexing
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeterLED module bench test rig / known-good driverThermal imaging cameraReplacement LED module of correct type and legend
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before removing or replacing the module.
  • Allow the module and driver to cool before handling if recently energised.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm via prior diagnostic steps (SGN-311/312/316) that the driver and feed test good, isolating the fault to the module itself.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, remove the module and bench-test it against a known-good driver to confirm the failure is in the module rather than elsewhere.
  3. MeasurementInspect the module PCB for signs of thermal damage, corrosion, or physical damage, and check housing ventilation/thermal pad condition.
  4. DecisionModule fails bench test → confirmed module failure, replace. Module tests intermittently good → treat as marginal and replace proactively rather than reinstalling.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the module as the fault, distinct from the driver (SGN-318) or internal wiring (SGN-320).
  6. Root CauseDetermine whether the failure is age-related, thermal, moisture-related, or driver-overdrive related, to guide preventive action.
Test Points
Module bench-test result against known-good driver
Module PCB visual/thermal condition
Housing ventilation and thermal pad condition
Root Cause Verification

After replacement with a correctly matched module, re-energise and confirm even illumination across the full legend at all intensity steps, and re-check for excess housing temperature after a settling period.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed LED module with a correctly specified, matched replacement.
  • Correct a driver overdrive condition if identified as a contributing cause.
  • Restore housing ventilation/thermal pad if thermal damage was implicated.
Preventive Action
  • Include thermal imaging of sign housings in the periodic PM cycle to catch developing thermal issues before module failure.
  • Maintain matched-set module spares for each sign type and legend in service.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SGN-02 (Sign Module Replacement Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5340-18, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

Case Study: Division 12 — SGN-340 (Sign Repeated LED Failure)

SGN-318 LED Driver Failure

Symptom — The sign's LED driver/ballast has failed, confirmed as producing no output, unstable output, or incorrect output current after the module and feed have tested good in isolation.

Equipment / System

Airfield Guidance Signs — L-858 mandatory instruction and L-853/L-857 informational/direction signs, internally illuminated by an LED module or light guide plate (LGP) assembly, driven by an LED driver/ballast and fed via an isolating transformer on a series circuit or a dedicated constant-voltage branch circuit, in a weatherproof frangible-mounted aluminium housing.

Possible Causes
HighElectrolytic capacitor degradation within the driver, the most common age-related driver failure mode
HighDriver failure from a voltage surge or transient on the supply, particularly following a nearby lightning event
MediumThermal stress from prolonged operation in an under-ventilated housing
MediumOutput short from a downstream module fault having damaged the driver before the module fault was identified
LowManufacturing defect or a driver that was already marginal at installation
LowWater ingress into the driver compartment causing internal short or corrosion
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeterClamp-on ammeterBench power supply for module isolation testingReplacement driver of correct type/rating
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before removing or replacing the driver.
  • Allow the driver to cool before handling; capacitors can retain a charge briefly after de-energisation — verify discharge before handling terminals.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm via prior diagnostic steps that the feed voltage is present and the module tests good on a bench supply, isolating the fault to the driver.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, check driver input voltage and output current against nameplate rating.
  3. MeasurementInspect the driver casing for bulging capacitors, discolouration, or evidence of internal arcing/overheating.
  4. DecisionDriver shows no or incorrect output with good input voltage → confirmed driver failure, replace. Driver output correct but module still dark → route back to module-level fault (SGN-317).
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the driver as the fault, distinct from the feed/transformer (SGN-319) or the module (SGN-317).
  6. Root CauseBench-test the removed driver if practical to confirm the specific failure mode, and check whether a downstream module short contributed to the failure.
Test Points
Driver input voltage
Driver output current/voltage vs. nameplate
Driver casing condition (bulging, discolouration)
Root Cause Verification

After replacement with a correctly rated driver, re-energise and confirm the sign operates correctly across all intensity steps, and re-check output stability after a settling period.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed driver with a correctly rated unit.
  • Address any water ingress found in the driver compartment to prevent recurrence.
  • Investigate and correct any downstream module short that may have contributed to driver failure.
Preventive Action
  • Include driver output testing in the periodic PM programme, trending results to catch degrading units before failure.
  • Verify driver rating against the design specification at every replacement.
  • Maintain correctly rated driver spares matching the sign types in service.
Related References

Case Study: Division 12 — SGN-319 (Power Supply Failure)

Rev 0 — Initial issue
SGN-319 Power Supply Failure

Symptom — The sign's upstream power supply — the isolating transformer on a series circuit or the dedicated branch circuit feed — has failed, cutting off power before it reaches the driver, confirmed distinct from a driver-level fault.

Equipment / System

Airfield Guidance Signs — L-858 mandatory instruction and L-853/L-857 informational/direction signs, internally illuminated by an LED module or light guide plate (LGP) assembly, driven by an LED driver/ballast and fed via an isolating transformer on a series circuit or a dedicated constant-voltage branch circuit, in a weatherproof frangible-mounted aluminium housing.

Possible Causes
HighIsolating transformer failure (open primary or secondary winding) on a series-circuit-fed sign, from age-related insulation breakdown or water ingress
HighTripped branch circuit breaker or blown fuse on a branch-circuit-fed sign
MediumInternal short in the transformer or branch feed developing from moisture tracking
MediumPhysical/impact damage to the transformer body or feed cable during nearby excavation or base-can work
LowManufacturing defect or a transformer/feed component already marginal at installation
LowCascading damage from a nearby lightning strike or major surge event
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance testerTrue-RMS multimeterClamp-on ammeterReplacement transformer of correct wattage/tap ratingBase-can lifting tool
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before disconnecting or removing any transformer or feed component.
  • Handle a transformer suspected of internal arcing carefully — do not assume it is fully de-energised even after the circuit is isolated, until tested.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm via CCR/ALCMS or branch panel status whether the fault pattern is consistent with a supply-level fault to this sign specifically or a wider circuit issue.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, disconnect the transformer/feed at both ends and megger primary-to-earth, secondary-to-earth, and primary-to-secondary (series-fed), or check branch circuit continuity and breaker status.
  3. MeasurementConfirm the transformer's wattage/tap rating matches the design specification for the fitted driver.
  4. DecisionTransformer/feed fails insulation or continuity test, or breaker repeatedly trips → replace/repair the supply component. Supply tests good but wattage/tap mismatched → replace with correctly rated unit even though not fully failed.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the transformer or branch feed as the fault, distinct from the driver (SGN-318) or internal wiring (SGN-320).
  6. Root CauseBench-test the removed transformer if practical to confirm the specific failure mode for the maintenance record.
Test Points
Transformer primary/secondary winding insulation resistance
Primary-to-secondary insulation resistance
Branch circuit continuity and breaker status
Transformer wattage/tap vs. design specification
Root Cause Verification

After replacement with the correctly rated transformer or feed repair, re-energise and confirm the sign operates correctly across all intensity steps, and re-check insulation resistance at the next scheduled maintenance visit.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed transformer with a correctly rated unit, or repair the branch feed/breaker fault.
  • Correct a wattage/tap mismatch even where the existing transformer has not yet fully failed.
  • Address any water ingress found in the base can to prevent recurrence.
Preventive Action
  • Include transformer insulation resistance testing in the periodic PM programme.
  • Verify wattage/tap rating against the design specification at every transformer replacement.
  • Maintain correctly rated transformer spares on hand for the range of sign types in service.
Related References

SGN-320 Sign Internal Wiring Failure

Symptom — A fault has been isolated to the internal wiring harness within the sign housing — between the feed terminal, driver, and module(s) — rather than to any single component, confirmed after the transformer/feed, driver, and module have each tested good individually.

Equipment / System

HighFault in the sign's remote control receiver/transceiver module, not receiving or correctly decoding commands
HighLoss of communication between the ALCMS/control system and the sign's remote control module — cross-reference SGN-339
MediumIncorrect addressing/configuration of the sign's remote control module, causing it to ignore commands meant for it or respond to commands meant for another sign

LowFirmware fault or a module requiring a reset/reboot to restore normal operation

Required Tools
Manufacturer configuration/programming toolTrue-RMS multimeterCommunication signal tester/loggerReplacement remote control module of correct type
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before opening the housing to inspect or replace the remote control module.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm via the ALCMS/control system log whether commands are being sent to this specific sign and whether any acknowledgement is received back.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, check the remote control module's power supply and communication link status.
  3. MeasurementVerify the module's address/configuration matches its intended identity in the control system.
  4. DecisionModule confirmed unpowered or physically faulted → replace. Module powered and addressed correctly but not communicating → route to communication link fault, SGN-339. Address/configuration mismatch found → correct the configuration.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the remote control module hardware, its configuration, or the communication link itself.
  6. Root CauseDetermine whether the cause is a module hardware fault, a configuration/addressing error, or an environmental factor (moisture, power fluctuation) affecting the module specifically.
Test Points
Remote control module power supply status
Module address/configuration vs. intended identity
Command acknowledgement at the ALCMS/control system
Root Cause Verification

After correction or replacement, send a test command sequence from the ALCMS/control system and confirm the sign responds correctly and acknowledges each command.

Corrective Action
  • Replace a faulted remote control module.
  • Correct module address/configuration mismatches.
  • Reset or reboot the module where a firmware fault is suspected.
Preventive Action
  • Include remote command response verification in the periodic PM programme for all remotely controllable signs.
  • Maintain an accurate address/configuration register for all addressable signs.
  • Protect remote control module compartments against moisture ingress as a specific inspection point.

Case Study: Division 12 — SGN-339 (Sign Communication Failure)

Rev 0 — Initial issue
SGN-339 Sign Communication Failure

Symptom — The data communication link between the ALCMS/control system and a sign (or group of signs) on a shared communication bus/network has failed, resulting in loss of status reporting, remote control, or both, even though the sign's illumination circuit continues to operate on its last-known state.

Equipment / System

Airfield Guidance Signs — L-858 mandatory instruction and L-853/L-857 informational/direction signs, internally illuminated by an LED module or light guide plate (LGP) assembly, driven by an LED driver/ballast and fed via an isolating transformer on a series circuit or a dedicated constant-voltage branch circuit, in a weatherproof frangible-mounted aluminium housing.

Possible Causes
HighPhysical break or damage to the communication cable/bus between the ALCMS and the affected sign(s)
HighFailed communication interface/gateway module at the sign or at a shared junction point serving multiple signs
MediumAddress conflict or configuration error on a shared communication bus, disrupting traffic to multiple devices
MediumElectrical noise/interference on the communication line from nearby power cabling or equipment
LowSoftware/firmware fault at the ALCMS server or gateway affecting communication with this sign or group
LowWater ingress into a communication cable joint or termination point
Required Tools
Communication network tester/loggerTrue-RMS multimeter/continuity tester for the comms cableManufacturer network diagnostic toolCommunication cable route drawing
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the illumination circuit before working inside the sign housing, even though the communication fault itself may not be electrically hazardous.
  • Coordinate with the control system administrator before taking any gateway or bus segment offline for testing, since it may affect other signs sharing the bus.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm via the ALCMS whether the loss of communication affects a single sign or a group sharing the same bus segment/gateway, which strongly indicates where the fault lies.
  2. MeasurementTest the communication cable for continuity/damage along the affected run, and check the interface/gateway module's status and configuration.
  3. MeasurementIf interference is suspected, check the communication cable's routing and separation from power cabling against the design specification.
  4. DecisionSingle sign affected → isolate to that sign's local communication interface module. Group of signs affected → isolate to the shared bus segment, gateway, or a cable fault upstream of the group.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the communication cable, an interface/gateway module, an addressing conflict, or interference.
  6. Root CauseDetermine whether the cause is physical cable damage, hardware failure, configuration, or an environmental/interference factor.
Test Points
Communication cable continuity along the affected run
Interface/gateway module status
Communication cable separation from power cabling
Bus addressing configuration for affected devices
Root Cause Verification

After repair, confirm the ALCMS re-establishes communication and correct status reporting with the affected sign(s), and verify remote command/response works correctly.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the damaged communication cable.
  • Replace a faulted communication interface/gateway module.
  • Correct an addressing conflict on the shared bus.
  • Improve cable separation/shielding where interference was implicated.
Preventive Action
  • Include communication link status checks in the periodic PM programme, not only in response to a reported loss of control.
  • Maintain an accurate communication network topology drawing.
  • Route communication cabling with adequate separation from power cabling per the design specification.

Case Study: Division 12 — SGN-338 (Sign Remote Control Failure)

Rev 0 — Initial issue
SGN-340 Sign Repeated LED Failure

Symptom — A specific sign (or sign location) has experienced multiple LED module failures over a relatively short period, well beyond the expected failure rate, indicating a systemic underlying cause at that location rather than a series of unrelated, coincidental component failures.

Equipment / System

Airfield Guidance Signs — L-858 mandatory instruction and L-853/L-857 informational/direction signs, internally illuminated by an LED module or light guide plate (LGP) assembly, driven by an LED driver/ballast and fed via an isolating transformer on a series circuit or a dedicated constant-voltage branch circuit, in a weatherproof frangible-mounted aluminium housing.

Possible Causes
HighA persistent driver overdrive or misconfiguration condition repeatedly damaging replacement modules, if the driver itself was never corrected after the first module failure
HighChronic thermal issue at this specific housing — inadequate ventilation, a failed thermal pad, or an environmental heat source — shortening every module's service life at this location
MediumRecurring water/moisture ingress at this specific sign (cross-reference SGN-321/322) causing repeated corrosion-related module damage
MediumVoltage instability or surge exposure specific to this circuit position (e.g., end-of-line or near a known lightning-exposure point)
LowA pattern of non-OEM or mismatched replacement modules being fitted, each with a shorter service life than the original specification
LowVibration source specific to this location (e.g., proximity to a taxiway/runway with heavy traffic) causing repeated mechanical stress on the module
Required Tools
Maintenance history record for the sign locationThermal imaging cameraTrue-RMS multimeter/clamp-on ammeterDriver configuration reference sheetPower quality logger
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before any diagnostic work inside the housing.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationPull the full maintenance history for this sign location to confirm the failure pattern (frequency, which component keeps failing, what was done at each repair).
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, verify driver output current/configuration against the module's rated specification, and confirm the same check was actually done at each prior replacement.
  3. MeasurementUse thermal imaging during a live period to check for elevated housing/module temperature, and check whether water ingress has recurred (cross-reference SGN-321/322).
  4. MeasurementLog source-side voltage/current at this circuit position over a period to check for surge or instability specific to this location.
  5. DecisionDriver overdrive/misconfiguration found and never previously corrected → correct driver configuration, this is likely the root cause of the repeated failures. Thermal issue confirmed → address ventilation/thermal pad. Recurring moisture confirmed → prioritise the housing seal repair (SGN-321/322/324) over further module replacement alone.
  6. Fault LocationIdentify the specific systemic factor (driver, thermal, moisture, voltage, non-OEM parts, vibration) responsible for the repeated failures at this location, rather than treating this as another isolated module failure.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the systemic root cause and correct it before or alongside the current module replacement, to break the failure pattern.
Test Points
Driver output current/configuration vs. module rating
Housing/module operating temperature
Water ingress history at this location
Source-side voltage/current stability at this circuit position
Root Cause Verification

After correcting the identified systemic cause and replacing the module, monitor this sign location more closely than standard PM frequency for at least two to three maintenance cycles to confirm the failure pattern has been broken.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed module with a correctly matched, OEM-specification part.
  • Correct the identified systemic cause (driver configuration, thermal, moisture, or voltage issue) rather than replacing the module alone.
  • Flag the location for enhanced monitoring following the correction.
Preventive Action
  • Review maintenance history for repeated component failures at the same location as a standard step whenever any module is replaced, not only after a pattern becomes obvious.
  • Ensure driver configuration is verified against module specification at every replacement, not assumed correct.
  • Maintain a location-based failure log across the sign inventory to catch systemic issues early.
Related References
