ALS-271 Approach Light System Not Working

Symptom — The entire Approach Lighting System (ALS) fails to illuminate when commanded, reported via pilot report, ALCMS status, or routine night inspection — distinct from a partial or single-fixture outage.

Equipment / System

Approach Lighting System (ALSF-2/MALSR/simplified configuration as installed) — elevated crossbar and centreline barrette fixtures on frangible masts/poles beyond the runway threshold, sequenced flasher units, dedicated ALS feeder circuit(s) and control cabinet, fed from the CCR/ALS regulator and ALCMS.

Possible Causes
HighMain ALS feeder breaker tripped or the ALS regulator/CCR itself at zero output
HighALS control cabinet or master controller fault preventing the energise command from reaching the field circuit
MediumUpstream power supply failure (mains and standby/generator) affecting the ALS distribution board
MediumA main feeder cable fault between the control building and the ALS structure, particularly on systems with long feeder runs over unpaved or wetland approach terrain
LowALCMS command not being issued correctly to the ALS circuit, distinct from a true field-side power failure — verify command versus status feedback
Required Tools
Clamp-on ammeterTrue-RMS multimeter (CAT III)1000V/2500V insulation resistance testerALS as-built circuit and control schematicPortable generator or backup supply test point, if applicable
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate all approach-area access through ATC/airfield operations — ALS structures are frequently in the approach surface and may require additional clearance beyond a standard runway/taxiway closure.
  • Treat all ALS circuits as energised until confirmed isolated and locked out at the regulator/CCR.
  • Where ALS structures are over water, wetland, or difficult terrain, follow the site's specific access and fall-protection procedures before approaching any fixture.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCheck the ALCMS/CCR panel to confirm whether the ALS regulator is commanded on and whether it is showing an output fault, a feedback fault, or genuinely zero output.
  2. MeasurementConfirm incoming supply to the ALS regulator/CCR and the main feeder breaker status before assuming a field-side fault.
  3. ObservationIf the regulator/CCR is confirmed healthy and outputting current, inspect the main ALS feeder cable route and control cabinet for a fault preventing power reaching the field.
  4. DecisionRegulator/CCR itself faulted or not commanded → route to Division 01 (CCR Troubleshooting). Regulator/CCR healthy and outputting but field remains dark → main feeder cable or field distribution fault, continue below.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the regulator/CCR, the main feeder cable/control cabinet, or a full-system field distribution fault.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the specific point of failure before restoring the system, given the safety-critical nature of a complete ALS outage during low-visibility approach operations.
Test Points
ALS regulator/CCR output current and status
Main feeder breaker and supply status
Main feeder cable insulation resistance
ALCMS command vs. feedback status
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-energise the system and confirm every crossbar, centreline barrette, and sequenced flasher operates correctly at all commanded intensity steps during a full night inspection.

Corrective Action
  • Reset or repair the tripped breaker or faulted regulator/CCR.
  • Repair the identified main feeder cable fault.
  • Restore the ALCMS command path if the fault was a control/communication issue rather than a power fault.
  • Escalate immediately to airfield operations given the operational significance of a complete ALS outage.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a tested backup/standby power path for the ALS given its criticality to low-visibility approach operations.
  • Include full-system ALS energisation as part of the routine night flight-check.
  • Periodically megger the main feeder cable to catch a developing insulation fault before it causes a complete outage.

ALS-272 Partial Approach Light Failure

Symptom — A defined section of the ALS — for example one or more crossbars, a segment of the centreline barrette run, or the sequenced flasher string — is dark or malfunctioning while the rest of the system operates normally.

Equipment / System

The affected ALS sub-circuit or structural section (crossbar group, centreline segment, or flasher branch) and its dedicated feeder from the control cabinet.

Possible Causes
HighA sub-circuit breaker or fuse specific to the affected section tripped or blown
HighA cable fault on the feeder branch serving only the affected section
MediumA junction box or distribution point serving that section showing a connection fault
MediumIsolation transformer failure at a specific structure if the affected section is fed as an individual series-circuit loop
LowA control cabinet relay or contactor specific to that section's control channel failed
Required Tools
Clamp-on ammeterTrue-RMS multimeter1000V/2500V insulation resistance testerALS sub-circuit schematic showing section breakdownSeries circuit fault locator / TDR if the affected section is series-wired
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the affected sub-circuit at the control cabinet before working on it, even though the rest of the system remains live.
  • Clearly identify and isolate only the affected branch to avoid disrupting the operating sections of the ALS.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the exact extent of the affected section from a night inspection and cross-reference against the ALS structural/circuit layout drawing to identify which sub-circuit or branch is involved.
  2. ObservationCheck the control cabinet for a tripped breaker, blown fuse, or fault indication specific to that section's control channel.
  3. MeasurementWith the sub-circuit isolated, megger the feeder cable to that section and inspect the relevant junction box/distribution point.
  4. DecisionBreaker/fuse trips again on reset → downstream fault (cable, junction box, or fixture), continue field investigation. Breaker/fuse holds after reset → likely a transient event or nuisance trip; monitor closely.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific sub-circuit protective device, feeder cable segment, junction box, or fixture group affected.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the specific component before returning the section to service, given the importance of complete approach light coverage for low-visibility operations.
Test Points
Sub-circuit breaker/fuse status
Feeder cable insulation resistance to the affected section
Junction box/distribution point condition
Control cabinet relay/contactor status for that channel
Root Cause Verification

Re-energise the section and confirm it matches the operating pattern (steady, sequenced, or intensity-stepped as applicable) of the rest of the system before closing out.

Corrective Action
  • Reset or replace the tripped/blown protective device.
  • Repair the identified feeder cable fault.
  • Repair or replace the faulted junction box connection.
  • Replace a failed control cabinet relay/contactor for that channel.
Preventive Action
  • Include sub-circuit breaker and protective device checks in the periodic maintenance programme.
  • Maintain an accurate, current sub-circuit breakdown drawing to speed section-level fault isolation.
  • Trend nuisance trips on any given sub-circuit as an early indicator of a developing fault.
Related References

ALS-273 Multiple Approach Lights Failure

Symptom — Two or more individual ALS fixtures — crossbar lights, centreline barrette elements, or flasher heads — are dark or malfunctioning together within an otherwise energised section, distinct from a full sub-circuit outage.

Equipment / System

The group of individual ALS fixtures affected and the series/parallel wiring segment feeding them within their sub-circuit.

Possible Causes
HighA shared series cable fault or bad connection upstream of the affected fixture group
HighA shared isolation transformer or distribution point failure feeding multiple fixtures
MediumWater ingress into a shared junction box or splice affecting several fixtures downstream
MediumStructural/mechanical damage to a mast or crossbar affecting the wiring to multiple fixtures on that structure
LowCoincidental multiple independent fixture failures — verify this is genuinely a shared-cause fault before assuming a common circuit issue
Required Tools
Series circuit fault locator / TDR1000V/2500V insulation resistance testerClamp-on ammeterALS circuit layout and wiring drawingExcavation permit and utility locate request if cable work requires digging
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the affected sub-circuit before any cable or connection work.
  • Where the affected fixtures are on an elevated mast/crossbar structure, follow fall-protection and elevated-work procedures for the site.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the exact fixtures affected and their physical/electrical relationship — same crossbar, same mast, same series loop segment — from the ALS layout drawing.
  2. MeasurementWith the sub-circuit isolated, megger the cable segment feeding the affected fixture group, working from the last known-good point toward the affected group.
  3. ObservationInspect the shared junction box, splice, or distribution point serving the affected fixtures for water ingress or connection failure.
  4. DecisionInsulation resistance fails on the shared segment → cable/joint fault, proceed to physical fault location. Segment tests clear but fixtures remain out → investigate for a shared structural/mechanical cause (e.g., mast damage) instead.
  5. Fault LocationPhysically locate the faulted cable segment, joint, or structural damage point affecting the group.
  6. Root CauseConfirm cause once exposed — cable/joint failure, water ingress, or structural damage — before repair.
Test Points
Shared cable segment insulation resistance
Junction box/splice condition
Structural condition of the mast/crossbar serving the group
CCR/regulator output current before and during isolation
Root Cause Verification

After repair, megger the full affected segment, re-energise, and confirm every fixture in the previously affected group operates correctly.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the faulted cable segment or splice.
  • Repair the shared junction box/distribution point.
  • Address structural damage to the mast/crossbar if identified as the root cause, coordinating with structural inspection as needed.
Preventive Action
  • Include periodic insulation resistance testing of ALS feeder and series segments in the PM programme.
  • Inspect junction boxes and splices on elevated structures for weatherproofing integrity as part of routine maintenance.
  • Coordinate structural inspection of ALS masts/crossbars on a defined interval given their exposure to wind and weather.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ALS-02 (Sub-Circuit Isolation Procedure), SOP-ALS-03 (Series Circuit Fault Location Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5345-46

ALS-274 Approach Light Flickering

Symptom — One or more ALS fixtures flicker, pulse, or vary in brightness instead of holding steady output (for steady-burning elements) or a consistent flash characteristic (for sequenced flashers) at the commanded intensity step.

Equipment / System

ALS fixture, its isolation transformer or flasher driver, connector, and the circuit supplying it.

Possible Causes
HighLoose or intermittent connection at the fixture connector, worsened by wind-induced vibration on elevated mast/crossbar structures
HighA partially failed or overheating isolation transformer or flasher driver with an intermittent internal fault
MediumLamp/LED driver instability or a failing flash tube/LED module nearing end of life
MediumIntermittent cable fault (chafed insulation making and breaking contact) rather than a solid open or short
LowCCR/regulator output instability itself rather than a fixture-level fault
LowMoisture inside a fixture or junction box causing an intermittent leakage path
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeter with min/max holdOscilloscope or power quality logger for waveform captureThermal imaging cameraConnector test adaptor as applicable to fixture type
Safety Precautions
  • Treat intermittent faults as live until proven otherwise.
  • Follow elevated-work and fall-protection procedures when inspecting fixtures on masts/crossbars.
  • LOTO the circuit before opening any connection point even if the light appears steady at the time of access.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm whether the flicker/inconsistent flash is on a single fixture or several, and whether it correlates with wind conditions, a particular intensity step, or weather.
  2. MeasurementLog CCR/regulator output current over a period spanning the reported flicker to rule out a source-side instability.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, inspect and re-torque the fixture connector, and test the isolation transformer or flasher driver under light mechanical flexing to try to reproduce an intermittent fault.
  4. ObservationUse thermal imaging on the connector and transformer/driver during a live period to spot a hot connection consistent with intermittent high resistance.
  5. DecisionFlicker follows CCR/regulator current logging → source-side issue, route to Division 01. Flicker isolated to the fixture and reproducible by flexing the connector → connector, transformer, or driver fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the connector, transformer/driver, lamp/LED/flash-tube module, or cable.
  7. Root CauseConfirm by replacing the suspect component and re-testing under conditions that previously produced the flicker, including a windy period if wind-induced vibration was implicated.
Test Points
Connector contact resistance
Isolation transformer/flasher driver under mechanical flex
CCR/regulator output current trend log
Lamp/LED/flash-tube module bench test
Root Cause Verification

Monitor the repaired fixture over several operating cycles, including at least one period of the wind or weather condition previously associated with the flicker, before closing out.

Corrective Action
  • Clean, re-grease, and properly seat the connector.
  • Replace a transformer/driver confirmed to have an intermittent internal fault.
  • Replace a failing lamp/LED/flash-tube module.
  • Repair the identified intermittent cable fault.
Preventive Action
  • Torque-check connectors as a standard step in periodic maintenance, given the wind-vibration exposure of elevated ALS structures.
  • Include thermal imaging of connectors and drivers in the periodic PM cycle.
  • Trend CCR/regulator output logs to distinguish source-side from fixture-side intermittent issues early.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ALS-01 (ALS Fault Isolation Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-46 (ALSF-2/MALSR), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

ALS-275 Approach Light Dim

Symptom — An ALS fixture illuminates but appears noticeably dimmer than adjacent fixtures on the same crossbar or centreline run at the same commanded intensity step.

Equipment / System

ALS fixture optical/lamp assembly, isolation transformer or driver output, and lens condition.

Possible Causes
HighLens contaminated with dirt, salt spray, or weathering film, particularly on approach structures exposed to open terrain or coastal conditions
HighIsolation transformer/driver secondary output below rated value due to partial internal fault
MediumLamp/LED module degraded or nearing end of life without having fully failed
MediumIncorrect or mismatched transformer/driver rating installed at a previous replacement
LowSeries circuit current below the correct value for the commanded step, affecting the whole loop rather than one fixture
LowLens misaligned so peak beam intensity is directed away from the design approach sightline
Required Tools
Light output/luminance meterTrue-RMS multimeterClamp-on ammeterLens cleaning kitIsolation transformer/driver test set
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before removing or cleaning a lens or accessing the fixture interior.
  • Follow elevated-work procedures on masts/crossbars, and allow the fixture to cool before handling if recently energised at a high intensity step.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompare the reported fixture's apparent brightness against neighbouring fixtures on the same crossbar/run at the same commanded step, ideally with a luminance meter.
  2. MeasurementConfirm the circuit current at the CCR/regulator matches the correct value for the commanded intensity step, to rule out a circuit-wide cause.
  3. ObservationInspect the lens for contamination, weathering, or degradation, and clean if required.
  4. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, check the isolation transformer/driver secondary output and confirm its rated wattage/tap matches the design.
  5. DecisionCircuit current correct and lens clean but output still low → transformer/driver or lamp/LED degradation. Lens found heavily contaminated → cleaning likely resolves it; re-check before replacing parts.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to lens contamination, transformer/driver output, or lamp/LED degradation.
  7. Root CauseConfirm with a before/after luminance reading once the identified cause is addressed.
Test Points
Fixture luminance output vs. neighbouring fixtures
Circuit current at CCR/regulator
Isolation transformer/driver secondary voltage/wattage
Lens transmission condition
Root Cause Verification

Re-measure luminance output after corrective action and compare against neighbouring fixtures and the manufacturer's rated output for the commanded step.

Corrective Action
  • Clean the lens assembly.
  • Replace an isolation transformer/driver with an incorrect or degraded rating.
  • Replace a degraded lamp/LED module.
  • Correct any lens misalignment found during inspection.
Preventive Action
  • Include lens cleaning in the routine ALS maintenance cycle, given exposure to open-terrain weathering beyond the runway threshold.
  • Verify transformer/driver rating matches the specified lamp/LED module at every replacement.
  • Track luminance readings over time to catch gradual degradation before it becomes a reported fault.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ALS-04 (Lens Cleaning and Inspection Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I (light intensity requirements), FAA AC 150/5340-30

ALS-276 Approach Light Wrong Color

Symptom — An ALS fixture shows a colour other than specified for its position — for example, a white steady-burning light showing amber/red-tinted output, or a fixture in the last 300 m section not showing the correct red side-row colour where applicable.

Equipment / System

ALS fixture lens/filter and lamp/LED module colour characteristics relative to its position in the approach lighting configuration (e.g., red terminating/side row barrettes in ALSF-2 systems).

Possible Causes
HighLens discolouration/fading from UV degradation or weathering over the fixture's service life
HighWrong lamp/LED module or colour filter fitted — a mismatch installed by mistake at a previous replacement
MediumContamination film producing an apparent colour shift
MediumFixture installed in the wrong position relative to the ALS colour scheme (e.g., a red side-row/terminating-bar fixture installed in a white steady-burning position)
LowLens cracked or partially melted, altering colour transmission
Required Tools
Colour temperature meter (if available)Torch/inspection lightReplacement lens/filter and correct-specification lamp/LED moduleALS colour-scheme layout drawing
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before removing the lens or accessing the fixture internals.
  • Follow elevated-work procedures on masts/crossbars during inspection.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the colour discrepancy visually against neighbouring fixtures and against the ALS colour-scheme layout drawing for that fixture's specific position.
  2. ObservationInspect the lens/filter for discolouration, contamination, or damage, and clean before further diagnosis if deposits are present.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, confirm the lamp/LED module and filter fitted match the specified part number and colour for this position in the ALS configuration.
  4. DecisionLens discoloured/contaminated but correct module fitted → clean or replace lens/filter. Lens clear but wrong module/filter or fixture position → replace with correct-specification part, or relocate if a position error is confirmed.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to lens/filter condition, lamp/LED module mismatch, or fixture-position error.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the corrected fixture matches its designed colour for that position during a night inspection.
Test Points
Lens/filter condition and clarity
Lamp/LED module part number and colour specification
Fixture position vs. ALS colour-scheme drawing
Comparative colour appearance vs. neighbouring fixtures
Root Cause Verification

Visually confirm the correct colour is displayed at the correct position during a night inspection, alongside the fixtures immediately either side.

Corrective Action
  • Clean or replace the discoloured/contaminated lens/filter.
  • Replace a mismatched lamp/LED module with the correct specification.
  • Relocate the fixture if it is confirmed to be in the wrong position for the colour scheme.
Preventive Action
  • Include lens/filter colour checks in the periodic maintenance schedule.
  • Verify lamp/LED module and filter part numbers against specification before stocking or fitting.
  • Maintain the ALS colour-scheme layout drawing current, particularly if the approach configuration has been modified.
Related References

ALS-277 Approach Light Intensity Step Failure

Symptom — The ALS fails to change output correctly when a different intensity step is commanded — either remaining at a fixed intensity, failing to reach the commanded step, or responding incorrectly compared to neighbouring circuits.

Equipment / System

ALS regulator/CCR step control output and the fixture/transformer response across the system's intensity range.

Possible Causes
HighRegulator/CCR step-control fault — a specific tap or output stage not functioning correctly
HighALCMS command not correctly reaching the regulator for a specific step
MediumA fixture or transformer group mismatched to a specific step's current level, causing correct steps elsewhere but incorrect response at this fixture
MediumA control relay or contactor within the regulator dedicated to a specific step failed
LowFeedback/monitoring fault reporting an incorrect step status while actual field output is correct — verify with an independent field measurement before assuming a genuine fault
Required Tools
Clamp-on ammeterTrue-RMS multimeterALCMS command/feedback log accessRegulator/CCR step-control schematic
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the regulator/CCR before any internal inspection.
  • Coordinate step-change testing with ATC, as commanding intensity changes during testing affects the operational lighting state.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCommand each intensity step in turn and record actual field current/output at the regulator, comparing against the expected value for each step.
  2. MeasurementCross-check the ALCMS commanded step against the regulator's actual output using an independent ammeter reading, to distinguish a genuine field fault from a feedback/reporting fault.
  3. ObservationIf a specific step is confirmed faulty at the regulator, inspect the corresponding internal stage, relay, or contactor for that step.
  4. DecisionField output confirmed incorrect for a specific step → regulator internal fault, escalate to Division 01. Field output confirmed correct but ALCMS reports it incorrectly → feedback/monitoring fault, route to ALS-288.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific regulator stage/step, the ALCMS command path, or the feedback/monitoring path.
  6. Root CauseConfirm correct operation across every intensity step, not just the one originally reported, before closing out.
Test Points
Field current output for each commanded intensity step
ALCMS commanded step vs. actual field output
Regulator internal stage/relay condition for the affected step
Root Cause Verification

Cycle through every available intensity step and confirm correct field output and correct ALCMS feedback at each before returning the system to normal operation.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the faulted regulator stage, relay, or contactor.
  • Correct the ALCMS command path if the fault is confirmed to be a control/communication issue.
  • Escalate to CCR-level troubleshooting per Division 01 if the fault is within the regulator itself.
Preventive Action
  • Include a full intensity-step cycle test in the periodic maintenance and night flight-check programme, not just a single-step check.
  • Log and trend step-change response times and accuracy to catch a developing regulator fault early.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ALS-01 (ALS Fault Isolation Procedure), SOP-CCR-08 (Intensity Step Verification)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5345-46

ALS-278 Approach Light Circuit Failure

Symptom — A defined ALS circuit — a series loop, sub-circuit, or feeder branch — shows a confirmed electrical fault (open circuit, short circuit, or protection trip) at the circuit level, distinct from an individual fixture or cable-segment fault already isolated to a single point.

Equipment / System

The affected ALS circuit as a whole, including its protective device, feeder, and the fixture group it serves.

Possible Causes
HighProtective device (breaker/fuse) tripped or failed for the affected circuit
HighA short circuit somewhere on the circuit loading down or tripping protection
MediumAn open circuit at any point in the loop preventing current flow to the fixture group
MediumA regulator output stage feeding this specific circuit faulted
LowA control/command fault preventing the circuit from being energised even though the circuit itself is electrically sound
Required Tools
Clamp-on ammeterTrue-RMS multimeter1000V/2500V insulation resistance testerSeries circuit fault locator / TDRALS circuit schematic
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the affected circuit at the regulator/control cabinet before investigation.
  • Do not attempt to reset a tripped protective device more than once without investigating the cause — repeated reset attempts on a genuine fault risk further damage.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the protective device status and reported symptom (no output, tripped breaker, partial output) at the control cabinet.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the full circuit loop to check for a short circuit or insulation breakdown, and check for continuity to confirm there is no open circuit.
  3. ObservationIf insulation resistance and continuity are both acceptable, inspect the regulator output stage and control/command path for this circuit.
  4. DecisionMegger fails or continuity is broken → field-side cable/fixture fault, proceed to physical fault location per ALS-273/279. Circuit tests electrically sound → regulator or control-side fault, escalate to Division 01.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to a field-side circuit fault or a regulator/control-side fault.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause before re-energising, particularly if a protective device tripped, to avoid repeated nuisance trips or further damage.
Test Points
Circuit insulation resistance (full loop)
Circuit continuity
Protective device trip history/status
Regulator output stage for this circuit
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-energise the circuit and confirm the protective device holds under normal load, and that every fixture on the circuit operates correctly across all intensity steps.

Corrective Action
  • Reset the protective device once the underlying fault is repaired.
  • Repair the identified cable/fixture fault causing the short or open circuit.
  • Repair or replace the faulted regulator output stage if the issue is source-side.
Preventive Action
  • Include full-circuit megger and continuity testing in the periodic PM programme for each ALS circuit.
  • Investigate any protective device trip fully before reset, rather than assuming a nuisance trip.
  • Maintain accurate circuit schematics reflecting the current as-built configuration.
Related References

SOP: SOP-ALS-02 (Sub-Circuit Isolation Procedure), SOP-ALS-03 (Series Circuit Fault Location Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

ALS-279 Approach Light Cable Failure

Symptom — A confirmed insulation or conductor fault in ALS feeder or series cable, requiring cable-level repair or replacement, distinct from a connector or fixture fault.

Equipment / System

ALS feeder and series cable routed through ducts, pull boxes, and structures across the approach lighting area, which frequently spans unpaved, wetland, or difficult-access terrain beyond the runway threshold.

Possible Causes
HighInsulation breakdown from age, moisture tracking, or cumulative thermal cycling under load
HighMechanical damage from ground movement, vehicle access tracks, or wildlife/rodent activity in the often less-controlled terrain beyond the threshold
MediumA degraded or improperly made field splice/joint failing under load or moisture exposure
MediumFlood or standing-water exposure in low-lying or wetland approach areas degrading buried cable over time
LowManufacturing defect or an original installation with an undersized bend radius causing insulation stress over time
Required Tools
1000V/2500V/5000V insulation resistance tester as appropriate to cable ratingSeries circuit fault locator / TDRALS cable route and joint location drawingExcavation permit and utility locate request if digging is requiredCable splicing kit rated for the cable classSite access equipment appropriate to the terrain (e.g., all-terrain vehicle, boat, or walkway access for wetland sections)
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the regulator/CCR before any cable testing or repair work.
  • Obtain excavation clearance and utility locates before opening any pavement, verge, or ground in the approach area.
  • Assess terrain and access conditions before dispatching personnel to remote sections of the approach lighting area, particularly wetland or flood-prone sections.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm from circuit-level symptoms and prior fault-location work that the fault is confirmed to be in the cable itself rather than a fixture, connector, or transformer.
  2. MeasurementMegger the cable in accessible segments from pull boxes or structure bases, and use a TDR/fault locator to pinpoint distance to fault along the identified segment.
  3. ObservationOnce physically exposed, inspect the cable and any joints for the specific failure mode — insulation breakdown, mechanical damage, or a failed splice.
  4. DecisionDamage isolated to a short section → repair by splice. Cable degraded over an extended length, of an age/condition warranting it, or in a flood-prone section with repeated failure history → replace the full segment rather than repeated spot repairs.
  5. Fault LocationConfirm the precise fault location by physical exposure before cutting or splicing.
  6. Root CauseDetermine and record the failure mode (age, mechanical damage, water exposure, joint failure) to inform whether adjacent cable sections need proactive attention.
Test Points
Cable insulation resistance by segment
TDR distance-to-fault reading
Splice/joint condition at exposed points
Duct/route condition, including flood or ground-movement exposure
Root Cause Verification

After repair, megger the full repaired segment and the adjoining sections, re-energise, and confirm all fixtures on the loop operate correctly across all intensity steps.

Corrective Action
  • Repair the cable by a correctly rated splice where damage is localised.
  • Replace the full cable segment where damage or age warrants it.
  • Restore duct protection where mechanical damage was the cause.
  • Consider elevated or protected cable routing for chronically flood-prone approach sections.
Preventive Action
  • Include periodic insulation resistance testing and trending of ALS feeder and series cable in the PM programme, given the criticality and often difficult access of the approach lighting area.

ALS-289 Approach Light Earthing Failure

Symptom — An ALS structure, fixture, or circuit is found with an earthing/bonding fault — high resistance to earth, a broken earth conductor, or a missing bond — identified either during a scheduled earth resistance test or following an electrical fault or lightning event.

Equipment / System

ALS earthing and bonding system, including structure earth electrodes, bonding conductors between structural elements, and the connection to the overall airfield earthing grid.

Possible Causes
HighCorrosion of the earth electrode or bonding conductor connections, particularly in exposed or coastal-influenced approach areas
HighA broken or disconnected bonding conductor following structural work, a previous fault, or a lightning strike
MediumSoil condition changes (drying, seasonal moisture variation) increasing earth electrode resistance beyond the acceptable threshold
MediumAn earth electrode damaged or displaced during ground works near the structure
LowOriginal installation deficiency — inadequate electrode depth or configuration for the local soil conditions
Required Tools
Earth resistance tester (fall-of-potential or clamp-on method as appropriate)Continuity tester for bonding conductorsVisual inspection kit for above-ground connectionsReplacement earthing hardware and conductor as required
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before any earthing system work that involves disconnecting a bond, since removing a bond can create an unexpected touch-voltage hazard if the circuit is not fully isolated.
  • Treat any structure with a confirmed earthing fault as an elevated shock/lightning risk until repaired — restrict unnecessary contact with the structure in the interim.
  • Follow standard electrical safety and, where applicable, confined-space or elevated-access procedures for accessing earth test points.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the earthing fault from the scheduled test result or triggering event (electrical fault, lightning strike, ground works near the structure).
  2. MeasurementTest earth resistance at the structure and check continuity of each bonding conductor back to the main earthing grid connection point.
  3. ObservationInspect all above-ground earthing and bonding connections for corrosion, physical damage, or looseness.
  4. DecisionHigh resistance but conductors intact → likely a soil/electrode condition issue, may require additional electrodes or treatment. Broken or disconnected conductor found → straightforward conductor repair/replacement.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to a specific broken/corroded conductor, a degraded electrode, or a genuine soil-resistivity issue requiring system enhancement.
  6. Root CauseConfirm earth resistance is restored to within the required threshold and record the result for the maintenance history.
Test Points
Earth resistance at each structure electrode
Bonding conductor continuity between structural elements
Above-ground connection condition
Comparison against the site's required earth resistance threshold
Root Cause Verification

Re-test earth resistance and full bonding continuity after repair, and repeat the test at the next scheduled interval to confirm the result remains stable, particularly through a seasonal soil-moisture cycle.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace corroded or broken bonding conductors.
  • Add supplementary earth electrodes or treatment if soil resistivity is the root cause of a persistent high-resistance reading.
  • Re-secure and clean any loose or corroded above-ground connections.
Preventive Action
  • Include earth resistance testing of every ALS structure in the periodic maintenance programme, given the elevated lightning exposure of tall approach lighting structures.
  • Test earthing systems after any significant lightning event in the vicinity, even without an obvious fault symptom.
  • Maintain accurate earthing system as-built records for each structure.

ALS-290 Approach Light Water Ingress

Symptom — Water is found inside a fixture housing, junction box, or structure base enclosure during inspection or maintenance, with or without an associated electrical fault already having occurred.

Equipment / System

ALS fixture housing, junction box, structure base enclosure, and their gasket/seal systems, across an area that frequently includes low-lying, unpaved, or wetland-adjacent terrain beyond the runway threshold.

Possible Causes
HighEnclosure gasket degraded, cracked, or incorrectly seated, allowing rainfall or splash to enter
HighStructure base or junction box not properly sealed against the surrounding ground, allowing groundwater or surface runoff to collect — a heightened risk in low-lying or wetland approach areas
MediumCable gland or entry point seal damaged or not fully tightened
MediumEnclosure mounting bolts loose, breaking the seal between housing sections
LowEnclosure drainage (weep hole) blocked, causing water to pool and rise above the acceptable level inside
LowSeasonal flooding or high water table temporarily submerging a low-lying structure base beyond its designed ingress protection rating
Required Tools
Insulation resistance tester (1000V/2500V)Wet/dry vacuum for enclosure pump-outReplacement gaskets, seals, and cable glandsTorque wrench for enclosure fixing bolts
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before opening any enclosure with standing water.
  • Do not assume a wet fixture or enclosure is de-energised; test with a rated meter before hand contact.
  • Assess flood/access conditions before dispatching personnel to low-lying approach-area structures during or after significant rainfall.
  • Dispose of pumped-out water per site environmental procedure if contamination is suspected.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the extent of water ingress — fixture housing, junction box, or structure base — and note whether any electrical fault (dim, dark, flickering) is already present.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the fixture, transformer, and any affected cable to confirm whether insulation has already been compromised.
  3. ObservationInspect the relevant gasket, cable gland, and enclosure seal for the specific ingress path, and check whether the structure base is in a known flood-prone or low-lying section.
  4. DecisionInsulation resistance already low → treat as an active electrical fault, replace affected components before re-energising. Insulation resistance still acceptable → address the sealing issue and monitor, with particular attention if the location is flood-prone.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the specific ingress path — gasket, cable gland, enclosure seal, or drainage blockage — or confirm seasonal flooding as the cause.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the enclosure is fully dry and re-test insulation resistance before closing out.
Test Points
Insulation resistance, fixture/transformer/cable to earth
Enclosure gasket and cable gland condition
Enclosure drainage (weep hole) function
Structure base flood/ground-water exposure history
Root Cause Verification

Re-test insulation resistance after drying and resealing, and re-inspect the enclosure after the next significant rainfall or flood-risk period to confirm the ingress path has been fully resolved.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the degraded gasket or cable gland.
  • Clear or restore enclosure drainage.
  • Torque the enclosure fixing bolts to specification.
  • Pump out and dry the enclosure fully before closing up.
  • Consider raising or better-protecting enclosures at structures with a confirmed history of seasonal flooding.
Preventive Action
  • Include gasket, seal, and drainage condition in the periodic maintenance checklist, replacing proactively rather than only on failure.
  • Identify and track structures in low-lying or flood-prone sections of the approach area for closer monitoring, particularly before the wet season.
  • Review enclosure ingress-protection ratings against actual site exposure conditions for chronically affected locations.
