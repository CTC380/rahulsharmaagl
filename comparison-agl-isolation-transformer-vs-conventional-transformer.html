AGL Isolation Transformer VS Conventional Transformer

An AGL isolation transformer is specifically designed for airfield series lighting circuits, providing electrical isolation between the series circuit and an individual light fixture. A conventional transformer is normally used to change voltage levels in general electrical power systems. The key difference is application and circuit function, not simply the presence of two windings.

02 · AGL Isolation Transformer

Interfacing a Series Circuit With an Individual Fixture

A simplified AGL arrangement connects the Constant Current Regulator (CCR) through the series circuit into an isolation transformer, which then feeds the individual light fixture. In a full circuit, multiple isolation transformers are distributed along the same series run, with one transformer typically dedicated to each fixture.

03 · Why Isolation Is Required

Isolation From the Series Circuit

The AGL series circuit can operate at a relatively elevated, medium-voltage level (series transformers are rated for a service voltage up to 5kV under IEC 61823), particularly when many fixtures are connected. The isolation transformer sits between this medium-voltage series circuit and the individual light fixture, so the fixture is not directly connected to the full series-circuit electrical arrangement.

Simplified path: Medium-Voltage Series Circuit → Isolation Transformer → Individual Light Fixture. The fixture only ever sees the isolated secondary side, not the series-circuit primary.

04 · Conventional Transformer

Voltage Transformation for General Power

A conventional transformer is generally used to transfer electrical power between different voltage levels — for example, stepping 11 kV down to 415 V for distribution. Its primary purpose is normally voltage transformation, rather than supplying individual lights from an AGL series circuit.

06 · Circuit Architecture

Series Interface vs Parallel Distribution

CCR
SERIES CIRCUIT

Conventional — Parallel Arrangement

POWER SUPPLY

Each load receives the supply voltage independently — fundamentally different from an AGL series circuit.

07 · Isolation Function

Galvanic Separation

The AGL transformer provides galvanic separation between the primary AGL series circuit and the secondary light fixture. This is one of its most important functions — it allows an individual light to be connected to the series circuit without directly exposing the fixture to the full series-circuit voltage.

Each transformer along an AGL series cable is associated with an individual light, which is why AGL isolation transformers are fundamentally different from a large conventional substation transformer serving multiple loads.

09 · Location

Where Each Is Installed

AGL isolation transformers may be installed within a light base, or in an appropriate nearby pit or enclosure — the exact installation can vary depending on the fixture and project arrangement, including transformer-in-base versus remote transformer installations.

Conventional transformers are typically installed at a substation, in a panel, or in an equipment room, serving general electrical distribution rather than an individual fixture.

11 · Advantages

Where Each Design Wins

Advantages of AGL Isolation Transformer

  • Galvanic isolation of each fixture from the series circuit
  • Series-circuit compatibility for long distributed lighting runs
  • Contributes to maintainability of individual fixtures
  • An individual failure primarily affects only its associated fixture

Advantages of Conventional Transformer

  • Efficient voltage conversion between power levels
  • Well suited to general electrical distribution
  • Serves many loads from a single unit
  • Widely available in familiar substation/panel formats
12 · Do Not Confuse

Not Just "Two Windings"

The key difference is application and circuit function, not simply the presence of two windings. In AGL, the isolation transformer is part of the series-circuit architecture, interfacing one fixture with a controlled-current series circuit — not performing general voltage transformation for a distribution system.

13 · Failure Behaviour

Effect of an Individual Transformer Failure

In an AGL series circuit, an individual isolation transformer failure primarily affects its associated fixture, although the exact system behaviour depends on the transformer failure mode and the circuit design. The isolation transformer therefore contributes to the maintainability and electrical separation of individual lights along the series circuit.

14 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: Any two-winding transformer used in an AGL circuit is a conventional transformer
Fact

What defines an AGL isolation transformer is its role — isolating one fixture from the series circuit — not merely the winding arrangement shared with conventional transformers.

Myth: AGL isolation transformers are used to change voltage levels the same way a conventional transformer does
Fact

Its primary purpose is isolation and interfacing an individual fixture with a series circuit, not general-purpose voltage transformation for distribution.

Myth: A single large transformer could replace individual isolation transformers on a series circuit
Fact

AGL series circuits rely on one isolation transformer per fixture so each light is individually isolated and interfaced; a single shared transformer would not provide that per-fixture isolation.

15 · Engineering Selection

Choosing the Right Transformer for the Application

When identifying or specifying a transformer in an airfield lighting context, consider:

  • Whether the transformer interfaces an individual fixture with a series circuit, or transforms voltage for general distribution
  • Isolation requirements between the series circuit and the fixture
  • Installation location — light base/pit versus substation/panel/equipment room
  • Applicable ICAO/national AGL requirements
  • Transformer ratings, losses and current characteristics for AGL series-circuit operation
  • Maintainability and failure-mode considerations for individual fixtures
  • Airport-specific technical specifications
16 · Engineering Verdict

Which Transformer Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

An AGL isolation transformer provides electrical isolation and interfaces an individual light fixture with the series airfield lighting circuit, which makes it purpose-built for the series-circuit architecture used in airfield ground lighting. A conventional transformer transfers power between voltage levels for general electrical distribution, which suits substations, panels and parallel-connected loads.

The final selection for an actual airport project should always be based on the applicable current ICAO/national AGL standard, the approved circuit design and equipment requirements — not on a generic comparison alone.

An AGL transformer is designed for series-circuit behaviour and isolation. A conventional unit is not an equivalent replacement.

17 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What does an AGL isolation transformer actually do?

It provides electrical isolation between the AGL series circuit and an individual light fixture, interfacing that one fixture with the series circuit without exposing it to the full series-circuit voltage.

Why not just use a conventional transformer in an AGL series circuit?

A conventional transformer is designed around voltage transformation for general distribution, not per-fixture isolation and interfacing with a controlled-current series circuit — the AGL isolation transformer is purpose-built for that role.

How many isolation transformers are used on an AGL series circuit?

Typically one isolation transformer per light fixture, distributed along the series circuit.

Where are AGL isolation transformers installed?

Commonly within the light fixture base or in a nearby pit/enclosure, depending on the fixture and project arrangement.

What happens if one isolation transformer fails?

The failure primarily affects its associated fixture, although exact behaviour depends on the failure mode and circuit design — the isolation transformer supports maintainability of individual lights.

Is the difference between the two transformer types just the winding arrangement?

No. The key difference is application and circuit function — how the transformer is used within its circuit — not simply the presence of two windings.

How does the AGL isolation transformer relate to the CCR?

The CCR controls the series-circuit current, while each isolation transformer provides the electrical isolation/interface for its individual fixture on that circuit.

Electrical installations for lighting and beaconing of aerodromes — specifies AGL series (isolation) transformer characteristics for 6.6A series circuits at service voltages up to 5kV, fed by CCRs up to 30kVA.

FAA AC 150/5345-47

Specification for Series to Series Isolation Transformers for Airport Lighting Systems — the FAA equipment spec for the isolation transformers this comparison discusses.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — series circuit requirements, isolation transformer characteristics and light-circuit configuration for AGL.

ICAO Aerodrome Design Manual

Applicable material covering series circuit design, isolation transformer requirements and electrical infrastructure guidance.

National CAA / DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Exact transformer rating, isolation class and circuit design requirements can vary with the governing standard, equipment manufacturer and approved airport design. For final design, use the current applicable standard and manufacturer datasheets rather than a generic comparison page.
