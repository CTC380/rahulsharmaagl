Airport Lighting Maintenance, Inspection & Fault Finding

The 30 divisions below hold the detailed fault topics. This guide shows how they fit into day-to-day AGL work: finding a fault, inspecting the field, keeping to a maintenance schedule and replacing failed lights. Each block links to the divisions that carry the full diagnostic records.

Airport Lighting Fault Finding, Fault Diagnosis & Fault Detection

Airport lighting fault finding starts with the symptom and works inward: which lights are out, on which circuit, at which brightness step. Airport lighting fault diagnosis then narrows that symptom to a section of the circuit using CCR readings, insulation resistance and continuity checks. Airport lighting fault detection is what flags the problem in the first place, through ALCMS alarms, CCR output monitoring and field inspection.

  • Emergency quick-diagnosis for a fast first response
  • Diagnostic decision trees for step-by-step fault location
  • Series circuit electrical faults for earth faults and open circuits

AGL Fault Testing

AGL fault testing follows a fixed order: confirm the CCR output, check the circuit for earth leakage, then isolate the faulty section by splitting the loop.

Airport Lighting Fault Monitoring & the Fault Monitor

Airport lighting fault monitoring relies on the control and monitoring system to report lamp-out counts, circuit current, earth-fault status and CCR alarms to the tower and maintenance room. An airport lighting fault monitor is the unit or software function that collects these signals and raises the alarm when a threshold is crossed.

Monitoring tells you that a fault exists and roughly where. It does not replace a field check, so alarms should always be confirmed on site.

  • ALCMS / control & monitoring
  • Difficult / intermittent faults when alarms come and go

Runway Lighting Common Faults & Runway Lighting Partial Failure

Runway lighting common faults include lamp failure, cracked or flooded fixtures, damaged connectors, isolating transformer failure, cable insulation breakdown and CCR faults. A runway lighting partial failure means some lights on a circuit are out while others stay lit, which usually points to individual lamps, transformers or connectors rather than the CCR.

What Causes Runway Light Failure?

When people ask what causes runway light failure, the usual answers are moisture ingress, lamp ageing, connector damage, surge and lightning damage, cable faults and aircraft or vehicle impact. A whole circuit going dark points to the CCR, the main cable or an open circuit.

What Causes Taxiway Light Failure?

The causes of taxiway light failure are similar to runway lights but with more damage from ground vehicles, aircraft wheel loads and jet blast. Typical causes are water in the fixture, lamp burnout, damaged base can or connector, failed isolating transformer and cable damage from excavation.

Blue edge lights and green centreline lights can also lose colour or intensity from dirty lenses or a worn filter, so a light that is on but dim still counts as a fault.

  • Taxiway light troubleshooting
  • Taxiway centreline / guidance lighting

Airport Lighting Night & Day Inspection, Periodic Inspection & Inspection Report

An airport lighting night inspection checks what only shows when the lights are on: outages, uneven brightness, wrong colour, misaligned or obscured lights. An airport lighting day inspection checks the physical condition: lenses, fixtures, base cans, covers, signs, paint, drainage and vegetation.

The same split applies to specific areas. Runway lighting night inspection and runway lighting day inspection cover runway edge, threshold, centreline and touchdown lights, and approach lighting night inspection covers the approach bars and lamps from the air side and, where permitted, from an aircraft or vehicle.

AGL Periodic Inspection & AGL Inspection Report

AGL periodic inspection covers scheduled checks at set intervals set by the aerodrome operator and regulator. Each one should end in an AGL inspection report that records date, area, defects found, action taken and who signed it off.

Maintenance Types & Schedule: Preventive, Corrective, Emergency & Breakdown

An airport lighting maintenance schedule sets out what is checked daily, weekly, monthly and yearly. Airport lighting preventive maintenance follows that schedule to catch wear before it causes an outage: cleaning, torque checks, insulation tests and lamp changes.

Airport lighting corrective maintenance fixes a known defect on a planned job. Airport lighting emergency maintenance and airport lighting breakdown maintenance deal with failures that affect operations and need an immediate response, often under a NOTAM or reduced-visibility restrictions.

  • Preventive troubleshooting
  • Emergency quick-diagnosis
  • CAT I / II / III troubleshooting for low-visibility requirements

Airport Lighting Replacement Procedure

An airport lighting replacement procedure follows the same safe order in every case: get permission and isolate the circuit, lock out and earth the CCR output, prove it dead, replace the part, test, and restore with approval from control.

Runway Light Replacement, Taxiway Light Replacement & Approach Lighting Replacement

  • Runway light replacement: match the fixture type, lamp and filter; check the seal, torque and alignment
  • Taxiway light replacement: check colour, lens orientation and base can condition
  • Approach lighting replacement: replace the fixture or lamp, then check aiming and frangible mounting
  • Runway light maintenance procedure: clean, inspect, test and record on every visit, not only when replacing

Never work on a live series circuit. The current in a series loop can stay present even when lights look off.

  • Isolating transformer troubleshooting
  • L-823 / connector troubleshooting

Airfield Lighting Troubleshooting & Fault Diagnosis Guide

AGL electrical fault finding

AGL electrical fault finding starts with the symptom and the circuit, then uses measurements to narrow down where the fault is. Typical checks are CCR output current, circuit resistance, insulation resistance and continuity, and they should be done with the circuit isolated and made safe.

AGL fault alarm system

An AGL fault alarm system reports problems such as an open circuit, an earth fault, a CCR fault or lamps out. Alarms point to the circuit or unit, but the fault still has to be found and confirmed in the field.

Airport lighting fault indication

Airport lighting fault indication is the information the CCR and control system give about the state of each circuit: status, alarms and fault codes. Use it as the starting point and check the field before concluding.

AGL earth fault

An AGL earth fault is an unwanted path from the circuit to earth, usually from damaged insulation, water in a connector or transformer, or a damaged cable. It can trip the CCR or leave the circuit running with reduced protection, so find and repair it promptly.

Airfield lighting intermittent fault

An airfield lighting intermittent fault comes and goes, often because of moisture, a loose or corroded connector, a damaged cable or temperature changes. Record when it occurs, look for patterns, and test under the conditions in which it appears.

Airfield lighting troubleshooting guide

This airfield lighting troubleshooting guide takes you from symptom to cause: observe, identify the circuit, isolate, measure, verify, correct and retest. The divisions of the Atlas give the detailed diagnostic records for each type of fault.

Airfield lighting fault diagnosis

Airfield lighting fault diagnosis separates the possible causes by looking at what is out, where, and at which brightness step. Common-cause checks, such as the CCR, a feeder or a cable section, come before replacing individual lights.

Airfield ground lighting troubleshooting

Airfield ground lighting troubleshooting covers the whole chain: the CCR, series circuit, isolating transformers, connectors, fixtures and control. Work from the symptom and prove the cause before changing parts.

Airfield lighting troubleshooting

Airfield lighting troubleshooting is a structured way to find why lights are out, dim, flickering or alarmed. Safe isolation, measurement and records matter as much as technical knowledge.

Airport lighting troubleshooting

Airport lighting troubleshooting brings together electrical, optical and control checks to restore the lighting system. After repair, retest and record the result so the fix is confirmed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about the AGL Troubleshooting Atlas and its 30 divisions.

Q1What is the AGL Troubleshooting Atlas?

The AGL Troubleshooting Atlas is a 30-division diagnostic reference for airfield ground lighting. All 30 divisions are published, with 700 topics in total across equipment, circuit, installation, photometry, control, operational, emergency and root-cause divisions. Each topic follows an 11-field diagnostic record.

Q2How is the Atlas organised?

The 30 divisions are grouped into nine categories: Equipment (11); Circuits & Electrical (5); Installation & Commissioning (1); Photometry (1); Control & Monitoring (1); Operations & CAT (2); Diagnostics & RCA (4); Emergency & Case Studies (2); Preventive & Reference (3). The page's filter tabs let you browse by category, and the search box filters divisions by keyword or fault topic.

Q3Which divisions cover lighting equipment faults?

The Equipment category has 11 divisions: Division 01 CCR Troubleshooting; Division 03 Isolating Transformer Troubleshooting; Division 04 Runway Edge Light Troubleshooting; Division 05 Runway Centreline Light Troubleshooting; Division 06 Touchdown Zone Light Troubleshooting; Division 07 Taxiway Light Troubleshooting; Division 08 Taxiway Centreline / Guidance Lighting; Division 09 Stop Bar / Guard Light Troubleshooting; Division 10 Approach Lighting System Troubleshooting; Division 11 PAPI / VASI Troubleshooting; Division 12 Airfield Signage Troubleshooting.

Q4Which divisions cover circuits and electrical faults?

The Circuits & Electrical category has 5 divisions: Division 02 AGL Series Circuit Troubleshooting; Division 13 AGL Cable Troubleshooting; Division 14 L-823 / Connector Troubleshooting; Division 15 Series Circuit Electrical Faults; Division 16 Earthing / Lightning / Surge Troubleshooting.

Q5Which divisions cover installation, photometry, control and operations?

Installation & Commissioning: Division 17 Installation & Commissioning Faults. Photometry: Division 18 AGL Photometry Troubleshooting. Control & Monitoring: Division 19 ALCMS / Control & Monitoring. Operations & CAT: Division 20 CAT I / CAT II / CAT III Troubleshooting; Division 21 Operational & Maintenance Problems.

Q6Which divisions cover root cause analysis and structured diagnosis?

Diagnostics & RCA: Division 22 Difficult / Intermittent Faults; Division 23 Root Cause Analysis / Failure Investigation; Division 26 Advanced Diagnostic Topics; Division 29 Diagnostic Decision Trees. Preventive & Reference: Division 27 Preventive Troubleshooting; Division 28 Troubleshooting Checklist Library; Division 30 Advanced RCA / Engineering Investigation.

Q7Where should I start when a fault is happening now?

Division 24 (Emergency Quick-diagnosis) gives fast diagnosis guides for total or partial lighting failure, major faults and airport-wide communication loss. Division 29 (Diagnostic Decision Trees) gives structured decision trees for CCR, circuit, transformer, connector, fixture and communication faults. For a specific equipment fault, go straight to its equipment or circuit division.

Q8What does each troubleshooting topic contain?

Every topic follows the same diagnostic record: the symptom, equipment/system, possible causes ranked by likelihood, required tools, safety precautions, diagnostic procedure, test points, root cause verification, corrective action, preventive action and related references such as SOPs, standards, diagrams and case studies.

Q9How do I find a specific fault in the Atlas?

Type a keyword such as 'earth fault', 'PAPI' or 'connector' into the search box to filter divisions and their fault topics, or use the category tabs. Each division page also has its own search box for its topics.

Q10What is an AGL maintenance checklist?

An AGL maintenance checklist is a written list of the inspection, cleaning, testing and repair steps for a type of lighting, signed off for each visit. It covers items such as lamp and lens condition, connector and base can checks, CCR readings, insulation resistance and brightness steps, so that work is consistent and recorded. The Troubleshooting Checklist Library (Division 28) holds ready-made checklists.

Q11How is an airport lighting fault found?

An airport lighting fault is found by working from the symptom to the faulty section. Start with the alarm or the inspection report, check the CCR output current and brightness step, test the circuit for earth leakage and continuity, then split the circuit to isolate the section. The final check is a field test of the individual light, transformer or connector. Division 24 (Emergency Quick-diagnosis) and Division 29 (Diagnostic Decision Trees) set this out step by step.

Q12What causes runway light failure?

Common causes of runway light failure are lamp burnout, moisture or flooding in the fixture, damaged connectors, failed isolating transformers, cable insulation breakdown, surge or lightning damage, and impact from vehicles or aircraft. If a whole circuit is out, check the CCR and main cable first. See Divisions 04, 05, 06 and 09 for the individual light types.

Q13What causes taxiway light failure?

Taxiway light failure is usually caused by water in the fixture, lamp burnout, damaged base cans or connectors, failed isolating transformers, and cable damage from excavation or heavy loads. Taxiway lights also see more wheel loading and jet blast than most runway lights. See Divisions 07 and 08 for taxiway edge and centreline lighting faults.

Q14How to identify an AGL earth fault?

Signs of an AGL earth fault include a CCR earth fault alarm, a trip, or a low insulation resistance reading on the circuit. With the circuit isolated and made safe, test insulation resistance and divide the circuit into sections to find the faulty cable, connector or transformer. Follow the test method and limits in your standard and manufacturer data.
