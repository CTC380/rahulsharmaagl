Taxiway Lighting Comparison

High-Intensity VS Medium-Intensity

Same Meaning, Different Strength — high-intensity taxiway lighting produces a higher light output for greater visual conspicuity where strong guidance is required, while medium-intensity taxiway lighting produces a lower light output suitable for normal taxiway visual guidance. Intensity changes how strongly a light is presented to the pilot — it does not change what the light means.

02 · Definitions

What Is High-Intensity & Medium-Intensity Taxiway Lighting?

High-Intensity

Produces a stronger visual signal. The purpose is not to change the fundamental meaning of the light — it is to make the lighting more conspicuous under demanding visual conditions.

Typically applied for

  • Critical, complex or demanding operations
  • Poor visibility conditions
  • Greater viewing distances
  • Situations requiring strong guidance

Medium-Intensity

Provides the required visual guidance with a lower light output. It is appropriate where extremely high conspicuity is not required.

Typically applied for

  • Routine taxiway operations
  • Normal visibility conditions
  • Lower glare and energy demand
  • Simpler intensity management
03 · Biggest Difference: Strength, Not Meaning

Intensity Is Not the Same as Colour

This is important. Intensity describes the strength of the light output; colour describes what the light communicates. The same lighting concept can have different intensity settings without changing its basic colour or function — high-intensity does not mean a different colour.

For blue taxiway edge lighting, for example, changing intensity does not change the fundamental message — blue still means taxiway edge reference — it only changes how strongly the lights are presented to the pilot. The same logic applies to green taxiway centreline lighting: the guidance to follow the designated taxi route remains, whichever intensity level is in use.

04 · Why It Matters

Conspicuity vs Comfort

The simplest way to remember it: high intensity answers "how do I stay conspicuous when conditions are demanding?" while medium intensity answers "what's enough for normal, routine guidance?" More light is not automatically better — excessive intensity can produce glare, reduced visual comfort, difficulty distinguishing nearby lights and unnecessary energy consumption, which is why lighting systems commonly provide selectable intensity control rather than simply operating everything at maximum output.

At high intensity, the lights are easier to distinguish when visual conditions are challenging. At medium intensity, the same basic visual guidance is provided at a lower output — sufficient for normal operating conditions.

06 · Intensity Control

Selectable Output Levels

A conceptual lighting system may provide several selectable intensity levels — low, medium and high output — rather than a single fixed setting. The appropriate setting depends on the operational and environmental requirements at the time, and the exact intensity setting is governed by the applicable aerodrome lighting system and operating procedures.

07 · Low Visibility

Which Matters More When Visibility Drops?

When visibility deteriorates, stronger conspicuity can become more valuable. In normal visibility, medium intensity is generally adequate for routine visual guidance; in poor visibility, higher intensity offers greater conspicuity and can be the more useful setting — though the precise threshold and setting are governed by the applicable aerodrome lighting system and operating procedures rather than a fixed rule.

Easy memory: medium intensity = normally visible, high intensity = more conspicuous. Neither setting changes what the light is telling the pilot — only how strongly that message comes across.

09 · Do Not Confuse These Terms

Intensity, Function and Installation Are Different Things

High vs Medium Intensity

Answers "how bright is it?" This is a question of photometric output and conspicuity — nothing more.

Edge vs Centreline

Answers "where, and what function?" This is a question of location and guidance role, entirely separate from intensity.

A third, related distinction is elevated vs inset — a question of how the fixture is installed. High/medium intensity, edge/centreline, and elevated/inset are three different characteristics of a taxiway light, and none of them determines the others.

10 · Myth vs Fact

Common Taxiway Lighting Confusion

Myth: High-intensity lights are a different colour from medium-intensity lights
Fact

Intensity and colour are independent characteristics. A blue taxiway edge light remains blue whether it is operating at high or medium intensity — only the strength of the signal changes, not its meaning.

Myth: More light output is always better
Fact

Excessive intensity can cause glare, reduce visual comfort, make it harder to distinguish nearby lights, and increase energy consumption unnecessarily. This is why systems provide selectable intensity control rather than running everything at maximum output.

Myth: Medium intensity is inadequate or a lesser system
Fact

Medium-intensity lighting is commonly suitable for routine taxiway operations. It is not a downgraded system — it is the appropriate setting where extremely high conspicuity is not required.

11 · Engineering Verdict

High-Intensity vs Medium-Intensity Taxiway Lighting

Engineering Verdict

High- and medium-intensity taxiway lighting are the same basic system operated at different strengths, not two different systems. One is tuned for maximum conspicuity; the other for normal, routine visual guidance.

Intensity changes how strongly the visual signal is presented to the pilot — not the fundamental meaning of the taxiway light. The choice between them, and the exact setting used, is governed by the applicable aerodrome lighting system and operating procedures.

HighMore light output + greater conspicuity + demanding/critical conditions.
MediumModerate light output + normal conspicuity + routine operations.

Easy memory: High → more light → more conspicuity. Medium → less light → normal conspicuity.

Quick Answer

Taxiway Light Intensity Testing

Taxiway light intensity testing checks that each taxiway edge or centreline light gives the required output in candela, and the correct colour, at the brightness steps in use. High-intensity and medium-intensity taxiway circuits are tested against different required values, so it is important to know which class the circuit belongs to before testing.

12 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between high-intensity and medium-intensity taxiway lighting?

Light output and conspicuity — high-intensity lighting produces a higher light output for greater visual conspicuity, while medium-intensity lighting produces a lower, moderate output suitable for normal taxiway visual guidance.

Does high-intensity taxiway lighting use a different colour?

No. Intensity describes the strength of the light output, while colour describes what the light communicates. A light's colour and function stay the same regardless of its intensity setting.

Why not just always use the highest intensity setting?

Because more light is not automatically better. Excessive intensity can produce glare, reduce visual comfort, make it harder to distinguish nearby lights, and increase energy consumption without necessarily improving guidance.

When is high-intensity taxiway lighting typically used?

It is typically used where stronger conspicuity is required — for example, critical, complex or demanding operations, greater viewing distances, or poor visibility conditions — subject to the applicable aerodrome lighting system and operating procedures.

Is medium-intensity lighting sufficient for normal taxiway operations?

Yes. Medium-intensity lighting is commonly suitable for routine taxiway operations, providing adequate visual guidance under normal conditions with lower glare potential and lower energy demand.

Is intensity control the same as elevated vs inset installation?

No. High vs medium intensity is about how bright the light is; elevated vs inset is about how the fixture is installed; edge vs centreline is about where the light is located and what function it serves. These are three separate characteristics.

13 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

FAA AC 150/5340-30

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids — taxiway lighting configuration, installation, photometric requirements and intensity control systems.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — taxiway lighting colour, siting, intensity levels and operational requirements.

DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Do not design a taxiway lighting intensity scheme from a generic web diagram. The final installation drawing and intensity-control philosophy should always be developed from the current governing standard, approved airport design and project-specific requirements.

High-intensity = more light output, greater conspicuity. Medium-intensity = moderate light output, normal conspicuity — the same basic function, presented at a different strength.
