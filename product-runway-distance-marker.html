Product Overview

Runway Distance Marker

What It Is

Runway distance markers give clear visual information about runway distance and help pilots maintain operational awareness. Positioned alongside the runway or at designated locations, the marker boards present distance information in a simple, highly visible format.

Supplied as non-illuminated, retroreflective or illuminated boards, selected according to runway layout, operational requirements, ambient lighting and aerodrome standard.

Five Design Elements

  • Legend — distance or identification information
  • Marker face — surface carrying the legend
  • Visibility treatment — painted, retroreflective or illuminated
  • Support structure — posts, brackets, foundations
  • Installation interface — physical and any electrical connection

Treat the assembly as an integrated airfield product, not a printed board.

Sign Type & Colours

Distance-Remaining Signs and Their Colours

FAA Practice

Under FAA practice (AC 150/5340-18H and AC 150/5345-44L) a runway distance-remaining sign has a black face with a white numeral. The numeral gives the remaining runway length in thousands of feet. The sign is normally internally illuminated and installed beside the runway. Legend colour is therefore set by the standard and is not a free project choice. On FAA-governed projects the distance-remaining sign is the illuminated type under AC 150/5345-44L, so confirm with the authority before offering painted or retroreflective-only boards for such a project.

ICAO and Other Authorities

Annex 14 deals with runway distance information differently from the FAA, so on ICAO-governed projects confirm with the national authority or airport specification whether a distance-remaining sign is required, its legend format (metres or feet), colours, size and spacing, before drawings are approved.

Illuminated distance boards are normally powered from the airfield series circuit through an isolating transformer and follow the runway lighting brightness steps. Confirm the electrical interface before ordering.

Retroreflective & Illuminated

Choosing the Visibility Method

Retroreflective Configuration

Non-powered; enhanced visibility under aircraft lighting.

  • Improved night-time recognition
  • No dedicated electrical supply
  • Reduced electrical maintenance
  • Simple installation, reduced infrastructure

Performance can decline from contamination, weathering, UV, damage or ageing: include inspection and cleaning in maintenance.

Illuminated Configuration

Where active illumination is required: internal LED, edge or face illumination, or project-specific assemblies, with adequate visibility, contrast and no excessive glare.

  • Required luminance, viewing direction
  • Sign-face construction, power availability
  • Control requirements, maintenance access
  • Airport electrical architecture
LED Illumination & Electrical

Illuminated Versions

LED Benefits

  • Long operating life, reduced lamp replacement
  • Efficient power consumption, rapid switching
  • Consistent illumination, lower maintenance

Select LED modules and power supplies as a coordinated system for uniform illumination.

Electrical Interface Considerations

  • Supply voltage, AC or DC operation
  • Series-circuit (constant-current, via isolating transformer) or parallel-circuit supply for illuminated boards, with brightness-step control
  • Dedicated circuits, airfield lighting control, switching
  • Surge protection, earthing and bonding
  • Cable entry, maintenance isolation

Review before procurement when installing in an existing airfield lighting network.

Mounting & Frangibility

Designed Together With the Marker

Mounting Arrangements

  • Single- or two-post mounting
  • Ground-supported structures, bracket-mounted assemblies
  • Project-specific support frames
  • Frangible mounting: equipment in the operational area must be frangible and mounted as low as its function allows (Annex 14, Vol. I, Chapter 9)

Consider marker height, wind loading, foundation type, pavement and aircraft clearance, maintenance access, cable routing.

Frangibility & Airfield Safety

Where the marker is in an aircraft operating environment, evaluate against applicable frangibility and obstacle-clearance requirements across:

  • Marker board, support posts, base assembly
  • Fasteners, breakaway interfaces
  • Foundation, cable routing

Requirements depend on marker location and aerodrome design criteria.

FAQ

Runway Distance Marker — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a runway distance marker?

A runway distance marker is a highly visible board, positioned alongside the runway or at designated locations, that gives clear distance information in a simple format. It is supplied as a non-illuminated, retroreflective or illuminated board, selected according to runway layout, operational requirements, ambient lighting and aerodrome standard. The assembly includes the legend, marker face, visibility treatment, support structure and installation interface.

What does an FAA runway distance-remaining sign look like?

Under FAA practice (AC 150/5340-18H and AC 150/5345-44L) a distance-remaining sign has a black face with a white numeral giving the remaining runway length in thousands of feet. It is normally internally illuminated and installed beside the runway. The legend colour is set by the standard, so on FAA-governed projects confirm with the authority before offering painted or retroreflective-only boards.

Does ICAO require runway distance markers?

Annex 14 deals with runway distance information differently from the FAA. On ICAO-governed projects, confirm with the national authority or airport specification whether a distance-remaining sign is required, its legend format (metres or feet), colours, size and spacing, before drawings are approved.

What is the difference between retroreflective and illuminated distance markers?

A retroreflective marker is non-powered and relies on aircraft lighting, so it needs no dedicated electrical supply and reduces electrical maintenance, but its performance can decline from contamination, weathering, UV, damage or ageing. An illuminated marker uses internal LED, edge or face illumination and must be matched to the required luminance, viewing direction, power availability, control requirements and airport electrical architecture.

How are illuminated distance markers powered?

Illuminated boards are normally powered from the airfield series circuit through an isolating transformer and follow the runway lighting brightness steps. A parallel-circuit supply is also possible. Check supply voltage, AC or DC operation, surge protection, earthing and bonding, cable entry and maintenance isolation, and confirm the electrical interface before ordering.

How is character height decided for a distance marker?

Character height depends on pilot viewing distance, runway geometry, marker position, the aircraft operating environment, ambient lighting, required recognition time, marker dimensions and regulatory requirements. Larger is not automatically better, so the height should follow the applicable standard and the viewing requirement.

Do runway distance markers need to be frangible?

Where the marker is in an aircraft operating environment, equipment must be frangible and mounted as low as its function allows (ICAO Annex 14, Volume I, Chapter 9). Evaluate frangibility and obstacle clearance across the marker board, support posts, base assembly, fasteners, breakaway interfaces, foundation and cable routing. Requirements depend on marker location and aerodrome design criteria.

What materials are used for runway distance marker boards?

Typical construction uses aluminium or another corrosion-resistant material, a weather- and UV-resistant face and finish, a durable legend surface, corrosion-resistant fasteners and reinforced mounting points, with a sealed enclosure for illuminated versions. Coastal, humid, industrial or high-temperature locations may need additional material and protective-finish considerations.

How should runway distance markers be inspected and maintained?

Routine work includes cleaning the face, inspecting retroreflective material for fading and corrosion, checking fasteners, support posts, foundations and cable entries, and checking LED modules and power supplies on illuminated boards. Damaged, faded, heavily contaminated or poorly illuminated markers should be assessed and repaired or replaced. A night-time inspection helps verify practical visibility of illuminated markers.

Can existing runway distance markers be replaced or retrofitted?

Yes. Existing installations can be reviewed for board dimensions, support arrangement, foundation, legend and visibility condition, retroreflective performance and electrical configuration. Where practical, replacement dimensions are coordinated with existing infrastructure to minimize installation work.

What information is needed to request a runway distance marker?

Provide the airport or project name, runway designation, required legend, quantity, character height, approximate dimensions, marker location, mounting arrangement, material requirement, retroreflective and illumination requirements, electrical supply if applicable, frangibility requirement, environmental conditions, applicable airport standard and required documentation.
