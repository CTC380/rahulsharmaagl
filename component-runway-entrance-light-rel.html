Runway Entrance Light (REL)

A Runway Status Light (RWSL) fixture installed at taxiway/runway intersections that illuminates steady red to warn a pilot or vehicle operator that it is unsafe to enter or cross the runway ahead, because of high-speed traffic on the runway or an aircraft on final approach. RELs are a dynamic stop signal driven by surface surveillance, not a routine positioning or lead-in guidance aid.

Overview

What Is It?

A Runway Entrance Light (REL) is a Runway Status Light (RWSL) fixture installed at taxiway intersections with the runway. These in-pavement lights indicate the precise location where a taxiway enters the runway, illuminating steady red to warn pilots and vehicle operators when it is unsafe to enter or cross. They are particularly valuable at complex airport layouts where multiple taxiways intersect the runway.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The REL exists to give a pilot or vehicle operator a dynamic, real-time warning at exactly the point where a taxiing aircraft is closest to entering an active runway — an intersection where the consequence of proceeding at the wrong moment is a runway incursion. Rather than marking a fixed boundary the way holding-position markings and stop bars do, the REL row stays dark under normal conditions and illuminates steady red only when the runway ahead is genuinely unsafe to enter or cross, driven by surface surveillance data on runway traffic and approaching aircraft. Its purpose is best understood as an automated, occupancy-aware layer of protection at complex intersections, reinforcing — not replacing — ATC clearance and the pilot's own judgement.

Application

Where Is It Used?

REL is deployed as part of an RWSL installation at taxiway/runway intersections, which requires airport surface surveillance data to drive it. They are used on:

  • Busy, surveillance-equipped airports with high traffic density and frequent runway crossings
  • Complex taxiway layouts with multiple runway entry points where the safe-to-enter moment is hard to judge unaided
  • Runway/taxiway intersections with a documented history or elevated risk of incursion
  • Airports with surface movement radar or multilateration (e.g. ASDE-X/ASSC), since RWSL depends on that surveillance feed
  • Night and low-visibility operations, where a red illuminated cue is more reliable than judging runway occupancy visually
Placement

Installation & Location

Fixtures are installed in a row across the taxiway at the runway holding position, spaced evenly across the taxiway width so an aircraft crossing the position sees a continuous line of lights rather than isolated points, with each unit recessed flush into the pavement on an L-868 base can. Cabling runs in pavement ducting back to the REL control cabinet, which interfaces with the Airport Lighting Control and Monitoring System (ALCMS) and, on RWSL-equipped fields, with the surface surveillance sensor feed that determines runway occupancy in real time. The control cabinet is typically located near the taxiway edge, clear of aircraft movement areas, and includes the interlock logic that illuminates the lights steady red only when the adjoining runway is occupied or a conflict is detected. Setting-out is tied to the holding position marking survey so the light row aligns precisely with the painted holding position line, and installation is commissioned alongside the surveillance system to confirm detection accuracy before the lights are placed in service.

Function

How Does It Work?

RELs operate as part of the taxiway guidance system:

  • Lighting: RELs are steady-burning red, unidirectional, in-pavement lights arranged in a row across the taxiway, illuminated only when the runway ahead is unsafe to enter or cross.
  • Indication: When lit, the lights give a clear "do not enter/cross" signal at that taxiway entrance; unlit conveys no meaning and is not an instruction to proceed.
  • Integration: RELs are commanded together with the co-located Stop Bar Light so the two always present the same state, and operate alongside — but independently of — Runway Guard Lights at the same holding position.
  • Control: The lights are controlled by the ALCMS and can be activated or deactivated based on runway status.
Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

Runway entrance lights are not powered from the constant-current runway edge circuit — they run on a dedicated switched circuit, a row of steady-burning red LED fixtures fed through their own isolating transformers from a REL-specific feeder. Control originates in the ALCMS, which derives a runway-occupied or runway-clear state from tower input, the Runway Status Light (RWSL) surveillance feed, or local pilot-controlled procedure, and issues a single ON/OFF command for the entire REL row at that taxiway entrance. This command is passed to the REL switching controller, which energises or de-energises every fixture in the row simultaneously — there is no way to switch a single REL independently of the rest of the row. The same ALCMS occupancy decision is sent at the same time to the co-located Stop Bar Light, which is why the two are always seen in the same state by an approaching pilot (see the interlock logic below). Fault supervision works at the circuit level: the switching controller or ALCMS interface monitors current draw on the REL feeder, and any anomaly — an open circuit, a failed transformer, a fixture that has failed to illuminate with the rest of the row — is reported as a REL circuit fault for follow-up field testing rather than pinpointed automatically to one fixture.

ALCMS → REL Switching Controller → REL Circuit → Isolating Transformer → Fixture (with circuit-level feedback)

System Integration

ALCMS Interlock Logic

What sets REL apart from a standalone edge light is the interlock: it never operates on its own. The ALCMS reads runway occupancy status and commands the REL and the adjacent Stop Bar Light as a single coordinated pair, so a pilot at the holding position always sees one consistent picture.

ALCMS command signal
Occupied — hold state
Clear — entry permitted
Construction

Physical Construction

The fixture is a low-profile, inset (flush) unit that seats into an L-868 base can recessed into the taxiway pavement, with the top surface load-rated for repeated aircraft wheel loading at the runway-taxiway intersection. The housing is a die-cast aluminium alloy body, anodised or powder-coated for corrosion resistance against de-icing fluid, fuel spillage, and tyre wear. The red LED array sits under a high-strength polycarbonate or borosilicate glass lens, gasket-sealed to the housing with an O-ring to achieve an IP67/IP68 rating against water and dust ingress, and shaped to project the uni-directional beam pattern focused toward the taxiway holding position. Internal wiring terminates at L-823 primary connectors, which mate to the secondary leads of the isolating transformer housed in an adjacent base can, with control cabling routed separately to the ALCMS interface. Stainless-steel fasteners are used throughout, and the base can assembly includes a drainage provision to keep water from pooling around the transformer and cable entry points.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Lens Contamination

RELs are exposed to rubber deposits from aircraft tyres and de-icing fluids. Regular cleaning with approved solvents and soft brushes is required to maintain light output and visibility.

Switching Mechanism Failure

The switching controller in RELs can fail due to electrical or mechanical issues, causing the lights to latch on or fail to illuminate on command. Regular inspection and maintenance of the switching control system is essential.

Communication with ALCMS

RELs rely on communication with the ALCMS for control. Network failures can cause the lights to fail to activate or deactivate at the correct time. Redundant communication paths are recommended.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Entire REL Row Fails to Illuminate on Command
Likely Cause
Loss of the ALCMS ON/OFF command to the REL switching controller, an open circuit on the dedicated REL feeder, or a failed isolating transformer feeding the row.
Diagnostic Steps
Confirm ALCMS is sending the activation command and that the switching controller is receiving it; check current draw on the REL feeder against expected values; megger the circuit to check for an open or failed transformer.
Corrective Action
Restore the ALCMS-to-controller command path if the signal isn't arriving, or repair the open circuit/replace the failed transformer on the REL feeder; re-test the full row before returning to service.
One Fixture Dark or Out of Step Within the Row
Likely Cause
Failed LED array on that unit, or a fixture that has failed to illuminate in step with the rest of the row — since the switching controller reports circuit-level faults rather than pinpointing a single fixture, this requires field isolation.
Diagnostic Steps
Visually identify the dark or out-of-step unit during a test activation; check L-823 connectors and the isolating transformer at that specific fixture; confirm the fault clears once that unit is isolated and bypassed.
Corrective Action
Replace the failed LED array, connector, or transformer at the affected fixture; re-verify all units illuminate together in step before reinstating the full row.
REL and Stop Bar Light Showing Inconsistent States
Likely Cause
A fault in the ALCMS interlock logic or its distribution path is causing the REL and the co-located Stop Bar Light — which should always be commanded together from the same occupancy decision — to fall out of agreement.
Diagnostic Steps
Check the ALCMS occupancy decision and confirm the same command is being issued to both the REL and Stop Bar Light controllers; verify each controller's own circuit is healthy independently to rule out a local fault masquerading as an interlock fault.
Corrective Action
Repair the ALCMS interlock distribution path if the command isn't reaching one of the two systems consistently; escalate a persistent ALCMS-side logic fault for system-level correction, since this is a safety-critical mismatch.
Lights Remain Illuminated After Runway Clears
Likely Cause
The switching controller has latched in the ON state, or the ALCMS is still receiving a stale occupancy indication from the RWSL surveillance feed or tower input.
Diagnostic Steps
Compare the ALCMS's current occupancy state against actual runway status; issue a manual OFF command directly at the switching controller and observe response; check the RWSL/tower input feed for a stuck or delayed update.
Corrective Action
Reset or replace a latched switching controller; escalate a stale surveillance/tower feed to ALCMS or RWSL system maintenance to clear the underlying data fault.
Switching Mechanism Latches On Instead of Following ALCMS Command
Likely Cause
A failed switching element in the controller has left the circuit permanently energised rather than tracking the commanded ON/OFF state.
Diagnostic Steps
Confirm the fixture state against the current ALCMS command during a test activation; inspect the switching controller's relay/driver circuit; substitute a known-good controller module to isolate the fault.
Corrective Action
Replace the faulted switching controller module; re-verify correct ON/OFF tracking across the full row before returning to service.
Reduced Output / Insulation Resistance Drop at REL Position
Likely Cause
Rubber deposits or de-icing fluid contamination on the lens, or moisture ingress at the base can, L-823 connectors, or isolating transformer under the taxiway pavement.
Diagnostic Steps
Visually inspect the lens for contamination; megger the isolating transformer and REL circuit segment; check top-cap O-rings and gaskets for compression set or cracking that could allow water ingress.
Corrective Action
Clean the lens with approved solvents; dry out or replace the affected transformer/connector and renew seals; confirm insulation resistance is restored above the acceptable minimum before re-energising.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

Switching Controller Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Failed switching element, a control board fault from thermal or surge stress, or component wear from continuous duty cycling in a high-activation location.
Effect
Entire REL row fails to illuminate on command, or latches on steady after the runway clears and the ALCMS has commanded OFF.
Detection Method
Test activation with ON/OFF state verification, controller self-diagnostics, and substitution with a known-good controller module to isolate the fault.
ALCMS Command / Communication Loss
High Severity
Root Cause
Network or fieldbus interruption between the ALCMS and the switching controller, a stale or delayed occupancy indication from the RWSL surveillance feed, or a fault in the interlock logic shared with the co-located Stop Bar Light.
Effect
Lights fail to activate or deactivate at the correct time, remain illuminated after the runway clears, or fall out of agreement with the associated Stop Bar Light — a safety-critical mismatch.
Detection Method
Comparison of ALCMS occupancy state against actual runway status, verification that commands reach both REL and Stop Bar Light controllers together, and monitoring of the RWSL/tower input feed for stuck updates.
LED Array Failure
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Solder-joint fatigue from thermal cycling, moisture ingress through a compromised board seal, or ordinary end-of-life lumen depreciation.
Effect
Single fixture within the row goes dark or fails to illuminate in step with the rest of the row, since the switching controller reports circuit-level faults rather than pinpointing an individual unit.
Detection Method
Visual identification of the dark or out-of-step unit during a test activation, followed by field isolation of that fixture to confirm the fault clears.
Isolating Transformer / L-823 Connector Degradation
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Moisture ingress under the taxiway pavement from a compromised top-cap O-ring or gasket, improper seating or crimping at installation, or corrosion building up on the pins over time.
Effect
Reduced insulation resistance, an open circuit that darkens the affected fixture, or a partial ground fault on the REL feeder.
Detection Method
Megger testing of the transformer and REL circuit segment, and visual inspection of top-cap seals and connectors during scheduled maintenance.
Lens Contamination
Low Severity
Root Cause
Rubber deposits from aircraft tyres, de-icing fluid residue, and general grime accumulation on the lens surface.
Effect
Reduced light output and lower visibility of the REL indication to flight crews at the runway entrance point.
Detection Method
Visual inspection of the lens during scheduled maintenance and photometric intensity checks against ICAO/FAA thresholds.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Series Circuit Isolation
Electrical Safety

REL fixtures are fed from their own dedicated switched feeder, not the runway edge CCR loop, but the feeder and its isolating transformers still carry hazardous voltage. De-energise and lock out the REL switching controller/feeder, then verify zero energy at the base can before opening it or handling connectors.

Surveillance Interlock Dependency
Operational

On RWSL-equipped fields, the steady red indication is driven by the surface surveillance feed; any work on the fixture or control cabinet must be followed by an integration check confirming the interlock still correctly activates before returning the system to service.

Holding Position Work Coordination
Operational

These fixtures sit at the runway holding position, a primary incursion-prevention reference point for pilots and vehicle operators; work here requires ATC coordination and a NOTAM where lights will be dark, with the holding position clearly marked by other means in the interim.

Flush Fit & Wheel-Load Tolerance
Mechanical

The inset fixture must sit precisely flush with the pavement; a proud edge or depression becomes a wheel-impact point for every aircraft crossing the holding position.

Insulation Test Before Re-Energisation
Electrical Safety

After any cable, connector, or transformer work, megger test the affected feeder segment before restoring power, since a residual fault at re-energisation can trip the switching controller's protection or darken the row.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Do not return the fixture to service with an unverified surveillance interlock; confirm activation accuracy and photometric conformance before closing out and cancelling the associated NOTAM.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Verify the Interlock, Not Just the Fixture
Integration

A REL that photometrically passes in isolation can still be unsafe if the ALCMS command timing has drifted. After any surveillance sensor, control cabinet, or firmware change, run a full occupancy-to-illumination timing check rather than trusting a standalone lamp test.

Series Circuit Fault Isolates the Whole Row
Electrical

Because REL fixtures typically share a series loop with adjacent stop bar or edge lighting, a single degraded connector can quietly raise loop impedance long before it fails outright. Trend insulation and current readings loop-wide, not fixture-by-fixture, to catch this early.

Flush Tolerance Drifts With Pavement Work
Mechanical

Resurfacing or slab repair near the holding position can leave an REL sitting slightly proud or recessed even when the fixture itself is undamaged. Re-check flush tolerance any time pavement work occurs nearby, not only during scheduled fixture maintenance.

Activation Timing Consistency Across the Pair
Photometric

A subtle activation-timing mismatch between the REL and its paired Stop Bar Light is easy to miss on a single-fixture bench test but reads as confusing or contradictory to a pilot at the hold line. Verify the two illuminate in the same state, not as individually tested units.

Log Nuisance Trips Before They Escalate
Troubleshooting

Intermittent REL feeder trips at a holding position are often dismissed as one-offs, but they usually trace back to a specific connector or transformer under marginal load. Keep a running log tied to the affected feeder segment so the pattern surfaces before a hard failure during ops.

Coordinate NOTAM Scope With ATC Early
Operational

Because the REL/Stop Bar pair is a primary incursion-prevention cue, an underscoped NOTAM that only covers the REL and not the coordinated stop bar can leave a misleading picture at the hold line. Scope maintenance NOTAMs around the interlocked pair, not the single fixture being worked.
