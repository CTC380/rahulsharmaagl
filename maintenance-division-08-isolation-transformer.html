Isolation Transformer Maintenance

The dedicated maintenance chapter for AGL isolation transformers and their immediate installation environment — the transformer itself, primary and secondary leads, connectors, the transformer pit, sealing, drainage, and identification. Distinct from the fixture (Division 06) and the CCR (Division 07), this division covers the transformer, the pit, and the immediate cable/connection interfaces between them.

Philosophy: an isolation transformer should not be considered healthy simply because the associated light is operating. Every task in this division is judged against six conditions — mechanical, electrical, connection, environmental, installation, and functional — following a fixed sequence from inspection through to record.

Water-Ingress Decision Logic

Water found in a transformer pit is treated as a diagnostic branch point, not a housekeeping task. The key principle: water removal is not water-ingress correction — the source must be identified and corrected.

Transformer / Connector Wet

  1. Isolate / assess as required
  2. Inspect transformer, connectors, leads
  3. Assess insulation condition
  4. Remove water
  5. Correct the ingress source
  6. Test, then restore

Transformer / Connector Dry

  1. Investigate pit / drainage condition
  2. Correct drainage or sealing issue found
  3. Record the finding
Water removal is not water-ingress correction — the cause must be found and fixed, not just pumped out.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about isolation transformer maintenance, based on the topics in this division.

7 QUESTIONS
Q1What is isolation transformer maintenance in the AGL Maintenance Handbook?

It is the dedicated chapter for AGL isolation transformers and their immediate installation environment: the transformer itself, primary and secondary leads, connectors, the transformer pit, sealing, drainage and identification.

Q2What does Division 08 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 08 (Isolation Transformer Maintenance) contains 30 topics in five groups: Group A Routine Maintenance (10 topics), Group B Electrical Maintenance (6 topics), Group C Corrective Maintenance (7 topics), Group D Pit & Installation (6 topics) and Group E Testing & Record (1 topic).

Q3Is a transformer healthy if the associated light is operating?

No. Every task is judged against six conditions: mechanical, electrical, connection, environmental, installation and functional.

Q4What is the isolation transformer maintenance sequence?

Nine steps: Inspect, Clean, Check Connections, Check Insulation/Continuity, Check Pit, Correct Defects, Test, Restore and Record.

Q5What should be done when water is found in a transformer pit?

Water in a pit is treated as a diagnostic branch point, not a housekeeping task. Removing water is not correcting water ingress, so the source must be identified and fixed. If the transformer or connector is wet, isolate and assess as required, inspect the transformer, connectors and leads, assess insulation, remove the water, correct the source, then test and restore. If dry, investigate pit and drainage condition, correct any drainage or sealing issue and record the finding.

Q6How do you trace a fixture failure back through the transformer?

The troubleshooting flow keeps the whole circuit in view: check the fixture, secondary lead, secondary connector, isolation transformer, primary connector, primary lead, series circuit and CCR, then identify the root cause, repair or replace, and record.

Q7How does the maintenance decision matrix work?

A clean, dry, intact transformer is Good and gets routine PM; a dirty pit is cleaned; water in the pit is a concern and drainage or ingress is investigated; a damaged connector or lead is a defect to replace or repair; poor insulation condition is a high concern; repeated failures go to reliability or condition review; and major transformer damage is critical and assessed for repair or replacement. These are decision categories, not universal regulatory acceptance thresholds.
