Technical Direction

Engineering-Led. Knowledge-Driven.

Precision AGL follows a specialist-led technical model. Instead of treating engineering, learning, documentation, testing, and troubleshooting as separate activities, they are connected through one technical framework.

Knowledge

Understanding AGL fundamentals, systems, components, terminology, and engineering principles.

Engineering

Applying technical knowledge to systems, components, design decisions, project requirements, and documentation.

Verification

Connecting engineering requirements with testing, photometry, inspection, and technical verification.

Operations

Supporting understanding of maintenance, troubleshooting, procedures, and long-term system performance.

Development

Turning lessons learned into better resources, better tools, better training, and better engineering practice.

The Precision AGL Team Model

Industry Collaborators

Learning Community

Students, engineers, technicians, and aviation professionals form an important part of the ecosystem by learning, applying, asking questions, sharing experiences, and developing their technical understanding.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1Who is behind Precision AGL?

Precision AGL follows a specialist-led approach centred on its founder, Rahul Sharma, an Airfield Ground Lighting specialist who founded Precision AGL Solutions and Precision AGL Academy. It is supported by collaboration with professionals, organizations and technical contributors across the aviation and engineering community.

Q2What is Rahul Sharma's technical focus?

Airfield Ground Lighting systems, AGL photometry, runway and taxiway lighting, PAPI systems, constant current regulators, isolating transformers, AGL testing and commissioning, ICAO and FAA technical requirements, airport project support, and technical training and knowledge development.

Q3What is the Precision AGL team model?

Core Leadership provides technical direction, framework and vision. Technical Contributors add knowledge, reviews and specialist perspectives. Industry Collaborators are airport professionals, consultants, contractors, manufacturers, suppliers and service providers. The Learning Community is students, engineers, technicians and aviation professionals.

Q4What areas of expertise does the team cover?

AGL Systems, AGL Electrical, Photometry & Testing, Standards & Compliance, and Engineering Knowledge, which includes SOPs, inspection, maintenance, troubleshooting, component references, engineering forms, technical articles and training resources.

Q5How is the technical direction structured?

It runs through five connected areas: Knowledge, Engineering, Verification, Operations and Development, so that engineering, learning, documentation, testing and troubleshooting are not treated as separate activities.

Q6Can professionals or organizations join or contribute?

Yes. Potential areas include technical collaboration, knowledge contribution, training and education, technical review, research and innovation, project collaboration, testing and engineering support, and business partnership.

Q7What technical values does the team follow?

Precision, Practicality, Continuous Learning, Documentation, Collaboration and Responsibility, with respect for safety, standards, reliability and operational requirements.
