Asset Audit & Governance

The assurance, governance and accountability division of the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide. After Divisions 01–22 establish the complete lifecycle — from planning and asset registration through operation, maintenance, reliability, digital management, performance and replacement — Division 23 verifies that the lifecycle system is actually being followed, the asset information remains reliable, and identified deficiencies are formally corrected and closed. The framework identifies asset audit checklists as a major deliverable, so this division is structured around audit criteria, evidence, findings, corrective actions and closure — not another maintenance or inspection procedure.

Initial Issue

The question this division answers — Are we managing, recording, controlling and improving the AGL asset correctly — and can every claim of compliance be traced to objective evidence, a documented finding, a corrective action and a verified closure?

Where Division 23 Sits in the Lifecycle

Division 23 does not compete with inspection, testing or KPI management — it verifies that those functions are actually happening, are properly evidenced, and that any deficiency they surface is corrected and closed. It is the independent assurance layer wrapped around Divisions 01–22.

01–22 — Plan → Register → Assess → Design → Procure → Install → Commission → Operate → Maintain → Monitor → Measure → Improve → Replace → Decommission → Digitize

Most Important Design Principle

Division 23 stays in the assurance layer. It does not repeat inspection or KPI work — it confirms that inspection, testing, maintenance, and performance management are actually being carried out, correctly evidenced, and that identified gaps are closed with a verified corrective action.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Asset Audit & Governance in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide.

Q1What is Division 23 — Asset Audit & Governance in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

Division 23 is the assurance, governance and accountability division. It covers 20 topics on asset-management, register, data, criticality, maintenance, inspection, testing, warranty, spares, condition, lifecycle-cost, replacement, disposal, documentation, CMMS/EAM and compliance audits, plus governance review, findings, corrective action and closure.

Q2What is an asset-management audit?

It evaluates whether the overall AGL asset-management framework, Divisions 01 through 22, is being implemented effectively and not just documented. Division 23 is the assurance, governance and accountability division of the guide.

Q3What is the difference between an asset-register audit and an asset-data audit?

An asset-register audit verifies whether the physical AGL asset population agrees with the controlled register from Division 02, checking items such as asset ID, tag, type, manufacturer, model and serial number. An asset-data audit evaluates the quality and completeness of asset information, judged on accuracy, completeness, consistency, validity, timeliness and uniqueness.

Q4Which lifecycle areas are audited?

Division 23 has audits for criticality, maintenance, inspection records, testing records, warranty, spare parts, condition data, lifecycle cost, replacement plans, disposal records, documentation, CMMS/EAM systems and compliance.

Q5What does a criticality audit verify?

It verifies whether assets have been correctly classified according to the approved criticality methodology from Division 03, covering safety, operational and regulatory criticality and risk ranking.

Q6What is a CMMS/EAM audit?

It verifies whether the digital asset-management system accurately represents the physical asset-management process, checking asset master data, hierarchy, work orders, PM schedules, maintenance history and failure records.

Q7What is an asset-governance review?

Governance defines who is responsible for what within the asset lifecycle, covering asset ownership and asset-management, engineering, maintenance and data responsibility.

Q8How are audit findings, corrective actions and closure handled?

Every finding should be documented consistently, with a finding ID, audit area, asset or system, requirement, evidence and description, so it can be tracked, prioritized and closed. Findings should lead to controlled corrective actions that resolve the underlying deficiency, and an audit should be closed only after findings have been addressed and verified. Status tracking runs from open through action in progress, awaiting evidence and pending verification to closed or overdue.
