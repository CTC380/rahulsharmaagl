Division 24 of 25 · INS-DOC Series

Documentation & Compliance Inspection

A systematic inspection framework for verifying that the AGL system is not only physically compliant and serviceable, but that its compliance, maintenance, testing, configuration, and history can also be demonstrated through controlled, complete, current, and traceable records — the documentary evidence layer of the AGL inspection system.

Initial Issue
Core Principle
"Is the physical system compliant?"
AND
"Can we prove that it is compliant through records?"

Critical control — A technically acceptable AGL installation may still fail an airport's specific procedural/documentary requirements. Compliance should not be considered demonstrated solely because the physical system appears satisfactory, where the applicable requirement also demands documented evidence.

Final Compliance Status

Final status is kept more specific than a simple pass/fail:

C — CompliantPhysical system and documentary evidence satisfy applicable requirements.

PC — Partially CompliantPhysical/documentary compliance exists but identified gaps remain.

NC — Non-CompliantOne or more applicable requirements are not satisfied.

UE — Unverified EvidenceCompliance may exist physically, but adequate documentary evidence is unavailable or insufficient.

N/A — Not ApplicableRequirement does not apply to the asset/system.

Critical NC — Critical Non-ComplianceA significant compliance gap requiring immediate technical/management attention under the applicable airport/project procedure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about documentation & compliance inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook.

Q1What is Division 24 — Documentation & Compliance Inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook?

Division 24 contains 181 published topics in 19 sections (A–S). It is a systematic framework for verifying that the AGL system is physically compliant and serviceable and that its compliance, maintenance, testing, configuration and history can also be demonstrated through controlled, complete, current and traceable records. It is the documentary evidence layer of the AGL inspection system.

Q2What records does Division 24 inspect?

It covers maintenance records, test reports, calibration certificates, inspection history, asset identification and equipment labelling, as-built drawings, the asset register, defect-closure records, corrective-action evidence, spare-parts and training records, document revision control, airport-specific requirements, compliance matrices, record consistency and cross-verification, record integrity and auditability, and compliance deficiencies.

Q3What is the two-layer compliance model?

Layer 1 is physical compliance: whether the asset is actually installed, operating and maintained correctly. Layer 2 is documentary compliance: whether the organization can demonstrate that condition through controlled evidence. Compliance equals physical conformity plus documentary evidence plus traceability.

Q4What final compliance statuses are used?

Final status is more specific than pass or fail. C means Compliant, PC Partially Compliant, NC Non-Compliant, UE Unverified Evidence (compliance may exist physically but adequate documentary evidence is unavailable), N/A Not Applicable, and Critical NC a critical non-compliance needing immediate technical or management attention under the applicable procedure.

Q5What is the document-to-physical verification chain?

It links the physical asset, asset ID or label, asset register, as-built drawing, inspection record, maintenance record, test record, calibration evidence and defect or corrective-action record to the current compliance status. If the chain breaks, the system may be physically functional but its documentary compliance cannot be fully demonstrated.

Q6Why does Division 24 use a compliance verification matrix?

A requirement and evidence matrix is stronger than checking whether documents exist. Each requirement is checked against physical verification, document evidence and test evidence where applicable, and given a status.

Q7Do airport procedures take precedence over this handbook?

Yes. This handbook is an uncontrolled educational reference, not an approved procedure. The airport's approved inspection schedule, SMS procedures, ATC instructions, NOTAM procedures and local aerodrome regulations always take precedence, and the current revision of any official document should be verified before use.

Q8Can I download a printable checklist for each inspection topic?

Yes. Each inspection topic has a Download as Word option that produces an uncontrolled Word checklist template (description, a tick-box inspection table and an inspector sign-off block) for personal reference and internal training, to be reviewed and adopted under the user’s own document control.
