Light Source Photometry Comparison

LED PHOTOMETRY VS HALOGEN PHOTOMETRY

Same Measurement, Different Source — LED and halogen AGL fixtures are evaluated with the same fundamental photometric quantities (candela, lux, beam distribution), but the way each source generates light changes what a test needs to account for: spectrum, directionality, thermal behaviour, and ageing.

05 · Native Light Distribution

Directional vs Omnidirectional Emission

LED

Native LED emission tends to be more directional, with optics used to further shape and control the output.

Halogen

A halogen filament emits broadly in nearly all directions, relying on the reflector to redirect light forward.

These describe source behaviour only. The final AGL fixture distribution is determined by the complete optical design.

06 · Beam Formation

Two Different Routes to a Controlled Beam

Fixture Type Beam Formation Path
LED fixtureLED → lens / optics → controlled beam
Halogen fixtureFilament → reflector + lens → controlled beam

An LED is not automatically better simply because it is directional, and a halogen lamp is not automatically unsuitable because its source emission is broad. The required comparison is between the final fixture photometric distributions.

07 · Spectrum and Photometry

Narrow/Structured vs Broad/Continuous

LED

Spectral distribution depends on LED material/design, phosphor system, and colour-producing method.

Halogen

Typically a broader continuous spectrum, governed by filament temperature.

This spectral difference can affect colour measurement and the relationship between physical optical radiation and photometric quantities.

08 · Colour Measurement

Photometry vs Colorimetry

For AGL applications, colour performance must be considered independently from intensity — photometry asks "how much?", colorimetry asks "what colour?"

LED

Colour can depend on:

  • LED spectral characteristics
  • LED binning/design
  • Temperature
  • Drive conditions
  • Optical components

Halogen

Colour characteristics can depend on:

  • Filament operating temperature
  • Electrical operating conditions
  • Filters
  • Optical components
09 · Temperature Effects

Thermal Behaviour During Testing

LED

LED optical performance can be influenced by junction temperature, so the thermal condition during measurement can be important.

Halogen

Halogen output also depends on thermal equilibrium: power drives filament heating, temperature stabilises, and light output stabilises in turn.

Both technologies require measurements to be performed under the defined operating and stabilisation conditions.

12 · Example

Runway Edge Light: Two Technologies, Same Question

Fixture Path to Beam
LED fixturePower → driver → LED → optics → beam
Halogen fixturePower → filament → reflector → optics → beam

The photometric test ultimately asks the same question for both: does the completed fixture provide the required luminous intensity within the required angular sectors and other applicable optical characteristics? The internal technology does not change the fundamental compliance objective.

13 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: "LED fixtures require completely different photometry"
Fact

Not fundamentally. The measurement may require different operating, thermal, spectral, or stabilisation considerations, but the core photometric quantities remain the same.

Myth: "Halogen fixtures always have worse beam control"
Fact

Not necessarily. A well-designed reflector and optical system can produce the required beam distribution.

Myth: "Higher LED efficiency automatically means higher candela"
Fact

Not necessarily. Candela depends on how much luminous intensity is produced in a particular direction, not simply on source efficiency.

15 · Engineering Verdict

LED Photometry vs Halogen Photometry

Engineering Verdict

LED photometry and halogen photometry measure the same fundamental optical quantities. The photometric measurement itself is not fundamentally different between the two technologies.

The main difference is how the light source generates light, and how operating conditions, spectrum, temperature, ageing, and optics influence the measured result. For AGL compliance, the final question is essentially technology-independent: does the complete fixture, operated under the specified conditions, produce the required intensity, beam distribution, and colour characteristics?

LEDSemiconductor → optics → 💡 → photometry.
HalogenFilament → reflector/optics → 💡 → photometry.

For AGL: choose the source technology on engineering and lifecycle grounds — the photometric compliance question stays the same either way.

16 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Do LED and halogen AGL fixtures use different photometric units?

No. Both are evaluated using the same quantities — candela for luminous intensity, lux for illuminance, cd/m² for luminance, and intensity-versus-angle for beam distribution.

Why does LED photometry pay extra attention to temperature?

Because LED optical performance can be influenced by junction temperature, so the thermal condition of the LED during measurement can affect the recorded output and colour.

Why does halogen photometry require warm-up time?

A halogen filament's output changes as it heats toward thermal equilibrium, so measurements are taken once the lamp has stabilised under the defined operating conditions.

Is an LED fixture automatically brighter than a halogen fixture?

Not automatically. Candela depends on how much intensity the complete fixture produces in a specific direction, not simply on the source's efficiency or technology.

Does ageing affect photometric compliance for both technologies?

Yes. LEDs can show gradual lumen/intensity depreciation over operating hours, while halogen lamps experience filament and lamp ageing that can also change output over time.

Does the source technology change the AGL compliance objective?

No. The compliance question is essentially technology-independent — does the completed fixture provide the required intensity, beam distribution, and colour characteristics under the specified conditions?

17 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

FAA AC 150/5340-30

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids — photometric requirements for runway and approach lighting, applicable to both LED and halogen fixtures.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — photometric characteristics of aeronautical ground lights, intensity and colour requirements.

CIE Photometry Standards

International Commission on Illumination definitions and measurement methods for luminous intensity, illuminance and luminance.

ANSI/IES LM-79

Approved Method: Optical and Electrical Measurements of Solid-State Lighting Products — the specific test method for LED source/luminaire photometric and electrical measurement referenced on the LED side of this comparison; halogen sources are instead measured under conventional goniophotometric practice.

FAA AC 150/5345-46

Specification for Runway, Taxiway, Heliport, and Vertiport Light Fixtures — sets the actual photometric requirement (candela tables per L-type) that a given fixture's LED or halogen photometry must be measured against.

Final Engineering Note

Do not assume source technology changes the definition of the measured quantity. Confirm stabilisation, thermal, and drive conditions required by the governing test procedure for the specific source in use.

LED and halogen photometry ask the same question of the fixture — they just require different care in how the source is operated during the test.
