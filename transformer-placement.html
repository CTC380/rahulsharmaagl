The Fact Installation & construction

Transformer placement is setting the isolation transformer into its prepared pit or housing. The rating is checked against the design, the transformer is lifted in by its body, seated clear of the floor, and its primary and secondary leads are routed with slack ready for connection.

A transformer left to hang from its leads, standing in water or fitted at the wrong rating becomes a hidden fault: the moulded connections can be strained and let water in, and a mismatched rating can leave the fixture dim, overloaded or unreliable on the series circuit. Leave the nameplate readable from the lid so the rating can be checked later without lifting anything. Treat the primary cable as live until the circuit has been isolated, locked out and proved dead, do not energise the circuit with the secondary leads unconnected or exposed, because an open secondary can carry a hazardous voltage (isolating transformers are built to tolerate a lamp-out, but the leads are never safe to handle while the circuit is live), use safe manual handling for the weight, and work to the airport’s permit-to-work.

  1. Match. Check the nameplate rating against the design drawings.
  2. Lift. Lift it by the body, never by the leads, using safe manual handling.
  3. Seat. Set it upright on the support, clear of the pit floor.
  4. Route the leads. Lay them to their connectors with slack and without strain.
  5. Face the nameplate. Turn it so the rating can be read from the lid opening.
  6. Connect and close. Make and seal the connectors as the design specifies, refit the lid and clear debris.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is transformer placement in airfield ground lighting?

Transformer placement is setting the isolation transformer into its pit or housing once that is ready. The rating is checked against the design, the transformer is lifted in by its body and seated clear of the floor, and the primary and secondary leads are routed with slack ready for connection.

Q2Why check the nameplate before placing the transformer?

The nameplate shows the rating, and the transformer must match the fixture and circuit the design shows. Fitting the wrong rating can mean a fixture that is dim, overloaded or unreliable and a series circuit that does not behave as designed, so confirm the nameplate against the design before it goes into the pit.

Q3Why must the transformer never hang from its leads?

The leads and their moulded connections are not designed to carry the weight. Lifting or holding the transformer by them can strain or damage the connection and let water in later. Lift it by its body or the lifting provision it has, and take the weight on the support in the pit.

Q4Where should the transformer sit in the pit?

Upright and seated on the support or bed the design shows, clear of the pit floor so it is not left standing in any water that collects. Position it so the leads reach their connectors without strain and the nameplate can be read from the lid opening.

Q5Why leave slack in the transformer leads?

Ground movement, frost and later maintenance all pull on leads. A little slack in a gentle loop stops that load reaching the connections, and it allows the transformer to be lifted out for testing or replacement without cutting or re-making the cables.

Q6What should be checked before the pit is closed?

Check the rating against the design, a stable upright seating clear of the floor, leads routed with slack and not trapped by the lid, the nameplate readable, and the connectors made and sealed as the design specifies. Treat the primary cable as live until the circuit has been isolated, locked out and proved dead, and do not energise the circuit with the secondary leads unconnected or exposed, because an open secondary can carry a hazardous voltage. Isolating transformers are built to tolerate a lamp-out, but the leads are never safe to handle while the circuit is live.
