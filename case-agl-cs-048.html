AGL-CS-048 — Touchdown Zone Light Failure

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A precision-approach lighting investigation where one touchdown zone inset light failed while the rest of the circuit remained operational — from CCR verification through transformer secondary testing, fixture inspection, water-ingress assessment, and drainage restoration.

1Case Study Overview

During a pre-operational CAT II/III inspection, one touchdown zone inset light was found completely extinguished, interrupting the paired touchdown zone lighting pattern.

The Constant Current Regulator (CCR) was operating normally, and the adjacent touchdown zone light pairs remained fully operational.

Because touchdown zone lights are installed as paired white inset fittings, the investigation focused on the individual transformer, secondary circuit, connector assembly, and inset touchdown zone fixture.

A systematic investigation identified water ingress into the fixture base-can assembly, causing corrosion of the secondary connector and failure of the transformer secondary connection.

This case demonstrates that touchdown zone light failures are commonly localized inset-fixture or secondary-circuit faults, with moisture ingress and sealing deterioration being major reliability factors in precision-approach lighting systems.

3System Background

A touchdown zone light is supplied through:

Primary Series Circuit → Isolating Transformer → Secondary Cable → Connector Assembly → Inset TDZ Fixture

Touchdown zone lights provide:

  • White touchdown-zone reference.
  • Landing-area definition.
  • Flare guidance.
  • Landing-distance perception.
  • CAT II/III visual guidance.

Common failure causes include:

  • LED module failure.
  • Water ingress.
  • Connector corrosion.
  • Transformer secondary failure.
  • Secondary cable damage.
  • Base-can drainage failure.
  • Fixture sealing deterioration.

4Problem Statement

The reported condition was:

  • One TDZ light OFF.
  • Adjacent TDZ light pairs operational.
  • CCR healthy.
  • No circuit alarms.
  • CAT II/III inspection in progress.

7TDZ Diagnostic Principle

Primary current present → Transformer energized → Secondary voltage available → TDZ fixture illuminated

If adjacent TDZ light pairs remain operational, the fault is usually localized to the transformer secondary circuit or the inset fixture itself.

Step 5: Fixture Removal and Inspection

The inset TDZ fixture was removed.

Inspection revealed:

  • Water accumulation.
  • Corroded connector assembly.
  • Oxidized terminals.
  • Deteriorated sealing gasket.
  • Contaminated electrical compartment.

The fixture had experienced progressive water-ingress deterioration.

Step 6: Base-Can Drainage Inspection

The base can showed:

  • Blocked drainage holes.
  • Standing water.
  • Sediment accumulation.
  • Poor water evacuation.

The drainage failure had increased long-term moisture exposure.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The touchdown zone light failed because water ingress entered the inset fixture and base-can assembly through a deteriorated sealing gasket and blocked drainage system, causing corrosion of the secondary connector and interruption of the transformer secondary connection.

The primary circuit and transformer remained healthy.

The fault was localized to the inset fixture secondary connection.

11Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Isolated the circuit.
  2. Removed the affected inset fixture.
  3. Replaced the damaged connector assembly.
  4. Installed a new approved sealing gasket.
  5. Cleaned and dried the base can.
  6. Restored drainage.
  7. Verified secondary continuity.
  8. Tested photometric performance.
  9. Reinstalled the fixture.
  10. Updated maintenance records.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: One touchdown zone light OFF. Adjacent TDZ light pairs ON. CCR healthy. No circuit alarms. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace the CCR.
B. Increase the current setting.
C. Verify transformer secondary voltage and inspect the inset fixture, connector assembly, sealing system, and base-can drainage.
D. Replace all touchdown zone lights.
Correct answer: C. A single TDZ light failure with a healthy CCR and operational adjacent pairs points to a localized fixture or secondary fault. Secondary voltage verification and comprehensive fixture inspection will identify the cause without unnecessary equipment replacement.

Quick Reference: ICAO Touchdown Zone Lights & Intensity

ICAO touchdown zone lights are provided in the touchdown zone of precision approach runways, particularly for the lower-visibility categories. They are white, arranged in pairs of bars on each side of the centreline. Touchdown zone light intensity is checked as part of photometric testing, and a failed light in this zone should be treated as an operational defect for low-visibility operations.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about touchdown zone light failure, based on this case study.

Q1What is AGL-CS-048?

AGL-CS-048 is an engineering training case study on a single touchdown zone (TDZ) inset light that was OFF during a pre-operational CAT II/III inspection. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2Why is a single TDZ failure notable?

TDZ lights are installed as paired white inset fittings, so one extinguished light interrupts the paired lighting pattern. Adjacent TDZ pairs and the CCR were normal, which pointed to a localized field fault.

Q3What was the root cause?

Water entered the inset fixture and base-can assembly through a deteriorated sealing gasket and blocked drainage, corroding the secondary connector and interrupting the transformer secondary connection. The primary circuit and transformer stayed healthy.

Q4What should you investigate first?

Verify transformer secondary voltage and inspect the inset fixture, connector assembly, sealing system and base-can drainage. Replacing the CCR or all TDZ lights, or increasing the current setting, is not the right first step.

Q5Which test equipment was used?

The team used a true-RMS multimeter, secondary voltage test equipment, continuity tester, low-resistance ohmmeter, insulation resistance tester, thermal imaging camera and moisture inspection equipment.

Q6How was the light repaired?

The team isolated the circuit, removed the affected inset fixture and replaced the damaged connector assembly. They installed a new approved sealing gasket, cleaned and dried the base can, restored drainage, verified secondary continuity, tested photometric performance and reinstalled the fixture.

Q7How was the repair verified?

Transformer secondary and TDZ fixture operation were restored, the paired-light pattern was complete and drainage was restored.

Q8Why check drainage as well as the connector?

Blocked drainage and a deteriorated gasket together allowed the water in. Restoring drainage was part of the correction, not only replacing the corroded connector.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

A touchdown zone light failure is usually a localized inset-fixture or secondary-circuit fault, with water ingress, connector corrosion, and base-can drainage failure being among the most significant reliability factors affecting precision-approach touchdown zone lighting systems.

Systematic CCR verification, transformer secondary testing, fixture inspection, connector assessment, sealing evaluation, drainage inspection, and photometric verification can rapidly identify the failed component and restore touchdown zone lighting integrity.

In AGL engineering, a failed touchdown zone light often begins with moisture beneath the pavement long before it becomes visible on the runway surface.
