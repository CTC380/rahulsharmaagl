Fixture Compatibility

Fixture Compatibility

Inset bases must be selected according to the specific fixture installation interface. Check compatibility for:

  • Fixture make and model
  • Base dimensions
  • Mounting-hole arrangement
  • Fixture height and installation depth
  • Cable-entry position
  • Connector arrangement
  • Drainage requirements
  • Pavement construction

Do not select a base solely from the nominal fixture category. Confirm the actual fixture model and installation configuration before procurement.

Pavement Installation

Pavement Installation

Installation is closely tied to the pavement structure and finished surface level. Important considerations:

  • Pavement thickness
  • Base installation depth
  • Finished pavement elevation
  • Fixture seating arrangement
  • Base support and anchoring
  • Concrete or foundation requirements
  • Cable routing
  • Drainage path
  • Access for future maintenance

The finished installation should maintain the required relationship with the surrounding pavement and a suitable transition between base and pavement surface. Fixture seating height must stay within the protrusion limit set by the fixture specification and the aerodrome authority. Check the base size and depth against any shallow-base arrangement specified: see the Shallow Base page, and the L-830 isolating transformer page for the associated transformer.

Cable Entry & Junction Pit

Cable Entry, Protection and Junction Pit Interface

Cable Entry and Protection

  • Provides the interface for cable connection to the fixture
  • Coordinate routing with the complete AGL circuit design
  • Primary circuit cables and isolating transformer connections
  • Secondary leads and primary or secondary connectors
  • Cable protection and maintenance access
  • Confirm entry dimensions and positions before installation to avoid interference with the fixture or pavement

Junction Pit Interface

  • An inset circuit may connect through a nearby junction pit or cable infrastructure
  • Suitable cable routing
  • Mechanical cable protection
  • Accessible connection points where required
  • Protection against environmental ingress
  • Adequate clearance around the fixture connection
Drainage

Drainage Requirements

Effective drainage matters because the base is integrated into the pavement environment. The installation should consider:

  • Water collection around the fixture
  • Internal base drainage
  • Connection to an appropriate drainage path
  • Pavement slope
  • Sealing and ingress protection
  • Local groundwater and environmental conditions

Coordinate drainage with the civil and pavement design rather than treating it as an isolated fixture-base detail.

Structural

Structural Requirements

The base and its supporting installation must suit the mechanical conditions of the airfield pavement. Selection may depend on:

  • Fixture loading
  • Pavement type and thickness
  • Base construction and foundation arrangement
  • Vehicle or aircraft loading conditions
  • Installation environment
  • Project-specific structural requirements

For new installations, incorporate the base arrangement into the approved pavement and airfield-lighting design.

Installation

Installation Considerations

Before installing an inset base, verify:

  • Fixture model and mounting interface
  • Base dimensions and required installation depth
  • Pavement dimensions and construction
  • Finished surface level
  • Cable-entry arrangement
  • Transformer and connector configuration
  • Drainage provisions
  • Structural support
  • Alignment and orientation requirements
  • Fastener torque values from the fixture and base documentation
  • Base covers or temporary plugs to keep paving material, sealant and debris out of the base until the fixture is fitted

Follow the approved project drawings and the applicable requirements for the associated airfield lighting system.

Replacement and Rehabilitation

Inset bases are also relevant when replacing existing inset fixtures or rehabilitating older pavement lighting systems. Before selecting a replacement base, useful information includes:

  • Existing fixture make and model
  • Existing base dimensions
  • Pavement thickness and installation depth
  • Existing cable-entry arrangement
  • Transformer location
  • Junction-pit arrangement
  • Drainage configuration

Commissioning

Commissioning Checks

Following installation, check the complete assembly for:

  • Secure mounting
  • Correct cable connections and cable protection

  • Alignment and orientation

Complete any installation-specific inspection or testing per the project commissioning procedure.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures

FAQ

Inset Light Base — Frequently Asked Questions

What is an inset light base?

An inset light base is the structural interface for a compatible inset airfield lighting fixture in a pavement-integrated installation. It provides a fixture-specific mounting interface, provision for cable connection and routing, and drainage where the installation requires it, and it suits both new-build and rehabilitation projects.

Which fixtures use inset bases?

Inset bases are used for selected flush-mounted or inset fixtures, including runway centreline lighting, runway threshold and runway end lighting, touchdown zone (TDZ) lighting, taxiway inset lighting and other selected inset AGL fixtures. Always match the base configuration to the specific inset fixture and the pavement construction.

How do I choose the right base for a fixture?

Select the base according to the specific fixture installation interface. Check the fixture make and model, base dimensions, mounting-hole arrangement, fixture height and installation depth, cable-entry position, connector arrangement, drainage requirements and pavement construction. Do not select a base solely from the nominal fixture category; confirm the actual fixture model and installation configuration before procurement.

What pavement factors affect inset base installation?

Installation is closely tied to the pavement structure and finished surface level. Consider pavement thickness, base installation depth, finished pavement elevation, fixture seating arrangement, base support and anchoring, concrete or foundation requirements, cable routing, the drainage path and access for future maintenance. Fixture seating height must stay within the protrusion limit set by the fixture specification and the aerodrome authority.

How does the base connect to cables and the junction pit?

The base provides the interface for cable connection to the fixture, and routing should be coordinated with the complete AGL circuit design, including primary circuit cables, isolating transformer connections, and secondary leads and connectors. An inset circuit may connect through a nearby junction pit or cable infrastructure. Confirm entry dimensions and positions before installation to avoid interference with the fixture or pavement.

How should drainage be handled around an inset base?

Drainage matters because the base is integrated into the pavement environment. Consider water collection around the fixture, internal base drainage, connection to an appropriate drainage path, pavement slope, sealing and ingress protection, and local groundwater and environmental conditions. Coordinate drainage with the civil and pavement design rather than treating it as an isolated fixture-base detail.

What structural requirements apply to an inset base?

The base and its supporting installation must suit the mechanical conditions of the airfield pavement. Selection may depend on fixture loading, pavement type and thickness, base construction and foundation arrangement, vehicle or aircraft loading conditions, the installation environment and project-specific structural requirements. For new installations, incorporate the base arrangement into the approved pavement and airfield lighting design.

What should be verified before installing an inset base?

Verify the fixture model and mounting interface, base dimensions and installation depth, pavement construction, finished surface level, cable-entry arrangement, transformer and connector configuration, drainage provisions, structural support, alignment and orientation, and the fastener torque values from the fixture and base documentation. Use base covers or temporary plugs to keep paving material, sealant and debris out of the base until the fixture is fitted.

What information is needed to replace an inset base during rehabilitation?

Useful information includes the existing fixture make and model, existing base dimensions, pavement thickness and installation depth, existing cable-entry arrangement, transformer location, junction-pit arrangement, drainage configuration, and site photographs and drawings. Where existing components are retained, confirm dimensional compatibility before ordering.

What is checked when commissioning an inset base?

Check correct fixture seating and finished elevation, secure mounting, correct cable connections and cable protection, drainage performance, the pavement interface, alignment and orientation, and electrical continuity and circuit operation. Complete any installation-specific inspection or testing required by the project commissioning procedure.

Which standards apply to inset light bases?

References include FAA AC 150/5345-42K (airport light bases, transformer housings, junction boxes and accessories, including load-bearing bases for inset fixtures), FAA AC 150/5340-30J, FAA AC 150/5345-46F, ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I Chapter 5 with Doc 9157 Part 4, and DGCA India CAR Section 4 with airport specifications. The base must match the fixture manufacturer documentation, and the base type, class and size designation should be confirmed in the current edition against the fixture data.

Is it safe to work on inset fixtures while the circuit is energised?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage, and opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death. De-energise the circuit at the regulator, apply the site lock-out / tag-out procedure, use the series circuit cut-out switch or an approved isolation device, and work under the airport permit-to-work system. See the electrical safety warning on this page.
