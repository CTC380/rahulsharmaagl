Overview

What Is Airfield Ground Lighting?

Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) is the system of visual aids and associated equipment used to guide aircraft during approach, landing, takeoff, and movement on the airport surface — especially at night or in reduced visibility.

New to airfield lighting? Start with What Is Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL)?

A complete AGL system can include approach lighting, runway lighting, taxiway lighting, PAPI and other visual approach aids, illuminated airfield signs, stop bars and runway guard lights, Constant Current Regulators, series-circuit cabling, isolating transformers, connectors, and control, monitoring, testing and maintenance systems.

Guides Landing

Approach and runway lighting gives pilots continuous visual reference from several miles out through touchdown.

Guides Ground Movement

Taxiway lighting and signage prevent runway incursions and guide aircraft safely between runway, apron, and gate.

Supports Low-Visibility Operations

AGL provides visual guidance during night operations and, where applicable, supports reduced-visibility operations in conjunction with the airport's procedures, navigation systems and other required facilities.

The CCR supplies controlled current to the airport lighting series circuit. That circuit connects individual isolating transformers, each feeding a light fixture over its own secondary cable. The isolating transformer provides electrical separation between the shared primary series circuit and its own fixture circuit; the secondary cable then carries that isolated, lower-current supply the short distance to the fixture itself.

Circuit Design

Primary & Secondary Circuits

Primary Circuit

The high-voltage series circuit supplied by the CCR. Typical AGL systems use a constant-current series arrangement rather than a conventional constant-voltage distribution setup — that's the key difference from ordinary building wiring.

Secondary Circuit

The secondary side of an isolating transformer supplies the individual lighting fixture: CCR → primary AGL cable → isolating transformer → secondary cable → light fixture.

Why Use a Series Circuit?

The series arrangement lets multiple lighting loads operate as part of one controlled airport lighting circuit. Because the CCR controls circuit current directly, it also controls the lighting system's intensity steps for the entire circuit at once.

A CCR is the major component of an AGL system that supplies controlled current to the series lighting circuit — think of it as the heart of the whole chain.

How to Select an AGL Isolation Transformer

Selection starts from the fixture's rated burden and the series circuit's rated current, then checks that against the transformer's rated power, turns ratio and insulation level for the applicable circuit voltage class. Environmental rating, mounting method (e.g. in a light base or pit) and compliance with the applicable ICAO/FAA/IS specification round out the decision.

Basics & Overview

Airfield Lighting Basics, Overview, Purpose & Principles

Airfield Lighting Basics

Airfield lighting basics: lights along runways, taxiways and aprons give pilots visual guidance in darkness and poor visibility. They run on series circuits fed by constant current regulators, with each light connected through an isolating transformer.

Airfield Lighting Overview

An airfield lighting overview covers approach lights, runway lights, taxiway lights, PAPI, signs and beacons, plus the power, regulators, cables and control behind them. Together they form one visual guidance system.

Purpose Of Airfield Lighting

The purpose of airfield lighting is to let pilots find the runway, line up, land, taxi and park safely at night and in low visibility, by showing position, direction and distance through light colour and pattern.

Airfield Lighting For Beginners

Airfield lighting for beginners is easiest to learn in order: first what each light shows, then the series circuit that powers it, then how it is controlled, tested and maintained. This page follows that sequence.

Airfield Lighting Principles

Airfield lighting principles are consistency, reliability and clarity: patterns and colours follow international standards so pilots read them the same everywhere, and circuits are designed so failures do not leave large dark areas.

Airfield Lighting Functions

Airfield lighting functions include marking runway edges and centrelines, identifying thresholds and ends, guiding aircraft along taxiways, giving glide path information and signalling hazards such as obstacles or runway holding positions.

Airfield Lighting Types

Airfield lighting types are grouped by where they are used: approach lighting, runway lighting, taxiway lighting, glide path indicators such as PAPI, apron and stand lighting, beacons and obstruction lights. Fixtures are elevated or inset.

Airfield Lighting Working Principle

Airfield lighting working principle: a constant current regulator forces one set current through a series loop of cable, and each light draws power through an isolating transformer. Changing the current changes brightness for every light on the circuit.

Airfield Lighting Visual Aids

Airfield lighting visual aids are part of the wider group of visual aids in ICAO Annex 14, which also includes markings, signs and markers. Lights, markings and signs must be designed to work together.

Airport Lighting Signals

Airport lighting signals are the information carried by light colour, steadiness, flashing and arrangement, for example green threshold, red runway end and flashing sequences on approach. They differ from light gun signals sent from the tower.

AGL Explained

Airfield Ground Lighting Working Principle, Types, Functions & Specification

Airfield Ground Lighting Working Principle

Airfield ground lighting working principle is series-circuit power: each circuit has one regulator, one cable loop and many lights on isolating transformers, so a single lamp failure does not stop the others.

Airfield Ground Lighting Types

Airfield ground lighting types cover elevated and inset fixtures, unidirectional, bidirectional and omnidirectional beams, and white, green, red, blue, yellow and other colours chosen for the function of each light.

Airfield Ground Lighting Functions

Airfield ground lighting functions are guidance and information: where the runway is, where its ends are, where to turn or stop on taxiways, and whether the aircraft is on the correct approach path.

Airfield Ground Lighting Specification

An airfield ground lighting specification sets photometric output, colour, mechanical strength, ingress protection and electrical data for each product. It cites standards such as ICAO Annex 14, FAA advisory circulars and IEC standards.

Airfield Ground Lighting Selection Guide

Airfield ground lighting selection guide: start from the runway and taxiway category, then choose fixture type, light source, colour and intensity, and check compatibility with the existing regulators and transformers.

Airfield Ground Lighting Technical Guide

An airfield ground lighting technical guide ties together design, installation, testing and maintenance. Use it with the manufacturer data and the standards that apply to your aerodrome.

Airport Lighting

Airport Lighting Infrastructure, Types, Functions & Engineering Guide

Airport Lighting Infrastructure

Airport lighting infrastructure includes substations and CCRs, series cable and ducts, light fixtures, control and monitoring, standby power and earthing. It is long-life infrastructure, so design for maintainability from the start.

Airport Lighting Working Principle

Airport lighting working principle for airfield lights is series current control, while apron floodlights and building lighting normally use ordinary parallel supply. Do not confuse the two systems.

Airport Lighting Types

Airport lighting types include airfield ground lighting for aircraft guidance, apron floodlighting for ground handling, obstruction lights and beacons, and terminal and road lighting, each with different standards.

Airport Lighting Functions

Airport lighting functions range from aircraft guidance on the airfield to safe working light on aprons and security and safety lighting around the airport.

Airport Lighting Selection Guide

Airport lighting selection guide: separate airfield lighting from general lighting, select each by its standard, and weigh energy use, maintenance access, life and spare parts availability.

Airport Lighting Technical Guide

Airport lighting technical guide: this site's AGL pages cover the technical side of airfield lighting, from basics to design, testing and troubleshooting, and link to the relevant standards.

Airport Lighting Engineering Guide

An airport lighting engineering guide covers load calculation, circuit design, cable sizing, regulator selection, control philosophy and standby power. See the AGL Engineering Design section above.

Airfield Lighting Engineering

Airfield lighting engineering combines electrical design, optics and photometry, civil installation, control systems and maintenance planning, all applied to meet aviation standards.

AGL Technical Guide

An AGL technical guide should explain the system in layers: field fixtures, series circuits, regulators, control and monitoring, and the standards and tests that prove compliance.

Requirements

Airfield Lighting Requirements, Operational Requirements & Selection

Airfield Lighting System Requirements

Airfield lighting system requirements follow from the runway type, approach category and operations. Annex 14 and national rules state what lights are needed, their performance, and the supply and monitoring they require.

Airfield Lighting Requirements

Airfield lighting requirements are set by ICAO Annex 14, national regulators such as DGCA in India, and the airport operator, and cover which lights are needed, their intensity and colour, and serviceability limits.

Airfield Lighting Operational Requirements

Airfield lighting operational requirements cover availability, response to failures, switching time for standby power, and procedures with air traffic control, especially for low-visibility operations.

Airfield Lighting Selection Guide

Airfield lighting selection guide: define the operational need, pick fixtures and light sources that meet the standard, then size circuits, regulators and control to suit, with spares and maintenance in mind.

Airfield Lighting Technical Guide

Airfield lighting technical guide: use the manufacturer's data with the design standard to check layout, photometry, electrical ratings and installation details before purchase and again at commissioning.

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist

Trace One Light Back to the Source

Learning Method

Pick a single fixture and follow it through transformer, cable, CCR and supply. Once you can walk that path on a drawing, faults stop looking random and start pointing to a place.

Constant Current Changes How You Read Faults

Series Circuit

Open circuits, earth faults and high-resistance joints behave differently from a constant-voltage system. Learn the symptom of each before you pick up a meter, and treat every series circuit as live until proved dead.

Most Field Faults Sit at Connections, Not in the Big Equipment

Fault Finding

Connectors, joints and secondary terminations fail far more often than CCRs or transformers. Check and test them first, and look for the water path that caused the failure.

A Light That Is On May Still Be Wrong

Photometry

Electrical health does not prove the right intensity, colour or aim. Verify optical performance and alignment, and keep commissioning data as a baseline.

Learn Which Document Answers Which Question

Standards

Design standards, equipment specifications and national rules serve different purposes. Cite the current edition, and say which framework governs when a project mixes them.

Records Are Part of the System

Documentation

Accurate as-builts, test results and fault logs make every later job faster and safer. Update them when the work is closed, not weeks later.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Airfield Ground Lighting basics.

Q1What is Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL)?

Airfield Ground Lighting is the system of visual aids and associated equipment used to guide aircraft during approach, landing, takeoff and movement on the airport surface, especially at night or in reduced visibility. It includes approach, runway and taxiway lighting, PAPI, illuminated signs, stop bars and runway guard lights, plus the CCRs, series-circuit cabling, isolating transformers, connectors and control and monitoring systems behind them.

Q2How does the AGL power and lighting chain work?

Airport power feeds a Constant Current Regulator (CCR), which supplies a primary series circuit. Along that circuit, each light fixture is fed by its own isolating transformer and secondary cable. Every fixture, whatever its category, traces back through this same chain.

Q3What is a Constant Current Regulator (CCR)?

A CCR is the major component that supplies controlled current to the series lighting circuit. It maintains the selected current level, provides multiple intensity steps, monitors operating conditions and gives protection and fault indication. A typical circuit is a 6.6 A series circuit.

Q4Why does AGL use a series circuit?

A series arrangement lets many lighting loads operate as part of one controlled circuit. Because the CCR controls circuit current directly, it also sets the brightness step for the whole circuit at once. This differs from ordinary constant-voltage building wiring.

Q5What does an isolating transformer do in an AGL circuit?

It sits between the primary series circuit and one light fixture, electrically separating the fixture circuit from the shared primary. It lets each fixture connect to the series circuit and supports continued operation of the rest of the circuit under appropriate fault conditions.

Q6What is the difference between the primary and secondary circuit?

The primary circuit is the high-voltage series circuit supplied by the CCR. The secondary circuit is the isolated supply from the secondary side of an isolating transformer to an individual fixture: CCR, primary cable, isolating transformer, secondary cable, then light fixture.

Q7What do the colours of airfield lights mean?

As a general orientation, runway edge lights are white, runway ends are red, thresholds are green, taxiway edges are blue, taxiway centrelines are green and stop bars are red. Exact colour, location, spacing and configuration depend on the applicable standard and the specific runway or taxiway layout.

Q8How does PAPI show whether an aircraft is on the approach slope?

PAPI uses four light units in a row. The balance of white and red changes in single-unit steps as the aircraft moves through the slope, and two white and two red means the aircraft is on the correct approach slope.

Q9What are common faults in an AGL series circuit?

Typical faults include open circuits (cable, connector or transformer connection failure), insulation or high-leakage failures, an individual fixture being off, CCR trips from earth faults or overcurrent, transformer problems and fixture failures. Continuity and insulation resistance tests are the usual first checks.

Q10Is a fixture acceptable just because it lights up?

No. A fixture's optical performance must meet the applicable requirements, including intensity, distribution, beam characteristics, colour, coverage, alignment and uniformity. Testing covers both the laboratory and the installed field condition.
