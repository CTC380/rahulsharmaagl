Overview

AGL Outage Response and Runway Closure

An Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) outage can affect the availability of a runway, taxiway, approach lighting system or other movement-area facility. Depending on the affected equipment and operating conditions, the outage may require operational restrictions, suspension of specific operations, a runway closure or activation of the airport’s contingency arrangements.

An effective outage response prioritizes operational safety, timely reporting, accurate technical information, coordination between engineering and airport operations, and compliance with approved procedures.

Technical status

What has failed, what remains available, and what corrective work is required?

Operational status

Which facilities or operations remain authorized under the airport’s applicable requirements?

A maintenance team may identify the technical fault, but a technical assessment alone does not authorize a runway to remain open or return to service.

This guide provides a general framework for planned and unplanned lighting outages, risk assessment, operational coordination, NOTAM processes, maintenance, testing and restoration.

Important: This is general educational guidance, not an airport-specific runway closure or reopening procedure. The airport’s approved contingency plan, applicable DGCA requirements, current aeronautical information, authorized operational decisions and established communication channels take precedence.

1 · Identify & Assess

Identify and Assess the AGL Outage

The first step is to establish what has failed and communicate the known information to the appropriate airport personnel.

An outage may involve a single light, several lights, an entire circuit, a CCR, the primary electrical supply, standby power, a control interface or a wider lighting system.

The initial report should distinguish confirmed facts from suspected causes. For example, a reported failure of several runway lights is an observation; a failed CCR is a diagnosis that requires supporting evidence.

Common types of AGL outage

Outage typeExamples of affected equipmentInitial assessment focus

Circuit failurePrimary series cable, circuit connection or associated equipmentIdentify the affected circuit and review available indications

Control-system failureALCMS, remote control interface or communications linkEstablish whether the indication concerns control, monitoring or field equipment

These categories help structure an investigation. They do not establish the operational consequence of a specific failure.

Do not delay an urgent operational notification while waiting for the complete technical diagnosis. Report the known facts through the approved process, then update the record as additional information becomes available.

2 · Risk Assessment

Airfield Lighting Outage Risk Assessment

An outage risk assessment considers how the loss or degraded performance of a lighting system affects the intended operation.

The assessment must consider the relevant runway or taxiway, the type and extent of the failure, operating conditions and the applicable airport requirements.

Factors to consider

FactorQuestions for the authorized assessment
Affected facilityWhich runway, taxiway, approach or other area is affected?
Lighting functionWhich specific visual aid or lighting component is unavailable or degraded?
Extent of failureHow many lights are affected, and where are they located?
Failure patternAre failures isolated, adjacent, distributed or associated with a common circuit?
Runway configurationWhat operational requirements apply to the affected runway and direction?
Operating conditionsAre night operations or low-visibility procedures relevant?
Other available systemsWhich other lighting or operational facilities remain available and serviceable?
Power availabilityAre primary power, standby power and required changeover functions available?
Applicable criteriaWhat serviceability and operational restrictions are defined by the governing documents?
DurationIs the fault expected to be repaired promptly, or will the outage continue?
Repair conditionsWhat access, isolation, equipment and work-zone protections are required?
CommunicationWhich authorized personnel and affected stakeholders must be notified?

The assessment should record the applicable requirements, evidence reviewed, decision-maker, controls and any limitations or follow-up actions.

Why low-visibility operations need specific review

Low-visibility operations may depend on the availability and serviceability of particular lighting systems, runway visual range equipment, power supplies and other facilities.

The required response can differ by runway category, operational approval and airport-specific Low Visibility Procedures (LVP).

The Airports Authority of India publishes aerodrome-specific information identifying required lighting and equipment, serviceability criteria, and operational restrictions. For example, some current Indian AIP entries specify different criteria for different runway lighting elements and require LVP to be suspended under defined conditions.

See the AAI Aeronautical Information Publication .

Do not use one airport’s lighting outage thresholds as a universal rule. The applicable aerodrome entry, current DGCA requirements, approved procedures and operational authorization determine the actual response.

Record the decision and its basis

Affected facility and known outage conditionApplicable operational and serviceability requirementsEvidence used in the assessmentRequired operational restrictions or controlsAuthorized decision-makerNotifications and acknowledgementsConditions requiring reassessmentCriteria and authority for restoration
Where the available information is incomplete, record the uncertainty and obtain the required assessment. Do not substitute an informal engineering judgement for the approved operational decision.
3 · Who Decides

Who Decides Whether a Runway Must Close?

A runway closure or operational restriction must follow the airport’s approved operational decision-making process.

Maintenance personnel are responsible for reporting technical conditions accurately, supporting the investigation and carrying out authorized work. They must not independently decide that a runway is operationally acceptable merely because a technical repair appears possible.

Depending on the airport’s organizational arrangements, operational decisions and associated notifications may involve the designated airport operations authority, air traffic services, engineering personnel and other authorized stakeholders.

General division of responsibilities

ActivityGeneral responsibility
Identify and report the faultPerson or team detecting the defect
Provide technical assessmentAuthorized AGL engineering or maintenance personnel
Assess operational implicationsRelevant authorized airport operational personnel
Apply operational restrictions or closureDesignated authority under the approved airport procedures
Coordinate maintenance accessResponsible airport operations and work-authorization functions
Carry out repair and testingAuthorized technical personnel
Process aeronautical informationPersonnel authorized under the applicable NOTAM process
Verify technical completionDesignated engineering or inspection authority
Authorize operational restorationAuthorized authority under the airport’s return-to-service procedure
The precise allocation of duties differs between airports. The applicable aerodrome manuals and local procedures govern.
4 · Response Procedure

Airport Lighting Outage Response Procedure

The following framework can support the preparation of an airport-specific outage response procedure. It must be adapted, reviewed and approved before operational use.

Phase 1 — Detection and Reporting

An outage may be detected through:

ALCMS alarm or monitoring indicationCCR alarm or abnormal operating indicationField inspectionPilot or air traffic services reportAirport operations or maintenance reportFailure of primary or standby powerA defect discovered during planned maintenance

Record the observed condition, time, affected asset and any available supporting information.

Phase 2 — Immediate Notification

Report the condition through the airport’s approved operational and engineering communication channels.

Provide the known facts, identify any uncertainty and follow instructions from the authorized personnel. Do not wait for a full root-cause investigation before reporting a potentially significant operational issue.

Where required, the designated operational authority will determine the interim restrictions, notifications or contingency measures.

Phase 3 — Operational Assessment

Authorized personnel assess the available technical information against the applicable operational criteria.

The assessment should establish which systems are affected, whether required serviceability criteria remain satisfied, and what restrictions or suspension of operations may be necessary.

Record the decision and the authority responsible for it.

Phase 4 — Maintenance Planning and Authorization

Once the permitted work scope and operational arrangements are established, the maintenance team should:

Confirm the affected equipment and circuitIdentify the suspected fault and required diagnostic workObtain the required permit and access authorizationDefine isolation and hazardous-energy controlsConfirm the work-zone boundaries and communicationsArrange tools, test instruments, spare parts and competent personnelEstablish the required verification and handback process

Work must stay within the authorized scope. Any material change requires reassessment and the relevant authorization.

Phase 5 — Fault Investigation and Repair

Authorized personnel investigate the fault under the approved maintenance and testing procedures.

The investigation may involve inspection of the affected fixture, connectors, cables, transformer, CCR, control interface or associated power equipment, depending on the evidence.

Record observations, test results and the confirmed cause where established. Do not present an unverified suspected cause as a proven finding.

Phase 6 — Verification and Handover

After the corrective work, complete the required inspections, tests and functional checks.

Record the results, any remaining defects, the technical completion status and required approvals. Notify the designated authority through the approved process.

Technical completion is not, by itself, operational authorization to use the affected runway or facility.

Phase 7 — Operational Restoration

The authorized authority assesses the required information and follows the airport’s formal restoration process.

This may involve confirmation of technical serviceability, closure of relevant restrictions, updates to aeronautical information and communication to affected airport personnel. The exact steps depend on the airport’s approved procedure and the outage involved.

5 · Contingency Plan

Airport Lighting Outage Contingency Plan

An airport should have documented arrangements defining the response to an AGL failure, including who reports it, who assesses it, how operational decisions are made, how maintenance is authorized and how restoration is confirmed.

Recommended contingency plan contents

SectionInformation to include
Purpose and scopeLighting systems and outage events covered
ReportingFault-reporting channels, required information and escalation route
Responsibility matrixTechnical, operational, safety and information-management responsibilities
Initial assessmentRequired information, risk-assessment process and decision authority
Operational restrictionsReference to applicable procedures and criteria

Maintenance authorizationWork permits, access, isolation and work-zone arrangements
Backup powerApproved response to supply, generator, UPS or transfer failures
Technical investigationApplicable procedures, tools, tests and records

Incident documentationTime line, findings, decisions, corrective actions and closure
Post-incident reviewRoot cause, recurrence prevention and procedure improvements
The plan should provide a clear interface between engineering and airport operations. It should also address events that affect several systems at once, such as a shared power supply or control-system failure.

Backup power and changeover failures

The loss of primary power may affect several AGL systems. Conversely, a failure of the standby supply or changeover function can create a separate operational concern even if the normal supply remains available.

The contingency plan should identify the systems supported by backup power, the required performance criteria, the responsible personnel and the response to failure of the backup arrangement.

Do not assume that a generator or UPS is operationally adequate merely because it starts or displays a normal status. The applicable procedure and verification criteria determine the required checks.

6 · NOTAM & Information

NOTAM and Aeronautical Information Management

Aeronautical information must accurately reflect relevant changes in the availability of aerodrome facilities.

The Airports Authority of India lists interruption or return to operation of major components of aerodrome lighting systems among the information promulgated by NOTAM. See the official AAI explanation of information promulgated by NOTAM .

When a NOTAM process may be required

Depending on the applicable aeronautical information requirements and the nature of the change, the process may involve information about:

Runway closure or operational restrictionsRunway edge, centreline, threshold or runway-end lighting unserviceabilityApproach lighting system unserviceabilityTaxiway lighting or other relevant visual aidsStandby power supply or other significant equipment unserviceabilityPlanned works affecting the availability of aerodrome facilitiesRestoration of equipment or facilities previously reported unavailable
The exact reporting requirement, wording, duration and dissemination process must be established by the authorized personnel using the applicable regulations and aeronautical information procedures.

Responsibilities for NOTAM actions

The maintenance team should promptly provide accurate technical information through the airport’s designated process. The responsible authorized personnel determine whether a NOTAM or other formal aeronautical information action is required and ensure that the applicable process is followed.

A NOTAM should not be issued, extended, amended or cancelled solely on the basis of an informal maintenance message. Equally, completing a technical repair does not automatically remove a published restriction.

For comparison, the FAA’s official guidance on lighting-aid NOTAMs describes the reporting of specific runway and taxiway lighting systems. That guidance applies within its US framework and should not be substituted for Indian requirements.

Information consistency

The outage record, maintenance status, operational communication and aeronautical information should be consistent. Before concluding the information-management part of an outage, verify that:

The reported system and location are correctThe restriction or serviceability information accurately reflects the approved decisionRelevant stakeholders have been informed as requiredAny required follow-up information has been processed through the authorized channelRestoration information is handled through the same controlled process

7 · Planned Closure

Planned Runway Closure for AGL Maintenance

Planned maintenance may require an approved work window, temporary lighting unavailability or a formal runway closure.

Planning should begin early enough to coordinate engineering work, airport operations, required permits, access arrangements and any applicable aeronautical information.

During planned maintenance

The team should remain within the authorized work area and scope. Maintain communication with the responsible airport authority and keep the work status current. If the work scope changes, a new defect is discovered or the work cannot be completed as planned, record the condition and follow the applicable reassessment and notification process. Do not extend the outage or alter the operational status independently.

If work cannot be completed within the approved window

Notify the designated authority before the authorized work period expires, or as soon as the situation becomes apparent. The subsequent decision, such as further restriction, extension of the maintenance window or another approved arrangement, must follow the airport’s procedures. Update work records, operational information and any necessary aeronautical information through the authorized process.

8 · Unplanned Failure

Unplanned Runway Lighting Failure

An unexpected failure during operations requires prompt communication and an assessment based on the actual condition and applicable operational requirements.

The initial response should focus on reporting and containment through authorized channels rather than attempting an immediate repair without coordination.

9 · Post-Maintenance Checks

AGL Operational Checks Following Corrective Maintenance

Before a technical restoration can be accepted, the required inspections and tests must be completed under the applicable approved procedure.

The test scope should reflect the actual work performed and the affected equipment. A single successful test may not cover all relevant failure modes or establish that the entire system is serviceable.

Possible verification activities

Verification areaExample of relevant evidence
Repaired fixtureInspection record and required functional check
Circuit conditionApplicable electrical or circuit test records
CCRRequired status, alarm and functional-check results
Control and monitoringRequired command, indication and interface checks
Backup powerRequired verification of the affected supply or changeover functions
Lighting performanceRequired visual or photometric assessment, where specified
InstallationConfirmation against approved drawings and equipment requirements
Defect closureRecord of the identified cause, corrective action and verification
DocumentationUpdated work order, equipment record and controlled drawing, where required

Acceptance criteria

Testing must use the specified method and acceptance criteria for the equipment and work performed. Avoid using generic test values or assumptions about how much of a lighting circuit must be operational.

For runways intended for low-visibility operations, verify the airport-specific serviceability requirements and operational restrictions. A lighting arrangement that supports one operating condition does not necessarily support a more demanding one.

Record the test results

Work order and equipment identificationFault reference and confirmed cause, where establishedTests and inspections performedTest procedure and specification referenceInstrument identification and calibration details where requiredActual measured results and units, where applicableAcceptance criteria and resultOutstanding defects or limitationsResponsible inspector or reviewerRequired approval and closure record

10 · Return to Service

Return-to-Service and Runway Reopening

Technical restoration and operational reopening are separate decisions and should be documented accordingly.

Technical restoration

Establishes that the required maintenance has been completed and the equipment has passed the prescribed inspections and tests.

Operational restoration

Establishes that the responsible airport authority has completed the required process to remove applicable restrictions or authorize use of the affected facility.

Return-to-service checklist

Technical completion

Corrective work completed or remaining work formally documented.
Required inspection and testing completed.
Equipment and circuit status verified against applicable criteria.
CCR and control-system indications checked where required.
Relevant defects and restrictions reviewed.
Work area cleared and required safeguards managed through the approved procedure.
0 / 6 complete

Records & authorization

Technical records and test reports completed.
Engineering or inspection acceptance recorded as required.
Operational authority notified of the verified technical status.
Required aeronautical information actions completed through authorized personnel.
Formal operational restoration recorded by the responsible authority.
Relevant personnel informed of the updated status.
0 / 6 complete
Not every item applies to every outage; the airport’s approved process determines the required checks and approvals.

Who authorizes reopening?

The decision follows the airport’s authorized operational procedure. Maintenance personnel provide evidence of technical completion and identify any remaining limitations, but must not independently declare a runway reopened unless that authority has explicitly been assigned under the governing procedure.

A completed work order, a normal CCR display or a single successful field test does not automatically establish operational readiness.
11 · Communication

AGL Outage Communication and Reporting

Clear communication reduces ambiguity during an outage, especially when several airport departments are involved.

The report should separate the observed condition, suspected cause, confirmed diagnosis, operational decision, corrective work and current status.

Suggested outage status categories

Use the airport’s approved terminology. If a reporting framework is being developed, it may be helpful to distinguish between:

These are suggested tracking labels, not prescribed aviation status codes. Use existing airport terminology wherever it is defined.

13 · Post-Incident Review

Post-Incident Review and Reliability Improvement

Once the outage has been resolved, review the event to identify opportunities to reduce recurrence and improve the response process.

The review should consider the time line, technical cause where established, operational effects, communications, availability of spare parts, maintenance response and adequacy of the contingency arrangements.

Questions for the post-incident review

Was the failure reported promptly through the correct channel?Was the affected equipment and circuit identified accurately?Were the appropriate people notified?Were the operational implications assessed under the approved procedure?Was access and maintenance work properly authorized?Were suitable tools, spares and competent personnel available?Was the root cause established or does further investigation remain necessary?Were the required tests and acceptance records completed?Were technical and operational statuses clearly distinguished?Were aeronautical information actions handled by the authorized personnel?Was the restoration process completed and documented?Are changes needed in preventive maintenance, training, spares or procedures?

Reliability indicators

An airport or maintenance organization may track indicators such as:

Number and duration of AGL outagesRepeat failure rate by asset or equipment typeTime from report to technical assessmentTime from authorization to corrective actionTime to complete required verificationAvailability of critical spare partsRecurring defects awaiting permanent correctionQuality and completeness of outage recordsCompletion of corrective and preventive actions
These indicators should be interpreted in context. A short repair time is not a success if required safety controls, testing or operational authorization have been bypassed.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What should happen when an AGL circuit fails?

Report the fault through the approved airport process, identify the affected system and review available technical information. Authorized personnel assess the operational implications, establish any required restrictions and coordinate the approved repair and restoration process.

Q2Who decides whether a runway must close?

The decision follows the airport’s authorized operational procedures and applicable requirements. Engineering personnel provide the technical assessment, while the designated authority makes the operational decision under the established arrangements.

Q3Does every AGL outage require a runway closure?

Not every outage has the same operational consequence. The response depends on the failed lighting function, the extent and location of the failure, runway category, operating conditions and applicable serviceability requirements. The authorized assessment determines the required action.

Q4Can a runway remain open if some runway lights have failed?

That decision cannot be made from a generic percentage or a general article. It depends on the applicable requirements, current operating conditions, the affected lighting elements and the airport’s authorized procedure.

Q5When is a NOTAM required for a lighting outage?

The requirement depends on the applicable aeronautical information rules and the nature of the outage or operational change. The authorized personnel must determine, prepare and process the required information through the established channel.

Q6Who should cancel or update a NOTAM after repair?

The process must be handled by the authorized personnel under the applicable aeronautical information procedure. A maintenance message or completed work order does not automatically cancel a published restriction.

Q7What checks are needed after AGL maintenance?

Complete the inspections, tests and functional checks required by the approved procedure for the affected equipment and work. Record results against the specified acceptance criteria and address any outstanding defects before submitting the required restoration information.

Q8Can the maintenance engineer reopen the runway after a successful test?

Only where that authority has explicitly been assigned under the airport’s governing procedures. In general, technical verification and operational reopening are separate steps.

Q9Why are airport-specific outage criteria important?

Different lighting elements and operational conditions have different requirements. Indian AIP entries demonstrate that serviceability criteria and restrictions can vary by aerodrome, runway category and low-visibility procedure. Always use the applicable current information for the airport concerned.

Q10Why is an airfield lighting risk assessment needed after an outage?

An airfield lighting risk assessment judges how a lighting failure affects aircraft operations: which lights are lost, what visibility and approach category apply, and what mitigation is possible. It informs whether operations continue with restrictions or the runway must close. It should be carried out by the responsible airport staff against the airport's own procedures.

Q11What does airfield lighting risk management involve?

Airfield lighting risk management means identifying lighting failure scenarios, assessing their effect on operations, setting controls such as redundancy, spares and response procedures, and reviewing results after incidents. It supports the contingency plan and keeps outage decisions consistent rather than improvised.

Q12When is a runway closure for lighting maintenance required?

A runway closure for lighting maintenance is planned when work cannot be done safely while aircraft use the runway, for example excavation or circuit work in the movement area. The closure is coordinated with air traffic control and airport operations, published by NOTAM where required, and the runway is reopened only after checks are complete.

Q13What are airfield lighting operational checks after repair?

Airfield lighting operational checks confirm that repaired circuits work as intended before the runway returns to service. They typically cover control from the normal and backup positions, brightness steps, circuit current and a visual check for outages. The airport's return-to-service procedure sets the exact content and who signs off.

Q14What is airport movement area safety during lighting work?

Airport movement area safety during lighting work means controlling access to runways, taxiways and aprons so people, vehicles and aircraft do not conflict. It relies on air traffic control clearance, work permits, escorts, marked work zones and communication discipline. Follow the airport's own rules for the movement area.

Q15What is an airport lighting failure contingency plan?

An airport lighting failure contingency plan sets out in advance what to do when lights fail: who to inform, how to assess operational effect, which backup supplies or portable lights may be used, and what notices to issue. Airport lighting failure contingency arrangements should be tested and reviewed after real events.

Final Note

Final Note

An airfield lighting outage requires coordinated technical and operational management. Accurate reporting, a documented risk assessment, authorized decisions, controlled maintenance, prescribed testing and formal restoration help ensure that a fault is managed without confusing technical completion with operational availability.

Use this guide as an educational framework for developing an airport-specific procedure. All actual decisions about runway closure, operational restrictions, NOTAM actions and reopening must follow the applicable airport procedures and authorized decision-making process.

Official reference resources

1. Airports Authority of India — Information promulgated by NOTAM — includes interruption or return to operation of major components of aerodrome lighting systems.

2. AAI Aeronautical Information Publication (AIP) — current aerodrome-specific facilities, procedures and relevant lighting serviceability information; check the applicable current publication and amendments.

3. FAA — Lighting Aid and Obstruction NOTAMs — reference for US lighting-aid NOTAM processes; not a substitute for Indian aeronautical information requirements.

Identify which lights are affected, the runway in use and the visibility conditions. The impact decides urgency and who must be informed.

Know the Decision Chain in Advance
Authority

Define who can decide on restrictions or closure and how to contact them. Searching for names during an outage costs time.

Issue Accurate Information Quickly
NOTAM

Clear information about what is unavailable helps pilots and controllers. Update it when the situation changes.

Prepare Spares and Procedures for Likely Failures
Preparedness

Review past outages and make sure the parts, access and procedures for common failures are ready.

Verify Before Returning to Service
Return to Service

Confirm function and, where required, photometric performance before the facility is declared available.

Review Every Event Afterwards
Learning

Hold a short review to capture causes and improvements. Close actions so the same outage does not recur.
