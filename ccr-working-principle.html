CCR Working Principle in Airport Lighting

How a Constant Current Regulator controls the series circuit — current steps, wiring, remote control, fault indications, troubleshooting, efficiency and commissioning.

Overview

What Is a CCR?

A Constant Current Regulator supplies and controls the current in a series-circuit AGL system.

A Constant Current Regulator (CCR) regulates the series-circuit current to a selected operating value, unlike a conventional supply that mainly maintains a fixed voltage. This allows many fixtures to share one series circuit while giving selectable lighting intensity.

The CCR is not simply a power source. It is part of a complete system: series circuit, cables, isolation transformers, connectors, fixtures, control system and monitoring system.

The output voltage is not necessarily constant — it changes with the electrical characteristics and total load of the connected series circuit. That is the fundamental principle of CCR operation.

Purpose

Why Is a CCR Used in Airport Lighting?

Runways, taxiways and approach systems have many fixtures spread over a large area.

Controlled light intensity

Different CCR current steps give different lighting intensity levels.

Long-distance distribution

One series circuit supplies numerous fixtures around the airfield through individual isolation transformers.

Controlled operation

Operate from local controls or integrate into an airport lighting control system.

Monitoring

Monitored CCRs detect selected abnormal conditions in the regulator or series circuit.

System integration

The CCR forms part of the larger airport electrical and lighting-control architecture.

Core Concept

CCR Working Principle

Remember one principle: the CCR regulates current, while the connected series circuit determines the voltage needed to maintain it.

Current = controlled

The CCR holds the selected series current (for example 6.6 A).

Voltage = load-dependent

Higher circuit impedance needs higher output voltage; lower impedance needs less.

V = I × Z

V is circuit voltage, I is series current, Z is circuit impedance.

Try it: V = I × Z at a fixed 6.6 A

Drag the slider to change total series-circuit impedance. The CCR holds current; the required output voltage follows the load.

Common misunderstanding: "A 6.6 A CCR should always produce the same output voltage." That is not correct. Always evaluate CCR output voltage together with current and circuit condition.

Input & Output

CCR Input and Output

Two major electrical sides.

CCR Input

Connects to the airport distribution system. May include incoming feeder, circuit protection, main switching, surge protection, control power, grounding and bonding, and input monitoring.

CCR Output

Connects to the series circuit: output terminals, series cables, primary connectors, isolation transformers, secondary connectors and fixtures. Output voltage can be substantial on a long circuit — treat it as a high-energy system.

Monitoring & Faults

CCR Monitoring and Fault Codes

Monitoring adds a layer of awareness where faults affect operational availability.

Depending on equipment and specification, monitored conditions may include input power loss, regulator protective shutdown, significant series-circuit load loss, failure to maintain selected current and selected lamp-load failure conditions.

There is no universal CCR fault-code language: one manufacturer's F01 may mean something entirely different from another's. Always use the manufacturer's fault-code table.

Possible causes

  • Broken primary cable
  • Disconnected connector
  • Failed isolation transformer / open winding
  • Incorrect termination
  • Damaged cable joint
  • Open switching device
  • Disconnected fixture circuit

Short-circuit / over-current

Possible causes

  • Wiring fault
  • Incorrect connection
  • Damaged equipment
  • Circuit fault
  • Internal CCR malfunction
  • Switching abnormality

Low output current

Possible causes

  • Control setting
  • Input supply
  • Current sensing
  • Regulation system
  • Protection status
  • Output circuit / series-circuit load

High output voltage

Possible causes

  • Open or partially open series circuit
  • Disconnected transformer
  • Failed component / missing load
  • Incorrect circuit configuration
  • High circuit impedance
  • CCR control problem

An open circuit leaves no complete current path, so the CCR may be unable to maintain the selected current; its protection and monitoring may detect it. Never bypass CCR protection during troubleshooting. A low-current problem is not automatically a cable fault — investigate both the regulator and the whole circuit. A high voltage by itself does not mean the CCR is defective: always compare output voltage with output current.

Performance

CCR Efficiency, Power Factor & Losses

Related but different.

Efficiency

Efficiency (%) = Output Power ÷ Input Power × 100. Example: 9.2 kW out for 10 kW in = 92%. Depends on design, loading, current step, input conditions, power factor, temperature and manufacturer technology.

Power factor

Relates real power to apparent power. A CCR can have good efficiency yet a different power-factor characteristic; review both.

Heating & losses

Heat, switching, transformer, semiconductor, control and magnetic losses. CCR rooms and vaults need suitable thermal conditions; excess temperature shortens life and can cause abnormal operation.

Evaluate against the applicable equipment specification and manufacturer's published data.

LED

CCR and LED Lighting

LED fixtures have changed the electrical behaviour of AGL circuits.

LED systems contain electronic power supplies that can introduce different power factor, VA characteristics, current waveform, harmonic behaviour, startup characteristics and dynamic loading. An LED retrofit should include a compatibility review of LED fixture + isolation transformer + series circuit + CCR — especially when replacing many traditional fixtures on an existing circuit.

Troubleshooting

CCR Troubleshooting Method

The location where the symptom appears is not necessarily where the fault originates.

1. Check the CCR

Input power, status, selected intensity, output current and voltage, fault indication, local/remote mode.

2. Check the series circuit

Continuity, open circuit, ground fault, cable condition, circuit configuration.

3. Check the isolation transformer

Primary and secondary connection, condition, correct rating, insulation, moisture ingress.

4. Check connectors

Primary and secondary connectors, plug/receptacle condition, moisture, mechanical damage.

5. Check the fixture

Lamp/LED, optical system, internal wiring, lens, housing, physical condition.

6. Retest

Confirm CCR current, operation, fixture operation and monitoring; photometry where applicable.

Single-light fault → investigate local components first (secondary connector, transformer, fixture). Multiple-light fault → investigate common upstream components first (CCR, series circuit, primary cable, common switching, control system). Replacing components based only on visual symptoms is far less reliable than measuring.

Fault Finding

Airport Lighting Fault Finding: Detection, Diagnosis, Earth Fault & Short Circuit

How a fault is noticed, located and confirmed, using the CCR as the starting point.

How Is an Airport Lighting Fault Found?

Start with where the symptom appears, which is not necessarily where the fault originates. One light out points to local components first: the secondary connector, transformer and fixture. Several lights out point to common upstream items first: the CCR, series circuit, primary cable, common switching and control system. Then follow the electrical path and measure at each point, because replacing parts on visual symptoms alone is far less reliable.

Airport Lighting Fault Detection

Airport lighting fault detection depends on the equipment and specification. A CCR with monitoring may detect input power loss, protective shutdown, significant series-circuit load loss, failure to maintain the selected current and lamp-load failure conditions, and report them to the control system. See ALCMS for how these signals reach the operator.

Airport Lighting Fault Monitor

An airport lighting fault monitor is the monitoring function, in the CCR or the control and monitoring system, that reports status, alarms and fault codes. There is no universal fault-code language, so always use the manufacturer's fault-code table. A monitor shows that a fault exists and roughly where, and it does not replace measurement in the field.

Airport Lighting Fault Diagnosis

Airport lighting fault diagnosis turns the symptom into a cause. Check the CCR first, then the series circuit, the isolation transformer, the connectors and the fixture, and retest. Final diagnosis needs measured values and the manufacturer's service procedure. A high output voltage by itself does not mean the CCR is defective, so compare output voltage with output current.

Airport Lighting Earth Fault

An airport lighting earth fault, also called a ground fault, is an unintended path from the series circuit to earth. When checking the series circuit, look at continuity, open circuit, ground fault and cable condition, and check the transformer and connectors for moisture ingress and insulation condition.

Airport Lighting Short Circuit

An airport lighting short circuit, or over-current condition, can come from a wiring fault, incorrect connection, damaged equipment, a circuit fault, an internal CCR malfunction or a switching abnormality. The CCR protection may operate. Never bypass CCR protection during troubleshooting.

Field Examples

Practical Fault Examples

The location where the symptom appears is not necessarily the location where the fault originates — follow the complete electrical path.

One runway light is not operating

Start at the fixture and work back along the path:

  • Is the fixture physically intact?
  • Is the secondary connection secure?
  • Is the transformer healthy?
  • Is the primary connection intact?
  • Is the series cable continuous?
  • Is the CCR delivering the correct current?
  • Is the circuit correctly commanded by the control system?

A complete section of lights is not operating

First question: is the problem individual or common?

  • Many lights failing together → common upstream items first: CCR, series circuit, primary cable, common switching, control system, upstream connection
  • Only one fixture failing → local items first: secondary connector, transformer, fixture, local connection
This approach prevents unnecessary replacement of expensive components.

Pitfalls

8 Common CCR Engineering Mistakes

1. Treating the CCR like a fixed-voltage supply

It is fundamentally a current-regulating device.

2. Looking only at the fixture

The fault may be upstream.

3. Ignoring transformer losses

Consider the complete series-circuit load.

4. Using generic current settings

Verify the actual CCR configuration.

5. Ignoring LED compatibility

LED fixtures can behave differently from traditional lamps.

6. Replacing before measuring

Electrical measurements should guide diagnosis.

7. Ignoring historical fault patterns

Repeated failures can indicate a common circuit or equipment problem.

8. Treating control faults as power faults

The regulator may be healthy while the control interface is defective.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is a Constant Current Regulator in airport lighting?

A CCR is an electrical power-control device that regulates current through a series-circuit AGL system.

Q2How does a CCR work in airport lighting?

The CCR measures and controls the output current so that the selected current level is maintained. Its output voltage varies according to the electrical load of the series circuit.

Q3Why does airport lighting use a CCR?

A CCR allows many AGL fixtures to operate on a common series circuit while providing controlled lighting intensity.

Q4What is the common AGL CCR current?

6.6 A is a widely used AGL series-circuit current. Other current classes and configurations are also used.

Q5What is a CCR series circuit?

A continuous AGL circuit in which the CCR supplies controlled current through primary cables and the primaries of isolation transformers.

Q6What does the isolation transformer do?

It electrically separates the series primary circuit from the secondary side feeding the individual AGL fixture.

Q7Why does CCR output voltage change?

Because the CCR regulates current. The voltage required depends on the total impedance of the connected series circuit.

Q8What are CCR brightness steps?

Predefined CCR current settings used to provide different lighting intensity levels.

Q9What is an L-828 CCR?

An L-828 is a constant current regulator without integral monitoring under the FAA equipment classification.

Q10What is an L-829 CCR?

An L-829 is a constant current regulator with monitoring under the FAA equipment classification.

Q11What is an L-827?

L-827 is the FAA designation associated with a regulator monitor.

Q12What are CCR fault codes?

Indications generated by a particular CCR to identify abnormal operating conditions. The exact codes differ by manufacturer.

Q13What causes a CCR to trip?

Possible causes include abnormal output conditions, over-current, open-circuit conditions, protection activation, internal faults or control problems.

Q14What causes low CCR current?

Possible causes include regulator faults, sensing issues, supply problems, protection operation or abnormal circuit conditions.

Q15What causes high CCR output voltage?

Possible causes include high circuit impedance, an open or partially open series circuit, disconnected loads, transformer problems or regulator malfunction.

Q16How is CCR efficiency calculated?

Output Power ÷ Input Power × 100. The required performance depends on the applicable specification and equipment design.

Q17Can CCRs operate with LED AGL fixtures?

Yes, but compatibility must be verified. LED fixtures can have different electrical characteristics from traditional incandescent loads.

Q18Is CCR testing the same as photometric testing?

No. CCR testing evaluates electrical power regulation and control. Photometric testing evaluates the optical performance of the lighting fixtures.

Q19How do I troubleshoot an airport lighting circuit?

Start with the CCR, verify current and voltage, then trace the series circuit through the cable, connectors, isolation transformers and light fixtures.
