Division 03 of 25 · INS-TWY Series

Taxiway Lighting Inspection

A standardized methodology for assessing the operational, physical, optical, electrical, mechanical, geometric, and environmental condition of taxiway lighting and associated visual guidance equipment. Covers edge, centreline, clearance bar, exit/junction, rapid-exit, curve, lead-on/lead-off, and intermediate holding position lighting, plus fixture geometry, mechanics, optics, cable/connector interfaces, environmental condition, illumination/brightness, photometric condition, and defect recording.

Initial Issue

Important control — Taxiway lighting inspection shall consider not only whether a fixture is illuminated, but also whether the lighting continues to provide correct, continuous, reliable, and operationally meaningful visual guidance. A visual inspection does not replace formal photometric testing, electrical testing, insulation-resistance testing, circuit testing, CCR testing, preventive maintenance, or detailed engineering assessment where these are required.

Quick Reference

A short answer on the taxiway lighting inspection checklist, taken from the standard parameters in this division. Acceptance criteria come from the approved design and current standard.

Taxiway lighting inspection checklist

A taxiway lighting inspection checklist assesses each fixture against the standard parameters: operational status, illumination, brightness, colour, spacing, location, height or elevation, orientation, alignment, frangibility, mounting and stability, lens and prism condition, secondary cable, connector, base can, cable entry, mechanical condition, contamination, water ingress, corrosion, photometric condition and defect status. The workflow runs from layout review through classification, recording, escalation, correction and verification to close-out.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about taxiway lighting inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook.

Q1What is Division 03 — Taxiway Lighting Inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook?

Division 03 contains 89 published topics in 17 sections (A–Q). It gives a standardized methodology for assessing the operational, physical, optical, electrical, mechanical, geometric and environmental condition of taxiway lighting and associated visual guidance equipment.

Q2Which taxiway lighting types does Division 03 cover?

It covers taxiway edge, centreline, clearance bar, exit and junction, rapid exit, curve, lead-on/lead-off and intermediate holding position lighting. Further sections cover fixture geometry, mechanical condition, optical and lens condition, electrical connection and cabling, environmental condition, illumination and brightness, photometric condition, and defect identification and recording.

Q3What parameters is each taxiway fixture assessed against?

The common checklist includes operational status, illumination, brightness, colour, spacing, location, height/elevation, orientation, alignment, frangibility, mounting stability, lens/prism, secondary cable, connector, base can, cable entry, contamination, water ingress, corrosion and photometric condition, each with defined status values.

Q4What is the standard taxiway lighting inspection workflow?

The sequence is review layout, identify lighting type, inspect location and spacing, check height/elevation, check orientation and alignment, verify illumination, and inspect optical, mechanical and cable/connector condition. It then checks water and contamination, assesses photometric condition, and finishes with classify, record, escalate, correct, verify and close.

Q5How are taxiway lighting defects classified?

Defect status is recorded using the four-level scheme Normal, Defective, Unserviceable and Immediate Action Required, so every observation ends with a clear classification before it is recorded and escalated.

Q6Why does Division 03 check more than whether a taxiway light is illuminated?

Taxiway lighting inspection considers not only whether a fixture is illuminated but whether the lighting continues to provide correct, continuous, reliable and operationally meaningful visual guidance. The checklist therefore records location, spacing, height/elevation, orientation and alignment, and treats illumination, brightness and colour as separate checks.

Q7Can a visual taxiway lighting inspection replace formal testing?

No. A visual inspection does not replace formal photometric testing, electrical testing, insulation-resistance testing, circuit testing, CCR testing, preventive maintenance or detailed engineering assessment where these are required.

Q8Do airport procedures take precedence over this handbook?

Yes. This handbook is an uncontrolled educational reference, not an approved procedure. The airport's approved inspection schedule, SMS procedures, ATC instructions, NOTAM procedures and local aerodrome regulations always take precedence, and the current revision of any official document should be verified before use.

Q9Can I download a printable checklist for each inspection topic?

Yes. Each inspection topic has a Download as Word option that produces an uncontrolled Word checklist template (description, a tick-box inspection table and an inspector sign-off block) for personal reference and internal training, to be reviewed and adopted under the user’s own document control.

Q10What is on a taxiway lighting inspection checklist?

A taxiway lighting inspection checklist covers operational status, illumination, brightness, colour, spacing, location, height, orientation, alignment, frangibility, mounting, lens and prism, secondary cable, connector, base can, cable entry, contamination, water ingress, corrosion, photometric condition and defect status, followed by classification, recording, escalation, correction and verification.
