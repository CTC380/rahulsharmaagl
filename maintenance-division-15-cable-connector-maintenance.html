Cable & Connector Maintenance

The complete AGL electrical connection chain — from the CCR through the primary cable network, cable joints, isolation transformer, secondary lead, and connector, to the lighting fixture itself. The objective is not only to maintain individual cables or connectors, but to maintain the complete electrical continuity and mechanical / environmental integrity of the AGL series circuit.

Core principle: maintain the complete path, not just the failed component. When a cable/connector fault is identified, investigate the complete chain rather than automatically replacing the visibly defective component.

Core Principle

A professional maintenance procedure should be able to trace a failure from the fixture back through the complete electrical path, identify the actual failure point, repair it, electrically test it, restore the mechanical/environmental protection and finally verify the complete AGL circuit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about cable & connector maintenance, based on the topics in this division.

8 QUESTIONS
Q1What is cable and connector maintenance in the AGL Maintenance Handbook?

It covers the complete AGL electrical connection chain from the CCR through the primary cable network, cable joints, isolation transformer, secondary lead and connector to the lighting fixture itself.

Q2What does Division 15 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 15 (Cable & Connector Maintenance) contains 42 topics in four groups: Group A Primary Cable Maintenance (15 topics), Group B Cable Joint Maintenance (9 topics), Group C Connector Maintenance (12 topics) and Group D Secondary Lead Maintenance (6 topics).

Q3What is the core principle of this division?

Maintain the complete path, not just the failed component. When a cable or connector fault is identified, investigate the complete chain rather than automatically replacing the visibly damaged part.

Q4What is the primary-to-secondary chain?

CCR, primary cable, cable joint, isolation transformer, secondary lead, connector and fixture. A cable system is treated as a complete transmission path, not a collection of independent components.

Q5What is the 13-step maintenance workflow?

Identify Circuit, Identify Component, Inspect Route, Inspect Cable, Inspect Joint, Inspect Transformer Interface, Inspect Secondary Lead, Inspect Connector, Repair/Replace, Test, Restore Protection, Functional Verification and Update Records.

Q6How do you trace a fixture that is not operating?

The fault-trace logic checks the fixture, secondary connector, secondary lead, isolation transformer and its connections, primary cable, cable joint and termination, the complete primary circuit and the CCR or series circuit before identifying the failure location. This prevents unnecessary fixture replacement when the failure is elsewhere in the circuit.

Q7What does the cable and connector decision matrix recommend?

For example, a missing or damaged cable marker is replaced, joint moisture means correcting the sealing or source, connector contamination is cleaned, connector overheating is investigated and repaired, a damaged secondary lead is replaced, an abnormal insulation test is investigated and a continuity failure is located and repaired.

Q8How are cable and connector conditions classified?

Good (routine PM), Acceptable (monitor), Deteriorating (planned maintenance), Defective (corrective maintenance) and Critical (priority or emergency response). The classification is used consistently so condition can be trended across Division 03 (Predictive) and Division 04 (Condition-Based) maintenance.
