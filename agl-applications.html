AGL APPLICATIONS

Airfield Ground Lighting is not a single, fixed specification. What a commercial hub needs, a regional strip, a military airfield, a heliport or a disaster-relief runway needs are all different — in scale, redundancy, category of operation and budget. This page walks through how AGL requirements change by airport type and project type, and links to a dedicated guide for each.

Browse Applications Discuss a Project
01 · Why This Matters

One System, Many Contexts

The fundamentals of AGL don't change — but the application does. A runway's ICAO reference code, its category of approach (non-precision, CAT I, CAT II/III), its traffic density, its operating budget and its operating environment all shape which fixtures, circuits, control philosophy and redundancy level actually make sense.

Specifying a full CAT III approach lighting system for a general aviation strip is as much a mismatch as under-speccing a CCR bank for a busy international runway. Matching the AGL solution to the application is where engineering judgement — not just a standards checklist — does the work.

02 · By Airport Type & Project Type

Explore AGL by Application

Nine ways AGL work is commissioned — by the kind of airport it serves, or the kind of project it is. Each links to a dedicated page going deeper into that application's specific requirements, standards and challenges.

Commercial Aviation

Commercial Airports

Full-service passenger airports handling scheduled traffic, typically ICAO Code C/D with standard runway and taxiway lighting, PAPI, and redundant CCR configurations.

  • Standard runway/taxiway edge, threshold & centreline systems
  • Redundant CCR / changeover arrangements where the design calls for them
  • Scheduled inspection & maintenance cycles
Explore Commercial Airport Lighting
Commercial Aviation

International Airports

High-traffic hub airports operating in low-visibility conditions, often with CAT II/III precision approach lighting, advanced monitoring and multi-runway coordination.

  • CAT II/III approach lighting & stop bar systems
  • SCADA-level monitoring & centralised control
  • Compliance with ICAO Annex 14 and the national regulator's requirements
Explore International Airport Lighting
Defence

Military Airfields

Combat and training airfields with operational requirements civilian standards don't fully address — rugged fixtures, rapid-deployment options and covert or reduced-signature lighting modes.

  • Ruggedised fixtures suited to the operating environment
  • Covert / NVG-compatible lighting where required
  • Rapid installation & mobile lighting kits
Explore Military Airfield Lighting
Commercial Aviation

Regional Airports

Smaller-scale, single-runway operations balancing regulatory compliance against tighter capital and operating budgets.

  • Simplified CCR & circuit configurations
  • Right-sized PAPI & basic runway edge systems
  • Budget-conscious design & phased upgrades
Explore Regional Airport Lighting
General Aviation

General Aviation Airports

Small-aircraft airfields, often uncontrolled or lightly staffed, favouring low-cost, low-maintenance and increasingly solar-powered lighting solutions.

  • Low-intensity runway lighting (LIRL) systems
  • Pilot-controlled lighting (PCL) options
  • Solar & battery-powered fixtures for unstaffed strips
Explore GA Airport Lighting
Rotary Wing

Heliports

Helicopter-specific lighting geometry — perimeter and FATO/TLOF lighting, floodlighting and approach path indicators — scaled to a fraction of a fixed-wing runway's footprint.

  • FATO & TLOF perimeter and floodlighting where required
  • Helicopter approach path indicator (HAPI)
  • Rooftop & confined-area installation constraints
Explore Heliport Lighting
Special Operations

Temporary & Emergency Airfield Lighting

Rapidly deployable lighting for rehabilitation works, emergency restoration and contingency airfields, where speed of setup matters alongside photometric performance.

  • Portable, battery- or generator-powered systems
  • Quick-deploy kits sized to the mission
  • Deployment in unprepared or damaged infrastructure
Explore Temporary & Emergency Lighting
Capital Projects

Airport Runway Rehabilitation & Retrofit

Upgrading AGL infrastructure on a live or partially live runway — coordinating staged cutovers, temporary lighting and outage windows around continuing operations.

  • Phased cutover & staged commissioning planning
  • Temporary lighting during works
  • Coordination with NOTAMs & operational windows
Explore Rehabilitation & Retrofit
Capital Projects

LED AGL Retrofit Projects

Converting incandescent and halogen fixtures to LED for energy savings and lower maintenance, while preserving photometric compliance and CCR compatibility.

  • CCR & series-circuit compatibility checks
  • Post-retrofit photometric re-verification
  • Energy-savings & lifecycle cost analysis
