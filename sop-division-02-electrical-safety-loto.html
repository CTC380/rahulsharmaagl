SOP-26 Lock-Out/Tag-Out (LOTO)

Critical Rule — Never remove another person's personal LOTO device except under the organization's formally approved emergency/removal procedure.

Purpose

To prevent accidental energization or release of electrical energy while AGL maintenance or testing is being performed.

Scope

Applicable to CCRs, distribution panels, AGL circuits, control equipment, cable systems and other equipment requiring electrical isolation.

Procedure

Before Isolation

  1. Identify the equipment and circuit.
  2. Review drawings and circuit identification.
  3. Identify all electrical energy sources.
  4. Obtain the required work permit.
  5. Notify affected personnel.
  6. Determine the isolation point.
  7. Review the isolation sequence.

Isolation

  1. Stop the equipment using the normal control.
  2. Open the designated isolating device.
  3. Apply the approved lock.
  4. Apply an identification/safety tag.
  5. Prevent unauthorized operation.
  6. Where required, isolate secondary/control sources as well.

Verification

  1. Attempt normal operation where safe.
  2. Perform approved absence-of-voltage verification.
  3. Confirm the equipment cannot be energized.

Work

Only after isolation has been verified may work commence.

Restoration

  1. Inspect the work.
  2. Remove tools and personnel.
  3. Confirm covers and guards are installed.
  4. Ensure all personnel have removed their personal locks.
  5. Remove LOTO according to the approved procedure.
  6. Re-energize under controlled conditions.
SOP-27 Electrical Isolation
Purpose

To establish a safe method for isolating electrical equipment before maintenance.

Procedure

IDENTIFY → NOTIFY → SWITCH OFF → ISOLATE → LOCK/TAG → PROVE → WORK

  1. Identify the correct equipment.
  2. Confirm the circuit number.
  3. Identify all possible sources.
  4. Notify affected personnel.
  5. Switch equipment OFF.
  6. Open the designated isolation device.
  7. Apply LOTO.
  8. Verify absence of voltage.
  9. Confirm stored energy has been controlled.
  10. Commence work only after the safe condition is established.

Isolation shall be maintained for the entire duration of the work unless a controlled testing procedure specifically requires temporary energization.

SOP-28 CCR Electrical Isolation
Purpose

To safely isolate a Constant Current Regulator (CCR) before maintenance.

Procedure
  1. Identify the CCR and associated circuit.
  2. Confirm CCR output configuration.
  3. Notify the responsible operational authority.
  4. Disable the CCR using the approved operating procedure.
  5. Open the designated incoming supply isolation.
  6. Isolate control/remote sources where applicable.
  7. Apply LOTO.
  8. Verify the CCR cannot start or energize.
  9. Prove absence of voltage at the designated test points.
  10. Verify the output circuit is in the required safe condition.
  11. Discharge stored energy where applicable.
  12. Perform maintenance.
Before Re-Energization

Verify:

  • Covers installed
  • Internal components secure
  • Tools removed
  • Connections complete
  • No personnel remain inside the hazard area
  • Control settings restored
  • Protective devices installed
  • Alarms cleared or understood
SOP-29 Series Circuit Isolation

Critical Rule — AGL series circuits shall not be assumed safe simply because the CCR output indication is zero. The actual safe condition must be verified using the approved test method.

Purpose

To establish safe isolation of an AGL series circuit supplied by a CCR.

Procedure
  1. Identify the CCR and circuit number.
  2. Confirm the complete circuit route.
  3. Identify the intended isolation point.
  4. Notify affected personnel.
  5. Switch OFF the CCR.
  6. Isolate the supply as required.
  7. Apply LOTO.
  8. Prevent remote or automatic re-energization.
  9. Verify the circuit is in a safe state.
  10. Test for absence of hazardous voltage at the intended work location.
  11. Control stored energy.
  12. Begin maintenance.
SOP-30 Transformer Circuit Isolation
Purpose

To safely isolate an AGL isolating transformer and connected light fixture/circuit.

Procedure
  1. Identify the transformer and circuit.
  2. Determine whether the primary circuit is energized.
  3. Isolate the upstream source according to the approved procedure.
  4. Apply LOTO.
  5. Verify absence of hazardous voltage.
  6. Control secondary-side energy as applicable.
  7. Prevent accidental contact with exposed terminals.
  8. Perform maintenance.
Transformer Replacement

Before removing the transformer:

  • Confirm circuit isolation.
  • Verify correct transformer identification.
  • Disconnect conductors safely.
  • Protect cable ends.
  • Prevent moisture/dirt ingress.
  • Install the replacement correctly.
  • Verify connections.
  • Inspect bonding/earthing arrangements where applicable.
  • Test before return to service.
SOP-31 Cable Circuit Isolation
Purpose

To safely isolate AGL primary or secondary cables before cutting, disconnecting, repairing or testing.

Procedure
  1. Identify the cable/circuit.
  2. Confirm circuit drawings and cable identification.
  3. Isolate the source.
  4. Apply LOTO.
  5. Verify the circuit is de-energized.
  6. Prove the test instrument.
  7. Test the cable as required.
  8. Confirm the cable is safe before handling.
  9. Protect exposed cable ends.
  10. Perform the work.
Cable Identification

Never cut or disconnect an AGL cable based solely on physical appearance or assumed routing.

SOP-32 Proving Dead

Critical Rule — Do not touch conductors merely because the instrument indicates zero until the complete isolation procedure has been verified.

Purpose

To verify that electrical equipment or a circuit is de-energized before work.

Principle

The approved sequence should be:

TEST INSTRUMENT → TEST CIRCUIT → RE-TEST INSTRUMENT

This verifies that the instrument was functioning before and after the measurement.

Procedure
  1. Select a suitable approved tester.
  2. Inspect the instrument.
  3. Verify correct rating.
  4. Test the instrument on a known live source or approved proving unit.
  5. Test the isolated circuit/equipment.
  6. Confirm the expected absence of voltage.
  7. Re-test the instrument.
SOP-33 Voltage Verification
Purpose

To establish a controlled procedure for measuring voltage on AGL electrical equipment.

Procedure

Before testing:

  • Identify the circuit.
  • Select the correct instrument.
  • Check instrument rating.
  • Inspect leads and probes.
  • Wear required PPE.
  • Establish safe test position.

During testing:

  1. Use the approved test points.
  2. Keep hands behind appropriate protection.
  3. Avoid unnecessary exposure to energized parts.
  4. Record readings.
  5. Stop if readings are unexpected.

Unexpected voltage shall be treated as a potentially hazardous condition until investigated.

SOP-34 Absence-of-Voltage Testing

Critical Rule — Absence-of-voltage testing shall be performed at the actual point of work or an approved representative point, according to the site's electrical safety procedure.

Purpose

To confirm that the intended work area is not energized before personnel begin work.

Procedure
  1. Establish the isolation.
  2. Apply LOTO.
  3. Select an appropriately rated voltage tester.
  4. Verify the tester.
  5. Test all relevant conductors/test points.
  6. Test phase-to-phase or equivalent applicable points.
  7. Test phase-to-earth/ground where required.
  8. Verify the expected result.
  9. Re-prove the tester.
  10. Record the test when required.
SOP-35 Discharge of Stored Energy
Purpose

To control electrical energy that may remain after the primary source has been disconnected.

Possible Sources

Stored energy may exist in:

  • Capacitors
  • CCR power electronics
  • DC control circuits
  • Filters
  • UPS systems
  • Batteries
  • Cable capacitance
  • Electronic components
Procedure
  1. Isolate the primary source.
  2. Apply LOTO.
  3. Allow manufacturer-specified discharge time.
  4. Use approved discharge equipment where required.
  5. Verify the energy has been removed.
  6. Prevent re-accumulation of hazardous energy.
  7. Begin work only after the safe condition is confirmed.

Never short circuit capacitors or stored-energy devices using improvised methods.

SOP-36 Temporary Earthing

Critical Rule — Temporary earthing requirements depend on system design and the applicable electrical safety rules. It shall not be improvised.

Purpose

To establish controlled temporary earthing where required by the electrical safety procedure.

Procedure
  1. Confirm isolation.
  2. Prove absence of voltage.
  3. Inspect the earthing equipment.
  4. Verify rating and suitability.
  5. Connect the approved earthing equipment using the prescribed sequence.
  6. Secure the connections.
  7. Clearly identify the temporary earth.
  8. Maintain the earth during the required work.
  9. Remove it only under an authorized restoration procedure.
SOP-37 Electrical Re-Energization
Purpose

To prevent injury and equipment damage during restoration of electrical power.

Procedure

Before energization:

  • Confirm work is complete.
  • Inspect equipment.
  • Verify covers and guards.
  • Remove tools and temporary materials.
  • Confirm personnel are clear.
  • Confirm all permits allow restoration.
  • Confirm LOTO removal requirements.
  • Verify protective devices.
  • Notify affected personnel.

Then:

  1. Remove isolation/LOTO under the approved sequence.
  2. Energize the source.
  3. Monitor equipment.
  4. Check for abnormal noise, smell, indication or alarm.
  5. Verify correct electrical parameters.
  6. Perform functional testing.
SOP-38 Controlled Energization
Purpose

To establish a controlled method of energizing equipment after maintenance or repair.

Procedure
  1. Establish an energization plan.
  2. Confirm the equipment is ready.
  3. Clear personnel from hazardous areas.
  4. Confirm test instruments are removed where not required.
  5. Confirm guards/covers are installed.
  6. Notify relevant personnel.
  7. Energize from the approved source.
  8. Observe initial response.
  9. Check current/voltage/indications as applicable.
  10. Investigate abnormal conditions immediately.
  11. Complete functional testing.

For AGL equipment, controlled energization should include verification of the associated circuit and operational indications where applicable.

SOP-39 Emergency Electrical Shutdown
Purpose

To provide a rapid response to electrical emergencies.

Examples

Emergency shutdown may be required for:

  • Electrical fire
  • Smoke
  • Severe arcing
  • Electric shock incident
  • Equipment explosion/rupture
  • Major insulation failure
  • Uncontrolled electrical fault
  • Dangerous overheating
Procedure
  1. Stop work.
  2. Warn personnel.
  3. Operate the emergency isolation device if safe and authorized.
  4. Keep personnel away from the hazard.
  5. Notify Airport Operations/emergency response.
  6. Do not touch an injured person who may still be electrically energized.
  7. Prevent unauthorized re-energization.
  8. Preserve the scene where required for investigation.
  9. Do not restore power until authorized.
SOP-40 Electrical Panel Access
Purpose

To control access to electrical distribution and control panels.

Procedure

Before opening:

  • Identify the panel.
  • Review the electrical hazard.
  • Determine whether isolation is required.
  • Obtain authorization.
  • Wear required PPE.
  • Inspect panel condition.

During access:

  • Keep unauthorized personnel away.
  • Do not place tools on energized components.
  • Maintain safe working position.
  • Do not bypass protective devices.
  • Do not leave the panel open unattended.

After work:

  • Verify connections.
  • Install covers.
  • Secure the panel.
  • Record abnormalities.
SOP-41 CCR Cabinet Safe Access
Purpose

To provide additional controls for maintenance access to CCR cabinets.

Procedure
  1. Identify CCR.
  2. Review manufacturer's instructions.
  3. Confirm operating condition.
  4. Isolate according to the approved CCR isolation procedure.
  5. Apply LOTO.
  6. Prove absence of voltage.
  7. Allow required discharge time.
  8. Verify internal stored energy is controlled.
  9. Open cabinet using the approved method.
  10. Perform maintenance.
  11. Inspect all internal components.
  12. Reinstall guards/covers.
  13. Remove tools.
  14. Close and secure cabinet.

Prohibited Actions

Do not:

  • Bypass safety interlocks without authorization.
  • Defeat protective devices.
  • Work inside an energized cabinet unless a specifically authorized energized-work procedure applies.
  • Use damaged test equipment.
SOP-42 Electrical PPE Selection
Purpose

To ensure PPE is selected according to the actual electrical hazard.

PPE Assessment

The assessment should consider:

  • System voltage
  • Fault energy
  • Arc-flash risk where applicable
  • Shock hazard
  • Work activity
  • Exposure duration
  • Environmental conditions
  • Manufacturer requirements
Typical PPE

Depending on the risk assessment:

  • Safety helmet
  • Safety footwear
  • Safety glasses
  • Face/eye protection
  • Electrical insulating gloves
  • Protective clothing
  • Arc-rated clothing where required
  • Hearing protection where required
  • Insulating mats/barriers where applicable

PPE shall be inspected before use and maintained according to the manufacturer's requirements.

SOP-43 Insulated Tool Usage
Purpose

To prevent accidental electrical contact when using hand tools.

Requirements

Insulated tools shall:

  • Be suitable for the intended voltage/application.
  • Be inspected before use.
  • Have intact insulation.
  • Be clean and dry.
  • Be properly stored.
  • Not be modified.

Damaged or questionable insulated tools shall be removed from service.

Insulated tools are not a substitute for electrical isolation.

  • Fuses

  • Test category/suitability

SOP-45 High-Voltage Test Safety
Purpose

To control hazards associated with high-voltage testing of AGL equipment, cables or components.

Requirements

HV testing shall be conducted only by authorized personnel using approved equipment and procedures.

Before testing:

  1. Define the test area.
  2. Establish barriers.
  3. Restrict access.
  4. Identify test equipment.
  5. Verify test leads/connections.
  6. Establish communication.
  7. Review emergency procedure.
  8. Confirm equipment ratings.
  9. Notify affected personnel.

During testing:

  • Maintain required clearances.
  • Keep unauthorized personnel outside the test boundary.
  • Do not touch the test object.
  • Monitor the test continuously.

After testing:

  1. Switch off the test source.
  2. Isolate the test equipment.
  3. Discharge the test object using the approved procedure.
  4. Verify the absence of residual voltage.
  5. Apply temporary earth where required.
  6. Remove barriers only when safe.
SOP-46 Insulation Resistance Test Safety

Critical Rule — Insulation resistance testing can place a significant test voltage on a circuit even though its normal operating voltage may be lower.

Purpose

To safely perform insulation resistance testing on AGL cables and electrical equipment.

Procedure

Before testing:

  • Isolate the circuit.
  • Apply LOTO.
  • Disconnect sensitive electronic equipment where required.
  • Confirm the test voltage is appropriate.
  • Establish a controlled test area.
  • Warn affected personnel.

During testing:

  1. Connect the insulation tester correctly.
  2. Keep personnel away from test conductors.
  3. Apply the specified test voltage.
  4. Record the result.
  5. Stop if abnormal conditions occur.

After testing:

  1. Stop the test.
  2. Allow the tester's discharge function to operate where provided.
  3. Discharge the circuit using the approved method.
  4. Verify the circuit is safe.
  5. Restore connections.
SOP-47 Earth Resistance Test Safety

Critical Rule — Never disconnect a protective earth or bonding conductor merely to perform a test unless the approved procedure specifically requires it and an equivalent safe arrangement is established.

Purpose

To safely perform earth/ground resistance measurements associated with AGL electrical installations.

Procedure
  1. Identify the earth system under test.
  2. Review the test method.
  3. Determine whether disconnection from the normal earth system is permitted.
  4. Prevent unsafe touch potentials.
  5. Establish the test area.
  6. Install test electrodes safely.
  7. Use suitable test equipment.
  8. Conduct the measurement.
  9. Record results.
  10. Restore all earth connections after testing.
SOP-48 Electrical Work Risk Assessment
Purpose

To identify and control hazards before electrical work begins.

Risk Assessment Process

Step 1 — Identify the Task

Example:

  • CCR repair
  • Cable fault investigation
  • Transformer replacement
  • Insulation testing
  • Panel maintenance

Step 2 — Identify Hazards

Consider:

  • Electric shock
  • Arc flash
  • Unexpected energization
  • Stored energy
  • Induced voltage
  • Damaged insulation
  • Wet environment
  • Poor access
  • Aircraft/vehicle movement
  • FOD
  • Weather
  • Communication failure

Step 3 — Determine Controls

Apply the hierarchy:

Eliminate → Isolate → Engineer → Administrative Controls → PPE

Step 4 — Establish Safe Work Method

Document:

  • Isolation points
  • LOTO
  • Test method
  • PPE
  • Tools
  • Personnel
  • Emergency arrangements

Step 5 — Review

The risk assessment shall be reviewed when:

  • Scope changes
  • Conditions change
  • Equipment differs from expected
  • New hazards appear
  • An incident/near miss occurs
SOP-49 Electrical Permit Closure
Purpose

To formally close an electrical work permit after the system has been made safe and the work is complete.

Closure Requirements

Before closure:

  • Work completed.
  • Equipment inspected.
  • Tools removed.
  • FOD removed.
  • Temporary earths removed where authorized.
  • Temporary connections removed.
  • Covers installed.
  • Guards installed.
  • Electrical tests completed.
  • Functional tests completed.
  • Personnel accounted for.
  • Equipment status confirmed.
  • Operational authority notified.
  • System status recorded.
Closure

The responsible authorized person shall:

  1. Confirm completion.
  2. Confirm the equipment's operational status.
  3. Complete the permit documentation.
  4. Record outstanding defects.
  5. Return the system to the appropriate operational authority.

An electrical permit shall not be closed merely because the physical repair is complete.

Division-Wide Requirements

Core electrical safety rules, the master isolation sequence, and the golden rule that governs all 25 Division 02 procedures.

Core Electrical Safety Rules

  • Rule 01 — Identify Before Isolate — Never operate an isolation device based only on physical location or assumption.
  • Rule 02 — Isolate Before Work — Where the task requires electrical isolation, establish the safe electrical condition before starting work.
  • Rule 03 — Lock and Tag — Isolation without the required LOTO controls is incomplete where LOTO is required.
  • Rule 04 — Prove Dead — Do not rely only on a switch position, indicator lamp, CCR display or control-system status.
  • Rule 05 — Control Stored Energy — Capacitors, electronic systems and cables may retain hazardous energy after supply isolation.
  • Rule 06 — Protect Against Re-Energization — Prevent local, remote, automatic or inadvertent re-energization.
  • Rule 07 — Test Instruments Are Safety Equipment — A defective meter can create a serious hazard. Inspect and verify test equipment before use.
  • Rule 08 — Unexpected Voltage Means STOP — Unexpected voltage shall be treated as a potentially hazardous condition until its source is identified and controlled.
  • Rule 09 — Never Bypass Protection — Safety interlocks, protective devices and emergency systems shall not be bypassed unless specifically authorized under an approved procedure.
  • Rule 10 — Restore Only Under Control — Electrical equipment shall be re-energized only after inspection, personnel clearance, LOTO control and authorization requirements have been satisfied.

Master Electrical Isolation Sequence

For applicable AGL electrical maintenance:

01 · IDENTIFY 02 · ASSESS RISK 03 · AUTHORIZE WORK 04 · NOTIFY AFFECTED PERSONNEL 05 · SWITCH OFF 06 · ISOLATE ALL ENERGY SOURCES 07 · LOCK & TAG 08 · CONTROL STORED ENERGY 09 · PROVE TEST INSTRUMENT 10 · TEST FOR ABSENCE OF VOLTAGE 11 · RE-PROVE TEST INSTRUMENT 12 · APPLY TEMPORARY EARTHING IF REQUIRED 13 · PERFORM WORK 14 · INSPECT 15 · TEST 16 · CLEAR PERSONNEL & TOOLS 17 · REMOVE TEMPORARY EARTHS WHERE AUTHORIZED 18 · REMOVE LOTO UNDER APPROVED PROCEDURE 19 · CONTROLLED ENERGIZATION 20 · FUNCTIONAL VERIFICATION 21 · RETURN TO SERVICE 22 · DOCUMENT & CLOSE PERMIT

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Electrical Safety, LOTO & Isolation SOPs in the AGL SOP Library.

Q1What is Division 02 — Electrical Safety, LOTO & Isolation SOPs?

Division 02 is a set of 25 procedures (SOP-26 to SOP-50, Rev 0) for isolating and proving dead CCRs, AGL series circuits, isolating transformers, primary cables, distribution equipment and control systems. It covers lockout/tagout, absence-of-voltage testing, stored-energy discharge, temporary earthing, controlled re-energization, and electrical PPE and test-instrument safety.

Q2What is the Golden Rule for AGL electrical work?

Never work on an AGL electrical circuit on the assumption that it is dead. Identify it, isolate it, lock it, test it, prove it safe, control stored energy, perform the work, inspect it and only then restore it to service.

Q3Is a switch position, indicator lamp or CCR display proof that a circuit is dead?

No. Rule 04 (Prove Dead) says not to rely only on a switch position, indicator lamp, CCR display or control-system status. Isolation must be verified by testing, and LOTO is required where the task calls for it, because isolation without the required LOTO controls is incomplete.

Q4How is a circuit proved dead?

The approved sequence is test instrument, test circuit, re-test instrument (SOP-32). This shows the tester worked before and after the measurement. Absence-of-voltage testing is done at the actual point of work or an approved representative point, covering phase-to-phase and phase-to-earth points where required (SOP-34).

Q5Can someone remove another person's LOTO lock?

No. SOP-26 states that another person's personal LOTO device must never be removed except under the organization's formally approved emergency/removal procedure. LOTO removal during restoration is done only by authorized personnel under the approved sequence.

Q6How is stored energy controlled after isolation?

Capacitors, CCR power electronics, DC control circuits, filters, UPS systems, batteries, cable capacitance and electronic components may retain energy after the supply is disconnected. SOP-35 requires allowing the manufacturer-specified discharge time, using approved discharge equipment where required, verifying the energy has gone, and never short-circuiting capacitors by improvised methods.

Q7What must be done before re-energizing equipment?

Confirm the work is complete, inspect the equipment, verify covers and guards, remove tools and temporary materials, confirm personnel are clear, confirm permits allow restoration and verify protective devices. Notify affected personnel, remove LOTO under the approved sequence, energize, monitor for abnormal noise, smell, indication or alarm, verify electrical parameters and perform functional testing (SOP-37).

Q8What should be done in an electrical emergency such as arcing, fire or shock?

Stop work, warn personnel, operate the emergency isolation device if safe and authorized, and keep people away from the hazard. Notify Airport Operations or emergency response, do not touch an injured person who may still be energized, prevent unauthorized re-energization, and do not restore power until authorized (SOP-39).

Q9What does the unexpected-voltage and protection-bypass rule say?

Rule 08 says unexpected voltage means stop: treat it as hazardous until its source is identified and controlled. Rule 09 says safety interlocks, protective devices and emergency systems must not be bypassed unless specifically authorized under an approved procedure.

Q10When can an electrical work permit be closed?

Not merely because the physical repair is complete (SOP-49). Work must be finished and inspected, tools and FOD removed, temporary earths and connections removed where authorized, covers and guards installed, electrical and functional tests completed, personnel accounted for and the operational authority notified. The authorized person then records outstanding defects and returns the system to the operational authority.
