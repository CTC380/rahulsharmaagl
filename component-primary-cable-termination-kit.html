Primary Cable Termination Kit

A comprehensive kit containing all components required to terminate the end of an L‑824 primary AGL cable for connection to CCRs, distribution panels, L‑823 connectors, or other equipment. Includes heat‑shrink tubing, cable lugs, screen connectors, insulating tape, and sealing materials to create a reliable, waterproof, and electrically sound termination that meets 5kV insulation requirements.

Overview

What Is It?

The Primary Cable Termination Kit is a pre‑assembled collection of materials and components specifically designed for terminating the ends of L‑824 primary AGL cables. It provides everything needed to prepare the cable end, attach termination hardware, restore insulation, and create a waterproof seal, ensuring a safe and reliable connection to equipment such as Constant Current Regulators (CCRs), distribution panels, L‑823 connectors, or junction boxes.

Typical contents of a primary cable termination kit include:

  • Conductor Terminals (Lugs): Copper or tinned‑copper compression lugs sized to match the conductor (6 AWG to 2 AWG) and the equipment stud size.
  • Heat‑Shrink Tubing: Dual‑wall adhesive‑lined tubing for insulating the conductor connection and restoring the cable's insulation profile.
  • Screen Connector: A clamp or lug for terminating the metallic screen (shield) to earth or to the equipment's earth terminal.
  • Insulating Tape: Self‑amalgamating or high‑voltage tape for additional insulation and stress relief at the termination point.
  • Sealing Compound: Mastic or resin for waterproofing the cable entry into the equipment.
  • Cleaning Materials: Wipes and abrasive pads for preparing the conductor and screen surfaces.
  • Installation Instructions: Step‑by‑step guide with torque and crimp specifications.

The kit is designed for use with L‑824 shielded, XLPE‑insulated primary cable and is compatible with various equipment termination requirements. It ensures that the termination meets the electrical and mechanical performance requirements of the 5kV series circuit, including insulation integrity, screen continuity, and moisture resistance.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The termination kit exists because the point where a primary AGL cable connects to a CCR, distribution panel, or junction box is one of the highest-risk points in the entire series circuit — it's where the cable's continuous insulation and shielding are interrupted to make an actual electrical connection, and if that termination isn't done correctly, it becomes the weak point most likely to fail under the circuit's 5kV operating stress or let in moisture over time.

By bundling everything needed — lugs sized to the conductor, shrink tubing to restore insulation, a screen connector for earth continuity, and sealing compound for waterproofing — into one kit with clear instructions, it ensures every termination on the airfield is built to the same standard regardless of which crew installs it, rather than depending on individual technicians sourcing and matching compatible components correctly on their own.

Application

Where Is It Used?

The primary cable termination kit is used throughout the airfield wherever primary cable ends need to be terminated, including:

  • CCR Output Terminals: Terminating the primary cables at the output of the Constant Current Regulator (CCR) for connection to the field distribution panel or series loop.
  • L‑823 Connector Installation: Preparing the cable end for insertion into the L‑823 plug or receptacle assembly, ensuring proper contact and sealing.
  • Junction Boxes and Splice Enclosures: Terminating cables inside junction boxes for connection to terminal blocks, bus bars, or other equipment.
  • Distribution Panels: Connecting primary cables to the distribution panel's bus bars or circuit breakers.
  • Test Points and Disconnect Panels: Terminating cables at test points to allow for circuit isolation and testing.
  • Transition Points: Preparing cable ends for transition from underground to elevated installations, or from cable to equipment.

The kit is available for cable sizes from 6 AWG to 2 AWG (6mm² to 35mm²) and includes components suitable for copper conductors. It is rated for continuous operation at the full primary current (6.6A or 20A) and voltage (5kV).

Placement

Installation & Location

Terminations are made inside the enclosure they serve — a CCR output cabinet, a junction box, a distribution panel, or the base of an L-823 plug/receptacle — rather than in open ground, so the finished termination sits in a controlled, accessible space rather than a buried joint. Cable entering the enclosure is glanded and strain-relieved at the enclosure wall before the kit's own stress-cone or crimp termination is made, keeping the cable's outer sheath integrity intact right up to the termination point. Where the termination feeds a distribution panel or CCR, sufficient cable slack is left inside the enclosure to allow the termination to be remade once or twice over its service life without pulling additional cable from the duct run.

Function

How Does It Work?

The primary cable termination kit provides all necessary components to create a reliable, waterproof, and electrically sound termination. The typical installation process involves:

  • Cable Preparation: Stripping the outer jacket to the required length, removing the insulation screen and XLPE insulation to expose the conductor, and preparing the metallic screen for termination.
  • Screen Termination: Connecting the screen to the earth terminal using the screen connector (e.g., a clamp or lug). The screen must be bonded to earth at the termination point to ensure EMI protection and safety.
  • Conductor Termination: Attaching the compression lug to the exposed conductor using a hydraulic crimping tool. The lug is then bolted to the equipment's stud or terminal.
  • Insulation Restoration: Applying self‑amalgamating tape over the conductor connection and then covering with heat‑shrink tubing. The tubing is heated to shrink and seal, providing electrical insulation and moisture protection.
  • Stress Relief and Sealing: Applying additional sealing compound (mastic or resin) at the cable entry into the equipment to prevent moisture ingress and provide mechanical strain relief.
  • Testing: After installation, the termination is tested for insulation resistance (5000V megger) and continuity to verify the integrity of the connection.

The kit is designed for use with standard tools (cable strippers, crimping tools, heat gun, torque wrench) and follows the manufacturer's instructions for proper installation. The materials are selected to match the cable's XLPE insulation and 5kV rating, ensuring compatibility and long‑term reliability.

Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

The termination kit carries no control logic of its own — it is the fixed interface where the L-824 primary cable's conductor and screen are bonded to a piece of equipment (a CCR output, a transformer, or a junction) that is itself part of the loop the ALCMS and CCR monitor. Its job is to make that boundary electrically transparent: a properly torqued lug and a correctly bonded screen should add no meaningful resistance to the loop and no leakage path to earth, so the equipment sees the same fixed current the CCR is regulating. A degraded termination — a loose lug, a compromised heat-shrink seal letting in moisture — behaves exactly like any other loop impedance fault: the CCR sees a rising voltage at constant current and reports it to the ALCMS as a loop fault, with no indication that the cause is a termination rather than a cable, splice, or fixture fault elsewhere on the same loop. This is why the kit's installation is verified immediately with an insulation-resistance (megger) test and a continuity check, rather than being left for the ALCMS to catch later.

A correct termination is meant to disappear electrically into the loop — its quality is confirmed once, at installation, with an insulation-resistance and continuity test, rather than relying on the ALCMS to flag a problem later.
Operating Principle

Termination Sequence

Each step depends on the one before it — screen bonding must be in place before insulation is restored, and stress relief must be done before the assembly is closed up — so the sequence is followed in order, finishing with the megger and continuity test that confirms the work.
Construction

Physical Construction

A termination kit is supplied as a set of pre-formed or field-assembled components: a compression or crimp lug to terminate the conductor at the equipment connection point, stress-relief cones or mastic/grading tape to control the electric field where the cable's shield is cut back and terminated, and layers of insulating tape, heat-shrink tubing, or a moulded boot to rebuild the insulation over the exposed conductor and lug. A separate ground braid or tail lead restores continuity from the cable shield to the equipment's earthing point, and a weatherproof outer sleeve or heat-shrink jacket seals the finished termination against moisture where it is installed outdoors or in a below-grade enclosure. Kits are typically sized to match the specific cable gauge and voltage class, with the components pre-cut or moulded to length so the termination can be completed in the field without specialised tooling beyond a crimp tool and a heat gun.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Improper Crimping of Lugs

Using the wrong die or insufficient crimping pressure can result in a high‑resistance connection that overheats. Always use the correct hydraulic crimping tool and die set, and perform a pull‑test to verify the connection.

Incomplete Insulation Restoration

If the heat‑shrink tubing is not fully shrunk or the adhesive does not flow, moisture can ingress. Ensure the tubing is heated evenly to the correct temperature and that the adhesive is visible at the ends.

Screen Termination Failure

If the screen is not properly connected to earth, the cable's shielding effectiveness is lost. Use the provided screen connector and ensure it is securely clamped to the screen and bonded to the earth terminal.

Incorrect Lug Size or Stud Hole

Using a lug with the wrong stud hole diameter or conductor size will result in a poor mechanical and electrical connection. Always match the lug to the equipment stud and the cable conductor size.

Moisture Ingress at Cable Entry

If the cable entry into the equipment is not properly sealed, moisture can track along the cable and cause failure. Apply sealing compound (mastic or resin) generously around the cable entry.

Over‑Torquing of Bolted Connections

Excessive torque on the lug stud can damage the equipment or strip the threads. Use a torque wrench set to the manufacturer's specified value (typically 10‑30 N·m).

Failure to Test After Installation

Not performing insulation resistance and continuity tests after termination can leave undetected faults. Always test the termination with a 5000V megger and a continuity tester before energising the circuit.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Termination Runs Hot or Shows High Resistance
Likely Cause
Improper crimping of the lug from the wrong die or insufficient crimping pressure.
Diagnostic Steps
Measure resistance across the termination under load; inspect the crimp for the correct die profile if accessible.
Corrective Action
Remake the termination with the correct hydraulic crimping tool and die set, and verify with a pull-test before re-energising.
Insulation Resistance Test Fails at the Termination
Likely Cause
The heat-shrink tubing was not fully shrunk or the adhesive did not flow, allowing moisture ingress.
Diagnostic Steps
Megger-test the termination at 5000V; inspect the tubing for incomplete shrink or missing adhesive flow at the ends.
Corrective Action
Redo the insulation restoration, heating the tubing evenly to the correct temperature until adhesive is visible at both ends, then re-test.
Screen Not Bonded to Earth
Likely Cause
The screen connector was not securely clamped to the screen or bonded to the earth terminal.
Diagnostic Steps
Test continuity from the screen to the earth terminal; visually inspect the screen connector clamp.
Corrective Action
Re-secure the screen connector and confirm a solid bond to the earth terminal.
Moisture Ingress at the Cable Entry
Likely Cause
The cable entry into the equipment was not properly sealed with sealing compound.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect the cable entry for visible moisture tracking or missing sealant.
Corrective Action
Apply sealing compound generously around the cable entry and confirm the seal is complete before closing the enclosure.
Lug Loose or Stud Thread Damaged
Likely Cause
Wrong lug/stud hole size was used, or the connection was over-torqued during installation.
Diagnostic Steps
Check the lug and stud size match the equipment and conductor; inspect the stud threads for stripping or damage.
Corrective Action
Fit the correctly sized lug and re-torque to the manufacturer's specified value using a torque wrench, replacing the stud if threads are damaged.

Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

High-Voltage Isolation & Verification
Electrical Safety

This kit terminates a primary series loop rated up to 5kV; lock out the CCR and verify zero energy with test equipment rated for that voltage class before opening the enclosure or handling the cable end.

Adjacent Live Circuits in Shared Enclosures
Electrical Safety

Distribution panels and multi-way cabinets can carry several independently switched series loops; confirm which specific circuit is isolated and physically identify the correct cable before working, since a neighbouring terminal in the same enclosure may remain live.

Heat-Shrink Application Safety
Personnel Safety

Use a heat gun rather than an open flame torch wherever practical inside an enclosure, keep the heat source clear of adjacent cables and cabinet components, and ensure adequate ventilation, particularly when working inside a confined cabinet or below-grade pit.

Crimping Tool Pinch Hazard
Personnel Safety

Keep fingers clear of the die during compression lug crimping, and use a calibrated tool matched to the lug size to avoid an under- or over-crimped joint as well as tool slip injury.

Screen/Shield Bonding for Shock Protection
Electrical Safety

Terminate and bond the cable screen to the cabinet/equipment earth point before closing up the termination; a missed or loose screen bond removes a protective earth path and leaves the termination and enclosure at risk of a floating fault potential.

Insulation Test Before Energising
Electrical Safety

Megger test the completed termination and confirm it meets the ≥ 50 MΩ insulation resistance requirement before the circuit is energised; a marginal reading at this stage is far cheaper to fix than a fault found later in a buried or enclosed joint.

Sign-Off Before Closing the Enclosure
Compliance

Record termination location, lug torque, and seal completion before the enclosure is closed, since the environmental seal and internal connections are not inspectable again without reopening a completed termination.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Under-Shrinking Is Harder to Spot Than Over-Shrinking
Installation

An over-heated sleeve is visually obvious from scorching or bubbling, but an under-shrunk termination can look acceptable while still leaving voids that compromise the seal or dielectric strength. Confirm full, even shrink-back and adhesive flow-out along the entire sleeve length, not just check for the absence of visible damage.

Lug Torque Affects Long-Term Resistance More Than Initial Test Shows
Reliability

An under-torqued lug connection can pass an initial insulation and continuity test while still being prone to loosening and localized heating under years of thermal cycling. Torque to the documented value with a calibrated tool rather than "snug plus a bit more," and don't treat a passing initial test as proof the torque was correct.

Screen Bonding Method Should Match the Site's Earthing Design
Design

Defaulting to a generic screen bonding approach without checking the site's specific earthing and bonding design can create an unintended ground loop or leave a gap in fault current return path. Confirm the intended bonding method against the site design before terminating, especially on retrofit work.

Shared Enclosure Terminations Need Extra Verification Before Work
Electrical Safety

Where a termination sits in an enclosure with other live circuits, confirming isolation on the target circuit isn't enough on its own; the physical working space and adjacent conductors need positive identification too. Verify circuit identification against as-built records, not just against labels that may be outdated or mismatched.

Contamination During Prep Undermines the Whole Termination
Failure Mode

Dirt, moisture, or handling residue introduced during cable prep, before the sleeve or seal is even applied, is a common root cause of a termination that fails prematurely despite otherwise correct technique. Keep prepared conductor ends clean and protected between prep and final assembly, especially in field conditions.

Document the Termination Before It Becomes Inaccessible
Compliance

Once an enclosure is closed, verifying torque or seal quality again means undoing completed work, so the sign-off record is effectively the only lasting evidence of build quality. Treat that documentation step with the same rigor as the physical termination itself, not as paperwork to complete afterward from memory.
