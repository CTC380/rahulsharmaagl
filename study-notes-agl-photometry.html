AGL PHOTOMETRY COMPLETE NOTES

WHAT IS AGL PHOTOMETRY?

Photometry is the measurement of light weighted by human visual response (radiometry measures optical radiation physically). In AGL it answers: how bright, in which direction, does the beam cover the required area, is the colour right, is it aimed correctly, and does the installed fixture still meet its specified performance?

QuantityUnitMeaning
Luminous intensitycandela (cd)Light in a particular direction; the key AGL quantity
Luminous fluxlumen (lm)Total light flow
Illuminancelux (lx) = lm/m²Light falling on a surface
Luminancecd/m²Brightness of a surface in a direction
Core rule
  • LIGHT ON ≠ PHOTOMETRICALLY COMPLIANT
  • Photometry = intensity + direction
  • Never judge a light by whether it is on; judge the required light, direction, colour and beam distribution at the specified operating condition

FAA AC 150/5340-26C says regular photometric measurement is the practical way to check output and find misalignment; AC 150/5345-46F holds the fixture photometric requirements. Visual inspection finds dark fixtures, broken lenses, contamination and obvious problems; photometry gives objective measurement.

2

THEORY: INTENSITY, ANGLES AND THE INVERSE SQUARE

I = Φ / Ω (cd = lm / sr). For a point source, E = I / d² and I = E × d². AGL fixtures are highly directional, so follow the applicable test geometry rather than applying the inverse-square law blindly. If the measurement distance is wrong, the computed intensity is wrong.

Pilots need light in specific directions, so candela and angular distribution matter more than total lumens. A lux meter alone is not an AGL photometric system. "Candlepower" is an older term for intensity; use candela.

Horizontal angle

Azimuth: left/right coverage, spread, toe-in.

Vertical angle

Elevation: vertical spread, aiming, main beam position.

Beam centre

Reference may be the fixture or optical axis, a red/white transition (PAPI) or a specified coordinate.

Beam spread and main beam

Angular extent meeting the specified performance; the main beam is the primary useful output.

Wattage ≠ photometric performance: two 200 W fixtures can differ in intensity, beam, colour and distribution. A 30 W LED is not automatically the optical equal of another 30 W fixture. Higher lumens is not automatically better.

Every fixture type has an expected photometric signature (intensity, angle, colour, spread, direction). Field work checks that the installed fixture still resembles it. There is no universal AGL curve: runway centerline, edge, taxiway edge or centerline, stop bar, threshold and PAPI each have their own requirement, and the report must state the fixture type (L-850A/B/C/D, L-852 variants, L-861, L-862, etc., per AC 150/5345-46F).

4

COLOUR AND PAPI TRANSITION

Photometry also asks "what colour?". Chromaticity (CIE x, y) describes colour independent of brightness; correlated colour temperature is a different idea, and "3000 K" does not establish aviation colour compliance. Meaning in AGL comes from colour + direction + location + function. LED ageing can shift colour, so a bright fixture can fall outside its colour region. Colour is measured per the applicable method, for example filtered detectors or chromaticity coordinates; a spectroradiometer gives more but the standard's method governs. Coloured light also introduces detector spectral-mismatch error, so use the instrument's correction factors or a suitably matched detector.

PAPI is special: its critical feature is the red/white transition (AC 150/5345-28H sets transition-sharpness limits; take the exact angular limits and test geometry from the edition in force). PAPI photometry covers intensity, colour, transition, beam centre, aiming angle, horizontal coverage and per-unit results; aiming defines where the beam points, photometry shows what it actually looks like. See Note 11.

5

ALIGNMENT, TOE-IN AND BEAM ORIENTATION

Photometry detects mechanical misalignment: a fixture can be bright yet aimed wrong, giving too little intensity at the pilot's viewing angle. Intensity without correct aiming is not sufficient. Some directional fixtures have intentional toe-in; a fixture can have correct optics and incorrect toe-in. Beam orientation depends on application (runway edge, centerline, taxiway, stop bar, threshold, approach, PAPI), each with its own viewing geometry, and measurement angles are referenced to the specified coordinate system, so wrong fixture orientation means wrong interpretation.

Pattern vs approved curveLikely cause
Uniform reductionAgeing, contamination, reduced input, source degradation
Beam shiftedMechanical alignment: rotation, bolt torque, base, installation
One side reducedObstruction, optical damage, misalignment, or sensor geometry error
Random spikesMeasurement or data issue
Reduced centre but little shiftRubber contamination (confirm with before/after cleaning)

Evaluate centre, edges and required angles, not centre intensity alone. Main-beam average (where the standard defines it) represents the beam better than a single point.

6

MEASUREMENT: INSTRUMENTS, CALIBRATION, QUALITY

Photometer

Calibrated detector with spectral response matched to V(λ), the CIE photopic efficiency function, plus electronics and data acquisition.

Handheld vs mobile

Handheld: portable, good for targeted checks, but slow and operator-dependent. Mobile systems measure many fixtures quickly and give isocandela output, misalignment indication and GPS location, with high repeatability (AC 150/5340-26C).

Calibration

Traceable certificate, valid date, instrument ID, sensor response verified. Record instrument, model, serial, calibration and next due date.

Uncertainty

Sources: detector calibration, angular position, distance, alignment, ambient light, temperature, repeatability, resolution, processing.

Accuracy is closeness to the reference; precision is repeatability: 520, 523, 519 cd is good; 520, 690, 410 cd means investigate the process. Reproducibility covers different operators, instruments and sessions. Control ambient light (sun, aircraft, vehicles, apron lights, floodlights) and record weather and fixture temperature, since LED and lamp output can depend on temperature. Periodically repeat a known reference measurement; investigate calibration, sensor, software, geometry or ambient light if it drifts.

7

FIELD SURVEY AND DATA

PreparationIdentify circuitCheck calibrationSetup and geometryIdentify lightMeasureValidateAnalyseReport

Before field work review the approved layout and fixture schedule, circuit numbers, weather, access and calibration, and coordinate with operations and ATC. Verify distance, sensor height, reference direction, horizontal and vertical angle, fixture orientation and vehicle position; sensor misalignment causes wrong intensity, shifted beam and false maintenance indications. Give every fixture a unique ID matching the asset register (e.g. RWY-27-E-001); a database holds ID, circuit, type, GPS or chainage, intensity, colour, beam result, status and history.

Validate data: if neighbours read 400–500 cd and one reads 5,000, check angle, fixture ID, sensor, data entry or fixture type before accepting. Outlier detection flags very low or high values, beam shift, odd colour, missing fixtures and wrong sequence. Software can class results as pass, monitor or service, but limits must come from the governing standard. Always compare at the correct CCR brightness step and current, and keep raw data, not just a pass/fail table.

ELECTRICAL AND OPTICAL DIAGNOSIS

Low output is not automatically a fixture fault. CCR current error, transformer problems, high-resistance connectors, cable voltage drop and driver faults all show up photometrically, so check the electrical chain before replacing a healthy fixture: CCR current → transformer → connector → secondary cable → fixture → lens/optics → alignment → re-measure. See Notes 07, 08 and 10.

Scattered low fixturesContamination, ageing, mechanical condition

Optical components (lens, reflector, filter, prism, light pipe, LED optic, diffuser) each shape the beam; damage, scratches, ageing or movement of any one changes distribution, colour or intensity.

9

LED AND INCANDESCENT

LEDIncandescent
Semiconductor source, usually with electronic driverFilament lamp
Thermal management and junction temperature matterFilament temperature matters
Colour can shift with temperature and ageColour shifts with filament condition
Optic often integratedReflector, filter and lens arrangement
Can degrade before failing, including single LED or module failureFails more suddenly

LED output can degrade through thermal stress, driver ageing, optical degradation and contamination, so periodic photometry reveals loss before failure. LED fixtures may respond to CCR step current differently from incandescent ones, so measure at each step the specification requires. Pulsed (PWM) drive can also upset fast-sampling photometers, so confirm the instrument's integration time suits the fixture. When converting to LED, do not assume lower power means the same optics: verify intensity, beam, colour, distribution, compatibility and electrical behaviour.

10

MAINTENANCE PHOTOMETRY

Before and after: measure, clean or re-lamp or replace, then measure again (AC 150/5340-26C recommends this). Example: 260 cd before, 440 cd after proves contamination was a major cause. Rubber builds up on centerline and touchdown fixtures and can need frequent attention, especially on CAT II/III runways; dirt, mud, oil, salt, insects and de-icing residue also cut output.

Low outputMeasureInspectCleanRe-measureRecovered? return to service, else service or replace
The FAA 70% figure is a maintenance target. AC 150/5340-26C targets an in-pavement runway light below 70% of the minimum output required at maximum intensity for cleaning or maintenance. It is not a universal acceptance limit. Similarly, ICAO's approach of treating a light as unserviceable relative to a 50% main-beam-average figure is a serviceability criterion, not a commissioning acceptance limit. Verify both against the current documents.

Balance matters too: fixtures of 100, 100 and 45 cd leave an uneven pattern even if the weak one clears a minimum. Maintenance should consider the installed design value, not only a generic minimum, where the governing criterion says so. Compare against adjacent fixtures, the same type, circuit, step and zone.

Illustrative trend values only. Here the reference is the fixture's own baseline, used for trending. The FAA 70% maintenance target is judged against the minimum required output in the applicable AC table, so check the actual requirement for the fixture type before deciding.

Water in shallow light bases, loose bolts (check torque with a calibrated wrench) and moved fixtures cause electrical and alignment faults; after water damage recheck insulation, fixture, optics and output. Trending gives condition-based and predictive maintenance: measure, assess, and maintain only where needed. Low-visibility and CAT II/III operations depend on light output; ICAO recommends in-field measurement of intensity, beam spread and orientation for CAT II/III precision-approach lighting, with mobile units where appropriate.

11

PROGRAM, COMMISSIONING AND PRODUCT DATA

Baseline

At commissioning: prove output, beam, colour and alignment, and set the benchmark ("Baseline 0"). See Note 14.

Periodic

At defined intervals. There is no single universal interval; consider traffic, fixture age, pollution, rubber, criticality, history, low-visibility use and local requirements.

Triggered

After major maintenance, fixture replacement, runway rehabilitation or resurfacing, PAPI adjustment, redesign or incident. Pavement work can change fixture elevation, beam angle, drainage and base condition.

Qualification vs field

Qualification and production tests prove the product meets the specification; field photometry proves the installed unit still performs. Field results need not equal lab results exactly.

FAA Airport Lighting Equipment Certification

Qualified fixtures per AC 150/5345-46F can be listed in the FAA Airport Lighting Equipment Certification Program; verify actual certification and project approval.

Use the manufacturer's approved photometric distribution or the standard's required distribution for the correct fixture type; never compare a centerline fixture with a taxiway fixture's curve. Signs follow the sign specification, and pavement markings are judged by colour, geometry, contrast and condition, not luminous intensity (Notes 12 and 13). Weather affects both measurement and real pilot visibility; a measurement shows what the fixture produces under test conditions.

12

REPORTING AND DEFICIENCIES

A report should cover: project, airport, runway or taxiway, date and weather; instrument, model, serial and calibration; fixture type, circuit and CCR step; method, test distance and angular grid; results (intensity, colour, beam, aiming); analysis, pass/fail and deficiencies; corrective action and retest; final status. No result should be untraceable to a field fixture: report → fixture ID → location → circuit → measurement file → instrument → calibration certificate.

Formulas: Output % = Measured / Reference × 100 (440/500 = 88%); Degradation % = (Reference − Measured) / Reference × 100 (500 → 400 = 20%); Deviation % = (Measured − Reference) / Reference × 100. These are comparison metrics, not pass/fail criteria. A uniformity ratio (min/max) must not replace the standard's uniformity criterion. Retest after correction: FAIL → action → measure → compare → PASS or FAIL. A photometric NCR records requirement, measured value, result, action, retest and final result, which beats "light repaired".

Never accept "this fixture gives 500 cd". Ask: 500 cd at which angle, in which direction, at which CCR step, for which fixture type, under which specification?

13

TEAM, AIRSIDE SAFETY AND CHECKLISTS

A survey team may include a photometry engineer, AGL engineer, electrical technician, driver or operator, operations coordinator and QA/QC inspector. A survey is done in a live airport: obtain access, coordinate with ATC and operations, use an approved vehicle, keep radio contact, follow escort, speed and lighting restrictions, control FOD (loose tools, covers, cables, fasteners, packaging), and use the approved night airside procedure. Do not assume a test can go ahead because the equipment is ready. Cleaning, adjusting, re-lamping or replacing a fixture means working on a series circuit: isolate and lock off the circuit, prove it dead and follow the approved procedure; never open a fixture or disconnect an L-823 connector on an energised loop.

Before accepting a result ask

Which fixture and application? Which standard? Which CCR step and current? Which distance, angles, instrument and calibration? What conditions? Design vs measured value and deviation?

Survey checklist

Layout and fixture list, types identified, calibration valid, geometry and step confirmed, conditions recorded, IDs verified, outliers reviewed, raw data saved, results compared, report issued.

Commissioning CCR table

Record current and intensity at each step; illustrative Class 1, Style 2 (five-step) nominal currents 2.8, 3.4, 4.1, 5.2, 6.6 A. Follow the installed CCR specification.

Common mistakes

Measuring only the brightest point, using a lux meter alone, ignoring orientation, distance or CCR current, wrong fixture type, expired calibration, calling every low reading a fixture fault, no raw data, no retest.

Exam & Interview

EXAM / INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

What is photometry?

Measurement of light weighted to human visual response.

What are the units of intensity, flux, illuminance and luminance?

Candela, lumen, lux and cd/m².

What is an isocandela diagram?

A graphic of contours of equal luminous intensity showing beam shape, centre and spread.

Why does photometry matter in AGL?

To verify intensity, beam distribution, colour and alignment against the applicable requirement.

Can a light be on but fail photometry?

Yes: intensity, colour, beam or aiming may be wrong.

What can cause low photometric output?

Contamination, ageing, electrical problems, optical damage, misalignment.

Why does CCR current matter?

Fixture electrical input affects light output.

Photometry vs radiometry?

Photometry weights light by human vision; radiometry measures optical radiation physically.

Lumen vs candela?

Lumen is total flux; candela is intensity in a direction.

Why use a mobile photometric system?

Fast, accurate fixture-by-fixture measurement with misalignment indication and location.

Why measure before and after cleaning?

To know the real condition and verify the cleaning restored output.

What is the FAA 70% criterion?

A maintenance target: in-pavement runway lights below 70% of required minimum output at full intensity should be cleaned or serviced. Not a universal acceptance limit.

What are the key PAPI photometric parameters?

Intensity, colour, red/white transition, beam angle and aiming.

Why does the red/white transition matter?

It produces the indication the pilot uses to judge the approach path.

Why trend photometric data?

To spot degradation over time and support condition-based maintenance.

One fixture is low, others normal: what do you check?

Fixture, optics, connector, transformer, secondary circuit, alignment.

All fixtures on a circuit are low: what do you check?

CCR output current and step setting first (all fixtures on a series loop carry the same current), then transformer or fixture type mismatch and widespread contamination.

Beam shifted but centre intensity acceptable: likely cause?

A mechanical or optical alignment error.

Output rises a lot after lens cleaning: what does it mean?

The deficiency was largely optical contamination.

Why is a lux meter not enough?

AGL assessment needs candela, beam distribution, angles, colour and position.

Final Study Takeaway

FINAL STUDY TAKEAWAY

AGL photometry is the optical verification layer of an airport lighting system: measuring the right amount of light, in the right direction, with the right colour, from the right fixture, under the right operating condition, and proving it with calibrated data.

AGL photometry = intensity + beam + colour + alignment + electrical condition + calibrated measurement + analysis + compliance.

Align Before You Conclude Anything

Method

A fixture turned slightly off axis can look like a weak one. Check aim and level before judging the optics.

Clean, Measure, Then Decide

Maintenance

Contamination is the simplest explanation for low readings. Record values before and after cleaning so the loss that is recoverable is visible.

Settings
