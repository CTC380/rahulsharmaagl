Product Overview

Approach Centreline Light

What It Is

Approach centreline lights form the principal longitudinal visual reference of many approach lighting systems, extending outward from the runway threshold along the extended runway centreline.

The centreline gives the pilot a key reference during the transition from instrument navigation to the visual portion of the approach.

It Assists With

  • Runway alignment
  • Approach-path recognition
  • Horizontal reference
  • Visual transition toward the runway
  • Runway orientation during night operations
  • Reduced-visibility operations

Selected According To

  • Approach category and runway classification
  • Approach lighting system type and length
  • Centreline light-bar and crossbar arrangement
  • Steady-burning or flashing configuration
  • Optical direction, colour, intensity
  • Mounting arrangement and spacing
  • Electrical circuit
  • Control and sequencing requirements
  • Project technical specification

The exact arrangement depends on the approach category, runway configuration, applicable lighting system and governing standard.

ICAO specifies a 900 m extended-centreline arrangement for a precision approach Category I system wherever practicable. FAA MALS/MALSF/MALSR systems use approach lighting along the extended runway centreline with system-specific steady-burning bars and sequenced flashers.

Function

A Visual Reference Leading to the Threshold

Primary Functions

  • Establishing runway alignment
  • Longitudinal approach guidance
  • Identifying the runway approach axis
  • Visual transition from instrument to visual flight
  • Horizontal reference through the approach area
  • Roll and alignment perception in configured systems

FAA MALSR

FAA describes MALSR as giving pilots runway alignment, height perception, roll guidance and horizontal references to support the visual portion of an instrument approach.

Simple Approach Lighting

Simple Approach Lighting System

ICAO

ICAO specifies fixed centreline lights, each a single light source or a barrette of at least 3 m. Where point sources form a barrette, 1.5 m spacing between adjacent lights has been found satisfactory. The row extends wherever possible for not less than 420 m from the threshold, with a crossbar of 18 m or 30 m at 300 m.

Phased Development

ICAO notes longer barrettes may be appropriate where future development into a precision approach system is anticipated — making centreline design particularly relevant during phased airport development.

Precision Approach

Precision Approach Centreline Lighting

Category I

ICAO specifies a row of centreline lights on the extended runway centreline, extending wherever possible to 900 m from the threshold, with crossbar elements per the applicable configuration.

Category II / III — Coordinate With

  • Crossbars and side rows
  • Sequenced flashing lights where provided
  • Runway threshold and centreline lights
  • PAPI / precision approach visual aids
  • ILS / other approach navigation systems
FAA Systems

FAA Approach Lighting Systems

MALSMedium Intensity Approach Lighting System.
MALSFMALS with Sequenced Flashing Lights.
MALSRMALS with Runway Alignment Indicator Lights.

MALSR Provides

A combination of steady-burning light bars and sequenced flashers giving runway alignment, height perception, roll guidance and horizontal references.

MALSR Centreline Arrangement

Nine sets of five white steady-burning light sources: seven bars at 200 ft intervals and two offset bars associated with the 1,000-ft bar. The sequenced-flasher array has five white flashing lights at 200-ft intervals on the extended centreline.

Take the exact configuration from the current approved FAA design and applicable system drawing.

Operation

Steady-Burning vs Sequenced-Flashing Elements

Steady-Burning Centreline Lights

Provide continuous longitudinal and horizontal visual references.

Sequenced Flashers

Provide dynamic directional information and help identify the approach path (MALSF, MALSR). The two functions are not interchangeable.

Flasher Integration — Coordinate

  • Flasher spacing and flash sequence
  • Synchronization and flash rate
  • Intensity and direction
  • Control system

Treat Flashers Separately

MALSR uses five sequenced flashers on the extended centreline. Treat flasher equipment as a separate functional element even when supplied with the complete system.

Light Colour

White Centreline, Verified Per System

Colour

White is the principal colour for the centreline reference. ICAO selects colour so the approach system is readily distinguishable from other aeronautical ground lighting and extraneous lighting. FAA MALSR uses white steady bars and white flashers, with green threshold lighting.

Verify Against

  • Approach system type
  • Runway threshold arrangement
  • Applicable authority
  • Project drawing
  • Manufacturer configuration
Optics

Optical Direction & Beam Distribution

Designed Around Viewing Geometry

  • Fixed and directional, or broad enough for the approach sector
  • Integrated within a light bar
  • Steady-burning or sequenced-flashing output

For simple systems ICAO requires the necessary azimuth visibility and adequate intensity for the visibility and ambient-light conditions.

Photometric Design Considers

  • Horizontal beam spread and vertical coverage
  • Main beam axis and viewing range
  • Aircraft approach angle
  • Centreline alignment
  • Ambient background lighting

The objective is a continuous, identifiable visual reference from the intended approach region without unnecessary optical spill.

Electrical Characteristics

Architecture Is System-Specific

Design May Include

  • Constant-current supply
  • Dedicated approach-lighting circuits
  • Series-circuit supply
  • Individual isolation transformers
  • Local control equipment
  • Sequenced-flasher control
  • Intensity switching, monitoring circuits, control cabinets

Do Not Assume

Approach lighting may use electrical architectures different from runway and taxiway series circuits. Match the product to the specific approach lighting system, not to a runway centreline light.

Series circuit: AGL Power / CCR → Primary Circuit → Isolating Transformer → Secondary Connection → Approach Centreline Light
Centralized: Control Panel / ALCMS → Approach Lighting Control → Steady-Burning / Flasher Circuit → Approach Centreline System

The actual system architecture is project-specific.

Standards & References

Governing Standards

ICAO

Annex 14 Vol. I is the 9th Edition (July 2022). Amendment 18 took effect on 4 August 2025; its aerodrome-design and visual-aids provisions apply from 27 November 2025 (ground handling from 26 November 2026, obstacle limitation surfaces from 21 November 2030). Annex 14 distinguishes simple and precision approach lighting systems. Simple: fixed centreline lights, single sources or barrettes of at least 3 m. Category I: centreline row on the extended centreline, wherever possible 900 m from the threshold, with the applicable crossbar arrangement. Verify the edition and amendments for each project.

FAA

FAA Order JO 6850.2C (Visual Guidance Lighting Systems, 30 August 2022) gives the installation criteria for approach lighting systems including MALS, MALSF and MALSR.

AC 150/5340-30J carries installation details for MALSF and other airport visual aids.

AC 150/5345-46F is the active fixture specification; its L-850 series covers in-pavement runway fixtures, including L-850A/L-850B for runway centreline and touchdown zone and L-850E, the unidirectional threshold light introduced for use with medium-intensity approach lighting. These are in-pavement (semi-flush) fixture types. Elevated approach-light fixtures and the sequenced flashers (AC 150/5345-51B) are separate types. Confirm the classification from the approved system.

FAQ

Approach Centreline Light — Frequently Asked Questions

What does an approach centreline light do?

Approach centreline lights form the principal longitudinal visual reference of many approach lighting systems, extending outward from the runway threshold along the extended runway centreline. They assist with runway alignment, approach-path recognition, horizontal reference, the visual transition toward the runway, and runway orientation during night and reduced-visibility operations.

What does ICAO specify for approach centreline lights?

Annex 14 distinguishes simple and precision approach lighting systems. A simple system uses fixed centreline lights, each a single light source or a barrette at least 3 m long (1.5 m spacing between point sources has been found satisfactory), extending wherever possible not less than 420 m from the threshold, with a crossbar of 18 m or 30 m at 300 m. A Category I precision system has a centreline row on the extended runway centreline, wherever possible 900 m from the threshold, with the applicable crossbar arrangement. Verify the edition and amendments for each project.

What are FAA MALS, MALSF and MALSR approach lighting systems?

MALS is the Medium Intensity Approach Lighting System, MALSF is MALS with Sequenced Flashing Lights, and MALSR is MALS with Runway Alignment Indicator Lights. FAA describes MALSR as a combination of steady-burning light bars and sequenced flashers giving pilots runway alignment, height perception, roll guidance and horizontal references for the visual portion of an instrument approach.

What is the MALSR centreline arrangement?

The centreline has nine sets of five white steady-burning light sources: seven bars at 200 ft intervals and two offset bars associated with the 1,000-ft bar. The sequenced-flasher array has five white flashing lights at 200-ft intervals on the extended centreline. Take the exact configuration from the current approved FAA design and the applicable system drawing.

What is the difference between steady-burning centreline lights and sequenced flashers?

Steady-burning centreline lights provide continuous longitudinal and horizontal visual references, while sequenced flashers provide dynamic directional information that helps identify the approach path (MALSF, MALSR). The two functions are not interchangeable, so treat flasher equipment as a separate functional element even when supplied with the complete system, and coordinate flasher spacing and sequence, synchronization and flash rate, intensity and direction, and the control system.

What colour are approach centreline lights?

White is the principal colour for the centreline reference. ICAO selects colour so the approach system is readily distinguishable from other aeronautical ground lighting and extraneous lighting, and FAA MALSR uses white steady bars and white flashers with green threshold lighting. Verify the colour against the approach system type, runway threshold arrangement, applicable authority, project drawing and manufacturer configuration.

How are approach centreline lights powered?

The architecture is system-specific. It may include a constant-current supply, dedicated approach-lighting circuits, series-circuit supply with individual isolation transformers, local control equipment and sequenced-flasher control. Approach lighting may use electrical architectures different from runway and taxiway series circuits, so match the product to the specific approach lighting system and not to a runway centreline light.

Do approach centreline lights need to be frangible?

ICAO requires elevated approach lights and their supports to be frangible; beyond 300 m from the threshold, where a support is taller than 12 m, frangibility applies to the top 12 m only (Annex 14, Chapter 5, confirm against your edition). FAA guidance identifies low-impact-resistant support structures for approach-light installations. Installation may use elevated supports and towers, frangible or low-level supports, modular light bars, or flush arrangements where approved.

How does an approach centreline light differ from a runway centreline light?

An approach centreline light gives approach alignment and visual transition on the extended runway centreline outside the threshold, is typically white or system-specific, is usually elevated or system-specific, is often associated with crossbars, and may include sequenced flashers; its design driver is the approach category and system. A runway centreline light gives longitudinal guidance on the runway pavement centreline, changes from white to alternating red/white and then red toward the runway end, is typically inset, and is driven by runway classification and operation.

What are the common faults in approach centreline lighting?

Typical symptoms are a light that is off, low light output, incorrect alignment, an uneven centreline appearance, a flasher not operating and an inconsistent approach pattern. Troubleshoot in sequence: approach control system, CCR or power supply, primary circuit, transformer or local power, secondary connection, centreline fixture, light source, optical alignment and photometry. Where flashers are installed, check the sequencer, flasher controller, flasher circuit, individual flasher and synchronization.

What should be confirmed before ordering approach centreline lights?

Confirm the runway designation and threshold, approach category, approach lighting system, system length, centreline light-bar configuration, crossbar and side-row configuration, steady-burning and sequenced-flasher requirements, light colour, beam distribution, intensity steps, light spacing, support structure and frangibility, circuit and transformer arrangement, control interface, and the applicable standard. Request the datasheet, photometric report, centreline layout information, mounting drawing, frangibility information and compliance certificates.

Engineering Note
