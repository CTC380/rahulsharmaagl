Conventional Signage VS LED Signage

Conventional illuminated signage generally uses fluorescent lamps or other traditional light sources, while LED signage uses solid-state LEDs. In AGL, both must provide the required sign colour, luminance, uniformity, legibility and visibility — LED mainly changes the light source and electrical architecture, not the underlying photometric requirement.

03 · Energy Consumption

One of LED's Major Advantages

For a large airport containing many continuously operated signs, lower power consumption can provide significant energy savings. Actual savings depend on the sign size, source rating, duty cycle and intensity requirements.

Conventional

Electrical energy → lamp → light + substantial heat.

LED

Electrical energy → LED driver → LED array → light + heat.

04 · Maintenance

Shifted, Not Eliminated

Conventional signage

  • Lamp replacement
  • Ballast inspection/replacement
  • Cleaning & wiring inspection
  • Connector & sign-face inspection
  • Photometric/luminance checks

LED signage

  • LED/driver inspection
  • Sign-face cleaning
  • Electrical & surge-protection checks
  • Connector/wiring inspection
  • Functional & luminance/uniformity verification

LED reduces routine light-source replacement but does not eliminate sign maintenance.

05 · Source Life

Longer Life, More Dependencies

Conventional

Lamp → operating hours → aging → reduced output → failure.

LED

LED → operating hours → gradual degradation → reduced output over time.

LEDs generally have a much longer useful life, but the driver, thermal conditions and other components can determine the actual service life of the complete sign.

08 · Colour

A Whole-Sign Requirement, Not Just the Source

Airport signs depend on prescribed colours, and different sign functions use different colour combinations. The colour requirement applies to the complete sign, not merely the LED or lamp. The design must account for source spectrum, filters, sign-face material, optical transmission, aging and environmental effects.

09 · Electrical Architecture

The LED Driver Becomes a Key Design Element

Conventional

Supply → ballast / conventional control → lamp → sign.

LED

Supply → protection → LED driver → LED array → sign.

The LED driver should be assessed for input voltage, current, power consumption, power factor where applicable, surge protection, thermal behavior, failure mode and control compatibility.

10 · AGL Series Circuit Consideration

Not a Drop-In Replacement

If the signage is supplied from an AGL constant-current series circuit, the LED sign must be compatible with that circuit architecture.

The designer must verify CCR compatibility, isolation transformer compatibility, current range, minimum/maximum load, driver behavior, intensity-step operation and fault response.

An LED sign is not automatically a drop-in replacement for a conventional sign simply because the mechanical dimensions are identical.

11 · Failure Modes

Different Ways to Go Dark

Conventional

Typical failure: lamp failure → sign becomes dark or partially illuminated. Other possibilities include ballast failure or wiring faults.

LED

Possible failures: driver failure, LED-array failure, individual LED degradation, surge damage, thermal degradation, wiring/connector failure. A well-designed system may use multiple LEDs so one element's failure doesn't necessarily darken the whole sign.

13 · Visibility & Environment

Both Technologies Face the Same Elements

Airport signs operate outdoors and may be exposed to rain, dust, UV radiation, temperature extremes, humidity, vibration, wind, cleaning chemicals and electrical surges. Both technologies need suitable enclosures, sealing, corrosion protection, mechanical strength and environmental protection — LED technology does not remove these requirements.

20 · Engineering Verdict

Evaluate the Complete System, Not Just the Source

Engineering Verdict

Conventional signage relies on traditional lamps and associated control gear, whereas LED signage uses LED arrays and electronic drivers to provide the required airport-sign illumination with longer source life, lower energy consumption and reduced routine maintenance.

For AGL, the retrofit decision should be based on the complete system — electrical compatibility, luminance, uniformity, colour, intensity control, environmental performance and applicable aerodrome requirements — not simply on replacing the conventional lamp with an LED source.

ConventionalEstablished AGL compatibility, simpler electronics, lower initial cost, higher lifecycle cost.
LEDLower energy and maintenance over time, but compatibility with series circuits, driver behavior and thermal design must be verified.

21 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an LED sign a direct replacement for a conventional sign of the same size?

Not automatically. Mechanical dimensions being identical does not guarantee electrical compatibility with the existing series circuit, CCR, isolation transformer or driver behavior — each must be verified.

Does LED signage eliminate maintenance?

No. LED reduces routine light-source replacement but maintenance shifts toward driver inspection, surge-protection checks, and luminance/uniformity verification rather than disappearing entirely.

Does a working LED sign automatically meet photometric requirements?

No. Electrical operation does not equal signage photometric compliance — luminance, uniformity, colour and legibility must be separately verified.

Do LEDs still require thermal management?

Yes. LED signage still produces heat, and poor thermal design can cause LED degradation, reduced luminance, driver failure and colour shift.

What is the main design risk in a conventional-to-LED sign retrofit?

Assuming equivalence without verifying electrical compatibility, luminance, uniformity, colour and environmental performance under the actual AGL operating conditions.

Is LED signage always cheaper overall?

Initial cost is generally higher for LED, but lifecycle cost is generally lower due to reduced energy consumption, longer source life and lower maintenance labour — actual savings depend on sign size, duty cycle and intensity requirements.

22 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Considerations

For an actual airport project, verify the current applicable national and ICAO photometric, colour and electrical requirements before treating this comparison as project design guidance.

Sign Photometric Requirements

Luminance, uniformity, colour, contrast, legibility and visibility apply to the complete sign, regardless of light-source technology.

Series Circuit / CCR Compatibility

LED signs supplied from an AGL constant-current series circuit must be verified for isolation transformer, current range, load and fault response.

FAT / SAT Procedures

Both technologies require luminance, uniformity, colour and functional verification at FAT and SAT; LED adds driver and thermal/surge verification.

Final Engineering Note

Evaluate a retrofit against the complete system requirement, not against light-source replacement alone — verify with the applicable national aerodrome and ICAO references.

Conventional signage = traditional lamp + ballast + sign face. LED signage = LED array + driver + optics + sign face. Both must satisfy the same colour, luminance, uniformity, legibility and visibility requirement — LED changes the light source and electrical architecture, not the underlying compliance standard.
