STR-01 AGL Innovation Strategy
Purpose

Establishes the overall strategic direction for innovation in Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL). The strategy defines what problems should be solved, which technologies should be developed or adopted, what capabilities should be built, and how innovation should create measurable engineering and airport value.

Key Areas
  • AGL system modernization
  • Intelligent AGL
  • Digital transformation
  • AI-enabled engineering
  • IoT and connected assets
  • Predictive maintenance
  • Advanced photometry
  • Automation
  • Robotics and autonomous inspection
  • Sustainable AGL
  • Product innovation
  • Engineering-method innovation
  • Safety and reliability improvement
  • Asset lifecycle optimization
  • Digital documentation
  • Future airport integration
  • Research and technology development
  • Commercialization of validated innovations
Strategic Principle

AGL innovation should not be technology-driven alone. The preferred sequence is:

Engineering ProblemOpportunityTechnologyConceptValidationImplementationMeasurable Benefit

This is consistent with the hub's 12-stage operating cycle: Problem → Idea → Technology Review → Concept → Feasibility → Prototype → PoC → Pilot → Validation → Technical Review → Product/Engineering Implementation → Knowledge Publication.

STR-03 Innovation Roadmap
Purpose

Defines where AGL technology should move over time, structured as five sequential phases.

Phase 1 — Foundation

Data standardization, asset databases, failure databases, digital documentation, engineering calculators, technology benchmarking, basic automation.

Phase 2 — Connected AGL

Smart sensors, connected fixtures, CCR monitoring, transformer monitoring, cable-condition monitoring, remote asset monitoring, digital dashboards.

Phase 3 — Intelligent AGL

AI fault diagnosis, predictive maintenance, automated inspection, AI photometry, automated compliance checking, asset-health prediction.

Phase 4 — Autonomous AGL

Robotic inspection, autonomous measurement, automated testing, automated photometry, autonomous maintenance support, intelligent decision-support systems.

Phase 5 — Future AGL

Autonomous airport infrastructure, fully connected airfield, digital twins, AI-assisted operations, advanced communications, climate-resilient systems, next-generation airport energy systems.

STR-05 Airport Technology Priorities

STR-18 Annual Innovation Planning
Purpose

At the beginning of each year, establish an Annual AGL Innovation Plan that ties the year's activity back to strategy, objectives, and roadmap.

Annual Planning Cycle
  • January–February: review previous year's innovation performance, review failures, review technology trends, identify new problems
  • March: identify innovation opportunities, conduct technology-gap analysis
  • April: rank innovation projects, approve annual portfolio
  • May–June: begin concept development, begin technology reviews
  • July–September: prototype development, PoC execution, laboratory testing
  • October: pilot implementation, performance validation
  • November: technical review, ROI assessment, implementation decision
  • December: annual innovation report, lessons learned, next-year roadmap

Most Important Design Principle

Division 01 should remain strategic. It defines what AGL innovation should focus on and where it should go — not the technology mechanics of AI, IoT, robotics or photometry, and not the prototyping/validation pipeline itself.

The technology domains move into Divisions 02–07 (Smart Lighting, AI, IoT, Digital Engineering, Robotics, Sustainability), engineering and product innovation move into Divisions 08–17, and the prototyping, PoC, pilot and validation mechanics move into Divisions 18–25. These functions are deliberately kept separate.

Its strongest connection is to the AGL Failure Database, which supplies recurring engineering problems as raw input, and to the AGL Research Papers, Product Encyclopedia, Design Manual, Engineering Calculators, Asset Lifecycle Guide, and Learning / Training Academy, which each receive the validated output once it clears the hub's 12-stage cycle.

Division 01 acts as the strategic "control tower" for the entire AGL Innovation Hub, while Divisions 02–25 provide the technology, research, prototype, validation, commercialization and knowledge-development engines.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Innovation Strategy & Roadmap in the AGL Innovation Hub.

Q1What is Division 01 — Innovation Strategy & Roadmap in the AGL Innovation Hub?

Division 01 is the strategic control tower of the AGL Innovation Hub. Across 18 topics (STR-01 to STR-18) it sets what AGL innovation should focus on, which engineering problems are worth solving, what capabilities should be built and where the technology should go over the short, medium and long term. Divisions 02–25 then provide the technology, research, prototype, validation and knowledge engines.

Q2Why does Division 01 stay strategic rather than technical?

Division 01 defines what innovation should focus on and why, not the technology mechanics. Technology domains such as Smart Lighting, AI, IoT, Digital Engineering, Robotics and Sustainability sit in Divisions 02–07, product, engineering and automation innovation in Divisions 08–17, and the prototyping, proof-of-concept, pilot, validation, case-study and master-database work in Divisions 18–25. Keeping these functions separate keeps strategy from being diluted by technical detail.

Q3Which topics does Division 01 cover?

The 18 topics are AGL Innovation Strategy, Innovation Objectives, Innovation Roadmap, Future AGL Technology Roadmap, Airport Technology Priorities, Innovation Opportunity Identification, Technology-Gap Analysis, Engineering Problem Identification, Innovation Portfolio Management, Innovation Priority Ranking, Short-, Medium- and Long-Term Innovation Priorities, Innovation Investment Planning, Innovation KPI Framework, Innovation Governance, Innovation Maturity Assessment and Annual Innovation Planning.

Q4What sequence should AGL innovation follow?

AGL innovation should not be technology-driven alone. The preferred sequence runs from Engineering Problem to Opportunity to Technology. The master workflow expands this into problem, opportunity, technology-gap analysis, prioritization, technology review, concept development, feasibility study, prototype, proof of concept, pilot project, validation, technical review, implementation or product development, knowledge publication, and monitoring and continuous improvement.

Q5What are the five phases of the AGL innovation roadmap?

The roadmap has five sequential phases: Foundation (data standardization, asset and failure databases, digital documentation, engineering calculators), Connected AGL (smart sensors, CCR, transformer and cable monitoring, remote asset monitoring), Intelligent AGL (AI fault diagnosis, predictive maintenance, automated inspection, AI photometry), Autonomous AGL (robotic inspection, autonomous measurement and testing) and Future AGL (digital twins, a fully connected airfield, climate-resilient systems and next-generation airport energy systems).

Q6How are airport technology priorities ranked?

Innovation is aligned with actual airport operational requirements across five priority tiers: Priority 1 Safety, Priority 2 Reliability, Priority 3 Operational Efficiency, Priority 4 Sustainability and Priority 5 Digitalization. Safety covers items such as runway lighting reliability, stop-bar integrity, PAPI availability and fault detection, so safety-critical needs come ahead of efficiency and digitalization goals.

Q7What is a technology-gap analysis?

A technology-gap analysis determines the difference between current AGL capability and the required future capability. It assesses areas such as performance, reliability, safety, energy efficiency, automation, data availability, monitoring capability, maintainability, lifecycle cost, digital integration and regulatory readiness. In the worked example, manual fixture inspection has limited frequency and depends on human observation, which points to computer vision and AI as the technology for an AI-based AGL fixture inspection system.

Q8Why maintain an AGL Engineering Problem Register?

Engineering Problem Identification is one of the most important areas of Division 01, because innovation should start from real engineering problems. The register covers problem categories such as design, installation, commissioning, testing, photometry, maintenance, reliability, safety, product, procurement, environmental, operational, documentation, data and integration problems. Each record captures fields including problem ID, affected system and equipment, frequency, severity, operational, safety and cost impact, existing limitations, potential innovation, priority, responsible engineer and status.

Q9How are innovation projects managed as a portfolio?

The hub manages innovations as a portfolio rather than as isolated ideas, across eight stages: Research, Concept Development, Prototype, Proof of Concept, Pilot, Validation, Implementation and Commercialization. A portfolio dashboard tracks the number of ideas, active projects, prototypes, PoCs, pilots, validated innovations, commercialization candidates, failed and suspended projects, investment and expected benefit.

Q10How are innovation projects prioritized?

Not every innovation should receive equal investment, so projects are scored against weighted criteria: safety impact 20%, reliability impact 15%, operational benefit 15%, and technical feasibility, lifecycle-cost benefit, energy or sustainability benefit and strategic importance at 10% each, with scalability and commercial potential at 5% each. Projects are then classified as P1 Critical, P2 High, P3 Medium or P4 Long-term.

Q11What should an innovation investment plan cover?

Every major innovation should have an investment plan covering the full cost from research through deployment. Components include research, engineering, software, hardware, prototype, testing, laboratory, field-trial, certification or compliance, training, deployment and maintenance costs. The investment decision weighs investment against expected benefit, risk, ROI and scalability.

Q12How is innovation governed, and what is stage-gate governance?

Governance defines who decides, who reviews and who is accountable. The structure has an Innovation Steering Committee (direction, investment and priority approval, portfolio review), a Technical Review Board (feasibility, safety, reliability, standards, validation), Innovation Project Owners (execution and reporting) and Subject Matter Experts. Under stage-gate governance a project moves through Idea, Screening, Feasibility, Prototype, PoC, Pilot, Validation and Implementation, and it should not progress to the next stage without technical evidence.

Q13Which KPIs and targets does the Innovation KPI Framework use?

The framework tracks pipeline health, output and benefit realization: counts of ideas, problems identified, technology reviews, active projects, prototypes, PoCs, pilots, validated, implemented and commercialized innovations, alongside innovation investment, ROI, cost and energy savings, failure reduction, MTBF improvement, MTTR reduction, safety improvement, inspection-time reduction and maintenance-cost reduction. Overall hub targets include 500+ innovation concepts, 300+ technology reviews, 100+ PoCs, 100+ prototype designs, 200+ innovation case studies, 100+ future-trend reports and 100+ patent concepts.

Q14How do the maturity assessment and annual innovation plan work?

The maturity assessment rates the organization's ability to generate and implement innovation on a five-level scale: Initial, Repeatable, Defined, Managed and Optimized. Annual Innovation Planning then sets an Annual AGL Innovation Plan each year that ties activity back to strategy, objectives and roadmap. The cycle runs from reviewing the previous year and identifying problems in January–February, through gap analysis, ranking and approval, prototyping, pilots and validation, to the annual innovation report and next-year roadmap in December.
