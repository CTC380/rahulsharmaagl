Documentation & Maintenance Records

The document-control and traceability backbone of the AGL Maintenance Handbook. This division standardizes how maintenance, testing, and asset history are recorded, how documents are controlled and revised, and how records are retained — turning every maintenance activity into a controlled, auditable engineering record.

Fundamental principle: if maintenance is performed but cannot be demonstrated through a controlled record, the maintenance activity is not fully traceable. This division is critical because maintenance is incomplete without traceable records.

Maintenance Record Quality Check

Before closing any maintenance record, verify it against these four checkpoints.

Identification

  • Correct asset
  • Correct location
  • Correct circuit

Maintenance

  • Maintenance activity recorded
  • Defect recorded where applicable
  • Parts / components recorded

Testing

  • Required testing completed
  • Actual results recorded
  • Instrument identified

Closure & Document Control

  • Final condition and return-to-service status recorded
  • Technician and reviewer identified where required
  • Correct form / SOP revision, drawing revision identified where applicable

Documentation Failure Conditions

The following are treated as documentation deficiencies wherever a record is missing this information.

Missing asset identification Missing maintenance date Missing maintenance description Missing test result where testing was required Unidentified replacement component Missing technician identification Missing approval / review where required Uncontrolled checklist Obsolete SOP being used Missing calibration information Missing post-maintenance verification Inability to trace the maintenance to the asset
Core Principle

Every maintenance activity shall leave a traceable record linked to the asset, the work performed, the test results, and the final condition.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about documentation & maintenance records, based on the topics in this division.

8 QUESTIONS
Q1What is Documentation & Maintenance Records in the AGL Maintenance Handbook?

It is the document-control and traceability backbone of the handbook. It standardizes how maintenance, testing and asset history are recorded, how documents are controlled and revised, and how records are kept traceable to the asset.

Q2What does Division 21 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 21 (Documentation & Maintenance Records) contains 30 topics in four groups: Group A Maintenance Records (10 topics), Group B Testing Records (7 topics), Group C Asset Records (7 topics) and Group D Document Control (6 topics).

Q3What is the fundamental principle of maintenance documentation?

If maintenance is performed but cannot be demonstrated through a controlled record, the activity is not fully traceable. Every maintenance activity shall leave a traceable record linked to the asset, the work performed, the test results and the final condition.

Q4What is the documentation chain for an asset?

Asset, Inspection, Defect/Condition, Maintenance Action, Testing, Verification, Return to Service, Asset History, and Trend/Future Maintenance. Each record preserves one continuous engineering thread for the asset.

Q5How is a maintenance record kept traceable?

Every record should be traceable end to end through the asset ID, inspection or failure reference, work order, maintenance record, test record, parts and components, post-maintenance verification and closure, so records never become isolated documents disconnected from the asset.

Q6Which record fields are mandatory?

Mandatory fields are asset ID, location and equipment; date and maintenance type; work performed; final condition; return-to-service status; and technician. Defect or finding, parts replaced, test performed and result, and work-order reference are required where applicable, and supervisor or reviewer as applicable.

Q7What should be checked before a maintenance record is closed?

Four checkpoints: identification (correct asset, location and circuit), maintenance (activity, defect and parts recorded), testing (required testing completed, actual results recorded, instrument identified), and closure and document control (final condition and return-to-service status, technician and reviewer, and the correct form, SOP or drawing revision).

Q8What counts as a documentation failure?

Twelve conditions are treated as deficiencies, including missing asset identification, date or maintenance description, a missing test result where testing was required, an unidentified replacement component, missing technician identification, missing approval or review, an uncontrolled checklist, an obsolete SOP in use, missing calibration information, missing post-maintenance verification, and inability to trace the maintenance to the asset.
