Methodology

How This Calculator Works

The engineering basis behind each result, kept visible so the numbers are auditable rather than a black box.

Core spacing calculation

For a uniform section of design length L against a maximum permitted interval Smax, the number of intervals is found by rounding up — never ordinary rounding, since any excess over the maximum is a non-compliance:

N = ⌈ L / Smax ⌉  →  Sactual = L / N

Where a light position is defined at both ends of the section, positions per side are N + 1, and both sides together give the nominal longitudinal count 2(N + 1). This is labelled nominal because it assumes a simple uniform layout — it is not automatically the final bill-of-quantities figure.

Threshold / runway-end lights are separate

FAA AC 150/5340-30 treats threshold/runway-end lights as their own transverse arrangement — perpendicular to the runway axis, on their own spacing rule, not the 2–10 ft edge-light offset. The planning quantities used here are 3 per group for visual operation and 4 per group for instrument operation; confirm them against the threshold-lighting tables of the AC edition in force (currently AC 150/5340-30J). Two symmetrical groups at each of the two ends give 12 or 16 combination fixtures in total. They are counted as one combined threshold/runway-end set, and kept out of the longitudinal N + 1 total to avoid double-counting. The suggested count follows edge-light system and runway use: LIRL → 3; MIRL → 3 (visual) or 4 (instrument); HIRL → 4; manual override always allowed.

Existing-installation check

For field verification, enter the number of positions actually installed per side. The achieved spacing is back-calculated and compared to the applicable maximum:

Sexisting = L / (Pexisting − 1)

A PASS/FAIL result follows directly from whether that achieved spacing sits within Smax.

Special conditions are flagged, not folded in

Displaced thresholds and runway intersections both change the lighting arrangement in ways a simple formula shouldn't silently absorb. A displaced threshold shows red edge lights toward the approach ahead of the threshold; an intersection may use irregular spacing or omitted positions where pilot guidance remains adequate. Both are surfaced as review flags rather than recalculated automatically.

FAQ

Runway Edge Light Spacing Calculator: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on edge-light spacing, nominal quantity and the existing-installation check.

What does the Runway Edge Light Spacing Calculator do?

It calculates the number of runway edge-light intervals and positions for a uniform runway section against the maximum permitted spacing of the design basis you select. It reports the actual spacing, the nominal longitudinal edge-light count for both sides, a preliminary threshold and runway-end fixture count, a layout diagram and a PDF report. It can also check an existing installation.

What is the maximum edge-light spacing under ICAO, FAA and project criteria?

ICAO Annex 14 allows 60 m for instrument runways and 100 m for non-instrument runways. FAA AC 150/5340-30 allows 200 ft (60.96 m) for standard edge lighting. Under a Project-Specific basis you enter the approved criterion in its own unit, and the calculator never defaults to FAA values for a non-FAA project. Check these values against the exact edition of the standard that applies to your project.

How is the number of edge lights calculated?

For a uniform section of length L and maximum interval Smax, the number of intervals is N = ⌈L ÷ Smax⌉, rounded up because any excess over the maximum is a non-compliance, and the actual spacing is Sactual = L ÷ N. With a light at both ends, positions per side are N + 1, so the nominal longitudinal count for both sides is 2(N + 1). For example, a 1,000 m runway at ICAO 60 m gives 17 intervals at 58.82 m.

Are threshold and runway-end lights included in the edge-light count?

No. Threshold and runway-end lights form their own transverse arrangement and are counted separately, never added into N + 1. The suggested planning quantity is 3 lights per group for visual operation and 4 per group for instrument operation; confirm it against the threshold-lighting requirements of the standard that applies to your project. Two groups at each of the two ends give 12 or 16 combination fixtures in total. You can override the group count if the project calls for a different one.

Does the edge-light system type (LIRL, MIRL, HIRL) change the spacing?

No. The lighting-intensity system does not change the spacing maximum. Together with the runway classification it sets the suggested threshold and runway-end group count. Under ICAO the runway classification does change the spacing maximum, with 100 m for non-instrument and 60 m for all instrument categories. For FAA and project bases it does not.

How does the existing-installation check work?

Enter the number of positions actually installed per side. The calculator back-calculates the achieved spacing as Sexisting = L ÷ (Pexisting − 1) and compares it with the applicable maximum, giving a PASS or FAIL depending on whether that spacing is within Smax.

How are displaced thresholds and runway intersections handled?

They are flagged for review and are not folded into the count. A displaced threshold shows red edge lights toward the approach ahead of the threshold, and an intersection may use irregular spacing or omitted positions where pilot guidance remains adequate. Check both against the approved lighting layout drawing.

Is the quantity a final bill of quantities?

No. The quantity is nominal and preliminary. It assumes a simple uniform section with a defined light position at both ends, and the threshold and runway-end counts assume the same configuration at both ends. Verify the final quantity and any asymmetry between ends against the approved lighting layout drawing before using it as a BOQ figure. For Indian projects the design-basis order is the project specification or approved drawing, then the applicable DGCA requirement, then ICAO Annex 14 where adopted, and FAA only where the project specification calls for it.
