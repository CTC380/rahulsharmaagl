AGL-CS-089 — Sign Water Ingress

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A sign environmental failure investigation where water ingress through deteriorated seals caused condensation, corrosion, and optical degradation — from leak testing through gasket replacement, electronic restoration, and enclosure integrity verification.

Primary Skill

Investigation of water ingress into illuminated airfield signs and restoration of long-term enclosure reliability

Important: This is an engineering simulation created for training. It is not presented as an actual airport incident. All readings, values, dates and outcomes are illustrative, and no airport, organisation, person or manufacturer is identified or implied.

1Case Study Overview

Following a period of heavy monsoon rainfall, maintenance personnel reported that several illuminated taxiway signs exhibited condensation inside the sign enclosure, reduced brightness, and intermittent illumination.

One runway location sign failed completely within two weeks of the first condensation report.

The associated Constant Current Regulator (CCR) remained healthy, field cables were satisfactory, and no external mechanical damage was observed.

Because repeated failures occurred after rainfall events, a detailed investigation was initiated covering:

  • Enclosure sealing.
  • Gasket condition.
  • Drainage.
  • Moisture ingress paths.
  • Internal condensation.
  • LED driver condition.
  • Corrosion.
  • Insulation resistance.
  • Optical contamination.

The investigation identified water ingress through deteriorated enclosure gaskets and cable-entry seals, resulting in progressive moisture accumulation, corrosion of electronic components, and optical degradation.

This case demonstrates that water ingress is a progressive environmental reliability failure that can damage electrical, optical, and mechanical components simultaneously.

3System Background

An illuminated sign enclosure includes:

  • Weatherproof housing.
  • Front optical panel.
  • Rear access cover.
  • Environmental gaskets.
  • Cable-entry seals.
  • Drainage provisions.
  • Ventilation system.
  • LED driver.
  • LED arrays.
  • Internal wiring.

Water ingress may occur through:

  • Gasket deterioration.
  • Cable glands.
  • Damaged seals.
  • Cracked housings.
  • Improper maintenance.
  • Condensation.
  • Pressure cycling.
  • Drainage blockage.

Environmental protection is critical for electronic and optical reliability.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The sign water-ingress failure was caused by deterioration of enclosure gaskets and cable-entry seals, resulting in:

  • Rainwater ingress.
  • Internal condensation.
  • Corrosion.
  • Insulation deterioration.
  • Optical contamination.
  • LED driver degradation.

The primary failure mechanism was loss of enclosure environmental integrity.

11Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Removed all moisture.
  2. Replaced enclosure gaskets.
  3. Replaced cable-entry seals.
  4. Cleaned optical surfaces.
  5. Replaced corroded driver electronics.
  6. Replaced damaged connectors.
  7. Restored drainage paths.
  8. Performed leak testing.
  9. Conducted insulation testing.
  10. Implemented enclosure inspection intervals.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: Condensation inside an illuminated sign. Reduced brightness. Repeated failures after rainfall. Corrosion visible internally. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace the LED module.
B. Increase sign brightness.
C. Perform enclosure leak testing, inspect gaskets and cable-entry seals, measure internal humidity, assess corrosion, verify insulation resistance, and restore enclosure integrity.
D. Replace the transformer.
Correct answer: C. Condensation, corrosion, and repeated failures after rain point to an enclosure integrity problem. Leak testing and sealing restoration must be performed before electronic components are replaced.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about sign water ingress, condensation and enclosure sealing.

Q1What is AGL-CS-089?

AGL-CS-089 is an engineering training case study. It covers illuminated signs that developed condensation, reduced brightness and failures after heavy monsoon rainfall. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2How does water ingress damage a sign?

It affects electrical, optical and mechanical parts at once: condensation, corrosion of electronics, insulation loss and contaminated optics, leading to repeated failures.

Q3What did measurements show in AGL-CS-089?

Relative humidity inside the enclosure was 98% against an expected level below 60%. Insulation resistance was 1.8 MΩ against above 100 MΩ, and sign luminance was 58% of the reference value.

Q4Where was water entering?

Controlled water-spray testing found leakage through the cable-entry gland, the rear-access gasket and the upper enclosure seal, so there were multiple ingress paths.

Q5What did the internal inspection find?

Water droplets, a condensation film, PCB corrosion, oxidized terminals and optical-panel contamination such as diffuser haze and water spotting.

Q6What external signs were visible beforehand?

Minor gasket aging, cable-gland discoloration and partially blocked drainage holes. The housing looked generally intact, so damage was not obvious from outside.

Q7What was the root cause of AGL-CS-089?

Loss of enclosure environmental integrity from deteriorated gaskets and cable-entry seals, leading to rainwater ingress, condensation, corrosion, insulation deterioration, optical contamination and driver degradation.

Q8How were the signs repaired?

The team removed moisture, replaced gaskets, cable-entry seals, corroded driver electronics and damaged connectors, cleaned optical surfaces and restored drainage paths. They performed leak and insulation testing and set enclosure inspection intervals.

Q9What is the key lesson from this case?

Water ingress is a progressive reliability failure. Fix the seals and drainage, and inspect enclosures regularly, especially before and after rainy seasons.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

Water ingress into illuminated airfield signs is a progressive environmental reliability failure caused by deterioration of enclosure seals and cable-entry systems, and effective restoration requires elimination of the ingress path, removal of moisture damage, restoration of optical performance, and verification of enclosure integrity rather than only replacement of failed electronic components.

Systematic leak testing, humidity assessment, gasket inspection, corrosion evaluation, insulation testing, optical restoration, and post-repair environmental verification provide a complete engineering methodology for restoring long-term illuminated-sign reliability.

In AGL engineering, the most damaging sign failure is often not the failed LED driver itself, but the water that entered months earlier and quietly began destroying the entire enclosure from the inside.
