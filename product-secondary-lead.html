Product Overview

AGL Secondary Lead

What It Is

AGL Secondary Leads provide the flexible electrical connection between an airfield lighting isolating transformer and the associated light fixture.

The lead carries power from the secondary side of the transformer to the individual AGL fixture, so it must be selected according to the transformer interface, fixture requirements, connector arrangement, cable construction and installation environment.

What We Supply

Precision AGL Solutions supplies secondary lead configurations for runway, taxiway, approach and other compatible series-circuit airfield lighting applications.

The lead provides the flexible connection required where the transformer and fixture are installed separately, for example within a transformer pit or light base. Select it as a complete connection system rather than by lead length alone.

Compatibility

Transformer and Fixture Compatibility

Transformer Compatibility

The lead must match the secondary interface of the selected transformer. Confirm:

  • Transformer manufacturer
  • Transformer model
  • Transformer rating
  • Secondary connection arrangement
  • Secondary lead termination
  • Required connector type
  • Cable/lead dimensions

Do not select a lead solely on physical length. Verify the transformer interface and electrical characteristics.

Fixture Compatibility

The opposite end must be compatible with the airfield light fixture. Compatibility may depend on:

  • Fixture manufacturer
  • Fixture model
  • Fixture wattage
  • Fixture lead arrangement
  • Connector configuration
  • Termination method
  • Required electrical characteristics
  • Physical connection dimensions

For replacement projects, identify the existing fixture and connector before selecting the replacement lead.

Flexibility

The lead should permit:

Lead Length

Select According to the Physical Arrangement

Required Length May Depend On

  • Transformer location
  • Fixture location
  • Light base configuration
  • Transformer pit dimensions
  • Added secondary load (a long lead adds resistance and must stay within the transformer rating)
  • Cable routing
  • Connector position
  • Required maintenance flexibility

The lead should be long enough to permit proper installation and maintenance without creating excessive loose cable within the installation.

Installation Types

Inset, Elevated and Transformer Pit Installations

Inset Lights

The lead may be routed through the light base or associated installation arrangement. Consider:

  • Limited installation space
  • Cable bend radius
  • Fixture removal
  • Moisture exposure
  • Connector accessibility
  • Mechanical protection
  • Lead routing

Do not pinch the lead or subject it to excessive stress during fixture installation.

Elevated Lights

The lead may connect the transformer to a fixture on a base, stake or mounting assembly. Allow sufficient movement for maintenance while avoiding:

  • Excessive slack
  • Cable tension
  • Sharp bends
  • Abrasion
  • Mechanical interference

Transformer Pit

Route the lead to allow:

  • Transformer access
  • Fixture connection
  • Inspection
  • Maintenance
  • Drainage
  • Appropriate cable separation
  • Protection from mechanical damage

The lead should not obstruct the pit cover or other maintenance access components.

Safety

Electrical Safety — Isolation and Lock-Out

WARNING — never open a live series circuit. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage. Opening, cutting or disconnecting any part of an energised circuit can cause serious injury or death and damage equipment.

  • De-energise the circuit at the regulator and apply the site lock-out / tag-out (LOTO) procedure before any work
  • Where the circuit must be isolated or shorted, use the series circuit cut-out switch or approved isolation device — not an improvised bypass
  • Do not disconnect the secondary lead with the circuit energised: an open secondary can still present hazardous voltage and damage the transformer
  • Verify the circuit is de-energised and the output is isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers
  • Work only under the airport's permit-to-work system, using qualified personnel and the manufacturer's instructions
  • Follow the applicable national electrical safety regulations and site procedures

FAQ

AGL Secondary Lead — Frequently Asked Questions

What is an AGL secondary lead?

An AGL secondary lead is the flexible electrical connection between an airfield lighting isolating transformer and the associated light fixture. It carries power from the secondary side of the transformer to the individual fixture, so it must be selected according to the transformer interface, fixture requirements, connector arrangement, cable construction and installation environment. It is used where the transformer and fixture are installed separately, for example within a transformer pit or light base.

Where are secondary leads used?

They are used with runway edge, threshold, end, centreline and touchdown zone lights, taxiway edge and centreline lights, stop bar and runway guard lights, approach lighting fixtures, and inset and elevated fixtures. They are also used for new installations, rehabilitation, fixture and isolating transformer replacement, maintenance and spare-parts requirements.

How do I select the right secondary lead?

Select it as a complete connection system rather than by lead length alone. Identify the isolating transformer (manufacturer, model, rating, secondary interface and existing lead details), the light fixture (manufacturer, model, type, wattage and existing connector arrangement), the cable requirements, the required length, the connector requirements and the installation environment.

What must a secondary lead be compatible with?

One end must match the secondary interface of the selected transformer, and the opposite end must be compatible with the fixture, which can depend on the fixture manufacturer, model, wattage, lead arrangement, connector configuration, termination method and physical connection dimensions. It must also be compatible with the selected connectors, including lead diameter, conductor size, insulation construction, termination and sealing system. For replacement projects, identify the existing fixture and connector before selecting the replacement lead.

How long should a secondary lead be?

The required length can depend on the transformer and fixture locations, light base configuration, transformer pit dimensions, cable routing, connector position and the required maintenance flexibility. It also depends on the added secondary load, because a long lead adds resistance and must stay within the transformer rating. The lead should be long enough to allow proper installation and maintenance without creating excessive loose cable.

What are the main construction requirements?

The lead should provide suitable electrical insulation, an appropriate conductor construction, the required flexibility, mechanical durability, environmental resistance, and compatibility with the transformer, fixture and connectors. Conductor configuration, size, material, insulation, overall diameter and temperature rating should match the electrical requirements of the fixture and transformer, and flexibility should not come at the expense of required insulation or environmental performance.

What are the rated voltage and current of a secondary lead?

They are model-specific, and the lead must suit the transformer secondary output and the fixture lamp current. Confirm the exact conductor, insulation, temperature rating, minimum bending radius and other values against the manufacturer’s documentation for the selected configuration.

How should a secondary lead be routed and installed?

Route it without excessive bending, maintain the specified minimum bending radius, and avoid sharp bends, excessive tension, pinching, abrasion, contact with sharp edges, unnecessary loops and mechanical interference with fixture components. In inset lights, consider limited space, fixture removal and moisture exposure. In a transformer pit, route it to allow transformer access, fixture connection, inspection, maintenance and drainage, without obstructing the pit cover. Allow freedom of movement for maintenance while keeping the lead controlled.

What information is needed when replacing a secondary lead?

Provide the existing transformer and fixture nameplate details, the existing secondary lead, connector photographs, lead dimensions, existing cable markings and installation photographs. If the transformer or fixture has been replaced with a different model, review compatibility before reusing the existing lead.

How is a secondary lead inspected and tested?

Checks may include visual inspection, lead identification, connector inspection, mechanical security, insulation condition, continuity testing where applicable, insulation resistance testing where specified, fixture operation and series-circuit verification, following the approved project procedure. During maintenance, look for damaged insulation, cuts or abrasion, excessive bending, loose terminations, connector damage, moisture ingress, mechanical stress and heat damage.

Why does a fixture not operate, or a lead keep failing?

A fixture that is not operating can come from a damaged secondary lead, an open conductor, a loose termination, a failed secondary connector, a transformer secondary fault, a fixture fault or a primary series-circuit fault. Intermittent operation can come from a loose connection, damaged conductor, cable movement, moisture ingress or mechanical stress. Repeated lead failure can come from an incorrect lead specification, excessive stress, incorrect routing, excessive bending, inadequate environmental protection or incorrect connector installation. Investigate it as a complete transformer-to-fixture connection.

Is it safe to disconnect a secondary lead while the series circuit is energised?

No. An open series circuit driven by a constant-current regulator can produce dangerously high voltage, and an open secondary can still present hazardous voltage and damage the transformer. De-energise the circuit at the regulator, apply the site lock-out / tag-out procedure, verify the circuit is de-energised and the output isolated before touching conductors, connectors, splices or transformers, and work under the airport’s permit-to-work system. See the electrical safety warning on this page.
