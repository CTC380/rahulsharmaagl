AGL Maintenance Handbook

A strategy-wise reference for keeping an installed AGL system serviceable — not how to install it, and not what to verify on it, but what maintenance to actually perform, when, and why. Organized across 25 divisions spanning maintenance strategy, asset type, and the planning and records that hold the programme together.

All 25 divisions (835 topics) are published.

How this differs from the other references: the SOP Library documents procedure — how to install, test, or repair. The Inspection Handbook documents verification — what to check on equipment already in service. This handbook documents the maintenance decision itself — which strategy applies (preventive, corrective, predictive, condition-based, or emergency), and the specific task that strategy calls for on every AGL asset.

Five Maintenance Strategies

Every maintenance topic in the handbook is driven by one of five strategies. The strategy sets the trigger, the timing, and how the work gets authorised and closed.

01

Preventive

Calendar / Interval

Scheduled, routine work — cleaning, tightening, alignment, and functional checks — performed regardless of condition to prevent failure.

MNT-PRV-001Runway Edge Light Preventive Maintenance
02

Corrective

Defect-Triggered

Repair or replacement once an inspection, failure, or abnormal condition has confirmed a specific defect.

MNT-CRR-001Failed Lamp Replacement
03

Predictive

Trend-Based

Condition and performance data tracked over time — insulation resistance, CCR output, LED degradation — to forecast failure before it happens.

MNT-PRD-004CCR Output-Current Trending
04

Condition-Based

Assessment-Driven

Work scoped to the asset's actual measured condition rather than a fixed interval — physical, environmental, and performance state decide what's justified.

MNT-CBM-001Fixture Condition Assessment
05

Emergency

Immediate

Rapid-response work for critical AGL failures affecting runway or approach availability — distinct in urgency, authorisation, and reporting from routine corrective work.

MNT-EMG-001Runway Circuit Failure Response

Runway Light Preventive Maintenance

Preventive work on the main runway lights follows the interval-driven approach described above. These are the three runway circuits most often scheduled first.

Runway Edge Lights Preventive Maintenance

Scheduled cleaning of lenses, checking of fixture mounting and alignment, connector and seal checks and functional checks along the runway edge circuits, before faults develop.

See Division 01: Preventive Maintenance

Runway Centreline Lights Preventive Maintenance

Routine inspection of inset fixtures, lens condition, sealing and fixing, and functional checks of the centreline circuits, including after heavy-wheel traffic and pavement works.

See Division 01: Preventive Maintenance

Threshold Lights Preventive Maintenance

Scheduled checks of threshold and wing bar lights: cleaning, alignment, colour and output checks, connector condition and circuit function.

See Division 01: Preventive Maintenance

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about the AGL Maintenance Handbook and how its 25 divisions fit together.

Q1What is the AGL Maintenance Handbook?

It is a strategy-wise reference for keeping an installed AGL system serviceable, organized across 25 divisions that span maintenance strategy, asset type, and the planning and records that hold the programme together. It covers what maintenance to perform, when, and why, not how to install equipment or what to verify on it.

Q2How many divisions and topics does the handbook contain?

It has 25 divisions with 835 topics, and all 25 divisions are published.

Q3What are the five maintenance strategies in the handbook?

Preventive (calendar or interval based routine work), Corrective (defect-triggered repair or replacement), Predictive (trend-based forecasting of failure), Condition-Based (work scoped to the asset's measured condition rather than a fixed interval) and Emergency (immediate rapid-response work for critical AGL failures). The strategy sets the trigger, the timing, and how the work is authorised and closed.

Q4How is the handbook organized?

Divisions 01 to 05 cover the five maintenance strategies. Divisions 06 to 20 cover equipment-, asset- and interface-specific maintenance, such as fixtures, CCRs, isolation transformers, cables, connectors, PAPI, signs, earthing, ALCMS, approach lighting, stop bars and base cans. Divisions 21 to 25 cover documentation, spares, post-maintenance testing, planning and scheduling, and audit.

Q5How does the Maintenance Handbook differ from the SOP Library and the Inspection Handbook?

The SOP Library documents procedure: how to install, test or repair. The Inspection Handbook documents verification: what to check on equipment already in service. The Maintenance Handbook documents the maintenance decision itself: which strategy applies and the specific task that strategy calls for on every AGL asset.

Q6What record does every maintenance topic follow?

Every topic follows the same seven-step record flow: trigger, strategy, plan, execute, verify, record and close.

Q7Which division covers PAPI, airfield signs or CCRs?

PAPI / APAPI maintenance is Division 13, airfield sign maintenance is Division 14, and CCR maintenance is Division 07. Isolation transformers are Division 08, cables Division 09, connectors Division 10, earthing and bonding Division 16, ALCMS Division 17, approach lighting Division 18, stop bar and runway guard lights Division 19, and base can and civil interface Division 20.
