Why Convert Halogen Airfield Fixtures to LED?

Modernise Suitable Fixtures, Retain Existing Infrastructure

Potential Benefits

  • Lower light-source power consumption, longer LED operating life
  • Reduced lamp replacement and routine maintenance
  • Improved availability of replacement light-source technology
  • Support for phased modernisation
  • Retention of suitable existing fixture infrastructure

Caveat

Halogen fixtures can stay mechanically serviceable while their light-source technology becomes less attractive over the lifecycle. Actual benefit depends on the fixture, LED module, electrical system and operating conditions.

Optical Compatibility

Equivalent Performance Is Not Automatic

Assessment May Consider

  • LED light-emitting area, optical position, beam characteristics
  • Reflector geometry, lens arrangement, filter requirements
  • Light distribution, fixture alignment, required photometric performance

Principle

Simply replacing a halogen lamp with an LED does not guarantee equivalent airfield-light performance. Where the optical system is retained, position and configure the LED source appropriately within the fixture.

Colours must stay inside the chromaticity limits for green, yellow, blue, red or white, so the module colour must match the original fixture colour and be verified.

Electrical Interface

Critical to Any Conversion

Check Against

  • Existing supply arrangement, isolating-transformer output
  • Load characteristics, LED input and driver requirements
  • Current and voltage characteristics
  • Connector arrangement, existing fixture wiring
  • Monitoring or control requirements

Caution

Do not treat a retrofit as a simple lamp substitution where the original fixture was designed around a different electrical load.

The converted fixture must also give the required intensity at every brightness step of the regulator, not only at full output.

Isolating Transformer Load

Series-Circuit Considerations

Relevant Considerations

  • Transformer type and rating, secondary output characteristics
  • LED driver input requirements
  • Minimum and maximum operating conditions
  • Regulator circuit behaviour, circuit monitoring, load compatibility

Principle

Verify the kit and driver arrangement suit the existing transformer and series circuit. Where the LED load differs substantially from the halogen lamp, additional system-level assessment may be required.

The CCR holds the series circuit at constant current, so a lower or different LED load changes the circuit load, power factor and brightness-step behaviour. Re-check the CCR rating and minimum load for the whole circuit, and plan conversion circuit by circuit. Never open an energised series circuit: isolate and lock off the CCR first.

An LED load is usually much lower than the halogen lamp load the transformer was rated for. A lightly loaded transformer can show higher secondary voltage and a distorted current waveform, so confirm the driver is designed for a series-circuit secondary and that the CCR and transformer suppliers accept the combined load.

Driver Arrangement

Integrated or Separate

  • Compatibility with the LED module and the existing series-circuit system

Principle

Select the driver to provide the electrical interface the LED module needs while remaining compatible with the existing fixture and circuit.

FAQ

Halogen-to-LED Conversion Kit — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a halogen-to-LED conversion kit for AGL fixtures?

A conversion kit lets selected runway and taxiway fixtures be modernised from halogen to LED without necessarily replacing the complete fixture assembly. A properly selected kit supports better lifecycle management, reduced maintenance and continued use of suitable existing airfield lighting infrastructure. Because fixtures vary in optical design and electrical configuration, every conversion is assessed against the existing fixture, isolating transformer, driver arrangement and project requirements before installation.

Can every halogen fixture be converted?

No. Not every fixture can use the same kit, and compatibility must be confirmed against the existing fixture model. Useful identification details are the fixture manufacturer, model and type, the installed halogen lamp type and rating, the optical assembly, lens or filter arrangement and reflector configuration, internal dimensions, the existing electrical connection, the isolating-transformer characteristics and any existing driver or ballast arrangement. The fixture model and photographs help determine whether a suitable conversion configuration is available.

Is swapping a halogen lamp for an LED enough?

No. Replacing a halogen lamp with an LED does not guarantee equivalent airfield-light performance. The assessment considers the LED’s light-emitting area and optical position, beam characteristics, reflector geometry, lens arrangement, filter requirements, light distribution, alignment and the required photometric performance. Colours must stay inside the chromaticity limits for green, yellow, blue, red or white, so the module colour must match the original fixture colour and be verified.

What electrical checks apply on a series circuit?

A retrofit should not be treated as simple lamp substitution. The CCR holds the series circuit at constant current, so a lower or different LED load changes the circuit load, power factor and brightness-step behaviour. Re-check the CCR rating and minimum load for the whole circuit, plan conversion circuit by circuit and confirm the converted fixture gives the required intensity at every brightness step. An LED load is usually much lower than the halogen load the transformer was rated for, and a lightly loaded transformer can show higher secondary voltage and a distorted current waveform. Never open an energised series circuit; isolate and lock off the CCR first.

What should be checked on 50 Hz series circuits in India?

Confirm the circuit frequency and the transformer type. 50 Hz circuits use the L-831, and a 60 Hz L-830 is not an equivalent substitute. Work out the new total circuit load, because lower-wattage LED units can fall below the regulator’s minimum load or regulation range. Do not leave a large transformer lightly loaded, check circuit current at every brightness step including the regulator’s current tolerance, and confirm compatibility with existing lamp-out or circuit monitoring. Record before-and-after readings of circuit current, secondary current and insulation resistance at handover.

Does the conversion need a driver?

The driver arrangement may be integrated or separate, according to the configuration. It should provide the electrical interface the LED module needs while remaining compatible with the existing fixture and series circuit, considering input and output characteristics, mounting arrangement, connector configuration, environmental conditions, thermal performance and protection features. Confirm the driver is designed for a series-circuit secondary and that the CCR and transformer suppliers accept the combined load.

What temperature and mechanical checks are needed?

Assess the fixture internal temperature, LED and driver operating temperature, heat dissipation, mounting arrangement and ventilation under continuous operation, without compromising the fixture enclosure or optical assembly. Mechanical checks cover module dimensions, mounting points, fixing method, LED position, connector clearance, driver location, cable routing, heat-management provisions and access for future maintenance. If the fixture needs modification, establish the scope before conversion begins.

What are the benefits of converting halogen fixtures to LED?

Potential benefits are lower light-source power consumption, longer LED operating life, reduced lamp replacement and routine maintenance, better availability of replacement light-source technology, support for phased modernisation and retention of suitable existing fixture infrastructure. The actual benefit depends on the fixture, the LED module, the electrical system and the operating conditions.

How are runway and taxiway retrofits different?

Runway lighting retrofit is safety-critical and should be undertaken only where the resulting configuration meets the applicable project and operational requirements, considering the existing fixture and position, light distribution, required intensity, optical alignment, series-circuit characteristics, transformer compatibility and environmental conditions. A taxiway retrofit should maintain the intended function of the original installation, considering module compatibility, optical distribution, colour requirements, the series-circuit interface, transformer loading, driver arrangement, mechanical fit and environmental performance.

What maintenance and records are needed after conversion?

Monitor LED light output and uniformity, driver operation, connector condition, fixture temperature, moisture ingress, lens and optical condition, mechanical security, series-circuit behaviour and regulator or monitoring alarms. Records should identify the converted fixture and installed LED module so future teams know which configuration is in use, including the fixture manufacturer, model and location, original lamp type and rating, conversion kit identification, LED module rating, driver type, transformer information, installation date and replacement parts required. This also helps airports plan spare LED module and driver inventories.

Which standards and approvals apply to a converted fixture?

A converted fixture must still meet the original photometric requirements, and its certification status should be confirmed. Where a retrofit changes the original fixture configuration, establish whether additional inspection, testing, approval or documentation is needed before return to service. Reference documents include FAA AC 150/5345-46F (light fixtures), AC 150/5345-47C (isolating transformers), AC 150/5345-53D (equipment certification), FAA Engineering Brief 67 (non-incandescent light sources), ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I Appendices 1 and 2, and Doc 9157 Parts 4 and 5. Verify the current edition of each.

50 Hz Series Circuits (India): Check Before You Order

Indian airfield series circuits run from a 50 Hz supply. Replacing lamps or fixtures changes the load on the regulator and transformers, so check the whole circuit, not only the new unit.

  • Confirm the circuit frequency and the transformer type. 50 Hz circuits use the L-831; a 60 Hz L-830 is not an equivalent substitute.
  • Work out the new total circuit load. Lower-wattage LED units can fall below the regulator's minimum load or regulation range; confirm limits with the CCR manufacturer.
  • Do not leave a large transformer lightly loaded. Match the transformer rating to the new fixture load.
  • Check circuit current at every brightness step after the change, including the regulator's current tolerance.
  • Confirm compatibility with existing lamp-out or circuit monitoring.
  • Record before-and-after readings (circuit current, secondary current, insulation resistance) at handover.
