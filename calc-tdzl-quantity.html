Calculator #04

Touchdown Zone Lights

Extent, station/bar/fixture quantity, spacing, and toe-in tool for touchdown zone lighting (TDZL) — FAA AC 150/5340-30J and ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I.

About this calculator

Each longitudinal station is 2 transverse bars × 3 unidirectional lights — 6 physical fixtures per station, not per bar. Stations are generated from a fixed first-bar distance and nominal spacing, not from a blind length ÷ spacing division, so short-runway (half-length) extents are handled correctly. TDZL fixtures are unidirectional and face the landing threshold, so a runway lit for landing in both directions carries one complete set per landing direction; the calculator counts sets from the landing-direction selection. Requirement status is entered by the engineer and cross-checked against the approach category.

Extent & Geometry

Rule note

Engineering basis: FAA imperial criterion — the 6,000 ft / 3,000 ft-extent boundary is always evaluated in feet internally, then converted to metres for display, to avoid rounding a boundary-adjacent length (e.g. 1,850 m) the wrong way.

Rule note

Does not change the quantity. Under the FAA basis it checks that the outermost TDZL lights (46 ft / 14.0 m from centreline) lie within the pavement. Lateral positions are listed in the reference table below.

Rule note

FAA — 3 lights per bar (fixed). ICAO — not fewer than 3, not more than 1.5 m apart.

Methodology

How This Calculator Works

TDZL quantity is generated from a station position sequence, then multiplied by a fixed per-station fixture count — not derived from a single runway-length division, which conflates bars with individual lights and mishandles short runways.

Extent — full or half-runway rule

Evaluated in feet, never from a rounded metric value:

FAA: LTDZ = 3,000 ft if Lrunway ≥ 6,000 ft, else Lrunway / 2  |  ICAO: 900 m if Lrunway ≥ 1,800 m, else Lrunway / 2

The FAA criterion is written as 6,000 ft (1,800 m), but 1,800 m is only 5,906 ft. The FAA basis therefore tests the boundary in feet; the ICAO basis tests it in metres (1,800 m). A runway of 1,800–1,828 m is thus a full-extent runway under ICAO and a half-extent runway under a strict FAA reading — pick the standard your project is governed by.

Station position engine

Stations are generated from the first-bar distance and nominal spacing, then bounded by the extent — never by dividing the extent by the spacing alone:

xi = Dfirst + (i − 1)·S,   i = 1 … N   →   N = ⌊(LTDZ − Dfirst) / S⌋ + 1

For the FAA configuration (Dfirst = S = 100 ft) a full 3,000 ft extent gives 30 stations. For ICAO (first pair 60 m, spacing 30 m, 900 m extent) it gives 29 pairs; at 60 m spacing it gives 15. If your layout starts the first pair at 30 m (30 m spacing), the same extent gives 30 pairs — confirm the first-pair distance against the approved drawing.

Bars vs. individual fixtures

A transverse bar (ICAO: barrette) is not one fixture — it contains 3 unidirectional lights facing the landing threshold in the standard configuration, and each station has 2 bars (symmetrical about the centreline). ICAO permits more than 3 lights per barrette, so the calculator has a lights-per-bar input (fixed at 3 for FAA):

Qbars = 2N   |   Qfixtures = 2N × nbar = 6N (nbar = 3)

Reporting only bars (2N = 60 for the full configuration) understates the true fixture count by a factor of 3 — the individual-fixture figure (6N = 180) is what matters for BOQ, circuit loading, and transformer sizing.

Bidirectional runway = two sets

TDZL fixtures are unidirectional and face the landing threshold. A runway lit for landing in both directions has one complete set in each touchdown zone, so the total is the per-set figure × 2. The two sets never overlap because each stops at or before the runway midpoint. Select a single threshold only where TDZL are provided for one landing direction.

Requirement — entered, not computed

This calculator collects approach category and RVR for documentation and asks the engineer to select the applicable requirement status directly, so the result is never presented as a computed standards citation. It does warn when CAT II/III is selected with TDZL marked "Not Required" or "To Be Determined", because TDZL are normally provided on those runways.

FAQ

Touchdown Zone Lights: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on how the calculator sizes touchdown zone lighting.

What does the Touchdown Zone Lights (TDZL) calculator do?

It works out the TDZL extent, the number of longitudinal stations, transverse bars and individual light fixtures, the spacing tolerance and the optical toe-in, using FAA AC 150/5340-30J or ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I. It also has an optional inspection check for measured station positions.

How many light fixtures are there in one TDZL station?

Each station has 2 transverse bars with 3 unidirectional lights each, so 6 fixtures per station. With N stations, the bar count is 2N and the fixture count is 6N. Stopping at the bar count is a common miscount that understates the fixtures by a factor of 3. ICAO allows more than 3 lights per barrette (not fewer than 3, not more than 1.5 m apart), so the calculator has a lights-per-bar input.

How is the TDZL extent determined?

On the FAA basis the extent is 3,000 ft when the runway is 6,000 ft or longer, otherwise half the runway length. On the ICAO basis it is 900 m when the runway is 1,800 m or longer, otherwise not beyond the midpoint. The FAA boundary is tested in feet, and 1,800 m is only 5,906 ft, so runways of 1,800 to 1,828 m can fall on different sides of the boundary under the two standards.

How does the calculator count stations?

Stations are generated from the first-bar distance and the nominal spacing, then bounded by the extent: N = floor((L_TDZ − D_first) ÷ S) + 1. For the FAA configuration (first bar and spacing both 100 ft) a full 3,000 ft extent gives 30 stations. For ICAO with the first pair at 60 m and a 900 m extent, 30 m spacing gives 29 pairs and 60 m spacing gives 15. Confirm the first-pair distance against the approved drawing.

Do I need two sets of TDZL for a runway used in both directions?

Yes. TDZL fixtures are unidirectional and face the landing threshold, so each lit landing direction has its own complete set. The two sets never overlap because each stops at or before the runway midpoint. Select a single threshold only where TDZL are provided for one landing direction.

What FAA spacing, first-bar and toe-in values does the calculator use?

The first bar is at 100 ft ± 25 ft from the threshold and bars are spaced 100 ft ± 2 ft apart. The lights sit at 36, 41 and 46 ft each side of the centreline, and the optical toe-in is 4° ± 1° toward the centreline. Confirm tolerances against the exact AC or Annex 14 amendment that applies to your project.

Does the calculator decide whether TDZL are required?

No. The requirement status is selected by the engineer from the approved project specification, and the approach category and RVR are kept for documentation. The calculator warns if CAT II or CAT III is selected with TDZL marked Not Required or To Be Determined, because TDZL are normally provided on those runways.

Is the result a final bill of quantities figure?

No. It is a calculated nominal quantity of installed fixtures. Lateral geometry, spares, serviceability allowances and any displaced-threshold layout must be confirmed against the approved lighting layout drawing before issue.
