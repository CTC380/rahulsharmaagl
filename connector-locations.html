The Fact Series circuit

Connections in an AGL layout sit in four places. Primary connectors join the primary cable to each isolating transformer, in and out, so the series circuit passes through it. A secondary connector joins the short secondary cable between the transformer and the fixture. In-line joints connect lengths of primary cable, and the primary cable is terminated at the regulator.

Most of these connectors live in a base can or transformer pit beside the light, protected from water and traffic but still reachable for testing and replacement. Because the lights are in series, one poor primary-side connection can affect the whole circuit, while a poor secondary connection usually affects one light, so their positions are planned for access, and they are checked during inspection and insulation-resistance testing.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What are connector locations in an AGL layout?

The points on a series circuit where cables are joined by connectors or splices: at the isolating transformer, between the transformer and the light, along the primary cable where lengths are joined, and at the regulator end.

Q2Where are the primary connectors?

Normally at the isolating transformer, where the primary cable comes in and goes out, so the circuit runs through the transformer in series. The transformer and these connectors usually sit in a base can or transformer pit beside the light.

Q3Where is the secondary connector?

On the short secondary cable between the isolating transformer and the light fitting. With the circuit isolated and proved dead, it lets the fixture be unplugged and replaced without disturbing the primary circuit.

Q4Why are connectors kept in base cans or pits?

So they are protected from water, traffic and damage, and can still be reached for inspection, testing and replacement. A connector buried with no access is difficult to test or repair.

Q5What goes wrong at connectors?

Moisture, corrosion, poor crimps and damaged seals can raise resistance or reduce insulation resistance. In a series circuit a bad primary-side connection affects the whole loop, while a bad secondary connection usually affects one light, so connectors are checked during inspection and insulation-resistance testing.

Q6Where are the standards for connectors?

FAA AC 150/5345-26 covers plug and receptacle cable connectors (L-823), and ICAO Aerodrome Design Manual Part 5 covers electrical systems. Always confirm against the current edition and the manufacturer's installation data.
