Intermediate Holding Position Fixture

A complete assembly of lighting elements and mounting hardware designed to clearly mark intermediate holding positions on taxiways. This fixture typically includes a row of yellow/amber lights, frangible mounting bases, isolating transformers, and cable connections, providing a ready-to-install solution for defining holding boundaries at non-runway intersections and critical areas.

Overview

What Is It?

An Intermediate Holding Position Fixture is a complete assembly of lighting elements and mounting hardware designed to clearly mark intermediate holding positions on taxiways. This fixture typically includes a row of yellow/amber lights, frangible mounting bases, isolating transformers, and cable connections, providing a ready-to-install solution for defining holding boundaries at non-runway intersections and critical areas. Unlike individual lights, the fixture is a pre-assembled unit that ensures consistent spacing, alignment, and electrical connectivity, reducing installation time and improving reliability. The fixture is designed to withstand the harsh airfield environment while providing clear visual guidance to pilots and ground vehicle drivers.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

This fixture exists to give an intermediate holding point — a taxiway crossing or entry to a critical area away from any runway — a complete, consistent lighting installation rather than a collection of individually sourced and field-assembled components. Because holding position accuracy directly affects how safely ground traffic is sequenced through that intersection, having every light, transformer, and mounting element pre-matched to the same specification removes the variability that could come from mixing components from different sources.

Pre-assembly also directly supports the reliability this holding point depends on: a unit built and tested as a complete assembly is less likely to develop the alignment or connectivity issues that can arise from field-improvised installations, which matters because a pilot relying on this fixture to stop safely before crossing an active taxiway needs the indication to be dependable every time, not just when installed correctly by chance.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Intermediate holding position fixtures are installed at various locations on the airfield where holding is required, particularly where a pre-assembled solution offers installation efficiency. They are used on:

  • Intersecting taxiways where holding is required before crossing
  • Approaches to active runways (intermediate holding positions)
  • ILS critical area boundaries where holding is required
  • Apron entry/exit points where traffic flow is managed
  • Maintenance and construction zones where temporary holding is required
  • Complex taxiway layouts requiring intermediate holding points
Placement

Installation & Location

The pre-assembled unit is installed as a complete row across the taxiway at the intermediate holding position, with the factory-matched spacing and alignment of its individual lights setting the row's geometry rather than requiring that spacing to be re-established in the field. Frangible mounting bases are set into the pavement or shoulder at the positions fixed by the assembly's own frame, with the isolating transformers supplied as part of the unit typically housed in a below-grade pit adjacent to the row rather than integrated into the light housings themselves. Because the fixture is delivered pre-wired between its own lights and transformers, field installation is largely limited to positioning the assembly, connecting it into the site's primary series circuit, and confirming alignment against the taxiway's surveyed holding position, rather than assembling and wiring each light individually. Where the fixture marks a holding point serving an ILS critical area or similar sensitive boundary, its surveyed position is checked against that boundary specifically, since the holding line's accuracy is what the fixture exists to enforce.

Function

How Does It Work?

Intermediate holding position fixtures operate as a pre-engineered, integrated lighting system:

  • Assembly: The fixture consists of three light units mounted on a common base or individually on frangible couplings, with pre-wired connections to isolating transformers and junction boxes.
  • Power: Powered from the constant current series circuit via an isolating transformer (typically 15W – 45W per light) integrated into the fixture assembly.
  • Light Source: Utilizes yellow/amber LED arrays or yellow-filtered halogen lamps to provide a clear visual indication of the holding position.
  • Configuration: The lights are arranged in a row across the taxiway at the holding position, with uniform spacing defined by the fixture design.
  • Guidance: The fixture provides a consistent and reliable visual boundary, helping pilots and drivers to stop at the correct position and maintain safe separation.
Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

An intermediate holding position fixture has no control logic beyond that of the ordinary AGL series circuit it's wired into — what distinguishes it architecturally is that the transformers, wiring, and light spacing arrive pre-assembled from the factory rather than being built up in the field. The same command that switches the adjacent taxiway edge lighting reaches this row through the shared CCR, and the row lights or extinguishes as the single assembly it was delivered as.

Because the internal wiring between lights and transformers is fixed at manufacture, fault isolation within the row itself depends on the assembly's own design — the CCR still only reports a fault at the loop level, so pinpointing which of the three lights in the row has failed relies on the same sectionalised testing used for any other loop-level fault.

Construction

Physical Construction

As a pre-assembled fixture rather than a single moulded part, the unit is built up from a die-cast aluminium housing per light point, yellow/amber lens gasket-sealed against the housing, and a frangible mounting base that lets each unit shear cleanly under aircraft or vehicle impact. The individual light points are factory-spaced and pre-wired into a single harness during assembly, so the installer sets the row into its trench or base cans and makes only the primary and transformer connections rather than positioning and wiring each fixture separately. Each fixture in the row carries its own isolating transformer, housed in a base can beneath or beside it, with the secondary leads terminated to the fixture via L-823-style connectors and the whole assembly rated for outdoor, submersible service (IP66/IP67) against moisture, fuel spillage, and de-icing fluid.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Fixture Alignment and Leveling

Pre-assembled fixtures require precise alignment during installation to ensure the row of lights is straight and level. Misalignment can cause uneven light distribution and reduce the effectiveness of the holding position marking. Regular checks with a digital level are essential.

Yellow Filter/LED Degradation

Yellow light sources and filters are susceptible to UV degradation and thermal stress. Over time, output can decrease or colour can shift. Regular photometric testing and replacement of individual light units or filters is essential.

Connector and Cable Integrity

Fixtures with pre-wired connections can experience corrosion or loosening of connectors over time, leading to intermittent faults or complete failure of one or more lights. Regular inspection of connectors and cable glands is recommended.

Field Notes

Troubleshooting Guide

Uneven Light Distribution Across the Row
Likely Cause
The pre-assembled fixture was not precisely aligned or levelled during installation, or has drifted since.
Diagnostic Steps
Check alignment and level across the full row of lights with a digital level against the design layout.
Corrective Action
Re-align and re-level the fixture, and re-secure mounting hardware to prevent further drift.
Yellow Colour Fade or Reduced Intensity
Likely Cause
UV degradation and thermal stress on the yellow filter or LED source over time.
Diagnostic Steps
Take a photometric colour and intensity reading and compare against the yellow chromaticity and intensity spec.
Corrective Action
Replace the yellow filter or LED module and re-verify against spec.
Intermittent Fault or One or More Lights Out
Likely Cause
Corrosion or loosening of pre-wired connectors within the fixture has degraded over time.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect connectors and cable glands within the fixture for corrosion, looseness, or moisture ingress.
Corrective Action
Clean or replace the affected connector, re-seat and re-seal cable glands, and re-test the full row of lights.

Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Series Circuit Isolation
Electrical Safety

The fixture's light row runs on a constant-current series loop that can carry several kV. De-energise and lock out the CCR, then verify zero energy at the fixture before opening a base can or handling cable connectors.

Frangibility Not to Be Defeated
Mechanical

Never substitute a frangible mounting base with a rigid fitting or over-torque it during installation or repair; each base must still shear cleanly on aircraft or vehicle impact to limit damage under the frangibility requirement.

Taxiway/Vehicle Movement Coordination
Operational

Intermediate holding positions are worked by both aircraft and airside vehicles; coordinate with ATC/airside before taking the fixture out of service, and keep personnel and equipment clear of the taxiway centreline during active movements.

FOD Control at the Assembly
Operational

Keep tools, fasteners, and removed gaskets accounted for at all times when a base can in the assembly is open, and secure or remove any loose material before reinstating the fixture, to prevent foreign object debris on the movement area.

Insulation Test Before Re-Energisation
Electrical Safety

After any cable, connector, or isolating transformer work on the assembly, megger test the affected series loop before restoring power, since a residual fault at re-energisation can trip the CCR or damage healthy fixtures on the same circuit.

PPE & Grounding
Personnel Safety

Use insulated tools and appropriate PPE when working on series-circuit connections, and confirm each fixture and base can are bonded/grounded per the electrical design before reconnecting the loop.

Row Alignment & Spacing
Compliance

Maintain the assembly's designed spacing and alignment when replacing or resetting any individual light, since uneven spacing weakens the visual boundary the row is meant to present to pilots and drivers.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Do not leave a fixture dark, dim, or misaligned after maintenance; verify photometric and positional conformance across the full row before closing out the work.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Row Spacing Tolerance Compounds Across the Full Fixture Row
Design

A small spacing error between adjacent fixtures in an intermediate holding position row can compound across the full row width into a visibly irregular line. Check cumulative spacing across the entire row against the design layout, not just fixture-to-fixture tolerance.

Fixture Status Should Reflect the Holding Position's Actual Interlocked State
Operational

Where the intermediate holding position lighting is tied to a stop bar or other control logic, confirm the fixture row's illuminated state actually reflects the current interlocked holding position status, rather than confirming only that the fixtures themselves are electrically functional.

Wheel-Load Exposure at IHP Fixtures Is Comparable to Centreline, Not Edge Lighting
Mechanical

Intermediate holding position fixtures sit across a taxiway route that vehicles and aircraft regularly cross; prioritise housing and lens wheel-load inspection at the same frequency as centreline fixtures rather than the lighter schedule sometimes applied to less-trafficked edge positions.

Confirm Circuit Segment Boundaries Before Isolating Near Adjacent Taxiway Lighting
Electrical

IHP fixture circuits can share segment boundaries with adjacent taxiway centreline or stop bar circuits; confirm exactly which segment is being isolated before starting work to avoid leaving an adjacent circuit unexpectedly live or dark.

Moisture Ingress at One Fixture in the Row Can Present as a Row-Wide Symptom
Troubleshooting

Where IHP fixtures share a common feed point or short loop segment, a single degraded connector can present as multiple fixtures in the row dimming together, which is easy to misread as several independent fixture faults. Check the shared connection point first when more than one fixture in the row shows the same symptom.

Verify Row Alignment Against the Surveyed Taxiway Centreline, Not Just Adjacent Fixtures
Verification

Individual fixtures in the row can be well-aligned relative to their immediate neighbours while the row as a whole is offset from the taxiway's actual surveyed centreline. Check alignment against the surveyed reference, not only against neighbouring fixtures within the row.
