01 · Low-Visibility AGL

Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) is one part of this wider precision-approach infrastructure — the exact arrangement is airport- and runway-specific and must be established from the applicable aerodrome documentation, approved design and project requirements.

The approach lighting configuration for a CAT II / CAT III runway follows the runway, the approach system and the approved airport design. 08 · Holding Positions

Stop Bar Lighting & Runway Guard Lights

Stop bars are associated with aircraft holding positions and runway-access control. Runway guard lights provide an additional visual indication at runway/taxiway interfaces, and can form part of the low-visibility taxi guidance system alongside stop bars, taxiway centreline lighting and holding-position markings.

A stop bar system can involve inset lighting fixtures, series circuits, isolating transformers, connectors, a CCR, and an associated control and monitoring system. For low-visibility operations, stop-bar serviceability can have direct operational consequences.

Current Kolkata AIP material, for example, specifies stop-bar serviceability conditions for operations at very low RVR values.

11 · Continuity of Supply

UPS Support & Automatic DG Takeover

A UPS can provide continuity of supply to critical AGL loads during transfer from the normal power source to standby generation, while a standby Diesel Generator (DG) may provide the longer-duration backup source after loss of commercial power. The exact design parameters must be verified from the airport's approved electrical system.

13 · Airport Example

Hyderabad Airport (VOHS) Example

Current AAI eAIP information for Rajiv Gandhi International Airport, Hyderabad demonstrates one airport-specific CAT II / CAT III AGL arrangement — not a universal design for every Indian airport.

Precision Approach CAT II/III lighting is installed for RWY 09R, while CAT I precision approach lighting is installed for RWY 27L, 09L and 27R.

Critical runway edge, runway centreline and runway end AGL circuits are supported by UPS with zero switch-over time and 30 minutes of power backup; during low visibility, the UPS takes over without interruption.

If commercial power is not restored after 30 minutes, the DG takes over automatically. Stop bars and taxiway centreline lights are provided on specified taxiways, and the airport's LVP documentation includes AGL serviceability requirements and operational actions when required facilities become unserviceable.

14 · Airport Example

Kolkata Airport (VECC) Example

Current AAI eAIP information for NSCBI Airport, Kolkata shows a different airport-specific arrangement — and should not be copied directly into another airport's design.

Kolkata's primary runway, 01R/19L, has ILS CAT III facilities from both ends as of the northern-end (19L) upgrade commissioned in late 2025; the AGL system is identified as a required serviceable facility for the applicable low-visibility operations.

UPS / standby DG arrangements are provided for the aeronautical ground lighting system, designed around the changeover requirements associated with the applicable DGCA CAR.

The airport's LVP procedures identify specific AGL facilities that must be inspected for serviceability, and specify operational consequences for certain lighting unserviceabilities during low-visibility operations.

Critical lighting systems can use multiple circuits or independent power arrangements to reduce the operational effect of a single circuit failure. Some airport systems use interleaved circuits so that lighting points are distributed between circuits — the operational consequence of losing one circuit depends on the actual configuration.

17 · Series Circuit

CCR, Transformers & Connectors

The CCR remains a core part of the AGL series-circuit system and should be assessed together with the connected series circuit and critical lighting load. A typical series circuit runs CCR → Primary Cable → Primary Connector → Isolating Transformer → Secondary Connector → Light Fixture, and for critical circuits the complete chain should be maintained and tested as an integrated system.

CCR

Current output, intensity steps, protection, monitoring, control, circuit status and fault indication.

Isolating Transformers

Transformer condition, insulation, connections, environmental condition, rating, compatibility and replacement availability.

Connectors

Primary and secondary connectors, L-823 plug/receptacle arrangements, mechanical condition, connection integrity and environmental sealing.

Important engineering principle: lighting availability is not only a fixture issue. It is a complete system issue involving lighting, series circuits, power supply, control, monitoring, inspection, maintenance and operational procedures — a runway light can be perfectly functional while the overall system still has a problem elsewhere in the chain.

The Hyderabad and Kolkata examples on this page demonstrate how airport-specific requirements can differ; an airport-specific AGL configuration should not be copied from one Indian aerodrome to another without checking the applicable design and operational requirements, current AIP/eAIP, DGCA requirements, approved design and project specification.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

What AGL systems support CAT II/III operations in India?
Precision approach lighting, runway edge, centreline and end lighting, threshold and touchdown zone lighting, taxiway centreline lighting, stop bars, runway guard lights and associated control and monitoring.
How is power supply handled for critical AGL circuits?
Indian airports document arrangements such as UPS support and automatic DG takeover for critical AGL circuits, as seen in current AIP information for Hyderabad Airport.
Why does serviceability matter more for CAT II/III airports?
CAT II/III operations place greater importance on AGL serviceability, with AIP procedures for Indian aerodromes specifying inspection and operational consequences for unserviceable critical lighting facilities.
Why do stop bars matter more during low-visibility operations?
Stop bars control runway access at holding positions, and current Kolkata AIP material specifies stop-bar serviceability conditions for operations at very low RVR values, making their availability directly relevant to low-visibility taxi operations.
What happens if a critical runway light becomes unserviceable during CAT III operations?
The consequence depends on the facility, circuit configuration and RVR, and can range from an advisory to aircraft through to suspension of CAT II/III operations, as defined by the airport's own LVP procedures.
How is a CAT II/III AGL system commissioned?
Commissioning typically covers electrical testing (cable, insulation, CCR), functional testing (fixtures, stop bars, control), photometric verification, power testing (UPS/DG changeover) and a final integrated system test before operational handover.
