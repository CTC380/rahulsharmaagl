Product & Component Reliability Failures

The dedicated product-level reliability classification connecting the AGL Failure Database with the AGL Product Encyclopedia. Divisions 01–18 describe what failed in the field, system, equipment, installation, environment, testing or maintenance. Division 19 asks whether that failure indicates a product, component, material, design, manufacturing, supplier or lifecycle reliability problem — surfacing repeat product failures, weak components, supplier-quality trends, batch problems and premature-life failures.

Initial Issue

Core principle — A single component failure does not automatically constitute a product reliability failure. Division 19 classification should normally be strengthened by repeat occurrence, premature-life evidence, common failure mode, batch correlation, supplier evidence, design analysis or other credible reliability evidence — making the Failure Database technically defensible and feeding directly into the Product Encyclopedia, supplier evaluation, product selection, BOQ specifications and warranty analysis.

PCR-01 Component Premature Failure
Failure Definition

A component fails significantly earlier than its expected or specified service life under the applicable operating conditions.

Examples
  • LED module failure after unusually short operation
  • Driver failure soon after installation
  • Connector failure after limited service
  • Transformer failure before expected life
Possible Indicators
  • Repeated early failures
  • Same component type failing across multiple locations
  • Similar failure mode in multiple units
  • Failure concentrated within a particular installation period or batch
Investigation

Compare expected life, actual service duration, operating conditions, failure history, batch/supplier and maintenance history.

Reliability Significance

One of the most important categories for MTBF and product reliability analysis.

PCR-02 LED Module Reliability Issue
Failure Definition

An LED module demonstrates a reliability problem beyond an isolated individual failure.

Possible Indicators
  • Repeated LED failures
  • Accelerated lumen degradation
  • Multiple modules failing in the same product family
  • Similar failures across installations
  • Thermal/optical degradation
Investigation
  • Product model
  • LED type/module
  • Operating hours
  • Temperature exposure
  • Driver compatibility
  • Batch
  • Supplier
  • Failure pattern

PCR-05 Connector Reliability Issue
Failure Definition

A connector type or product family demonstrates repeated or systematic reliability problems.

Possible Indicators
  • Repeated connector failures
  • High contact resistance
  • Seal deterioration
  • Moisture-related failures
  • Repeated contact problems
  • Failure concentrated in a specific product/batch
Cross-Reference

Cross-reference with Division 05 — Cable & Connector Failures.

PCR-06 Cable Reliability Issue
Failure Definition

A cable product/type demonstrates abnormal or repeated failures under its intended operating conditions.

Possible Indicators
  • Repeated insulation breakdown
  • Premature ageing
  • Repeated mechanical failure
  • Water-related deterioration
  • Similar failures across installations
Investigation
  • Cable type
  • Manufacturer
  • Batch
  • Installation date
  • Operating environment
  • Installation method
  • Failure history
Important Distinction

An isolated cable damaged by excavation is normally an installation/mechanical failure, not automatically a product reliability failure. Division 19 should be used when evidence indicates the cable product itself or its material/systematic quality is contributing to repeated failures.

PCR-10 Material Degradation
Failure Definition

Material used in an AGL product deteriorates during service in a manner that affects product performance, durability or reliability.

Possible Examples
  • Housing deterioration
  • Seal degradation
  • Optical material degradation
  • Corrosion of metallic components
  • Potting-material degradation
Possible Causes
  • Environmental exposure
  • UV
  • Temperature
  • Moisture
  • Salt
  • Chemical exposure
  • Ageing
Investigation

Determine whether degradation is environmental, installation-related, maintenance-related, or material/product-related.

PCR-11 Manufacturing Defect
Failure Definition

A defect originating during manufacturing affects the product's performance or reliability.

Examples
  • Defective component
  • Poor material
  • Manufacturing dimensional error
  • Electrical assembly defect
  • Internal connection defect
Indicators
  • Failure immediately after installation
  • Similar defect in multiple units
  • Failure concentrated in a production batch
  • Manufacturer investigation confirms defect
PCR-12 Assembly Defect
Failure Definition

A defect introduced during product assembly causes or contributes to failure.

Examples
  • Incorrect internal connection
  • Poor internal solder/joint
  • Incorrect component installation
  • Incorrect sealing
  • Improper mechanical assembly
Important Distinction

Manufacturing defect is the broader manufacturing-origin category. Assembly defect specifically identifies a defect associated with the assembly process.

PCR-13 Design Weakness
Failure Definition

A product design characteristic contributes systematically to inadequate reliability, maintainability, performance or durability.

Possible Indicators
  • Repeated same-mode failures
  • Failure occurs despite correct installation and maintenance
  • Failure occurs under normal specified conditions
  • Design modification significantly reduces recurrence
Examples
  • Inadequate thermal management
  • Weak mechanical interface
  • Inadequate sealing design
  • Insufficient component rating
  • Poor serviceability
Reliability Significance

This category should generally require stronger evidence than an isolated failure before being assigned.

PCR-14 Component Incompatibility
Failure Definition

Two components or systems are not adequately compatible, resulting in degraded performance or failure.

Examples
  • LED module ↔ driver incompatibility
  • Component ↔ control system incompatibility
  • Connector ↔ cable mismatch
  • Equipment ↔ power-system compatibility problem
Possible Consequences
  • Premature failure
  • Intermittent operation
  • Abnormal electrical performance
  • Thermal stress
  • Reduced component life
Investigation

PCR-15 Supplier-Quality Issue
Failure Definition

Evidence indicates that supplier-related product quality is contributing to repeated or abnormal failures.

Possible Indicators
  • Multiple failures from same supplier
  • Supplier-specific failure pattern
  • Quality-control documentation issue
  • Repeated non-conformance
  • Manufacturer/supplier investigation
Investigation
  • Supplier
  • Manufacturer
  • Product model
  • Inspection records
  • Quality certificates
  • Failure history
  • Corrective-action reports
Important Principle

Do not classify an individual failure as a supplier-quality issue solely because the component came from a particular supplier. There should be evidence of a supplier/product quality relationship.

PCR-17 Product-Life Degradation
Failure Definition

Product performance progressively deteriorates as the product approaches or progresses through its service life.

Examples
  • Progressive LED output degradation
  • Material ageing
  • Seal deterioration
  • Insulation deterioration
  • Optical degradation
  • Mechanical deterioration
Important Distinction

PCR-18 Obsolescence-Related Failure
Failure Definition

Product reliability, maintainability or service continuity is affected because a component/product is obsolete or no longer adequately supported.

Possible Causes
  • Product discontinued
  • Spare parts unavailable
  • Replacement component unavailable
  • Legacy technology
  • Manufacturer support ended
  • Replacement requires system modification
Consequences
  • Extended repair time
  • Increased downtime
  • Forced component substitution
  • Compatibility issues
  • Increased lifecycle cost
Important Point

Obsolescence is not necessarily a physical component failure. It is a product lifecycle reliability/maintainability issue that can eventually create operational failures.

C. Product Reliability vs. Field Failure

This distinction is built into the Failure Database — a field failure is logged against its own division; a reliability finding is logged against Division 19 once a pattern is evident.

Example 1 — LED

Field failure: LED module failed. → Division 01

Product-level finding: Multiple LED modules from the same product family fail prematurely. → Division 19

Example 2 — Connector

Field failure: Connector has high contact resistance. → Division 05

Product-level finding: Same connector model repeatedly develops high contact resistance under normal conditions. → Division 19

Example 3 — Transformer

Field failure: Transformer winding failure. → Division 04

Product-level finding: Multiple transformers from the same batch develop winding failures prematurely. → Division 19

Example 4 — Cable

Field failure: Cable damaged during excavation. → Division 05 / relevant mechanical category

Product-level finding: Cable insulation repeatedly deteriorates under normal specified installation/service conditions. → Division 19

Failure Database vs. Troubleshooting Atlas

The Troubleshooting Atlas answers “I have a fault now — how do I diagnose and fix it?” The Failure Database answers “this failure happened — what happened, why, what evidence proves it, what was done, and how do we stop it recurring?” Division 19 is the layer that turns individual, isolated failures into fleet-wide product intelligence. It connects directly with Division 01 Lighting Fixture Failures, Division 03 CCR Failures, Division 04 Isolation Transformer Failures, Division 05 Cable & Connector Failures, Division 11 PAPI/APAPI Failures, Division 12 Airfield Sign Failures, Division 21 RCA and Division 23 Reliability Trend Analysis — and feeds directly into the AGL Product Encyclopedia.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about product and component reliability failures, as covered in Division 19 of the AGL Failure Database.

Q1What is Division 19 — Product & Component Reliability Failures in the AGL Failure Database?

Division 19 connects the Failure Database with the AGL Product Encyclopedia. It contains 18 published topics (PCR-01 to PCR-18) covering premature component failure, LED module and driver reliability, transformer, connector and cable reliability, material degradation, manufacturing and assembly defects, design weakness, component incompatibility, supplier-quality and batch-related failure, product-life degradation and obsolescence.

Q2How are the 18 product reliability topics grouped?

They fall into six groups: Component Reliability (6 topics), Equipment-Specific Component Reliability (3), Material & Manufacturing (3), Design & Compatibility (2), Supplier & Batch Quality (2) and Product Lifecycle (2).

Q3What is the difference between a field failure and a reliability finding?

A field failure is logged against its own division, while a reliability finding is logged against Division 19 once a pattern is evident. For example, one failed LED module is a Division 01 field failure, but multiple LED modules from the same product family failing prematurely is a Division 19 product-level finding. The same applies to connectors, transformers from one batch and cables.

Q4How is a suspected reliability failure investigated?

The workflow starts by identifying the field failure and asking whether it is isolated or repeated. It then checks operating conditions and product information, compares the failure population, classifies reliability, runs a supplier or manufacturer RCA, applies corrective and preventive action and monitors performance. The population comparison runs from same model to same batch, same airport, same installation period and same failure mode.

Q5What failure chains does Division 19 capture?

Division 19 records relationships between reliability findings. In the premature-component chain, a premature failure leads to repeated same-component failures, an identified reliability trend, a product investigation, a supplier or manufacturer RCA and corrective action. In the LED-driver chain, a driver design or manufacturing issue leads to repeated driver failures and LED fixture outages.

Q6How does Division 19 link to the AGL Product Encyclopedia?

It turns the Product Encyclopedia from a simple product catalogue into a field-reliability knowledge base. Each product entry can eventually hold product identity and technical information alongside reliability information drawn from failure records.

Q7What evidence supports a product reliability record?

Visual evidence includes product, nameplate, batch and serial photographs, photographs of failed components across the population, and degradation, corrosion or defect comparisons. Technical evidence includes failure-rate and repeat-failure data, manufacturer or supplier investigation reports, test reports, operating-hours records and batch traceability.

Q8How are failure severity and operational impact classified?

Every failure receives a severity classification, plus operational-impact tagging. The classes are Critical / Safety-Critical (immediate operational or safety risk), Major / Operationally Critical (significant availability impact), Moderate (reduced performance, not yet critical), Minor / Non-Critical (limited or no operational effect), and Degradation / Intermittent / Latent (a developing or hidden fault).

Q9How does the Failure Database differ from the Troubleshooting Atlas?

The Troubleshooting Atlas answers “I have a fault now — how do I diagnose and fix it?” The Failure Database answers “this failure happened — what happened, why, what evidence proves it, what was done, and how do we stop it recurring?” Kept strictly separate, then linked end to end, the two form an AGL Failure Intelligence Engine rather than a simple fault list.
