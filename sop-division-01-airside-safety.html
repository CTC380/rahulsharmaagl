SOP-01 Airside Work Permit
Purpose

To establish a controlled process for authorizing maintenance, inspection, testing, installation and repair activities within the airport operational area.

Scope

This SOP applies to all AGL-related personnel performing work on runways, taxiways, aprons, shoulders, cable routes, AGL pits, electrical rooms and other restricted airside locations.

Procedure
  1. Identify the proposed work location and activity.
  2. Define the equipment, personnel, vehicles and tools required.
  3. Conduct a job safety assessment/risk assessment.
  4. Obtain the required airside work permit from the authorized airport authority.
  5. Confirm access restrictions, work duration and operating limitations.
  6. Coordinate with Airport Operations and ATC where required.
  7. Brief all personnel before entering the work area.
  8. Display/carry the required permit and identification.
  9. Maintain continuous communication with the responsible airport authority.
  10. Stop work immediately if the permit becomes invalid or conditions change.
  11. On completion, remove personnel, vehicles, tools and temporary equipment.
  12. Obtain work-area clearance and close the permit.
Records
  • Airside Work Permit
  • Risk Assessment/JSA
  • Personnel attendance
  • Equipment/vehicle details
  • Work completion/clearance record
SOP-02 AGL Maintenance Work Authorization
Purpose

To ensure that AGL maintenance is formally authorized before any system, circuit or field equipment is disturbed.

Procedure

Before commencing work:

  • Identify the AGL system/circuit/equipment.
  • Review drawings and circuit identification.
  • Determine whether the circuit is operational.
  • Identify affected runway/taxiway lighting.
  • Obtain maintenance authorization.
  • Coordinate with ALCMS/CCR/ATC/Operations as applicable.
  • Establish isolation requirements.
  • Issue instructions to the maintenance team.
  • Conduct the work only within the authorized scope.

Any work outside the original scope shall require reauthorization.

Safety Requirements

No technician shall assume that an AGL circuit is de-energized merely because the lights are OFF.

SOP-03 Electrical Work Permit

Critical Rule — OFF indication is not proof of electrical isolation.

Purpose

To control electrical work involving CCRs, transformers, distribution panels, AGL circuits, control systems and associated equipment.

Procedure
  1. Identify the electrical source.
  2. Identify all possible sources of energy.
  3. Switch OFF/isolate the appropriate supply.
  4. Apply approved Lockout/Tagout (LOTO).
  5. Verify isolation.
  6. Test for absence of voltage using approved equipment.
  7. Discharge stored energy where applicable.
  8. Establish a safe working condition.
  9. Perform the work.
  10. Inspect the system before restoration.
  11. Remove tools and personnel.
  12. Remove LOTO only by authorized personnel.
  13. Restore power under controlled conditions.
  14. Verify normal operation.
SOP-04 Working Near Active Runway
Purpose

To prevent personnel, vehicles and equipment from creating a hazard during work near an operational runway.

Procedure

Before entering the runway environment:

  • Obtain explicit authorization.
  • Coordinate with ATC and Airport Operations as required.
  • Confirm runway status.
  • Establish approved entry/exit routes.
  • Confirm radio communication.
  • Minimize personnel and equipment in the operational area.
  • Maintain continuous situational awareness.
  • Keep tools and materials controlled.
  • Immediately vacate the runway when instructed.
  • Report completion and runway clearance.
Emergency Requirement

If instructed to vacate, all non-essential work shall stop immediately and the team shall withdraw using the approved route.

SOP-05 Working Near Active Taxiway
Purpose

To control AGL work adjacent to or within the vicinity of an operational taxiway.

Procedure
  1. Obtain authorization.
  2. Coordinate with Airport Operations/ATC.
  3. Establish the work boundary.
  4. Keep personnel and vehicles outside aircraft clearance areas unless specifically authorized.
  5. Maintain radio communication.
  6. Secure tools and materials.
  7. Monitor aircraft and vehicle movements.
  8. Suspend work when required.
  9. Clear the area promptly when instructed.
  10. Confirm the taxiway area is safe before resuming work.
SOP-06 Night-Time AGL Maintenance
Purpose

To control additional risks associated with AGL maintenance performed during hours of darkness.

Requirements

Personnel shall have:

  • Approved PPE
  • High-visibility clothing
  • Suitable communication equipment
  • Adequate task lighting
  • Personal identification
  • Approved airside vehicle lighting
  • Reflective markings
  • Emergency equipment as required
Procedure

Before starting:

  1. Conduct night-work briefing.
  2. Confirm lighting conditions and visibility.
  3. Inspect vehicles and portable lighting.
  4. Confirm communication.
  5. Identify hazards that may not be visible during daylight.
  6. Control cables, tools and components.
  7. Maintain FOD discipline.
  8. Ensure temporary lighting does not create an aviation hazard.
  9. Conduct final inspection before leaving.
SOP-07 Restricted Area Entry
Purpose

To control access to restricted airport areas.

Procedure

Personnel shall:

  1. Possess valid authorization/identification.
  2. Enter only through approved access points.
  3. Follow airport security requirements.
  4. Carry only authorized tools/equipment.
  5. Remain within the approved work area.
  6. Never allow unauthorized persons to enter with them.
  7. Report lost identification immediately.
  8. Exit through the designated route.
  9. Return temporary access permits where required.
SOP-08 Airside Vehicle Movement
Purpose

To ensure safe operation of vehicles within the airside environment.

Requirements

Drivers shall:

  • Hold the required airport authorization.
  • Follow airport speed limits.
  • Use designated vehicle routes.
  • Maintain required radio communication.
  • Use approved vehicle lighting.
  • Give way according to airport rules.
  • Maintain safe separation from aircraft.
  • Never enter a protected area without authorization.
Vehicle Breakdown

If an airside vehicle becomes disabled:

  1. Stop safely.
  2. Notify Airport Operations immediately.
  3. Warn approaching traffic if permitted.
  4. Do not abandon the vehicle without authorization.
  5. Arrange controlled recovery.
SOP-09 AGL Maintenance Vehicle Positioning
Purpose

To ensure maintenance vehicles are positioned without creating an aviation or electrical hazard.

Procedure

Before positioning:

  • Obtain authorization.
  • Confirm aircraft/vehicle movement conditions.
  • Use the approved parking/work location.
  • Activate required vehicle warning lights.
  • Apply parking brake.
  • Prevent vehicle movement.
  • Keep doors, equipment and materials within the approved work boundary.
  • Do not obstruct runway/taxiway lighting or critical markings unless authorized.
  • Maintain an escape route.

Vehicles shall be removed immediately when requested by Airport Operations or ATC.

SOP-10 Work Area Barricading
Purpose

To clearly identify and control an AGL maintenance work area.

Procedure

The supervisor shall:

  1. Establish the work boundary.
  2. Use airport-approved barriers/barricades.
  3. Mark hazards clearly.
  4. Prevent unauthorized access.
  5. Keep cables and tools inside the controlled area.
  6. Ensure barricades cannot become FOD.
  7. Inspect the boundary periodically.
  8. Remove all temporary barriers after completion.

Barricades shall never be positioned where they create an aircraft clearance or navigation hazard.

SOP-11 Temporary Airfield Lighting Protection
Purpose

To prevent maintenance activities from damaging operational airfield lighting or creating misleading visual guidance.

Procedure

During work:

  • Identify nearby operational lights.
  • Protect light fixtures from vehicles and tools.
  • Do not cover operational lights unless specifically authorized.
  • Protect exposed cables and connectors.
  • Prevent accidental movement of fixtures.
  • Ensure temporary lighting cannot be confused with operational AGL.
  • Restore disturbed equipment immediately after work where practicable.
  • Conduct an operational inspection before handover.

Any temporary outage affecting operational lighting shall be reported through the airport's approved process.

SOP-12 FOD Prevention During AGL Work
Purpose

To prevent Foreign Object Debris/Damage (FOD) generated by AGL maintenance.

Procedure

Before work:

  • Count tools and equipment.
  • Inspect the work area.
  • Establish a controlled tool/material area.

During work:

  • Keep components contained.
  • Do not leave screws, wire pieces, cable ties, packaging or waste on the airside.
  • Use suitable containers for small parts.
  • Keep removed components under control.

After work:

  • Perform a systematic FOD inspection.
  • Check around pits, fixtures, cable routes and vehicle areas.
  • Remove all waste and packaging.
  • Account for tools.
SOP-13 FOD Inspection After Maintenance
Purpose

To verify that the work area is free from FOD before being released.

Procedure

The responsible supervisor shall inspect:

  • Work area
  • Runway/taxiway shoulder
  • AGL pits
  • Light fixtures
  • Cable routes
  • Vehicle parking position
  • Access route
  • Adjacent pavement
  • Tool/material storage area

The area shall be released only after satisfactory inspection.

SOP-14 Tool Control During Airside Work
Purpose

To prevent tools from becoming FOD or being left inside operational areas.

Procedure
  1. Prepare a tool inventory before work.
  2. Assign tools to personnel where practical.
  3. Use tool bags/boxes.
  4. Keep small tools contained.
  5. Conduct periodic tool checks.
  6. Count all tools before leaving.
  7. Investigate any missing tool immediately.
  8. Do not leave tools unattended in an operational area.
Missing Tool

A missing tool shall be treated as a potential FOD event until its location is confirmed.

SOP-15 Portable Equipment Control
Purpose

To control portable electrical and maintenance equipment used airside.

Equipment Examples
  • Multimeters
  • Insulation testers
  • Clamp meters
  • Test lamps
  • Portable power supplies
  • Drills
  • Test equipment
  • Portable lighting
  • Extension cables
  • Communication equipment
Procedure

Before use:

  • Inspect equipment condition.
  • Verify suitability for the environment.
  • Check electrical safety.
  • Check battery condition.
  • Confirm calibration where applicable.
  • Secure loose accessories.

After use:

  • Clean equipment.
  • Inspect for damage.
  • Account for accessories.
  • Remove from the work area.
  • Return to designated storage.
SOP-16 Personnel Identification
Purpose

To ensure that all personnel working airside can be readily identified and authorized.

Procedure

Personnel shall:

  • Carry/display valid airport identification.
  • Wear required PPE and high-visibility clothing.
  • Follow access restrictions.
  • Not lend identification to another person.
  • Report lost/stolen identification immediately.
  • Remain within authorized work areas.
  • Follow airport security instructions.

Supervisors shall maintain an accurate list of personnel assigned to the work.

SOP-17 Airside Communication
Purpose

To establish reliable communication between maintenance personnel and airport stakeholders.

Communication Requirements

Communication may include:

  • Radio
  • Telephone
  • Approved digital communication systems
  • Face-to-face communication
  • Emergency communication channels
Procedure

Before work:

  1. Confirm communication equipment.
  2. Verify the correct communication channel.
  3. Conduct a communication check.
  4. Establish the responsible contact person.

During work:

  • Keep communication equipment accessible.
  • Listen continuously where required.
  • Use clear and concise messages.
  • Repeat critical instructions when necessary.
  • Report abnormal conditions immediately.
SOP-18 Coordination With ATC

Critical Rule — ATC clearance/authorization shall be treated as conditional and subject to operational requirements.

Purpose

To ensure that AGL maintenance activities do not interfere with aircraft operations.

Procedure

Where required by airport procedures:

  1. Obtain permission before entering controlled operational areas.
  2. Identify the work location.
  3. Clearly state the intended activity.
  4. Follow ATC instructions.
  5. Maintain required communication.
  6. Monitor operational changes.
  7. Vacate the area immediately when instructed.
  8. Report runway/taxiway clearance.
  9. Do not assume permission continues indefinitely.
SOP-19 Coordination With Airport Operations
Purpose

To establish coordination between AGL maintenance and Airport Operations.

Responsibilities

Airport Operations shall be the appropriate operational coordination point as defined by the airport's procedures.

The AGL team shall communicate:

  • Work location
  • Work scope
  • Expected duration
  • Equipment/vehicles
  • Affected AGL systems
  • Safety restrictions
  • Completion status
  • Any abnormal condition

Any change to the planned work shall be communicated before proceeding.

SOP-20 Coordination With CNS/ATM Teams
Purpose

To control AGL work that may interface with CNS/ATM infrastructure or airport operational systems.

Examples

Coordination may be required where work affects:

  • ALCMS interfaces
  • Control/monitoring systems
  • Communications infrastructure
  • Navigation-related infrastructure
  • Data/network connections
  • Airport operational systems
  • Shared electrical/control infrastructure
Procedure
  1. Identify potential interface.
  2. Notify the responsible CNS/ATM team.
  3. Establish isolation/control requirements.
  4. Obtain authorization.
  5. Perform work within approved limits.
  6. Test affected interfaces.
  7. Obtain confirmation before returning systems to service.
SOP-21 Maintenance During Low Visibility
Purpose

To control AGL maintenance during reduced visibility conditions such as fog, heavy rain, dust or other conditions affecting visual awareness.

Procedure

Before work:

  • Assess visibility.
  • Confirm whether maintenance is permitted.
  • Coordinate with Airport Operations/ATC as applicable.
  • Confirm communication.
  • Minimize personnel and vehicle exposure.
  • Review escape routes.

During work:

  • Maintain heightened situational awareness.
  • Keep personnel together where practical.
  • Avoid unnecessary movement.
  • Stop work if safe navigation or communication cannot be maintained.
Stop-Work Condition

Work shall be suspended when environmental conditions prevent the team from maintaining safe control of personnel, vehicles, tools or equipment.

SOP-22 Maintenance During Adverse Weather
Purpose

To protect personnel and AGL equipment during adverse weather.

Examples
  • Heavy rain
  • Lightning
  • Thunderstorms
  • Strong winds
  • Flooding
  • Extreme heat
  • Dust storms
  • Poor visibility
Procedure
  1. Monitor weather conditions.
  2. Assess the changing risk.
  3. Secure tools and equipment.
  4. Protect open electrical equipment.
  5. Avoid exposed electrical work during unsafe conditions.
  6. Stop work when required by airport procedures.
  7. Withdraw personnel to a safe location.
  8. Resume only after authorization and reassessment.
SOP-23 Airside Emergency Withdrawal
Purpose

To establish a rapid withdrawal procedure when continued work becomes unsafe.

Withdrawal Triggers

Withdrawal may be required because of:

  • Aircraft movement
  • ATC instruction
  • Emergency vehicle movement
  • Fire
  • Electrical incident
  • Fuel spill
  • Security incident
  • Severe weather
  • Loss of communication
  • Loss of situational awareness
  • Airport emergency declaration
  • Unsafe work-area conditions
Procedure
  1. Stop work immediately.
  2. Make equipment safe if this can be done without delaying withdrawal.
  3. Collect only essential equipment if safe.
  4. Follow the designated escape route.
  5. Move to the approved safe location.
  6. Account for all personnel.
  7. Notify Airport Operations/supervisor.
  8. Report missing personnel immediately.
  9. Do not re-enter until formally authorized.
SOP-24 Work Completion Clearance
Purpose

To ensure that an AGL work area is safe before being returned to Airport Operations.

Completion Checklist
  • Work completed according to scope.
  • Electrical connections checked.
  • Covers and fasteners secured.
  • AGL fixtures correctly installed.
  • Cable/connector condition verified.
  • Tools accounted for.
  • Materials removed.
  • Waste removed.
  • FOD inspection completed.
  • Vehicles removed or positioned as authorized.
  • Temporary barriers removed where appropriate.
  • Temporary protections removed where appropriate.
  • Operational tests completed.
  • Defects recorded.
  • Work area inspected.
  • Airport Operations/ATC notified as required.

The area shall not be declared clear until the responsible supervisor is satisfied that the work area is safe.

SOP-25 Returning Equipment to Service
Purpose

To ensure that AGL equipment is safely and correctly returned to operational service after maintenance.

Procedure

Step 1 — Physical Inspection

Inspect:

  • Fixture
  • Housing
  • Lenses
  • Connectors
  • Cables
  • Isolating transformer
  • Junctions
  • Earthing/bonding where applicable
  • Fasteners
  • Covers
  • Mounting arrangements

Step 2 — Electrical Verification

Perform applicable tests such as:

  • Continuity
  • Insulation resistance
  • Circuit verification
  • Transformer checks
  • Control circuit verification
  • Protective device verification

Testing shall follow the applicable equipment manufacturer's requirements and approved airport procedures.

Step 3 — Functional Test

Verify:

  • Correct ON/OFF operation
  • Correct intensity/step operation where applicable
  • Correct control response
  • Correct monitoring indication
  • Correct ALCMS indication where applicable
  • No abnormal alarm condition

Step 4 — Operational Coordination

Notify the responsible Airport Operations/ATC/technical authority as applicable before returning affected equipment or circuits to service.

Step 5 — Restoration

Remove authorized isolation/LOTO only according to the approved procedure.

Restore the equipment in a controlled sequence.

Step 6 — Final Inspection

Confirm:

  • No personnel remain in the hazard area.
  • No tools remain.
  • No FOD remains.
  • All covers are secured.
  • All temporary arrangements are removed or formally documented.
  • Equipment is correctly positioned.
  • The system operates as intended.

Step 7 — Documentation

Record:

  • Date/time
  • Equipment identification
  • Circuit identification
  • Work performed
  • Tests performed
  • Test results
  • Defects identified
  • Corrective actions
  • Personnel responsible
  • Final operational status
  • Work permit/authorization reference

Division-Wide Requirements

Common rules, briefing minimums, safety sequence and records that apply across all 25 Division 01 procedures.

A. Stop-Work Authority

Every person involved in AGL maintenance has a responsibility to stop work when an immediate or uncontrolled safety risk is identified.

Examples include

  • Unexpected aircraft movement
  • Loss of communication
  • Electrical hazard
  • Severe weather
  • Uncontrolled FOD
  • Unauthorized personnel entering the work area
  • Vehicle conflict
  • Unexpected AGL energization
  • Security/emergency event

Work shall resume only after the hazard has been controlled and the responsible authority has authorized continuation.

B. Minimum Pre-Work Briefing

Before commencing airside AGL work, the supervisor should brief the team on:

  • Work scope
  • Work location
  • Permit requirements
  • AGL circuit/equipment identification
  • Electrical isolation requirements
  • LOTO requirements
  • ATC/Operations coordination
  • Vehicle movement
  • Communication method
  • Emergency withdrawal route
  • FOD controls
  • PPE
  • Weather/visibility
  • Work completion requirements

C. Minimum AGL Safety Principle

The following sequence should be applied whenever applicable:

IDENTIFY AUTHORIZE ISOLATE VERIFY WORK INSPECT TEST RESTORE CLEAR DOCUMENT

D. Mandatory Safety Philosophy

Never assume

  • A light being OFF means the circuit is de-energized.
  • A runway/taxiway is inactive without confirmation.
  • A permit remains valid after conditions change.
  • A missing tool is harmless.
  • A vehicle is visible to aircraft.
  • A temporary light is acceptable merely because it provides illumination.
  • A work area is safe because the physical work is complete.

Always

  • Obtain authorization.
  • Maintain communication.
  • Control personnel and vehicles.
  • Control tools and materials.
  • Protect operational AGL.
  • Maintain FOD discipline.
  • Follow LOTO requirements.
  • Coordinate with airport operational authorities.
  • Conduct a final inspection.
  • Document the work.

Division 01 — SOP Approval Principle

Each airport should adapt these SOPs to its own Aerodrome Manual, Safety Management System (SMS), Airside Operations Manual, Permit-to-Work system, Electrical Safety Rules, LOTO procedure, ATC procedures, airport security procedures, emergency response procedures, FOD Management Programme, Airside Driving Rules, AGL maintenance procedures, and applicable national and international requirements.

Local airport procedures and authorized operational instructions shall take precedence wherever they impose more restrictive requirements.

Airside Work Hold Points

  • HP-01 — Permit — Valid airside work permit issued and displayed before entry.
  • HP-02 — Briefing — Job safety assessment and toolbox briefing completed.
  • HP-03 — ATC/Operations — Clearance from ATC and airport operations confirmed for the work location.
  • HP-04 — Access Control — Personnel and vehicle identification, escort and routes verified.
  • HP-05 — NOTAM/Status — Lighting status changes notified for NOTAM action where operations are affected.
  • HP-06 — FOD and Tool Control — Tools counted in; FOD inspection completed before leaving.
  • HP-07 — Withdrawal — Work stops and area is vacated when conditions or permit validity change.
  • HP-08 — Clearance — Work-area clearance obtained and permit closed before equipment returns to service.

Reference Basis

Technical references behind the procedures in this division:

  • ICAO Annex 14, Vol. I — Aerodrome Design and Operations (aerodrome operational services and maintenance chapters) — aerodrome works, vehicle operations and safe working in the movement area.
  • ICAO Doc 9981, PANS-Aerodromes — procedures for aerodrome works safety, vehicle control and aerodrome safety management.
  • ICAO Doc 9870, Manual on the Prevention of Runway Incursions — personnel and vehicle discipline near active runways and taxiways.
  • ICAO Doc 9774 (Certification of Aerodromes), Doc 9859 (Safety Management Manual) and Annex 19 — Aerodrome Manual, SMS and the permit-to-work system as an SMS control.
  • FAA AC 150/5370-2, Operational Safety on Airports During Construction — work-area marking, barricades, lighting, haul routes and vehicle control.
  • FAA AC 150/5210-24 (Airport FOD Management) and AC 150/5210-5 (Painting, Marking and Lighting of Vehicles) — FOD control and airside vehicle conspicuity.
  • DGCA India CAR Section 4 (Aerodrome Standards and Licensing) — the regulatory framework, together with the airport's Aerodrome Manual and Airside Driving Rules.
  • ISO 45001, Occupational health and safety management systems — risk assessment, hierarchy of controls and worker participation.

References are given at document or chapter level for technical orientation. Confirm the current edition, amendments and national adoption (for example DGCA CAR Section 4, FAA Advisory Circulars or EASA material) for each project. Approved project specifications, manufacturer instructions and the airport's own procedures take precedence, as stated in the precedence note above.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about airside safety and work permit procedures in the AGL SOP Library.

Q1What is Division 01 — Airside Safety & Work Permit SOPs?

Division 01 is a set of 25 Standard Operating Procedures (SOP-01 to SOP-25, Rev 0) for authorizing, executing and closing out AGL work inside the airport operational area. It covers work permits, runway and taxiway proximity, night, low-visibility and adverse-weather maintenance, FOD and tool control, coordination with ATC, Airport Operations and CNS/ATM teams, emergency withdrawal, and return to service.

Q2Do these SOPs override the airport's own rules?

No. These SOPs are a technical framework. The airport's approved Airside Driving Rules, Permit-to-Work system, SMS procedures, ATC instructions, NOTAM procedures, emergency procedures and local aerodrome regulations always take precedence, and local procedures apply wherever they are more restrictive.

Q3What is required before starting AGL work airside?

The work must be authorized through an airside work permit issued by the responsible airport authority. Before entering, the team identifies the work location and activity, defines equipment, personnel, vehicles and tools, completes a job safety or risk assessment, confirms access restrictions and duration, coordinates with Airport Operations and ATC where required, and briefs all personnel (SOP-01).

Q4Is a light being OFF proof that a circuit is electrically isolated?

No. The Electrical Work Permit (SOP-03) states that an OFF indication is not proof of isolation. The circuit must be isolated, locked and tagged out under an approved LOTO procedure, isolation verified, absence of voltage tested with approved equipment, and stored energy discharged where applicable before work begins.

Q5What must be done before working near an active runway or taxiway?

Obtain explicit authorization, coordinate with ATC and Airport Operations as required, confirm runway status, establish approved entry and exit routes, and confirm radio communication. Keep personnel and equipment to a minimum, maintain situational awareness, control tools and materials, and vacate immediately when instructed (SOP-04, SOP-05).

Q6Does ATC clearance stay valid for the whole job?

No. ATC clearance or authorization is conditional and subject to operational requirements (SOP-18). Teams must follow ATC instructions, monitor operational changes, vacate the area immediately when instructed, report runway or taxiway clearance, and never assume permission continues indefinitely.

Q7How is FOD controlled during and after AGL maintenance?

Tools and equipment are counted before work, a controlled tool and material area is set up, and small parts are kept in containers. Screws, wire pieces, cable ties, packaging and waste must not be left airside. After work the supervisor inspects the work area, pits, fixtures, cable routes, vehicle position, access route and adjacent pavement, and the area is released only after a satisfactory inspection (SOP-12, SOP-13).

Q8What extra controls apply to night, low-visibility and adverse-weather work?

Night work requires approved PPE, high-visibility clothing, task lighting, communication equipment, approved vehicle lighting and a night-work briefing (SOP-06). In low visibility, work is suspended if the team cannot maintain safe control of personnel, vehicles, tools or equipment (SOP-21). In adverse weather such as lightning, heavy rain or strong winds, teams protect open electrical equipment, avoid exposed electrical work, withdraw to a safe location, and resume only after authorization and reassessment (SOP-22).

Q9When must work stop, and who can stop it?

Every person involved in AGL maintenance has stop-work authority when an immediate or uncontrolled safety risk is identified. Examples include unexpected aircraft movement, loss of communication, an electrical hazard, severe weather, uncontrolled FOD, unauthorized personnel in the work area, a vehicle conflict or unexpected AGL energization. Work resumes only after the hazard is controlled and the responsible authority authorizes continuation.

Q10What is the airside emergency withdrawal procedure?

Stop work immediately, make equipment safe only if this does not delay withdrawal, and collect only essential equipment if safe. Follow the designated escape route to the approved safe location, account for all personnel, notify Airport Operations or the supervisor, and report missing personnel immediately. Do not re-enter until formally authorized (SOP-23).

Q11How is an AGL work area cleared and equipment returned to service?

Work completion clearance (SOP-24) requires work completed to scope, connections and fasteners secured, tools and materials accounted for and removed, FOD inspection completed, operational tests done, defects recorded, and Airport Operations or ATC notified as required. The area is not declared clear until the responsible supervisor is satisfied it is safe. Return to service (SOP-25) follows physical inspection, electrical verification and functional testing per manufacturer requirements and approved airport procedures.

Q12What records should an AGL maintenance organization keep?

As applicable: Airside Work Permit, AGL Maintenance Authorization, Electrical Work Permit, LOTO Record, Risk Assessment/JSA, Personnel Attendance Record, Vehicle Entry/Movement Record, Tool Control Record, FOD Inspection Record, Equipment Inspection Record, Test/Measurement Record, Work Completion Certificate, Return-to-Service Record, Defect/Failure Report, Emergency/Incident Report and Communication/Coordination Record.
