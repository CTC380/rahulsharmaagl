The strategic maintenance-management layer of the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide. MSO-01 Maintenance Strategy Selection
Purpose

Every AGL asset should have an appropriate maintenance strategy, selected deliberately rather than applied uniformly across all assets.

Strategy Should Be Based On
  • Criticality
  • Failure consequence
  • Reliability
  • Condition
  • Utilization
  • Operating environment
  • Failure history
  • Maintainability
  • Manufacturer recommendations
  • Regulatory requirements
  • Availability requirements
  • Lifecycle cost
Strategy-Selection Logic
ASSET FUNCTIONFAILURE CONSEQUENCEFAILURE MODEDETECTABILITYCONDITION/RELIABILITY DATAMAINTENANCE STRATEGY
Core Principle

Do not automatically apply the same maintenance strategy to every asset — the strategy should follow evidence, not habit.

MSO-02 Preventive-Maintenance Strategy
Purpose

Preventive maintenance is planned maintenance intended to reduce the probability of failure or maintain the asset in an acceptable condition.

Strategic Considerations
  • Maintenance interval
  • Maintenance task
  • Asset criticality
  • Manufacturer recommendations
  • Historical failures
  • Operating exposure
  • Regulatory requirements
  • Environmental conditions
Typical AGL Examples

Fixture inspection, CCR inspection, transformer inspection, cable-system testing, PAPI inspection, sign inspection, earthing inspection, control-system checks.

Boundary

The specific inspection/test procedure for each of these should remain in the AGL Maintenance Handbook.

MSO-03 Predictive-Maintenance Strategy
Purpose

Predictive maintenance uses measured condition/performance information to anticipate deterioration or failure before it happens.

Possible Inputs
  • Electrical test trends
  • Photometric trends
  • CCR performance
  • Temperature
  • Energy consumption
  • Operating hours
  • Failure history
  • Condition-monitoring information
  • Alarm history
Lifecycle Logic
BASELINEPERIODIC MEASUREMENTTRENDDETERIORATION DETECTEDPREDICTED INTERVENTIONPLANNED MAINTENANCE
Where This Is Most Valuable

Particularly valuable where deterioration can be detected before functional failure occurs.

MSO-04 Condition-Based Maintenance
Purpose

Condition-based maintenance bases intervention decisions on the actual condition of the asset, rather than relying solely on a fixed calendar interval.

Inputs May Include
  • Inspection findings
  • Test results
  • Photometric results
  • Electrical measurements
  • Physical condition
  • Failure history
  • Performance deviation
  • Environmental exposure
Example Instead of a Fixed-Age Replacement
INSPECTMEASUREASSESS CONDITIONDETERMINE DEGRADATIONDECIDE INTERVENTION
Register Link

This should be linked directly with Division 07 baseline data and Division 08 operational trends.

MSO-05 Corrective-Maintenance Strategy
Purpose

Corrective maintenance addresses a detected defect, deterioration or failure.

Strategic Categories
  • Minor corrective: small defects that do not immediately affect critical operation
  • Major corrective: defects requiring significant resources or a planned outage
  • Emergency corrective: failure requiring immediate intervention due to safety or operational consequences
Strategy Should Define

Priority, response requirement, escalation, resource requirement, spare requirement, restoration objective, root-cause review requirement.

Boundary

The detailed repair procedure remains in the AGL Maintenance Handbook.

MSO-06 Risk-Based Maintenance
Purpose

Risk-based maintenance prioritizes maintenance according to the consequence and likelihood of failure. It connects directly to Division 03 — Asset Criticality & Risk Assessment.

Basic Model
RISK = PROBABILITY OF FAILURE × CONSEQUENCE OF FAILURE
Consider
  • Safety consequence
  • Operational consequence
  • Regulatory consequence
  • Financial consequence
  • Environmental consequence
  • Failure probability
  • Detectability
  • Redundancy
Example

A highly critical runway lighting asset may receive greater maintenance attention than a lower-consequence asset, even if both have similar physical condition.

MSO-07 Maintenance Optimization
Purpose

Maintenance optimization seeks the best balance between reliability, safety, availability, resources and cost.

The Objective

Not "do maximum maintenance." Not "do minimum maintenance." The objective is: do the right maintenance at the right time on the right asset.

Optimize
  • Task frequency
  • Task type
  • Inspection depth
  • Testing frequency
  • Resources
  • Spare inventory
  • Contractor support
  • Planned outages
MSO-08 Maintenance Frequency Optimization
Purpose

Maintenance frequency should be based on evidence, rather than automatically repeating historical schedules forever.

Consider
  • Asset criticality
  • Failure history
  • Condition trends
  • Operating hours
  • Environmental exposure
  • Manufacturer guidance
  • Regulatory requirements
  • Maintenance effectiveness
  • Cost
Review Question

Is the current maintenance interval preventing failures effectively without creating unnecessary maintenance effort?

Possible Outcomes

Increase frequency if deterioration is occurring earlier than expected. Reduce frequency if condition remains stable and risk remains acceptable. Change strategy from preventive to condition-based/predictive where sufficient data exists.

MSO-09 Maintenance Task Optimization
Purpose

Individual maintenance tasks should also be reviewed on their own merits, not just the overall strategy.

For Every Task, Ask
  • What failure does this task prevent/detect?
  • Is the task effective?
  • What evidence supports the task?
  • How often should it be performed?
  • Can another test replace it?
  • Can condition monitoring reduce the need?
  • Does the task create unnecessary disruption?
  • What is the cost?
  • What is the consequence of not performing it?
Strategic Principle

Every recurring maintenance task should have a defined lifecycle purpose.

MSO-10 Maintenance-Resource Planning
Purpose

Maintenance strategy must consider the resources required to execute it — a strategy that cannot be resourced is not a real strategy.

Resources
  • People: engineers, technicians, specialists, contractors
  • Equipment: test instruments, photometric equipment, electrical test equipment, access equipment
  • Materials: spare parts, consumables, connectors, transformers, lamps/LED modules
  • Time: planned maintenance windows, airside access, runway closures, operational restrictions
Strategic Objective

Ensure that the selected maintenance strategy is actually executable, not just theoretically correct.

MSO-12 Maintenance Backlog Management
Purpose

A maintenance backlog represents work that has been identified but has not yet been completed.

Backlog Should Be Classified By
  • Criticality
  • Risk
  • Age
  • Failure consequence
  • Regulatory requirement
  • Operational impact
  • Required resources
  • Spare availability
Priority Principle

A backlog should not simply be managed by age of work order. A newer critical safety-related task may require higher priority than an older low-risk task.

Backlog Categories

Critical, High, Medium, Low, Planned, Deferred with justification.

MSO-13 Maintenance Effectiveness
Purpose

Maintenance effectiveness measures whether the maintenance strategy is actually achieving its intended purpose.

Indicators
  • Failure frequency
  • Repeat failures
  • Availability
  • MTBF
  • MTTR
  • Emergency maintenance
  • Preventive-maintenance compliance
  • Condition deterioration
  • Maintenance backlog
  • Maintenance cost
  • Downtime
  • Number of corrective interventions
Key Question

Did the maintenance activity improve or preserve asset performance? A maintenance activity should not automatically be considered effective merely because the work order was closed.

MSO-14 Maintenance Cost Analysis
Purpose

Maintenance cost should be analyzed as part of lifecycle decision-making, not tracked in isolation from asset performance.

Consider
  • Preventive-maintenance cost
  • Predictive-maintenance cost
  • Corrective-maintenance cost
  • Emergency-maintenance cost
  • Labour
  • Spares
  • Contractor cost
  • Testing
  • Equipment
  • Access/closure cost
  • Downtime
Important Analysis

Compare preventive cost vs. failure cost vs. downtime consequence — this helps determine whether maintenance strategy is economically justified.

MSO-15 Maintenance Strategy Review
Purpose

Maintenance strategy should not remain unchanged for the entire asset life — it should be periodically reviewed using actual lifecycle evidence.

Review Inputs
  • Failure history
  • Condition history
  • Performance trends
  • Availability
  • Reliability
  • Maintenance cost
  • Operating hours
  • Utilization
  • Criticality changes
  • Technology changes
  • Manufacturer recommendations
  • Obsolescence
  • Repeated failures
Review Outcomes

Continue current strategy, modify maintenance frequency, change maintenance type, increase monitoring, reduce unnecessary maintenance, refurbish, or replace.

Division 09 — Core Principle

Maintenance strategy should be determined by asset risk, failure behaviour, condition, utilization, operational requirements and lifecycle cost — not simply by a fixed calendar schedule.

Division 09 operates as a continuous optimization loop, not a one-time decision:

Asset Criticality
Maintenance Strategy
Maintenance Plan → Work Orders → Execution
Inspection & Testing → Performance Measurement
Failure / Condition Data
Effectiveness Analysis + Cost Analysis
Maintenance Strategy Review → Optimized Strategy → Repeat

This is the mechanism by which the handbook evolves from a static maintenance schedule into a data-driven AGL asset-management system — the strategic bridge between asset performance data and the detailed AGL Maintenance Handbook, while keeping the two documents clearly separated: the Lifecycle Guide governs the maintenance strategy; the Maintenance Handbook governs the maintenance procedure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Maintenance Strategy & Optimization in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide.

Q1What is Division 09 — Maintenance Strategy & Optimization in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

Division 09 is the strategic bridge between asset performance data and the AGL Maintenance Handbook. It covers 15 topics on maintenance strategy selection, preventive, predictive, condition-based and corrective maintenance, risk-based maintenance, optimization, resource planning, work orders, backlog, effectiveness, cost and strategy review.

Q2How should a maintenance strategy be selected for an AGL asset?

Every AGL asset should have an appropriate strategy, selected deliberately rather than applied uniformly across all assets. Selection considers criticality, failure consequence, reliability, condition, utilization and operating environment.

Q3What is the difference between preventive, predictive, condition-based and corrective maintenance?

Preventive maintenance is planned work intended to reduce the probability of failure. Predictive maintenance uses measured condition or performance information to anticipate deterioration before it happens. Condition-based maintenance bases intervention on actual condition rather than only a fixed calendar interval. Corrective maintenance addresses a detected defect, deterioration or failure.

Q4What is risk-based maintenance?

Risk-based maintenance prioritizes maintenance according to the consequence and likelihood of failure. It connects directly to Division 03 and considers safety, operational, regulatory and financial consequence.

Q5How are maintenance frequency and tasks optimized?

Maintenance optimization seeks the best balance between reliability, safety, availability, resources and cost. Frequency should be based on evidence rather than repeating historical schedules forever, and each task should be reviewed on its own merits, asking what failure it prevents or detects and whether it is effective.

Q6Why does maintenance strategy need resource planning?

A strategy that cannot be resourced is not a real strategy. Resource planning considers people, such as engineers, technicians, specialists and contractors, as well as equipment such as test instruments and photometric equipment.

Q7How are work orders and backlog managed?

Work orders convert maintenance strategy into controlled maintenance activities. A backlog is work identified but not yet completed, and it should be prioritized using criticality, risk, age, failure consequence, regulatory requirement and operational impact.

Q8How is maintenance effectiveness and strategy reviewed?

Effectiveness measures whether the strategy achieves its purpose using failure frequency, repeat failures, availability, MTBF, MTTR and emergency maintenance. Strategy should not stay unchanged for the whole asset life and is reviewed periodically using actual failure, condition, performance and cost evidence.
