Visual Aid vs Instrument Guidance Comparison

PAPI VS Precision Approach System

PAPI is a visual lighting aid — a precision approach system is a full instrument approach guidance system. The word "Precision" in PAPI's name describes the approach-path indicator itself, not a navigation category. This page separates the two so the terminology is never confused.

02 · Definitions

What Is PAPI & What Is a Precision Approach System?

PAPI

PAPI consists of light units beside the runway that produce different red/white combinations depending on the aircraft's vertical position. A four-unit PAPI provides five principal visual sectors, from too high to too low. The FAA describes PAPI as the current standard Visual Glide Slope Indicator (VGSI).

What it provides

Visual vertical approach-path information — it helps the pilot establish and maintain the desired descent path toward the runway once it is in sight.

Precision Approach System

A precision approach system provides instrument guidance allowing the aircraft to follow a defined approach path using onboard navigation equipment. A classic example is the ILS, which combines a localizer and glide slope.

What it provides

Lateral guidance and vertical guidance together — a full 3D approach path that the pilot can fly on instruments, without necessarily having visual contact with the runway.

03 · Guidance Axes

One Axis vs Two Axes

This is one of the most important differences between the two. PAPI tells the pilot high, low, or on the approach path — it says nothing about left or right of the instrument course.

PAPI

Vertical only
LEFT / RIGHT UP / DOWN

Precision Approach System

Lateral + vertical, e.g. ILS localizer + glide slope
LEFT / RIGHT UP / DOWN

On a runway with an ILS, lateral course guidance associated with the visual approach comes from the runway and runway lighting itself, not from the PAPI.

04 · ILS as a Precision Approach System

Localizer + Glide Slope = 3D Guidance

Localizer

Provides lateral guidance — keeps the aircraft aligned left/right with the extended runway centreline.

Glide Slope

Provides vertical guidance — keeps the aircraft on the correct descent angle toward the runway.

Together, localizer plus glide slope give full 3D approach guidance — a capability PAPI, as a purely visual vertical indicator, does not provide on its own.

05 · Visibility & Aircraft Equipment

What Each System Depends On

PAPI

  • The pilot must be able to see the PAPI lights
  • Normal use depends on visual conditions
  • No special aircraft navigation receiver required
  • Pilot simply observes the white/red combination directly

Precision Approach System

  • Aircraft can receive and display guidance without visual contact with the runway
  • Important for low-visibility instrument operations
  • Aircraft normally needs compatible navigation equipment
  • For ILS: ground equipment → radio signal → aircraft antenna → receiver → flight display
06 · Used Together

PAPI + Precision Approach System

These are not mutually exclusive — they are commonly installed and used together on the same runway. The precision approach system provides instrument guidance throughout the approach, while PAPI provides a visual approach-slope reference and cross-check once the pilot has the runway environment in sight.

PAPI descent should not be initiated until the aircraft is visually aligned with the runway. Its role is a visual cross-check that complements instrument guidance, not a replacement for it.

07 · Important Terminology

Don't Confuse the Two "Precision" Words

PAPI

Precision Approach Path Indicator. A PAPI's name contains the word "Precision", but that does not make PAPI itself a precision instrument approach system.

Precision Approach System

The word "Precision" here describes a navigation category — an instrument system such as ILS or GLS that provides lateral and vertical guidance to a defined minima.

In PAPI's name, "Precision" describes the approach-path indicator itself, not the navigation category of the approach.

08 · Examples of Precision Approach Systems

ILS, GLS & Related Systems

Depending on the applicable aviation standards and installation, precision approach capability may be provided by systems such as:

  • ILS — Instrument Landing System
  • GLS — GBAS Landing System

These provide instrument-based approach guidance to the aircraft, unlike PAPI, which is a purely visual reference.

09 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: PAPI is a precision instrument approach system
Fact

No. PAPI is a visual lighting aid. "Precision" in its name refers to the indicator, not to instrument approach category.

Myth: PAPI provides lateral guidance
Fact

No. PAPI provides vertical visual guidance only — high, low, or on path.

Myth: An aircraft needs a receiver to use PAPI
Fact

No. PAPI is read visually by the pilot — no aircraft navigation receiver is required.

Myth: PAPI and ILS can't be used on the same runway
Fact

No. They are frequently installed together — ILS for instrument guidance, PAPI as a visual cross-check once the runway is visible.

Myth: PAPI works the same in low visibility as an ILS
Fact

No. PAPI requires the pilot to see the lights, so its usefulness is limited in low visibility, unlike an ILS-type precision approach system.

11 · Engineering Verdict

PAPI vs Precision Approach System

Engineering Verdict

PAPI is a visual reference, not an instrument approach system. It gives the pilot a vertical visual cue once the runway environment is in sight, but it does not provide lateral guidance, does not require an aircraft receiver, and cannot by itself establish a precision instrument approach.

A precision approach system such as ILS or GLS provides both lateral and vertical instrument guidance and can support operations in visibility conditions where PAPI alone would not be usable. The two are complementary — PAPI can supplement a precision approach system as a visual cross-check, but it is not a substitute for one.

PAPILook outside → up/down → visual vertical cross-check only.
Precision Approach SystemLook at instruments → left/right + up/down → full instrument guidance.

PAPI can complement a precision approach system, but PAPI itself is not a precision instrument approach system — the "Precision" in its name refers to the indicator, not the navigation category.

12 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PAPI a precision approach system?

No. PAPI is a visual lighting aid providing vertical approach-slope reference. The word "Precision" in its name describes the indicator, not an instrument approach navigation category.

Does PAPI provide lateral guidance?

No. PAPI only tells the pilot whether the aircraft is high, low, or on the approach path vertically — not left or right of the instrument course.

Can PAPI and an ILS be used on the same runway?

Yes. They are commonly installed together — the ILS provides instrument lateral and vertical guidance, while PAPI provides a visual cross-check once the runway environment is visible.

Does an aircraft need special equipment to use PAPI?

No. PAPI is read visually by the pilot and requires no aircraft navigation receiver, unlike an ILS or GLS which generally requires compatible onboard equipment.

Can PAPI be used to fly an approach in low visibility?

PAPI's usefulness is limited by visibility, since the pilot must be able to see the lights. Precision approach systems such as ILS are designed to support instrument operations, including in lower-visibility conditions, subject to the applicable system and operational category.

What are examples of precision approach systems?

Common examples include the Instrument Landing System (ILS) and the GBAS Landing System (GLS), both of which provide instrument-based lateral and vertical approach guidance.

13 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations, §5.3.5 — visual approach slope indicator systems, including PAPI/APAPI configuration, siting and photometric requirements.

ICAO Annex 10, PANS-OPS & FAA Orders

Radio navigation aids and instrument-approach standards — ILS and GBAS performance (Annex 10, Volume I), instrument procedure design (PANS-OPS, Doc 8168), and FAA equivalents such as Order 6750.16 (ILS siting criteria) and Order 8260.3 (TERPS).

IS 12290 & IS 7785 (BIS)

IS 12290 covers isolating transformers for airport lighting systems; IS 7785 is the multi-part series for elevated-type aerodrome lighting fittings. Neither is an equipment specification for PAPI units — for those units, follow ICAO Annex 14 / Doc 9157 Part 4 or the FAA PAPI specification, as the project requires.

Final Engineering Note

Do not treat PAPI as a substitute for a precision instrument approach system in project design or operational planning. Instrument approach category, minima and equipment must always be developed from the current governing standard and project-specific requirements.

PAPI and a precision approach system serve different, complementary purposes — visual vertical reference on one side, full instrument guidance on the other. Confusing the two on the basis of shared terminology is a common but avoidable mistake.
