Light Source & Fixture Technology Comparison

HALOGEN AGL VS LED AGL

Halogen AGL uses a filament lamp as the light source, while LED AGL uses semiconductor light-emitting diodes. LED systems generally offer longer life, lower energy consumption, better vibration resistance, and more stable operation, while halogen systems are simpler, familiar, and can be easier to service by replacing the lamp. For an airport retrofit, the real question isn't "LED or halogen?" — it's whether the LED fixture meets the required photometric performance and is electrically compatible with the complete series-circuit system.

Halogen AGL

A halogen fixture uses a tungsten filament inside a halogen-filled lamp.

Advantages:

  • Mature technology
  • Simple lamp replacement
  • Familiar maintenance procedures
  • Relatively simple electrical arrangement
  • Well-understood photometric behavior

Disadvantages:

  • Shorter lamp life
  • Higher energy consumption
  • High operating temperature
  • Filament sensitivity to vibration and shock
  • Regular lamp replacement
  • Gradual reduction in performance as the lamp ages

LED AGL

An LED fixture replaces the filament with one or more LEDs. The driver is particularly important because LEDs require controlled electrical conditions.

  • Long operating life
  • Lower power consumption
  • Reduced maintenance
  • High resistance to vibration
  • Rapid switching
  • Good intensity control
  • Better repeatability
  • No filament burnout
  • Potentially lower lifecycle cost

04 · Lamp Life

Lamp Life vs LED Life

Halogen

The filament gradually deteriorates until it fails.

NEW LAMP
   ↓
OPERATING HOURS
   ↓
FILAMENT DEGRADATION
   ↓
   FAILURE

LED

There is generally no filament to burn out. Instead, performance can gradually decrease due to:

  • LED degradation
  • Driver aging
  • Thermal stress
  • Optical degradation
  • Electronic component failure

LED life should not simply be described as "the LED never fails." A better engineering description: LED systems generally provide substantially longer useful life, subject to thermal, driver and environmental conditions.

06 · Thermal Management

LEDs Still Generate Heat

This is a major difference that is sometimes misunderstood. LEDs are more efficient than halogen lamps, but they still generate heat.

       LED
        ↓
JUNCTION TEMPERATURE
        ↓
HEAT SINK / HOUSING
        ↓
THERMAL DISSIPATION

Poor thermal management can result in:

  • Reduced LED life
  • Reduced luminous output
  • Driver stress
  • Photometric instability
  • Premature failure

With halogen, the lamp itself intentionally operates at a very high temperature — the thermal risk profile is simply different, not absent, for LED.

08 · CCR Compatibility

A Resistive Load vs an Electronic Load

This is one of the most important AGL engineering considerations. A conventional halogen lamp is fundamentally a resistive filament load. An LED fixture is an electronic load.

Halogen
CCR → TRANSFORMER →
RESISTIVE FILAMENT
LED
CCR → TRANSFORMER →
ELECTRONIC DRIVER → LEDs

Therefore, LED conversion must consider:

  • CCR regulation
  • Minimum circuit load
  • Current waveform
  • Power factor
  • Harmonics where applicable
  • Driver behavior
  • Open-circuit response
  • Fault detection
  • Intensity-step compatibility

A fixture that physically fits the same base does not automatically mean it is electrically interchangeable.

09 · Photometric Difference

Not a Simple Bulb Swap

Halogen

The filament acts as a relatively compact light source. The optical system may use a reflector, lens, filter, or prism.

LED

The optical design is often more integrated: multiple LEDs, secondary optics, lens, reflector, and light guide working together.

Therefore, LED replacement is not simply "remove bulb → insert LED." The LED optical system must produce the required:

  • Candela
  • Beam distribution
  • Vertical distribution
  • Horizontal distribution
  • Colour
  • Angular coverage

13 · Field / SAT Considerations

More Than "Does It Turn On?"

For an LED conversion project, don't only check whether the light turns on. Also verify:

  • CCR compatibility
  • Correct intensity steps
  • Circuit current
  • Fixture operation
  • Alignment
  • Photometric performance
  • Control response
  • Fault monitoring
  • Installation configuration

A successful SAT should demonstrate that the installed LED system works correctly within the existing or modified AGL system.

16 · Strengths & Weaknesses

Key Engineering Comparison

Halogen — Strengths

Simple; mature; easy lamp replacement; familiar technology; relatively straightforward electrical behavior.

Weaknesses

Shorter life; higher energy use; high temperature; more maintenance; filament failure; more frequent replacement.

LED — Strengths

Long life; lower energy consumption; lower maintenance; better vibration resistance; fast switching; good intensity control; better long-term stability when properly designed.

Weaknesses

Higher initial cost; driver/electronic complexity; thermal management required; more sensitive to poor electronic design; retrofit requires system compatibility verification.

18 · Engineering Verdict

Halogen AGL vs LED AGL

Engineering Verdict

Halogen AGL uses a filament lamp as the light source, offering simple, mature, well-understood technology at a lower initial cost but with higher energy consumption and maintenance workload.

LED AGL uses semiconductor light-emitting diodes, offering longer life, lower energy consumption, better vibration resistance, and generally lower lifecycle cost — provided the driver, thermal design, CCR compatibility, and photometric performance are all properly verified. The question for a retrofit is never simply "LED or halogen?" — it's whether the LED fixture is electrically and photometrically interchangeable with the system it's replacing.

HalogenResistive filament load — simple, familiar, but higher energy and lamp-replacement burden.
LEDElectronic driver + LED array load — efficient and long-lived, but demands proper CCR and thermal engineering.

A fixture that physically fits the same base is not automatically electrically interchangeable — CCR compatibility, driver behavior, and photometric verification are the real deciding factors in a halogen-to-LED conversion.

19 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fundamental difference between halogen and LED AGL?

Halogen AGL uses a tungsten filament lamp as the light source. LED AGL uses semiconductor light-emitting diodes driven by dedicated LED driver electronics. One is a resistive load, the other is an electronic load.

Can an LED fixture simply be swapped into an existing halogen circuit?

Not automatically. A fixture that physically fits the same base does not mean it is electrically interchangeable. CCR regulation, minimum circuit load, current waveform, power factor, driver behavior, and fault detection all need to be verified.

Does LED eliminate maintenance?

No. LED reduces routine lamp replacement, but functional checks, photometric verification, and driver/electronics inspection are still required as part of ongoing maintenance.

Do LEDs really not generate heat?

LEDs are more efficient than halogen lamps, but they still generate heat at the junction. Poor thermal management can reduce LED life, luminous output, and photometric stability, so heat sinking and thermal design remain important.

Which technology has the lower lifecycle cost?

LED generally has a lower lifecycle cost due to reduced energy use, lamp replacement, and labour, despite a higher initial fixture cost. The exact economics depend on operating hours, energy price, labour cost, and replacement frequency.

What should a proper halogen-to-LED retrofit include?

It should include an electrical assessment, CCR and transformer compatibility checks, cable/circuit assessment, LED fixture selection, photometric verification, installation, SAT, and field performance verification — not simply removing the halogen lamp and installing an LED fixture.

20 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

ICAO Annex 14 / Aerodrome Design Manual

Photometric, intensity, colour, and beam distribution requirements that both halogen and LED fixtures must satisfy regardless of light source technology.

CCR / Fixture Manufacturer Documentation

Defines the actual electrical compatibility, driver requirements, minimum load, and fault-detection behavior between a specific CCR and an LED or halogen fixture.

DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Do not select a fixture based on wattage or physical fit alone. The final selection should always be developed from CCR compatibility testing, photometric verification, and the approved design.

Halogen and LED AGL are not two versions of the same lamp — one is a resistive filament load with mature, simple behavior; the other is an electronically driven solid-state system that needs its own compatibility, thermal, and photometric verification.
