Base Can & Civil Interface Maintenance

The physical-interface division of the handbook. Division 20 covers the maintenance of the base can, frangible coupling, cable entry, internal pit, foundation, pavement interface and drainage that together support every AGL fixture — ensuring stability, correct elevation, correct alignment and protection from water ingress.

A base-can defect is never "just civil." A deteriorated base can or civil interface can cause fixture movement, incorrect elevation, misalignment, water ingress, cable damage, connector deterioration, frangible-component problems and FOD — so both sides of the interface are inspected and restored together, never in isolation.

Safety before work: isolate and lock out the affected series circuit before opening a base can or disconnecting any connector, transformer or cable — never open a live series circuit. Obtain the airside work permit and operational coordination before working on or near active pavements.

MNT-BCC-010Water Removal
Sequence
  • Identify where water has accumulated (base can, pit, fixture) and how much
  • Confirm the affected circuit is isolated where electrical parts are exposed
  • Remove the water by an approved method without disturbing cables or connectors
  • Remove silt and debris and allow the base can / pit to dry
  • Inspect seals, cable entries, connectors and the fixture for moisture damage
  • Identify the source and correct it (see Drainage Correction, MNT-BCC-011)
  • Carry out applicable electrical testing before return to service
Acceptance / Record

Rule: removing water is not the correction — identify and correct the entry path, or the water will return.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about base can & civil interface maintenance, based on the topics in this division.

8 QUESTIONS
Q1What is base can and civil interface maintenance?

It is the physical-interface division covering the base can, frangible coupling, cable entry, internal pit, foundation, pavement interface and drainage that support the AGL installation.

Q2What does Division 20 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 20 (Base Can & Civil Interface Maintenance) contains 30 topics in three groups: Group A Base Can (12 topics), Group B Civil Interface (12 topics) and Group C Post-Maintenance (6 topics).

Q3Why is a base-can defect never just a civil issue?

A deteriorated base can or civil interface can cause fixture movement, incorrect elevation, misalignment, water ingress, cable damage and connector deterioration.

Q4What safety rule applies before opening a base can?

Isolate and lock out the affected series circuit before opening a base can or disconnecting any connector, transformer or cable, and never open a live series circuit.

Q5What makes a reliable AGL installation?

A stable base can, a sound civil interface, proper drainage and correct fixture geometry together.

Q6What is the core maintenance sequence?

Eleven steps working outward from the fixture: Inspect, Clean, Check Structure, Check Level, Check Alignment, Check Cable Entry, Check Water/Drainage, Repair, Restore, Verify and Record.

Q7How are base-can and civil findings classified?

Good (stable, intact, routine PM), Acceptable (minor deterioration without functional impact, monitor), Deteriorating (developing civil or mechanical defect, planned maintenance), Defective (affecting equipment condition, corrective maintenance) and Critical (significant movement, structural damage, severe water ingress or an unsafe condition, priority or emergency response).

Q8What does the civil interface decision matrix recommend?

Base-can settlement is investigated and corrected, a loose mounting bolt is tightened to the specified torque, an open cable entry is sealed, water accumulation is removed and investigated, poor drainage is corrected, a damaged pit cover is replaced, and a disturbed area is restored.
