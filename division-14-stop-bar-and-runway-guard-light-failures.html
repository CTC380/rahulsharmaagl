Stop Bar & Runway Guard Light Failures

The dedicated failure-intelligence section for Stop Bar Lighting and Runway Guard Lights (RGLs) — the two systems most directly tied to runway-entry control, runway incursion prevention and the visual control of aircraft/vehicle movement at runway holding positions. Do not record a stop bar or RGL simply as “failed.” Identify the failed component, the resulting functional failure, the lighting-service failure, the operational consequence, the root cause and the corrective action.

Initial Issue

Key principle — Component failure → functional failure → lighting-service failure → operational consequence. Division 14 keeps technical failure, functional failure and performance degradation strictly separate — this matters most for RGLs, because a Runway Guard Light can be physically illuminated but still be functionally failed if its required flashing behaviour is absent or incorrect.

SBR-01 Stop-Bar Light Failure
Failure Definition

Failure of an individual stop-bar light fixture resulting in loss or degradation of its intended stop-bar lighting function.

Possible Causes
  • LED failure
  • Driver failure
  • Cable failure
  • Connector failure
  • Circuit failure
  • Control failure
  • Physical damage
Possible Consequences
  • Individual light outage
  • Uneven stop-bar appearance
  • Partial stop-bar outage
  • Reduced visual effectiveness
Investigation

Check fixture, LED, driver, cable, connector, circuit, control status and intensity/output.

SBR-08 Intensity Failure (Stop Bar)
Failure Definition

Stop-bar lights operate but do not provide the required or expected intensity.

Possible Causes
  • LED degradation
  • Driver degradation
  • Electrical supply abnormality
  • Optical degradation
  • Circuit problem
  • Incorrect control/intensity setting where applicable
Important Distinction

Light failure = no required output. Intensity failure = output exists but does not meet the required/expected level.

Investigation

Check electrical input, driver, LED, optical condition, intensity setting and measured/verified output where applicable.

SBR-09 Partial Outage (Stop Bar)
Failure Definition

Part of a stop bar is unavailable while other stop-bar lights remain operational.

Possible Causes
  • Individual LED failures
  • Driver failures
  • Local cable failure
  • Connector failure
  • Circuit sectional failure
Investigation

Determine number of failed lights, position of failed lights, remaining operational lights, circuit arrangement and common cause.

Reliability Significance

A partial outage should not automatically be classified as a complete stop-bar failure.

SBR-10 Complete Outage (Stop Bar)
Failure Definition

Complete loss of the intended stop-bar lighting function.

Possible Causes
  • Circuit failure
  • Power loss
  • Control failure
  • CCR failure
  • Multiple component failures
  • Maintenance isolation
  • Major electrical fault
Investigation
POWERCCR/CONTROLCIRCUITCABLECONNECTORSFIXTURESLEDS
Operational Importance

Because a stop bar is an operationally significant visual control system, complete outage should receive a high-priority operational assessment.

SBR-12 Flashing Failure
Failure Definition

RGL light operates but fails to produce the required flashing function.

Possible Causes
  • Control failure
  • Driver failure
  • LED failure
  • Timing/control electronics failure
  • Communication/control problem
Possible Symptoms
  • Steady light instead of flashing
  • Irregular flashing
  • Incorrect flash sequence
  • Intermittent flashing
  • One side flashing incorrectly
Investigation

Check LED, driver, control signal, flashing/timing function, wiring, connector and control system.

Important Distinction

A light can be illuminated but still be functionally failed if the required flashing behaviour is absent or incorrect.

SBR-18 Intensity Degradation (RGL)
Failure Definition

RGL continues to operate and flash but its light output has degraded.

Possible Causes
  • LED ageing
  • Driver degradation
  • Optical contamination
  • Electrical abnormality
  • Thermal degradation
  • Environmental exposure
Investigation

Check LED output, driver, optical condition, electrical supply and intensity performance.

Important Distinction

RGL failure = functional failure. Intensity degradation = performance deterioration while operation may continue.

SBR-19 Alignment Failure (RGL)
Failure Definition

RGL is no longer correctly positioned or oriented relative to its intended installation.

Possible Causes
  • Fixture movement
  • Mounting failure
  • Structural damage
  • Vehicle impact
  • Foundation movement
  • Incorrect installation
Consequences
  • Reduced conspicuity
  • Incorrect light direction
  • Reduced effectiveness of the RGL indication
Investigation

Check fixture position, mounting, orientation, support, foundation and physical damage. Cross-reference with Division 06 — Mechanical & Structural Failures.

SBR-20 Structural Damage (RGL)

Possible Causes

  • Equipment impact
  • Weather
  • Corrosion
  • Mechanical stress
  • Construction activity
Consequences
  • Alignment failure
  • Fixture failure
  • Cable/connector damage
  • Complete RGL outage
Investigation

Inspect fixture, housing, mounting, support, foundation, cable, connector and alignment.

F. Failure Severity

Every failure receives a severity classification, plus operational-impact tagging. Complete stop-bar outage and RGL flashing failure are treated as high-priority given their runway-incursion-prevention role:

Critical / Safety-Critical — immediate operational or safety risk

Major / Operationally Critical — significant availability impact

Moderate — reduced performance, not yet critical

Minor / Non-Critical — limited or no operational effect

Degradation / Intermittent / Latent — developing or hidden fault

G. Evidence Layer

Visual evidence

  • Fixture, housing and mounting photographs
  • Cable, connector and terminal photographs
  • Impact, corrosion and structural-damage photographs

Technical evidence

  • Continuity, insulation and driver output readings
  • Intensity/output and flashing-function verification
  • Test reports, control logs, maintenance and operating history

The Troubleshooting Atlas answers “I have a fault now — how do I diagnose and fix it?” The Failure Database answers “this failure happened — what happened, why, what evidence proves it, what was done, and how do we stop it recurring?” Kept strictly separate, then linked end-to-end, the two form a genuine AGL Failure Intelligence Engine rather than a simple fault list. Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Stop Bar and Runway Guard Light (RGL) failures, as covered in Division 14 of the AGL Failure Database.

Q1What is Division 14 — Stop Bar & Runway Guard Light Failures in the AGL Failure Database?

Division 14 covers failures of Stop Bars and Runway Guard Lights (RGL). It contains 20 published topics (SBR-01 to SBR-20) spanning LED, driver, cable, connector, circuit and control failures, flashing failure, intensity degradation, alignment failure and structural damage.

Q2Which Stop Bar failures does Division 14 cover?

Stop-bar light failure, LED failure, driver failure, cable failure, connector failure, circuit failure, control failure, intensity failure, partial outage and complete outage.

Q3Which Runway Guard Light failures does Division 14 cover?

RGL failure, flashing failure, LED failure, driver failure, control failure, cable failure, connector failure, intensity degradation, alignment failure and structural damage.

Q4How are the 20 Stop Bar and RGL topics grouped?

They fall into four groups: Stop Bar Component & Circuit Failures (8 topics), Stop Bar Outage Classification (2), Runway Guard Light Component & Circuit Failures (7) and Runway Guard Light Performance & Structural Failures (3).

Q5What Stop Bar failure chains does Division 14 capture?

In the individual-light chain, an LED failure leads to stop-bar light failure, partial outage and reduced visual uniformity. In the circuit chain, a cable failure leads to circuit failure, multiple stop-bar lights unavailable and partial or complete outage. In the control chain, a control failure leads to an incorrect stop-bar command, so the stop bar does not achieve its intended state.

Q6What RGL failure chains does Division 14 capture?

In the flashing chain, control failure leads to flashing function failure, RGL failure and reduced runway-holding-position visual indication. In the structural chain, vehicle impact leads to structural damage, alignment failure and reduced RGL effectiveness. In the electrical chain, a connector failure leads to a high-resistance or unstable connection, driver or LED instability and intermittent RGL operation.

Q7How does a Stop Bar or RGL failure become engineering intelligence?

Every failure follows a loop: failure event, failure mode, failure mechanism, root cause, corrective action, preventive action and post-repair test. For example, a steady light reported instead of flashing is traced to a control failure and a flashing-function failure caused by a timing or control-electronics fault, leading to control-module replacement and a review of control logic and configuration.

Q8How are failure severity and operational impact classified?

Every failure receives a severity classification, plus operational-impact tagging. The classes are Critical / Safety-Critical (immediate operational or safety risk), Major / Operationally Critical (significant availability impact), Moderate (reduced performance, not yet critical), Minor / Non-Critical (limited or no operational effect), and Degradation / Intermittent / Latent (a developing or hidden fault).

Q9How does the Failure Database differ from the Troubleshooting Atlas?

The Troubleshooting Atlas answers “I have a fault now — how do I diagnose and fix it?” The Failure Database answers “this failure happened — what happened, why, what evidence proves it, what was done, and how do we stop it recurring?” Kept strictly separate, then linked end to end, the two form an AGL Failure Intelligence Engine rather than a simple fault list.
