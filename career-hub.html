HR Interview & Resume Prep

The non-technical half of getting hired

Technical knowledge gets you shortlisted — these are the questions that decide the offer.

Common HR Questions

  • Tell me about yourself as an AGL engineer.
  • Why do you want to work in AGL / aviation infrastructure?
  • Describe a technical problem you solved.
  • How do you handle pressure on an active airside site?
  • Are you comfortable working night shifts and safety procedures?
  • What are your strengths as an engineer?
Sample: "Tell me about yourself"
Structure it as background → relevant experience → why this role. Lead with your electrical engineering foundation, connect it to any hands-on testing/commissioning exposure you have, and close by naming specifically why AGL and this role interest you — keep the whole answer to about 60–90 seconds.

Resume Preparation Tips

  • Lead with hands-on AGL/electrical exposure, not just qualifications.
  • Quantify where possible — airports worked on, systems tested, circuits commissioned.
  • Name specific standards you've worked with (ICAO Annex 14, FAA ACs).
  • List specific equipment: CCRs, isolating transformers, photometric test kits.
  • Keep it to one page for fresher/junior roles, two for senior/specialist.
  • Match your listed skills to the question categories above.

Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about the career hub: the question bank, mock interviews, troubleshooting practice, the fresher roadmap and HR preparation.

Q1What is the AGL Career & Interview Hub?

It is a preparation hub for Airfield Ground Lighting careers that brings together structured interview questions, a mock interview simulator, troubleshooting scenarios, a fresher career roadmap and HR and resume preparation. It is designed to take you from AGL fundamentals to interview-ready in one place.

Q2How many interview questions are there?

The hub is organised into 12 categories with 4,000 questions in total. All twelve category banks are live.

Q3What are the 12 question categories?

AGL Fundamentals, Runway Lighting, Taxiway Lighting, Approach & Visual Guidance, CCR & Electrical Systems, AGL Components, Photometry, ICAO / FAA / Standards, Troubleshooting & Maintenance, Installation & Commissioning, Electrical Engineering for AGL, and Career & HR Interview. Each links to its own question bank, filterable by level and searchable by keyword.

Q4How does the AGL mock interview work?

You pick a level (Fresher, Junior / AGL Engineer or Senior / Specialist) and get a short round of real interview questions with a place to type your answer. You then see a model answer and key points to self-assess against, and the prompts encourage you to practise out loud.

Q5What does each mock interview level cover?

Fresher covers basic AGL terminology and fundamentals. Junior / AGL Engineer covers technical systems, electrical concepts and standards. Senior / Specialist covers scenario-based troubleshooting and judgement calls.

Q6What is the AGL troubleshooting interview section?

It presents realistic field scenarios for practice, structured as Problem, Causes, Investigation, Solution. You think through your own answer first, then reveal a structured model response to compare against.

Q7What is the fresher career roadmap?

An eight-stage path from electrical engineer to AGL engineer: learn airport fundamentals, learn AGL components, learn lighting systems, learn electrical systems, learn ICAO and FAA standards, learn installation and testing, prepare for interviews, and start your AGL career. Each stage assumes the one before it, and the last stage points toward fresher, junior engineer or site-engineer roles.

Q8Where should I start on the hub?

Choose the path that matches you. Freshers should start from AGL fundamentals and follow the 8-stage roadmap. Electrical engineers can move from general electrical work into AGL-specific systems through the categories. AGL engineers can sharpen depth with mock interviews and troubleshooting, and experienced engineers can go straight to senior-level scenario and troubleshooting practice.

Q9What HR questions come up in AGL interviews?

Common ones include: tell me about yourself as an AGL engineer, why you want to work in AGL or aviation infrastructure, describe a technical problem you solved, how you handle pressure on an active airside site, whether you are comfortable with night shifts and safety procedures, and what your strengths are as an engineer.

Q10How should I answer "Tell me about yourself" in an AGL interview?

Structure it as background, then relevant experience, then why this role. Lead with your electrical engineering foundation, connect it to any hands-on testing or commissioning exposure, and close by naming why AGL and this role interest you. Keep the whole answer to about 60–90 seconds.

Q11What should an AGL resume include?

Lead with hands-on AGL or electrical exposure, not just qualifications, and quantify where possible, such as airports worked on, systems tested and circuits commissioned. Name the standards you have worked with (ICAO Annex 14, FAA ACs) and specific equipment such as CCRs, isolating transformers and photometric test kits. Keep it to one page for fresher and junior roles and two for senior and specialist roles.
