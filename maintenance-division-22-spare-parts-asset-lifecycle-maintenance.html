Spare Parts & Asset Lifecycle Maintenance

The spares-management and asset-lifecycle control layer of the handbook. It connects maintenance with the practical questions of what spare is required, how much should be stocked, whether it is available, how it is stored, and when the asset itself should be repaired, replaced or retired.

The key question: is the required spare available, identifiable, serviceable and traceable — and is the asset it supports still worth maintaining?

Distinction: Divisions 01–05 decide what maintenance to perform. Division 22 decides whether the parts exist to perform it, and tracks the asset across its full life — from installation to retirement and disposal — using the maintenance and failure history captured in Division 21.

Spare-Part Status Control

A simple controlled inventory status keeps unserviceable parts out of the usable-spares pool.

StatusMeaning
ServiceableAvailable for issue
ReservedAllocated to a specific requirement
Inspection RequiredAwaiting technical inspection
DamagedNot available for service
RejectedNot approved for use
ObsoleteNo longer approved / supported
ExpiredShelf-life requirement exceeded, where applicable

Rule: damaged, rejected, obsolete or expired parts must be clearly segregated from serviceable inventory to prevent accidental issue of a defective spare.

Replacement Priority

How an asset's replacement priority is set once condition, age and support risk are combined.

Priority 1

Critical

High operational / safety consequence combined with poor reliability or support.

Priority 2

High

Increasing failures, deterioration, or difficult spare availability.

Priority 3

Planned

Aging asset requiring a planned, budgeted replacement.

Priority 4

Monitor

Serviceable asset with no immediate replacement requirement.

Note: the actual classification should follow the airport's approved asset-management criteria — these bands are a proposed management framework.

Lifecycle Risk Indicators

Combinations that should trigger an engineering review of the asset.

Indicator CombinationTriggers
High age + increasing failuresReplacement assessment
High repair frequency + high maintenance costLifecycle review
Obsolete equipment + poor spare availabilityPlanned replacement
Critical asset + long supplier lead timeIncrease critical spare strategy
Repeated component failureFailure / root-cause investigation
End-of-life asset + limited manufacturer supportPriority lifecycle planning

Spare Issue & Replenishment Flow

The twelve-step sequence that carries a spare from a maintenance defect through to an updated inventory record.

1
Maintenance Defect

A defect requiring a spare is identified.

2
Spare Required

Confirm the specific part needed.

3
Check Inventory

Verify availability and status.

4
Issue Spare

Release the part for the work order.

5
Record Consumption

Log date, asset, quantity, technician.

6
Update Stock

Reduce recorded quantity available.

7
Check Minimum Level

Compare remaining stock to the minimum.

8
Reorder If Required

Initiate procurement below minimum.

9
Receive

Accept the incoming shipment.

10
Inspect

Verify condition and specification.

11
Store

Place in the controlled storage location.

12
Update Inventory

Close the loop on the stock record.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about spare parts & asset lifecycle maintenance, based on the topics in this division.

8 QUESTIONS
Q1What is Spare Parts & Asset Lifecycle Maintenance?

It is the spares-management and asset-lifecycle control layer of the handbook. It connects maintenance with practical questions: what spare is required, how much should be stocked, whether it is available, how it is stored, and when an asset should be replaced or retired.

Q2What does Division 22 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 22 (Spare Parts & Asset Lifecycle Maintenance) contains 34 topics in three groups: Group A Spare Parts Management (12 topics), Group B Equipment Lifecycle (12 topics) and Group C Critical AGL Spares (10 topics).

Q3How does Division 22 differ from Divisions 01 to 05?

Divisions 01 to 05 decide what maintenance to perform. Division 22 decides whether the parts exist to perform it, and tracks the asset across its full life from installation to retirement and disposal using the maintenance and failure records.

Q4What is the key principle for AGL spares?

The required spare shall be available, identifiable, serviceable and traceable when the AGL asset requires maintenance.

Q5What spare-part status categories are used?

Serviceable (available for issue), Reserved (allocated to a specific requirement), Inspection Required, Damaged, Rejected, Obsolete, and Expired where a shelf-life requirement applies. Damaged, rejected, obsolete or expired parts must be clearly segregated from serviceable inventory to prevent accidental issue of a defective spare.

Q6What is the spare issue and replenishment flow?

Twelve steps: maintenance defect, spare required, check inventory, issue spare, record consumption, update stock, check the minimum level, reorder if required, receive, inspect, store, and update inventory.

Q7How is replacement priority set?

Priority 1 Critical is high operational or safety consequence combined with poor reliability or support; Priority 2 High is increasing failures, deterioration or difficult spare availability; Priority 3 Planned is an aging asset needing a budgeted replacement; and Priority 4 Monitor is a serviceable asset with no immediate replacement requirement. These bands are a proposed management framework, and actual classification should follow the airport's approved asset-management criteria.

Q8What lifecycle risk indicators should trigger an engineering review?

High age with increasing failures triggers a replacement assessment; high repair frequency with high maintenance cost triggers a lifecycle review; obsolete equipment with poor spare availability triggers planned replacement; a critical asset with a long supplier lead time means increasing the critical-spare strategy; repeated component failure triggers a failure or root-cause investigation; and an end-of-life asset with limited manufacturer support triggers priority lifecycle planning.
