Product Overview

PAPI Alignment Telescope / Aiming Device

What It Is

PAPI light units must be accurately positioned and aimed to establish the intended visual approach path. Precision AGL Solutions supplies PAPI alignment telescopes and aiming devices for installation, commissioning, maintenance and periodic verification.

Depending on configuration, the equipment provides optical line-of-sight alignment, angular reference measurement, or both.

For FAA systems, AC 150/5345-28H requires an aiming tool that measures the vertical angle of the light-beam centre from 2° to 8°, in 10-minute-of-arc increments, with repeatable accuracy of ±3 arc minutes.

Establishes the Relationship Between

  • PAPI light-unit optical axis
  • Runway centreline
  • Approved approach path
  • PAPI installation reference
  • Required vertical aiming angle
  • Horizontal alignment reference
  • Survey or engineering reference points

Configurations may include a telescope, angular scale, sighting arrangement, bubble level, digital angular measurement, adjustable mount, reference reticle, targeting system or PAPI-specific fixture. Select according to the PAPI manufacturer, installation procedure and airport authority requirements.

Why Alignment Equipment Is Required

A PAPI Is Not Just Lights at an Approximate Angle

A Defined Visual Glide Path

FAA installation guidance requires the PAPI to be sited and aimed so the approach path provides the required obstacle clearance and threshold crossing height. Where a runway has a published instrument approach glide path or vertical descent angle, the PAPI path should be coordinated with it where practicable.

Optical Reference

Each light unit produces a red/white transition. Its centre is the light-beam centre used for vertical aiming. AC 150/5345-28H defines it as the transition band between red and white and requires built-in vertical adjustment across the specified range.

Alignment Functions

Five Distinct Functions

Vertical Aiming

Measure or verify the vertical angle of the light-beam centre.

Horizontal Alignment

Verify the optical axis relates correctly to the runway centreline or approved horizontal reference.

Optical Sighting

Establish a repeatable line of sight with a telescope or sighting system.

Reference Transfer

Transfer a known survey or installation reference to the PAPI light unit.

Installation Verification

Confirm the installed equipment corresponds to the approved design and adjustment settings.

AC 150/5345-28H requires this aiming tool to be furnished with the PAPI system, and it may be built into the light units. Alternatively the light units may be factory-calibrated to a fixed vertical angle specified by the purchaser, in which case the manufacturer must provide a field procedure to check the calibration to ±3 minutes of arc. A separate alignment telescope therefore complements the supplied tool for survey-referenced sighting and verification; it does not replace it.

Optical Telescope Function

Controlled Line of Sight

Optical Characteristics to Review

  • Magnification
  • Field of view
  • Reticle design
  • Optical clarity
  • Focusing range
  • Target visibility
  • Angular reference
  • Sight-line stability

Higher magnification helps identify distant targets but narrows the field of view and increases vibration sensitivity. The appropriate specification depends on target distance and required accuracy.

Reticle and Angular Reference

The reticle may provide a central crosshair, angular reference, target alignment marks, horizontal and vertical references or calibration markings.

Angular measurement may use a mechanical scale, optical graduated scale, vernier, digital inclinometer, digital encoder or integrated display. For FAA PAPI aiming, meet the system's performance requirements rather than relying on the nominal resolution of a generic instrument.

PAPI Light-Unit Aiming

Vertical, Horizontal and Per-Unit

Vertical Aiming

The units in a row are aimed at different elevation angles around the desired glide path to give the required red/white indications. FAA Order JO 6850.2C describes the siting and aiming criteria.

For a four-unit PAPI the setting angles are normally the approach angle θ −30′, θ −10′, θ +10′ and θ +30′. A 3° path therefore gives 2°30′, 2°50′, 3°10′ and 3°30′, which is why a 10-minute graduation is the minimum useful resolution. Confirm the settings, including those for a two-unit system, against the applicable Annex 14 or FAA edition.

The installation angle is set by approved approach geometry, obstacle clearance, threshold crossing height and design criteria. Use the device to reproduce the approved engineering value, not to select an angle independently.

Horizontal / Azimuth Alignment

The row of PAPI units is laid out perpendicular to the runway centreline, and each unit is aimed in azimuth parallel to the runway centreline (the vertical angle is a separate setting). A telescope can establish the line of sight related to the centreline, particularly where:

  • The centreline is not visually obvious
  • The PAPI is offset
  • Terrain affects the installation
  • Obstacle clearance needs a modified arrangement
  • The installation is being relocated
  • Existing alignment is under investigation
Typical PAPI Aiming Workflow

Survey → Reference → Position → Sight → Aim → Verify → Record

The actual sequence must follow the PAPI manufacturer's installation procedure and project acceptance requirements.

  1. Survey — confirm approved location, elevation and runway relationship
  2. Reference — establish runway centreline, threshold and vertical reference
  3. Position — place the device at the prescribed reference location
  4. Sight — establish the line of sight with the telescope or target
  5. Aim — adjust the unit to required vertical and horizontal alignment
  6. Verify — repeat the measurement to confirm the setting
  7. Record — document alignment values and inspection data

FAA Testing

FAA PAPI requirements include testing the aiming device to demonstrate that light-unit movement is correctly indicated, with the measuring device checked at one-degree intervals from 2° to 8°. The device is controlled measurement equipment, not merely a viewing telescope.

Why It Matters

A damaged or incorrectly referenced aiming device can produce a consistent but incorrect alignment. After realignment or unit replacement, the authority may require a flight inspection or a notified unserviceability before the PAPI returns to service. Follow the airport procedure.

PAPI Installation Verification

What the Equipment Helps Confirm

Vertical & Horizontal

Optical & Mechanical

The optical reference is clear and repeatable. FAA AC 150/5345-28H requires adjustment hardware to be vibration-resistant and to prevent movement of the optical system.

Post-Impact

Units can be disturbed by maintenance vehicles, grass-cutting, snow-removal and service equipment, or construction. Even with an intact enclosure, a small alignment change can affect the optical indication. The device determines whether the unit has moved relative to its established reference. FAA-qualified PAPI units also include an excessive-tilt cut-out that switches the system off when a unit moves out of its aiming angle by a fraction of a degree, so a unit that is still operating can nevertheless be slightly out of alignment.

FAQ

PAPI Alignment Telescope / Aiming Device — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a PAPI alignment telescope or aiming device?

It is field equipment that supports the installation, aiming, alignment and verification of PAPI systems, transferring the approved runway and approach geometry to the individual light units. Depending on configuration, it provides optical line-of-sight alignment, angular reference measurement, or both. It is used for installation, commissioning, maintenance and periodic verification.

What does the FAA require of a PAPI aiming tool?

FAA AC 150/5345-28H requires an aiming tool that measures the vertical angle of the light-beam centre from 2° to 8°, in 10-minute-of-arc graduations, with repeatable accuracy of ±3 minutes of arc. The tool must be furnished with the PAPI system and may be built into the light units. Alternatively the units may be factory-calibrated to a fixed vertical angle, in which case the manufacturer must provide a field procedure to check the calibration to ±3 minutes of arc.

Can an alignment telescope replace the supplied PAPI aiming tool?

No. A separate alignment telescope complements the supplied tool for survey-referenced sighting and verification; it does not replace it. A generic survey telescope should not automatically be described as an FAA-compliant PAPI aiming device, and for procurement it helps to state whether the offer is a dedicated aiming tool, an optical alignment telescope, a combined device or supporting survey equipment.

How are PAPI unit setting angles established?

For a four-unit PAPI the setting angles are normally the approach angle minus 30 minutes, minus 10 minutes, plus 10 minutes and plus 30 minutes, so a 3 degree path gives 2°30′, 2°50′, 3°10′ and 3°30′, which is why a 10-minute graduation is the minimum useful resolution. Confirm settings, including those for a two-unit system, against the applicable Annex 14 or FAA edition. The device reproduces the approved engineering value; it is not used to select the angle independently.

How is horizontal (azimuth) alignment checked?

The row of PAPI units is laid out perpendicular to the runway centreline, and each unit is aimed in azimuth parallel to the runway centreline, with the vertical angle as a separate setting. A telescope can establish the line of sight related to the centreline, particularly where the centreline is not visually obvious, the PAPI is offset, terrain affects the installation, obstacle clearance needs a modified arrangement, or the installation is being relocated.

What is the typical PAPI aiming workflow?

The sequence is survey, reference, position, sight, aim, verify and record: confirm the approved location, elevation and runway relationship, establish the runway centreline, threshold and vertical reference, place the device at the prescribed location, establish the line of sight, adjust the unit to the required vertical and horizontal alignment, repeat the measurement and document the values. The actual sequence must follow the PAPI manufacturer's installation procedure and project acceptance requirements.

How does an alignment telescope differ from a clinometer or a check stick?

A telescope is primary for optical line of sight, target alignment and horizontal reference, while a clinometer or digital inclinometer is primary for angular measurement; for detailed installation the two may be used together. A check stick provides a physical reference and is primary for mechanical verification, whereas the telescope provides an optical reference and is suitable for initial commissioning and detailed aiming where designed for PAPI.

When is PAPI alignment equipment used?

Typical uses are new installation, commissioning, alignment and re-alignment, periodic inspection, post-maintenance verification after unit replacement, post-impact inspection, PAPI relocation, runway rehabilitation and troubleshooting. After an impact, a unit that is still operating can nevertheless be slightly out of alignment, because FAA-qualified units switch off only when a unit moves out of its aiming angle by a fraction of a degree.

How is a PAPI aiming device calibrated and checked?

FAA PAPI requirements include testing the aiming device to demonstrate that light-unit movement is correctly indicated, with the measuring device checked at one-degree intervals from 2° to 8°. Before an alignment activity, verify instrument identification and condition, reference target, optical cleanliness, mounting stability, angular scale, zero setting, the applicable PAPI model and the current installation procedure. A damaged or incorrectly referenced device can produce a consistent but incorrect alignment.

What should I do if the telescope reading does not repeat or does not match earlier records?

If the angular reading does not repeat, check for a loose mounting, unstable stand, mechanical backlash, operator sighting variation, a damaged mechanism or an incorrect reference, and repeat under a controlled procedure before adjusting the PAPI. If alignment differs from previous records, first establish whether the reference changed, such as the survey reference, runway works, threshold or PAPI relocation, or the published approach geometry, and do not adjust until the reference is confirmed.

What information is needed to select a PAPI alignment telescope?

Provide the PAPI manufacturer and model, number of light units, installation drawing, required aiming angle, required measurement accuracy, existing aiming equipment, existing survey references, airport design standard, applicable FAA or ICAO requirements, the commissioning or maintenance procedure and any required calibration documentation.

Engineering Note

A PAPI alignment telescope should be specified according to its actual measurement and reference function, not simply its optical magnification. For FAA applications, the dedicated aiming tool must measure 2° to 8° in 10-minute-of-arc increments with ±3 arc-minute repeatable accuracy, so a general optical telescope may be an important part of the toolkit without being the complete PAPI aiming instrument.

For procurement, identify whether the offered equipment is: (1) a dedicated PAPI aiming tool; (2) an optical alignment telescope; (3) a combined telescope / aiming device; or (4) supporting survey / alignment equipment. This keeps the technical submittal clear and prevents a general-purpose optical instrument being represented as meeting a PAPI-specific aiming requirement.
