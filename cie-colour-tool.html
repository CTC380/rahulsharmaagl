This is an educational orientation tool. The spectral locus (outer curve) is plotted from the precise CIE 1931 2° standard observer chromaticity coordinates (the same values published in the CIE 1931 colour space reference), and the coloured zones are positioned using published reference boundary equations in the style of FAR §25.1397 / SAE J578 signal-colour definitions — a closer, line-by-line approximation than a simple grid, but still not a reproduction of the current ICAO Annex 14 Attachment 1 or FAA AC 150/5345-53 chromaticity tables, which are periodically revised. For compliance testing and certification, always use calibrated photometric equipment and the exact boundary tables in the current applicable standard.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about the CIE Colour Orientation Tool and aviation signal-colour chromaticity.

Q1What does the CIE Colour Orientation Tool do?

It plots a CIE 1931 chromaticity coordinate (x, y) on the chromaticity diagram and shows which general aviation signal-colour family it sits nearest to: red, yellow, green, blue or white. It is an educational learning aid for AGL colour concepts.

Q2Can I use this tool for compliance or certification?

No. It is an orientation tool. The colour zones are an illustrative approximation and do not reproduce the current ICAO Annex 14 Attachment 1 or FAA AC 150/5345-53 chromaticity tables, which are periodically revised. Compliance testing needs calibrated photometric equipment and the exact boundary tables in the current applicable standard.

Q3What is the CIE 1931 chromaticity diagram?

It is a two-dimensional map of colour using x and y coordinates. The outer curve is the spectral locus, plotted here from the CIE 1931 2° standard observer chromaticity coordinates, and colours are located inside it.

Q4How do I plot a point?

Enter x and y coordinates and select Plot Point, choose a quick preset for a typical red, yellow, green, blue or white, or click or tap directly on the diagram. Reset clears the point.

Q5What are the colour zones based on?

They are positioned using published reference boundary equations in the style of FAR §25.1397 and SAE J578 signal-colour definitions. This is a closer approximation than a simple grid, but it is still not the current ICAO or FAA table.

Q6What does the nearest colour family result mean?

It tells you which illustrative zone your coordinate falls in, with a short description of where that colour is used in airfield lighting. It gives a sense of orientation, not a pass or fail result.

Q7Why does chromaticity matter for airfield lighting?

Colour is an important part of airport visual communication, and different systems use specified colours for different operational purposes. Evaluating colour covers chromaticity coordinates, colour boundaries, luminous intensity and colour consistency.

Q8Can chromaticity change over time?

Yes. Lens ageing and contamination can shift a fixture's chromaticity over time, which is why colour is checked as part of photometric testing and maintenance.

Q9Where can I find the exact colour boundaries?

In the current applicable standard, such as ICAO Annex 14 or FAA AC 150/5345-53. Use the exact tables in the latest edition for any compliance work.
