Calculator #40

PAPI BOQ Calculator

Bill of quantities for 2-box (APAPI) and 4-box PAPI installations: light units, lamps, isolating transformers, frangible legs and bases, foundations, primary cable, connectors, trench and duct, CCR and aiming equipment.

About this calculator

Add one row per runway-end installation. Choose 2-box or 4-box, one or both sides of the runway, the lead-in distance from the nearest AGL circuit or vault, and whether a new CCR is needed. Quantities come from the construction assumptions below, all editable. The calculator does not position the units or set the aiming angles — use the PAPI Installation & Glide-Path Geometry calculator for that — and its output is a nominal estimate, not a final BOQ.

Methodology

How This Calculator Works

Each runway-end row is expanded into units, cable, trench, foundation and circuit quantities from geometry and the editable assumptions, then summed across the project.

Units and bar length

Nunits = boxes × sides    Lbar = (boxes − 1) × S

S is the spacing between adjacent units. The default is a typical value only; take the spacing from the applicable standard and your layout drawing. Each unit gets one isolating transformer, primary and secondary connector sets, frangible legs, a base and a foundation pad.

Primary cable, trench and duct

Lcable = 2 (Llead-in + Lbar) + 2 (Lcross + Lbar) [both sides] + Nunits × tail
Lcross = runway width + 2 × distance of inner unit from runway edge

The inner-unit distance defaults to 15 m for a 4-box PAPI and 10 m for a 2-box APAPI (typical ICAO values; confirm against the applicable standard). A series circuit is a loop, so cable runs out and back (factor 2). The slack allowance is applied on top. The trench follows the single route; for both-sided installations a duct is added across the runway, plus any extra duct you enter per row.

Transformer selection

Wunit = lamps per unit × W per lamp    VAreq = Wunit × (1 + margin)

The transformer is the smallest standard size (45 / 65 / 100 / 150 / 200 / 300 / 500 W) not below the required VA.

CCR sizing

Pcircuit = Nunits × Wunit / ηtr + I² × R′ × Lcable
kWCCR ≥ Pcircuit × (1 + margin)

One dedicated CCR per runway-end row marked "New", selected from the standard sizes 4 / 7.5 / 10 / 15 / 20 / 25 / 30 / 50 / 70 / 100 kW. The check includes cable I²R loss. For a shared circuit, size the CCR with the CCR Selection & Loading calculator.

Foundation and structure

One concrete pad per unit (length × width × depth), reinforcement per m³, and legs, couplings, base plates and anchor bolts per unit. Excavation covers the trench (length × width × depth) and each foundation pit (pad volume, net); the sand bed comes from trench length × width × bed thickness.

What this calculator does not do

It does not locate the units, set the aiming angles or check obstacle clearance, and it does not price the BOQ. It does not size a shared circuit's CCR or check voltage drop on the series loop. Frangibility, wind loading and foundation design must be verified against the manufacturer's design.

FAQ

PAPI BOQ Calculator: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on how the PAPI bill of quantities is built from runway-end schedules and assumptions.

What does the PAPI BOQ Calculator do?

It builds a calculated bill of quantities for PAPI and APAPI installations. Each runway-end row is expanded into units, cable, trench, foundation and circuit quantities from geometry and editable assumptions, then summed across the project. It also gives a per-installation breakdown, CSV import and export, and a printable BOQ.

How do I enter the PAPI installations?

Add one row per runway end, with the system (2 or 4 boxes), the sides (1 or 2), the lead-in cable length, any extra duct, and whether the CCR is new or existing. Units per installation = boxes per bar × sides, and choosing both sides adds the cable and duct crossing the runway automatically. You can also import a CSV with the columns runway_end, boxes, sides, leadin_m, duct_m and ccr.

How is the primary cable length calculated?

Lcable = 2(Llead-in + Lbar) + 2(Lcross + Lbar) for both sides + Nunits × tail, where Lbar = (boxes − 1) × S and Lcross = runway width + 2 × the distance of the inner unit from the runway edge. The inner-unit distance defaults to 15 m for a 4-box PAPI and 10 m for a 2-box APAPI, which are typical ICAO values to confirm against your standard. The factor of 2 is there because a series circuit is a loop, and the slack allowance is applied on top.

How is the isolating transformer size chosen?

Wunit = lamps per unit × watts per lamp and VAreq = Wunit × (1 + margin). The transformer is the smallest standard size (45, 65, 100, 150, 200, 300 or 500 W) not below the required VA. Selecting LED or halogen loads a preset for lamps per unit and watts per lamp, which you should replace with your PAPI unit datasheet values.

How is the CCR sized?

Pcircuit = Nunits × Wunit ÷ ηtr + I² × R′ × Lcable, and the CCR must be at least Pcircuit × (1 + margin). One dedicated CCR is counted per runway-end row marked New, selected from the standard sizes 4, 7.5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 50, 70 and 100 kW. A CCR marked Existing is excluded from the BOQ, and for a shared circuit you should size the CCR with the CCR Selection and Loading calculator.

What foundation and structure items does it include?

One concrete pad per unit (length × width × depth), reinforcement per m³, and legs, couplings, base plates and anchor bolts per unit. Excavation covers the trench and each foundation pit, and the sand bed comes from trench length × width × bed thickness. Each unit also gets one isolating transformer, primary and secondary connector sets, frangible legs, a base and a foundation pad.

Are the default assumptions manufacturer data?

No. They are typical starting values, not manufacturer data, so replace them with your PAPI unit datasheet, project specification and cable data. The order quantity applies the allowance, rounding counted items up to whole pieces and measured items up to 2 decimals (3 for m³). PAPI siting, unit spacing, setback, aiming angles, frangibility and circuit design must come from the applicable standard and the approved manufacturer design.

Is the result a final BOQ, and what does it not do?

No. It is a calculated nominal quantity, not a final BOQ quantity. It does not locate the units, set the aiming angles, check obstacle clearance or price the BOQ, and it does not size a shared circuit's CCR or check voltage drop on the series loop. Verify the result against the approved PAPI layout drawing and manufacturer data before issue.
