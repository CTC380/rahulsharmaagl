Overview of FAA Airport Lighting Standards

A practical introduction to the FAA framework for airfield lighting — the key Advisory Circulars, the systems they govern, the photometric and electrical requirements behind them, and how FAA compares with ICAO on Indian and international projects.

RS

Rahul Sharma

Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist

Sep 4, 2026 Standards Guide

Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) systems are critical for safe aircraft operations, especially during night, low visibility, and adverse weather conditions. In the United States and on many international projects, these systems are governed by the standards of the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA).

FAA standards are known for their strict performance requirements, detailed engineering specifications, and strong focus on safety and reliability. For AGL engineers, understanding these standards is essential when working on international tenders, ICAO-aligned projects, or FAA-compliant installations.

Key Takeaway

FAA airport lighting standards define the design, performance, installation, and testing requirements for every type of airfield lighting system — from a documented AC 150/5340-30 layout down to a single L-823 connector. For AGL engineers, that knowledge is a competitive advantage on international and high-specification airport projects.

01

What Are FAA Airport Lighting Standards?

FAA airport lighting standards define the design, performance, installation, and testing requirements for different types of airfield lighting systems. These standards are intended to ensure:

  • Uniform light output in terms of intensity and colour
  • Proper beam alignment and coverage
  • Electrical safety and reliability
  • Compatibility with aircraft navigation requirements
  • Standardization across airports

FAA standards are primarily documented through the AC 150/5340 series (visual aids: installation, maintenance, signs and marking), the AC 150/5345 series (equipment specifications), AC 150/5370-10 (construction standards, including Item L-125 for lighting installation), the AC 150/5300 series (airport design), and Engineering Briefs (EBs) that provide updates and clarifications. AC numbers and revision letters change, so always confirm the current edition on the FAA website.

02

Key FAA Advisory Circulars

Three groups of Advisory Circulars matter most for day-to-day AGL work:

AC 150/5340-30 Design and installation details — runway and taxiway lighting layout, approach lighting systems (ALS), and spacing, positioning, and aiming.
AC 150/5345 Series Equipment specifications, e.g. AC 150/5345-46 (fixtures), AC 150/5345-28 (PAPI), AC 150/5345-53 (equipment certification).
AC 150/5340-26 Maintenance of airport lighting — inspection procedures, preventive maintenance, and troubleshooting for O&M teams.

Correct installation geometry is essential because lighting performance depends not only on the fitting itself but also on its position and aiming, as set out in AC 150/5340-30.

03

Key Components Covered Under FAA Standards

FAA standards address five main groups of airfield visual aids:

Runway lighting — edge, centerline, threshold, and end lights, including High Intensity Runway Lighting (HIRL) with multiple brightness steps
Taxiway lighting — blue edge lights and green centerline lights, with controlled spacing and beam angles
Approach Lighting Systems (ALS) — ALSF-1, ALSF-2, and MALSR, for visual alignment and distance perception on landing
PAPI / VASI — precise angle settings, red/white colour transition, and photometric accuracy
Airfield signage (L-858) — legend size and font, colour coding for mandatory and information signs, and illumination levels

Together these systems provide the visual connection between the approach path, the runway environment, and safe ground movement.

04

Photometric Requirements — Core of FAA Standards

Photometry is a major focus of FAA airport lighting requirements. Key photometric parameters include:

Intensity Candela output
Beam Spread Horizontal and vertical
Colour Specifications Consistent, standard-compliant colour output
Uniformity Consistent performance across the fitting population

Fixtures demonstrate performance through laboratory photometric testing as part of equipment certification, while in-service performance is addressed through maintenance guidance such as AC 150/5340-26, ensuring pilots receive clear and consistent visual signals under the intended operating conditions.

For AGL engineers, photometric compliance is not simply a laboratory requirement — correct installation, aiming, optical condition, and field performance are all important to maintaining the intended lighting characteristics.

05

Electrical System Requirements

FAA standards also address important electrical aspects of AGL systems, with reliability and redundancy as key priorities:

  • Series circuits, typically operating at 6.6 A
  • Use of isolating transformers
  • Secondary cable specifications
  • L-823-type connector standards

The electrical system works as an integrated chain — from the power source and regulator through the series circuit and isolation transformer to the individual airfield light.

CCR (6.6A) → Series Circuit → Isolation Transformer → L-823 Connector → Light Fitting

This is a generalised comparison, and the differences vary item by item. In many Indian airport projects, ICAO is the primary reference, while FAA is often cited for equipment specifications (the L-series), higher-specification requirements or specific tenders.

07

Why FAA Standards Matter in India

Even in ICAO-based projects, FAA standards are often referenced for:

  • High-performance lighting systems
  • Imported fittings
  • Premium airport infrastructure
  • Projects involving international consultants

Knowledge of FAA requirements adds strong technical credibility, particularly when working with international equipment manufacturers, consultants, and airport infrastructure projects.

08

Key Takeaways

  • FAA standards are documented mainly through the AC 150/5340 series, AC 150/5345 series, AC 150/5370-10, and Engineering Briefs
  • They cover runway, taxiway, approach, PAPI/VASI, and L-858 signage systems, along with photometric and electrical requirements
  • FAA's approach is detailed and prescriptive with an equipment certification program, compared to ICAO's performance-based, adaptable framework
  • ICAO is the primary reference on most Indian projects, with FAA used for higher-specification or internationally consulted work
  • Understanding both frameworks strengthens an engineer's ability to deliver safe, reliable, and compliant airfield lighting systems

Conclusion

FAA airport lighting standards provide a highly structured and performance-driven framework for designing, installing, testing, and maintaining airfield lighting systems. From photometric precision to electrical reliability, the requirements are developed with safety as the top priority.

For engineers working in Airfield Ground Lighting, understanding FAA standards is not just useful — it is a competitive advantage, especially for international and high-specification airport projects. A strong AGL professional should understand not only how an airfield lighting system works, but also why its design, photometry, installation, equipment certification, and maintenance requirements are defined the way they are.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are FAA airport lighting standards?

They define the design, performance, installation and testing requirements for the different types of airfield lighting systems, to ensure uniform light output, proper beam alignment and coverage, electrical safety and reliability, compatibility with aircraft navigation requirements and standardisation across airports. In the United States and on many international projects, AGL systems are governed by these FAA standards.

Which FAA documents contain the airport lighting standards?

They are documented mainly through the AC 150/5340 series (visual aids: installation, maintenance, signs and marking), the AC 150/5345 series (equipment specifications), AC 150/5370-10 (construction standards, including Item L-125 for lighting installation), the AC 150/5300 series (airport design) and Engineering Briefs. AC numbers and revision letters change, so always confirm the current edition on the FAA website.

What do AC 150/5340-30, AC 150/5345 and AC 150/5340-26 cover?

AC 150/5340-30 covers design and installation details, including runway and taxiway lighting layout, approach lighting systems and spacing, positioning and aiming. The AC 150/5345 series gives equipment specifications such as AC 150/5345-46 (fixtures), AC 150/5345-28 (PAPI) and AC 150/5345-53 (equipment certification), and AC 150/5340-26 covers maintenance, including inspection, preventive maintenance and troubleshooting.

Which airfield lighting systems do FAA standards cover?

They address five main groups: runway lighting (edge, centerline, threshold and end lights, including HIRL), taxiway lighting (blue edge and green centerline lights), approach lighting systems (ALSF-1, ALSF-2 and MALSR), PAPI and VASI, and airfield signage (L-858).

What photometric requirements do FAA standards set?

Key parameters include intensity (candela output), horizontal and vertical beam spread, colour specifications and uniformity. Fixtures demonstrate performance through laboratory photometric testing as part of equipment certification, while in-service performance is addressed through maintenance guidance such as AC 150/5340-26.

What electrical requirements do FAA standards address?

They address series circuits, typically operating at 6.6 A, the use of isolating transformers, secondary cable specifications and L-823-type connector standards. The system works as an integrated chain from the CCR through the series circuit and isolation transformer to the individual light fitting, with reliability and redundancy as key priorities.

How do FAA and ICAO standards differ?

In general terms, the FAA approach is detailed and prescriptive, with an equipment certification program (AC 150/5345-53) required on US federally funded projects and fixture-specific photometric tables in the AC 150/5345 series. ICAO is performance-based, compliance is set by each State and photometry is defined through isocandela diagrams in Annex 14 Appendix 2. This is a generalised comparison and the differences vary item by item.

Why do FAA standards matter on projects in India?

ICAO is the primary reference on most Indian projects, but FAA standards are often referenced for high-performance lighting systems, imported fittings, premium airport infrastructure and projects involving international consultants. FAA equipment specifications (the L-series) and higher-specification or tender-specific requirements are commonly cited alongside ICAO.
