Pre-Commissioning & Acceptance Inspection

A structured inspection framework for verifying that a newly installed, modified, upgraded, or rehabilitated AGL system is complete, correctly installed, identifiable, test-ready, technically acceptable, documented, and suitable for commissioning/acceptance and eventual handover — spanning runway, taxiway, approach-lighting, PAPI/APAPI, signage, CCR, transformer, cable, earthing, and ALCMS installations, through to final visual acceptance, asset identification, punch-point closure, and handover-condition sign-off.

Initial Issue

Core principle — Installation completion does not automatically mean commissioning readiness. The inspection must establish the full chain from installed to handover-ready.

INSTALLATION COMPLETE INSTALLATION VERIFIED TEST-READY DEFECTS/PUNCH POINTS IDENTIFIED ACCEPTANCE EVIDENCE VERIFIED DOCUMENTATION VERIFIED HANDOVER-READY

Three states should not be confused — "Installed" ≠ "Commissioning Ready" ≠ "Accepted." Installation Complete means physical works are done. Commissioning Ready means the required pre-commissioning inspection has been passed. Accepted/Handover Ready means inspections, tests, documentation, punch-point closure, and acceptance requirements have all been satisfied. That distinction makes this division a project completion and quality-assurance gate, not just another visual checklist.

INS-PCA-2152 Pre-Commissioning Runway Inspection
What's Inspected

Inspection of installed runway lighting fixtures — edge, threshold, runway-end, centreline, touchdown-zone, runway guard, and wing-bar lighting where applicable — against the approved design, including spacing, alignment, elevation, mounting, frangibility, and optical condition.

Inspection Points
  • Edge, threshold, runway-end, centreline, TDZ, guard-light, and wing-bar installation
  • Fixture spacing/location against approved layout/design
  • Fixture alignment/orientation and applicable elevation
  • Mounting hardware and stability
  • Applicable frangible components
  • Lenses/prisms and optical surface condition
Classify As
Acceptable Defective Unserviceable Immediate Action Required

A. Pre-Commissioning Decision Gate

A strong control for Division 23 is passing through ten sequential gates before handover:

01
Installation

Is everything physically installed according to the approved design?

02
Identification

Can every major asset be positively identified and traced?

03
Electrical

Are cables, transformers, connectors, earthing, and electrical interfaces complete?

04
Equipment

Are runway, taxiway, approach, PAPI, signs, CCR, and control equipment correctly installed?

05
Test-Ready

Is the system ready for formal testing and commissioning?

06
Test Evidence

Have required test results and calibration records been verified?

07
Defects

Are critical punch points absent or formally resolved?

08
Documentation

Are as-built drawings, asset records, manuals, and acceptance documents complete?

09
Final Acceptance

Does the completed installation meet the applicable acceptance requirements?

10
Handover

Is the system ready for formal handover/operational use?

B. Standard Inspection Classification

Every observation should be assigned an appropriate status:

Normal / AcceptableEquipment/condition satisfactory and installed as expected.

DefectiveAbnormality or defect identified but does not require immediate withdrawal.

UnserviceableEquipment or system is not available for its intended function.

Immediate Action RequiredCondition presents an operational, electrical, safety, or airside risk requiring immediate escalation/action.

Commissioning / Handover ReadyRequired inspections, tests, punch-point closure, and documentation have been satisfied — or, if not, "Not Ready" pending closure.

C. Minimum Inspection Record

Each pre-commissioning/acceptance inspection record should include:

  • Date and inspection time
  • Inspector name and designation
  • Project/contract reference
  • Asset/subsystem inspected
  • Inspection observation
  • Status classification per parameter
  • Punch-point description, where applicable
  • Punch-point classification (critical/technical/civil/electrical/control/documentation)
  • Corrective-action reference
  • Test/calibration evidence reference
  • Documentation reference
  • Closure date/time and sign-off

D. Three States That Should Not Be Confused

1. Installation CompleteThe physical installation has been completed.

2. Commissioning ReadyThe installation has passed the required pre-commissioning inspection and is ready for commissioning/testing.

3. Accepted / Handover ReadyRequired inspections, tests, documentation, punch-point closure, and acceptance requirements have all been satisfied.

Therefore: "Installed" ≠ "Commissioning Ready" ≠ "Accepted." That distinction makes Division 23 a project completion and quality-assurance gate, rather than simply another visual inspection checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about pre-commissioning & acceptance inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook.

Q1What is Division 23 — Pre-Commissioning & Acceptance Inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook?

Division 23 contains 14 published topics in 18 sections (A–R). It is a structured framework for verifying that a newly installed, modified, upgraded or rehabilitated AGL system is complete, correctly installed, identifiable, test-ready, technically acceptable and documented, and suitable for commissioning, acceptance and eventual handover.

Q2Which systems does the pre-commissioning inspection cover?

It spans runway, taxiway, approach-lighting, PAPI/APAPI, signage, CCR, transformer, cable, earthing and ALCMS installations, through to final visual acceptance, asset identification, punch-point closure and handover-document verification.

Q3What are the three states that should not be confused?

Installation Complete means the physical installation is finished. Commissioning Ready means the installation has passed the required pre-commissioning inspection and is ready for commissioning and testing. Accepted / Handover Ready means all required inspections, tests, documentation, punch-point closure and acceptance requirements are satisfied. Installed is not the same as Commissioning Ready, and neither is the same as Accepted.

Q4What is the pre-commissioning decision gate?

It is a sequence of ten gates before handover: installation, identification, electrical, equipment, test-ready, test evidence, defects, documentation, final acceptance and handover. Each gate asks a question, such as whether everything is installed according to the approved design or whether critical punch points are absent or formally resolved.

Q5What should a pre-commissioning record include?

It includes the project or contract reference, the asset or subsystem inspected, observations, status classification per parameter, punch-point description and classification (critical, technical, civil, electrical, control or documentation), corrective-action reference, test and calibration evidence reference, documentation reference, and closure date and time with sign-off.

Q6What status classifications are used in this division?

This division uses Normal / Acceptable, Defective, Unserviceable and Immediate Action Required, together with a Commissioning / Handover Ready status meaning that required inspections, tests, punch-point closure and documentation have been satisfied, or otherwise Not Ready pending closure.

Q7Do airport procedures take precedence over this handbook?

Yes. This handbook is an uncontrolled educational reference, not an approved procedure. The airport's approved inspection schedule, SMS procedures, ATC instructions, NOTAM procedures and local aerodrome regulations always take precedence, and the current revision of any official document should be verified before use.

Q8Can I download a printable checklist for each inspection topic?

Yes. Each inspection topic has a Download as Word option that produces an uncontrolled Word checklist template (description, a tick-box inspection table and an inspector sign-off block) for personal reference and internal training, to be reviewed and adopted under the user’s own document control.
