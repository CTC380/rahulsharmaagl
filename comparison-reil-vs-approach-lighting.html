Approach & Landing Lighting Comparison

REIL VS APPROACH LIGHTING

Two Visual Aids, Two Very Different Jobs in the Approach — REIL (Runway End Identifier Lights) gives the pilot rapid, high-conspicuity identification of the runway threshold, while a full Approach Lighting System (ALS) extends a structured visual guidance path outward from the threshold into the approach area. Both help a pilot find and align with the runway, but at very different scales and levels of complexity.

02 · What Is REIL?

Runway End Identifier Lights

Despite the name suggesting a "runway end" light, REIL primarily provides a highly conspicuous visual identification of the runway threshold.

For the big picture, read what an approach lighting system is and how it works.

REIL (gold) is a pair of synchronized flashing lights positioned symmetrically at the threshold.

The lights help the pilot quickly recognize: "The runway threshold is here."

03 · What Is Approach Lighting?

Approach Lighting System

An Approach Lighting System extends outward from the runway threshold into the approach area, providing a structured visual reference leading toward the runway.

Approach lighting (cyan) forms a centreline of lights with a crossbar, extending outward from the threshold.

The system helps answer: "Where is the runway, and how am I visually approaching it?"

04 · Main Difference

Identification Point vs Guidance Path

REIL

Identifies one critical location: the threshold. It does not extend a visual path into the approach area.

Approach Lighting

Provides a visual path toward the threshold, extending well before the runway itself.

05 · Location

Where Each System Sits

REIL is located close to the runway threshold, normally positioned symmetrically with respect to the runway centreline. Approach lighting extends before the threshold, providing visual guidance over a significant portion of the final approach area.

REIL

Localized at the threshold — a fixed, compact pair of fixtures.

Approach Lighting

Extends outward into the approach area, ahead of the threshold.

06 · Lighting Pattern

What Each System Is Built From

REIL is usually a pair of synchronized flashing lights. A full approach lighting system may include, depending on the configuration:

REIL

  • Two synchronized flashing lights
  • Positioned at the threshold

Approach Lighting Components

  • Centreline lights
  • Crossbars
  • Barrettes
  • Sequenced flashing lights

The exact configuration depends on the applicable approach lighting system type (e.g. ALSF, MALSR and similar configurations).

07 · Flashing vs Steady-Burning

Light Operation Characteristics

REIL

Its principal visual characteristic is high-conspicuity flashing.

Approach Lighting

May include both steady-burning lights and sequenced flashers, depending on the system.

In short: REIL is primarily a threshold-identification system, whereas approach lighting can provide both continuous visual structure and dynamic approach guidance.

08 · Pilot Perspective

What the Pilot Actually Sees

With REIL

Two flashing lights suddenly stand out, prompting recognition: "Threshold!" REIL answers "Where is the runway threshold?"

With Approach Lighting

A lighted path with a centreline and crossbar leads the eye toward the threshold. Approach lighting answers "Where is the runway, and how am I visually approaching it?"

10 · System Complexity

From Two Lights to a Full Circuit

REIL

A relatively simple visual identification system: a control unit driving a synchronized pair of flashing lights.

Approach Lighting

A complete approach lighting system can contain many fixtures and circuits — centreline lights, crossbars, and sequenced flashers all coordinated from a central control.

11 · REIL vs Larger Approach Systems

Where REIL Fits Against ALSF / MALSR-Type Systems

REIL provides a compact, localized threshold-identification function. Systems such as ALSF, MALSR, and other approach-lighting configurations provide substantially more approach guidance than REIL, extending a lighted path with centreline lights, crossbars and sequenced flashers well ahead of the threshold.

REIL can also be used as a standalone enhancement on runways that do not warrant a full approach lighting system, or alongside one for added threshold conspicuity.

12 · When Each Is Used

Application Scenarios

REIL Is Especially Useful When

  • The runway threshold needs greater conspicuity
  • The runway environment is difficult to distinguish visually
  • A full approach lighting system is not installed
  • Additional threshold identification is required

Approach Lighting Is Preferred When

  • Instrument approaches are conducted
  • Night operations require enhanced visual guidance
  • Reduced visibility operations are expected
  • Precision approach capability requires extensive visual aids

The exact system depends on the applicable aerodrome design standard and operational requirements.

14 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: REIL is short for "runway end lights" and marks the far end of the runway
Fact

REIL stands for Runway End Identifier Lights, but functionally it identifies the threshold — the start of the landing runway — not the far end.

Myth: REIL and approach lighting provide the same level of guidance
Fact

REIL offers rapid, localized threshold identification. Approach lighting provides an extended, structured visual path with far greater alignment and guidance capability.

Myth: A runway either has REIL or approach lighting, never both
Fact

REIL can be installed as a standalone enhancement or alongside a full approach lighting system, depending on the runway's requirements.

Myth: Approach lighting is always flashing
Fact

Approach lighting systems often combine steady-burning lights with sequenced flashers; not every component flashes.

15 · Engineering Verdict

REIL vs Approach Lighting

Engineering Verdict

REIL and Approach Lighting solve different problems at different distances from the runway. REIL answers "where is the threshold, right now" with fast, high-conspicuity flashing. Approach lighting answers "how do I get aligned and stay aligned" over an extended approach path.

Depending on the runway's category and operational requirements, one, the other, or both may be installed together to give the pilot the appropriate level of visual guidance.

REILFast, localized threshold identification via a synchronized flashing pair; lower complexity and maintenance.
Approach LightingExtended, structured visual path with centreline, crossbars and/or sequenced flashers; higher complexity, greater guidance.

REIL → Identify. Approach Lighting → Guide. The applicable system and configuration for a specific runway should always be verified against ICAO Annex 14, FAA standards, or the applicable national standard.

16 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What does REIL stand for?

REIL stands for Runway End Identifier Lights — a pair of synchronized flashing lights that help a pilot quickly identify the runway threshold.

What is the main difference between REIL and an approach lighting system?

REIL provides quick, localized identification of the runway threshold using flashing lights. An approach lighting system provides an extended, structured visual guidance path leading the aircraft toward the runway.

Can a runway have both REIL and approach lighting?

Yes. REIL can be installed as a standalone enhancement or alongside a full approach lighting system, depending on the runway's requirements and applicable standard.

Does approach lighting always flash?

No. Approach lighting systems can include both steady-burning lights and sequenced flashers, depending on the specific system configuration.

Which system provides centreline and crossbar guidance?

Approach lighting systems can include centreline lights and crossbars, depending on the configuration. REIL does not provide this type of guidance.

When is REIL especially useful?

REIL is particularly useful where the runway threshold needs greater conspicuity, the runway environment is visually difficult to distinguish, or a full approach lighting system is not installed.
