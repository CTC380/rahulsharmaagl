How is a taxiway junction lit?

Where one taxiway meets another, three layers overlap — blue edge lights that follow the corner fillets, green centreline lights that carry each route through the turn, and yellow holding-position lights set back from the junction.

The Fact ICAO Annex 14

A taxiway junction is the point where two or more taxiways meet — a T-junction, an angled (Y) junction or a cross. The lighting has to answer three questions at once: what shape is the pavement, which routes can I follow, and where might I be required to hold. Each question is answered by a different system, and the junction is where they overlap. For the full layered picture see Entry 8 · Taxiway Intersection Lighting.

1Edge lights outline the pavement

Blue edge lights follow the full outline of the pavement, including the rounded corner fillets where the branch opens onto the through taxiway. On those curves spacing is closer than 60 m, as on any taxiway curve (see Entry 24 · Taxiway Edge-Light Arrangement). Annex 14 also requires that edge lights at an intersection, exit or curve are shielded as far as practicable, so they cannot be seen from angles where they might be confused with other lights.

2Centreline lights carry each route

Green centreline lights do not stop at the junction. The through route runs straight on, and each branch route curves off tangent to it, so a pilot always has one continuous line to follow for the route they have been cleared to take. Spacing on the branch curves tightens with radius — see Entry 18 · Taxiway Centreline Configuration.

3Holding is added on top

Where ATC needs traffic to wait before a junction, yellow holding-position lighting is added to the base layer — a clearance bar (Entry 12) or intermediate holding-position lights (Entry 13). The matching marking is set across the taxiway at a sufficient distance from the near edge of the intersecting taxiway to keep taxiing aircraft safely apart, so the bar sits before the junction rather than inside it. Where the junction meets a runway, a stop bar can be used instead (it is required at runway holding positions for operations below RVR 350 m), interlocked so that the centreline lights beyond it are switched off while it is lit.

Layer Colour Treatment at a junction
Edge lights Blue Follow the pavement outline and fillets; <60 m on curves; shielded against confusion
Centreline lights Green Through route continuous; branch routes curve off tangent; spacing by radius
Clearance bar / IHP lights Yellow Where ATC requires a hold; set back before the junction
Stop bar Red Where the junction meets a runway; interlocked with centreline lights beyond it
Requirements are drawn from ICAO Annex 14, Volume I, Chapter 5 (lights and markings) — clause numbers shift between editions, so confirm against the edition in force and any State difference filed by your regulator. Junction geometry is a design matter (ICAO Doc 9157, Part 4); FAA practice under AC 150/5340-30 sets fillet edge-light spacing by radius and differs in detail.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is a taxiway junction?

The point where two or more taxiways meet — a T-junction, an angled (Y) junction or a cross. Lighting there has to show the pavement shape, the route each aircraft can follow, and where a hold is required.

Q2How are taxiway edge lights arranged at a junction?

They outline the pavement including the rounded corner fillets, with spacing closer than 60 m on those curves. Annex 14 also says edge lights at an intersection, exit or curve are shielded as far as practicable so they cannot be confused with other lights.

Q3Do taxiway centreline lights continue through a junction?

Yes. Each route has its own continuous green line: the through route runs straight on, and branch routes curve off tangent to it, with spacing tightened according to the curve radius.

Q4How is a hold at a taxiway junction lit?

Where ATC needs traffic to hold, a clearance bar or intermediate holding-position lights are added in yellow. The intermediate holding position marking is set back across the taxiway so there is safe clearance from the near edge of the intersecting taxiway.
