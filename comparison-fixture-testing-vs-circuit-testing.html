FIXTURE TESTING VS CIRCUIT TESTING

Two levels of the same verification job — fixture testing verifies the condition and performance of an individual AGL light fixture, while circuit testing verifies the electrical integrity and performance of the complete series circuit serving multiple fixtures. Fixture testing checks the destination; circuit testing checks the path — and a good result on one never proves the other.

02 · Basic Difference

Fixture Testing

Fixture testing focuses on one lighting unit — its electrical operation, optical output, and physical condition.

Depending on the fixture and applicable requirements, checks may include physical condition, lens and housing condition, connectors, lamp/LED operation, alignment, light intensity, beam spread, colour, photometric performance, waterproofing, and mechanical mounting.

13 · Engineering Verdict

Fixture Testing vs Circuit Testing

Engineering Verdict

Fixture testing is the verification of an individual AGL light's physical, electrical, mechanical, optical, and photometric performance, as applicable.

Circuit testing is the verification of the electrical integrity and functional performance of the complete AGL series circuit, including cables, connectors, isolation transformers, and associated fixtures. Both are required for complete AGL verification: a good fixture does not prove a good circuit, and a good circuit does not prove that every fixture meets its photometric requirements.

Fixture Test"Is the light OK?" → electrical + optical + mechanical.
Circuit Test"Is the series circuit OK?" → cable + connector + transformer + circuit.

The exact tests, methods, and acceptance criteria for either level always depend on the circuit design, fixture type, and the approved test procedure.

14 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fundamental difference between fixture testing and circuit testing?

Fixture testing verifies the condition and performance of one individual AGL light fixture. Circuit testing verifies the electrical integrity and performance of the complete series circuit — cables, isolation transformers, and connectors — serving multiple fixtures.

Can a fixture pass its test while the circuit still has a fault?

Yes. A fixture can be perfectly healthy while the fault sits in the cable, a connector, or an isolation transformer elsewhere on the circuit. This is why both levels of testing are performed.

Does passing a circuit test mean every fixture is performing correctly?

No. Circuit testing normally focuses on electrical integrity — continuity, insulation resistance, and circuit-wide parameters — and is usually not the primary check of photometric performance. A fixture can be electrically part of a healthy circuit yet still fail its photometric requirements.

Does fixture testing include photometric checks?

Where applicable, yes — light intensity, beam spread, colour, and photometric compliance are typically part of fixture testing, alongside physical, electrical, and mechanical checks.

Where do isolation transformers fit into the two test types?

Isolation transformers sit between the series circuit and the fixture. Fixture testing is concerned primarily with the fixture side, while circuit testing includes the transformer as part of the complete circuit and may verify its electrical integrity per the approved procedure.

What's the recommended sequence when a light isn't operating?

A common approach is to check the fixture first. If the fixture is good, move on to checking the circuit — cable, transformer, connector, and CCR. This prevents replacing a good fixture when the fault is actually in the series circuit.

Governing Documents

Equipment specifications governing individual AGL fixture performance, including photometric and mechanical requirements, as applicable.

IEC 60364 / Local Wiring Regulations

Requirements for continuity and insulation resistance testing of AGL series and control circuits.

DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Do not select an acceptance value or test scope from a generic web diagram. The final test criteria should always be developed from the current governing standard, approved design, and the manufacturer-approved installation arrangement.

Fixture testing and circuit testing are not interchangeable checks — one verifies the light itself, the other verifies the path that feeds it. Both are required for complete AGL verification.
