Airfield Lighting Advantages and Disadvantages By System Type

Compare the benefits, limitations and engineering considerations of series circuits, CCRs, isolating transformers, cables, ALCMS, runway, taxiway and approach lighting, and LED versus incandescent sources.

Overview

Why Compare AGL Systems?

Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) is a safety-critical visual aid system that helps pilots identify runways, taxiways, approach paths and other movement-area references. It supports aircraft operations during darkness and, where the appropriate facilities and operational approvals are available, in reduced-visibility conditions.

The performance of an AGL installation depends on correct system design, suitable equipment selection, reliable electrical supplies, effective control and monitoring, regular maintenance, photometric performance and compliance with applicable requirements.

There is no single lighting system or electrical arrangement that is best for every airport. Runway configuration, traffic, operational requirements, visibility conditions, infrastructure, maintainability and lifecycle cost must all be considered during system selection.

1 · Advantages

Main Advantages of Airfield Ground Lighting

1.1 Supports Safe Aircraft Movement

Runway, taxiway and approach lighting provide visual references that help pilots identify movement areas, runway alignment, thresholds, taxi routes and other relevant features. The system must be correctly designed, installed, aligned, tested and maintained to provide its intended visual function.

1.2 Supports Operations in Reduced Visibility

Appropriate lighting facilities can support reduced-visibility operations when they form part of an aerodrome's approved operational arrangements. Installing lighting equipment alone does not establish approval for a particular instrument approach, landing category or low-visibility procedure.

1.3 Provides Controlled Light Intensity

CCR-based series circuits can provide selectable intensity settings according to the regulator design and approved control configuration. The number of steps and control functions depend on the installed equipment.

1.4 Supports Systematic Inspection and Maintenance

Circuit identification, equipment records, monitoring functions and inspection procedures help maintenance teams identify defects and plan corrective work. Photometric measurements can evaluate performance against the applicable criteria.

1.5 Supports Long-Term Asset Management

Well-documented AGL installations provide a foundation for preventive and corrective maintenance, equipment replacement planning, spare-parts management, energy-use assessment, technical performance reviews, upgrades and expansion, and lifecycle cost planning.

1.6 Supports Integrated Airport Lighting Control

Where an ALCMS is installed, centralised control and status visibility can help operators manage lighting circuits and identify reported equipment or communication issues. Capabilities depend on architecture, field interfaces, monitoring coverage and approved operating procedures.

2 · Disadvantages

Disadvantages and Limitations of AGL Systems

AGL systems provide important operational benefits, but they also involve technical, financial and maintenance challenges.

2.1 High Initial Installation Cost

A complete AGL project may require light fittings and accessories, CCRs and distribution equipment, primary cables and connectors, isolating transformers and secondary leads, cable pits, ducts and civil work, signs and control equipment, ALCMS hardware and software, testing, photometry and commissioning, and documentation and training. Total cost depends on airport size, configuration, specifications, construction conditions and project requirements.

2.2 Specialised Maintenance Requirements

Maintenance requires trained personnel, suitable test equipment, approved procedures and coordination with airport operations. Some faults cannot be diagnosed reliably by visual inspection alone; electrical testing, equipment checks, control-system investigation or photometric measurements may be necessary.

2.3 Exposure to Environmental and Mechanical Damage

Field equipment can be affected by moisture, contamination, temperature changes, vibration, pavement loading, lightning-related disturbances and cable damage. Exposure depends on location, installation details, conditions and maintenance practices.

2.4 Fault Diagnosis Can Be Complex

A lighting defect may originate from a fixture, transformer, connector, primary cable, CCR, control interface or monitoring system. Finding the root cause may require checking several interconnected components and reviewing circuit schedules, alarm records, test results and equipment documentation.

2.5 Ongoing Lifecycle Costs

The initial purchase price is not the complete cost. Inspection, maintenance, replacement components, testing, energy consumption, software support and future upgrades all contribute to lifecycle expenditure.

Engineering takeaway: AGL system selection should consider operational requirements, reliability, maintainability and total lifecycle cost, not only initial procurement cost.
3 · Series Circuit

Series Circuit AGL: Advantages and Disadvantages

Series circuits are widely used in airfield lighting installations. A CCR supplies regulated current to the primary circuit, while isolating transformers provide electrical separation for the associated light-fitting loads.

Simplified series-circuit concept

Advantages

  • Provides a regulated current supply for the series circuit.
  • Can serve multiple lighting loads within the designed circuit.
  • Supports selectable intensity settings through compatible CCR and control equipment.
  • Uses isolating transformers to separate the primary series circuit from individual secondary lighting loads.
  • Supports established AGL installation, inspection and maintenance practices.

Disadvantages and limitations

  • A primary circuit interruption can affect multiple fittings on that circuit.
  • CCR or control-equipment problems may affect the associated lighting circuit.
  • Cable insulation, joints and connectors require suitable inspection and maintenance.
  • Fault location may require specialised procedures and test equipment.
  • The design and maintenance process must account for the electrical characteristics of the complete circuit.

Engineering considerations

When assessing a series-circuit arrangement, review circuit loading, CCR ratings, cable requirements, transformer compatibility, insulation requirements, protection, circuit identification and fault-finding procedures.

A fault affecting one fitting should not automatically be assumed to have the same cause as a fault affecting an entire circuit.

4 · CCR

Constant Current Regulator (CCR): Advantages and Disadvantages

A Constant Current Regulator supplies the specified current to an AGL series circuit within its operating limits. CCRs may use different regulation technologies and may provide local or remote control functions.

Advantages

  • Regulates series-circuit current according to the equipment design.
  • Supports the intensity settings provided by the installed system.
  • Can interface with remote control and monitoring equipment where specified.
  • Provides a central point for supplying and managing an associated series circuit.
  • May provide status indications and alarm functions depending on the model.

Disadvantages and limitations

  • Requires suitable electrical supply, installation space and maintenance.
  • Has electrical losses that contribute to operating costs.
  • Requires correct sizing and compatibility with the intended circuit.
  • Can introduce a single equipment-level failure affecting its associated circuit.
  • May depend on external control interfaces and communications for remote operation.
  • Repairs and internal testing may require manufacturer-specific procedures.

Comparing CCR technologies

Ferroresonant and thyristor-based CCR designs should be compared using actual manufacturer data and the project specification. Relevant comparison criteria include:

Rated output and supported circuit loadsRegulation performanceEfficiency and power consumptionAvailable intensity stepsResponse to operating conditionsControl and monitoring interfacesMaintenance requirementsEnvironmental and installation requirementsAvailability of technical support and spare parts
Avoid assuming that one CCR technology is always more efficient or reliable. The result depends on the particular equipment design, operating conditions and system requirements.

5 · Isolating Transformers

AGL Isolating Transformers: Advantages and Disadvantages

AGL isolating transformers provide electrical separation between the primary series circuit and the associated secondary light-fitting circuit.

Advantages

  • Provide primary-to-secondary electrical separation.
  • Allow individual lighting loads to be connected to the series circuit through appropriately rated transformers.
  • Support the standardised architecture used in many series-circuit AGL installations.
  • Allow transformer ratings to be selected for the intended lighting load.

Disadvantages and limitations

  • Add components, connectors and installation points that require inspection.
  • Can develop faults due to insulation deterioration, moisture ingress, mechanical damage or connection problems.
  • Require correct wattage, current rating, frequency and connector compatibility.
  • Need appropriate replacement parts and documented maintenance procedures.

Engineering considerations

Verify the transformer rating, primary and secondary current requirements, connector arrangement, installation environment and applicable product specifications.

The correct transformer must be selected for the approved system and associated load. A physically compatible connector alone does not establish electrical compatibility.

6 · Cables & Connectors

Primary Cables and Connectors: Advantages and Disadvantages

Primary cables and connectors form an essential part of the AGL electrical infrastructure. Their performance affects circuit continuity, insulation integrity and maintainability.

Advantages

  • Provide the electrical path between the CCR and field circuit components.
  • Allow equipment to be interconnected according to the approved circuit design.
  • Support structured circuit identification and maintenance.
  • Enable installation arrangements suited to the airport's infrastructure.

Disadvantages and limitations

  • Cable insulation can deteriorate or become damaged.
  • Poor joints or incorrect terminations can introduce electrical faults.
  • Incompatible connectors may compromise connection integrity.
  • Underground cable faults can require specialised location and testing methods.
  • Replacement work may involve pavement access, pits, ducts or other civil coordination.

Engineering considerations

Cable and connector selection should account for rated voltage, current, insulation requirements, environmental exposure, mechanical conditions, connector compatibility and the approved installation method.

Inspection and testing should follow the applicable project procedures and manufacturer instructions.

7 · ALCMS

ALCMS: Advantages and Disadvantages

An Airfield Lighting Control and Monitoring System provides control and, where configured, monitoring interfaces for airport lighting systems.

Advantages

  • Provides centralised lighting control where supported.
  • Can improve visibility of reported equipment status and alarms.
  • Can support operational coordination and maintenance planning.
  • May provide event records and fault indications.
  • Can reduce the need for certain local operating actions, depending on the system design.

Disadvantages and limitations

  • Adds hardware, software and communication infrastructure.
  • Requires correct configuration, access control and maintenance.
  • Communication failures may affect remote control or status visibility.
  • Incorrect configuration can result in misleading indications or unintended control behaviour.
  • Software, network and interface dependencies require appropriate technical support.
  • The level of monitoring depends on the devices and signals actually connected to the system.

Engineering considerations

Review the control architecture, field interfaces, communication paths, alarm logic, user permissions, backup arrangements and applicable operational procedures.

An ALCMS indication should be interpreted according to the system design. A displayed status is not automatically equivalent to independent confirmation of the physical condition or photometric performance of a light fitting.

8 · Runway, Taxiway & Approach

Runway, Taxiway and Approach Lighting: Advantages and Limitations

Different lighting systems serve different operational purposes. They should not be compared as interchangeable alternatives.

Runway lighting

Advantages

  • Provides visual references for runway identification and alignment.
  • Helps pilots identify runway edges, thresholds and runway ends.
  • Additional systems can provide centreline or touchdown-zone guidance where installed.

Limitations

  • Performance depends on correct configuration, installation, aiming and maintenance.
  • Defects in important lighting elements can affect the intended visual guidance.
  • The required arrangement depends on runway characteristics and the intended operation.
Read more

Taxiway lighting

Advantages

  • Helps identify taxi routes, taxiway edges and centreline paths where provided.
  • Supports ground movement guidance.
  • Can form part of a wider system incorporating signs, stop bars and runway guard lights.

Limitations

  • Complex junctions require careful design and clear visual guidance.
  • Pavement work and vehicle movements can expose fixtures to damage.
  • Lighting alone does not replace applicable taxi procedures, signs, markings or operational controls.
Read more

Approach lighting

Advantages

  • Provides visual references for approach alignment.
  • Can support the intended visual transition during the approach phase.
  • Forms part of the overall visual aid facilities for the applicable runway operation.

Limitations

  • Requires correct layout, installation, aiming and maintenance.
  • The required configuration depends on the runway and applicable operational requirements.
  • Its presence alone does not establish approval for a particular approach or landing category.
Read more

Other AGL systems

Systems such as runway centreline lighting, touchdown zone lighting, runway guard lights and stop bars serve specific functions. Their benefits and limitations should be evaluated against their intended operational purpose and the approved aerodrome design.

10 · How to Compare

How to Compare AGL Systems

A structured comparison helps engineers, consultants, airport authorities and procurement teams make informed decisions. Assess each option using the following criteria:

01

Operational purpose

Confirm the visual guidance function and intended operation.

02

Applicable requirements

Identify the relevant ICAO, FAA, DGCA and project-specific requirements.

03

Electrical compatibility

Check ratings, circuit configuration, CCR compatibility, transformer ratings and connectors.

04

Reliability

Consider equipment performance, failure modes and the consequences of a circuit or component fault.

05

Maintainability

Evaluate access, test methods, spare parts, repair time and technical support.

06

Initial project cost

Include equipment, installation, civil work, control equipment and commissioning.

07

Lifecycle cost

Consider energy use, maintenance, replacement and future upgrades.

08

Monitoring and control

Confirm the required level of remote control, alarms and status visibility.

09

Testing and commissioning

Identify the required electrical, functional and photometric verification.

10

Documentation

Ensure that approved drawings, equipment data, test records and maintenance information are available.

The preferred system is the one that meets the applicable operational and technical requirements while providing suitable reliability, maintainability and lifecycle value.

11 · Misconceptions

Common Misconceptions About AGL Advantages and Disadvantages

Misconception 1: Installing AGL automatically enables low-visibility operations.

Reality

  • The actual capability depends on the installed facilities, their performance and maintenance, the applicable operational arrangements and required approvals.

Misconception 2: An ALCMS eliminates the need for field inspections.

Reality

  • Control and monitoring functions can support maintenance, but they do not automatically replace physical inspections, electrical testing or photometric verification.

Misconception 3: A higher-rated transformer is always a better choice.

Reality

  • Transformer selection must match the approved circuit design and the required electrical and product ratings.

Misconception 4: LED replacement always reduces total project cost.

Reality

  • Energy and maintenance savings must be assessed against initial cost, electrical compatibility, photometric requirements and the full lifecycle.

Misconception 5: One circuit fault always affects the entire airport lighting system.

Reality

  • The impact depends on the fault location, circuit arrangement, equipment configuration and available redundancy. A fault may affect one fitting, a circuit or a wider part of the installation.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What are the main advantages of Airfield Ground Lighting?

AGL provides visual references for aircraft movement and can support operations in darkness and reduced visibility when the required facilities and operational approvals are in place.

Q2What are the main disadvantages of AGL?

The main challenges include installation cost, specialised maintenance, environmental exposure, electrical and control-system faults, testing requirements and long-term lifecycle expenditure.

Q3What are the disadvantages of series-circuit AGL?

A primary cable interruption or CCR problem may affect multiple fittings on the associated circuit. Diagnosis may require specialised equipment and approved fault-finding procedures.

Q4What are the advantages of a CCR?

A CCR provides regulated current for its associated series circuit and can support selectable intensity settings. Additional control and monitoring functions depend on the equipment and system configuration.

Q5Is an ALCMS always necessary?

Not necessarily. The required control and monitoring arrangement depends on the airport design, operational needs and applicable requirements.

Q6Are LED AGL lights always better than incandescent lights?

Not in every situation. LED products may offer energy and maintenance advantages, but selection must also consider photometric performance, circuit compatibility, initial cost and lifecycle requirements.

Q7Does AGL lighting alone guarantee safe airport operations?

No. AGL is one part of the airport's visual aid and operational safety arrangements. Correct design, installation, maintenance, operating procedures and required approvals remain essential.

Q8How can AGL lifecycle cost be reduced?

Lifecycle cost can be managed through appropriate equipment selection, preventive maintenance, accurate asset records, effective fault diagnosis, suitable spare-parts planning and technically justified upgrades.

Q9What are the airfield ground lighting advantages and airfield lighting disadvantages?

Airfield ground lighting advantages include consistent brightness, proven reliability, standardised equipment and good visual guidance. Airfield lighting disadvantages include cost, maintenance needs, and sensitivity to circuit faults. The balance depends on the application.

Q10What are the airport lighting advantages and airport lighting disadvantages?

Airport lighting advantages include safe operations in low visibility and at night. Airport lighting disadvantages include installation and maintenance cost and energy use. Good design and maintenance reduce the disadvantages.

Q11What are runway lighting advantages and runway lighting disadvantages?

Runway lighting advantages include clear guidance and standardised colours. Runway lighting disadvantages include exposure to damage and the need for regular inspection and maintenance.

Q12What are taxiway lighting advantages and taxiway lighting disadvantages?

Taxiway lighting advantages include clear guidance and reduced risk of taxiing errors. Taxiway lighting disadvantages include the large number of fixtures to maintain and exposure to vehicle damage.

Q13What are approach lighting disadvantages?

Approach lighting disadvantages include large structures, exposure to weather and lightning, and the cost and effort of maintenance and alignment.

Q14What are AGL series circuit advantages and AGL series circuit disadvantages?

AGL series circuit advantages include the same current to every light and therefore consistent brightness. AGL series circuit disadvantages include the impact of any open circuit on the whole loop.

Q15What are constant current regulator advantages and constant current regulator disadvantages?

Constant current regulator advantages include stable current regardless of load changes and brightness control. Constant current regulator disadvantages include cost, size and the need for maintenance.

Q16What are airfield lighting isolation transformer advantages and airfield lighting isolation transformer disadvantages?

Airfield lighting isolation transformer advantages include keeping the circuit running when a lamp fails and electrical isolation. Airfield lighting isolation transformer disadvantages include additional cost, losses and a further item that can fail.

Q17What are airfield lighting cable advantages and airfield lighting cable disadvantages?

Airfield lighting cable advantages include durability and suitability for direct burial. Airfield lighting cable disadvantages include costly repairs, difficulty locating faults and vulnerability to excavation damage.

Q18What are airfield lighting control system advantages, airfield lighting control system disadvantages and ALCMS disadvantages?

Airfield lighting control system advantages include remote control, monitoring and fault indication. Airfield lighting control system disadvantages include complexity, cost and dependence on software and communications. ALCMS disadvantages include cybersecurity and maintenance of software.

Q19What are runway edge lights advantages and runway edge lights disadvantages?

Runway edge lights advantages include simple, standard, visible runway boundaries. Runway edge lights disadvantages include exposure to damage and many fixtures to maintain.

Q20What are runway centreline lights advantages and runway centreline lights disadvantages?

Runway centreline lights advantages include precise guidance in low visibility. Runway centreline lights disadvantages include inset installation cost and damage from wheel loads.

Q21What are threshold lights advantages and threshold lights disadvantages?

Threshold lights advantages include clear indication of the runway start. Threshold lights disadvantages include exposure to damage and the need for careful alignment.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Compare Systems Against the Same Operational Need
Selection

An advantage on one airfield is a limitation on another. Define traffic, visibility category, maintenance capability and budget first, then weigh each option against that need rather than a generic ranking.

The Series Circuit Is Efficient but Couples Everything Together
Architecture

One current feeding every fixture gives uniform brightness and simple regulation, but it also means a single open circuit, cable fault or poor connection can affect the entire loop.

Lifecycle Cost Rarely Matches Purchase Price
Cost

Cheaper equipment can cost more through frequent access, failures and non-standard spares. Compare maintenance hours, spares range and expected service life, not the quotation alone.

Maintainability Is a Design Property
Maintenance

Ask how long a typical fault takes to find and fix on each option. Features such as monitoring, accessible bases and common spares often decide long-term reliability more than headline specifications.

More Automation Adds Dependencies as Well as Capability
Control

A control or monitoring layer improves visibility and response, but it adds software, networks and support needs. Confirm there is a safe manual fallback and a team able to maintain the system.

Beware Blanket Claims in Either Direction
Judgement

Statements that a technology is always better or always problematic usually ignore the installation conditions. Look for how the claim was tested, under what load and in which climate.
