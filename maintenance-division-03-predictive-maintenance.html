Predictive Maintenance

The data-driven layer of the handbook. Predictive maintenance uses historical measurements, condition indicators, operating trends and failure patterns to identify AGL asset deterioration before it reaches an unacceptable condition or causes failure — moving the framework from reactive repair to forward-looking risk management.

Philosophy: prediction is not simply collecting more data — it is identifying a change in condition over time. A single reading may look acceptable in isolation; the trend across successive readings is what reveals developing risk. This division completes the progression Preventive → Corrective → Predictive, feeding trend intelligence upward into the airport-wide condition audit.

Why Trend Beats a Single Reading

The core predictive-maintenance principle, illustrated with insulation resistance — the same logic applies to CCR current stability, transformer condition, LED degradation, connector failures and fixture failures.

DateAsset / CircuitInsulation Resistance
JanCircuit A520 MΩ
AprCircuit A460 MΩ
JulCircuit A390 MΩ
OctCircuit A280 MΩ
Trend is more informative than a single measurement.

Each individual reading may still appear usable in isolation. Read as a sequence, the same four values show a progressive, continuous decline — the signal predictive maintenance exists to catch before the circuit fails outright.

Evaluate every trended indicator for: absolute value, rate of change, seasonal influence, sudden deterioration, repeated deterioration, and comparison against historical performance.

Predictive Data Sources & Quality

Predictive analysis draws on evidence already generated by Divisions 01 and 02, plus dedicated test and condition records — and is only as reliable as the data feeding it.

Data Sources

  • Inspection & preventive-maintenance records
  • Corrective-maintenance records
  • Electrical test & photometric reports
  • CCR logs, ALCMS event logs, alarm history
  • Component-replacement & failure records
  • Energy, asset-register, calibration & environmental records

Data-Quality Requirements

  • Correct asset ID and circuit identification
  • Consistent measurement method, instrument and units
  • Valid instrument calibration
  • Correct date / time on every reading
  • Accurate maintenance history and failure coding
  • Consistent defect classification across every asset

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about predictive maintenance, based on the topics in this division.

9 QUESTIONS
Q1What is predictive maintenance in AGL?

Predictive maintenance uses historical measurements, condition indicators, operating trends and failure patterns to identify asset deterioration before it reaches an unacceptable condition or causes failure. It moves the maintenance framework from reactive repair to forward-looking risk management.

Q2What does Division 03 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 03 (Predictive Maintenance) contains 25 topics in four groups: Group A Electrical Trending (7 topics), Group B Equipment Performance (6 topics), Group C Reliability Data (7 topics) and Group D Predictive Outputs (5 topics).

Q3Why is a trend more useful than a single reading?

A single reading can look acceptable in isolation while the sequence reveals developing risk. The page's example is insulation resistance on Circuit A: 520 MΩ in January, 460 in April, 390 in July and 280 in October. Each value may look usable alone, but together they show a progressive decline that predictive maintenance is meant to catch before the circuit fails.

Q4What should be evaluated for every trended indicator?

Absolute value, rate of change, seasonal influence, sudden deterioration, repeated deterioration, and comparison against historical performance.

Q5What is the predictive-maintenance sequence?

Measure, Trend, Compare, Detect Deterioration, Assess Risk, Predict, Act and Verify.

Q6What data sources feed predictive maintenance?

Inspection and preventive-maintenance records, corrective-maintenance records, electrical test and photometric reports, CCR logs, ALCMS event logs and alarm history, component-replacement and failure records, and energy, asset-register, calibration and environmental records.

Q7What data-quality requirements apply?

Predictive analysis is only as reliable as its data. It requires correct asset ID and circuit identification, a consistent measurement method, instrument and units, valid instrument calibration, correct date and time on every reading, accurate maintenance history and failure coding, and consistent defect classification across every asset.

Q8What is the predictive-maintenance workflow?

Every predictive output follows an eleven-step pipeline: data collection, data validation, historical comparison, trend analysis, deterioration detection, failure-risk assessment, early warning, maintenance recommendation, planned intervention, post-intervention verification, and trend updated.

Q9How does predictive maintenance relate to Divisions 01, 02 and 25?

Division 01 (preventive) asks what to maintain routinely, Division 02 (corrective) asks what must be repaired because a defect occurred, and Division 03 (predictive) asks what the data says is deteriorating and likely to fail next. Division 25, the airport-wide condition assessment and audit, uses Division 03's trend intelligence as an upstream input.
