AGL Inspection Routine, Runway/Taxiway & Post-Maintenance SOPs

Controlled procedures for routine airfield ground lighting inspection — daily, runway, taxiway, approach lighting, PAPI, signage and stop bar checks, runway guard light, fixture, cable route, transformer pit, base can and connector inspection, CCR/control room and earthing inspection, FOD and water ingress inspection, and post-maintenance verification prior to return to service. Applicable SOP range: SOP-321 to SOP-340.

SOP-321 Daily AGL Inspection
Purpose

To establish a systematic daily inspection of AGL assets to identify defects that could affect aerodrome safety, operational availability or maintenance condition.

Inspection Coverage
  • Runway lighting.
  • Taxiway lighting.
  • Approach lighting.
  • PAPI.
  • Airfield signs.
  • Stop bars.
  • Runway guard lights.
  • Fixtures.
  • Base cans.
  • Visible cable/connector areas.
  • Transformer pits.
  • CCR/control equipment.
  • FOD.
  • Water accumulation.
  • Physical damage.
Procedure
  1. Obtain inspection authorization.
  2. Coordinate with Airport Operations/ATC as required.
  3. Conduct airside safety briefing.
  4. Verify inspection vehicle and communication equipment.
  5. Inspect designated routes.
  6. Check lighting condition.
  7. Identify unserviceable/misaligned/damaged fixtures.
  8. Check signs and visual aids.
  9. Check FOD.
  10. Check standing water.
  11. Check visible civil defects.
  12. Record abnormalities.
  13. Immediately report safety-critical defects.
  14. Initiate corrective action according to priority.
  15. Complete inspection report.
SOP-322 Runway Inspection
Purpose

To inspect runway AGL systems and associated areas for defects affecting runway operational safety.

Inspect
  • Runway edge lights.
  • Threshold lights.
  • Runway end lights.
  • Centreline lights where installed.
  • TDZ lights where installed.
  • Threshold wing bars.
  • Fixture alignment.
  • Fixture height.
  • Damage.
  • Missing fixtures.
  • Foreign objects.
  • Pavement condition around fixtures.
  • Water accumulation.
Procedure
  1. Obtain runway inspection clearance.
  2. Coordinate with ATC.
  3. Enter runway only when authorized.
  4. Follow approved inspection route.
  5. Inspect fixtures systematically.
  6. Identify defective/misaligned lights.
  7. Assess serviceability against the aerodrome's approved criteria (e.g. proportion of lights lit, no adjacent lights out) and inform ATC/Operations if below the required level.
  8. Check physical damage.
  9. Check FOD.
  10. Record location using approved identification.
  11. Exit runway when instructed.
  12. Report defects.

Master Inspection Classification

Every inspection finding should be categorized consistently. The exact classification criteria should be defined by the airport's approved maintenance system.

  • Critical — Potential immediate operational/safety impact. Action: immediate escalation/action.
  • Major — Significant equipment or reliability issue. Action: priority corrective maintenance.
  • Minor — Does not immediately affect operation. Action: planned maintenance.
  • Observation — Condition requiring monitoring. Action: track/trend.

Post-Maintenance Final Release Sequence

Equipment shall only be returned to normal operational service after this sequence is complete (see SOP-340):

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about AGL Inspection SOPs in the AGL SOP Library.

Q1What is Division 16 — AGL Inspection SOPs?

Division 16 is a set of 20 procedures (SOP-321 to SOP-340, Rev 0) for routine airfield ground lighting inspection. It covers daily, runway, taxiway, approach, PAPI, signage and stop bar checks, runway guard light, fixture, cable route, transformer pit, base can and connector inspection, CCR and control room inspection, earthing, FOD and water ingress inspection, and post-maintenance verification before return to service.

Q2Who sets inspection frequency and acceptance criteria?

The approved airport maintenance program, applicable aerodrome standards, equipment manufacturer requirements and local regulatory requirements. An inspection finding must not be treated as acceptable merely because the light remains operational.

Q3What does a daily AGL inspection cover?

SOP-321 covers runway, taxiway and approach lighting, PAPI, airfield signs, stop bars, runway guard lights, fixtures, base cans, visible cable and connector areas, transformer pits, CCR and control equipment, FOD, water accumulation and physical damage. It starts with inspection authorization, coordination with Airport Operations/ATC as required, and an airside safety briefing.

Q4How are inspection findings classified?

Critical (potential immediate operational or safety impact, needing immediate escalation), Major (significant equipment or reliability issue, priority corrective maintenance), Minor (no immediate effect on operation, planned maintenance) and Observation (a condition to monitor, tracked and trended). The exact criteria come from the airport's approved maintenance system.

Q5What is checked when inspecting a fixture's condition?

Lens or prism, housing, gasket, fasteners, mounting, frangible coupling, cable connection, corrosion, cracks, contamination, water ingress and alignment (SOP-329). Findings are classified as Critical, Major, Minor or Observation per the airport's defect classification.

Q6How is FOD handled during inspection?

SOP-338 inspects runways, taxiways, shoulders, AGL work areas, areas around fixtures, base cans, transformer pits, maintenance areas and vehicle routes, before and after work. FOD is removed when safe and authorized, significant FOD is reported, the source is checked where possible, and recurring FOD issues are recorded.

Q7What does post-maintenance inspection verify?

SOP-340 verifies that equipment and the work area are safe, complete and operational. Tools, materials, waste and FOD are cleared, covers installed, excavations closed and temporary protection removed. Electrical connections and earthing are verified, insulation and continuity testing is done where applicable, LOTO is removed through the approved process, and equipment is energized and checked.
