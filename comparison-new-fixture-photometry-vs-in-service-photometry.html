AGL Photometry Comparison

NEW FIXTURE PHOTOMETRY VS IN-SERVICE PHOTOMETRY

Baseline vs Actual Performance — new fixture photometry evaluates an AGL light when it is new and in its specified test condition, establishing the baseline/design performance. In-service photometry evaluates the photometric performance of a fixture that has been installed and operated over time, revealing its actual performance after operational exposure.

02 · New Fixture Photometry

Establishing the Baseline

New Fixture Photometry

New fixture photometry establishes the initial optical performance of the AGL fixture. Typical characteristics assessed may include luminous intensity, peak intensity, beam distribution, horizontal coverage, vertical coverage, beam spread, and colour characteristics where applicable.

It essentially answers: "Does this new fixture provide the required photometric performance?"

05 · Effect of Ageing

How Output Can Change Over Time

A new fixture may initially provide a specified peak intensity. After a period of operation, factors such as ageing may affect its output. The actual amount and cause of any change must be determined by measurement and should not simply be assumed.

06 · Typical In-Service Influences

What Can Affect a Fixture in Service

An AGL fixture in service may experience a number of factors that can potentially affect its actual photometric performance:

  • Dirt accumulation
  • Lens contamination
  • Optical ageing
  • LED ageing
  • Lamp degradation
  • Mechanical wear
  • Vibration
  • Environmental exposure

08 · Worked Example

Comparing Measured Intensity

Suppose a new AGL fixture has a measured centre-beam intensity of 10,000 cd. After a period in service, this is measured at 8,000 cd.

10,000 − 8,000 = 2,000 cd

Difference between the new and in-service measured intensity

2,000 / 10,000 × 100 = 20%

Percentage change from the new measured value

Therefore, the measured intensity has decreased by 20% relative to that baseline. Whether the fixture remains acceptable depends on the applicable specification, maintenance criteria, and measurement method.

09 · Optical Condition

Clean Baseline vs Actual Condition

New Fixture

Testing generally seeks to characterize the fixture under its defined test condition — clean, unweathered, as-manufactured.

In-Service Fixture

Its measurement can reflect its actual operational condition — dust, wear — unless the test procedure requires cleaning, removal, or other preparation before testing.

The test condition must always be defined before comparing new and in-service photometric results.

10 · Installation Effects

Fixture-Only Testing vs As-Installed Testing

New Fixture

New fixture photometry often focuses on the fixture itself, tested independently of its final installation.

In-Service Fixture

In-service photometry may involve the fixture as installed, so results may potentially include effects from alignment, mounting condition, installation geometry, electrical operating condition, and actual optical condition, depending on the measurement method.

13 · Important Testing Principle

Comparable Conditions Are Essential

A meaningful comparison requires comparable conditions. Otherwise, a difference in results may be caused by the test setup rather than actual fixture degradation.

1

Same measurement geometry? Confirm the test setup matches between new and in-service measurements.

2

Same operating condition? Confirm the fixture is powered and operated equivalently.

3

Same measurement method? Confirm the instrument and procedure are consistent.

4

Comparable results. Only then can a difference be reasonably attributed to the fixture itself.

16 · Engineering Verdict

New Fixture vs In-Service Photometry

Engineering Verdict

New fixture photometry establishes the initial/baseline optical performance. In-service photometry measures the current performance after operational use.

For AGL maintenance and lifecycle assessment, new-fixture photometry provides the reference performance, while in-service photometry reveals the fixture's actual optical performance after exposure to real operating conditions.

17 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the simplest way to remember the difference?

New fixture photometry → BASELINE (how good the fixture started out). In-service photometry → CURRENT (how good the fixture is performing now).

Does a lower in-service reading always mean the fixture has failed?

Not necessarily. Whether a measured difference is acceptable depends on the applicable specification, maintenance criteria, and measurement method — the amount and cause of any change should be determined by measurement, not assumed.

Should an in-service fixture be cleaned before testing?

That depends on the defined test procedure. The test condition must always be defined before comparing new and in-service photometric results, since an uncleaned fixture reflects actual operational condition while a cleaned one may not.

Can in-service results be affected by something other than the fixture itself?

Yes. As-installed measurements may include effects from alignment, mounting condition, installation geometry, and electrical operating condition, not just the optical condition of the fixture.

Is new-vs-in-service the same comparison as laboratory-vs-field photometry?

No. Laboratory vs field describes where/how a measurement is performed. New vs in-service describes the condition and service history of the fixture being measured — the two dimensions can be combined in different ways.

What causes in-service photometric performance to change?

Typical influences include dirt accumulation, lens contamination, optical ageing, LED ageing, lamp degradation, mechanical wear, vibration, and environmental exposure.

18 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

FAA AC 150/5345-46

Specification for Runway, Taxiway, Heliport, and Vertiport Light Fixtures — the actual fixture equipment specification, including the isocandela/photometric performance requirements a new fixture must meet at type approval.

FAA AC 150/5340-30

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids — photometric requirements relevant to interpreting new and in-service AGL performance.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — photometric characteristics of aeronautical ground lights, including maintenance-related performance considerations.

CIE Photometry Standards

International Commission on Illumination definitions and measurement methods for luminous intensity and related photometric quantities.

Final Engineering Note

Do not compare new and in-service photometric results without first confirming the test condition, geometry, and method are comparable between the two measurements.

New fixture photometry is not in-service photometry — one describes the design baseline, the other describes what the fixture actually delivers in the field.
