Conventional AGL vs IoT-Enabled AGL

CONVENTIONAL AGL
VS
IOT-ENABLED AGL

Conventional AGL is primarily based on dedicated control, monitoring and maintenance systems, whereas IoT-enabled AGL adds connected sensors, networked devices, data analytics and remote monitoring to make the airfield lighting system more observable and condition-aware. IoT should be treated as an additional intelligence and connectivity layer around the AGL system — not as a substitute for its fundamental safety, electrical, photometric, mechanical and operational requirements.

06 · Important Distinction

IoT Does Not Automatically Mean "Smart Fixture"

An LED fixture can exist at several different levels of connectivity:

  • LED but not connected
  • LED + local diagnostics
  • LED + digital monitoring
  • LED + network connectivity
  • LED + IoT platform + analytics

LED ≠ IoT. Similarly, remote monitoring ≠ necessarily IoT. IoT generally involves connected devices that collect/exchange data and can be managed or analyzed through a broader digital infrastructure.

12 · Example — Series Circuit

Granular Fault Localization

A conventional system may monitor only the CCR output. An IoT-enabled system can potentially collect information from multiple points along the circuit.

CCR
 ↓
SERIES CIRCUIT
 ↓
ISOLATION TRANSFORMERS
 ↓
FIXTURES
 ↓
SENSORS / SMART DEVICES

Instead of only knowing "circuit current abnormal," the system may help identify "abnormal condition associated with fixture/transformer location X."

The actual ability to localize a fault depends entirely on the sensor architecture in place. A conventional AGL with an ALCMS and individual lamp monitoring (common on CAT II/III circuits) can already report lamp-level status; the added value of IoT is broader data such as temperature, trends and asset history, plus analytics.

13 · IoT + A-SMGCS

A Common Data Environment

                 A-SMGCS
                    ↕
           AGL CONTROL SYSTEM  (safety-critical control path)
                    ↕
   ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
   ↓                ↓                ↓
  CCRs           FIXTURES          SIGNS
   │                │                │
   └── status / sensor data (monitoring copy, one-way) ──┘
                    ↓
             EDGE / GATEWAY
                    ↓
     IoT PLATFORM (monitoring + analytics)
                    ↓
    DASHBOARDS / MAINTENANCE SYSTEMS

The IoT platform can then provide a common data environment for AGL status, equipment health, alarms and maintenance information. The A-SMGCS link (for functions such as stop-bar or route-guidance control) stays on the AGL control system; the IoT platform normally receives a monitoring copy of status data through a controlled, preferably one-way, interface.

The IoT platform should not sit in the control path between the AGL control system and the CCRs, fixtures or signs, and should not be assumed to replace the safety-critical AGL control architecture.

21 · Advantages & Limitations

Where Each Approach Wins and Falls Short

Conventional AGL — Advantages

Simpler architecture; lower digital complexity; easier to understand; lower cybersecurity exposure; established maintenance practices; lower initial technology investment.

Limitations

Less equipment visibility; more manual inspection; limited historical data; less granular diagnostics; limited condition-based maintenance capability.

IoT-Enabled AGL — Advantages

Continuous monitoring; rich equipment data; remote diagnostics; better asset visibility; supports CBM; potential predictive maintenance; better historical records; easier large-scale asset management.

Limitations

Higher initial complexity; more devices to maintain; network dependency; cybersecurity requirements; data-management requirements; more complicated FAT/SAT; requires skilled personnel.

24 · Engineering Verdict

Conventional AGL vs IoT-Enabled AGL

Engineering Verdict

Conventional AGL primarily controls and monitors lighting equipment — simpler, with lower cybersecurity exposure, but limited equipment visibility and less granular diagnostics.

IoT-enabled AGL adds connected sensing, digital communication, data analytics and asset intelligence to enable continuous visibility and condition-based maintenance — at the cost of higher initial complexity, network dependency, and a critical cybersecurity burden. The most important point: IoT should be treated as an additional intelligence and connectivity layer around the AGL system, not as a substitute for its fundamental safety, electrical, photometric, mechanical and operational requirements.

ConventionalAnswers "Is the lighting system operating?" — proven, simple, lower exposure.
IoT-EnabledAnswers "What condition is it in, and what will it need?" — richer, but demands network and security engineering.

A monitoring failure should never be allowed to cause loss of required AGL lighting — the operational lighting function and the monitoring/data function must remain clearly separated in the design.

25 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fundamental difference between conventional and IoT-enabled AGL?

Conventional AGL is based on dedicated control, monitoring and maintenance systems that mainly answer whether the lighting is operating. IoT-enabled AGL adds connected sensors, networked devices and data analytics to provide continuous visibility into equipment condition.

Does using LED fixtures automatically make an AGL system IoT-enabled?

No. LED ≠ IoT. An LED fixture can range from unconnected to fully integrated with an IoT platform and analytics — the light source technology and the connectivity level are independent choices.

Does IoT monitoring mean the system can also control the AGL remotely?

Not necessarily. IoT monitoring does not necessarily mean IoT control. For safety-critical airport lighting, automatic commands must be carefully segregated, authorized and validated separately from monitoring.

Can IoT-enabled AGL replace scheduled inspections and physical testing?

No. IoT data should support, not replace, required scheduled inspections, photometry, alignment checks and mandatory electrical tests.

What happens to the AGL lighting function if the IoT/monitoring layer fails?

A well-designed system separates the operational lighting function from the monitoring/data function, so a monitoring failure should not cause loss of required AGL lighting. The exact behavior depends on the system's safety and control philosophy.

What is the biggest new risk introduced by IoT-enabled AGL?

Cybersecurity. Every additional connected device, gateway, network and platform introduces additional considerations around authentication, access control, segmentation, encryption, firmware management and incident response.

26 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I — Chapters 8 & 10

Aerodrome Design and Operations: electrical systems for visual aids (power supply, system design and monitoring of lighting systems, particularly for low-visibility operations) and maintenance, including lighting serviceability levels. Verify exact provisions in the current edition.

ICAO Aerodrome Design Manual, Doc 9157 Part 5

Electrical Systems: guidance on electrical practices and installations for aerodromes. Guidance material, not a Standard.

ICAO A-SMGCS Manual (Doc 9830)

Guidance on advanced surface movement guidance and control systems, relevant where the AGL control system interfaces with A-SMGCS.

FAA AC 150/5345-56

Specification for L-890 Airport Lighting Control and Monitoring System (ALCMS): minimum requirements for control and monitoring of airport lighting.

FAA AC 150/5345-10

Specification for Constant Current Regulators and Regulator Monitors: defines CCR types (L-828 regulator without monitoring, L-829 regulator with monitoring, L-827 monitor), classes (6.6 A and 20 A) and 3- or 5-step brightness styles.

FAA AC 150/5340-26

Maintenance of Airport Visual Aid Facilities: recommended maintenance practice for lighting circuits, CCRs and ALCMS.

IEC 61820 series & IEC 61821

Electrical installations for AGL at aerodromes (including requirements for series circuits), and maintenance of AGL constant current series circuits.

IEC 62443 / Applicable Cybersecurity Frameworks

Security of industrial automation and control systems: relevant wherever gateways, networks or platforms are added around AGL control and monitoring.

DGCA / AAI Requirements (India)

For Indian airport projects, verify applicable DGCA and AAI requirements, applicable national cybersecurity requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Do not assume an IoT platform can substitute for the safety-critical AGL control architecture. The monitoring/data layer and the operational lighting function must be clearly and deliberately separated in the design.

Conventional and IoT-enabled AGL are not competing philosophies — IoT is a connectivity and intelligence layer added around the same fundamental safety-critical lighting system.
