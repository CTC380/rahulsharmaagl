AGL-CS-004 — Short Circuit in an AGL Series Circuit

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A systematic investigation into a short-circuit fault causing CCR protection operation — from initial CCR analysis through cable-joint inspection and root-cause determination.

Case ID

AGL-CS-004

Category

Series Circuit & Fault Finding

Level

Intermediate to Advanced

Case Type

Engineering Simulation

Primary Skill

Short-circuit diagnosis & CCR protection analysis

Important: This is an engineering simulation created for training. It is not presented as an actual airport incident. All readings, values, dates and outcomes are illustrative, and no airport, organisation, person or manufacturer is identified or implied.

1Case Study Overview

During routine night operation, a runway edge lighting circuit suddenly became unavailable. The Constant Current Regulator (CCR) indicated a circuit fault shortly after energization, and the affected circuit could not maintain normal operation.

Unlike an open circuit, where the CCR output voltage typically rises while attempting to maintain current, this event was characterized by abnormally low circuit impedance, unstable CCR operation, and repeated protection responses.

The maintenance team initially suspected a CCR malfunction. However, systematic field investigation identified a short circuit between the primary series cable conductors inside a flooded cable joint pit.

This case demonstrates the importance of distinguishing short-circuit faults from open-circuit faults in AGL systems.

2Learning Objectives

After completing this case study, the learner should be able to:

  • Understand the effect of a short circuit in a series lighting circuit.
  • Differentiate between open-circuit and short-circuit behavior.
  • Interpret CCR voltage and protection indications.
  • Apply systematic fault localization techniques.
  • Understand cable-joint failure mechanisms.
  • Perform post-repair electrical verification.
  • Analyze root causes related to moisture and insulation failure.

3System Background

An AGL series circuit operates with constant current supplied by the CCR.

A simplified arrangement is:

CCR → Primary Cable → Isolating Transformers → Light Fixtures → Return

The CCR automatically adjusts its output voltage to maintain the selected current.

A short circuit reduces the effective circuit impedance, potentially causing:

  • Increased fault current tendency.
  • Reduced circuit voltage.
  • CCR protection operation.
  • Circuit instability.

4Problem Statement

The reported condition was:

  • Runway edge lighting circuit unavailable.
  • CCR fault indication active.
  • Circuit trips during energization.
  • No visible fixture damage.
  • Recent heavy rainfall.
Engineering Question

Is the fault located in the CCR, the field cable, a cable joint, an isolating transformer, or a field connector?

7Open Circuit vs Short Circuit

A useful diagnostic comparison is:

Open circuit Voltage rises while current cannot be maintained
Short circuit Voltage falls and protection may operate
High resistance Elevated voltage with reduced performance
Normal circuit Stable voltage and current

This comparison guided the investigation toward a low-impedance fault.

8Test Equipment

The investigation used:

  • True-RMS multimeter
  • Insulation resistance tester
  • Cable continuity tester
  • Approved AGL circuit test equipment
  • Cable fault localization equipment
  • Circuit drawings
  • Airport-approved PPE

All testing was performed with the circuit isolated according to approved safety procedures.

9Investigation

Step 1: Verify CCR Operation

The CCR was tested using approved procedures.

Selected current 6.6 A
Output voltage Abnormally low
Protection status Active
Internal diagnostics Normal

The CCR itself was healthy. The fault was therefore investigated in the field circuit.

Step 2: Circuit Isolation

The field circuit was disconnected from the CCR. Insulation resistance testing showed a significant reduction compared with normal maintenance values. This confirmed that the fault was external to the CCR.

Step 3: Sectional Testing

The circuit was divided into sections using approved isolation points. Testing showed that the fault was confined to one cable section.

The affected section included:

  • Two cable joints.
  • Three isolating transformers.
  • One underground junction pit.

Step 4: Cable Joint Inspection

The junction pit was opened. Inspection revealed:

  • Standing water.
  • Damaged heat-shrink protection.
  • Deteriorated insulation.
  • Carbon tracking.
  • Conductor-to-conductor contact.

Electrical testing confirmed a short circuit within the cable joint assembly.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

A water-damaged cable joint developed insulation failure, allowing electrical contact between the primary conductors.

The resulting short circuit reduced the circuit impedance and caused CCR protection operation. The failure was associated with moisture ingress and progressive insulation deterioration.

11Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Isolated the circuit.
  2. Removed the damaged cable joint.
  3. Cut back affected cable insulation.
  4. Installed an approved cable joint kit.
  5. Performed insulation resistance testing.
  6. Verified cable continuity.
  7. Inspected adjacent joints.
  8. Improved pit drainage.
  9. Restored the circuit.
  10. Verified normal CCR operation.

14Why This Case Was Important

A common mistake is to treat all CCR fault indications as CCR failures.

In this case:

  • The CCR was responding correctly.
  • The protection system prevented equipment damage.
  • The actual failure was in the field cable system.

Understanding CCR response characteristics is essential for efficient troubleshooting.

15Engineering Lessons

01

Short vs open circuits differ.

Short circuits and open circuits produce different CCR voltage behavior.

02

Cable joints are critical.

Cable joints are critical reliability points in underground AGL systems.

03

Water ingress degrades insulation.

Water ingress can progressively reduce insulation resistance before a complete fault occurs.

04

Sectional testing saves time.

Sectional testing significantly reduces fault localization time.

05

Drainage matters.

Drainage deficiencies can become electrical reliability problems.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: CCR trips immediately after circuit energization. Output voltage is unusually low. No visible fixture damage. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace the CCR
B. Replace all field lights
C. Investigate the field circuit for a low-impedance or short-circuit fault
D. Increase CCR current
Correct answer: C. Low output voltage and protection operation suggest a short circuit in the field. The CCR itself is likely responding correctly.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about short circuits and CCR protection operation, based on this case study.

Q1What is AGL-CS-004?

AGL-CS-004 is an engineering training case study on a short circuit in an AGL series circuit that caused CCR protection operation on a runway edge lighting circuit. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2How does a short circuit differ from an open circuit at the CCR?

In an open circuit the CCR output voltage typically rises while it tries to maintain current. In this case the circuit had abnormally low impedance, unstable CCR operation and repeated protection responses, which is the signature of a short circuit.

Q3Why shouldn't you assume the CCR is faulty when it trips?

The team initially suspected a CCR malfunction, but low output voltage with protection operation suggests a short circuit in the field. In this case the CCR was responding correctly to the fault.

Q4What was the root cause?

A water-damaged cable joint developed insulation failure, allowing electrical contact between the primary conductors inside a flooded cable joint pit. The short circuit reduced circuit impedance and caused CCR protection operation, associated with moisture ingress and progressive insulation deterioration.

Q5Which tests were used to locate the fault?

The investigation used a true-RMS multimeter, insulation resistance tester, cable continuity tester, approved AGL circuit test equipment and cable fault localization equipment, with circuit drawings. All testing was done with the circuit isolated under approved safety procedures.

Q6How was the circuit repaired?

The team isolated the circuit, removed the damaged cable joint, cut back affected cable insulation and installed an approved cable joint kit. They tested insulation resistance and continuity, inspected adjacent joints, improved pit drainage, restored the circuit and verified normal CCR operation.

Q7How was the repair verified?

Post-repair checks confirmed normal CCR operation, stable output current, normal output voltage, acceptable circuit insulation, operational field lights and completed documentation, with additional verification according to the airport maintenance programme.

Q8What should you investigate first if the CCR trips immediately after energization with unusually low output voltage?

Investigate the field circuit for a low-impedance or short-circuit fault. Replacing the CCR or all field lights, or increasing CCR current, is not the right first step.

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist

Read the CCR Response as Evidence

Diagnosis

The way the regulator trips or limits tells you the circuit impedance has fallen. Record the indication before resetting anything.

Split the Circuit to Find the Section

Fault Location

Sectional isolation narrows a short far faster than walking the whole route. Re-test each half and keep a written sequence.

Inspect the Joint Environment, Not Just the Joint

Moisture

A water-damaged joint implies a path for water. Look for damaged sheath, drainage failure or earlier excavation nearby.

Do Not Repeatedly Re-Energise a Suspected Short

Safety

Each re-start stresses the cable and the regulator. Test with the approved instruments and procedure, then energise only when the section is cleared.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

A short circuit in an AGL series circuit should be diagnosed through CCR behavior analysis, insulation testing, and systematic fault localization rather than immediate component replacement.

Proper interpretation of CCR voltage, current regulation, and protection indications can rapidly distinguish a low-impedance field fault from a regulator malfunction.

In AGL maintenance, understanding the electrical behavior of the circuit is often more valuable than replacing components based on symptoms alone.
