Division 06 · FIX · Fixture-Level Physical Maintenance

Lighting Fixture Maintenance

The dedicated maintenance division for the physical AGL light fixture itself — covering every fixture type independent of where it sits on the airfield. Where Divisions 01–05 define when and why maintenance happens (preventive, corrective, predictive, condition-based, emergency), Division 06 is the technical reference for how the fixture is physically maintained: mechanically, optically, electrically, and environmentally.

Core principle: a light fixture is not considered properly maintained merely because it turns ON. Its mechanical condition, optical condition, electrical condition, environmental protection, and alignment must all remain acceptable together.

Fixture Maintenance Decision Logic

A working sequence technicians can apply to any fixture, from first observation through to closed-out record.

StepGuiding Question
1 · InspectWhat is physically wrong?
2 · CleanIs contamination causing the condition?
3 · AssessIs the component damaged or deteriorated?
4 · Repair / ReplaceCan the component be restored?
5 · AlignIs the fixture correctly positioned?
6 · Seal / ProtectIs the fixture protected against environmental ingress?
7 · TestDoes the fixture function correctly?
8 · VerifyIs the final condition acceptable?
9 · RecordHas the maintenance history been updated?

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about lighting fixture maintenance, based on the topics in this division.

7 QUESTIONS
Q1What is Lighting Fixture Maintenance in the AGL Maintenance Handbook?

It is the dedicated division for the physical AGL light fixture itself, covering every fixture type regardless of where it sits on the airfield. It does not replace the maintenance-management divisions; it is the fixture-level technical reference they invoke.

Q2What does Division 06 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 06 (Lighting Fixture Maintenance) contains 35 topics in five groups: Group A Mechanical Maintenance (10 topics), Group B Optical Maintenance (7 topics), Group C Electrical Maintenance (8 topics), Group D Environmental Protection (6 topics) and Group E Restoration (4 topics).

Q3Is a fixture properly maintained just because it turns on?

No. Its mechanical, optical, electrical, environmental and functional condition, including alignment, must all remain acceptable together.

Q4Which fixture types does this division apply to?

Runway, taxiway, centreline, threshold and end, touchdown-zone, runway guard-light, approach-light and stop-bar fixtures, and other AGL light fixtures. The same mechanical, optical, electrical and environmental procedures apply wherever the fixture is installed.

Q5What is the core fixture maintenance sequence?

Seven steps: Inspect, Clean, Repair/Replace, Align, Seal/Protect, Test and Record.

Q6What questions guide fixture maintenance decisions?

Technicians work through a decision logic: what is physically wrong, is contamination causing the condition, is the component damaged or deteriorated, can it be restored, is the fixture correctly positioned, is it protected against environmental ingress, does it function correctly, is the final condition acceptable, and has the maintenance history been updated.

Q7How does Division 06 connect to Divisions 01 to 05?

Division 01 uses it for routine fixture cleaning, tightening, alignment and inspection; Division 02 for failed fixture and component repair; Division 03 for fixture failure-rate and performance trending; Division 04 for fixture condition assessment and maintenance decisions; and Division 05 for emergency fixture restoration after critical failure or damage.
