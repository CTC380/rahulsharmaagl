Common Isolating Transformer Issues and How to Detect Them

Most isolating transformer problems show warning signs before complete failure. The issue is that these signs are often ignored until runway lights start tripping, dimming, or failing during operations.

RS

Rahul Sharma

Manager – Technical & Commercial | AGL Photometry Specialist

Published May 11, 2026 Updated October 26, 2026 AGL Components Troubleshooting

In Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) systems, isolating transformers are often treated as passive components. But in reality, they are one of the most critical reliability points in a series circuit — a fault in a transformer’s primary winding or insulation can affect not only one fitting but the entire series circuit.

Understanding the early warning signs of transformer deterioration can help airport maintenance teams catch problems before they become operational incidents.

Key Takeaway

Most isolating transformer failures give warning signs first — rising primary voltage, uneven light intensity, excessive heating, or abnormal secondary readings. Transformers deserve preventive attention, not reactive replacement.

01

Rising Primary Voltage

One of the earliest indicators of isolating transformer deterioration is an abnormal increase in primary voltage during testing. This can point toward several possible conditions, including:

  • Increased losses from shorted turns or winding damage
  • Core deterioration or overheating damage
  • Increased impedance, or a load mismatch between transformer rating and fitting
  • Poor or high-resistance primary connections

When the transformer condition worsens, the Constant Current Regulator (CCR) compensates by increasing voltage to maintain constant current in the circuit. This is why rising primary voltage should never be ignored during routine maintenance — a gradual increase can be an early indication that a transformer is no longer operating under normal conditions.

Compare like with like: log the CCR output voltage at the same brightness step and rated current, and test individual units at the manufacturer’s test conditions. Insulation degradation and moisture ingress mainly show up as falling insulation resistance or leakage to earth, not as a higher primary voltage, so they need the insulation tests described below.

02

Uneven Light Intensity

If one light appears dimmer or brighter than adjacent fittings in the same circuit, the issue may not always be the lamp. The isolating transformer may be operating inefficiently, developing partial winding damage, or producing unstable secondary output.

In AGL systems, uniformity is extremely important — small intensity variations can become highly visible to pilots during low-visibility operations. When a fitting behaves differently from its neighbours, check the simple causes first — lens soiling, lamp or LED module, secondary connector — and then consider the transformer. Under Annex 14 maintenance provisions, a light whose main-beam average intensity falls below half the specified value is treated as unserviceable, so persistent dimming is a compliance matter as well as a reliability one (check the current edition for the exact rule).

03

Excessive Heating

Healthy isolating transformers generate heat during operation. However, abnormal heating is a warning sign. Possible causes include overloading, internal shorted turns, poor secondary connections, and age-related insulation breakdown.

Field technicians should pay particular attention to transformers that run noticeably hotter than nearby units under similar loading — assessed by thermal imaging or on de-energised, isolated equipment, never by touching live series-circuit equipment. Thermal irregularities can indicate internal deterioration long before total failure occurs — a transformer that consistently runs abnormally hot should receive further investigation, not be dismissed as normal operating heat.

04

Abnormal Secondary Readings

Routine testing can reveal secondary voltage or current values outside acceptable limits. Such abnormalities may indicate efficiency loss, winding damage, load mismatch, or partial internal faults.

Secondary abnormalities directly affect lamp performance and circuit stability. Ignoring these readings increases the probability of sudden outages during airport operations — secondary-side measurements should be a routine part of transformer condition monitoring.

Watch for an open secondary (failed lamp, broken lead or unplugged connector). An isolating transformer keeps the series circuit intact when the secondary opens, but the secondary voltage and heating rise, so it should be corrected promptly and within the manufacturer’s limits.

05

Why Routine Testing Matters

Preventive testing is not only about compliance — it is about identifying degradation before operational failure occurs. Routine transformer testing can help detect:

  • Insulation deterioration
  • Efficiency reduction
  • Load mismatch between transformer rating and fitting
  • Leakage current issues
  • Thermal stress conditions

Periodic testing also helps maintenance teams identify transformers approaching end-of-life conditions before they impact runway availability. The objective should be to identify trends and warning signs early rather than waiting for a complete component failure. Insulation resistance is tested between primary and secondary and from each winding to earth, only on an isolated, locked-out circuit, with results trended against earlier readings and the manufacturer’s limits rather than judged from a single number.

06

The Real Risk of Ignoring Early Signs

A transformer fault is not always limited to one fitting. In a series AGL circuit, an open primary winding or a developing insulation fault can affect the whole circuit, leading to:

Sudden OutagesAn open primary winding opens the series circuit and trips the CCR
Earth-Fault Build-UpOne insulation fault is tolerated; a second can trip the circuit
Increased CCR LoadThe regulator raises voltage to hold current
Emergency MaintenanceUnplanned intervention during live operations

Runway lighting failures can directly affect aerodrome operations and, in low visibility, safety. This makes early detection particularly important in AGL applications — a transformer may be physically small, but its failure can have consequences far beyond the individual component.

07

From Reactive Replacement to Preventive Maintenance

The traditional approach of replacing a transformer only after failure is reactive. A more reliable approach is to monitor the transformer throughout its operating life:

The earlier deterioration is identified, the greater the opportunity to plan maintenance before an unexpected outage occurs.

Standards and Further Reference

Series isolating transformers are specified in FAA AC 150/5345-47 (L-830/L-831) and IEC 61823; maintenance of visual aids is covered by ICAO Annex 14 Volume I and the Aerodrome Design Manual (Doc 9157) Part 5. Always work to the manufacturer’s test procedure and acceptance limits, and check current editions and your regulator’s requirements.

Final Thoughts

Isolating transformers are small components with massive operational importance. They quietly protect the integrity of the entire AGL series circuit every second the airfield is active — and most failures provide warning signs long before breakdown happens.

In AGL systems, reliability is not achieved simply by installing a component and forgetting about it. It is achieved through proper selection, monitoring, testing, and timely maintenance. The difference between reliable operation and an unexpected outage is often proactive monitoring.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are isolating transformers critical in an AGL series circuit?

They are one of the most critical reliability points, because a fault in a transformer's primary winding or insulation can affect not only one fitting but the entire series circuit. In a series AGL circuit, an open primary winding opens the series circuit and trips the CCR.

What are the early warning signs of isolating transformer deterioration?

The four main warning signs are rising primary voltage, uneven light intensity, excessive heating and abnormal secondary readings. Most transformer failures give warning signs first, which is why transformers deserve preventive attention and not reactive replacement.

What does rising primary voltage indicate?

It can point to increased losses from shorted turns or winding damage, core deterioration or overheating damage, increased impedance or load mismatch, or poor high-resistance primary connections. The CCR compensates by increasing voltage to hold constant current, so log CCR output voltage at the same brightness step and rated current to compare like with like. Insulation degradation and moisture ingress mainly show up as falling insulation resistance or earth leakage.

If one light is dimmer than its neighbours, is the transformer to blame?

Not always. Check the simple causes first, such as lens soiling, the lamp or LED module and the secondary connector, and then consider the transformer. Under Annex 14 maintenance provisions, a light whose main-beam average intensity falls below half the specified value is treated as unserviceable, so check the current edition for the exact rule.

How should excessive heating in a transformer be assessed?

Look for transformers that run noticeably hotter than nearby units under similar loading, assessed by thermal imaging or on de-energised, isolated equipment, never by touching live series-circuit equipment. Possible causes include overloading, internal shorted turns, poor secondary connections and age-related insulation breakdown.

What happens if the secondary of an isolating transformer opens?

An isolating transformer keeps the series circuit intact when the secondary opens, for example from a failed lamp, broken lead or unplugged connector. However, the secondary voltage and heating rise, so it should be corrected promptly and within the manufacturer's limits.

How should insulation testing of isolating transformers be done?

Insulation resistance is tested between primary and secondary and from each winding to earth, only on an isolated, locked-out circuit. Results should be trended against earlier readings and the manufacturer's limits, not judged from a single number.

What is the risk of ignoring early transformer warning signs?

A transformer fault is not always limited to one fitting. Risks include sudden outages when an open primary opens the series circuit and trips the CCR, earth-fault build-up (one insulation fault is tolerated, a second can trip the circuit), increased CCR load and emergency maintenance during live operations.
