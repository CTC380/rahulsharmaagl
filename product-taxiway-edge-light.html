Product Overview

Taxiway Edge Light

What It Is

Taxiway edge lights define the lateral limits of taxiways, taxi lanes and other applicable aircraft movement areas, giving continuous visual reference for aircraft manoeuvring between runways, holding positions, apron areas and associated surfaces.

They must be coordinated with the taxiway centreline, holding-position, stop-bar, runway lighting and other visual aids in the airport layout.

Selected According To

  • Taxiway geometry
  • Taxiway classification
  • Movement-area configuration
  • Runway / taxiway intersection arrangement
  • Visibility and RVR requirements
  • Elevated or inset installation
  • Light colour
  • Optical coverage
  • Fixture spacing
  • Series-circuit characteristics
  • Mounting arrangement
  • Environmental conditions
  • Project technical specification

Final selection should follow the approved airport lighting layout, applicable aerodrome standard and project specification.

Function of Taxiway Edge Lighting

A Continuous Indication of the Pavement Boundary

What the Lights Do

Taxiway edge lighting gives a continuous visual indication of the pavement boundary and helps pilots maintain awareness of the usable taxi route.

Treat it as part of the complete movement-area lighting system, not an isolated product.

The System Should Provide

  • Clear lateral definition
  • Adequate visibility from the operating direction
  • Appropriate colour and intensity
  • Suitable horizontal and vertical coverage
  • Consistent fixture spacing
  • Correct installation alignment
  • Reliable electrical operation
  • Compatibility with airport lighting controls
  • Maintainable performance over the lifecycle
Light Colour

Blue Edge Lighting

ICAO-Style Edge Lighting

The principal light characteristic is blue, shown as a fixed (steady-burning) light. ICAO Annex 14 calls for taxiway edge lights at the edges of runway turn pads, holding bays and aprons intended for night use, and on a night-use taxiway that has no centreline lights; confirm the exact provision in your edition.

Where FAA classifications are used, the current fixture specification identifies L-861T for elevated and L-852T for in-pavement (flush) taxiway and apron-edge applications, both with an omnidirectional blue output.

Under ICAO Annex 14, elevated taxiway edge lights must be frangible and low enough to keep clearance for propellers and engine pods. The project specification should still determine the exact fixture type and optical configuration.

Configurations

Elevated and Inset Taxiway Edge Lights

Elevated Taxiway Edge Light

Installed above the pavement where the approved taxiway design permits elevated fixtures. It should clearly identify the pavement edge without becoming an unacceptable obstruction or interfering with aircraft operations.

Typical assembly

  • Light fixture and optical lens
  • LED or approved light source
  • Housing
  • Mounting base
  • Frangible support
  • Cable connection and electrical interface
  • Fasteners

Inset / In-Pavement Taxiway Edge Light

Selected where geometry or operational requirements call for a flush installation, or where elevated equipment is undesirable. The exact configuration comes from approved drawings and manufacturer installation details.

Typical assembly

  • In-pavement light fixture and housing
  • Optical lens and LED / light-source module
  • Mounting hardware, gaskets and sealing system
  • Drainage arrangement
  • Secondary cable
  • Isolating transformer
  • Primary connector

Curved Taxiways

Pay particular attention to:

  • Fixture spacing
  • Optical coverage and orientation
  • Radius of curvature
  • Relationship to the marked edge
  • Visibility from approaching aircraft

Where the project specifies a spacing, maintain it as closely as practicable while preserving visual continuity. As an ICAO reference point (confirm in your edition), edge lights on straight sections and runway turn pads are spaced at uniform intervals of not more than 60 m, closer on curves so the curve is clearly shown, and are located as near as practicable to the pavement edge or not more than 3 m outside it.

The fixture layout should follow the approved airport design rather than be determined independently from pavement geometry.

FAQ

Taxiway Edge Light — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a taxiway edge light?

Taxiway edge lights define the lateral limits of taxiways, taxi lanes and other applicable aircraft movement areas, giving a continuous visual reference for aircraft manoeuvring between runways, holding positions, apron areas and associated surfaces. They give a continuous indication of the pavement boundary and must be coordinated with the taxiway centreline, holding-position, stop-bar, runway lighting and other visual aids.

Where are taxiway edge lights used?

They are used on taxiways, taxi lanes, holding areas, apron connections, runway turn pads where edge lighting is required, aircraft movement areas and taxiway intersections, and in rehabilitation projects, taxiway extensions, new airfield construction, AGL modernisation and fixture replacement.

What colour are taxiway edge lights, and when are they required?

The principal light characteristic is blue, shown as a fixed (steady-burning) light. ICAO Annex 14 calls for taxiway edge lights at the edges of runway turn pads, holding bays and aprons intended for night use, and on a night-use taxiway that has no centreline lights; confirm the exact provision in your edition. Where FAA classifications are used, L-861T is the elevated and L-852T the in-pavement (flush) taxiway and apron-edge fixture, both with an omnidirectional blue output.

What is the difference between elevated and inset taxiway edge lights?

An elevated light is installed above the pavement where the approved taxiway design permits, with a base, frangible support and highly visible fixture body, and aircraft clearance must be considered. An inset light is flush with the pavement, selected where geometry or operational requirements call for it or elevated equipment is undesirable, and its pavement interface, drainage and alignment are critical. Under ICAO Annex 14, elevated taxiway edge lights must be frangible and low enough to keep clearance for propellers and engine pods.

How far apart should taxiway edge lights be?

Where the project specifies a spacing, maintain it as closely as practicable while preserving visual continuity. As an ICAO reference point (confirm in your edition), edge lights on straight sections and runway turn pads are spaced at uniform intervals of not more than 60 m, closer on curves so the curve is clearly shown, and are located as near as practicable to the pavement edge or not more than 3 m outside it. The fixture layout should follow the approved airport design rather than pavement geometry alone.

How do edge lights relate to centreline lights and other visual aids?

Edge lights define the lateral boundary and are blue, while centreline lights provide longitudinal route guidance and are green or sequence-dependent, with L-852 series fixtures typical for centreline applications. The two may operate together, particularly in complex taxiway networks. At intersections, edge lighting may interact with centreline lighting, runway edge and centreline lighting, stop bars, runway guard lights, clearance bars and holding-position markings, and FAA AC 150/5340-30J gives detailed layout guidance. The project lighting layout determines the arrangement at every intersection.

What electrical arrangement do edge lights use?

A typical arrangement runs from the CCR through the primary AGL cable and isolating transformer to the secondary cable and the taxiway edge light. Confirm the series-circuit operating current, transformer rating, fixture power consumption, connector type, intensity-step and monitoring requirements and control-system compatibility. Incorrect electrical coordination can cause incorrect light output, poor intensity control, circuit instability, transformer stress, premature equipment failure or monitoring faults.

Does an LED light source make an edge light compliant?

No. LEDs offer long operating life, reduced lamp replacement and routine maintenance, consistent output, stable colour, controlled optical distribution and improved energy efficiency, but LED technology is not the compliance criterion by itself. The complete fixture must meet the applicable photometric, electrical, environmental and mechanical requirements.

What should be verified during installation?

For elevated lights, check fixture position and height, the frangible support, base installation, optical orientation, cable connection, mechanical stability and clearance. For inset lights, check the housing type, fixture seating and installation depth, pavement cut-out, fastener torque, drainage and gaskets, secondary connection and optical orientation. Also verify the distance from the pavement edge, spacing, curve alignment and intersection arrangement, because a visually functioning fixture can still be incorrectly installed if its position or orientation does not match the approved layout.

How are taxiway edge lights commissioned and maintained?

Commissioning covers visual checks, electrical tests (circuit continuity, insulation resistance, transformer verification, series-circuit current and CCR operation, intensity steps, control and monitoring) and, where required, photometric verification of blue colour, intensity, beam distribution, optical direction, visibility, alignment and fixture consistency. Inspection and maintenance cover the optical, mechanical and electrical condition of the fixture and the layout, including spacing, edge alignment, curve continuity and intersection arrangement.

What are common edge light failure modes?

Typical symptoms are a light off, low light output, incorrect colour, intermittent operation and an uneven edge appearance. Causes include an open series circuit, transformer, connector or cable failure, incorrect CCR intensity, optical contamination, LED degradation, loose connectors or moisture ingress, and incorrect fixture type, spacing or alignment. The diagnostic sequence runs from the CCR through the primary series circuit, isolating transformer, secondary cable, connector, fixture, light source, optical system, alignment and photometric verification.

Which standards apply to taxiway edge lights?

References include ICAO Annex 14 (taxiway visual aids and edge lighting; confirm fixture arrangement, spacing and use against the applicable edition, including any corrigenda to Volume I), FAA AC 150/5345-46F (L-861T elevated and L-852T in-pavement, omnidirectional blue), FAA AC 150/5340-30J, DGCA India requirements and airport-specific AGL technical specifications, the approved taxiway lighting layout, and applicable Indian Standards and airport operator requirements. Always verify the governing standard edition before procurement, approval or installation.

What should be confirmed before ordering taxiway edge lights?

Confirm the airport and project, taxiway identification, classification and geometry, straight or curved sections, intersections, holding areas and apron connections, visibility and RVR requirement, night-use requirement, elevated or inset configuration, light colour, optical distribution and intensity, series-circuit current, isolating transformer, connector type, mounting arrangement, installation depth and pavement cut-out where applicable, environmental requirements, applicable standard, and the datasheet, photometric and compliance documentation.
