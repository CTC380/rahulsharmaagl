02 · Definition

What Is Taxiway Lighting?

While runway lighting primarily identifies and supports operations on the runway, taxiway lighting provides guidance through the aircraft movement network connecting Runway → Taxiway → Intersection → Apron / Stand Area. The actual combination of facilities depends on the airport and approved configuration.

03 · Main Taxiway Lighting Systems

Core Taxiway Lighting Applications

Taxiway Edge Lighting

Defines the lateral limits of a taxiway, providing visual awareness of boundaries during night operations, reduced ambient visibility and complex taxiway movements.

Taxiway Centreline Lighting

Provides longitudinal guidance along the taxiway route, particularly relevant where enhanced taxi guidance is required. Identified among facilities used under low-visibility procedures.

Stop Bar Lighting

A critical visual guidance facility for holding and controlled movement areas, indicating a holding position as part of an airport's low-visibility movement-control system.

Runway Guard Lights

Associated with runway/taxiway intersections, providing an additional visual indication of the runway environment, particularly near active runways.

Holding-Position Lighting

Associated with locations where aircraft are required to hold, and may be integrated with taxiway lighting, stop bars, runway guard lights and centreline guidance.

Lead-On / Lead-Off Lighting

Provides visual guidance along a designated route through complex geometry or transitions, and guidance away from a runway toward a taxiway route.

19 · Standards, Compliance & Airport Category

Taxiway Lighting Standards & Airport Category

Indian taxiway lighting projects can involve multiple layers of requirements. A lighting system suitable for one airport should not automatically be assumed to apply to another.

ICAO — aerodrome visual-aid requirementsDGCA — regulatory requirementsAAI / Airport — project-specific requirementsBIS / IS — equipment & installation standardsIEC — where specifiedFAA — where referenced

Taxiway Lighting Standards India

Taxiway lighting in India is specified against ICAO aerodrome visual-aid requirements as implemented through DGCA, together with AAI or airport-specific project requirements and BIS / IS standards for equipment and installation. IEC and FAA references apply where the project specification calls for them. Confirm the governing documents for each airport against the current editions; the compliance matrix below records the project reference for each facility. See AGL Standards & Compliance and ICAO vs FAA.

When selecting a taxiway light manufacturer in India, check compliance with the specified standard, type-test and photometric reports, compatibility with the CCR and series circuit, service life, spares and local support. FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

What does taxiway lighting support at Indian airports?
Taxiway lighting helps provide visual guidance for aircraft movement between runways, aprons and other airside areas.
What taxiway applications are common under low-visibility procedures?
Indian AIP data illustrates the use of taxiway centreline lighting, stop bars and runway guard lighting at aerodromes operating under low-visibility procedures.
What taxiway lighting elements are typically installed?
Taxiway edge lights, taxiway centreline lights, stop bars, runway guard lights, holding-position lighting, and lead-on/lead-off guidance.
What is the typical electrical chain for a taxiway light?
A constant current regulator feeds a primary cable through a primary connector to an isolating transformer, then a secondary connector to the taxiway light fixture, the same series-circuit architecture used across other AGL systems.
Can an existing taxiway fixture simply be replaced with an LED fixture?
LED conversion should not be treated as a fixture-only replacement. The complete system should be reviewed for CCR compatibility, series-circuit compatibility, transformer compatibility, cable condition, connector compatibility, control/monitoring and photometric performance.
Why is the taxiway/runway interface treated as one system?
Multiple visual guidance systems can meet at the same location, such as taxiway centreline, stop bar, holding position, runway guard light and runway lighting. The design and operational relationship between these facilities should be considered together during design and commissioning rather than as isolated fixtures.
