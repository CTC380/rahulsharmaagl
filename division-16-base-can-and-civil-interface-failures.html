Base Can & Civil Interface Failures

The dedicated failure classification for the civil/mechanical interface between AGL fixtures and the airfield pavement/foundation system. A lighting fixture can be electrically healthy but still unserviceable due to base-can deterioration, water accumulation, incorrect elevation, settlement, alignment problems, cable-entry damage or pavement-interface deterioration — this division exists to catch exactly that class of failure.

Initial Issue

Key principle — Division 16 supports root-cause analysis, not just defect recording. If base-can flooding repeatedly occurs despite maintenance, investigate the underlying groundwater/drainage condition rather than repeatedly replacing the fixture. Every record should trace: fixture → base can → foundation/pavement → drainage/ground condition → cable interface → fixture mounting → elevation/alignment → failure mode → root cause → corrective action → verification → recurrence/seasonal trend.

BCF-01 Base-Can Corrosion
Failure Definition

Corrosion of the base can or associated metallic components that affects structural, mechanical or environmental integrity.

Possible Causes
  • Water accumulation
  • Moisture exposure
  • Salt contamination
  • Poor drainage
  • Damaged protective coating
  • Ageing
Possible Consequences
  • Structural weakening
  • Fixture mounting deterioration
  • Cable-entry deterioration
  • Difficulty removing/installing fixture
  • Frangible-interface degradation
Investigation
  • Base-can body
  • Internal surfaces
  • Mounting points
  • Bolts/fasteners
  • Cable-entry points
  • Drainage condition
  • Corrosion severity
BCF-02 Base-Can Water Accumulation
Failure Definition

Water remains within the base can instead of being effectively drained.

Possible Causes
  • Blocked drainage
  • Poor installation
  • Damaged drainage path
  • High water table
  • Groundwater ingress
  • Heavy rainfall
Consequences
  • Corrosion
  • Cable deterioration
  • Connector deterioration
  • Moisture ingress into fixture
  • Insulation deterioration
Failure Chain
WATER ACCUMULATIONCORROSION/MOISTUREELECTRICAL DETERIORATIONFIXTURE FAILURE
BCF-03 Base-Can Flooding
Failure Definition

Significant entry or accumulation of water in the base can that compromises the fixture/cable installation.

Possible Causes
  • Heavy rain
  • Flooding
  • Drainage failure
  • High water table
  • Pavement drainage problems
  • Damaged sealing
Possible Consequences
  • Fixture failure
  • Cable/connector damage
  • Corrosion
  • Insulation deterioration
  • Repeated failures
Important Distinction

Water accumulation may be a persistent/local condition. Base-can flooding represents a more severe water-ingress condition.

B. Failure Chains

Division 16 is built around five recurring failure chains that connect civil root causes to electrical/optical outcomes:

Chain 1 — Drainage

Drainage failure → base-can water accumulation → corrosion/moisture ingress → fixture/cable deterioration → lighting failure

Chain 2 — Ground Movement

Soil movement → foundation settlement → base-can/fixture movement → incorrect elevation/alignment → AGL performance degradation

Chain 3 — Cable Entry

Cable-entry damage → cable sealing failure → water ingress → insulation/connector deterioration → electrical failure

Chain 4 — Pavement

Road/pavement interface damage → water accumulation/mechanical instability → base-can deterioration → fixture mounting/alignment failure

Chain 5 — Corrosion

Water-table/drainage problem → persistent moisture → base-can corrosion → mounting degradation → fixture movement/failure

Why Division 16 Matters

The major value of Division 16 is that it captures the civil root causes behind recurring AGL failures. Repeated fixture failures associated with a flooded base can should lead the investigation toward drainage, groundwater and water-table conditions, rather than treating each failed fixture as an isolated electrical failure. Recommended database architecture: Civil/AGL Asset → Base Can → Foundation/Pavement → Drainage/Ground Condition → Cable Interface → Fixture Mounting → Elevation/Alignment → Failure Mode → Root Cause → Corrective Action → Verification → Recurrence/Seasonal Trend.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about base-can and civil-interface failures, as covered in Division 16 of the AGL Failure Database.

Q1What is Division 16 — Base Can & Civil Interface Failures in the AGL Failure Database?

Division 16 covers the civil and mechanical interface between AGL fixtures and the airfield pavement and foundation system. It contains 18 published topics (BCF-01 to BCF-18), from base-can corrosion and drainage failure to foundation settlement, cable-entry damage, soil movement and water-table problems.

Q2Which base-can and civil-interface failures does Division 16 cover?

Base-can corrosion, water accumulation, flooding and deformation, drainage failure, foundation settlement and cracking, fixture mounting failure, incorrect fixture elevation and alignment, frangible-coupling failure, civil-interface damage, cable-entry damage, cable sealing failure, pit-cover damage, road/pavement interface damage, soil movement and water-table-related problems.

Q3How are the 18 civil-interface topics grouped?

They fall into seven groups: Base-Can Condition (4 topics), Drainage & Water (3), Fixture Installation & Position (3), Frangible & Civil Interface (2), Cable & Sealing Interface (2), Pit & Pavement Interface (2) and Ground & Hydrogeological (2).

Q4What failure chains does Division 16 capture?

Division 16 is built around five recurring chains that connect civil root causes to electrical and optical outcomes. Drainage failure leads to base-can water accumulation, corrosion or moisture ingress, fixture and cable deterioration, and lighting failure. Soil movement leads to foundation settlement, fixture movement and incorrect elevation or alignment. Cable-entry damage, pavement-interface damage and water-table problems have their own chains.

Q5Why does Division 16 matter for recurring fixture failures?

It captures the civil root causes behind recurring AGL failures. Repeated fixture failures associated with a flooded base can should lead the investigation toward drainage, groundwater and water-table conditions, rather than treating each failed fixture as an isolated electrical failure.

Q6How can the complete interface be inspected?

Division 16 lets the engineer inspect the interface across seven layers: the fixture (mounting, elevation, alignment, frangible coupling), the base can, the cable interface, the foundation, the pavement, drainage and the ground (soil movement, groundwater and water-table condition).

Q7What evidence supports a base-can or civil-interface record?

Visual evidence includes photographs of base-can condition, corrosion, deformation and internal water, foundation and pavement cracking or settlement, the cable-entry point, sealing arrangement and pit cover, and reference photos of fixture mounting, elevation and alignment. Technical evidence includes elevation and alignment measurements against design, insulation resistance, continuity, drainage verification, historical and seasonal recurrence records and groundwater data.

Q8How are failure severity and operational impact classified?

Every failure receives a severity classification, plus operational-impact tagging. The classes are Critical / Safety-Critical (immediate operational or safety risk), Major / Operationally Critical (significant availability impact), Moderate (reduced performance, not yet critical), Minor / Non-Critical (limited or no operational effect), and Degradation / Intermittent / Latent (a developing or hidden fault).

Beyond the Flooded Base Can

DON’T JUST PUMP IT OUT. UNDERSTAND THE CIVIL INTERFACE.

Flooded base cans, cracked duct seals and settled foundations often trace back to the civil works around the fixture, not the fixture itself — real troubleshooting means understanding drainage, ducting and the base-can-to-pavement interface well enough to fix the actual cause.
