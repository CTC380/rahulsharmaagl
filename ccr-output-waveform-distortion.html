What is CCR output waveform distortion?

The current a CCR sends into the series circuit is not always a clean sine wave. How far it departs depends on the CCR type, and it changes how the output must be measured.

The Fact Output shape

A CCR is meant to deliver a steady effective (RMS) current to the series circuit on each brightness step, but the shape of that current is not always a clean sine wave. A phase-controlled (thyristor) regulator chops the wave, a ferroresonant regulator gives a flat-topped wave that is not a clean sine (filtered designs can be close to sinusoidal), and an IGBT regulator filters its output towards a clean one. Waveform distortion is how far the wave departs from a sine, often given as total harmonic distortion.

It matters because lamps respond to the RMS value and because distortion decides which meter can be trusted. A true-RMS instrument reads the effective current correctly, while a simple average-reading meter, calibrated for a sine wave, can read wrongly on a chopped wave. The inductance of the circuit, including the isolation transformers, partly smooths the wave, and harmonic current can add heat in the transformers. How much distortion is acceptable comes from the CCR’s specification, not from this page.

  1. Identify the CCR type. Thyristor, ferroresonant or IGBT, and read its stated output specification in the manual.
  2. Choose the instrument. A calibrated true-RMS meter or analyser with enough range and bandwidth for a chopped wave and for the circuit current.
  3. Connect at the approved test point. Under the site permit, and never open a live series circuit, because the voltage can be hazardous.
  4. Measure each step. Compare the current with the nominal step value and tolerance given in the manual.
  5. Look at the shape. If the instrument shows the waveform or a distortion figure, note it and compare it with the baseline.
  6. Log and trend. A change in shape at the same step can point to a developing problem such as a worn thyristor, an ageing capacitor or a failing filter.
Thyristor (phase-controlled)

Chopped by phase-angle firing and partly smoothed by the circuit. It needs a true-RMS meter, and filtering may be fitted to limit the effect.

Ferroresonant or IGBT

A classic ferroresonant output is flat-topped and not a clean sine; filtered designs can be close to sinusoidal. An IGBT output is filtered towards a clean wave, and the result depends on the filter and the design.

Cause The way the CCR controls current: phase-angle firing, a resonant core or a switched stage
Effect The output departs from a sine wave, so some meters read wrongly

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is CCR output waveform distortion?

It is how far the current a CCR sends into the series circuit departs from a clean sine wave. The regulator controls the effective (RMS) current on each brightness step, but the shape of the wave depends on the CCR type and can be chopped, flat-topped or close to sinusoidal.

Q2Why does a thyristor CCR distort its output?

A thyristor CCR controls the current by phase-angle firing, which passes only part of each half-cycle. The result is a chopped waveform, partly smoothed by the inductance of the series circuit and the isolation transformers. Filtering may be fitted to limit the effect.

Q3Why must a true-RMS meter be used on a CCR output?

A simple average-reading meter is calibrated for a sine wave, so on a chopped or flat-topped wave it can read wrongly, and a good circuit can look faulty or a faulty one can look fine. A calibrated true-RMS instrument reads the effective current correctly.

Q4Does distortion change lamp current or brightness?

Lamps respond to the effective (RMS) current, and the regulator controls to that value, so the target is not changed by the shape alone. Distortion does affect which instruments read correctly, and harmonic current can add heating in the isolation transformers and cables.

Q5How is the output waveform checked?

A competent person connects a true-RMS instrument, or a power quality analyser or oscilloscope arrangement approved for the purpose, at the approved test point under the site permit, and never opens a live series circuit. The step current, waveform shape and any distortion figure are compared with the manual and with the logged baseline.
