Environmental Failures

The dedicated environmental and weather-related failure section of the AGL Failure Database. Covers exposure to lightning, flooding, sand, snow/ice, UV radiation, temperature extremes, salt and coastal corrosion, moisture, humidity, dust, wind, storms, drainage conditions and seasonal deterioration. Every record should identify the environmental exposure, the physical damage mechanism, the affected AGL component, and the resulting operational consequence.

Initial Issue

Key principle — Environmental failure records should identify the environmental exposure, the physical damage mechanism, the affected AGL component, and the resulting operational consequence — for example: coastal exposure → salt contamination → corrosion → connector deterioration → high contact resistance → fixture failure. Do not record simply “corrosion due to environment” — trace the full chain.

Core categories — Lightning, flooding, sand, snow/ice, UV degradation, high temperature and salt corrosion are the seven original environmental-failure categories identified in the source framework. The remaining thirteen topics expand these into a more comprehensive environmental-failure classification, not a replacement for them.

ENV-01 Lightning Damage Core
Failure Definition

Damage to AGL electrical, electronic or control equipment resulting from lightning activity or associated electrical transients.

Potentially Affected Equipment
  • AGL fixtures
  • CCRs
  • Control systems
  • Communication equipment
  • Cables
  • Transformers
  • Protection equipment
Possible Symptoms
  • Equipment failure
  • Protection trip
  • Communication loss
  • Control-system failure
  • Component burnout
  • Insulation damage
Investigation

Check:

  • Lightning event history
  • Equipment condition
  • Surge-protection condition
  • Earthing/bonding
  • Protection devices
  • Associated equipment failures
Failure Chain
LIGHTNING EVENTSURGE/TRANSIENTPROTECTION FAILURE OR EQUIPMENT DAMAGEAGL/CONTROL FAILURE
ENV-02 Flooding Core
Failure Definition

Failure or damage caused by significant water inundation of AGL equipment or surrounding infrastructure.

Potentially Affected Areas
  • Runway/taxiway fixtures
  • Base cans
  • Cable systems
  • Transformers
  • CCR/control rooms
  • Signage
  • Approach-lighting systems
Possible Consequences
  • Water ingress
  • Insulation deterioration
  • Corrosion
  • Electrical faults
  • Equipment outage
Investigation

Record: flood extent, water level, duration, affected assets, drainage condition, and post-flood insulation/electrical condition.

ENV-03 Sand Ingress Core
Failure Definition

Entry or accumulation of sand within or around AGL equipment resulting in degradation or malfunction.

Possible Effects
  • Optical contamination
  • Mechanical wear
  • Seal deterioration
  • Cooling obstruction
  • Connector/housing contamination
Investigation

Check:

  • Fixture housing
  • Optical surfaces
  • Seals
  • Moving/mechanical components
  • Ventilation/cooling areas
  • Surrounding installation
ENV-04 Snow/Ice Effects Core
Failure Definition

Failure or performance degradation caused by snow or ice accumulation/freezing conditions.

Possible Effects
  • Optical obstruction
  • Fixture coverage reduction
  • Mechanical restriction
  • Water ingress after melting
  • Freeze/thaw deterioration
Investigation

Determine whether the problem resulted from snow/ice accumulation or freeze/thaw-related physical deterioration.

ENV-15 Storm Damage
Failure Definition

Damage caused by severe storm conditions involving one or more environmental stresses.

Potential Contributors
  • Strong wind
  • Heavy rain
  • Lightning
  • Flooding
  • Debris
  • Rapid environmental changes
Important Principle

Storm damage can be the environmental event, while the actual failure mechanism may be lightning damage, flooding, wind damage, or water ingress. These should be separately recorded where evidence supports them.

ENV-17 Coastal Exposure
Failure Definition

Environmental exposure associated with coastal conditions that increases the likelihood of corrosion and material degradation.

Typical Environmental Stresses
  • Salt-laden air
  • High humidity
  • Moisture
  • Wind
  • Rain
Potential Consequences
  • Salt corrosion
  • Connector deterioration
  • Hardware corrosion
  • Seal degradation
  • Electrical connection deterioration
Important Distinction

Coastal exposure describes the environmental condition. Salt corrosion (ENV-07) describes the resulting physical deterioration. Both can therefore be recorded in an RCA chain.

ENV-19 Seasonal Deterioration
Failure Definition

Progressive deterioration associated with recurring seasonal environmental conditions.

Examples
  • Monsoon-related moisture
  • Summer high-temperature exposure
  • Winter freezing
  • Seasonal humidity
  • Seasonal dust/sand exposure
Reliability Significance

Seasonal deterioration is particularly useful for trend analysis — for example, repeated connector failures during the annual high-humidity/rainfall period may indicate an environmental vulnerability rather than random component failure.

C. Classification Groups

The 20 topics are organised into six logical groups:

  • A — Electrical & Atmospheric (Topics 1–7 · core)
  • B — Water & Moisture (Topics 8–11)
  • C — Contamination & Temperature (Topics 12–13)
  • D — Wind & Storm (Topics 14–15)
  • E — Corrosive & Location-Specific (Topics 16–17)
  • F — Drainage & Seasonal (Topics 18–20)

The first seven remain the primary/core environmental-failure categories identified in the source framework; the remaining thirteen are expanded diagnostic and failure-intelligence categories, not replacements for the original seven.

D. Failure Severity

Every failure receives a severity classification, plus operational-impact tagging:

Critical / Safety-Critical — immediate operational or safety risk

Major / Operationally Critical — significant availability impact

Moderate — reduced performance, not yet critical

Minor / Non-Critical — limited or no operational effect

Degradation / Intermittent / Latent — developing or hidden fault

F. Important RCA Distinction

Environmental failure records should distinguish between the environmental event, exposure, failure mechanism, and technical failure. Do not record simply “corrosion due to environment.”

Example 1 — Coastal Connector

Environmental condition: Coastal exposure

Exposure: Salt-laden humidity

Failure mechanism: Corrosion

Affected component: Connector

Technical consequence: High contact resistance

Operational consequence: Fixture failure

Example 2 — Drainage/Earth Fault

Environmental event: Heavy rain

Infrastructure condition: Drainage-related failure

Physical condition: Water accumulation

Failure mechanism: Moisture ingress

Component condition: Insulation deterioration

Technical failure: Earth fault

Operational consequence: Series-circuit outage

Division 09 vs. Divisions 06 & 08

Division 09 captures the environmental exposure and weather-driven damage mechanism — lightning, flooding, salt, UV, temperature, wind and seasonal conditions. Where the environmental event ultimately produces a mechanical, structural or civil failure (a settled foundation, a blocked drain, a damaged support), cross-reference Division 06 — Mechanical & Structural Failures. Where moisture ingress or corrosion resulted from improper sealing or installation rather than the environment itself, cross-reference Division 08 — Human & Installation Failures. Kept strictly separate, then linked end-to-end, these divisions distinguish an environmental cause from a preventable human or design cause.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about environmental and weather-related failures, as covered in Division 09 of the AGL Failure Database.

Q1What is Division 09 — Environmental Failures in the AGL Failure Database?

Division 09 covers environmental and weather-related failures affecting AGL equipment. It contains 20 published topics (ENV-01 to ENV-20), from lightning, flooding and salt corrosion to UV degradation, wind, storm and seasonal deterioration.

Q2Which environmental failures does Division 09 cover?

Lightning damage, flooding, sand ingress, snow/ice effects, UV degradation, high-temperature damage, salt corrosion, moisture ingress, heavy rain, water accumulation, humidity-related failure, dust contamination, extreme temperature, wind damage, storm damage, corrosive environment, coastal exposure, drainage-related failure, seasonal deterioration and severe-weather damage.

Q3How are the 20 environmental topics grouped?

They fall into six groups: Electrical & Atmospheric (7 topics, the core categories), Water & Moisture (4), Contamination & Temperature (2), Wind & Storm (2), Corrosive & Location-Specific Environment (2) and Drainage & Seasonal (3).

Q4What four elements should an environmental failure record distinguish?

It should distinguish the environmental event, the exposure, the failure mechanism and the technical failure, rather than a single line such as “corrosion due to environment”. For example, coastal exposure to salt-laden humidity causes corrosion of a connector, with high contact resistance as the technical consequence.

Q5What environmental failure chains does Division 09 capture?

Chains include coastal exposure (salt deposition, salt corrosion, connector and hardware deterioration, high contact resistance), rain and drainage (heavy rain, water accumulation, moisture ingress, corrosion or insulation deterioration), lightning (transient or surge, protection stress, component damage), UV (material degradation, seal deterioration, moisture ingress), extreme temperature and seasonal exposure leading to progressive, recurring deterioration.

Q6How does Division 09 relate to Division 06?

Division 09 captures the environmental exposure and weather-driven damage mechanism. Where the environmental event ultimately produces a mechanical, structural or civil failure, such as a settled foundation, a blocked drain or a damaged support, the record should cross-reference Division 06 — Mechanical & Structural Failures.

Q7How are failure severity and operational impact classified?

Every failure receives a severity classification, plus operational-impact tagging. The classes are Critical / Safety-Critical (immediate operational or safety risk), Major / Operationally Critical (significant availability impact), Moderate (reduced performance, not yet critical), Minor / Non-Critical (limited or no operational effect), and Degradation / Intermittent / Latent (a developing or hidden fault).

Beyond the Weather Excuse

DON’T JUST BLAME THE ENVIRONMENT. UNDERSTAND THE EXPOSURE.

Lightning strikes, flooding, extreme heat and corrosion from coastal or industrial air all stress AGL systems differently — real troubleshooting means understanding how environmental exposure interacts with system design, not treating every failure as bad luck.
