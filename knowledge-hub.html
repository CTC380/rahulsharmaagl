Quick Answers

AGL Quick Answers:
Testing, Components & Control

Fundamentals

AGL Importance

AGL importance comes from safety: the lights give pilots guidance for approach, landing, taxiing and take-off at night and in low visibility.

AGL Advantages

AGL advantages include consistent visual guidance, long-life LED options and remote monitoring and control.

AGL Symbols

AGL symbols on layout drawings show each light type and its position.

AGL Site Survey

The AGL site survey is carried out before design.

AGL Equipment List

The AGL equipment list schedules the fixtures, transformers, regulators, cable and accessories in the system.

AGL Frangibility

AGL frangibility is the ability of an elevated light or its support to break away cleanly on impact.

AGL Beam Characteristics

AGL beam characteristics define the horizontal and vertical angles over which a fixture emits light, and the minimum intensity within them.

Other beginner topics include AGL light colours and AGL field testing.

Power, Automation & Assets

Airport Lighting Power Supply

Airport lighting power supply feeds the constant current regulators from the aerodrome supply, with a secondary supply for critical visual aids; switch-over requirements depend on runway type and approach category.

Airport Lighting Cable Sizing

Airport lighting cable sizing starts from the series circuit current and loop length, then checks conductor size, insulation rating and voltage drop against the CCR capability. Take the method and limits from the design standard and project specification. See the AGL toolkit.

Airport Lighting Automation

Airport lighting automation through the ALCMS lets operators call up preset lighting scenes.

Airport Lighting Asset Management

Airport lighting asset management tracks each fixture, transformer and CCR with its history.

Airport Lighting Equipment List & Spare Parts

Keep an airport lighting equipment list and stock airport lighting spare parts such as lamps or LED modules, transformers, connectors and splice kits.

Test Equipment & Reports

Airport Light Testing Equipment

Airport light testing equipment includes insulation resistance testers, loop resistance meters, series current meters, hi-pot testers and photometers, all in calibration.

Airport Lighting Acceptance Test

An airport lighting acceptance test checks installed work against the specification before handover.

Airport Lighting Commissioning Test

An airport lighting commissioning test confirms circuits, controls and light output together.

Airport Lighting Periodic Testing

Airport lighting periodic testing repeats key checks at intervals set by the maintenance programme.

Airport Lighting Test Report & AGL Test Report

Results go into an airport lighting test report, also called an AGL test report, listing the circuit, instrument, date, result, acceptance criterion and signatory. See AGL testing & inspection.

Electrical Tests

Airport Lighting Insulation Test

An airport lighting insulation test measures insulation resistance of the series circuit and equipment to earth.

Airport Lighting Continuity Test

An airport lighting continuity test confirms the series loop is unbroken.

Airport Lighting Resistance Test

An airport lighting resistance test measures loop and earth resistance.

Isolating Transformer Resistance Test

An isolating transformer resistance test measures the primary and secondary winding resistance with the circuit isolated and the transformer disconnected, and compares it with the manufacturer's values or earlier records.

Airport Lighting Voltage Test

An airport lighting voltage test checks voltage withstand and output voltage under load.

Airport Lighting Current Test

An airport lighting current test checks series current at each brightness step against the CCR step values; a five-step CCR commonly runs from 2.8 A to 6.6 A.

Circuit Faults

Airport Lighting Open Circuit

An airport lighting open circuit breaks the series loop, and the CCR open-circuit protection normally shuts the regulator down.

Airport Lighting Short Circuit & Earth Fault

An airport lighting short circuit or earth fault usually appears as falling insulation resistance and may trip the CCR.

Airport Lighting Low Current

Airport lighting low current gives dim lights and points to CCR output, a shorted section or the wrong brightness step.

Airport Lighting High Current

Airport lighting high current gives over-bright lights and shorter lamp life, so check CCR regulation and the meter.

Airport Lighting Flickering

Airport lighting flickering often comes from loose or corroded connectors, an intermittent open circuit, a failing lamp or LED driver, or moisture.

Light Bases & Mounting

Airport Light Base

An airport light base (base can) houses an in-pavement fixture and fixes its position.

Airport Light Base Cover

The airport light base cover protects the opening when the fixture is removed.

Airport Light Frangible Coupling

Elevated lights use an airport light frangible coupling that breaks cleanly on impact.

Low Impact Resistant Structure

The low impact resistant structure requirement applies the same idea to approach-light and similar supports.

Airport Lighting Splice Kit

An airport lighting splice kit joins cable in the field.

L-823 Airport Lighting Connectors

L-823 airport lighting connectors are the plug and receptacle that connect fixtures and transformers. See the AGL component library.

Control & Monitoring Equipment

Airport Lighting Photocell

The airport lighting photocell senses ambient light so the system can select day or night settings.

Airport Lighting Relay & Airport Lighting Contactor

An airport lighting relay and airport lighting contactor switch power to circuits or circuit sections.

Airport Lighting Monitor

The airport lighting monitor reports regulator and circuit status to the control system.

Airport Lighting Isolation Monitor

The airport lighting isolation monitor tracks insulation of the series circuit to earth, giving early warning before a ground fault.

Radio & Remote Control

Pilot Controlled Lighting

Pilot controlled lighting lets pilots switch on or change the intensity of airfield lights by keying the aircraft radio on the aerodrome frequency.

L-854 Radio Control

L-854 radio control equipment receives those signals, and L-854 airport lighting control then operates the lighting circuits.

Airport Lighting Radio Control & Radio Control Airport Lighting

Airport lighting radio control and radio control airport lighting are common where the tower is closed or absent.

Airport Lighting Remote Control

Airport lighting remote control from a tower or remote panel normally runs through the ALCMS.

Rapid Exit, Taxiway & Approach Categories

RETL Airport Lighting & RETL Lights

RETL airport lighting refers to rapid exit taxiway lights (ICAO calls them rapid exit taxiway indicator lights), a yellow light pattern that gives pilots distance-to-go information to a rapid exit taxiway. RETL lights are used on runways with low-visibility operations or high traffic.

Taxiway Lighting ICAO

Taxiway lighting ICAO requirements come from Annex 14. In general, taxiway centreline lights are green and taxiway edge lights are blue; confirm colour, spacing and intensity against the current edition and the national regulation. See Taxiway Lighting Systems.

Precision Approach Category Lighting

Precision approach category lighting scales with the approach category: CAT I systems, then CAT II and III systems with additional lights near the threshold and higher serviceability expectations.

Q14How does airfield lighting work?

Airfield lighting is powered through series circuits. A constant current regulator (CCR) supplies a fixed current to a loop of cable, and an isolating transformer at each light feeds the lamp or LED fixture. Brightness is set by changing the CCR current step, and an ALCMS or control panel switches circuits and monitors their status.

Q15How does ALCMS control airport lights?

The ALCMS sends commands to the CCRs and switching equipment over a control network. Operators select circuits, lighting scenes and brightness steps, and the system returns status and alarms from regulators and circuits so faults are visible at the control position.

Q16

How is airport lighting tested?

In stages: visual and mechanical checks, electrical tests on the series circuit (insulation resistance, continuity, loop resistance, current and voltage), functional tests of control and monitoring, and photometric checks of light output. Results are recorded in a test report.

Q17

What tests are done on AGL?

Typical tests are acceptance tests before handover, commissioning tests of circuits, controls and light output together, and periodic tests during service. The electrical tests include insulation, continuity, resistance, voltage and current checks, with photometry for light output.

Q18What is airport lighting insulation testing?

It measures the insulation resistance of cable, connectors, transformers and fixtures to earth, normally with an insulation resistance tester. Falling values warn of moisture or damage before they cause an earth fault.

Q19

What is airport lighting continuity testing?

It confirms the series loop is unbroken from the CCR through every isolating transformer and connector and back, so the circuit can carry its rated current. A break shows up as an open circuit.

Q20

What is airport lighting acceptance testing?

Acceptance testing checks the installed system against the specification before handover. It combines visual checks, electrical tests, control and monitoring checks and, where required, photometry, with results recorded and witnessed.

Q21

What is an airport lighting test report?

It is the record of a test: the circuit or equipment tested, the date, the instruments and their calibration, the results, the acceptance criteria, the pass or fail decision and the signatures of those who performed and witnessed the test.

Q22What is an AGL commissioning procedure?

It is the ordered set of checks that brings a new or modified system into service: review of documents, visual and installation checks, de-energised electrical tests, energisation and current-step checks, control and monitoring tests, photometry where required, then handover documents.

Q23How are airport lighting systems maintained?

Through preventive maintenance (inspection, cleaning, replacement of worn lamps or LED modules, and periodic electrical and photometric tests) and corrective maintenance for faults, with records kept for each circuit and asset. The AGL maintenance handbook covers the strategies in more detail.

Q24How often should airport lighting be inspected?

Frequency is set by the aerodrome maintenance programme and the applicable regulator. In general there are regular visual checks, with electrical and photometric checks at longer intervals; follow ICAO guidance and the DGCA and aerodrome manual requirements for your airport.

Q25

What is airport light luminous intensity?

It is the amount of light a fixture emits in a given direction, measured in candela. AGL specifications set minimum intensities within defined angles, usually shown on isocandela diagrams.

Q26

What is airport light beam angle?

It is the angular width over which the fixture emits light, set by its optics. Standards define the required beam through horizontal and vertical angles and minimum intensity within them, so the pilot sees the light from the intended approach or taxi directions.

Q27

What is the difference between LED and halogen airport lights?

Halogen fixtures use a tungsten-halogen lamp that dims with age and fails suddenly. LED fixtures use LED modules and a driver, draw less power and last longer, but they need checking for CCR and series-circuit compatibility. Both must meet the photometric requirements of the specification.

Q28

How are inset airport lights installed?

The light base is set at the surveyed position and level in the pavement, cable and conduit are brought into it, the isolating transformer and connectors are fitted, and the fixture is fastened with the specified torque and seals. Alignment and testing follow, all per the manufacturer and project specification.

Q29

How are elevated airport lights installed?

The fixture is mounted on a base or stake through a frangible coupling at the specified height and alignment, connected through its isolating transformer and connectors, and then checked for aim and electrical performance.

Q30What is an airport light base?

It is the base can that is set in the pavement or ground to house and position a light fixture and its connections. Bases are covered by the light base standards, and a base cover protects the opening when the fixture is not fitted.

Q31What is airport lighting redundancy?

It means a single failure should not remove visual guidance. Common measures are interleaved circuits so alternate lights sit on different series circuits, standby CCRs, a secondary power supply and monitoring that reports faults. The level required depends on runway type and approach category.

Q32

When is an AGL NOTAM required?

When lighting is unserviceable or changed in a way that affects aircraft operations, such as runway lights, approach lights or PAPI out of service. The aerodrome operator issues it under the AIP and DGCA procedures, so check the applicable rules for your airport.
