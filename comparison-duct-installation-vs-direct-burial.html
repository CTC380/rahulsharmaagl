AGL Cable Installation Comparison

Duct Installation VS Direct Burial

Two Ways to Get an AGL Series Cable Into the Ground — duct installation places the cable inside a protective conduit or duct, while direct burial places a suitably rated cable directly in the ground with appropriate bedding and protective layers. The choice affects protection, maintainability, replacement and lifecycle cost.

02 · Basic Physical Difference

What's Actually in the Ground

Duct Installation

The duct forms a physical pathway around the cable. The cable sits inside the duct rather than in direct contact with the surrounding soil, and can usually be pulled through the duct during or after construction.

Direct Burial

The cable is installed directly into the prepared ground and surrounded by bedding and backfill, with no continuous conduit around it — protection is provided by the bedding, cover and cable construction instead.

Easy memory: Duct installation = cable inside protection. Direct burial = protection around the route, not necessarily a continuous conduit around the cable itself.

04 · Mechanical Protection

What Protects the Cable

Duct Installation

The duct can provide additional protection against:

  • Soil movement
  • Minor excavation damage
  • Mechanical contact
  • External loading, depending on duct type and installation

Direct Burial

Protection depends on:

  • Burial depth
  • Cable construction
  • Bedding material
  • Protective slabs or covers
  • Warning tape
  • Soil conditions

06 · Water Considerations

Managing Water Around the Cable

Duct Installation

A common misconception is that a duct is always dry. Ducts can experience:

  • Water ingress
  • Condensation
  • Water accumulation

Therefore, drainage and sealing arrangements can be important.

Direct Burial

The cable insulation and associated joints must be suitable for the expected underground environmental conditions, since the cable sits in direct contact with groundwater and soil moisture.

07 · AGL Application

Where This Fits in an Airfield Ground Lighting System

A simplified airfield ground-lighting arrangement runs from the CCR, along the primary series cable, through either a duct route or a direct-buried route, to the isolation transformer and then the AGL fixture.

Circuit path: CCR → primary series cable → duct route or direct burial → isolation transformer → AGL fixture.

For AGL installations, selection depends on the applicable system design and the requirements for series cable protection, pavement crossings, maintenance access, drainage, future expansion and airfield operational constraints.

Thermal / ampacity note: A cable's current-carrying capacity is not identical in the two installation methods. Direct burial normally allows better heat dissipation into the surrounding soil, while a duct-enclosed cable is separated from the soil by an air gap that is a poorer heat conductor — so duct-installed cable ampacity is typically derated relative to the same cable direct-buried, per the applicable cable ampacity tables/standard. This should be checked against the manufacturer's rating and the governing cable-sizing standard, not assumed equal between the two methods.

08 · Pavement Crossings

Beneath Runways, Taxiways and Aprons

Duct Installation

Ducting can provide a defined route below or through pavement areas and can assist with future cable work without disturbing the pavement itself.

Direct Burial

Future repair beneath pavement can require excavation and pavement restoration, so maintainability can be a significant factor for direct-buried crossings.

12 · Selection Logic

Which One Fits the Project?

Key question: Is future cable replacement or expansion important? If yes, a duct system may be preferred. If no, direct burial may be suitable.

Also weigh:

  • Pavement crossings
  • Mechanical protection needs
  • Drainage
  • Soil conditions
  • Future expansion
  • Maintenance access
  • Applicable AGL standards
13 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: Duct installation means no excavation is ever required again
Fact

Not necessarily. Ducts can become blocked, damaged, flooded or collapsed, and access pits or excavation may still be required.

Myth: Direct-buried cable has no protection
Fact

Incorrect. A properly designed direct-burial installation can include warning tape, a protective cover, selected backfill and appropriate depth.

Myth: Duct installation is always better
Fact

Not necessarily. A duct system has advantages in protection and maintainability but adds cost, installation complexity, potential water-management issues and duct-maintenance requirements.

14 · Engineering Verdict

Duct Installation vs Direct Burial

Engineering Verdict

Duct installation places the cable inside a dedicated protective pathway, providing potential advantages in protection, replacement and future expansion.

Direct burial places the cable directly underground with appropriate bedding and protection, providing a simpler initial installation but more difficult future access.

DuctHigher initial cost + higher protection + easier future access, where duct capacity and access points exist.
Direct BurialLower initial cost + simpler installation + more disruptive future access.

For AGL systems, duct installation is generally preferred when future cable access, replacement, expansion, or protection beneath critical areas is important, while direct burial can be suitable for simpler permanent routes where future excavation is acceptable and the cable and installation method are designed for underground service.

15 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between duct installation and direct burial?

Duct installation places the cable inside a protective conduit, while direct burial places a suitably rated cable directly in the ground surrounded by bedding and backfill, with no continuous conduit around it.

Is duct installation always more expensive than direct burial?

Initial installation cost is generally higher for duct systems due to the duct material, joints and access points involved, but lower initial cost for direct burial does not necessarily mean lower lifecycle cost.

Does a duct guarantee the cable stays dry?

No. Ducts can experience water ingress, condensation and water accumulation, so drainage and sealing arrangements can be important in a duct system.

Is direct-buried cable unprotected?

No. A properly designed direct-burial installation can include warning tape, a protective cover, selected backfill and appropriate burial depth.

Which method is easier to maintain long term?

Duct systems can offer easier cable replacement and route expansion where spare duct capacity and access points exist, while direct-buried routes usually require excavation for fault repair or replacement.

Which method is preferred beneath pavement crossings?

Ducting can provide a defined route below or through pavement and can assist with future cable work without disturbing the pavement, so it is often favoured for critical crossings.

DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Do not select a cable installation method from a generic web comparison. The final installation drawing should always be developed from the current governing standard, approved airport design, cable route and project-specific requirements.

Duct installation is not automatically the safer choice, and direct burial is not automatically the cheaper one over the life of the system — the right method depends on protection needs, maintenance access, pavement crossings and future expansion plans.
