The Fact Installation & construction

Transformer-pit installation is setting the below-ground pit that will hold an isolation transformer. The pit is sited from the design, set on a firm, free-draining base, fitted with cable entries and a drain, and finished with a lid level with the surrounding surface.

A pit that is out of level, badly drained or left with a loose lid becomes a problem for years: water stands around the transformer and connectors, and a lid that is high or low is a hazard to aircraft and vehicles. Get the entries in the right places before backfilling, because moving them afterwards means digging again. Treat any cable in or near the pit as live until the circuit has been isolated, locked out and proved dead, never open a live series circuit, keep debris off the airfield surface and work to the airport’s permit-to-work.

  1. Set out. Mark the pit position from the design and survey, clear of existing services.
  2. Excavate and bed. Dig to the design size and depth and form a firm, free-draining base.
  3. Set the pit. Place it level, at the right height, with the lid finishing flush.
  4. Form the entries. Position and seal the primary and secondary entries as the design shows.
  5. Connect the drain. Provide the drain path or outlet and confirm it is clear.
  6. Backfill and check. Backfill as specified, check the lid fits, and clear all debris.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is transformer-pit installation in airfield ground lighting?

Transformer-pit installation is setting the below-ground pit that will hold an isolation transformer: siting it from the design, forming a firm free-draining base, fitting the cable entries and a drain, and finishing with a lid level with the surrounding surface.

Q2Why is the isolation transformer put in a pit?

The pit keeps the transformer and its connectors protected, dry and accessible, and keeps them out of the way of aircraft and vehicles. It also lets maintenance staff reach the transformer for testing or replacement without digging up the cables.

Q3How is the position of the pit decided?

From the design layout. The pit is normally set close to the light it supplies so the secondary cable stays short, clear of existing services and where the lid can be reached safely. Set it out and mark it from the survey rather than choosing the position on site.

Q4Why does the pit need drainage?

Water gets into pits through the lid, the cable entries and the ground around them. If it cannot drain it stands around the transformer and connectors and can cause insulation and earth faults. The pit sits on a firm free-draining base, with a drain path or outlet as the design specifies.

Q5What matters about the lid and finished level?

The lid must finish at the correct level with the surrounding surface and be rated for the loads it may see, as the design specifies. A lid that is high, low or loose is a hazard to aircraft and vehicles and lets water and debris in. Check it fits and is secured before handing the area back.

Q6What should be checked before the pit is handed on for the transformer?

Check position and level against the design, a firm base and a working drain, cable entries in the right places and sealed, backfill placed and compacted as specified, a lid that fits flush, and the area clear of debris. Treat any cable in or near the pit as live until the circuit has been isolated, locked out and proved dead.
