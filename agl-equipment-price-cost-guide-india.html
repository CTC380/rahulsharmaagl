AGL EQUIPMENT PRICE AND PROJECT COST GUIDE FOR INDIA

Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) project costs depend on the airport layout, lighting system configuration, equipment ratings, technical specifications, installation conditions and testing requirements.

A reliable AGL cost estimate must cover the complete project scope—not just the purchase price of runway lights or individual electrical components. Equipment supply, cables, connectors, isolating transformers, Constant Current Regulators (CCRs), civil works, control-system integration, testing, commissioning and documentation can all contribute to the final project cost.

Pricing note: There is no single equipment price or standard project cost applicable to every airport. Prices must be obtained against a defined technical specification, quantity, delivery location and scope of supply. This guide explains how to estimate and compare costs; it does not present unverified prices as current market quotations.

The cost of an AGL system is influenced by technical, commercial, installation and operational factors.

Equipment Cost Versus Project Cost

It is important to distinguish between these three commercial values:

Equipment supply cost

The price of the specified equipment and accessories supplied by the vendor.

Landed supply cost

The equipment supply cost adjusted for applicable freight, packing, handling, taxes and other relevant charges, according to the quotation terms.

Total project cost

The combined cost of supply, installation, civil and electrical works, integration, testing, commissioning, documentation and other items included in the approved project scope.

Remember: a low equipment price does not necessarily mean a low installed project cost.

What Is Included in the Cost of Runway Lighting?

A complete runway lighting estimate may include:

  • Runway edge light fittings.
  • Threshold and runway-end lighting equipment, where required by the design.
  • Runway centreline and touchdown zone lighting, where applicable.
  • Isolating transformers and compatible connectors.
  • Primary cables and associated accessories.
  • Secondary cables and connections, as applicable.

The exact scope will depend on the approved runway design and operational requirements.

03 · Taxiway & Approach

Taxiway Lighting and Approach Lighting Costs

Taxiway and approach lighting systems have different layouts and equipment requirements. Their costs should be estimated separately and then consolidated into the overall AGL project budget.

Taxiway Lighting Cost

The taxiway lighting budget may include:

  • Taxiway edge lights.
  • Taxiway centreline lights, where required.
  • Taxiway guidance signs.
  • Runway guard lights, where specified.
  • Stop bars and associated control interfaces, where specified.
  • Lead-on, lead-off or other installed visual aids, where applicable.
  • Isolating transformers, cables, connectors and associated equipment.
  • CCRs and circuit-control equipment.
  • Pits, ducting, foundations and pavement interfaces.
  • Installation, testing and commissioning.

The number of fittings should be calculated from the approved taxiway layout, equipment schedules and applicable design requirements rather than from a general cost-per-kilometre assumption. See also Taxiway Lighting in India.

Approach Lighting System Cost

Approach lighting project costs depend on the approved system configuration, installed light types, structures, foundations, electrical infrastructure and site conditions. Potential cost components include:

  • Approach light fittings.
  • Supporting structures and mounting arrangements.
  • Foundations and civil works.
  • Cable routing and electrical connections.
  • Transformers and associated circuit equipment.
  • CCRs and control interfaces.
  • Required monitoring and system integration.
  • Installation, inspection and commissioning.
  • Photometric testing and other specified acceptance activities.

Approach lighting costs can differ significantly between projects because the system layout, terrain, civil requirements and equipment configuration may be different.

PAPI System Cost

Precision Approach Path Indicator (PAPI) system pricing should be evaluated against the specified configuration and its full installation requirements. Consider the following:

  • PAPI unit configuration and required visual performance.
  • Electrical supply and circuit arrangement.
  • Mounting and foundation requirements.
  • Control and monitoring interfaces, where specified.
  • Cable routing and associated electrical works.
  • Alignment and adjustment requirements.
  • Prescribed testing and commissioning.
  • Required documentation and acceptance records.

Compare PAPI quotations on an equivalent scope, including all equipment and installation items required by the project. More on the system: PAPI in India.

04 · Electrical & Control

CCR, Isolating Transformer, Cable and ALCMS Costs

AGL electrical and control equipment should be priced against the same technical requirements before suppliers are compared.

4.1 Constant Current Regulator (CCR) Price

CCR pricing is influenced by the regulator’s rating, configuration, output circuits and control requirements.

Cost factorInformation required
Output ratingRequired rating and associated design requirements
Circuit configurationNumber and arrangement of required output circuits
Brightness controlRequired control steps and approved operating functions
ProtectionRequired protective functions and system interfaces
Remote controlRequired local and remote operating arrangements
MonitoringRequired alarms, indications and feedback
Communication interfacesRequired connections to the airport control and monitoring system
DocumentationDrawings, manuals, test records and specified supporting documents
Site servicesInstallation, testing, commissioning and support scope

When comparing CCR prices, confirm that all suppliers have quoted the same essential ratings, control functions and integration scope.

4.2 Airfield Lighting Isolating Transformer Cost

An airfield lighting isolating transformer is selected according to the required electrical ratings, equipment compatibility and installation application. Key pricing factors include:

  • Required wattage rating.
  • Primary and secondary current ratings.
  • Transformer construction and insulation arrangement.
  • Connector type and configuration.
  • Physical and environmental requirements.
  • Applicable product specifications and approvals.
  • Required routine or type-test documentation, as specified.
  • Packaging, delivery and warranty conditions.

The project schedule should identify each transformer rating and quantity separately. Combining different transformer ratings under one generic BOQ item may lead to inaccurate quotations. Technical background: Airport Isolating Transformer.

4.3 Airfield Lighting Cable Price

AGL cable pricing depends on the cable specification and the quantity required for the designed installation. Important cost drivers include:

  • Cable type and construction.
  • Conductor material and cross-sectional area.
  • Voltage rating and insulation requirements.
  • Cable length and drum quantities.
  • Installation route and access conditions.
  • Required joints, terminations and accessories.
  • Delivery and handling requirements.
  • Specified testing and documentation.

Calculate cable quantities using the approved circuit layout and routing details. Include only justified, documented allowances for route changes, termination requirements and installation waste.

4.4 ALCMS Cost

An Airfield Lighting Control and Monitoring System (ALCMS) may include hardware, software, communications, operator interfaces and integration with other airport systems. Its cost depends on:

  • Number and type of controlled circuits.
  • System architecture and redundancy requirements.
  • Operator workstations and interfaces.
  • Local and remote control requirements.
  • Monitoring, alarms and status indications.
  • Communications infrastructure and interfaces.
  • Software licensing and configuration.
  • Cybersecurity and access-control requirements specified for the project.
  • Integration with existing or new airport systems.
  • Factory and site acceptance activities, where required.
  • Training, documentation, maintenance and support.

For extensions to existing systems, assess compatibility with installed hardware, software, communication protocols, configuration and approved interfaces before finalising the budget.

Prepare a Technical and Commercial Comparison

A useful comparison sheet should have separate sections for:

Technical evaluation

Compliance, deviations, equipment configuration, required documentation and suitability for the specified application.

Commercial evaluation

Unit rates, quantity, total amount, taxes, freight, payment terms, delivery time, warranty and exclusions.

Risk evaluation

Long-lead items, compatibility with existing equipment, dependence on proprietary interfaces, spare-parts availability and unresolved technical issues.

Where a supplier offers an alternative product, record it as a deviation or alternative proposal and evaluate it through the project’s approved technical review process. Supplier-selection guidance: AGL Suppliers in India.

07 · Scope of Supply

Supply-Only Cost Versus SITC Project Cost

The scope of supply must be clear before a cost estimate is approved.

Supply-only quotation

Generally covers the specified equipment and the accessories explicitly included by the supplier.

Supply, installation, testing and commissioning (SITC)

Includes the agreed supply scope together with the specified installation, testing and commissioning activities.

An SITC quotation may also include civil works, control-system integration, temporary works, removal of existing equipment, disposal, training and documentation—but only when these activities are included in the quotation or contract.

Questions to Ask Before Comparing Quotations

  • Are the offered quantities based on the latest approved BOQ?
  • Are cables and connectors included or excluded?
  • Are installation and civil works included?
  • Who is responsible for integration with the existing CCR or ALCMS?
  • Which tests and reports are included?
  • Are factory or site witness tests required?
  • Are taxes, packing, freight and unloading included?
  • Are training, commissioning support and spare parts included?
  • Are existing equipment removal and site restoration included?
  • What assumptions or site conditions could affect the final cost?

Record the answers in the commercial comparison so that the selected offer covers the intended scope. Installation context: AGL Installation in India.

08 · Lifecycle Cost

Lifecycle Cost: Purchase Price Is Not the Whole Cost

The lowest initial quotation may not deliver the best long-term value. A lifecycle assessment considers both initial expenditure and the expected costs of owning and maintaining the equipment.

Lifecycle Cost Factors

FactorEvaluation question
Energy consumptionWhat is the expected energy demand under the specified operating profile?
MaintenanceWhat inspections, cleaning, adjustments or servicing are required?
Spare partsAre compatible replacements available when needed?
RepairabilityCan components be serviced, repaired or replaced efficiently?
WarrantyWhat failures and support activities are covered?
Product lifeWhat service-life assumptions are supported by the manufacturer?
Downtime riskWhat operational and maintenance implications may result from a failure?
CompatibilityWill the equipment work with the existing electrical and control infrastructure?
Technical supportWhat manufacturer or supplier support is available?
ObsolescenceIs the product range supported, and are future replacements likely to be available?

Avoid lifecycle comparisons based only on general service-life or energy-saving claims. Use documented product data and project-specific operating assumptions.

Evaluating an LED Conversion Project

When assessing an LED retrofit, consider:

  • Whether the proposed equipment meets the applicable technical requirements.
  • Compatibility with the existing circuits, CCRs, transformers and control system.
  • Any changes needed to cables, connectors, foundations or mounting arrangements.
  • Installation, integration, testing and commissioning costs.
  • Expected energy consumption based on the intended operating conditions.
  • Maintenance requirements and replacement-part availability.
  • Any required photometric verification and acceptance testing.
Retrofit caution: energy or maintenance savings should be calculated from defensible baseline data and a clearly stated operating profile. A retrofit should not be assumed compatible solely because the replacement fixture fits the existing mounting arrangement. See LED Airfield Lighting in India.

10 · Pitfalls

Common Mistakes in AGL Project Costing

Avoid the following budgeting and procurement errors:

Estimating only fixture prices.

A lighting fixture price does not represent the full cost of a lighting circuit or installed system.

Using incomplete BOQ quantities.

Missing transformers, connectors, cable accessories or control interfaces can create budget gaps.

Comparing technically different products.

Ratings, product configurations, accessories and required documentation should be aligned before prices are compared.

Ignoring site conditions.

Access restrictions, working windows, pavement conditions and coordination requirements can affect installation cost.

Leaving tests and documentation out of scope.

Required inspection, test, commissioning and acceptance documents should be included in the estimate.

Double-counting included items.

Check whether the vendor’s unit rate already includes accessories, freight, installation or other costs.

Using outdated quotations without review.

Confirm quotation validity, product availability, revised specifications and commercial conditions before relying on old prices.

Assuming an LED retrofit is a direct replacement.

Verify compatibility with the existing electrical and control infrastructure and include necessary integration and acceptance activities.

Selecting only by lowest price.

Evaluate technical compliance, delivery, warranty, lifecycle cost and support alongside the initial price.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the price of an airfield lighting system in India?
There is no single price applicable to every airport. The cost depends on the system configuration, equipment types, quantities, technical specification, site conditions and installation scope. Obtain quotations against a defined BOQ and scope before fixing a project budget.
What is the price of a runway edge light?
Runway edge light pricing depends on the specified fixture, light source, optical performance, mounting arrangement, electrical requirements, required documentation and order quantity. Compare equivalent configurations rather than relying on a generic unit price.
What determines the cost of a CCR for an airport?
CCR cost depends on the required rating, output circuit configuration, brightness control, protection, monitoring, communication interfaces and documentation. Installation, integration, testing and commissioning may add to the total cost if they are included in the project scope.
How much does an airfield lighting isolating transformer cost?
Transformer prices depend on the required wattage and current ratings, construction, insulation arrangement, connectors, specified testing and commercial terms. Different transformer ratings and configurations should be quoted as separate BOQ items where appropriate.
What determines the cost of an ALCMS?
The number and type of controlled circuits, system architecture, operator interfaces, monitoring features, communications, software, integration and commissioning requirements are important cost drivers.
How can I estimate a complete AGL project cost?
Start with the approved layout and BOQ. Calculate equipment and accessory costs, then include cables, civil works, installation, control integration, testing, commissioning, logistics, taxes and other applicable costs. Document assumptions, exclusions and risk allowances.
How can I reduce airfield lighting project costs?
Improve BOQ accuracy, coordinate electrical and civil interfaces early, compare technically equivalent quotations, plan installation efficiently and evaluate lifecycle costs. Cost optimisation should not compromise mandatory performance, specification compliance or safety requirements.
Should I use supply-only rates or SITC rates for budgeting?
Use the rate basis that matches the intended procurement scope. Supply-only rates cover the quoted equipment scope, while SITC rates should cover the agreed supply, installation, testing and commissioning activities. Check inclusions and exclusions before comparing totals.
What drives the taxiway light price and the PAPI system price?
The taxiway light price and the PAPI system price depend on the fixture type (elevated or inset), LED or lamp-based design, standards compliance, optics, quantity and what is included, such as transformers, connectors or mounting. A PAPI system price also reflects the number of units, the power supply and calibration or aiming support. Ask suppliers for itemised, written quotations rather than relying on a single quoted figure.
What affects the CCR price for airport projects?
The CCR price for airport projects depends on the kVA rating, number of brightness steps, control and monitoring interface, standards compliance, efficiency and whether the quotation includes commissioning and spares. Compare like-for-like specifications and check which options are included.
What is included in airfield lighting cost, airfield ground lighting cost and airport lighting cost?
Airfield lighting cost, airfield ground lighting cost and airport lighting cost all describe the total spend on the lighting system. That can include equipment, installation, testing, commissioning, documentation and sometimes civil works and power supply. The scope drives the number, so define it before comparing estimates.
What goes into the airfield lighting installation cost and the airport lighting project cost?
The airfield lighting installation cost covers labour, cable laying, civil and duct works, equipment fixing and connection, and the testing and commissioning needed to put the system into service. The airport lighting project cost adds equipment, design, project management, documentation and contingency. Confirm whether site access, airside restrictions and night working are priced.
How do you estimate the airfield lighting lifecycle cost?
Airfield lighting lifecycle cost combines purchase and installation cost with energy, maintenance, spares, replacement and downtime over the expected life. A lower purchase price can cost more over time if the equipment uses more energy or needs frequent maintenance.
What sets the constant current regulator cost and the airfield lighting isolation transformer cost?
Constant current regulator cost depends on rating, steps, control interface and standards. Airfield lighting isolation transformer cost depends on the power rating, connector type, quantity and compliance, and it is multiplied by the number of lights on the circuit. Ask for unit and total prices for each.
What affects the airfield lighting cable cost and the airfield lighting control system cost?
Airfield lighting cable cost depends on conductor size, insulation and voltage rating, route length and installation method. Airfield lighting control system cost depends on the scale of the system, controllers, workstations, redundancy, software and integration with CCRs and the tower. Specify performance requirements so quotations can be compared fairly.
How is the runway edge lights cost worked out?
The runway edge lights cost comes from the fixture price (elevated or inset, LED or lamp-based), the number of lights along the runway, the isolating transformers and connectors, cable and installation. A project estimate multiplies unit prices by quantities from the lighting layout, then adds installation, testing and contingency.
