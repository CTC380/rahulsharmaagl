Approach & Landing Systems Comparison

BARRETTE VS CROSSBAR

In an approach lighting system, a centreline barrette and a crossbar are both transverse groups of lights, but they have different locations, sizes, and functions. A centreline barrette is the repeated light bar forming the approach centreline; a crossbar is a wider transverse reference bar used at a specified distance from the threshold.

02 · Definitions

What Are They?

Centreline Barrette

A barrette is a group of closely spaced lights arranged transversely. In an ALSF-2 system, the centreline light bars are installed at regular intervals along the extended runway centreline. FAA specifies five equally spaced lights in the standard ALSF-2 centreline light bar arrangement.

03 · Key Difference

Alignment Reference vs Distance Reference

Centreline Barrette

Short + repeated + centreline.

"This is the runway centreline."

Crossbar

Wide + specific station + horizontal reference.

"This is a significant distance/reference point along the approach."

04 · ALSF-2 Example

Where the Difference Becomes Clear

The difference is especially clear in an ALSF-2. FAA specifies centreline light bars at 100-ft intervals, beginning 100 ft from the threshold and extending outward.

At the 1,000-ft station, the centreline bar is extended with additional lights on both sides to form a much wider 1,000-ft distance-marker crossbar. The ALSF-2 also has a 500-ft crossbar.

05 · Function

What Each One Does for the Pilot

Centreline Barrette Function

  • Runway alignment
  • Extended runway centreline reference
  • Approach direction
  • Lateral visual reference
  • Continuity of the approach-lighting pattern

Because multiple barrettes are positioned along the centreline, they form a continuous visual path toward the runway.

Crossbar Function

  • Horizontal visual reference
  • Distance reference
  • Enhanced conspicuity
  • Additional approach geometry
  • Assistance in judging the aircraft's position along the approach

The 1,000-ft bar is particularly important because its much greater width makes it visually distinctive.

09 · Barrette Terminology

Are All Barrettes Centreline Barrettes?

No. Barrette describes the physical arrangement of closely spaced lights. A barrette can be used for different purposes, including:

  • Centreline bar
  • Side-row bar
  • Other defined ALS elements

FAA's ALSF-2 description specifically identifies centreline light barrettes and side-row barrettes.

Barrette = physical light arrangement. Centreline barrette = barrette used on the approach centreline. Crossbar = larger transverse visual reference at a defined station.

10 · Terminology Care

Crossbar Is Not Simply a "Large Barrette"

They may look physically similar, but engineering terminology should be kept separate. A barrette describes the lighting element itself. A crossbar describes the functional/configurational element within the ALS.

For example, FAA specifically calls the 1,000-ft element the "1,000-foot distance marker crossbar."

11 · MALSR Example

A Useful Contrast

MALSR provides a useful contrast. FAA describes MALSR as having nine sets of five steady-burning light sources called light bars. Seven are located along the extended runway centreline at 200-ft intervals, while two are offset at the 1,000-ft bar.

Do not transfer the ALSF-2 500-ft/1,000-ft crossbar configuration to every ALS. Each ALS has its own defined configuration.

12 · Electrical Perspective

Electrical Considerations

Both centreline barrettes and crossbars normally contain multiple individual light fixtures. A simplified series-circuit arrangement may be:

CCR
 ↓
Primary Series Circuit
 ↓
Isolation Transformer
 ↓
Light Fixture

For a barrette: several fixtures → several isolation transformers → one defined lighting element. The crossbar may contain substantially more fixtures and therefore has a larger electrical load than a normal centreline barrette.

13 · Maintenance

Maintenance Difference

Centreline Barrette

Inspect:

  • Individual lamps/LEDs
  • Lens/prisms
  • Fixture alignment
  • Transformer
  • Wiring
  • Structural support

Crossbar

All of the above, plus:

  • Complete transverse alignment
  • Uniformity of the crossbar
  • Correct distance/station
  • Individual fixture orientation
  • Overall visual geometry

A missing fixture on a crossbar can be particularly noticeable because it disrupts the horizontal reference.

17 · Engineering Verdict

Barrette vs Crossbar — The Bottom Line

Engineering Verdict

A centreline barrette is a short transverse group of closely spaced lights repeated along the extended runway centreline to provide a stable alignment reference. A crossbar is a wider transverse lighting element installed at a defined distance from the threshold to provide a prominent horizontal and distance reference.

For ALSF-2, the FAA specifically identifies the 1,000-ft distance-marker crossbar and 500-ft crossbar, while the repeated centreline elements are the centreline light bars/barrettes.

BarretteShort + repeated + centreline.
CrossbarWide + specific distance + horizontal reference.

18 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Are a centreline barrette and a crossbar the same thing?

No. Both are transverse light arrangements, but their purpose and configuration are different — the barrette establishes alignment, the crossbar provides a distance/horizontal reference.

Does every approach lighting system have a 500-ft and 1,000-ft crossbar?

No. Those are configuration-specific — ALSF-2 explicitly has these crossbars, but this should not be assumed for every ALS.

Is every barrette located on the centreline?

No. ALSF-2 also uses side-row barrettes in addition to centreline barrettes.

Does the crossbar establish the runway centreline?

No. The centreline barrettes/light bars establish the longitudinal centreline; the crossbar provides a transverse reference.

How many lights are in a standard ALSF-2 centreline light bar vs the 1,000-ft crossbar?

The standard centreline light bar is approximately 13½ ft long with five equally spaced lights. The 1,000-ft distance-marker crossbar is approximately 100 ft wide and contains 21 lights.

Can I apply the ALSF-2 crossbar configuration to any approach lighting system?

No. Each ALS has its own defined configuration — for example, MALSR uses nine sets of five-light bars at different intervals rather than ALSF-2's 500-ft/1,000-ft crossbar arrangement.

19 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual airport project, verify the current applicable editions of the relevant standards before using this comparison for project design.

FAA Order 6850.2C / 6850.2B

Defines ALSF-2 centreline light bar spacing, the 500-ft and 1,000-ft distance-marker crossbars, and side-row barrette configuration.

AC 150/5300-18B, Part 2

General guidance defining a barrette as three or more closely spaced lights arranged transversely so that they appear as one short illuminated bar.

FAA VGLS — MALSR Reference

Describes MALSR's nine sets of five-light steady-burning light bars, spaced at 200-ft intervals with two offset at the 1,000-ft bar.

Final Engineering Note

Barrette and crossbar dimensions, light counts and spacing are configuration-specific to each ALS type. For final design, use the configuration drawings and dimensional criteria from the applicable current standard rather than a generic comparison page.

Barrette → short + repeated + centreline. Crossbar → wide + specific distance + horizontal reference.
