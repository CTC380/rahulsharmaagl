Preventive Maintenance

The routine, calendar-driven backbone of the handbook. Preventive maintenance is scheduled work performed regardless of condition — cleaning, tightening, alignment, and functional checks — carried out to prevent avoidable failure, detect deterioration early, and keep every AGL asset in reliable operating condition.

Philosophy: preventive maintenance is not simply "cleaning equipment." It addresses five asset conditions — electrical, mechanical, optical, environmental, and functional — and follows a fixed sequence from planning through to record and monitor, on every fixture, cable, connector, and control system in the system.

PM Frequency

Exact frequency is set by the airport's approved maintenance programme, equipment requirements, and operational criticality. A typical planning structure looks like this.

FrequencyTypical PM Scope
Daily / ShiftOperational condition and obvious defects
WeeklyCleaning, visual condition, and minor checks
MonthlyFixture / equipment detailed PM
QuarterlyEquipment and circuit condition checks
Half-yearlyDetailed mechanical / electrical PM
AnnualComprehensive PM and condition assessment
Event-basedAfter failure, lightning, flooding, construction, severe weather, or major maintenance

Pre-Maintenance Requirements

Before any preventive-maintenance task begins, four requirement groups must be closed out.

Work Planning

  • Identify equipment/asset and location
  • Identify circuit
  • Review previous maintenance history and defects
  • Confirm task, tools, spares, PPE, and test instruments

Operational Coordination

  • Obtain required work authorization
  • Coordinate with ATC / airport operations where applicable
  • Coordinate airside access
  • Establish work limits and communication arrangements

Safety

  • Identify electrical, movement, and FOD hazards
  • Identify excavation / pit hazards where applicable
  • Apply isolation / LOTO requirements where required
  • Verify equipment is safe to work on

Documentation

  • Date, time, asset ID, circuit ID, location
  • Maintenance team and work order / reference
  • Previous condition
  • Maintenance activity and parts used

Defect Handling During PM

When a defect is discovered mid-task, the same seven-step handling sequence applies across every topic in this division.

1
Identify

Determine the exact asset and defect.

2
Classify

Critical / Major / Minor / Observation.

3
Assess Impact

Safety, availability, function, photometry, mechanical or electrical integrity.

4
Correct

Repair or replace where authorized.

5
Verify

Confirm restoration.

6
Record

Update the maintenance record.

7
Escalate

Unresolved significant defects enter the corrective-maintenance process.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about preventive maintenance of AGL systems, based on the topics in this division.

12 QUESTIONS
Q1What is preventive maintenance in Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL)?

Preventive maintenance is scheduled, calendar-driven work performed regardless of asset condition. It covers cleaning, tightening, alignment and functional checks, and is carried out to prevent avoidable failure, detect deterioration early and keep every AGL asset in reliable operating condition.

Q2What does Division 01 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 01 contains 40 preventive-maintenance topics in four asset groups: Group A Runway Lighting (12 topics), Group B Taxiway Lighting (8 topics), Group C Other AGL Equipment (12 topics) and Group D Routine Maintenance Activities (8 topics). Group C includes PAPI / APAPI, airfield signs, CCRs, isolation transformers, primary cable, connectors, earthing, ALCMS, approach lighting, stop bars and base-cans.

Q3Is AGL preventive maintenance just cleaning the fixtures?

No. Preventive maintenance is not simply cleaning equipment. Each task is judged against five asset conditions: electrical, mechanical, optical, environmental and functional. It is applied to every fixture, cable, connector and control system in the AGL system.

Q4What is the standard sequence for an AGL preventive-maintenance task?

Every task follows the same ten-step sequence regardless of asset type: Plan, Isolate, Inspect, Clean, Adjust, Tighten, Test, Restore, Record and Monitor.

Q5How often should AGL preventive maintenance be performed?

The exact frequency is set by the airport's approved maintenance programme, the equipment requirements and operational criticality. A typical planning structure is daily or shift checks for operational condition and obvious defects, weekly cleaning and visual checks, monthly detailed fixture PM, quarterly circuit and equipment condition checks, half-yearly detailed mechanical and electrical PM, and an annual comprehensive PM and condition assessment. Event-based PM is also carried out after failure, lightning, flooding, construction, severe weather or major maintenance.

Q6What must be completed before starting AGL preventive maintenance?

Four requirement groups must be closed out first. Work planning covers identifying the asset, location and circuit, reviewing maintenance history, and confirming tools, spares, PPE and test instruments. Operational coordination covers work authorization, ATC or airport operations coordination, airside access, work limits and communications. Safety covers electrical, movement, FOD and pit hazards, plus isolation or LOTO where required. Documentation covers the date, time, asset ID, circuit ID, location, team, work order reference and previous condition.

Q7What does preventive maintenance of a runway edge light include?

It includes visually inspecting the fixture, cleaning the lens or prism, removing dirt and contamination, inspecting the housing, checking mounting and fasteners, verifying orientation and elevation, inspecting the frangible arrangement, checking the cable entry, secondary lead and connector, checking for moisture ingress and corrosion, checking optical condition and illumination, replacing defective components, and restoring the fixture and recording the work. The fixture is accepted when it is clean, secure, correctly oriented, free from unacceptable damage, properly connected, free from unacceptable moisture ingress and operational.

Q8Can lubricant be applied to AGL equipment during preventive maintenance?

Only where the manufacturer permits it, the component requires lubrication, the correct lubricant is specified and it will not contaminate optical or electrical components. Lubricant must never be applied indiscriminately to AGL equipment.

Q9How should fasteners be tightened during AGL preventive maintenance?

Confirm the correct hardware, inspect threads for corrosion or damage, tighten using the approved method, apply the specified torque where required, avoid overtightening and verify alignment after tightening. Any corrective action taken is recorded.

Q10How are defects found during preventive maintenance handled?

A seven-step sequence applies across every topic: identify the asset and defect, classify it as Critical, Major, Minor or Observation, assess the impact on safety, availability, function, photometry and mechanical or electrical integrity, correct it by repair or replacement where authorized, verify restoration, update the maintenance record, and escalate. Unresolved significant defects enter the corrective-maintenance process.

Q11What should an AGL preventive-maintenance record include?

The record should include the asset ID, equipment type, location and circuit; date, frequency, technician and supervisor; initial and final condition; work performed and parts replaced; test or functional result; defects found and their classification; corrective action and photographic evidence; and the next PM due date, remarks and sign-off.

Q12When does preventive maintenance lead to testing or corrective maintenance?

Preventive maintenance is mainly inspection, cleaning, tightening, alignment and functional checking. Where electrical testing is required, for example on primary cable or the earthing system, it follows the applicable approved test procedure separately. Deteriorated components, such as connectors, are assessed for replacement or corrective maintenance, and unresolved significant defects are escalated to the corrective-maintenance process.
