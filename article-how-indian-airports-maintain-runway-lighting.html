How Indian Airports Maintain Runway Lighting Systems for Safe Aircraft Operations

With CAT II and CAT III operations expanding across major Indian airports, the reliability of Airfield Ground Lighting has moved from a maintenance line item to a strategic operational priority. Here's how the engineering, testing, and field discipline behind it actually comes together.

RS

Rahul Sharma

Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) Specialist

August 18, 2026 8 min read

Runway Lighting Systems (RLS) are one of the most safety-critical infrastructures within an airport environment. They give pilots essential visual guidance during takeoff, landing, taxiing, and low-visibility operations — under day, night, and adverse weather conditions alike.

As India's aviation infrastructure expands, and CAT II and CAT III operations become more widespread at major airports, keeping this infrastructure reliable and maintainable has become a strategic operational priority rather than a routine maintenance task. This article walks through the engineering methodologies Indian airports use to maintain runway lighting in line with ICAO Annex 14, the ICAO Aerodrome Design Manual (Doc 9157, Part 5), FAA AC 150/5340, DGCA requirements, and AAI practice — architecture, maintenance philosophy, testing, failure analysis, reliability engineering, and lessons from real projects.

Key Takeaway

AGL reliability at Indian airports is not the product of any single practice. It comes from combining corrective, preventive, and predictive maintenance with rigorous testing, redundancy management, and disciplined workmanship — all measured against ICAO, FAA, DGCA, and AAI requirements.

01

Why Reliability Matters Now

Indian airports operate across a wide range of environmental conditions — dense fog in North India, heavy monsoon rainfall, high ambient temperatures, coastal corrosion, and dust and pollution exposure. Each of these can shorten the service life of AGL equipment and its supporting electrical infrastructure.

Runway lighting provides runway identification, landing alignment guidance, rollout guidance, taxi routing, runway incursion prevention, and visual support during low-visibility operations. An AGL failure carries real operational consequences:

  • Reduced airport operational capacity
  • Delayed aircraft movements
  • A NOTAM being issued
  • Restricted CAT operations
  • A potential safety hazard

For CAT III operations specifically, runway centreline lights and touchdown zone lights stop being supplementary aids and become essential operational elements.

02

Standards & Regulatory Framework

Indian AGL maintenance sits within a layered regulatory framework, combining international standards with Indian-specific requirements:

ICAO Annex 14 Vol. IEdge, threshold, end, centreline & TDZ lighting, stop bars, secondary power and switch-over times, serviceability levels (Ch. 8 & 10)
ICAO Doc 9157 Pt 5 & Doc 9137 Pt 9Electrical systems design philosophy; airport maintenance practices and inspection methodology
FAA AC 150/5340 & 150/5345 Series5340: design, installation & maintenance (e.g. AC 150/5340-26). 5345: equipment specifications – fixtures, CCRs, isolation transformers, connectors, signs
DGCA RequirementsCompliance with ICAO requirements at licensed aerodromes
AAI Engineering ManualsInspection intervals, maintenance procedures, spares & redundancy
BIS StandardsIS 694, IS 1554 and IS 7098 for cables; IS 12290 for isolating transformers

Compliance with this combined framework is what provides the engineering and operational basis for reliable AGL infrastructure — not any single standard on its own.

04

Three Maintenance Philosophies

Indian airports typically combine three maintenance approaches, weighted by system criticality, operational context, and available diagnostic capability:

01CorrectiveLamp, connector & transformer replacement after a detected failure
02PreventiveScheduled cleaning, torque checks, insulation & photometric testing
03PredictiveCCR trend analysis, IR trending, power-factor & waveform monitoring

Used together, these three approaches let maintenance teams handle immediate failures while steadily reducing the probability of unexpected outages.

05

Testing & Commissioning Requirements

Electrical functionality alone doesn't establish runway lighting performance — both electrical and photometric verification are required.

  • Insulation resistance: measured at the test voltage specified for the cable and circuit class. Commonly used reference values are above 50 MΩ for healthy circuits and below 10 MΩ as a trigger for investigation – always apply the limits in your project specification, AAI manual or manufacturer data, and trend readings over time
  • Series circuit continuity: cable continuity, connector integrity, and transformer connections
  • CCR output verification: current regulation, step transitions, harmonic distortion
  • Photometric testing: light intensity, beam spread, aiming and colour compliance using calibrated equipment; Annex 14 Chapter 10 calls for periodic in-service measurement on precision approach runways
  • Safety first: insulation-test voltages are hazardous. Isolate, lock out, prove dead and discharge each circuit under the aerodrome’s permit-to-work before connecting any test instrument

A circuit can pass every electrical test and still fail to deliver the light output pilots actually rely on — which is why photometric verification is treated as a separate step in its own right.

07

Reliability Engineering for CAT II/III

CAT II/III airports generally build in dual power sources, standby generators, UPS systems, redundant CCRs, interleaved (alternate) circuit arrangements so that one CCR failure does not blank an entire runway, and dual communication paths — resilience for when individual components fail. Annex 14 also sets minimum serviceability levels for lights on precision approach runways (for CAT II/III, at least 95% of lights serviceable in key elements, with no adjacent lights out); these are the regulatory floor, not a target.

Availability targets are set by the aerodrome operator in its reliability case (figures such as 99.9% or higher are common); they are met through maintenance intervention and redundancy management, not fixture count alone.

Even small failure percentages can create operational restrictions during CAT III conditions — which is exactly why reliability engineering matters most on the most critical circuits.

CAT II/III circuits are commonly tested more often than shown, so follow the aerodrome’s approved maintenance schedule and the AAI manual.

09

Field Lessons from Indian Airport Projects

Practical field experience across projects surfaces the same handful of issues repeatedly:

  • Water ingress remains a leading cause of inset fixture failures during monsoon seasons
  • Improper connector assembly causes intermittent faults that are genuinely difficult to diagnose
  • Cable insulation degradation can appear years before complete failure and is often detectable through insulation-resistance trend analysis
  • Contaminated fixture prisms reduce effective light output even when the electrical circuit is perfectly healthy
  • Many CCR alarms actually originate from field-side issues rather than the regulator itself

Troubleshooting has to consider the complete AGL circuit — not just the component that happened to raise the alarm.

These technologies are moving AGL maintenance steadily toward data-driven, condition-based approaches — but they extend the same three maintenance philosophies rather than replacing them.

Key Recommendations

A few practical priorities for Indian airport operators, drawn from this combined engineering and field experience:

  • Implement condition-based maintenance using real-time monitoring systems
  • Perform periodic insulation-resistance trend analysis, not just isolated measurements
  • Adopt LED lighting technology for new installations and upgrades, after confirming CCR compatibility, photometric compliance and monitoring behaviour
  • Maintain adequate redundancy and interleaved circuits for CAT II/III operations
  • Conduct annual photometric compliance surveys
  • Strengthen connector installation quality control
  • Develop airport-specific monsoon preparedness programs
  • Integrate predictive analytics into AGL maintenance planning

Conclusion

Runway lighting systems remain one of the most safety-critical electrical infrastructures within an airport environment — their performance directly shapes aircraft operational safety, airport capacity, regulatory compliance, and the success of Low Visibility Operations.

Across Indian airports, successful maintenance programs consistently rely on the same combination: preventive maintenance, predictive diagnostics, rigorous testing, skilled workmanship, and strict adherence to ICAO, FAA, DGCA, and AAI requirements. Airports that build structured, reliability-centered maintenance programs achieve higher system availability, fewer outages, and better operational resilience.

As Indian aviation keeps expanding, maintaining highly reliable runway lighting systems will stay fundamental to aerodrome safety and operational reliability.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does runway lighting reliability matter at Indian airports?

Indian airports face dense fog, heavy monsoon rainfall, high temperatures, coastal corrosion, and dust and pollution, each of which can shorten the life of AGL equipment. An AGL failure can reduce capacity, delay aircraft movements, lead to a NOTAM, restrict CAT operations and create a safety hazard. For CAT III operations, runway centreline and touchdown zone lights become essential operational elements.

Which standards frame AGL maintenance in India?

A layered framework: ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I (including serviceability levels and secondary power switch-over times), ICAO Doc 9157 Part 5 and Doc 9137 Part 9, the FAA AC 150/5340 and 5345 series, DGCA requirements, AAI engineering manuals and BIS standards (IS 694, IS 1554 and IS 7098 for cables, IS 12290 for isolating transformers). The article says reliability comes from this combined framework, not any single standard.

What are the three maintenance philosophies?

Corrective maintenance replaces lamps, connectors and transformers after a detected failure. Preventive maintenance covers scheduled cleaning, torque checks, and insulation and photometric testing. Predictive maintenance uses CCR trend analysis, IR trending, and power-factor and waveform monitoring. Indian airports typically combine all three, weighted by criticality, operational context and diagnostic capability.

What testing is required for runway lighting?

Both electrical and photometric verification: insulation resistance at the specified test voltage, series circuit continuity, CCR output verification (current regulation, step transitions, harmonic distortion) and photometric testing of intensity, beam spread, aiming and colour. The article gives commonly used insulation reference values (above 50 MΩ for healthy circuits, below 10 MΩ as a trigger for investigation) but says to always apply the limits in your project specification, AAI manual or manufacturer data. Test voltages are hazardous, so circuits must be isolated, locked out, proved dead and discharged under permit-to-work.

What are the main failure modes of AGL systems?

The article lists lamp or light source failure, water ingress, cable faults (from mechanical damage or insulation ageing), CCR failure, connector failure from poor installation, and isolating transformer failure from insulation breakdown or moisture ingress. Understanding root causes lets teams address the underlying problem rather than just replace failed parts.

How is reliability achieved for CAT II/III operations?

CAT II/III airports generally build in dual power sources, standby generators, UPS systems, redundant CCRs, interleaved circuit arrangements so one CCR failure does not blank an entire runway, and dual communication paths. Annex 14 also sets minimum serviceability levels for precision approach runways (for CAT II/III, at least 95% of lights serviceable in key elements, with no adjacent lights out), which are the regulatory floor rather than a target.

What does a preventive maintenance calendar look like?

As an indicative baseline: daily visual inspection and CCR alarm review, weekly fixture cleaning and monitoring verification, monthly circuit testing and connector inspection, quarterly insulation and earth-resistance testing, and annual photometric survey, comprehensive CCR servicing and cable diagnostics. The article says it must be tuned to airport procedures and criticality, and that CAT II/III circuits are commonly tested more often, so follow the approved maintenance schedule and the AAI manual.

What field lessons does the article highlight?

Water ingress is a leading cause of inset fixture failures in monsoon seasons, improper connector assembly causes intermittent faults that are hard to diagnose, cable insulation degradation can appear years before failure and can be detected by IR trend analysis, contaminated prisms reduce light output even when the circuit is healthy, and many CCR alarms originate from field-side issues rather than the regulator.
