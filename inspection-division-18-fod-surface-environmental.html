Division 18 of 25 · INS-FOD Series

FOD, Surface & Environmental Inspection

A standardized inspection methodology for identifying foreign object debris (FOD), pavement/surface deterioration, vegetation encroachment, water accumulation, contamination, drainage problems, wildlife-related damage, and weather-related impacts affecting AGL equipment and associated airfield infrastructure. Covers the area surrounding runway AGL, taxiway AGL, approach lighting, fixtures, bases/base cans, cable routes, pits, drainage interfaces, and associated airfield surfaces.

Initial Issue

Critical control — FOD and environmental conditions can directly affect AGL fixture integrity, visibility, drainage, electrical connections, pavement condition, aircraft/vehicle safety, and maintenance access. Findings that create an immediate operational or safety hazard shall be escalated in accordance with the applicable airport procedure.

Critical Operational Control

This division should operate on the principle: FIND → ASSESS → CONTROL → RECORD → VERIFY. Not every environmental observation is an AGL equipment defect, but environmental conditions can create or accelerate AGL defects.

Priority Attention Areas

  • FOD around runway and taxiway AGL
  • Loose stones, metal, hardware and maintenance debris
  • Standing water and flooding
  • Blocked drainage
  • Pavement settlement around fixtures
  • Vegetation affecting fixtures or drainage
  • Mud and dust contamination
  • Oil/fuel/chemical contamination
  • Wildlife-related damage
  • Storm and high-wind damage
  • Environmental conditions affecting optical, mechanical or electrical performance

Immediate Action / Escalation Conditions

The following should be considered for immediate action/escalation under the airport's applicable operational procedure, particularly when they affect an operational area:

  • Significant FOD
  • Flooding or extensive standing water
  • Damaged fixture creating an operational hazard
  • Major pavement failure around an AGL fixture
  • Severe fixture displacement
  • Significant structural damage
  • Exposed/damaged electrical infrastructure
  • Major oil/chemical contamination
  • Storm-related damage affecting airfield lighting
  • Any condition creating an immediate aircraft, vehicle, personnel, or electrical-safety concern

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about fod, surface & environmental inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook.

Q1What is Division 18 — FOD, Surface & Environmental Inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook?

Division 18 contains 92 published topics in 15 sections (A–O). It gives a standardized methodology for identifying foreign object debris, pavement and surface deterioration, vegetation encroachment, water accumulation, contamination, drainage problems, wildlife-related damage and weather-related impacts affecting AGL equipment and associated airfield infrastructure.

Q2Which areas and conditions does Division 18 cover?

It covers FOD around runway, taxiway and approach lighting, surface condition around fixtures, pavement settlement, vegetation encroachment, water accumulation and flooding, mud and debris, dust, oil and chemical contamination, drainage, wildlife-related damage, weather-related equipment condition, and the environmental impact on AGL performance.

Q3What is the operating principle of this division?

The division works on the sequence find, assess, control, record and verify. Not every environmental observation is an AGL equipment defect, but environmental conditions can create or accelerate AGL defects, so the inspection assesses the impact on equipment as well as the condition itself.

Q4Which conditions call for immediate action or escalation?

Under the airport's applicable operational procedure, the division lists significant FOD, flooding or extensive standing water, a damaged fixture creating an operational hazard, major pavement failure around a fixture, severe fixture displacement, significant structural damage, exposed or damaged electrical infrastructure, major oil or chemical contamination, storm damage affecting airfield lighting, and any condition creating an immediate aircraft, vehicle, personnel or electrical-safety concern.

Q5What is the standard FOD and environmental inspection workflow?

The sequence inspects the area, checks FOD and loose material, inspects fixture surroundings, and checks pavement, settlement, vegetation and standing water. It then inspects pits and base cans, checks mud, dust, oil, chemicals, drainage, wildlife and weather damage, assesses equipment impact, classifies risk, and records, removes or corrects, reinspects and closes.

Q6How are defects classified in Division 18?

Defect status is recorded using the four-level scheme Normal, Defective, Unserviceable and Immediate Action Required, so every observation ends with a clear classification before it is recorded and escalated.

Q7Do airport procedures take precedence over this handbook?

Yes. This handbook is an uncontrolled educational reference, not an approved procedure. The airport's approved inspection schedule, SMS procedures, ATC instructions, NOTAM procedures and local aerodrome regulations always take precedence, and the current revision of any official document should be verified before use.

Q8Can I download a printable checklist for each inspection topic?

Yes. Each inspection topic has a Download as Word option that produces an uncontrolled Word checklist template (description, a tick-box inspection table and an inspector sign-off block) for personal reference and internal training, to be reviewed and adopted under the user’s own document control.
