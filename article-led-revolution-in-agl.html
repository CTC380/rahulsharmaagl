LED Revolution in Airfield Ground Lighting

A field-based reference on the shift from halogen to LED in AGL — what actually changes in efficiency, lifecycle, photometric performance, and control-system behavior, and what it takes to implement it correctly.

RS

Rahul Sharma

Manager – Technical & Commercial · AGL Photometry Specialist

March 21, 2026

Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) is undergoing a major transformation with the shift from conventional halogen systems to LED technology. This is not just a change in light source — it is a complete upgrade in efficiency, performance, control, and long-term reliability of airfield lighting systems.

LED-based AGL systems are increasingly the default choice for new installations and upgrades, driven by operational benefits, regulatory acceptance, and lifecycle cost advantages. Photometry, electrical performance, and system behavior are all redefined with LED technology, making it essential for engineers, operators, and airport authorities to understand this transition clearly.

Key Takeaway

LED technology offers significant advantages, but the technology itself is only part of the solution. The focus should be on implementing LED correctly — with proper photometric validation, electrical compatibility, and control-system integration — rather than simply adopting it.

01

Higher Energy Efficiency and Lower Operating Cost

LED AGL fittings consume significantly less power compared to halogen lamps. This has direct downstream effects on the electrical system feeding them:

  • Reduced load on Constant Current Regulators (CCR)
  • Lower energy consumption across circuits
  • Reduced heat generation

This directly impacts airport operating costs, especially in large airfields with thousands of fittings, and contributes to more efficient operation of the overall AGL infrastructure.

02

Longer Life and Reduced Maintenance

One of the biggest advantages of LED AGL is extended service life. Manufacturer-declared LED life is typically 30,000 to 50,000 hours or more (to a stated lumen-maintenance limit such as L70), against roughly 1,000 to 2,000 hours for typical halogen AGL lamps, and that reliability translates into tangible operational benefits:

30,000–50,000 HrsTypical LED service life
No Frequent ReplacementFar fewer lamp change-outs
Reduced MaintenanceLower ongoing intervention needs
Improved AvailabilityFewer runway closures for maintenance
Lower ManpowerReduced dependency on field crews

For airports, reducing maintenance intervention is particularly valuable because AGL maintenance activities can involve operational coordination and restrictions on runway availability.

03

Improved Photometric Performance

LED fittings provide more stable and controlled light output than halogen lamps. Important advantages include:

  • Better beam precision due to optical design
  • Intensity at each CCR step set by the fitting’s driver design, and verified at every step, not only at full brightness
  • Faster response time with instant ON/OFF

Unlike halogen lamps, LEDs can maintain better photometric stability over time, within their declared lumen-maintenance limits, which is critical for compliance with ICAO Annex 14 and FAA standards.

However, adopting LED technology does not eliminate the need for photometric validation. The actual optical performance of the fitting still needs to be verified through appropriate photometric testing. This includes colour (chromaticity), since LED spectra differ from filtered halogen, and LED-specific criteria such as those in FAA Engineering Brief 67.

04

Enhanced Visibility for Pilots

LED fittings can improve visual clarity during aircraft operations when their optics are well designed. Key advantages include:

  • Higher contrast and sharper beam definition
  • Consistent, well-controlled output during low-visibility conditions and RVR operations
  • Reduced glare with proper optical control

These characteristics directly support safer operations across the airfield:

Landing operations
Take-off operations
Taxiing operations

The objective is not simply to produce more light, but to deliver the right optical performance in the right direction and intensity. LEDs emit little infrared, so they can be less detectable by infrared-based Enhanced Vision Systems; where EVS operations are in use, confirm compatibility with the operator and regulator.

05

Compatibility with Advanced Control Systems

LED AGL systems integrate well with modern airfield control and monitoring systems, providing capabilities such as:

  • Step control through CCR current levels, with LED drivers that respond quickly
  • Integration with AGL Control & Monitoring Systems (ALCMS)
  • Better fault detection and diagnostics (lamp-out thresholds must be re-set for the lower LED load)

The combination of LED fittings, CCRs, and modern control systems creates a more responsive and intelligent AGL infrastructure, enabling smarter airfield operations and real-time system monitoring.

06

Environmental and Sustainability Benefits

Airports are increasingly moving towards greener operations, and LED technology supports this objective directly:

  • Lower carbon footprint
  • No frequent lamp change-outs to dispose of, though LED electronics still need responsible end-of-life recycling
  • Reduced energy demand

Lower energy consumption contributes to more sustainable airport operations and aligns with broader sustainability initiatives in aviation.

07

Challenges in LED AGL Implementation

Despite its advantages, LED technology requires careful engineering. Some of the important challenges include:

  • Compatibility with existing CCR circuits
  • Light CCR loading after retrofit: low power factor, harmonic distortion and regulation stability
  • Open-circuit and flickering issues in retrofit applications
  • Need for proper surge protection of the LED electronics
  • Higher initial investment

Simply replacing a halogen fitting with an LED fitting does not guarantee successful system performance. Proper system design, compatibility checks, installation, and testing are critical to avoid operational issues.

08

Retrofit vs. New Installation

LED adoption generally happens through two approaches, and each requires different engineering considerations:

09

The Importance of Correct LED Implementation

LED technology offers significant advantages, but the technology itself is only part of the solution. For successful AGL implementation, engineers and airport authorities need to consider five things together:

Electrical CompatibilityMust operate correctly with the AGL electrical architecture
Photometric PerformanceOptical output must meet required performance criteria

The focus should therefore be on implementing LED technology correctly rather than simply adopting it.

10

Key Takeaways

  • LED reduces CCR load, energy consumption, and heat generation across the circuit
  • A declared 30,000–50,000 hour service life cuts maintenance intervention and improves runway availability
  • Better photometric stability does not remove the need for photometric validation
  • LED integrates well with CCR step control and modern AGL control and monitoring systems
  • Retrofit and new-build projects carry different engineering constraints and require different checks
  • Successful implementation depends on electrical compatibility, control integration, protection, and testing — not the LED source alone

Conclusion

The LED revolution in Airfield Ground Lighting is not just about saving energy. It is about delivering better performance, higher reliability, and smarter airfield operations.

For engineers and airport authorities, the focus should not only be on adopting LED, but on implementing it correctly — with proper photometric validation, system compatibility, correct electrical integration, appropriate protection, and compliance with ICAO and FAA standards. LED technology is setting the future of AGL, and those who understand it deeply will lead the next generation of airfield infrastructure.

In short, LED transforms AGL from a conventional lighting system into a high-performance, intelligent infrastructure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is LED replacing halogen in airfield ground lighting?

LED AGL fittings consume significantly less power than halogen lamps, which reduces load on the CCRs, energy consumption and heat generation, and lowers operating cost on large airfields with thousands of fittings. Lower energy demand also supports a lower carbon footprint and sustainability goals, although LED electronics still need responsible end-of-life recycling.

How long do LED AGL fittings last compared with halogen?

Manufacturer-declared LED life is typically 30,000 to 50,000 hours or more, to a stated lumen-maintenance limit such as L70, against roughly 1,000 to 2,000 hours for typical halogen AGL lamps. This means far fewer lamp change-outs, lower maintenance and manpower needs, and fewer runway closures for maintenance.

Does LED remove the need for photometric testing?

No. LEDs can maintain better photometric stability than halogen within their declared lumen-maintenance limits, but the actual optical performance of the fitting still has to be verified through appropriate photometric testing. This includes colour (chromaticity), since LED spectra differ from filtered halogen, and LED-specific criteria such as those in FAA Engineering Brief 67. Intensity should be verified at every CCR step, not only at full brightness.

Do LED fittings improve visibility for pilots?

When their optics are well designed, they can give higher contrast, sharper beam definition, consistent output in low-visibility conditions and RVR operations, and reduced glare. The objective is the right optical performance in the right direction and intensity, not simply more light. LEDs emit little infrared, so they can be less detectable by infrared-based Enhanced Vision Systems; where EVS operations are in use, confirm compatibility with the operator and regulator.

How do LED fittings work with CCRs and ALCMS?

LED AGL integrates well with step control through CCR current levels, using LED drivers that respond quickly, and with AGL Control & Monitoring Systems (ALCMS). It supports better fault detection and diagnostics, but lamp-out thresholds must be re-set for the lower LED load.

What are the main challenges of installing LED in AGL?

The article lists compatibility with existing CCR circuits, light CCR loading after retrofit (low power factor, harmonic distortion and regulation stability), open-circuit and flickering issues in retrofit applications, the need for proper surge protection of the LED electronics, and higher initial investment. Simply swapping a halogen fitting for an LED one does not guarantee success; system design, compatibility checks, installation and testing are critical.

What is the difference between an LED retrofit and a new LED installation?

A retrofit replaces existing halogen fittings with LED and must work within the limits of the existing infrastructure, so the CCR, cabling, connectors and existing circuit configuration need checking. A new installation designs the system around LED from a clean baseline, giving greater opportunity to optimise the complete system.

What does correct LED implementation involve?

Five things need to be considered together: electrical compatibility with the AGL architecture, photometric performance that meets the required criteria, control-system integration with the CCR and ALCMS, environmental protection including surge and operating-condition protection, and testing and validation before the system goes operational. The focus should be on implementing LED correctly rather than simply adopting it.
