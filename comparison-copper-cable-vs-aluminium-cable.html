Cabling & Electrical Comparison

Copper VS Aluminium

Copper cable has higher electrical conductivity, better mechanical flexibility and generally easier termination; aluminium cable is lighter and usually more economical for large cross-sections, but requires careful sizing, jointing and termination practices. For AGL, the choice matters most for series-circuit cables, feeder cables, transformer connections and underground distribution.

02 · Electrical Conductivity

Why Cable Size Differs

Copper conducts electricity better than aluminium — approximately, copper conductivity is about 1.6× aluminium conductivity. To obtain roughly the same resistance, an aluminium conductor generally needs a larger cross-sectional area than an equivalent copper conductor.

Copper

Higher conductivity → smaller conductor → smaller cable size, for the same approximate resistance.

Aluminium

Lower conductivity → larger conductor → larger cable size, for the same approximate resistance.

Aluminium's lower density means the larger conductor can still end up substantially lighter than the equivalent copper cable.

03 · Weight

Aluminium's Biggest Advantage

For comparable electrical performance, aluminium cable can be significantly lighter than copper cable. This matters particularly for long cable runs, large feeder cables, cable trays, transportation and installation handling.

For AGL, the weight advantage has to be considered alongside cable size, installation method, bending radius and termination space.

04 · Cost

Material Cost vs Installed Cost

Copper is generally more expensive as a conductor material, which drives higher cable cost. Aluminium is generally cheaper, which can mean a lower project cost — but total installed cost is not simply conductor price.

You also need to consider:

  • Larger aluminium cable size
  • Larger glands
  • Larger terminals
  • Special lugs
  • Jointing compounds
  • Installation labour
  • Termination procedures
05 · Flexibility

Handling Around AGL Equipment

Copper is generally easier to bend and handle, which is particularly useful around AGL transformers, light bases, junction boxes, CCR connections and tight equipment enclosures. Aluminium conductors are generally less flexible for equivalent constructions.

For compact AGL equipment connections, copper is often more convenient.

06 · Termination

The Biggest Practical Difference

Aluminium forms an oxide layer on its surface. Aluminium termination therefore requires appropriate aluminium-compatible lugs, terminals, jointing compounds where specified, surface preparation, torque control and installation procedures. Copper is generally less demanding in this respect.

Poor aluminium termination → higher contact resistance → local heating → joint deterioration → failure. Correct termination practice is essential.

07 · Thermal Expansion

Connection Stress Over Time

Aluminium expands and contracts more than copper with temperature changes, which matters at connections: temperature cycling causes conductor expansion/contraction, which creates connection stress and potential contact deterioration. Correct termination design and torque are particularly important for aluminium conductors.

08 · AGL Series Circuit

Cable in the Constant-Current Circuit

AGL series circuits follow a special architecture: CCR → series cable → isolation transformer → fixture → isolation transformer → fixture → and so on. The cable is part of a constant-current series circuit, so selection should consider:

  • Conductor resistance
  • Voltage drop
  • Cable insulation
  • Current rating
  • Environmental conditions
  • Mechanical protection
  • Installation method
  • Circuit length
  • Fault conditions
  • Compatibility with connectors and transformers

You should not select AGL series-circuit cable solely based on ampacity.

09 · Voltage Drop

Resistance and Circuit Length

Higher resistance produces greater voltage drop for a given conductor length and current. Copper's lower resistivity gives it an advantage: lower resistance → lower voltage drop. Aluminium's higher resistance means higher voltage drop for the same conditions.

For long AGL circuits, voltage drop and total circuit impedance therefore need to be checked during design.

10 · Cable Selection

What a Correct AGL Cable Selection Weighs

For a series circuit, cable selection should weigh current capacity, resistance, voltage drop, insulation, mechanical strength, installation method, termination and environmental conditions together.

The correct cable is the one that satisfies the complete design requirement, not simply the one with the lowest price.

12 · Jointing

Copper vs Aluminium Jointing Process

Copper jointing

Compatible connector → crimp/termination → joint. Generally straightforward.

Aluminium jointing

Surface preparation → compatible connector → jointing compound if specified → correct crimp/torque → joint. Requires more attention to installation quality.

13 · Corrosion

Oxide Layers & Galvanic Risk

Copper and aluminium behave differently in corrosive environments. Aluminium can develop a protective oxide layer, but that oxide is electrically problematic at contact surfaces. Also, when copper and aluminium are directly connected, galvanic corrosion can become a concern under suitable environmental conditions.

Cu-to-Al transitions should use connectors specifically designed and approved for the combination. Don't simply connect a copper conductor to an aluminium conductor with an ordinary connector.

14 · Maintenance

Maintenance Sensitivity

Copper

Usually more forgiving:

  • Easier visual assessment
  • Easier termination
  • Less sensitivity to termination practices

Aluminium

Requires greater attention to:

  • Terminal tightness
  • Oxidation
  • Joint condition
  • Correct connector type
  • Thermal cycling

This is particularly important in long-term underground installations.

15 · FAT / Testing

Tests Apply Regardless of Conductor

Cable conductor material doesn't eliminate the normal electrical tests. Depending on project requirements, tests may include:

  • Continuity
  • Insulation resistance
  • Conductor resistance
  • High-voltage/dielectric testing where applicable
  • Sheath testing where applicable
  • Phase/polarity checks for applicable circuits
  • Joint/termination inspection

Cable material is a design characteristic; electrical tests verify the installed cable system.

16 · Practical Trade-off

When to Choose Which

Choose copper when you prioritize

  • Compact cable size
  • Flexibility
  • Easier termination
  • Small equipment connections
  • High mechanical robustness
  • Simpler installation

Aluminium becomes attractive when you prioritize

  • Low cable weight
  • Large cable sizes
  • Long cable runs
  • Material cost reduction
  • Large feeder applications
17 · Common Misconception

What You Should Not Assume

Myth: "Aluminium is cheaper, therefore it is better"
Fact

A complete comparison should include cable + connectors + glands + lugs + joints + installation labour + maintenance + voltage drop + service life. A cheaper conductor can become expensive if the termination system is poorly designed.

18 · Memory Aid

Easy Memory

Think: Copper

Conductive + compact + flexible + easy termination

Think: Aluminium

Light + economical + larger size + more termination care

19 · Engineering Verdict

Which Conductor Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

Copper is generally preferred where compact size, flexibility and easy termination are important; aluminium is attractive for larger and longer cable runs where weight and material cost are significant.

For an AGL series-circuit installation, the final selection should be based on current rating, circuit resistance/voltage drop, insulation, mechanical requirements, installation method, connector/termination compatibility and applicable project standards — not conductor cost alone.

CopperCompact, flexible, easy to terminate — preferred for equipment connections and small cross-sections.
AluminiumLight and economical for large, long feeder runs — but demands careful termination.

20 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is aluminium cable always cheaper than copper for AGL?

Not necessarily on a total installed basis. Aluminium's material cost is lower, but larger cable size, larger glands and terminals, special lugs and more demanding installation labour can offset the saving.

Can copper and aluminium conductors be joined directly?

Not with an ordinary connector. Cu-to-Al transitions should use connectors specifically designed and approved for the combination, due to galvanic corrosion risk.

Why does aluminium termination need more care than copper?

Aluminium forms an oxide layer that can increase contact resistance if not properly prepared, and it expands/contracts more with temperature, which stresses connections. Both call for aluminium-compatible lugs, correct surface prep and torque control.

Which conductor is better for AGL series circuits?

Copper is generally preferred for compact equipment connections due to flexibility and easier termination; aluminium can be attractive for long feeder runs. The final selection should be based on the complete circuit design, not cable cost alone.

Does cable material affect the required electrical tests?

No. Continuity, insulation resistance, conductor resistance and other applicable tests apply to the installed cable system regardless of conductor material.

Is aluminium suitable for isolation transformer connections?

It's possible with suitable termination practice, but copper is generally the more straightforward and preferred choice for these compact, equipment-side connections.

Project / Client Cable Specification

Final Engineering Note

Conductor choice should follow current rating, voltage drop, insulation, mechanical and termination requirements together — not conductor price alone.

Copper favours compact, flexible, easy-to-terminate connections; aluminium favours large, long feeder runs where weight and cost dominate — but only with disciplined termination practice.
