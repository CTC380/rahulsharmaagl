Decommissioning, Disposal & Recycling

The asset retirement and final closure division of the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide. This is the point where an asset that has reached the end of its useful lifecycle is formally removed from service, isolated, de-energized, removed, disposed of or recycled, documented, and closed in the asset register.

Initial Issue

The principle this division answers — Asset replacement is not complete when the new asset is installed; the lifecycle is complete only when the old asset has been safely decommissioned, appropriately disposed of or recycled, and formally closed in the asset-management records.

Where Division 20 Sits in the Lifecycle

Division 20 connects directly with Division 06 (Installation & Asset Handover), Division 12 (Lifecycle Cost Management), Division 13 (Spare Parts & Inventory Lifecycle), Division 16 (Refurbishment & Life Extension), Division 18 (Obsolescence Management) and Division 19 (Replacement Planning & Decision Making). It is the final physical and administrative stage of the asset lifecycle.

DEC-02 Equipment Isolation
Purpose

Before removal, the asset must be safely isolated from all relevant energy and operational sources.

Identify
  • Electrical supply
  • Control supply
  • Communication connections
  • Associated equipment
  • Connected circuits
  • Stored energy
  • Mechanical hazards
Integration Requirement

The isolation process should integrate with the airport's approved LOTO and electrical-safety procedures.

DEC-03 De-Energization
Purpose

De-energization confirms that the equipment has been made electrically safe for removal.

Verify
  • Supply disconnected
  • Circuit isolated
  • Appropriate isolation applied
  • Absence of hazardous voltage verified
  • Relevant control circuits isolated
  • Equipment made safe for removal
Why It Matters for AGL

Particularly important where equipment forms part of an energized series circuit or is connected to CCR-controlled circuits.

DEC-05 Cable Removal
Purpose

Cable removal requires particular planning because cables may be installed underground and may be associated with active circuits.

Determine
  • Cable identification
  • Circuit association
  • Cable route
  • Active/inactive status
  • Removal requirement
  • Retention requirement
  • Environmental considerations
  • Disposal route
Not Every Cable Needs Physical Removal

The decommissioning decision should establish whether a redundant cable is to be removed, abandoned in place, isolated, or otherwise controlled, according to the applicable engineering and environmental requirements.

AGL Decommissioning Decision Hierarchy

Before any asset or material is sent to disposal, the decision should work down through a fixed hierarchy — not default straight to disposal.

1. Reuse — can the complete asset be reused?
2. Component Recovery — can useful components be recovered?
3. Material Recovery — can materials be recovered?
4. Recycling — can materials be recycled?
5. Controlled Disposal — dispose of remaining material through the approved route

Reuse → Recover → Recycle → Dispose — subject to technical suitability, safety and applicable environmental requirements.

The old asset record should never simply be deleted once closed. The asset lifecycle does not end at replacement. It ends only after the old asset has been safely decommissioned, its materials/components have been appropriately reused, recovered, recycled or disposed of, all required records have been updated, and the asset has been formally closed in the lifecycle management system.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Decommissioning, Disposal & Recycling in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide.

Q1What is Division 20 — Decommissioning, Disposal & Recycling in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

Division 20 is the asset retirement and final closure division. It covers 20 topics on decommissioning planning, isolation, de-energization, removal, disposal, recycling, hazardous materials, and asset-register closure.

Q2Why should decommissioning be planned before removal?

Decommissioning should be planned before physical removal begins. The plan covers asset identification and location, the reason for decommissioning, any replacement asset and the decommissioning scope.

Q3What are equipment isolation and de-energization?

Before removal the asset must be safely isolated from all relevant energy and operational sources, including electrical and control supply, communication connections and connected circuits. De-energization confirms the equipment is electrically safe for removal, including verifying the absence of hazardous voltage.

Q4Why does cable removal need special planning?

Cables may be installed underground and may be associated with active circuits. Planning covers cable identification, circuit association, route, active or inactive status and whether removal is required.

Q5How are AGL assets disposed of by type?

Division 20 has dedicated topics for transformer, fixture, sign, PAPI and CCR disposal. Each is managed according to its construction, condition and applicable requirements, and PAPI systems and CCRs contain multiple components needing controlled, separate handling.

Q6How are e-waste, metals and material recovery handled?

Electronic components such as LED drivers, control modules and PLC components should go through the applicable e-waste process. AGL equipment can contain significant recoverable metals such as copper, aluminium, steel, stainless steel and brass, and before disposal useful components should be evaluated for recovery.

Q7What environmental and hazardous-material factors apply?

Decommissioning should consider environmental impact including waste generation, electronic waste and metals. Some equipment may contain materials needing controlled handling, such as oils or fluids, batteries, contaminated materials or legacy materials.

Q8What records close the asset lifecycle?

Every significant decommissioning should have a documented disposal trail, with disposal and recycling records as evidence. The asset register must be updated with dates, disposal method and final condition, and final asset closure is the formal completion of the lifecycle.
