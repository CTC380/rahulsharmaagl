Top Cover

The uppermost protective component of an AGL fixture, sealing the housing and securing internal components. Designed to withstand impact, provide environmental sealing, and, in many cases, incorporate the optical window or lens for light output.

Overview

What Is It?

The Top Cover is the uppermost component of an AGL fixture, serving as the primary protective barrier between the internal optical and electronic components and the harsh outdoor environment. It is typically made from durable materials such as die-cast aluminium, stainless steel, or high-strength engineering plastics. The top cover may incorporate the optical window (lens), a prism assembly, or a clear cover plate that allows light to exit while protecting the light source and optics from moisture, dust, and mechanical impact. It is secured to the fixture housing using screws, bolts, or a threaded ring, and is sealed with a gasket or O-ring to maintain the fixture's IP67/IP68 rating. In inset fixtures, the top cover is designed to be flush with the pavement surface and must withstand heavy aircraft loads.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The top cover's purpose is to keep an AGL fixture's light output reliable and its interior safe from the environment, without compromising the beam that pilots depend on for guidance. It is the fixture's first line of defence against moisture, dust, bird strikes, and debris, so a fixture that has been sealed and protected for years will still perform to its original photometric specification rather than degrading or short-circuiting internally. Where the cover doubles as the optical window, this protective role has to be balanced against optical transparency and precise shape, since any distortion or fogging would blur or misdirect the beam. In inset fixtures the cover takes on a third, safety-critical role: bearing the repeated weight of aircraft wheels without deforming, so the fixture remains flush and does not itself become a hazard to tyres or landing gear.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Top covers are used in virtually every AGL fixture type, with design variations for specific applications:

  • Runway Edge Lights (Elevated): Top cover with lens/prism for beam projection, secured by a threaded ring or screws.
  • Runway Edge Lights (Inset): Heavy-duty steel or cast-iron cover with an optical window, designed to withstand 100,000 kg wheel loads.
  • Runway Centreline Lights: Flush-mounted cover with a bi-directional optical assembly.
  • Threshold & End Lights: Cover with green/red filter and uni-directional optics.
  • Taxiway Lights: Cover with blue or green lens, often with a low profile.
  • Approach Lighting: Covers for barrette housings, often with clear lenses.
  • PAPI & VASI: Precision covers with colour filters and optical alignment features.
  • Obstruction Lights: Covers with hemispherical or flat lenses for 360° visibility.
Placement

Installation & Location

The top cover seats onto the bottom housing over a gasket, secured by a threaded retaining ring, screws, or a bayonet-style twist-lock depending on fixture type, with bolts or the ring torqued evenly around the perimeter so the gasket compresses uniformly and the joint seals fully. On inset fixtures, the cover is set so its upper face finishes flush with the surrounding pavement once fitted, ready to take direct wheel loading, while elevated fixtures instead need the cover correctly orientated to any beam-shaping lens or prism it carries. Where the cover integrates the optical assembly, its rotational alignment is set to an index mark so the beam or colour filter faces the correct direction before the final fasteners are tightened, and it's refitted with a new gasket each time the fixture is opened for lamp, LED, or internal servicing.

Function

How Does It Work?

The top cover performs several essential functions in an AGL fixture:

  • Environmental Protection: Seals the housing against moisture, dust, and contaminants using a gasket or O-ring, maintaining the required IP rating.
  • Mechanical Protection: Protects the internal optical components (prism, lens, reflector) and light source (LED or lamp) from impact damage, bird strikes, and debris.
  • Optical Output: In many fixtures, the top cover incorporates the optical window or lens that allows light to exit with the required beam pattern.
  • Load Bearing (Inset Fixtures): For flush-mounted fixtures, the top cover is designed to withstand repeated aircraft tyre loads (up to 100,000 kg) without deformation or failure.
  • Thermal Management: The cover may act as a heat sink or provide ventilation paths to dissipate heat from the light source.
  • Security & Tamper Resistance: Covers are often secured with tamper-proof fasteners to prevent unauthorised access.

The top cover is typically the first component to be removed during maintenance, allowing access to the lamp, LED module, or optics for inspection and replacement.

Construction

Physical Construction

The top cover is die-cast, machined, or moulded from aluminium alloy, stainless steel, or a high-strength engineering plastic, formed as a single shell that carries the lens or prism window in a machined recess or moulded-in aperture. Where the cover incorporates the optical window directly, that window is gasket- or O-ring-sealed into its recess before the cover itself is fitted to the housing, so the cover ships as one pre-sealed sub-assembly rather than requiring the glazing to be fitted in the field. The cover-to-housing joint runs the full perimeter and is compressed against a gasket or O-ring by screws, bolts, or a threaded ring, chosen so the fixture's IP67/IP68 rating is maintained through repeated opening for lamp or driver access. On inset fixtures the cover is additionally built to a load-rated thickness and edge-support profile so it survives direct aircraft wheel loading while sitting flush with the pavement surface.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Corrosion of Metal Covers

In coastal or high-humidity environments, die-cast aluminium covers can corrode, especially if the powder coating is damaged. This can lead to seizing of fasteners and loss of structural integrity. Use stainless steel covers or marine-grade coatings in such environments.

Gasket/O-Ring Failure

The sealing gasket or O-ring can degrade over time, leading to moisture ingress and internal corrosion. Regular inspection and replacement of seals during maintenance is essential.

Lens Cracking or Scratching

The optical window (lens) in the top cover can crack due to thermal shock or impact, or become scratched from cleaning or debris. Cracks or deep scratches reduce light output and compromise the seal. Replace the top cover or lens assembly if damaged.

Stripped or Seized Fasteners

Over-tightening or corrosion can strip or seize the screws or bolts securing the top cover. This makes maintenance difficult and can lead to damage during removal. Use anti-seize compounds and torque wrenches to prevent this.

Paint Degradation (UV Fading, Chalking)

Powder-coated covers can fade or chalk due to UV exposure. While this is primarily cosmetic, severe degradation can expose the metal substrate to corrosion. Re-painting or replacing the cover may be required.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Moisture Found Inside the Fixture Under the Top Cover
Likely Cause
Degraded sealing gasket or O-ring under the top cover allowing moisture ingress.
Diagnostic Steps
Remove the cover and inspect the seal for compression set, cracking, or gaps; check for condensation or corrosion evidence just inside the cover.
Corrective Action
Replace the degraded gasket or O-ring, dry out the interior, and re-torque the cover fasteners evenly per the specified sequence.
Cover Won't Come Off / Fasteners Stripped or Seized
Likely Cause
Corrosion or over-tightening of the securing screws/bolts over previous maintenance visits.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect fastener heads for stripping or corrosion bonding them to the cover; check whether anti-seize compound was used previously.
Corrective Action
Carefully drill out or extract seized/stripped fasteners and replace with new hardware, applying anti-seize compound and torquing to spec on reassembly.
Visible Corrosion on the Cover
Likely Cause
Damaged powder coating on a die-cast aluminium cover exposing the substrate to coastal or high-humidity conditions.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect the coating for chips or peeling; assess whether corrosion has progressed to structural pitting or is still surface-level.
Corrective Action
Touch up minor coating damage promptly; replace the cover with a stainless steel or marine-grade coated version if corrosion has progressed, especially if fastener seizing has resulted.
Reduced Light Output Through the Cover's Optical Window
Likely Cause
Cracked or scratched lens window in the top cover from thermal shock, impact, or abrasive cleaning.
Diagnostic Steps
Visually inspect the lens window for cracks or deep scratches; run a photometric check if output loss is suspected but not visually obvious.
Corrective Action
Replace the top cover or lens assembly if cracked or significantly scratched, since damage here also compromises the seal.

Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Verify Isolation Before Removal
Electrical Safety

The top cover is the only barrier between exposed hands and the live optical/electronic assembly beneath it; de-energise and lock out the fixture's CCR and verify zero energy before removing the cover, even for a routine lens clean or inspection.

Inset Cover Load Rating
Mechanical

On inset fixtures the top cover sits flush with the pavement and carries direct aircraft wheel loads; use only the manufacturer-rated cover for the fixture and confirm it seats flush and secure before reopening the surface to traffic.

Seal Integrity on Reinstatement
Mechanical

Inspect the gasket or O-ring for damage, dirt, or compression set every time the cover is removed, and replace it if in doubt; a compromised seal lets in moisture and dust and defeats the fixture's IP67/IP68 rating.

Lens/Window Handling
Personnel Safety

Handle the optical window or prism section by its edges and avoid abrasive cleaning agents or dry wiping under grit, since scratches or thermal shock can crack the cover and reduce light output.

Airside Work Coordination
Operational

Any work that removes or leaves a fixture without its top cover requires ATC/airside coordination and a NOTAM where the fixture will be dark, out of tolerance, or an FOD/load risk, with vehicle and personnel movement kept clear per the aerodrome's safety management procedures.

FOD Control at the Fixture
Operational

Keep the retaining screws, threaded ring, or bolts accounted for at all times while the cover is off, and never leave a fixture open or loosely capped, to prevent foreign object debris on the movement area.

Correct Torque on Reinstatement
Mechanical

Tighten retaining fasteners or the threaded ring to the manufacturer's specified torque; under-tightening compromises the seal and traffic load rating, while over-tightening can crack the cover or strip the housing threads.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Do not leave a fixture with a cracked, hazed, or misaligned cover after maintenance; verify the seal, seating, and photometric output through the window before closing out the work and cancelling the associated NOTAM.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Load Rating Applies to the Whole Assembly, Not Just the Cover Material
Mechanical

An inset top cover's wheel-load rating depends on correct engagement with its housing and retaining hardware, not the cover material alone; a rated cover installed with under-torqued fasteners or a worn retaining thread can fail under load well below its stated rating.

IP Rating Is Lost the Moment the Gasket Compresses Unevenly
Durability

A top cover gasket compressed unevenly from a warped cover or debris trapped under one edge can leave the fixture's IP67/IP68 rating compromised even though the cover appears fully seated and secured; check for even gasket compression around the full perimeter, not just that the cover is tight.

Internal Corrosion Can Precede Any External Sign on the Cover
Troubleshooting

Moisture entry through a marginal cover seal corrodes internal components before it produces any visible sign on the cover itself; where a fixture shows unexplained intermittent faults, inspect the cover seal even if the cover looks undamaged.

Torque Sequence Matters as Much as Torque Value on Multi-Bolt Covers
Workmanship

Tightening multi-bolt or multi-screw covers in a random order can leave the gasket unevenly compressed even when every fastener reaches its specified torque; tighten in a cross or star pattern to seat the seal evenly.

A Cover That 'Mostly' Seats Isn't Seated
Mechanical

A cover that goes on with noticeable resistance or sits slightly proud on one side is often forced the rest of the way rather than investigated; a cover that doesn't seat smoothly usually indicates thread damage, a foreign object, or a warped cover, any of which will compromise the seal or load rating.

Track Cover Replacement History Per Fixture
Compliance

Because inset covers see direct traffic loading and are more failure-prone than most fixture components, logging cover replacement dates per fixture helps identify a fixture location experiencing unusually frequent cover failures, which may point to a paving or load issue rather than a cover defect.

Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about the top cover: what it is, how it works, specifications, standards, maintenance and common faults.

Q1What is a top cover on an AGL fixture?

The top cover is the uppermost component of an AGL fixture and the main protective barrier between the internal optics and electronics and the outdoor environment. It may incorporate the optical window, a prism assembly or a clear cover plate that lets light exit.

Q2What is a top cover made of?

Typical materials are die-cast aluminium (A380), 316L stainless steel or engineering plastics such as PC or PA, with powder-coated, anodised or polished finishes and UV-stabilised coatings. Optical windows are tempered glass, polycarbonate or acrylic.

Q3How does a top cover keep water out?

It is secured with screws, bolts or a threaded ring and sealed with a silicone or EPDM gasket or O-ring to maintain the fixture's IP67 or IP68 rating.

Q4What loads can a top cover withstand?

On inset fixtures, the cover is designed to be flush with the pavement and withstand repeated aircraft tyre loads of up to 100,000 kg wheel load. Elevated fixture covers are typically rated to IK10 (20 J) impact resistance.

Q5Where are top covers used?

In virtually every AGL fixture type, with designs varying by application, such as elevated runway edge lights (lens or prism with a threaded ring or screws), inset runway edge lights and flush-mounted centreline covers.

Q6What temperature and corrosion performance is typical?

The typical continuous range is -40°C to +80°C, with salt spray testing to ASTM B117 for 500 hours or more.

Q7Which standards apply to top covers?

The main references are FAA AC 150/5345-46 (cover and housing requirements), IEC 60529 (IP codes), IEC/TS 61827 (environmental and mechanical performance) and ASTM B117 (salt spray). Always verify the current edition and your aerodrome's approved requirements.

Q8How often should a top cover be inspected?

Typical intervals are quarterly for cleaning, and annual for corrosion and paint inspection, gasket and seal checks and fastener condition. Your airport's approved maintenance programme takes precedence.

Q9Why is there moisture under the top cover?

The usual cause is seal degradation of the gasket or O-ring. Inspect the seal, check the fasteners and cover condition, and replace the gasket as needed to restore the IP rating.

Q10What are the common problems with top covers?

Common problems are corrosion of metal covers, gasket or O-ring failure, lens cracking or scratching, stripped or seized fasteners, and paint degradation from UV fading and chalking.
