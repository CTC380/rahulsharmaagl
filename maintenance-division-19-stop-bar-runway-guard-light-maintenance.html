Stop Bar & Runway Guard Light Maintenance

Dedicated maintenance procedures for Stop Bar Lights and Runway Guard Lights (RGLs) — critical runway-incursion-prevention visual aids. Maintenance here must address physical and electrical condition alongside correct alignment, visibility, and functional operation, including RGL flashing performance and control/flasher response.

Core principle: Stop Bar & RGL maintenance = Visibility + Correct Alignment + Reliable Operation + Correct Control/Flashing Response + Rapid Restoration. A light being ON does not automatically mean the safety function is correctly maintained.

Safety before work: stop bars and RGLs are runway-incursion-prevention aids. Coordinate with ATC / airport operations before any isolation, switching or command test, and notify ATC of every stop bar or RGL fault and of return to service. Isolate and lock out the affected series circuit before touching fixtures or connectors — never open a live series circuit.

Special Attention — Safety-Critical Function

For both Stop Bars and RGLs, maintenance distinguishes between five separate dimensions of availability.

Physical

Is the fixture physically present and intact?

Electrical

Is power reaching the fixture?

Optical

Is the fixture producing the intended visual indication?

Functional

Is it operating in the intended mode?

Control

Where applicable, is the fixture responding correctly to its control system?

Runway Lighting Maintenance Quick Reference

Stop bars and runway guard lights are part of the runway lighting system, so the same maintenance discipline applies. Routine runway edge and centreline fixture work is covered in Division 06, with corrective work in Division 02 and return-to-service testing in Division 23. Confirm all criteria against the approved procedures and the current standard.

Runway light maintenance procedure

A runway light maintenance procedure follows one sequence. Coordinate with airport operations and obtain authorization before isolating or testing. Isolate the circuit as required and confirm a safe condition. Clean and inspect the fixture: housing, optical component, LED or lamp, mounting, alignment, cable, secondary lead and connector, including a check for water ingress. Replace or repair any defective part and verify the replacement. Then carry out functional verification, including flashing operation for RGL, check control response where applicable, confirm final visibility, complete the maintenance record and return the system to service.

Runway lighting maintenance checklist

A runway lighting maintenance checklist for stop bar and RGL work mirrors the Post-Maintenance Checklist above: fixture cleaned, housing inspected, optical component inspected, LED or lamp verified, alignment and mounting verified, cable inspected, secondary lead and connector verified, water ingress checked, functional operation confirmed, flashing operation confirmed for RGL, control response checked where applicable, final visibility confirmed and the maintenance record completed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about stop bar & runway guard light maintenance, based on the topics in this division.

10 QUESTIONS
Q1What is Stop Bar and Runway Guard Light maintenance?

It provides dedicated procedures for Stop Bar Lights and Runway Guard Lights, which are critical runway-incursion-prevention visual aids. Maintenance must address physical and electrical condition as well as correct operation.

Q2What does Division 19 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 19 (Stop Bar & Runway Guard Light Maintenance) contains 24 topics in two groups: Group A Stop Bar Maintenance (10 topics) and Group B Runway Guard Light Maintenance (14 topics).

Q3Is a stop bar or RGL satisfactory if it is simply lit?

No. A fixture that illuminates but is incorrectly aligned, physically damaged or not operating in its intended mode is not fully satisfactory. The core principle is visibility, correct alignment, reliable operation, correct control or flashing response and rapid restoration.

Q4What must be coordinated before isolating or testing a stop bar or RGL?

Coordinate with ATC or airport operations before any isolation, switching or command test, because stop bars and RGLs are runway-incursion-prevention aids.

Q5What five dimensions of availability are maintained?

Physical (is the fixture present and intact), electrical (is power reaching it), optical (is it producing the intended visual indication), functional (is it operating in the intended mode) and control (where applicable, is it responding correctly to its control system).

Q6What is the verification sequence before return to service?

Every task moves through eight stages: physical condition, optical condition, electrical condition, alignment, functional operation, control or operation, final visibility and return to service.

Q7How are a stop bar and an RGL troubleshot?

There are two independent sequences. For a non-operating stop bar, confirm the commanded state (ALCMS or ATC) and interlock, then check the fixture, LED or lamp, secondary lead, connector, isolation transformer or circuit and cable. For abnormal RGL flashing, check the fixture, LED or lamp, optical components, control or flasher arrangement where applicable, power supply or circuit, connector and secondary lead, then verify flashing and alignment.

Q8What is different between the stop bar and RGL post-maintenance checklists?

The same items apply to both, including cleaning, housing, optics, LED or lamp, alignment, mounting, cable, secondary lead, connector, water ingress, functional operation, control response where applicable, final visibility and the maintenance record. Flashing operation is RGL-specific and not applicable to stop bars.

Q9What is a runway light maintenance procedure?

A runway light maintenance procedure runs in sequence: coordinate and obtain authorization, isolate and confirm a safe condition, clean and inspect the fixture, replace or repair defective parts, verify functional operation (including flashing for RGL), confirm final visibility, complete the record and return to service.

Q10What is on a runway lighting maintenance checklist?

A runway lighting maintenance checklist for stop bar and RGL work covers cleaning, housing, optical component, LED or lamp, alignment, mounting, cable, secondary lead, connector, water ingress, functional operation, flashing operation for RGL, control response, final visibility and the maintenance record.
