Lamp Holder

The mechanical and electrical interface that securely mounts the halogen lamp or LED module within the AGL fixture. Provides reliable electrical contact, precise optical alignment, and thermal management, ensuring consistent performance and long lamp life.

Overview

What Is It?

A Lamp Holder (also called a lamp socket or lampholder) is a mechanical and electrical device that secures the lamp in the correct position within the AGL fixture, provides electrical connections to the lamp's terminals, and ensures proper thermal and optical alignment. In halogen lamp fixtures, the holder typically features ceramic or high-temperature plastic construction with spring-loaded contacts to accommodate thermal expansion and maintain a stable connection. For LED modules, the holder may be a dedicated PCB connector or a clamping mechanism that positions the LED array precisely relative to the optics. The lamp holder is a critical component for lamp replacement, as it allows quick and safe lamp changes without disturbing the fixture's optical alignment.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The lamp holder's purpose is to keep the light source electrically connected, thermally managed, and optically aligned for as long as it is in service, while also making replacement fast enough that a lighting outage on an active runway or taxiway is kept as brief as possible. A loose or corroded contact would cause flickering, intermittent failure, or a complete loss of the light with no obvious external sign, so the holder's spring-loaded, corrosion-resistant contacts are what make the fixture's electrical reliability possible over years of vibration and thermal cycling. Its precise, repeatable positioning of the filament or LED die is equally important for safety: even a correctly working lamp will not produce a compliant beam if it is misaligned relative to the reflector or prism.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Lamp holders are used in virtually every AGL fixture that employs a replaceable light source:

  • Halogen Runway Edge & Centreline Lights: Holders for 45W, 100W, and 200W 6.6A lamps, predominantly with a PK30d/PKX30d prefocus base; smaller bi-pin (GZ9.5/GY9.5) bases are used on some lower-wattage or elevated fixtures.
  • Threshold & End Lights: Holders for coloured lamps with specific base configurations.
  • Taxiway Lights: Holders for lower-wattage halogen lamps.
  • Approach Lighting: Holders for high-intensity halogen lamps in barrettes.
  • PAPI & VASI: Specialised holders with precision alignment for glide slope optics.
  • Obstruction Lights: Holders for lamps in obstruction beacons.
  • LED Modules: Holders or connectors that secure and align the LED PCB.
Placement

Installation & Location

The holder is fixed inside the housing at the point matching the fixture's optical focus, secured with screws or a bracket so its position doesn't shift under vibration from aircraft or maintenance traffic. Wiring is terminated to the holder's contacts before it's fitted, with enough slack left in the leads to allow the lamp to be inserted, removed, and reinserted without straining the connection. The holder's orientation is set so the lamp base type seats correctly (a push-fit or bayonet base needs its keyway or pins aligned to the holder's slot), and once mounted, the holder's position is checked against the reflector or prism to confirm the lamp filament will sit at the correct focal point once fitted.

Function

How Does It Work?

The lamp holder performs several essential functions:

  • Mechanical Retention: Holds the lamp securely in the fixture, preventing movement due to vibration or shock.
  • Electrical Connection: Provides low-resistance contacts that connect the lamp's terminals to the fixture's wiring, ensuring reliable current flow.
  • Thermal Management: Acts as a heat sink or thermal interface, conducting heat away from the lamp base to the fixture housing.
  • Optical Alignment: Positions the lamp filament or LED die precisely relative to the reflector and prism to achieve the required beam pattern.
  • Quick Replacement: Allows easy insertion and removal of the lamp without tools (or with a simple tool), minimising maintenance downtime.

The holder is designed to withstand high temperatures (up to 400°C for halogen lamps) and is typically made from ceramic, high-temperature thermoplastics, or metal with insulating coatings. Contact materials are often nickel-plated or gold-plated to resist corrosion and maintain low contact resistance.

Construction

Physical Construction

Halogen-lamp holders are moulded from ceramic or a high-temperature engineering plastic able to withstand close proximity to the lamp envelope's radiant heat without softening, with spring-loaded metal contacts that maintain positive pressure on the lamp's base pins through repeated thermal expansion and contraction cycles. The holder body is keyed — a prefocus collar or bipost slot — so the lamp can only seat in the correct rotational and axial position, fixing filament location relative to the reflector or prism without further adjustment. LED holders instead take the form of a dedicated PCB edge connector or a mechanical clamp/bracket that locates the LED module's MCPCB against a datum feature in the housing, again removing the need for field alignment. In both cases the holder is fixed to the bottom housing or a mounting bracket with screws, and wiring leaves the holder through a sealed or potted pigtail to keep the connection point protected inside the sealed fixture body.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Contact Corrosion & Arcing

Over time, the lamp holder contacts can corrode due to moisture ingress or oxidation, leading to increased contact resistance and arcing. This can cause lamp flickering, reduced intensity, and premature lamp failure. Regular inspection and cleaning of contacts is essential.

Thermal Degradation of Insulation

Excessive heat can cause the holder's insulation to become brittle and crack, leading to short circuits or ground faults. This is especially common in high-wattage halogen fixtures. Replace holders that show signs of thermal stress.

Loose Lamp Fitment

Repeated lamp changes can cause the holder's spring clips or retaining mechanism to weaken, resulting in a loose lamp fitment. This can affect optical alignment and cause vibration-induced failure. Check the holder's grip regularly and replace if loose.

Incorrect Lamp Type Installed

Using a lamp with a different base or wattage can damage the holder and the fixture. Always use the specified lamp type and wattage as per the fixture's rating label.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Lamp Flickering or Intermittent Operation
Likely Cause
Corroded or oxidised contacts in the holder increasing contact resistance and causing arcing.
Diagnostic Steps
Remove the lamp and inspect contacts for discolouration, pitting, or corrosion; check contact spring tension against a known-good holder.
Corrective Action
Clean contacts with an approved contact cleaner if corrosion is light, or replace the holder if pitting or arcing damage is present; re-verify stable lamp operation afterward.
Short Circuit or Ground Fault at the Holder
Likely Cause
Thermal degradation has made the holder's insulation brittle and cracked, especially common on high-wattage halogen fixtures running hot over time.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect the holder body and insulation for cracking, discolouration, or charring; megger test the holder circuit for insulation resistance.
Corrective Action
Replace the holder showing signs of thermal stress; confirm the fixture is running the correct lamp wattage to avoid recurring heat damage.
Lamp Sits Loose or Vibrates in the Holder
Likely Cause
Spring clips or retaining mechanism weakened from repeated lamp changes over time.
Diagnostic Steps
Check the holder's grip strength on a new lamp by hand; compare spring tension to a new, unused holder.
Corrective Action
Replace the holder if the retaining mechanism has weakened; re-check optical alignment after the lamp is securely seated.
Holder or Fixture Damaged Shortly After Lamp Replacement
Likely Cause
A lamp with the wrong base type or wattage was installed, stressing or damaging the holder and potentially the surrounding fixture.
Diagnostic Steps
Check the installed lamp's base type and wattage against the fixture's rating label; inspect the holder for melting, deformation, or a base that doesn't fully seat.
Corrective Action
Replace the holder if damaged, install the correct lamp type per the rating label, and confirm the spares inventory is correctly labelled to prevent recurrence.

Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Electrical Isolation
Electrical Safety

De-energise and lock out the fixture circuit and verify zero energy at the holder's contacts before removing a lamp or servicing the holder itself.

Hot Lamp / Holder Contact
Personnel Safety

Allow the lamp and holder to cool before handling after an active period; ceramic and high-temperature plastic holders are designed to withstand heat but can still transfer enough to burn skin shortly after power-off.

Halogen Bulb Handling
Mechanical

Avoid touching a halogen lamp's glass envelope with bare skin when reseating it in the holder; skin oils can cause localised hot spots that shorten the lamp's life or cause premature failure.

Optical Alignment Preservation
Compliance

Reseat the lamp or LED module fully and squarely in the holder; a partially seated or misaligned lamp shifts the beam pattern away from the fixture's designed photometric output even though the light still turns on.

PPE
Personnel Safety

Use insulated tools for electrical work and clean gloves when handling lamps or LED modules to avoid contamination or scratching.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Verify secure contact, correct polarity, and a stable connection before closing out the work and returning the fixture to service.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Contact Fretting Corrosion Builds Up Slowly
Failure Mode

Repeated thermal cycling of the holder's contacts through many on/off cycles gradually builds up a resistive oxide layer at the contact surface, increasing localised heating each cycle; a holder that's a common source of intermittent flicker complaints is often exhibiting this cumulative effect rather than a single defective part.

Discolouration Is a Leading Indicator, Not a Cosmetic Issue
Diagnostics

Visible browning or darkening on a holder's contact or body material indicates it has been running hotter than designed, usually from a poor connection or contact wear, well before it causes an outright failure; treat visible discolouration found during routine relamping as a reason to replace the holder proactively rather than only the lamp.

Seating Tolerance Varies More Between Manufacturers Than Expected
Compatibility

Lamps and holders that share the same nominal base designation can still have slightly different seating tolerances between manufacturers, which can leave a lamp seated correctly by feel but subtly misaligned optically; verify beam alignment after switching lamp or holder suppliers, not just physical fit.

Repeated Reseating Wears Spring Contacts Over Time
Mechanical

Spring-loaded contact holders lose retention force gradually with each lamp change; a holder that's been through many relamping cycles can develop a weak or intermittent contact well before it shows any visible sign of wear, so weight holder replacement toward relamping frequency at a given position, not just calendar age.

Match Holder Type to Actual Retrofit, Not Just Base Size
Retrofit

When retrofitting a fixture from lamp to LED module, confirm the new holder or socket is rated for the LED module's actual current and thermal profile, since a holder sized only by physical base compatibility with the old lamp may not be rated for the LED module's different electrical characteristics.

Moisture at the Holder Often Points Upstream
Troubleshooting

Corrosion found at the holder contacts is frequently a symptom of a failed gasket or seal elsewhere in the housing rather than a defect in the holder itself; trace and correct the actual moisture entry point when replacing a corroded holder, or the replacement will develop the same fault on the same timeline.

Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about AGL lamp holders: function, materials, base types, ratings, standards and maintenance.

Q1What is a lamp holder in an AGL fixture?

A lamp holder, also called a lamp socket or lampholder, is the mechanical and electrical device that secures the lamp in the correct position inside an AGL fixture. It connects the lamp's terminals to the fixture wiring and keeps the lamp thermally and optically aligned. It also allows quick, safe lamp changes without disturbing the fixture's optical alignment.

Q2What does a lamp holder do?

It does five jobs: it holds the lamp against vibration and shock, provides a low-resistance electrical contact, conducts heat away from the lamp base into the fixture housing, positions the filament or LED die precisely relative to the reflector and prism, and allows fast lamp replacement to keep outages short.

Q3What are lamp holders made of?

Halogen lamp holders are made from ceramic (alumina), high-temperature plastics such as PPS or PEEK, or metal with an insulating coating, and can withstand up to 400°C. Contacts are typically nickel-plated brass, beryllium copper or gold-plated to resist corrosion and keep contact resistance low.

Q4Which lamp base types do AGL lamp holders accept?

The most common is the PK30d/PKX30d prefocus base, used for 45 W, 100 W and 200 W 6.6 A halogen lamps. Some lower-wattage or elevated fixtures use GZ9.5/GY9.5 bi-pin bases, and some fixtures use a custom AGL base depending on the lamp type.

Q5What electrical ratings does a lamp holder have?

It is rated for the 6.6 A constant-current series circuit standard (IEC 61822/61823). The voltage is set by the lamp wattage, for example about 7 V for a 45 W lamp and about 30 V for a 200 W lamp. Typical insulation resistance is above 100 MΩ at 500 V DC, and dielectric strength is typically 2,500 V AC for one minute.

Q6Where does the lamp holder sit in the AGL circuit?

It is on the secondary side, at the lamp interface downstream of the isolating transformer. A poor contact at the holder stays confined to the secondary loop and does not affect the primary series circuit.

Q7Do LED fixtures use lamp holders?

In LED fixtures the holder is usually a dedicated PCB connector or a clamping mechanism that positions the LED array precisely relative to the optics. When retrofitting from lamp to LED module, confirm the new holder is rated for the module's actual current and thermal profile, not just its physical base size.

Q8Which standards apply to AGL lamp holders?

The main references are IEC 60061 (lamp caps and holders), IEC 60838 (miscellaneous lampholders), FAA AC 150/5345-46 (AGL luminaire specification, including holder requirements) and IEC 61827 as an electrical safety reference. Always verify the current edition and your aerodrome's approved requirements.

Q9How often should lamp holders be inspected?

Typical intervals are annual for contact inspection, insulation condition checks and lamp type verification, and semi-annual for fitment and retention checks. Your airport's approved maintenance programme takes precedence.

Q10Why does a lamp flicker even though the lamp itself is healthy?

The usual cause is corroded or oxidised contacts in the holder, which raise contact resistance and cause arcing. Remove the lamp and inspect the contacts for discolouration or pitting. Clean light corrosion with an approved contact cleaner, or replace the holder if pitting or arcing damage is present. Corrosion is often a symptom of a failed gasket or seal elsewhere in the housing, so trace the moisture entry point as well.

Q11What damages a lamp holder or shortens its life?

Common causes are thermal degradation of the insulation (especially in high-wattage halogen fixtures), weakened spring clips after repeated lamp changes, and installing a lamp with the wrong base or wattage. Always use the lamp type and wattage on the fixture's rating label, and replace holders that show cracking, charring or discolouration.
