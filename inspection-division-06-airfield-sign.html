Division 06 of 25 · INS-SGN Series

Airfield Sign Inspection

A standardized methodology for assessing the operational, visual, structural, mechanical, electrical, optical, environmental, and installation condition of airfield signs. Covers mandatory signs, direction signs, location signs, information signs, runway exit signs, sign messages, character arrangements, illumination, colour, visibility, housings, faces, mounting systems, foundations, cables, connectors, internal components, LED modules/drivers, and water ingress.

Initial Issue

Critical control — Airfield sign inspection shall treat message accuracy as an operational requirement, not merely a documentation issue. A sign may be physically intact and fully illuminated but still be considered defective or operationally unacceptable if the displayed message, location identification, character arrangement, orientation, or directional information is incorrect. Visual inspection does not replace formal electrical testing, photometric testing, insulation-resistance testing, circuit testing, or detailed engineering assessment where required.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about airfield sign inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook.

Q1What is Division 06 — Airfield Sign Inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook?

Division 06 contains 89 published topics in 17 sections (A–Q). It gives a standardized methodology for assessing the operational, visual, structural, mechanical, electrical, optical, environmental and installation condition of airfield signs.

Q2Which airfield signs does Division 06 cover?

It covers mandatory signs, direction signs, location signs, information signs and runway exit signs, along with sign messages and character arrangements, illumination, colour, visibility, housings, faces, mounting systems, foundations, cables, connectors, internal components, LED modules and drivers, and water ingress.

Q3What parameters is each sign assessed against?

The common checklist includes sign type, identification, message, character arrangement, location, orientation, visibility, illumination, brightness, colour, face, housing, mounting, foundation, cable and cable entry, connector, internal wiring, LED module, LED driver, water ingress, corrosion, vegetation, obstruction and photometric condition.

Q4Does the sign inspection check the message and character arrangement?

Yes. Section G covers sign message and character condition, and the checklist records message as Correct or Incorrect and character arrangement as Correct or Abnormal, in addition to sign type, identification and location.

Q5What is the standard airfield sign inspection workflow?

The sequence identifies the sign, verifies type, location and message, and checks character arrangement, orientation, visibility, illumination and colour. It then inspects the face, housing, mounting, foundation, cable, connector, internal wiring and LED module/driver, checks water ingress, corrosion, vegetation and obstruction, performs a photometric assessment where required, and ends with classify, record, escalate, correct, verify and close.

Q6How are sign defects classified?

Defect status is recorded using the four-level scheme Normal, Defective, Unserviceable and Immediate Action Required, so every observation ends with a clear classification before it is recorded and escalated.

Q7Can an airfield sign be illuminated and still be defective?

Yes. A sign may be physically intact and fully illuminated but still be considered defective or operationally unacceptable if the displayed message, location identification, character arrangement, orientation or directional information is incorrect.

Q8Can a visual sign inspection replace formal testing?

No. Visual inspection does not replace formal electrical testing, photometric testing, insulation-resistance testing, circuit testing or detailed engineering assessment where required.

Q9Do airport procedures take precedence over this handbook?

Yes. This handbook is an uncontrolled educational reference, not an approved procedure. The airport's approved inspection schedule, SMS procedures, ATC instructions, NOTAM procedures and local aerodrome regulations always take precedence, and the current revision of any official document should be verified before use.

Q10Can I download a printable checklist for each inspection topic?

Yes. Each inspection topic has a Download as Word option that produces an uncontrolled Word checklist template (description, a tick-box inspection table and an inspector sign-off block) for personal reference and internal training, to be reviewed and adopted under the user’s own document control.
