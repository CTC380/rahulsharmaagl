Calculator #07

Runway Edge Light Spacing & Quantity

The full engineering-grade edge-light engine — station generation, per-side and total quantity, lateral offset geometry, threshold/runway-end reference logic, end-specific instrument-approach caution-zone colour, and existing-installation compliance, in one tool.

About this calculator

Calculators #01/#02 give a spacing figure and a nominal quantity. This calculator generates the actual station-by-station position schedule the spacing figure implies — chainage, left/right fixture, and directional colour at every position — referenced to the threshold/runway-end starting point as ICAO and FAA require, with the ICAO 60 m and FAA 200 ft (60.96 m) maximum-interval criteria kept as separate, selectable values rather than treated as identical.

Method & Reference

How This Calculator Works

FAA AC 150/5340-30J specifies that runway-edge lights are parallel to the runway centreline, 0.6–3 m from the edge of full-strength pavement, uniformly spaced and symmetrical, with longitudinal spacing not exceeding 200 ft (≈ 60.96 m); threshold/runway-end lights are the starting reference points for the spacing calculation.

Edge-light lateral position

0.6 m ≤ DE ≤ 3.0 m

Measured from the edge of the full-strength pavement designated for runway use. 3.0 m (jet) / 0.6 m (non-jet) is pre-set as a planning default — this calculator applies it automatically and flags any custom entry outside the 0.6–3.0 m range.

Symmetrical edge-light rows

The two rows must be parallel to the centreline, uniformly spaced, symmetrical about the centreline, with opposite lights connected by a transverse line perpendicular to the centreline (DL ≈ DR, SL ≈ SR). Because this engine derives both rows from a single DE and a single station set, that symmetry holds by construction rather than by a separate check.

Engineering quantity formula

For usable longitudinal edge-light length L and selected design spacing Sdesign:

N = ⌈ L / Sdesign ⌉  →  Sactual = L / N  →  Sactual ≤ Smax

N intervals produce N + 1 generated positions per side — the calculator builds the actual station array (chainage 0 to L) rather than assuming this count from a formula alone, since the final figure depends on the endpoint/reference convention in use.

Threshold/runway-end reference architecture

Station chainage is generated from the Threshold/Runway-End A reference point, per the FAA design rule. Where a threshold is displaced from the physical runway end, two references exist: the physical runway end (used for runway-end lighting) and the landing threshold (used for threshold/approach geometry, handled in Calculators #02 and #05). This calculator's edge-light chainage and caution-zone reference always use the physical runway-length reference, independent of any displacement.

Caution-zone colour

For MIRL/HIRL, runway edge lights are white; in the caution zone, yellow replaces white — seen facing the instrument approach threshold, white in the opposite direction:

FAA: Lcaution = min(2000 ft, L / 2)
ICAO Annex 14: Lcaution = min(600 m, L / 3)

The zone lies at the far end from the instrument approach threshold (the rollout end), so an instrument approach to A gives yellow lights near B as seen from A. Applied independently to each approach, since an instrument approach can exist at one runway end and not the other. This calculator never applies the yellow caution zone to both directions automatically — it is driven by the per-threshold instrument-approach setting above.

Existing-installation compliance

For measured positions xi, achieved spacing Si = xi − xi−1 is checked against Smax (🟢 PASS / 🔴 FAIL), and reported as a measured value rather than PASS/FAIL alone. Cumulative deviation Di = xactual,i − xdesign,i is also reported, since individually acceptable intervals can still hide a growing positional drift along the runway.

FAQ

Runway Edge Light Spacing and Quantity Calculator: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on edge-light spacing, quantity, caution-zone colour and the field verification checks.

What does the Runway Edge Light Spacing and Quantity Calculator do?

It generates the edge-light station positions along the runway, checks the achieved spacing against the applicable maximum, and reports the quantity for the left and right rows. It also derives the caution-zone colour limits, checks the lateral geometry, verifies measured positions on an existing installation, and produces a station schedule and PDF report.

What is the maximum edge-light spacing under ICAO and FAA?

ICAO Annex 14 uses 60 m for an instrument runway and 100 m for a non-instrument runway. FAA AC 150/5340-30J uses 200 ft (60.96 m). The two are kept as distinct values and are not treated as equal. For example, an achieved spacing of 60.50 m fails the ICAO 60 m limit but passes the FAA 60.96 m limit.

How is the number of edge lights calculated?

For usable length L and design spacing Sdesign, the number of intervals is N = ⌈L ÷ Sdesign⌉ and the actual spacing is Sactual = L ÷ N, which must not exceed Smax. N intervals give N + 1 positions per side. The calculator builds the actual station array from chainage 0 to L rather than relying on N + 1 alone, because the final count depends on the endpoint and reference convention.

Where should edge lights be placed relative to the runway?

FAA AC 150/5340-30J places them parallel to the centreline, 0.6 to 3.0 m from the edge of full-strength pavement, uniformly spaced and symmetrical. The calculator pre-sets 3.0 m for jet runways and 0.6 m for non-jet runways as a planning default only, so confirm the offset against the AC and the approved layout, and a custom value outside 0.6 to 3.0 m is flagged. Under ICAO the lights sit on the runway edge or up to 3 m outside it. The distance between the two rows is W + 2DE, so a 45 m runway with a 3 m offset gives 51.00 m.

How is the yellow caution zone determined?

For MIRL and HIRL, yellow replaces white in the caution zone, seen facing the instrument approach threshold, and white shows in the opposite direction. The zone length is min(2000 ft, L ÷ 2) under FAA and min(600 m, L ÷ 3) under ICAO. It lies at the far end from the instrument approach threshold, and it is applied to each approach independently.

Does a displaced threshold change the edge-light count?

No. Edge-light chainage and the caution-zone reference run from Threshold or Runway-End A across the full physical runway length. Threshold displacement affects the landing-threshold reference used for threshold lighting in Calculators #02 and #05, and is entered here for documentation only.

How does the existing-installation check work?

Enter the measured chainage of the fixtures on one side. The calculator reports the achieved spacing between each pair of fixtures against Smax as PASS or FAIL, and also reports the cumulative deviation from the design stations. The cumulative figure matters because individually acceptable intervals can still hide a growing positional drift along the runway. Verify the opposite side separately if it may differ.

Is the result a final design or BOQ?

No. The calculator produces station positions, spacing and quantity from the values entered. It does not select an ALS or threshold configuration, does not maintain a figure-specific ICAO endpoint database, and does not replace the approved aerodrome lighting layout. Where a separate runway-end or threshold fitting is provided at chainage 0 or L, count it under Calculator #02 or #05 rather than as an extra edge light here.
