01 · Overview

PAPI India at a Glance

Precision Approach Path Indicator (PAPI) is one of the most important visual approach guidance systems used at airports. It gives the pilot a visual indication of the aircraft's position relative to the selected approach slope during final approach to a runway.

A PAPI installation normally consists of a horizontal array of light units beside the runway approach end. Each unit produces a precisely controlled transition between white and red light over defined elevation angles, and the pilot interprets the combination of white and red indications to judge whether the aircraft is high, low or approximately on the intended path.

PAPI is therefore not simply a set of four lights. It is a precision optical, mechanical and electrical system that depends on correct siting, elevation, spacing, aiming, optical performance, electrical supply and maintenance.

PAPI is widely represented in India's aeronautical information publications. Current AAI eAIP entries show PAPI installations at Indian aerodromes with published approach slopes and MEHT values, including examples of 3.00° PAPI systems.[1]

For airport projects, the PAPI system should be designed, installed, tested and maintained according to the applicable ICAO provisions, DGCA requirements, AAI / project specifications, aerodrome procedures and the manufacturer's approved requirements.

02 · How PAPI Works

What Is PAPI and How Does It Work?

PAPI is a visual approach slope indicator system that provides vertical guidance to pilots. A conventional installation uses four optical light units in a row, each with a precisely established elevation setting so the white-to-red transition occurs at a controlled angular position.

4 White

Aircraft significantly high

3 White / 1 Red

Above the intended path

2 White / 2 Red

Approximately on the intended path

1 White / 3 Red

Below the intended path

4 Red

Aircraft significantly low

The fundamental principle is an optical boundary between white and red light. The four units are aimed at progressively different elevation settings — they do not simply emit four identical lights. Their optical elevation settings are deliberately offset to create the required approach-path indication. The exact operational interpretation should always follow the approved aerodrome procedure and applicable documentation.

05 · Installation & Geometry

PAPI Installation in India: Siting, Slope, Aiming, Spacing, MEHT & TCH

PAPI installation requires more than placing four light units beside the runway. It should start with an engineering review, and final installation parameters should come from the approved design and governing requirements.

Siting

Siting runs Runway Threshold → PAPI Reference Position → Aircraft Approach Path. An incorrect location can create a mismatch between the guidance given and the intended runway approach geometry — so PAPI is an aerodrome engineering activity, not an ordinary lighting installation.

  • Threshold location
  • Approach slope and PAPI elevation
  • Horizontal distance
  • Threshold crossing height
  • Obstacle clearance requirements
  • Runway longitudinal profile
  • Aircraft operational assumptions

Approach slope

PAPI establishes a visual indication corresponding to a selected approach slope. Indian AAI eAIP entries frequently publish 3.00°, but the applicable slope is an aerodrome-specific design/operational parameter and should not be assumed identical for every runway.[2] A small aiming error can change the effective visual guidance given to the pilot.

Spacing

Spacing depends on the approved design and applicable standard, and should be set from controlled survey references rather than estimated during installation.

  • Centre-to-centre spacing
  • Perpendicularity / reference line
  • Distance from runway centreline
  • Distance from threshold
  • Unit elevation
  • Alignment with approved PAPI geometry

Horizontal alignment

Elevation aiming is only part of alignment. A unit that has moved horizontally may still appear operational but introduce an alignment error. Causes include:

  • Foundation movement
  • Accidental impact
  • Loose mounting hardware
  • Pavement works
  • Maintenance activity
  • Mechanical deterioration

MEHT

Minimum Eye Height Over Threshold relates the PAPI indication to pilot eye height over the runway threshold under the defined approach geometry. AAI eAIP entries publish PAPI data together with MEHT for individual runways, for example 3.00° with a published MEHT.[4] MEHT is not simply an equipment specification: it is linked to overall approach geometry and should be calculated and verified in the approved aerodrome design.

TCH

Threshold Crossing Height is part of approach guidance design. The objective is a coherent relationship between PAPI, approach slope, threshold and runway touchdown geometry, so PAPI should be evaluated with the overall approach system, not as an isolated fitting.

PAPI vs APAPI: a conventional PAPI normally uses four light units; an Abbreviated PAPI (APAPI) uses fewer units and therefore provides a different visual indication arrangement. The choice depends on the applicable aerodrome design and operational requirements.

07 · Optics & Colour

PAPI Optical System and Colour

The optical system generates the white/red transition.

Optical system

Performance depends on light source, reflector or lens, filter, optical alignment, housing condition, cleanliness and internal configuration. A PAPI lamp that is illuminated does not automatically prove the optical system is performing correctly.

  • High-intensity directional light
  • Defined horizontal coverage
  • Defined vertical distribution
  • White light above the transition
  • Red light below the transition
  • A controlled transition zone

Colour

The white/red indication is fundamental. Incorrect colour can result from the following. Visual inspection can detect obvious problems, but where acceptance or compliance depends on measured optical performance, use appropriate test procedures.

  • Damaged filters
  • Degraded optical components
  • Incorrect replacement parts
  • Lens contamination
  • Internal optical faults
  • Wrong equipment configuration

PAPI Maintenance in India

PAPI maintenance should be proactive.

Daily / operational checks

  • Confirm system serviceability
  • Switch on and check operation
  • Visually inspect units
  • Check obvious damage and displacement

Periodic inspection

  • Clean lenses
  • Inspect optical surfaces
  • Remove vegetation
  • Check aiming
  • Inspect mechanical components and alignment

Electrical maintenance

  • Check transformers
  • Inspect connectors
  • Examine moisture ingress
  • Verify earthing
  • Inspect cables and terminations

Photometric maintenance

  • Photometric verification as required
  • Investigate low or inconsistent output
  • Verify colour
  • Check beam characteristics
  • Retest after corrective action

AAI tender documentation provides a practical example of such a structure, including daily serviceability checks, weekly angle checks, monthly calibration/alignment checks and half-yearly or pre- and post-monsoon inspections. These are contract-specific AAI requirements and should not automatically be treated as a universal frequency for every airport.[3]

Common PAPI defects

Lens contamination

Dust and environmental deposits can affect optical output.

Vegetation obstruction

Vegetation can interfere with the pilot's view of the units.

Unit misalignment

Mechanical movement can change the intended aiming angle.

Moisture ingress

Water inside the fitting can damage optical or electrical components.

Damaged optical components

Reflectors, filters and lenses can deteriorate or become damaged.

Electrical connection failure

A poor secondary connection can cause intermittent or complete unit failure.

Foundation movement

A small physical displacement can create an optical aiming error.

Incorrect replacement

Replacement parts or units must be compatible with the approved PAPI system.

13 · Low Visibility & LED

PAPI in Low-Visibility Operations and LED PAPI

Low-visibility airport operations

PAPI is a visual aid and part of the aerodrome's broader visual guidance infrastructure. Where visibility conditions are demanding, continued availability and correct performance of visual aids is especially important. PAPI, approach lighting, runway lighting, marking and instrument approach procedures must be considered as an integrated system; the specific requirements depend on the runway and approved operating category.

LED technology

Modern PAPI equipment can use LED sources. LED does not mean maintenance-free: optical inspection, cleaning, alignment verification, mechanical inspection, electrical testing, photometric verification and corrective maintenance are still required.

  • Long service life
  • Lower routine lamp replacement
  • Stable optical performance
  • Reduced maintenance burden
  • Lower energy consumption
  • Improved monitoring possibilities

14 · Project Types

PAPI for New Airports, Runway Extension, Rehabilitation and Relocation

New airport projects

Consider PAPI from the design stage: Survey → Design → Civil Foundation → PAPI Supply → Electrical Installation → Aiming → Commissioning → Photometry → Approval/Handover. It should not be treated as an isolated equipment package.

  • Runway geometry
  • Obstacle limitation considerations
  • Approach lighting and threshold markings
  • Runway centreline and edge lights
  • Instrument approach and navigation procedures
  • Maintenance access and airfield control systems

Runway extension and rehabilitation

Changing runway geometry changes the relationship between PAPI position, threshold and approach path, so leaving the existing PAPI in its old position may not be appropriate. An engineering review should be completed whenever runway geometry or threshold position changes. Triggers include a runway being:

  • Extended or shortened
  • Shifted or resurfaced
  • Reprofiled
  • Re-thresholded
  • Geometrically modified

PAPI relocation

After relocation the system should be treated as an engineering installation requiring complete verification, not merely reconnecting the old equipment.

  • Survey
  • New foundation
  • Revised coordinates and elevations
  • Electrical modification
  • New cable / transformer arrangements
  • Mechanical installation and aiming
  • Photometry and documentation
  • Operational coordination

16 · Standards & Evidence

PAPI Standards in India and Current Indian Airport Evidence

PAPI design and installation should be evaluated against the applicable standards and project documentation rather than a single generic PAPI specification.

The international reference framework includes ICAO Annex 14, Volume I, Aerodromes — Design and Operations, while detailed visual-aid guidance is also addressed in ICAO's Aerodrome Design Manual Part 4, *Visual Aids*. The ICAO Store listing shows Annex 14 Volume I as the 9th Edition (July 2022), with Amendment 18 dated 4 August 2025 and 2026 corrigenda.[7]

For Indian airports, the applicable DGCA CAR requirements, AAI technical specifications, aerodrome operating procedures and approved project documents must also be incorporated. FAA requirements should not automatically be presented as Indian or ICAO requirements; where a contract specifically invokes FAA criteria, those can be evaluated separately.

Current Indian airport evidence

PAPI is not merely a theoretical or legacy system in India. Current AAI aeronautical publications identify PAPI at numerous Indian airports, with different runway directions, PAPI side/location and published MEHT values.[2] AAI procurement documentation also includes PAPI within maintenance programmes, in-field photometric testing, airport AGL maintenance and runway visual-aid systems. For example, AAI's Rajkot International Airport photometry specification includes PAPI units in the field-testing scope, and AAI maintenance documentation details PAPI inspection and maintenance activities.[5]

19 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What does PAPI stand for?
PAPI stands for Precision Approach Path Indicator.
What is the purpose of PAPI?
PAPI provides pilots with visual guidance indicating the aircraft's position relative to the intended approach slope during final approach.
How many lights are used in a PAPI?
A conventional PAPI normally consists of four light units arranged in a horizontal row.
What do 2 white and 2 red lights mean?
The 2-white/2-red indication generally represents the intended approach-path indication for a conventional PAPI system.
Is every PAPI set to 3 degrees?
No. The applicable approach slope is determined by the approved runway and aerodrome design. However, current Indian AAI eAIP examples frequently publish PAPI at 3.00°.[2]
What is MEHT in PAPI?
MEHT means Minimum Eye Height Over Threshold. It relates the visual approach guidance provided by the PAPI to pilot eye height over the runway threshold.
Does PAPI require photometric testing?
PAPI performance can require objective optical testing, particularly for commissioning, acceptance and periodic verification as specified by the applicable programme. AAI's field photometry specifications explicitly include PAPI units.[5]
Can PAPI be installed on both runway directions?
Yes. Indian AAI eAIP publications include aerodromes where PAPI is published for both runway directions.[2]
Can an existing PAPI be relocated?
Yes, but relocation requires engineering evaluation, survey, new or modified installation works, aiming, testing and appropriate documentation.
Is LED PAPI maintenance-free?
No. LED technology can reduce some routine maintenance requirements, but optical surfaces, aiming, mechanical alignment, electrical connections and overall performance still require inspection and maintenance.
Who can handle PAPI installation in India?
PAPI installation may be performed as part of an AGL contractor's scope, subject to the airport's procurement, technical, regulatory and competency requirements.
