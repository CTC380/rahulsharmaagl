Obstruction & Associated Lighting Inspection

A standardized inspection methodology for obstruction lighting and associated aerodrome lighting assets that fall outside the main runway/taxiway AGL divisions — obstruction lights and their supporting structures, apron lighting, apron-edge lighting, apron floodlighting, windcone lighting, and windsock assemblies. Covers physical, optical, mechanical, electrical, structural, and environmental condition, together with control, monitoring, and alarm functions where applicable.

Initial Issue

Critical control — Obstruction and associated lighting should be inspected according to the specific function and installation type of each asset. A single inspection criterion should not automatically be applied to all assets, because obstruction lights, apron floodlights, and windcone systems have different operational purposes and performance requirements. This division is kept separate from the main runway/taxiway AGL inspection divisions because these assets have different functions and different inspection priorities.

Illumination alone does not establish compliance — an obstruction light being ON does not necessarily mean correct intensity/characteristic, correct visibility, and correct structural installation. A windcone light being ON does not necessarily mean the windsock assembly is serviceable and clearly visible/moving correctly. Particular attention should be given to obstruction-light availability, flashing characteristics where applicable, lens/optical contamination, visibility, structural corrosion, mounting/support integrity, cable and power connections, apron floodlight aiming, apron-edge-light condition, windcone lighting, windsock fabric and support, vegetation/environmental encroachment, and failure monitoring/alarms.

INS-OBS-1398 Obstruction-Light Condition Inspection
What's Inspected

Inspection of the complete obstruction-light installation for physical, mechanical, and environmental condition, and verification of light identification, location, structure association, asset records, and correspondence with the approved application/design.

Inspection Points
  • Identification, location, and structure association
  • Type verification against approved application/design
  • Cracks, deformation, impact damage, broken components, deterioration
  • Dust, dirt, insects, bird deposits, oil, chemical contamination
  • Effects of weather, moisture, temperature, corrosion, wind
Classify As
Normal Defective Unserviceable Immediate Action Required
INS-OBS-1404 Obstruction-Light Illumination Inspection
What's Inspected

Verification of normal visible light output, applicable ON/OFF operating state, brightness/intensity, flashing operation and flash characteristic where required, applicable colour, visibility from relevant operational viewpoints, and review of applicable photometric/test records.

Inspection Points
  • Normal visible light output and applicable operating state
  • Visibly abnormal brightness/intensity
  • Flashing operation and flash characteristic where specified
  • Applicable light colour
  • Visibility from relevant operational viewpoints
  • Applicable inspection and test record review

Normal Defective Unserviceable Immediate Action Required
INS-OBS-1412 Obstruction-Light Lens & Optics Inspection
What's Inspected

Inspection of lens/optical window and applicable optical component condition, including cracking, contamination, discoloration, internal condensation, and evidence of water entering the fixture.

Inspection Points
  • Cracks, chips, fractures, or other lens damage
  • Dirt, dust, bird deposits, insects, other contamination
  • Clouding, yellowing, browning, abnormal colour change
  • Applicable optical component condition
  • Moisture/condensation inside the optical assembly
  • Evidence of water entering the fixture

A. Standard Inspection Classification

Every observation should be assigned an appropriate status:

NormalEquipment/condition satisfactory and operating as expected.

DefectiveAbnormality or defect identified but equipment remains operational or condition does not require immediate withdrawal.

UnserviceableEquipment or system is not available for its intended function.

Immediate Action RequiredCondition presents an operational, electrical, safety, or airside risk requiring immediate escalation/action.

B. Minimum Inspection Record

Each obstruction/associated-lighting inspection record should include:

  • Date and inspection time
  • Inspector name and designation
  • Asset type and location/structure association
  • Asset identification reference
  • Inspection observation
  • Status classification per parameter
  • Defect description, where applicable
  • Immediate action taken
  • Person/department notified
  • Corrective-action reference
  • Structural/foundation repair reference, where applicable
  • Closure date/time and sign-off

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about obstruction & associated lighting inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook.

Q1What is Division 17 — Obstruction & Associated Lighting Inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook?

Division 17 contains 12 published topics in 15 sections (A–O). It gives a standardized inspection methodology for obstruction lighting and associated aerodrome lighting assets that fall outside the main runway and taxiway AGL divisions, including their supporting structures.

Q2Which assets does Division 17 cover?

It covers obstruction lights and their supporting structures, apron lighting, apron-edge lighting, apron floodlighting, windcone lighting and windsock assemblies. It considers physical, optical, mechanical, electrical, structural and environmental condition, together with control, monitoring and alarm functions where applicable.

Q3Does an obstruction light being on mean it is compliant?

No. Illumination alone does not establish compliance. An obstruction light being on does not necessarily mean the intensity or characteristic, visibility and structural installation are correct, and a windcone light being on does not necessarily mean the windsock assembly is serviceable, visible and moving correctly.

Q4What parameters are recorded for obstruction and associated lighting?

The checklist includes asset identification, fixture condition, illumination, colour, flashing and flash characteristic, visibility, lens, mounting, orientation, cable and cable entry, power connection, earthing/bonding, structural support, foundation, corrosion, water/moisture, apron lighting types, windcone lighting, windsock condition and movement, control/monitoring and alarms.

Q5What status classifications are used in this division?

Every observation is assigned one of four statuses. Normal means the equipment or condition is satisfactory and operating as expected. Defective means an abnormality or defect exists but the equipment remains operational or the condition does not require immediate withdrawal. Unserviceable means the equipment or system is not available for its intended function. Immediate Action Required means the condition presents an operational, electrical, safety or airside risk requiring immediate escalation and action.

Q6What should an obstruction or associated-lighting inspection record include?

Alongside the usual date, inspector, observation, status, action and sign-off fields, the record includes the asset type and its location or structure association, the asset identification reference, and a structural or foundation repair reference where applicable.

Q7Do airport procedures take precedence over this handbook?

Yes. This handbook is an uncontrolled educational reference, not an approved procedure. The airport's approved inspection schedule, SMS procedures, ATC instructions, NOTAM procedures and local aerodrome regulations always take precedence, and the current revision of any official document should be verified before use.

Q8Can I download a printable checklist for each inspection topic?

Yes. Each inspection topic has a Download as Word option that produces an uncontrolled Word checklist template (description, a tick-box inspection table and an inspector sign-off block) for personal reference and internal training, to be reviewed and adopted under the user’s own document control.
