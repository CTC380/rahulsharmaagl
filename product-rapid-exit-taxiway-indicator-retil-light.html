Rapid Exit Taxiway Indicator (RETIL) Light

What It Does

Rapid Exit Taxiway Indicator Lights, commonly known as RETILs, give pilots visual information about the location of the next available rapid-exit taxiway during the runway rollout.

They are installed on the runway surface in a dedicated pattern associated with the rapid-exit taxiway. The arrangement gives distance-to-go information and improves situational awareness, particularly where visibility is reduced and efficient runway exit matters operationally.

What We Supply

Precision AGL Solutions supplies and supports RETIL solutions selected according to:

  • Rapid-exit taxiway layout and location
  • Runway geometry and centreline lighting arrangement
  • RVR / visibility conditions and traffic density
  • RETIL pattern, fixture spacing and light colour
  • Optical direction and photometric requirements
  • Circuit configuration and control philosophy
  • Pavement installation and project specification

The final arrangement should always follow the approved runway lighting layout and applicable aerodrome standard.

Function of RETIL Lighting

Telling the Pilot Where the Exit Is, While Still Rolling Out

What the Lights Do

RETILs indicate the location of the next rapid-exit taxiway while the aircraft is still on the runway. This improves situational awareness so the pilot can:

  • Identify the upcoming rapid-exit taxiway
  • Estimate distance to the exit
  • Maintain an appropriate rollout strategy
  • Concentrate on runway centreline alignment
  • Apply braking and deceleration at an appropriate stage
  • Prepare for the turn onto the rapid-exit taxiway

Low-Visibility Value

In low-visibility conditions RETILs provide useful situational-awareness information while allowing the pilot to remain focused on runway centreline tracking.

They supplement, rather than replace, runway centreline lighting: centreline lights give longitudinal alignment, RETILs give upcoming exit location and distance.

RETIL Visual Pattern

The 3-2-1 Sequence

RETILs use a distinctive pattern rather than a continuous line of lights. Under the ICAO arrangement the sequence gives progressive distance information toward the rapid exit: groups of 3, 2 and 1 lights at approximately 100 m intervals from the associated exit reference, per the applicable layout. A complete RETIL set is six lights (3 + 2 + 1). The Annex 14 figure places the groups at 300 m, 200 m and 100 m before the rapid-exit taxiway point of tangency, so the last single light sits about 100 m from it. The exact positioning must be taken from the approved project drawing.

Location of RETILs

Same Side as the Associated Rapid Exit

Positioning

RETILs are installed on the same side of the runway centreline as the associated rapid-exit taxiway. The applicable ICAO figure relates the runway centreline, RETIL set, light spacing, rapid-exit taxiway and point of tangency.

ICAO provisions specify lights spaced 2 m apart, with the light nearest the runway centreline displaced 2 m from the centreline.

Multiple Rapid-Exit Taxiways

Where a runway has more than one rapid exit, RETIL patterns must be arranged so the indication for one exit does not overlap that for another. Layout review should consider:

  • Exit-to-exit separation
  • RETIL pattern visibility and overlap
  • Runway length
  • Point of tangency
  • Runway centreline
  • Adjacent exit geometry
Light Colour & Operation

Fixed, Unidirectional, Yellow

Colour & Direction

ICAO specifies RETILs as fixed, unidirectional yellow lights, aligned to be visible to the pilot of a landing aircraft in the direction of approach. Evaluate fixtures for:

  • Yellow colour and chromaticity
  • Unidirectional output
  • Beam distribution and intensity
  • Optical direction
  • Visibility from the landing direction

Not a Flashing Warning Light

RETILs are fundamentally different from Runway Guard Lights, REIL systems and flashing runway warning lights. They are specified as fixed lights, not an alternating flashing warning system.

This distinction matters for product classification and technical procurement.

System Integration

RETIL in the Runway-Exit Guidance Sequence

Runway Centreline Guidance → RETIL Exit Indication → Rapid-Exit Turn → Rapid-Exit Taxiway Centreline → Taxiway Network

Design should consider RVR, traffic density, rapid-exit availability, aircraft operating characteristics, runway occupancy objectives, existing centreline lighting, ambient background lighting and adjacent exit geometry, so that RETILs give useful distance information without ambiguity with other runway visual aids. Once the aircraft begins turning onto the exit, taxiway centreline lighting becomes the principal route guidance.

Standards & References

Governing Standards

Design, select, install and test against the applicable edition of the governing aerodrome standard and approved project specification.

ICAO

Annex 14 — Aerodromes, Volume I (9th Edition, July 2022, with Amendment 18 at the time of writing), Chapter 5 (rapid exit taxiway indicator lights) establishes the principal characteristics:

  • Application to low-RVR / heavy-traffic runways
  • Prescribed 3-2-1 pattern at 100 m intervals before the rapid-exit taxiway point of tangency
  • Same side of centreline as the rapid exit
  • 2 m spacing; nearest light 2 m from centreline
  • No overlap between different rapid exits
  • Fixed unidirectional yellow lights, visible in landing direction

Colour and photometric requirements sit in Annex 14 Appendices 1 and 2. Section numbers change between editions, so check the clause against your copy. Doc 9157 Part 4 (Visual Aids) and Part 5 (Electrical Systems) give design guidance.

Guidance also addresses separate-circuit supply.

FAA

FAA practice does not use RETIL as a direct equivalent. Runway exits are lit with taxiway centreline lead-off lights under AC 150/5340-30J, and distance-to-go information is given by runway distance-remaining signs (AC 150/5340-18H).

On an FAA project, verify whether the design calls for RETILs, rapid-exit taxiway centreline lighting or another FAA-approved arrangement.

India

For Indian airport projects also verify:

  • DGCA requirements
  • Airport-specific AGL specifications
  • Approved rapid-exit taxiway layout
  • Centreline-light spacing and RVR / low-visibility procedures
  • Project BOQ and Employer's requirements
  • Applicable Indian Standards and airport operator requirements

Always verify the applicable document edition and project-specific requirements before procurement, technical approval or installation.

Rapid Exit Taxiway Indicator (RETIL) Light — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a rapid exit taxiway indicator light (RETIL)?

RETILs give pilots visual information about the location of the next available rapid-exit taxiway during the runway rollout. They are installed on the runway surface in a dedicated pattern associated with the rapid-exit taxiway, give distance-to-go information and improve situational awareness, particularly where visibility is reduced.

What is the RETIL 3-2-1 pattern?

The ICAO arrangement uses groups of 3, 2 and 1 lights at approximately 100 m intervals before the rapid exit, so a complete RETIL set is six lights. The Annex 14 figure places the groups 300 m, 200 m and 100 m before the rapid-exit taxiway point of tangency, so the last single light sits about 100 m from it. Take the exact positions from the approved project drawing.

Where are RETILs installed relative to the runway centreline?

RETILs are installed on the same side of the runway centreline as the associated rapid-exit taxiway. ICAO provisions specify lights spaced 2 m apart, with the light nearest the runway centreline displaced 2 m from the centreline. Where a runway has more than one rapid exit, the patterns must be arranged so that the indication for one exit does not overlap another.

What colour and mode of operation do RETILs use?

ICAO specifies RETILs as fixed, unidirectional yellow lights, aligned to be visible to the pilot of a landing aircraft in the direction of approach. They are fixed lights, not a flashing warning system.

When does ICAO recommend RETILs?

ICAO Annex 14 recommends RETILs on runways intended for use in RVR below 350 m and/or where traffic density is heavy. Confirm the figure against your edition of the standard.

How does a RETIL differ from a runway guard light or a runway centreline light?

A RETIL is a fixed yellow light indicating the location and distance of the next rapid exit, while a runway guard light is an alternating or flashing runway-entry warning at a holding position. RETILs supplement rather than replace runway centreline lighting: centreline lights give longitudinal alignment, and RETILs give upcoming exit location and distance. Once the aircraft turns onto the exit, taxiway centreline lighting becomes the principal route guidance.

Do RETILs need a separate circuit?

ICAO inspection guidance identifies provision for RETILs to be supplied from a separate circuit from other runway lighting, so that they can be used when other runway lighting is switched off. Where this applies, verify the dedicated circuit and CCR arrangement, circuit protection and switching, monitoring, transformer loading and control-system integration.

What happens if one RETIL light fails?

ICAO provides that the RETIL pattern should not be displayed when a lamp failure or other failure prevents the full prescribed pattern from being shown, to avoid presenting an incomplete or misleading indication. Design and control considerations include fault monitoring, inhibit logic and maintenance indication.

Does the FAA use RETILs?

FAA practice does not use RETIL as a direct equivalent. Runway exits are lit with taxiway centreline lead-off lights under AC 150/5340-30J, and distance-to-go information is given by runway distance-remaining signs under AC 150/5340-18H. On an FAA project, verify whether the design calls for RETILs, rapid-exit taxiway centreline lighting or another FAA-approved arrangement.

What are the common faults in a RETIL installation?

One light off usually points to an LED or light-source failure, a secondary cable fault, a connector problem, a transformer fault or an internal fixture failure. A complete pattern off points to the CCR or circuit, primary cable, control system or pattern-inhibit logic. An incomplete pattern, an incorrect position from a set-out error or pavement reconstruction, and poor visibility from contamination or beam orientation are also common. Troubleshoot from control to pattern.

What should be confirmed before ordering RETIL lights?

Confirm the runway and rapid-exit taxiway identification, runway length, centreline-light spacing, RVR and traffic-density requirement, the RETIL pattern and number of sets, same-side arrangement, 2 m spacing and 2 m centreline offset, and fixed, unidirectional yellow output. Also confirm the series-circuit arrangement and any dedicated circuit, isolating transformer, connector type, housing and installation depth, pavement cut-out and drainage, monitoring, and the datasheet, photometric and compliance documentation.

Engineering Note

RETILs are a specialised runway visual aid intended to provide distance-to-go information for a rapid-exit taxiway. Their effectiveness depends on the complete relationship between runway geometry, RETIL pattern, fixture spacing, 2 m centreline offset, yellow optical output, landing-direction visibility, circuit reliability and rapid-exit taxiway location.

For projects using FAA standards, do not automatically treat an ICAO RETIL configuration as an FAA-equivalent product. Verify the project-specific FAA visual-aid design and approved fixture requirements before procurement.
