Division-Wide Requirements

Common classification scheme, quality gates, minimum records and the maintenance-complete vs. asset-restored control that apply across all 12 Division 22 inspection topics.

A. Standard Inspection Classification

Every observation should be assigned an appropriate status:

NormalAsset fully restored and satisfactory for return to service.

DefectiveAbnormality identified but equipment remains operational or condition does not require immediate withdrawal.

UnserviceableEquipment or system is not available for its intended function.

Immediate Action RequiredCondition presents an operational, electrical, safety, or airside risk requiring immediate escalation/action.

B. Post-Maintenance Quality Gates

The eight sequential gates between maintenance activity and restored serviceability:

1

Work — Was the maintenance actually completed?

2

Physical — Correctly reassembled, mounted and restored?

3

Electrical — Connections, cables and earthing restored?

4

Functional — Does the equipment/system operate correctly?

5

Optical — Photometric performance acceptable where applicable?

6

Environmental — Work area, pit, pavement and surroundings restored?

7

Documentation — All required records complete and traceable?

8

Return to Service — Required authorization/approval completed?

C. Minimum Post-Maintenance Record

Each post-maintenance inspection record should include:

  • Maintenance work-order reference
  • Maintenance report/scope verification
  • Original defect and closure status
  • Replacement-component record
  • Workmanship assessment
  • Test records
  • Calibration evidence (instruments used)
  • Photometry evidence, where applicable
  • As-maintained/asset record update
  • Maintenance-history update
  • Rework requirement/completion, if any
  • Return-to-service authorization

D. Maintenance Complete vs. Asset Restored

Maintenance complete means

  • The technician has completed the assigned repair/replacement activity.

Asset restored requires the inspection to confirm

  • Correct component installed, connections secure
  • Cable/connector condition and earthing/bonding acceptable
  • Fixture/equipment correctly positioned
  • Required testing complete, functional operation satisfactory
  • Photometric condition satisfactory where applicable
  • Work area, pit/base/trench restored and FOD removed
  • Documentation complete, original defect closed

Critical control — Only when the asset is confirmed restored — not merely when maintenance is reported complete — should the activity be considered fully closed, subject to the airport's applicable authorization process.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about post-maintenance inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook.

Q1What is Division 22 — Post-Maintenance Inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook?

Division 22 contains 12 published topics (Level 2, post-repair and post-replacement). It gives a standardized methodology for verifying that an AGL maintenance activity was properly completed, the equipment restored to the required condition, all affected connections secured, the work area restored, and the system fit for return to service.

Q2Is maintenance complete once the repair is finished?

No. Maintenance is not complete merely because the repair is finished. Inspection should verify the complete restoration chain before return to service, and the activity is considered fully closed only when the asset is confirmed restored, subject to the airport's applicable authorization process.

Q3What is the difference between maintenance complete and asset restored?

Maintenance complete means the technician has finished the assigned repair or replacement. Asset restored requires the inspection to confirm the correct component is installed and connections are secure, cable, connector, earthing and bonding are acceptable, the equipment is positioned correctly, required testing is complete and operation is satisfactory, photometric condition is satisfactory where applicable, the work area is restored with FOD removed, and documentation is complete with the original defect closed.

Q4What are the post-maintenance quality gates?

There are eight sequential gates: work, physical, electrical, functional, optical, environmental, documentation and return to service. Each asks whether that part of the restoration is done, from whether the maintenance was actually completed through to whether the required authorization or approval was obtained.

Q5What should a post-maintenance record include?

It includes the maintenance work-order reference, report and scope verification, the original defect and closure status, the replacement-component record, workmanship assessment, test records, calibration evidence, photometry evidence where applicable, as-maintained asset record update, maintenance-history update, any rework requirement and completion, and the return-to-service authorization.

Q6What status classifications are used in this division?

Normal means the asset is fully restored and satisfactory for return to service. Defective means an abnormality exists but the equipment remains operational or does not need immediate withdrawal. Unserviceable means the equipment or system is not available for its intended function. Immediate Action Required means the condition presents an operational, electrical, safety or airside risk requiring immediate escalation and action.

Q7Do airport procedures take precedence over this handbook?

Yes. This handbook is an uncontrolled educational reference, not an approved procedure. The airport's approved inspection schedule, SMS procedures, ATC instructions, NOTAM procedures and local aerodrome regulations always take precedence, and the current revision of any official document should be verified before use.

Q8Can I download a printable checklist for each inspection topic?

Yes. Each inspection topic has a Download as Word option that produces an uncontrolled Word checklist template (description, a tick-box inspection table and an inspector sign-off block) for personal reference and internal training, to be reviewed and adopted under the user’s own document control.
