Earthing & Bonding Failures

The dedicated failure-classification section for AGL earthing, bonding, earth-electrode and lightning/surge-protection infrastructure — covering failures from the main earth network through individual AGL assets, including fixtures, isolating transformers, airfield signs, CCRs, panels and earth pits. Do not record an earthing or bonding fault simply as “earth fault.” Identify the failure mode, electrical condition, physical evidence, probable cause, root cause, corrective action and recurrence risk.

Initial Issue

Key principle — An earthing or bonding failure should be recorded separately from the equipment failure that it causes. For example: loose earth connection → poor bonding → high earth resistance/earth continuity problem → equipment safety risk. Every record should answer: what failed → how did it fail → why did it fail → what evidence confirmed it → how was it corrected → how can recurrence be prevented?

EBF-01 High Earth Resistance
Failure Definition

Measured earth resistance is higher than the applicable design, project, maintenance or acceptance requirement.

Possible Causes
  • Earth-electrode deterioration
  • Poor soil contact
  • Corrosion
  • Loose connection
  • Broken earth conductor
  • Inadequate earthing installation
  • Earth-pit deterioration
Possible Consequences
  • Ineffective fault-current path
  • Increased touch/step potential risk
  • Reduced protection effectiveness
  • Poor lightning/surge discharge path
Investigation
  • Earth resistance measurement
  • Earth conductor
  • Earth connections
  • Earth electrode
  • Earth pit
  • Corrosion
  • Bonding connections
  • Test records/trend history

EBF-02 Earth Continuity Failure
Failure Definition

The required electrical continuity of the earthing conductor/bonding path is not maintained.

Possible Causes
  • Broken conductor
  • Loose connection
  • Corrosion
  • Missing bonding link
  • Damaged terminal
  • Incorrect installation
Consequences
  • Equipment may become inadequately bonded
  • Fault-current path may be compromised
  • Protection performance may be affected
Investigation

Verify continuity from the relevant equipment to the designated earthing point/network.

EBF-04 Loose Earth Connection
Failure Definition

Earth conductor or bonding connection is not securely connected to its designated termination.

Possible Causes
  • Improper tightening
  • Vibration
  • Poor installation
  • Thermal cycling
  • Mechanical disturbance
Consequences
  • High contact resistance
  • Intermittent earth continuity
  • Poor bonding
  • Potential overheating at the connection
Corrective Action

Inspect, clean where appropriate, correctly secure and verify the connection according to the applicable procedure/specification.

EBF-06 Poor Bonding
Failure Definition

Bonding between conductive components/equipment is inadequate to maintain the intended equipotential and electrical connection.

Possible Causes
  • Loose connection
  • Corrosion
  • Incorrect bonding conductor
  • Missing bonding point
  • Poor installation
  • Inadequate contact surface
Consequences
  • Potential differences between conductive parts
  • Poor fault-current path
  • Equipment safety risk
  • Increased susceptibility to transient events
Failure Chain
LOOSE EARTH CONNECTIONPOOR BONDINGEARTH CONTINUITY DEGRADATIONPROTECTION/SAFETY-PATH DEGRADATION
EBF-07 Missing Bonding Connection
Failure Definition

A required bonding connection is absent.

Possible Causes
  • Installation omission
  • Maintenance error
  • Modification without restoration
  • Damaged/removed bonding conductor
  • Incorrect installation
Investigation

Compare the installed bonding arrangement against the approved design/installation requirement.

Important Distinction

Missing bonding connection is the failure mode. Poor bonding is the condition resulting from inadequate bonding.

EBF-18 Earth Fault Due to Poor Bonding
Failure Definition

An electrical earth fault or abnormal condition is associated with inadequate or defective bonding.

Possible Causes
  • Missing bonding
  • Loose bonding
  • Corroded bonding
  • Broken conductor
  • Incorrect installation
Investigation

Determine: fault location, bonding condition, earth continuity, conductor condition, connection resistance, equipment affected, root cause.

Important Classification

Do not record only “earth fault.” Where evidence establishes defective bonding as the underlying mechanism, record “earth fault due to poor bonding” and identify the specific bonding failure separately.

Moderate — reduced performance, not yet critical

Minor / Non-Critical — limited or no operational effect

  • Earth resistance measurement and trend history

H. Failure → RCA → Action → Reliability Loop

Every earthing/bonding failure should eventually become engineering intelligence:

FAILURE EVENT FAILURE MODE FAILURE MECHANISM ROOT CAUSE CORRECTIVE ACTION PREVENTIVE ACTION POST-REPAIR TEST

For example: High earth resistance reading → traced to electrode deterioration → corrosion of electrode connection → environmental exposure → electrode repair/replacement → review protective treatment → post-repair earth resistance test.

Failure Database vs. Troubleshooting Atlas

The Troubleshooting Atlas answers “I have a fault now — how do I diagnose and fix it?” The Failure Database answers “this failure happened — what happened, why, what evidence proves it, what was done, and how do we stop it recurring?” Kept strictly separate, then linked end-to-end, the two form a genuine AGL Failure Intelligence Engine rather than a simple fault list. Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about AGL earthing, bonding and surge-protection failures, as covered in Division 15 of the AGL Failure Database.

Q1What is Division 15 — Earthing & Bonding Failures in the AGL Failure Database?

Division 15 covers AGL earthing, bonding, earth-electrode and lightning or surge-protection failures. It contains 18 published topics (EBF-01 to EBF-18), including high earth resistance, earth continuity failure, broken earth conductor, poor bonding and lightning and surge protection failure.

Q2Which earthing and bonding failures does Division 15 cover?

High earth resistance, earth continuity failure, broken earth conductor, loose earth connection, corroded earth connection, poor bonding, missing bonding connection, fixture, transformer and sign bonding failure, CCR earth failure, panel earth failure, earth-pit and earth-electrode deterioration, lightning-protection failure, surge-protection failure, incorrect earthing installation and earth fault due to poor bonding.

Q3How are the 18 earthing and bonding topics grouped?

They fall into six groups: Earth Electrical Performance (2 topics), Earth Conductor & Connection Failures (5), Equipment Bonding Failures (5), Earth-Pit & Electrode Failures (2), Lightning & Surge Protection (2) and Installation & Consequential Failures (2).

Q4How can an earthing failure be traced to a specific AGL asset?

Division 15 lets failures be traced from the main earth network to the individual bonding point. Traces run from a runway or taxiway fixture through its bonding conductor, from an isolation transformer enclosure earth point, from an airfield sign structure bond, from a CCR enclosure earth terminal and from a panel body earth terminal, each back to the earth network, and finally from the earth connection and electrode to the surrounding earth.

Q5What earthing failure chains does Division 15 capture?

In the connection chain, a loose earth connection leads to poor bonding, earth continuity degradation and degradation of the protection or safety path. In the corrosion chain, moisture or salt exposure leads to a corroded earth connection, increased resistance, high earth resistance and earthing-system degradation.

Q6Where should the investigation start for an earthing or bonding failure?

The diagnostic matrix assigns a primary investigation to each topic. High earth resistance starts with earth-resistance measurement and trend, earth continuity failure with the continuity path, broken earth conductor with conductor condition, loose earth connection with connection integrity, corroded earth connection with corrosion and contact condition, poor bonding with bonding integrity, and missing bonding connection with design versus installed bond.

Q7How does an earthing failure become engineering intelligence?

Every failure follows a loop: failure event, failure mode, failure mechanism, root cause, corrective action, preventive action and post-repair test. For example, a high earth-resistance reading is traced to electrode deterioration, then to corrosion of the electrode connection caused by environmental exposure, leading to electrode repair or replacement.

Q8How are failure severity and operational impact classified?

Every failure receives a severity classification, plus operational-impact tagging. The classes are Critical / Safety-Critical (immediate operational or safety risk), Major / Operationally Critical (significant availability impact), Moderate (reduced performance, not yet critical), Minor / Non-Critical (limited or no operational effect), and Degradation / Intermittent / Latent (a developing or hidden fault).

Q9How does the Failure Database differ from the Troubleshooting Atlas?

The Troubleshooting Atlas answers “I have a fault now — how do I diagnose and fix it?” The Failure Database answers “this failure happened — what happened, why, what evidence proves it, what was done, and how do we stop it recurring?” Kept strictly separate, then linked end to end, the two form an AGL Failure Intelligence Engine rather than a simple fault list.
