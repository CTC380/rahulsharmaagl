What does the DGCA CAR require for aerodrome lighting in India?

The requirements sit in CAR Section 4 Series B Part I, whose paragraphs follow ICAO Annex 14. At a given airport they are applied through AIP India, its supplements and the owner’s specification.

The Fact Requirement map

CAR Section 4 Series B Part I, Aerodrome Design & Operations, is where DGCA sets the aerodrome lighting requirements. It is issued under India’s aviation law, now the Bharatiya Vayuyan Adhiniyam, 2024, which replaced the Aircraft Act, 1934.

The CAR uses the same paragraph numbers as Annex 14 in every paragraph shown on this page: visual aids in Chapter 5, obstacle marking in Chapter 6, electrical systems in paragraph 8. That makes it easy to read the CAR against the ICAO text, but a lighting requirement is still incomplete without the AIP and the project documents.

The CAR is amended in revisions, and DGCA issues drafts for comment first. Quote the revision and date with each paragraph, and check DGCA’s CAR page for the latest.

  1. Identify the airport and the design date. Revisions and supplements change over time.
  2. Find the paragraph in the CAR. Start from the Annex 14 subject: lights in 5.3, power in paragraph 8.
  3. Check AIP GEN 1.7. It lists where India differs from ICAO.
  4. Check AIP Supplements for the airport. LVP and other airport-specific procedures sit here.
  5. Check the tender and aerodrome manual. The owner may ask for more than the CAR.
  6. Record the revision and the source of each value.
Read CAR, AIP and project documents

The design follows the rule, the published differences and the airport’s own procedures, so a reviewer finds each value traceable.

Read Annex 14 or the CAR alone

A value may match the Annex yet miss an Indian difference, an airport supplement or an owner’s stricter requirement.

WORKED EXAMPLE

How a supplement applies the CAR: Bhopal

AAI’s low visibility procedures for Bhopal show the layers working together. The supplement does not restate the CAR. It points to Table 8-1 and sets out what the airport does about it.

Read the supplement itself. The summary above lists headline items. The full text names each light, time and procedure, so check it before sizing a UPS or generator for this airport.

CHANGES AND TIMING

The CAR, the supplements and ICAO move separately

The three documents below changed within weeks of each other in late 2025. Treat the dates as a reminder to check the revision your project works to.

  1. DGCA DRAFTNEXT
    Issued 4 Oct 2025 · comments closed 3 Nov 2025Check for the final text

    Draft amendment to CAR Section 4 Series B Part I. Adds a definition of ground handling to Chapter 1 and, in Chapter 3, an apron sub-section asking designers to consider criteria for safe ground handling. A final text had not been confirmed when this page was checked in November 2026.

  2. AIP SUPP204
    AAI · issued 16 Oct 2025 · effective 27 Nov 2025

    Low visibility procedures for Bhopal, with Category II operations down to 350 m RVR. It cites Table 8-1 of the CAR for switch-over times.

  3. ICAO AMDT18
    Applicable 27 Nov 2025 for aerodrome design, visual aids and apron management

    The ICAO baseline the CAR follows. It harmonises lighting for Category II operations below 350 m and adds ground handling provisions, which apply from 26 Nov 2026 (obstacle limitation surface changes follow on 21 Nov 2030). Whether and how India has taken each change is for the CAR and AIP GEN 1.7 to say.

THREE LAYERS TO READ TOGETHER

The rule, the publication and the project

An Indian lighting requirement is complete only when all three have been read for the same airport and date.

The CAR

What is required

The rule for the aerodrome. Read the paragraph, note the revision and date, and keep the Annex 14 paragraph beside it for comparison.

AIP and supplements

What is published

GEN 1.7 for differences from ICAO, and supplements for procedures at your airport, such as LVP. Check what is current on the date of your design.

Tender and manual

What the owner asks

The tender specification and aerodrome manual can add detail or a stricter value. Where they disagree with the CAR, ask the airport operator which governs.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1Where are the aerodrome lighting requirements in the DGCA CAR?

In CAR Section 4 Series B Part I, Aerodrome Design & Operations. Its paragraph numbers follow ICAO Annex 14: lights fall under visual aids in Chapter 5 (for example 5.3.18.3 for taxiway edge lights), obstacle marking is in Chapter 6, and secondary power switch-over times are in Table 8-1 of paragraph 8.

Q2Is the DGCA CAR the same as ICAO Annex 14?

In the paragraphs checked for this page, the CAR uses the same numbering and subject order as Annex 14. India publishes its differences from ICAO in AIP India, section GEN 1.7. Read the CAR paragraph and that section together, and see the next entry for the differences in detail.

Q3Where are the backup power switch-over times?

In Table 8-1 of paragraph 8 of CAR Section 4 Series B Part I. AAI's AIP Supplement 204/2025 for Bhopal cites this table and sets changeover times of under one second for key lights on a Category II runway. Read the table itself for the full list of lighting aids and times.

Q4Does an AIP Supplement change the CAR?

No. A supplement publishes information for one aerodrome, such as low visibility procedures, and cites the CAR for the requirement it applies. Use it to see how the CAR is being applied at that airport, not as a replacement for the CAR.

Q5Has India adopted ICAO Amendment 18?

DGCA issued a draft amendment to CAR Section 4 Series B Part I on 4 October 2025, with comments due by 3 November 2025. It adds a definition of ground handling and apron design criteria for safe ground handling, which are topics Amendment 18 also covers. Check DGCA's CAR page and AIP GEN 1.7 for the final text and for any difference India keeps.

Q6How should I cite the CAR?

Give the series and part, the paragraph, and the revision and date shown on the CAR, for example CAR Section 4 Series B Part I, paragraph 5.3.18.3. DGCA amends the CAR through draft and final revisions, so a paragraph number alone does not tell a reviewer which text you used.
