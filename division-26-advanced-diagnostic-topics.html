Advanced Diagnostic Topics

A cross-system reference of AGL fault-isolation methods — not tied to a single fixture type, but applicable across every division in the Atlas. Covers electrical isolation techniques (current, voltage, insulation resistance, resistance, continuity, clamp, thermal), inspection-based methods (visual, photometry), data-driven methods (event logs, CCR alarms, ALCMS data, maintenance history, failure pattern analysis, comparative measurement), and structural techniques (sectional testing, component substitution, circuit segmentation, environmental reproduction, and root cause confirmation). Each topic follows the full engineering diagnostic record: application → system/method scope → diagnostic indicators → procedure → test points → confirmation → follow-up action → best practice.

ADT-601 Fault Isolation Using Circuit Current

Application — Used whenever a series circuit (constant-current loop) shows abnormal behaviour — a dark fixture, dim run, tripped CCR, or unexpected current reading — to localise the fault to a circuit segment or component before physical excavation or fixture removal.

System / Method Scope

Series (constant-current) AGL circuits fed by a CCR, including runway/taxiway edge, centreline, and approach light loops.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighCurrent below the commanded step setpoint indicates a shorted or leaking component (transformer, cable) loading down the loop
HighCurrent at zero with the CCR healthy indicates an open-circuit fault somewhere in the loop
MediumCurrent within tolerance but fluctuating indicates an intermittent connection or partial short
MediumCurrent normal at the CCR but individual fixtures dark indicates a fixture-level (not circuit-level) fault
LowCurrent slightly high/low within tolerance band may simply reflect normal circuit loading variation
Required Tools
Clamp-on ammeter (true-RMS, AC)CCR output current display/ALCMS current trend logCircuit layout drawingPortable current probe for segment testing
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before opening any base can or joint for current-loop testing beyond clamp measurement at the CCR.
  • Series circuits can present hazardous voltages well beyond the immediate fault location.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationRecord the CCR’s commanded current step and compare to the actual output current shown on the CCR/ALCMS display.
  2. MeasurementClamp-measure current at accessible points along the loop (pull boxes, base cans) working from the CCR outward to bracket the fault.
  3. DecisionCurrent drops to zero beyond a specific point → open circuit between the last good and first zero reading. Current lower than setpoint throughout → a shorted/leaking component somewhere on the loop.
  4. Fault LocationNarrow the fault to the segment or component bracketed by the current readings.
  5. Root CauseProceed to component-level testing (insulation resistance, continuity) at the identified segment to confirm the specific fault.
Test Points
CCR output current vs. commanded step
Current at intermediate pull boxes/base cans
Current stability over multiple intensity steps
Confirmation of Findings

After repair, re-measure current at the CCR and at the previously bracketed segment and confirm it matches the commanded setpoint within tolerance across all steps.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Proceed to the appropriate component-level repair (cable, transformer, connector) once the current measurement has localised the fault.
  • Document the bracketing readings in the maintenance record for future reference.
Best Practice

Always record baseline current readings at commissioning and during routine PM visits so a future fault can be diagnosed against a known-good reference; test current at multiple intensity steps, not just one, since some faults only manifest under load.

Related References

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

ADT-602 Fault Isolation Using Circuit Voltage

Application — Used to localise faults in parallel or low-voltage circuits, or to check voltage drop across specific components (transformers, connectors, cable runs) within a series circuit, where a current reading alone does not pinpoint the exact fault location.

System / Method Scope

Parallel AGL circuits (e.g., obstruction lights, some sign circuits) and voltage-drop testing across components within series circuits.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighVoltage drop across a specific segment or component significantly exceeds the expected value, indicating high resistance at that point
HighZero voltage at a downstream point with normal voltage upstream indicates an open circuit between the two points
MediumVoltage present but below expected value at a fixture indicates a marginal connection or partial fault
MediumVoltage normal at the source but not at the fixture indicates cable or connector loss along the run
LowSmall voltage variations within tolerance may reflect normal cable length/resistance effects
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeterVoltage drop test leadsCircuit layout drawing with expected voltage valuesInsulated probes rated for the circuit voltage
Safety Precautions
  • Confirm the rated voltage of the circuit before testing (some AGL secondary circuits operate at several kV).
  • Use properly rated PPE and test equipment; never assume a circuit is de-energised based on a dark fixture alone.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationIdentify the circuit topology (series vs. parallel) and the expected voltage at each test point per the design drawing.
  2. MeasurementMeasure voltage at accessible points progressively along the circuit, from source to the affected fixture(s).
  3. DecisionVoltage drop concentrated across one segment/component → that segment/component is the fault. Voltage present but low throughout → check source/supply voltage itself.
  4. Fault LocationNarrow the fault to the specific segment or component showing the abnormal voltage drop.
  5. Root CauseConfirm with resistance/insulation testing on the identified segment or component once isolated.
Test Points
Source/supply voltage
Voltage at intermediate junctions
Voltage drop across individual components (connectors, transformers)
Voltage at the affected fixture
Confirmation of Findings

After repair, re-measure voltage at all previously tested points and confirm values match the expected design voltages within tolerance.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Repair or replace the component or segment identified as causing the abnormal voltage drop.
  • Update the circuit voltage reference log with as-found and as-left values.
Best Practice

Maintain an up-to-date voltage reference table for each circuit at commissioning so future voltage-drop testing has a valid baseline for comparison; test at multiple points systematically rather than jumping straight to the suspected fixture.

Related References

SOP: SOP-ADT-02 (Voltage Drop Fault Isolation Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30, IEC 60364

ADT-603 Fault Isolation Using Insulation Resistance

Application — Used to detect and localise insulation breakdown in cables, transformers, and fixture wiring — the primary method for identifying cable faults, water ingress, and winding failures before they cause a circuit-level outage.

System / Method Scope

Primary series cable, isolation transformers, and fixture internal wiring across all AGL circuit types.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighInsulation resistance well below the minimum acceptable value on a specific segment indicates a confirmed insulation fault
HighInsulation resistance that improves significantly after drying/isolating a wet section confirms water ingress as the cause
MediumInsulation resistance trending downward over successive PM cycles indicates degrading insulation before outright failure
MediumPrimary-to-secondary insulation failure on a transformer indicates internal winding breakdown
LowMarginally low but still-passing readings may simply reflect cable age/length and should be trended rather than acted on immediately
Required Tools
1000V/2500V insulation resistance tester (megger)Circuit isolation/LOTO equipmentCable route and joint location drawingMoisture meter (optional, for base can/pull box assessment)
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit fully before connecting a megger; insulation testing applies a high test voltage and must never be performed on an energised or improperly isolated circuit.
  • Warn any personnel who might be working elsewhere on the same loop.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm full circuit isolation and LOTO before connecting test leads.
  2. MeasurementMegger the cable/component from accessible points (pull boxes, base cans, transformer terminals), working segment by segment.
  3. DecisionA specific segment or component fails the insulation test while adjacent segments pass → fault localised to that segment/component. All segments pass but the full loop fails as one length → suspect a specific joint or connection not accessible for direct segment testing.
  4. Fault LocationPhysically expose or access the failing segment/component for repair or replacement.
  5. Root CauseOnce exposed, visually confirm the specific failure mode (cut insulation, water tracking, winding breakdown) for the maintenance record.
Test Points
Insulation resistance segment-by-segment along the cable route
Transformer primary-to-earth, secondary-to-earth, and primary-to-secondary
Fixture internal wiring to earth
Confirmation of Findings

After repair, megger the full affected loop end-to-end and confirm it meets or exceeds the minimum acceptable insulation resistance before re-energising.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Repair or replace the cable segment, joint, or transformer identified as failing.
  • Address any water ingress source found during exposure of the fault.
Best Practice

Include full-loop insulation resistance testing in the periodic PM programme and trend the results year over year per segment to catch degrading insulation before it causes an outage.

Related References

SOP: SOP-ADT-03 (Insulation Resistance Test Procedure), SOP-RCL-02 (Series Circuit Fault Location Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

ADT-604 Fault Isolation Using Resistance Measurement

Application — Used to measure the DC resistance of conductors, connections, and components directly, distinct from insulation testing, to identify high-resistance joints, corroded connectors, or an out-of-spec winding/loop resistance.

System / Method Scope

Cable conductors, connectors, splices, and low-voltage component windings across AGL circuits.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighMeasured loop or conductor resistance significantly higher than the value calculated from cable length and gauge indicates a high-resistance connection or partial conductor damage
HighA connector or splice reading noticeably higher resistance than an equivalent good connection elsewhere on the same circuit indicates corrosion or poor mating
MediumResistance within tolerance but trending upward across PM cycles indicates a developing connection issue
MediumComponent (e.g., relay coil, choke) resistance outside the manufacturer’s specified range indicates internal component degradation
LowMinor resistance variation between otherwise identical components may reflect normal manufacturing tolerance
Required Tools
Low-resistance ohmmeter/micro-ohmmeterCalculated reference resistance table (by cable gauge and length)Manufacturer component resistance specifications
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before any direct resistance measurement.
  • Do not measure resistance on an energised circuit or component.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCalculate the expected resistance for the conductor/component under test from its known length, gauge, and material, or reference the manufacturer specification.
  2. MeasurementMeasure actual resistance at the component or along the conductor, isolating suspect connectors/splices individually where possible.
  3. DecisionMeasured resistance significantly exceeds the expected/reference value → high-resistance fault at that point. Measured resistance matches expected value → component/conductor is not the fault; investigate elsewhere.
  4. Fault LocationNarrow the fault to the specific connector, splice, or component showing the abnormal resistance.
  5. Root CauseDisassemble/inspect the identified connector or component to confirm the physical cause (corrosion, damage, degradation).
Test Points
Conductor loop resistance vs. calculated expected value
Individual connector/splice resistance
Component winding resistance vs. manufacturer spec
Confirmation of Findings

After repair, re-measure resistance at the corrected point and confirm it now matches the expected/reference value.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Clean, re-terminate, or replace a high-resistance connector or splice.
  • Replace a component whose resistance is outside specification.
Best Practice

Maintain a reference table of expected resistance values by cable type/length and component model to speed future comparisons; treat a trending increase in resistance as an early warning rather than waiting for outright failure.

Related References

SOP: SOP-ADT-04 (Resistance Measurement Fault Isolation Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

ADT-605 Fault Isolation Using Continuity Testing

Application — Used to quickly confirm whether a conductor, fuse, connector, or simple component provides an unbroken electrical path, typically as a fast first check before more detailed resistance or insulation testing.

System / Method Scope

Fuses, control wiring, low-voltage signal/interlock circuits, and short conductor runs across AGL and CCR systems.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighNo continuity (open circuit) on a component expected to be continuous confirms a break, blown fuse, or failed contact
HighContinuity present on a component or wiring path that should be open (e.g., a supposedly isolated conductor) indicates an unintended short or cross-connection
MediumIntermittent continuity (changes when the conductor/connector is flexed or tapped) indicates a marginal connection
LowContinuity confirmed and stable clears the tested component/path as a suspect
Required Tools
Digital multimeter with continuity/audible functionFuse pullerControl wiring diagram
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before continuity testing on power conductors.
  • Low-voltage control/signal circuits may still be interlocked with energised equipment, so confirm full isolation before testing.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationIdentify the specific conductor, fuse, or component to be tested from the wiring diagram.
  2. MeasurementPerform a continuity check across the component/conductor with the circuit fully isolated.
  3. ObservationIf continuity is intermittent, gently flex or tap the conductor/connector while monitoring the meter to localise a marginal connection.
  4. DecisionNo continuity where expected → open circuit confirmed at that component/conductor. Continuity where none expected → unintended short/cross-connection confirmed.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific fuse, wire segment, or connector failing the continuity check.
  6. Root CauseVisually inspect the failed component/conductor once isolated to confirm the physical cause.
Test Points
Continuity across fuses
Continuity along control/signal wiring segments
Continuity at connector pins
Confirmation of Findings

After repair or replacement, re-run the continuity check and confirm a stable, expected result.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Replace a blown fuse or open conductor.
  • Correct a wiring error causing an unintended short.
  • Re-terminate a marginal connector identified by intermittent continuity.
Best Practice

Use continuity testing as a fast first-pass check before more time-consuming resistance or insulation testing, especially for control and interlock circuits.

Related References

SOP: SOP-ADT-05 (Continuity Test Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

ADT-606 Fault Isolation Using Clamp Measurement

Application — Used to measure current non-invasively at multiple points without breaking the circuit, particularly useful for quick comparative checks across parallel branches or for locating an unexpected current path.

System / Method Scope

Any accessible conductor carrying AC or DC current where a clamp meter can be applied without disconnection — series loops, parallel branch circuits, ground conductors.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighA branch or leg showing significantly different current than its parallel counterparts indicates an unbalanced load or partial fault in that branch
HighCurrent detected on a ground/earth conductor that should be carrying no current indicates a ground fault or unintended neutral-ground connection
MediumInrush or transient current spikes visible on a clamp meter with min/max/inrush capture indicate a component drawing abnormal starting current
LowSmall current imbalance between otherwise identical parallel branches may be within normal tolerance
Required Tools
Clamp-on ammeter (AC/DC as applicable, true-RMS with min/max/inrush capture)Circuit layout drawing identifying parallel branches and ground paths
Safety Precautions
  • Ensure the clamp meter is rated for the circuit voltage class in use.
  • Do not open the clamp jaw on or near an energised conductor without meeting the meter’s insulation/CAT rating.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationIdentify all parallel branches or conductors relevant to the suspected fault from the circuit layout drawing.
  2. MeasurementClamp-measure current on each relevant conductor without disconnecting anything, comparing branch-to-branch or against the expected total.
  3. ObservationCheck the ground/earth conductor for unexpected current as an indicator of a ground fault.
  4. DecisionOne branch shows abnormal current relative to its parallel counterparts → fault isolated to that branch. Ground conductor carries current → ground fault confirmed, escalate to insulation testing.
  5. Fault LocationNarrow to the specific branch or conductor showing the abnormal clamp reading.
  6. Root CauseProceed to detailed testing (insulation, resistance) on the identified branch to confirm the specific fault.
Test Points
Current per parallel branch
Ground/earth conductor current
Inrush/transient current on suspect components
Confirmation of Findings

After repair, re-clamp all previously tested points and confirm current is balanced across branches and zero on the ground conductor as expected.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Proceed to detailed component-level repair on the branch identified by the clamp survey.
  • Investigate and correct the source of any confirmed ground fault current.
Best Practice

Keep a clamp meter with min/max/inrush capture as a standard first-response tool for AGL field diagnostics, since it allows rapid non-invasive comparison across multiple points without circuit interruption.

Related References

SOP: SOP-ADT-06 (Clamp Current Survey Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, IEC 60364

ADT-607 Fault Isolation Using Thermal Inspection

Application — Used to detect abnormal heating in connectors, terminations, transformers, and CCR components, often revealing a developing high-resistance or overload fault before it causes an outright failure.

System / Method Scope

CCR terminals and busbars, transformer connections, in-pavement base-can connectors, and any accessible electrical termination point.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighA localised hot spot significantly above ambient and above adjacent equivalent connections indicates a high-resistance connection or overloaded component
HighA transformer or component running consistently hotter than its rated/expected operating temperature indicates internal degradation or an overload condition
MediumA moderately elevated but not extreme temperature differential may indicate an early-stage developing connection issue worth monitoring
LowMinor, uniform temperature variation consistent with normal load and ambient conditions is not indicative of a fault
Required Tools
Infrared thermal imaging camera or spot infrared thermometerManufacturer-rated operating temperature specificationsPPE appropriate for working near energised panels
Safety Precautions
  • Perform thermal imaging on live equipment only within the bounds of an approved live-work/arc-flash procedure and with appropriate PPE.
  • Do not open energised enclosures beyond what the imaging procedure permits.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConduct a thermal scan of CCR terminals, busbars, and accessible transformer/connector points under normal load conditions.
  2. MeasurementRecord the temperature of any hot spot found and compare it to adjacent equivalent points and to the manufacturer’s rated operating temperature.
  3. DecisionSignificant temperature differential confirmed relative to adjacent points or rating → high-resistance connection or component fault at that location. No significant differential → component operating normally under current load.
  4. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific terminal, connector, or component showing the abnormal thermal signature.
  5. Root CauseDe-energise and physically inspect the identified point (LOTO first) to confirm the specific cause — loose termination, corrosion, or component degradation.
Test Points
Temperature at CCR terminals/busbars
Temperature at transformer connections
Temperature at accessible base-can connectors
Ambient reference temperature
Confirmation of Findings

After correcting the identified termination or component, re-scan under load and confirm the temperature differential has resolved to within normal range.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Re-terminate or torque a loose connection identified by thermal imaging.
  • Replace a component confirmed to be running hot from internal degradation.
Best Practice

Include periodic thermal imaging of CCR terminals and major connections in the PM programme, particularly before peak seasonal loading, to catch developing connection issues before they cause a failure.

Related References

SOP: SOP-ADT-07 (Thermal Inspection Procedure), SOP-CCR-04

Standards: NFPA 70B (Electrical Equipment Maintenance, where adopted), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

ADT-608 Fault Isolation Using Visual Inspection

Application — Used as the first-pass, no-instrument diagnostic step for any reported AGL fault, often revealing an obvious cause (damage, contamination, missing component) without the need for electrical testing.

System / Method Scope

All AGL fixtures, base cans, cable runs, and CCR/control equipment accessible for direct visual examination.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighVisible physical damage (cracked lens, crushed conduit, impact marks) directly explains the reported fault
HighVisible water, debris, or corrosion inside a base can or enclosure strongly suggests an environmental cause
MediumA visibly discoloured, deformed, or previously repaired component suggests a likely fault location even without confirmed damage
MediumA visibly incorrect part (wrong colour lens, mismatched connector) suggests an installation/maintenance error
LowNo visible abnormality found does not rule out a fault, but redirects diagnosis to instrumented testing
Required Tools
Flashlight/inspection lightBorescope for confined spacesCamera for documentationPPE appropriate for base-can/enclosure access
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before opening any base can or enclosure for close visual inspection beyond what is visible externally.
  • Treat standing water in a base can as potentially live until tested.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConduct a systematic visual inspection of the fixture, its mounting, the base can/enclosure, and accessible cable, working from the reported symptom outward.
  2. ObservationDocument any damage, contamination, incorrect parts, or abnormal condition found, including photographs where practical.
  3. DecisionA clear, sufficient visual cause is found → proceed directly to corrective action for that specific cause. No clear cause found → proceed to instrumented testing (current, voltage, insulation, resistance as appropriate).
  4. Fault LocationWhere a visual cause is confirmed, it directly identifies the fault location; where not, visual inspection still narrows the search area for subsequent testing.
  5. Root CauseConfirm a visually-identified cause is sufficient to fully explain the reported symptom before closing out the fault as resolved by visual finding alone.
Test Points
Fixture and lens condition
Base-can/enclosure condition (water, debris, corrosion)
Mounting and connector condition
Cable/conduit condition where accessible
Confirmation of Findings

After correcting a visually-identified fault, re-inspect and, where applicable, re-test electrically to confirm the fixture or circuit now performs correctly.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Correct the specific visual finding (replace damaged part, clean/dry an enclosure, correct a wrong part).
  • If no visual cause is found, proceed to the appropriate instrumented fault-isolation method.
Best Practice

Always perform and document a thorough visual inspection before instrumented testing — it is fast, low-risk, and frequently identifies the cause directly, and its findings also inform which instrumented test to run next.

Related References

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

ADT-609 Fault Isolation Using Photometry

Application — Used when a fixture appears electrically and visually normal but is suspected of an optical or output-quality fault, or as part of scheduled acceptance/compliance testing, drawing on the full set of photometric diagnostic topics (Division 18).

System / Method Scope

Any AGL fixture requiring photometric verification of intensity, alignment, spread, distribution, or chromaticity.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighA measured parameter outside its specified tolerance directly confirms a photometric fault requiring correction (cross-reference Division 18 topics)
HighA parameter that passes at one operational step but fails at another indicates a step-dependent fault (e.g., driver/transformer issue at specific loads)
MediumA parameter within tolerance but near the edge of the band flags a fixture for closer monitoring even without an outright failure
LowAll parameters comfortably within tolerance clears photometry as a contributing factor to the reported issue
Required Tools
Calibrated field photometer/colorimeter with full angular scan capabilityReference isocandela and colour box curvesALCMS step records
Safety Precautions
  • Standard fixture-access and runway/taxiway coordination precautions apply.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm electrical and visual normality first (cross-reference PHM-469/470) before committing to a full photometric test.
  2. MeasurementPerform the relevant photometric measurement(s) — intensity, alignment, spread, distribution, or chromaticity — per the applicable Division 18 procedure.
  3. DecisionA parameter fails → proceed to the specific Division 18 topic covering that parameter for detailed root-cause diagnosis. All parameters pass → photometry is cleared as a cause; consider other fault-isolation methods.
  4. Fault LocationThe specific failed parameter and its associated Division 18 topic define the fault location and next diagnostic steps.
  5. Root CauseFollow the referenced Division 18 diagnostic procedure through to root cause confirmation.
Test Points
Luminous intensity
Beam alignment
Beam spread
Photometric distribution
Chromaticity — as applicable to the suspected fault
Confirmation of Findings

Once the identified photometric fault is corrected per its Division 18 procedure, re-run the full photometric test and confirm all parameters pass.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Apply the specific corrective action from the relevant Division 18 topic.
  • Log results in the fixture’s photometric history for future trending.
Best Practice

Use photometry as a targeted follow-up test once electrical and visual causes have been ruled out, rather than as the first response to every reported issue, to make efficient use of test time and equipment.

Related References

SOP: SOP-ADT-09 (Photometric Fault Isolation Procedure); cross-reference Division 18 — AGL Photometry Troubleshooting (PHM-451 through PHM-470)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Attachment A, FAA AC 150/5340-30

ADT-610 Fault Isolation Using Event Logs

Application — Used to reconstruct the sequence and timing of a fault from the CCR/ALCMS event/alarm log, particularly valuable for intermittent faults, faults reported after the fact, or faults that self-cleared before a technician arrived.

System / Method Scope

CCR and ALCMS event/alarm logging systems and their historical data records.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighA repeating alarm pattern at the same time of day or under similar conditions (e.g., every rain event) points to an environmental or thermal cause
HighAn alarm sequence showing a specific circuit or fixture tripping consistently before others narrows the fault to that circuit/fixture
MediumA single, non-repeating alarm event may indicate a transient cause not requiring immediate physical intervention, but should still be logged and monitored
MediumCorrelated alarms across multiple circuits at the same time suggest a common upstream cause (power supply, main feeder) rather than independent faults
LowAlarm log shows no clear pattern — insufficient data yet; continue monitoring or increase logging detail if possible
Required Tools
CCR/ALCMS event log export/review toolSpreadsheet or log-analysis softwareWeather/operations records for cross-referencing timing
Safety Precautions
  • No unique hazard; standard workstation/control-room precautions apply.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationExport or review the event/alarm log covering the period relevant to the reported fault, including any prior occurrences.
  2. ObservationIdentify timing patterns (time of day, weather correlation, specific circuit/fixture recurrence) across the logged events.
  3. DecisionA clear pattern is identified → target the fault-isolation effort at the specific circuit, time window, or environmental condition indicated. No clear pattern found → treat as an isolated event, document, and monitor for recurrence.
  4. Fault LocationThe pattern identified in the log narrows the physical fault-finding effort to a specific circuit, fixture, or condition.
  5. Root CauseCross-reference the log-derived pattern against the appropriate physical fault-isolation method (current, voltage, insulation, thermal) to confirm the actual fault.
Test Points
Alarm timestamp and type per event
Affected circuit/fixture per event
Correlation with weather/operational records
Confirmation of Findings

After the physical fault is corrected, monitor the event log over the following period and confirm the previously recurring alarm pattern does not reappear.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Direct the physical fault-isolation effort (current, voltage, insulation, thermal, or visual) to the circuit/condition identified by the log pattern.
Best Practice

Review event logs as a standard first step for any reported intermittent or previously self-cleared fault, since the pattern often narrows the search dramatically before any equipment is touched.

Related References

SOP: SOP-ADT-10 (Event Log Review Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

ADT-611 Fault Isolation Using CCR Alarms

Application — Used to interpret specific CCR alarm codes and indications directly as the starting point for fault isolation, since the alarm type itself often indicates the fault category (overcurrent, undercurrent, open circuit, ground fault, etc.).

System / Method Scope

CCR alarm/annunciation system and its defined alarm code set.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighAn overcurrent/short-circuit alarm indicates a shorted component or insulation failure on the loop
HighAn undercurrent/open-circuit alarm indicates a broken conductor, failed connector, or fully failed component
MediumA ground-fault alarm indicates insulation breakdown to earth somewhere on the circuit
MediumA regulator/output-fault alarm (distinct from a loop-current alarm) indicates a CCR internal fault rather than a field circuit fault
LowA nuisance or nonspecific alarm with no corresponding field symptom may indicate a CCR sensor/threshold issue rather than a true field fault
Required Tools
CCR alarm code reference manualCCR front-panel diagnostic display or ALCMS alarm detail viewBasic electrical test equipment for follow-up confirmation
Safety Precautions
  • Follow standard CCR access and LOTO procedures for any physical investigation prompted by an alarm.
  • Do not silence or reset a persistent alarm without investigating its cause.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationIdentify the specific alarm code/type displayed and reference the CCR manufacturer’s alarm code manual for its defined meaning.
  2. ObservationCorrelate the alarm with any reported field symptom (dark fixture, dim run) to confirm consistency between the alarm type and the observed condition.
  3. DecisionAlarm type and field symptom are consistent → proceed with the fault-isolation method appropriate to that alarm category (current for overcurrent/undercurrent, insulation for ground fault, etc.). Alarm present with no corresponding field symptom → suspect a CCR-side sensor/threshold issue.
  4. Fault LocationThe alarm category directs the choice of subsequent fault-isolation method and narrows whether the fault is in the field circuit or the CCR itself.
  5. Root CauseConfirm via the directed fault-isolation method (current, insulation, resistance) once the alarm category has set the diagnostic direction.
Test Points
Specific alarm code and category
Correlation with field-reported symptom
CCR internal diagnostic parameters if the alarm suggests a regulator-side fault
Confirmation of Findings

After the underlying field or CCR fault is corrected, confirm the alarm clears and does not recur under normal and test-step operation.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Route the investigation to the appropriate field fault-isolation method based on alarm category.
  • Route to CCR internal diagnostics/service if the alarm indicates a regulator-side issue.
Best Practice

Keep the CCR alarm code reference readily available to field technicians so alarm type can immediately direct the correct fault-isolation method rather than defaulting to a generic full-circuit search.

Related References

ADT-612 Fault Isolation Using ALCMS Data

Application — Used to leverage the broader Airfield Lighting Control and Monitoring System’s data set — individual fixture status, historical trending, and system-wide views — beyond a single CCR’s alarm output, particularly for faults spanning multiple circuits or requiring historical trend analysis.

System / Method Scope

ALCMS central monitoring system and its fixture-level and circuit-level data records across the airfield.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighALCMS shows a specific individual fixture reporting a fault status while its circuit-level CCR shows normal operation, confirming a fixture-level (not circuit-level) issue
HighALCMS trend data shows a specific fixture or circuit’s performance parameter degrading steadily over time, indicating a developing fault ahead of outright failure
MediumALCMS shows a cluster of fixtures across different circuits reporting similar faults simultaneously, suggesting a common cause (power quality, software/communication issue) rather than independent field faults
LowALCMS data shows no anomaly for the reported fixture; investigate for a possible ALCMS reporting/communication gap rather than assuming no fault exists
Required Tools
ALCMS workstation/dashboard accessHistorical trend/report export functionSystem architecture drawing showing fixture-to-CCR-to-ALCMS data paths
Safety Precautions
  • No unique hazard; standard workstation precautions apply.

  1. ObservationQuery ALCMS for the current and historical status of the specific fixture(s) or circuit(s) in question.
  2. ObservationReview trend data over an appropriate period to distinguish a sudden failure from a gradual degradation pattern.
  3. DecisionALCMS confirms a fixture-level fault distinct from CCR-level status → proceed with fixture-level fault isolation. ALCMS shows a multi-circuit cluster → investigate a common upstream cause. ALCMS shows no anomaly despite a reported field issue → investigate ALCMS communication/reporting for that fixture.
  4. Fault LocationALCMS data narrows the fault to a specific fixture, circuit, or system-wide cause, and distinguishes a field fault from a monitoring/communication gap.

Circuit-level status history

After the underlying fault is corrected, confirm ALCMS reports normal status for the affected fixture(s)/circuit(s) and that historical trending shows the parameter has stabilised.

  • Direct the physical fault-isolation effort based on the scope (single fixture, single circuit, or multi-circuit) identified by ALCMS data.
  • Escalate to IT/communications support if an ALCMS reporting gap is identified rather than a true field fault.

Review ALCMS trend data periodically as part of the PM programme, not only when a fault is reported, to catch gradually degrading fixtures or circuits before they cause an operational outage.

ADT-613 Fault Isolation Using Maintenance History

Application — Used to review a fixture’s, circuit’s, or component’s prior maintenance and repair records to identify recurring issues, recently replaced parts, or a documented pattern that shortens the current diagnostic effort.

System / Method Scope

Fixture/circuit/component maintenance and repair history records across the AGL asset base.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighThe exact same fault reported previously at this location within a short interval strongly suggests an incomplete prior repair or an unaddressed root cause
HighA recently replaced component now failing again suggests a defective part, incorrect installation, or an underlying condition (voltage, environment) causing repeat failures
MediumA documented history of a specific recurring issue type at this location (even if not identical) suggests a systemic or environmental factor at that site
LowNo relevant history found for this location/component; proceed with standard fault isolation without a historical shortcut
Required Tools
Maintenance management system (CMMS) or maintenance log recordsFixture/component service history by asset IDPrior work order/repair documentation
Safety Precautions
  • No unique hazard; standard workstation precautions apply for the record review itself.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationQuery the maintenance history for the specific fixture, circuit, or component asset ID associated with the current fault.
  2. ObservationIdentify any prior occurrence of the same or a related fault, and note the corrective action taken and its outcome.
  3. DecisionA relevant prior occurrence with an apparently incomplete or ineffective corrective action is found → prioritise investigating the root cause more thoroughly this time (cross-reference PHM-468). No relevant history → proceed with standard fault isolation.
  4. Fault LocationA relevant history entry may directly suggest the fault location or the specific component to inspect first.
  5. Root CauseConfirm using the appropriate physical fault-isolation method, informed by what previous corrective actions failed to resolve.
Test Points
Prior fault type and date at this asset
Prior corrective action taken
Part numbers/dates of previously replaced components
Recurrence interval
Confirmation of Findings

After applying a more thorough corrective action informed by the history review, monitor the asset through the next one to two maintenance cycles and confirm the fault does not recur.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Apply a deeper root-cause investigation where history shows a repeat failure.
  • Update the maintenance record clearly to support future history reviews at this location.
Best Practice

Make maintenance history review a standard first step for any fault at a location with prior recorded issues, and ensure every repair is logged with enough detail (part numbers, root cause, corrective action) to be useful for this purpose in the future.

ADT-614 Fault Isolation Using Failure Pattern Analysis

Application — Used to step back from a single fault event and analyse failure data across multiple fixtures, circuits, or time periods to identify systemic trends — a specific fixture model failing early, a specific circuit section failing repeatedly, or a seasonal pattern — that individual fault reports do not reveal on their own.

System / Method Scope

Airfield-wide or system-wide fault and maintenance data across multiple assets and time periods.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighA specific fixture model or manufacturer batch shows a statistically higher failure rate than comparable equipment, indicating a product-level issue
HighA specific circuit section or geographic area shows a disproportionate share of faults, indicating a localised systemic cause (drainage, soil conditions, installation quality)
MediumFailures cluster around a specific season or weather condition across multiple locations, indicating an environmental design/rating mismatch
LowFailure distribution appears random across fixtures, circuits, and time; no systemic pattern identified, continue routine monitoring
Required Tools
CMMS/maintenance database with reporting/query capabilitySpreadsheet or data-analysis softwareAsset register with model/manufacturer/installation-date data
Safety Precautions
  • No unique hazard; standard workstation precautions apply for the data analysis itself.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompile fault and maintenance records across the relevant population of fixtures/circuits over an appropriate time period.
  2. MeasurementAggregate and compare failure rates by fixture model, manufacturer batch, circuit section, location, and season to identify statistically meaningful patterns.
  3. DecisionA clear systemic pattern is identified (model, location, or seasonal) → escalate for a targeted engineering review or planned batch action. No clear pattern → continue routine individual fault handling.
  4. Fault LocationA systemic pattern points to a population of assets or a location requiring a broader corrective programme rather than a single fixture repair.
  5. Root CauseEngineering review of the identified pattern (e.g., a specific batch, a drainage-deficient section) to confirm the systemic root cause.
Test Points
Failure rate by fixture model/manufacturer batch
Failure rate by circuit section/location
Failure timing by season/weather condition
Confirmation of Findings

After a systemic corrective programme is implemented (batch replacement, drainage improvement, design change), monitor failure rates for the affected population over subsequent cycles and confirm the rate has returned to normal.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Initiate a planned batch replacement, targeted infrastructure improvement, or engineering design review based on the identified systemic pattern, rather than continuing to treat each occurrence as an isolated fault.
Best Practice

Periodically run failure pattern analysis across the full asset base as part of the PM programme, not only in response to an unusually high number of individual complaints, to catch systemic issues early.

ADT-615 Fault Isolation Using Comparative Measurements

Application — Used to diagnose a suspect fixture, circuit, or component by directly comparing its measured values against a known-good equivalent operating under the same conditions, particularly useful when an absolute specification is unavailable, ambiguous, or when instrument calibration itself is in question.

System / Method Scope

Any fixture, circuit segment, or component with a directly comparable equivalent elsewhere on the same or a similar system.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighThe suspect unit shows a significant, repeatable difference from the known-good comparator under otherwise identical conditions, confirming a genuine fault at the suspect unit
HighSwapping the suspect and known-good units (where practical) causes the fault to follow the swapped component, definitively confirming the fault is in that component
MediumA difference is observed but is within the expected unit-to-unit variation for that equipment type, and should not be treated as a confirmed fault on its own
LowNo meaningful difference found between suspect and comparator; the suspect unit is likely not the source of the reported issue
Required Tools
Calibrated test equipment appropriate to the parameter being compared (photometer, ammeter, ohmmeter, etc.)A confirmed known-good reference unit or circuit segmentConsistent test setup/conditions for both measurements
Safety Precautions
  • Follow the safety precautions applicable to the specific measurement type and equipment being compared (e.g., LOTO for resistance/insulation testing).
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationIdentify a genuinely comparable known-good unit or circuit segment operating under the same or closely matched conditions as the suspect.
  2. MeasurementMeasure the relevant parameter on both the suspect and the known-good comparator using identical test conditions and equipment.
  3. DecisionA significant, repeatable difference is found → suspect unit confirmed as the fault. Difference within normal unit-to-unit variation → suspect unit is likely not faulty; investigate elsewhere.
  4. Fault LocationWhere practical, physically swap the suspect and known-good units and re-test; the fault following the swap definitively confirms the faulty component.
  5. Root CauseBench-test or further inspect the confirmed faulty unit to establish the specific internal cause.
Test Points
The specific parameter under comparison (current, voltage, resistance, intensity, etc.) for both suspect and known-good units, under matched conditions
Confirmation of Findings

After replacing or repairing the confirmed faulty unit, re-compare it against the known-good reference and confirm the values now align within normal variation.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Replace or repair the component confirmed faulty by comparative measurement.
  • Where a swap test was used, restore the known-good unit to its original location once testing is complete.
Best Practice

Use comparative measurement particularly when absolute pass/fail criteria are unclear or when instrument calibration itself is in doubt, since a side-by-side comparison under identical conditions removes much of that ambiguity.

ADT-616 Fault Isolation by Sectional Testing

Application — Used to systematically divide a long circuit, cable run, or system into sections and test each in turn, bracketing the fault into a progressively smaller segment — the standard approach when a fault’s general presence is known but its exact location along an extended run is not.

System / Method Scope

Long series/primary cable loops, extended parallel circuit runs, and multi-segment systems where the fault location along the run is not yet known.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighA test performed at the midpoint of the suspect run cleanly separates a passing half from a failing half, allowing the search to be halved repeatedly (bisection approach)
HighA specific accessible test point (pull box, junction, base can) shows a clear pass/fail transition relative to its neighbours, bracketing the fault to that immediate section
MediumMultiple sections show marginal or inconsistent results, suggesting more than one contributing fault along the run rather than a single clean break
LowAll sections test consistently pass or consistently fail; the fault may not be a simple sectional/location-based issue and may require a different fault-isolation method
Required Tools
Appropriate test equipment for the parameter being sectionally tested (megger, ammeter, continuity tester)Circuit route drawing with identified accessible test pointsLOTO equipment as required
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit fully before any sectional testing that requires opening enclosures or disconnecting conductors.
  • Coordinate access/excavation clearances for any test points requiring ground disturbance.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationMap all accessible test points along the suspect run from the circuit route drawing.
  2. MeasurementTest at the midpoint (or another well-chosen accessible point) of the suspect run first, then continue bisecting the failing half at each subsequent step.
  3. DecisionA clean pass/fail transition is found between two adjacent accessible points → fault bracketed to that specific section for physical exposure and repair. Results are inconsistent across sections → suspect multiple contributing faults; test each section thoroughly rather than assuming a single break.
  4. Fault LocationThe bisection process narrows the fault to the smallest practically testable section along the run.
  5. Root CausePhysically expose the bracketed section to confirm and repair the specific fault.
Test Points
Sequential accessible test points along the run, tested in a bisecting/bracketing sequence
Confirmation of Findings

After repairing the identified section, re-test the full run end-to-end and confirm it now passes consistently across all previously tested points.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Physically expose, repair, or replace the cable/component within the bracketed section.
  • Restore any excavation or access points opened during sectional testing.
Best Practice

Favor a bisection (midpoint-first) approach over sequential end-to-end testing where the run has many accessible test points, since it locates the fault in far fewer test operations on a long run.

ADT-617 Fault Isolation by Component Substitution

Application — Used to confirm a suspected faulty component by temporarily substituting a known-good unit of the same type and observing whether the fault clears — particularly effective for components that are difficult to test in place but easy to swap (modules, transformers, relays, cards).

System / Method Scope

Field-replaceable AGL and CCR components — lamp/LED modules, isolation transformers, control relays/cards, and similar plug-in or bolt-in parts.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighThe fault clears immediately and completely when the suspect component is replaced with a known-good unit, confirming the original component as faulty
HighThe fault follows the suspect component when it is moved to a different, known-good location (and a known-good unit in the original location works correctly), definitively isolating the fault to that component rather than its location
MediumThe fault partially improves but does not fully clear after substitution, suggesting more than one contributing fault (the substituted component plus another issue)
LowThe fault persists unchanged after substitution; the substituted component was not the cause, return the original component to service unless independently confirmed faulty and continue diagnosis elsewhere
Required Tools
A confirmed known-good spare of the exact component type/ratingAppropriate hand tools for the specific component’s removal/installationLOTO equipment as required
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before removing or installing any component.
  • Confirm the substitute component is rated identically to the original before installation to avoid introducing a new fault (cross-reference PHM-452 for wattage/rating mismatch risk).
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the suspect component’s exact part number/rating and obtain a verified known-good substitute of the identical type.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, substitute the known-good component for the suspect one and re-energise to test.
  3. DecisionFault clears completely → original component confirmed faulty. Fault persists unchanged → substituted component was not the cause. Fault partially improves → suspect an additional contributing fault beyond the substituted component.
  4. Fault LocationWhere practical, confirm by also placing the original suspect component into a known-good location — the fault following the component definitively isolates it, as opposed to a location-specific cause.
  5. Root CauseBench-test the confirmed-faulty original component, where practical, to establish its specific internal failure mode for the maintenance record.
Test Points
Circuit/fixture performance before and after substitution
Confirmation of substitute component rating match
Result of a location-swap test where performed
Confirmation of Findings

After permanently replacing the confirmed-faulty component, re-verify full correct operation and, where applicable, confirm photometric or electrical parameters meet specification.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Permanently replace the confirmed-faulty component.
  • Return any temporarily borrowed known-good unit to stock or to its original location.
Best Practice

Keep a small stock of verified known-good substitute components for common field-replaceable parts specifically for diagnostic swap-testing, separate from general spares stock, to keep this method fast and reliable.

ADT-618 Fault Isolation by Circuit Segmentation

Application — Used to deliberately open and isolate specific circuit segments (via existing isolators, splices, or by temporarily disconnecting sections) to determine whether a fault lies within a given segment or is only present when segments are connected together — distinct from sectional testing in that it actively reconfigures the circuit rather than only measuring at fixed points.

System / Method Scope

Circuits with defined sectionalising points (isolators, splice kits, removable links) allowing controlled segment-by-segment isolation.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighThe fault disappears when a specific segment is isolated/disconnected from the rest of the circuit, confirming the fault resides within that segment
HighThe fault persists even with a specific segment fully isolated, ruling that segment out and directing attention to the remaining segments
MediumThe fault behaviour changes (but does not fully clear) when a segment is isolated, suggesting an interaction between segments (e.g., a marginal fault only manifesting under a specific combined load)
LowNo defined sectionalising points exist for the suspect run; this method is not available without creating a temporary disconnection point, which should only be done deliberately and with proper isolation
Required Tools
Circuit sectionalising/isolator hardware or temporary disconnection kitCircuit layout drawing showing defined segment boundariesLOTO equipment
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the full circuit before creating or removing any segment isolation point.
  • Ensure any temporary disconnection is clearly tagged and safely terminated (capped/insulated) to prevent a hazard while segments are separated.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationIdentify existing sectionalising points on the circuit, or determine where a temporary disconnection can safely be made.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, open the segment boundary and test each segment independently (using an appropriate method — insulation, continuity, current) to determine whether the fault is present in that segment alone.
  3. DecisionFault confirmed within an isolated segment → proceed to sectional/bisection testing within that segment to pinpoint the exact location. Fault not present in any single isolated segment → suspect an inter-segment interaction fault requiring combined-load testing.
  4. Fault LocationSegmentation narrows the fault to a specific segment or confirms it is an inter-segment/combined-condition issue.
  5. Root CauseApply the appropriate detailed fault-isolation method within the identified segment to reach final root cause.
Test Points
Each defined circuit segment tested independently
Segment boundary/isolator condition
Circuit behaviour with segments combined vs. isolated
Confirmation of Findings

After repairing the identified segment, reconnect all segments, re-energise, and confirm the full circuit operates correctly under all normal operating conditions.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Proceed to sectional/bisection or component-level testing within the confirmed faulty segment.
  • Permanently restore or properly document any temporary disconnection points used during diagnosis.
Best Practice

Where a circuit is being newly designed or upgraded, include permanent, clearly identified sectionalising points at logical intervals specifically to support this fault-isolation method in the future.

Related References

SOP: SOP-ADT-18 (Circuit Segmentation Test Procedure), SOP-RCL-02

ADT-619 Fault Isolation by Environmental Reproduction

Application — Used for intermittent faults that only occur under specific environmental conditions (rain, temperature extremes, vibration from aircraft/vehicle movement) that are not present at the time of a routine inspection, by deliberately reproducing or waiting for the relevant condition to observe the fault directly.

System / Method Scope

Any AGL circuit or fixture with a reported intermittent fault correlated with a specific environmental or operational condition.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighThe fault reliably reappears when the suspected environmental condition is reproduced (e.g., water spray test on a suspect connector, thermal cycling of a suspect component), confirming the environmental trigger
HighThe fault reliably reappears during or shortly after a specific operational event (heavy aircraft movement, snow removal pass), confirming a vibration/mechanical trigger
MediumThe fault reappears inconsistently even when the suspected condition is reproduced, suggesting the true trigger is only partially understood or involves an additional factor
LowThe fault does not reappear despite reproducing the suspected condition; reconsider the correlation identified from event log review (cross-reference ADT-610) and investigate alternative triggers
Required Tools
Water spray/ingress test equipment (for suspected moisture-related faults)Thermal source or wait-for-condition monitoringVibration/mechanical simulation where safe and practicalData logger for extended monitoring during the reproduction attempt
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO or otherwise secure the circuit appropriately before any deliberate environmental reproduction test that involves water, heat, or mechanical disturbance near energised equipment.
  • Never simulate a hazardous condition (e.g., water spray) on live equipment without a procedure specifically rated for that test.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the suspected environmental/operational trigger from event log correlation (cross-reference ADT-610) or from field reports.
  2. MeasurementUnder a controlled, safe procedure, reproduce or wait for the suspected condition while monitoring the circuit/fixture for the fault to reappear.
  3. DecisionFault reliably reappears with the reproduced condition → environmental/operational trigger confirmed; proceed to identify the specific component affected. Fault does not reappear → reconsider the suspected trigger and re-examine event log correlation.
  4. Fault LocationOnce the fault is reproduced, apply real-time measurement (current, voltage, continuity) during the reproduction to pinpoint the specific affected component.
  5. Root CauseConfirm the specific failure mechanism (e.g., a specific connector losing continuity under water ingress) once the fault has been reproduced and measured directly.
Test Points
Circuit/fixture behaviour before, during, and after the reproduced condition
Specific component measurements taken during the active fault state
Confirmation of Findings

After correcting the identified fault, repeat the environmental reproduction test and confirm the fault no longer occurs under the same condition that previously triggered it.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Repair or replace the specific component confirmed faulty under the reproduced condition.
  • Consider a design or material change if the environmental sensitivity is inherent to the component type rather than a single unit defect.
Best Practice

Reserve environmental reproduction testing for faults where event log correlation or field reports already point to a specific condition, since it is more time-and-resource-intensive than other fault-isolation methods and works best when targeted rather than exploratory.

ADT-620 Fault Isolation by Root Cause Confirmation

Application — Used as the final, closing step of any fault-isolation effort — regardless of which method(s) identified the likely fault — to positively confirm the true underlying root cause before closing out the work order, ensuring a symptom was not mistaken for the cause and preventing premature closure that leads to repeat failures.

System / Method Scope

Any AGL fault investigation, applied as the concluding step after any of the other Division 26 fault-isolation methods.

Diagnostic Indicators
HighThe corrected component/condition, once restored to normal, results in fully normal operation across all relevant tests (electrical, photometric, visual) with no residual symptom
HighThe identified root cause has a clear, physically demonstrable mechanism (e.g., a confirmed cracked insulation, a bench-tested failed winding) rather than an assumed or inferred cause
MediumOperation is restored to normal, but the physical mechanism of the original fault was not directly confirmed (e.g., a component was replaced without bench-testing the removed part); acceptable for closure but weaker for the maintenance record and future pattern analysis
LowOperation appears restored, but only a single verification check was performed; insufficient confirmation, repeat testing under multiple conditions/steps before closing
Required Tools
The full set of test equipment relevant to the specific fault type being closed outBench-test capability for removed components where practicalMaintenance record/work order system for documentation
Safety Precautions
  • Apply the safety precautions relevant to whichever verification tests are being performed as part of root cause confirmation.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationReview the corrective action taken and the specific test(s) planned to confirm it has resolved the true root cause, not merely masked the symptom.
  2. MeasurementRe-test the fixture/circuit across all relevant parameters and, where practical and safe, under multiple operating conditions (intensity steps, time of day, weather) rather than a single check.
  3. ObservationWhere a component was replaced, bench-test the removed part where practical to positively confirm its failure mode rather than assuming it was faulty.
  4. DecisionAll relevant tests pass consistently and the failure mechanism is positively confirmed → root cause closure justified. Any test is inconsistent, marginal, or the failure mechanism was not confirmed → continue investigation before closing the work order.
  5. Root CauseThis step is itself the formal root cause confirmation and documentation step for the investigation.
Test Points
All parameters relevant to the original reported fault, re-tested post-repair
Bench-test result of any removed component where practical
Confirmation of Findings

Full restoration of normal operation across all relevant tests, ideally repeated across more than one operating condition or over a short monitoring period, constitutes the confirmation itself.

Follow-Up Actions
  • Formally document the confirmed root cause, corrective action, and verification results in the maintenance record.
  • Flag the case for failure pattern analysis (cross-reference ADT-614) if it fits a recognisable systemic category.
Best Practice

Never close a fault investigation on the basis of a single "it works now" check alone; always re-test under representative conditions and, where practical, positively confirm the physical failure mechanism of the removed/repaired component, since this is what makes future pattern analysis and repeat-failure detection possible.

Q2What information does each topic include?

Every topic follows the same diagnostic record: the symptom, equipment/system, possible causes ranked by likelihood (high, medium or low), required tools, safety precautions, diagnostic procedure, test points, root cause verification, corrective action, preventive action and related references (SOPs, standards, diagrams and case studies).
