AGL-CS-035 — CCR Output Voltage Abnormally High

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A voltage-regulation investigation where progressive circuit impedance increase caused the CCR to operate near its voltage limit — from trend analysis through impedance measurement, sectional testing, thermal inspection, and targeted repairs.

Case ID

AGL-CS-035

Category

Constant Current Regulator (CCR) & Voltage Regulation Diagnostics

Level

Expert

Case Type

Engineering Simulation

Primary Skill

Diagnosis of abnormally high CCR output voltage & excessive circuit impedance

Important: This is an engineering simulation created for training. It is not presented as an actual airport incident. All readings, values, dates and outcomes are illustrative, and no airport, organisation, person or manufacturer is identified or implied.

1Case Study Overview

During a routine CCR performance review, the maintenance team observed that one runway edge lighting circuit required an unusually high output voltage to maintain the selected 6.6 A current.

Although the lighting circuit remained operational, the CCR output voltage had increased from a historical operating value of 2.4 kV to 3.8 kV.

No overcurrent or undercurrent trip had occurred, but the CCR was operating close to its maximum voltage capability.

A systematic investigation identified progressive increase in circuit impedance caused by multiple deteriorated cable joints, aging cable insulation, and one partially open transformer secondary connection.

This case demonstrates that abnormally high CCR output voltage is often an early warning indicator of deteriorating field-circuit conditions.

2Learning Objectives

After completing this case study, the learner should be able to:

  • Understand CCR voltage regulation principles.
  • Interpret high output voltage conditions.
  • Differentiate CCR voltage abnormalities from current abnormalities.
  • Analyze circuit impedance increases.
  • Investigate cable, connector, and transformer resistance.
  • Correlate voltage trends with circuit deterioration.
  • Apply preventive maintenance based on CCR voltage behavior.

3System Background

A CCR automatically adjusts output voltage to maintain constant current.

Normal regulation:

Circuit impedance changes → CCR adjusts voltage → Current maintained

High-voltage condition:

Circuit impedance becomes excessive → CCR increases voltage substantially → Current maintained → Voltage approaches regulation limit

The voltage required by the CCR is a valuable indicator of overall circuit health.

4Problem Statement

The reported condition was:

  • Selected current: 6.6 A.
  • Current regulation normal.
  • Output voltage abnormally high.
  • No circuit trip.
  • All lights operational.
  • Voltage increasing over several months.
Engineering Question

Why is the CCR requiring unusually high voltage to maintain normal current?

7Voltage Regulation Principle

For a CCR:

Required voltage = Current × Circuit impedance

With constant current:

Higher voltage → Higher circuit impedance

Therefore, abnormal voltage is often an impedance problem.

Multiple degraded sections were identified.

Step 5: Thermal Inspection

Thermal imaging revealed:

  • Three overheated cable joints.
  • Two warm transformer secondary connectors.
  • One transformer with elevated terminal temperature.

The heating pattern indicated distributed resistance increases.

Step 6: Transformer Secondary Testing

One transformer showed:

  • Stable primary continuity.
  • Elevated secondary resistance.
  • Intermittent contact resistance.
  • Moderate insulation deterioration.

The transformer contributed additional impedance.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The CCR output voltage became abnormally high because multiple distributed resistance increases accumulated throughout the field circuit.

The contributing factors were:

  • Deteriorated cable joints.
  • Aging cable insulation.
  • High-resistance connectors.
  • One partially degraded transformer secondary connection.

The CCR remained operational but required progressively higher voltage to maintain the selected current.

11Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Isolated the circuit.
  2. Replaced deteriorated cable joints.
  3. Replaced damaged connectors.
  4. Replaced the degraded transformer secondary connection.
  5. Performed insulation resistance testing.
  6. Measured circuit impedance.
  7. Verified CCR output voltage.
  8. Restored the circuit.
  9. Updated maintenance records.
  10. Implemented voltage-trend monitoring.

14Why This Case Was Important

A CCR can maintain current successfully while circuit conditions continue deteriorating.

High voltage is often the earliest measurable symptom of:

  • Cable aging.
  • Joint deterioration.
  • Connector resistance.
  • Transformer connection problems.

Voltage trending enables predictive maintenance before current regulation fails.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: Selected current: 6.6 A. Actual current: 6.6 A. CCR output voltage increased from 2.4 kV to 3.8 kV. All lights operational. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace the CCR.
B. Increase the current setting.
C. Measure circuit impedance and inspect cable joints, connectors, and transformer secondary connections.
D. Replace all transformers.
Correct answer: C. The normal current with elevated voltage points to increased circuit impedance. Impedance measurement and inspection of field components will identify the cause without premature CCR replacement.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about abnormally high CCR output voltage, based on this case study.

Q1What is AGL-CS-035?

AGL-CS-035 is an engineering training case study on a CCR output voltage that had risen abnormally high while still maintaining the selected current. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2How much had the CCR voltage increased?

Output voltage rose from a historical 2.4 kV to 3.8 kV while current stayed at the selected 6.6 A. All lights were operating and no trip had occurred, but the CCR was close to its maximum voltage capability.

Q3Why does normal current with higher voltage matter?

The important observation was that current remained normal while voltage increased significantly. That points to rising circuit impedance, and the CCR is compensating by using more voltage.

Q4What was the root cause?

Multiple distributed resistance increases accumulated through the circuit: deteriorated cable joints, ageing cable insulation, high-resistance connectors and one partially degraded transformer secondary connection.

Q5What should you investigate first?

Measure circuit impedance and inspect cable joints, connectors and transformer secondary connections. Replacing the CCR, increasing the current setting or replacing all transformers is not the right first step.

Q6How was the circuit repaired?

The team isolated the circuit and replaced deteriorated cable joints, damaged connectors and the degraded secondary connection. They tested insulation resistance, measured impedance, verified CCR output voltage, restored the circuit and implemented voltage-trend monitoring.

Q7How was the repair verified?

Output voltage returned to 2.46 kV with current at 6.6 A and circuit impedance of 23 Ω. Light operation was normal and the CCR margin was restored.

Q8Why implement voltage-trend monitoring?

The rise was gradual over several months, so tracking CCR output voltage over time can reveal developing impedance problems before the CCR reaches its limit.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

Abnormally high CCR output voltage is usually an indication of excessive circuit impedance caused by progressive deterioration of cable joints, connectors, transformer secondary connections, or cable insulation rather than CCR malfunction.

Systematic CCR verification, voltage-trend analysis, circuit impedance measurement, sectional resistance testing, thermal inspection, and targeted field repairs can accurately identify the source of the voltage increase and restore normal regulation margins.

In AGL engineering, high CCR voltage is often the circuit asking for maintenance long before it asks for repair.
