AGL-CS-073 — Low Intensity Due to Optical System Contamination

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A photometric investigation where internal optical contamination caused major intensity loss despite clean external lenses — from electrical verification through endoscopic inspection, reflector evaluation, seal integrity assessment, and internal optical restoration.

Case ID

AGL-CS-073

Category

Level

Expert

Case Type

Engineering Simulation

Primary Skill

Investigation of photometric intensity loss caused by internal optical-system contamination

Important: This is an engineering simulation created for training. It is not presented as an actual airport incident. All readings, values, dates and outcomes are illustrative, and no airport, organisation, person or manufacturer is identified or implied.

1Case Study Overview

During a scheduled runway centreline photometric inspection, several inset fixtures were found to have significantly reduced intensity despite clean external lenses, normal electrical operation, and stable CCR performance.

The maintenance team initially cleaned the external lenses, but the measured intensity improved only slightly.

A detailed optical investigation was initiated covering internal optical contamination, reflector condition, LED optical-window contamination, seal integrity, moisture ingress, and beam-distribution performance.

The investigation identified internal contamination of the optical chamber caused by long-term seal deterioration and moisture ingress, resulting in reduced optical transmission and increased internal light scattering.

This case demonstrates that low photometric intensity may originate inside the optical system even when the external lens appears clean and the electrical system is fully healthy.

  • Evaluate reflector contamination.
  • Assess optical-chamber cleanliness.
  • Investigate seal-related contamination.
  • Measure contamination-related photometric loss.
  • Determine repair or replacement requirements.

  • Internal optical window.

  • Reflector assembly.
  • LED optical module.
  • Optical chamber.

Internal contamination may include:

  • Dust ingress.
  • Moisture residue.
  • Condensation deposits.
  • Corrosion products.
  • Reflector oxidation.
  • Optical-film degradation.
  • Insect contamination.
  • Seal deterioration products.

Internal contamination affects optical efficiency.

7Internal Optical Contamination Principle

Clean optical chamber → High transmission → Controlled reflection → Correct beam → High intensity → ICAO compliance
Contaminated optical chamber → Reduced transmission → Internal scattering → Reflector loss → Reduced intensity → ICAO non-compliance

Internal contamination affects the entire optical path.

  • Reduced peak intensity.
  • Increased beam scatter.
  • Lower beam-edge intensity.
  • Reduced forward beam concentration.

Step 7: Seal Integrity Inspection

Inspection revealed:

  • Aged seals.
  • Micro-cracking.
  • Moisture ingress path.
  • Reduced sealing pressure.

Long-term environmental ingress had contaminated the optical chamber.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The low photometric intensity was caused by internal optical-system contamination, including:

  • Moisture residue.
  • Dust deposits.
  • Reflector contamination.
  • Optical-window film.
  • Seal deterioration.

The electrical system and external lens remained generally healthy.

The primary failure mechanism was internal optical transmission loss and reflector efficiency reduction.

11Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Disassembled the optical chamber.
  2. Replaced the contaminated optical assembly.
  3. Replaced the reflector.
  4. Replaced environmental seals.
  5. Cleaned internal optical components.
  6. Verified optical transmission.
  7. Performed photometric testing.
  8. Verified beam distribution.
  9. Conducted seal integrity testing.
  10. Updated maintenance procedures.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: Inset runway lights ON. CCR healthy. External lenses clean. Intensity still significantly below requirement. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace the CCR.
B. Increase current.
C. Inspect the internal optical chamber, reflector condition, optical-window contamination, seal integrity, and perform internal optical transmission assessment.
D. Replace the transformer.
Correct answer: C. Low intensity with clean external lenses and healthy electrical operation points to internal optical contamination. Endoscopic inspection, reflector evaluation, and transmission measurement will identify the cause without unnecessary equipment replacement.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about internal optical contamination and seal failure in inset lights.

Q1What is AGL-CS-073?

AGL-CS-073 is an engineering training case study. It covers runway centreline inset lights with low intensity caused by contamination inside the optical chamber, even though the external lenses were clean. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2Can low intensity come from inside the fixture when the outer lens is clean?

Yes. In AGL-CS-073 the external lenses were clean and the electrical system healthy, but internal contamination reduced optical transmission and increased scattering.

Q3Why did external cleaning not fix the problem?

Cleaning the lens raised intensity only from 27,400 cd to 28,100 cd, a 2.6% improvement. The intensity remained well below the required 45,000 cd because the contamination was inside the optical chamber.

Q4What did internal inspection find?

Endoscopic inspection found moisture deposits, dust accumulation, reflector contamination, optical-window film and seal degradation residue inside the optical chamber.

Q5How much did the internal contamination reduce optical performance?

Reflectance fell from 94% for a new reflector to 71%. Optical transmission fell from 92% clean to 63%, a loss of about 29 percentage points.

Q6What was the root cause of AGL-CS-073?

Long-term seal deterioration and moisture ingress. Aged seals with micro-cracking gave an ingress path, which contaminated the optical chamber and reduced transmission and reflector efficiency.

Q7What did the beam distribution show?

Reduced peak intensity, increased beam scatter, lower beam-edge intensity and reduced forward concentration. The optics were no longer producing the designed beam.

Q8How was compliance restored?

The team disassembled the optical chamber and replaced the contaminated optical assembly, reflector and environmental seals. They verified transmission, performed photometric testing and seal integrity testing, and updated maintenance procedures.

Q9What is the key lesson from this case?

If cleaning the outside barely helps, inspect inside the optical system. Check seals and internal surfaces before assuming the electrical or external cause.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

Low photometric intensity can be caused by contamination inside the optical system, where moisture deposits, reflector contamination, optical-window degradation, and seal deterioration reduce optical transmission and beam efficiency even though the external lens and electrical system remain healthy.

Systematic internal optical inspection, reflector evaluation, transmission measurement, seal integrity assessment, beam-distribution analysis, and post-repair photometric verification can identify hidden optical-system contamination and restore compliant airfield lighting performance.

In AGL engineering, the most significant photometric loss is often the contamination you cannot see from the outside.
