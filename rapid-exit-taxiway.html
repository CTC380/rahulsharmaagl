What is a rapid exit taxiway?

A taxiway that leaves the runway at a shallow angle, so a landing aircraft can turn off at speed and clear the runway sooner.

The Fact ICAO Annex 14

A rapid exit taxiway, or high-speed turn-off, joins the runway at an acute angle instead of a right angle. A landing aircraft can turn off while still moving at speed rather than slowing almost to a stop first, which shortens runway occupancy time and frees the runway for the next movement.

ICAO recommends an exit angle of 25° to 45°, with 30° preferred. The taxiway centreline leaves the runway in a large-radius curve, then runs straight long enough for the aircraft to stop clear of any taxiway ahead before it joins the parallel taxiway. Green centreline lights begin at least 60 m before the curve and continue beyond it to where an aircraft can be expected to reach normal taxiing speed. From the runway out to the edge of the ILS critical/sensitive area or the inner transitional surface, whichever is farther from the runway, they alternate green and yellow, with the light nearest that limit yellow; beyond it they are all green. Blue edge lights outline the pavement once it clears the runway.

RETIL sit on the runway on the same side of the centreline as the exit, each set laid out across the runway away from the centreline, with the single light 100 m before the point of tangency. RETIL are provided where the runway is used in low visibility (RVR below 350 m) and/or traffic is heavy. For the RETIL and centreline lighting detail, see the Rapid-Exit Taxiway Lighting entry.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is a rapid exit taxiway?

A taxiway that joins the runway at a shallow angle and curves away with a large radius, so a landing aircraft can turn off at higher speed and clear the runway sooner. It is also called a high-speed turn-off.

Q2What angle does a rapid exit taxiway make with the runway?

ICAO recommends an angle between 25 and 45 degrees, with 30 degrees preferred. A shallower angle lets the aircraft keep more speed through the turn, while a steeper one is closer to an ordinary exit.

Q3Why build a rapid exit taxiway?

It shortens runway occupancy time. The aircraft does not have to slow almost to a stop before turning, so the runway is free sooner for the next landing or take-off and the airport can handle more movements per hour.

Q4How is a rapid exit taxiway lit?

Blue edge lights outline the pavement and green centreline lights guide the aircraft along the curve, starting at least 60 m before it. Close to the runway, out to the edge of the ILS critical/sensitive area or the inner transitional surface, the centreline lights alternate green and yellow, and beyond that they are all green. Yellow RETIL lights on the runway give distance-to-go to the exit. See the Rapid-Exit Taxiway Lighting entry for the detail.
