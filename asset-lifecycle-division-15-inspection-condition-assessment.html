Initial Issue

The question this division answers — What is the current condition of the AGL asset, how is that condition changing, what does it mean for risk and reliability, and when should intervention, rehabilitation or replacement be considered?

Handbook boundary — Division 15 answers what did we find and what does it mean for the asset's lifecycle. The AGL Inspection Handbook answers how, exactly, is each inspection carried out in the field — methods, checklists, frequencies and step-by-step procedures live there, not here.

Where Division 15 Sits in the Lifecycle

Division 15 is the bridge between field inspection and the higher-level lifecycle framework — it turns raw inspection observations into condition scores, trends and risk-informed intervention recommendations that feed Asset Health, Reliability, Lifecycle Cost and Replacement Planning.

INS-05 Visual Inspection
Purpose

The first-level condition assessment. Results should be recorded against the individual asset ID.

Examine
  • Physical damage
  • Fixture condition, housing, lenses, covers
  • Mounting, fasteners, cable entries, connectors
  • Signs, PAPI structure, base cans, junction boxes
  • CCR external condition
Look For
  • Cracks
  • Damage
  • Deformation
  • Loose or missing components
  • Contamination
  • Corrosion
  • Water ingress
  • Abnormal appearance
INS-06 Electrical Condition
Purpose

Evaluates the electrical health of the asset. Depending on asset type, assess insulation condition, continuity, earthing, connections, terminations, connector condition, wiring, cable condition, electrical protection, abnormal heating and electrical test results.

INS-07 Mechanical Condition
Purpose

Concerns the physical and mechanical integrity of equipment.

Assess
  • Mounting, fasteners, hinges, covers, supports
  • Alignment
  • Mechanical damage
  • Structural connections
  • Moving mechanisms, where applicable
Examples
  • PAPI mounting
  • Sign mounting
  • Elevated fixture mounting
  • CCR mechanical components
  • Equipment cabinet condition
INS-08 Optical Condition
Purpose

Optical condition is particularly important for AGL. Where applicable, condition assessment should be supported by actual photometric measurements.

Assess
  • Lens condition
  • Optical surfaces
  • Light output
  • Optical contamination
  • LED / module condition
  • Beam characteristics
  • Alignment
  • Photometric performance
Important

A fixture may appear physically acceptable while its optical performance has deteriorated. Physical condition alone should not be treated as a complete indicator of AGL performance.

INS-09 Structural Condition
Purpose

Applies particularly to assets with physical supports, mounting systems or civil interfaces. Structural deterioration should be evaluated according to its potential safety and operational consequence.

Assess
  • Cracks
  • Deformation
  • Corrosion
  • Foundation condition
  • Mounting integrity
  • Structural connections
  • Base-can condition
  • Support condition
Examples
  • PAPI structures
  • Elevated lights
  • Signs and sign foundations
  • Equipment supports
  • Base cans
INS-10 Environmental Condition
Purpose

Identifies the environmental factors affecting asset deterioration.

Consider
  • Temperature, humidity
  • Water exposure, flooding, drainage
  • Dust, sand
  • Salt exposure, coastal environment
  • UV exposure
  • Chemical exposure
  • Airside contamination
Why It Matters

Two identical assets may have substantially different service lives because they operate in different environmental conditions. Environmental condition should therefore become an input to lifecycle planning.

INS-12 Moisture Assessment
Purpose

An important condition indicator for AGL electrical equipment. Moisture findings should be connected with electrical condition and failure history.

Look For
  • Water ingress, condensation
  • Moisture inside enclosures, wet base cans
  • Cable-entry leakage
  • Damaged seals
  • Drainage problems
  • Corrosion associated with moisture
Potential Consequences
  • Insulation deterioration
  • Corrosion
  • Electrical faults
  • Connector deterioration
  • Electronic failure
  • Reduced equipment life
INS-13 Performance Assessment
Purpose

Condition should not be assessed solely from physical appearance. Performance assessment determines whether the asset continues to deliver its intended function.

Possible Indicators
  • Light output, photometric performance
  • Electrical performance
  • CCR / PAPI / sign performance
  • ALCMS functionality
  • Energy performance
  • Availability
  • Response / functionality
Principle

Condition assessment should evaluate both physical condition and functional performance.

INS-14 Condition Grading
Purpose

A standardized condition grading system should be established. A simple five-level framework can be used if it matches the organization's approved methodology.

Practical Structure
GradeConditionLifecycle Interpretation

Important

The grade should be evidence-based and supported by inspection results, testing, performance, defects, history and risk.

INS-15 Defect Classification
Purpose

Inspection findings should be classified consistently.

Possible Classification
  • Observation — minor condition requiring monitoring
  • Defect — condition requiring corrective action
  • Major Defect — condition with significant operational / reliability impact
  • Critical Defect — condition requiring immediate risk control or intervention
Should Be Linked To
  • Asset criticality
  • Safety
  • Operational impact
  • Failure probability
  • Consequence

Most Important Design Principle

Division 15 should remain the condition-intelligence layer. It answers what the current condition of the asset is, how that condition is changing, what it means for risk and reliability, and when intervention, rehabilitation or replacement should be considered — it does not hold the detailed field procedures, checklists or inspection frequencies, which belong to the AGL Inspection Handbook.

Inspection is not simply the process of finding defects; it is the systematic collection of evidence required to understand asset condition, deterioration, risk, remaining life and the appropriate lifecycle intervention. Physical condition alone is never a complete indicator — optical, electrical and performance data must be assessed together, and condition grades must always be evidence-based.

Division 03 — ASSESS RISK & CRITICALITY
Divisions 07–12 — COMMISSION → OPERATE → MAINTAIN → MONITOR HEALTH → RELIABILITY → LIFECYCLE COST
Division 15 — INSPECT & ASSESS CONDITION
Division 16 onward — REHABILITATE / UPGRADE → REPLACE WHEN JUSTIFIED → DISPOSE

This makes Division 15 the principal bridge between the AGL Inspection Handbook and the higher-level Asset Health, Reliability, Lifecycle Cost and Replacement Planning framework — turning field observations into structured, trend-based, risk-informed lifecycle decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Asset Inspection & Condition Assessment in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide.

Q1What is Division 15 — Asset Inspection & Condition Assessment in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

Division 15 is the condition-intelligence layer. It covers 19 topics on inspection strategy, condition assessment, defect classification, condition scoring, trend analysis and inspection-based replacement recommendations.

Q2What is an AGL asset inspection strategy?

It defines which assets need inspection, what condition information is required, how often assessment should occur and how results will support lifecycle decisions. It is driven by factors such as criticality and safety significance.

Q3What are initial, periodic and detailed condition assessments?

Initial assessment establishes the starting condition and links to Division 07. Periodic assessment tracks how condition changes over time so deterioration is identified before it becomes a major failure. Detailed assessment is initiated when normal inspection identifies a significant concern, such as repeated failures or severe deterioration.

Q4Which condition parameters are assessed?

Division 15 covers visual, electrical, mechanical, optical, structural and environmental condition, plus corrosion and moisture assessment. Optical condition should be supported by photometric measurements where applicable.

Q5Why is performance assessment needed in addition to visual inspection?

Condition should not be assessed solely from physical appearance. Performance assessment determines whether the asset continues to deliver its intended function, using light output, photometric performance and electrical performance.

Q6How are condition grading and defect classification used?

A standardized condition grading system should be established, and a simple five-level framework can be used if it matches the organization's approved methodology. Defects are classified consistently, for example as an observation requiring monitoring, a defect requiring corrective action, or a major defect.

Q7What are the asset condition score, inspection history and condition trend?

The condition score converts multiple observations into a structured asset-health indicator. Inspection history retains every significant inspection so deterioration is visible over time, and condition trends are more valuable than a single result because they show the direction and rate of deterioration.

Q8How do inspection results support replacement decisions?

Inspection should ultimately support the lifecycle decision: continue operating, monitor, repair, rehabilitate, upgrade or replace. Division 15 connects with the AGL Inspection Handbook but stays focused on condition, lifecycle decisions, risk and strategic planning.
