Overview

How Does PAPI Work?

A Precision Approach Path Indicator (PAPI) gives pilots visual information about the aircraft's position relative to the desired approach slope. A conventional PAPI is four light units in a horizontal row beside the runway. Each unit produces a precisely controlled transition between white and red light, and as the aircraft moves above or below the designed path, the combination of white and red changes.

Think of it as four light units + four optical settings + one approach reference. For the pilot it is simple. For the engineer, the units need the correct location, elevation, spacing, horizontal alignment, vertical aiming and optical performance for the indication to represent the intended path.

IndicationMeaning
4 WhiteAircraft significantly above the indicated approach path
3 White + 1 RedSlightly above the indicated approach path
2 White + 2 RedApproximately on the indicated approach path
1 White + 3 RedSlightly below the indicated approach path
4 RedSignificantly below the indicated approach path
Exact response to any indication is governed by the approved approach procedure and aircraft operating practice.

Working Principle

Why Red and White? The Optical Transition

Why does PAPI show red and white?

Each unit is designed so that, viewed from the approach, one angular region appears white, another appears red, and the change between them happens at a controlled elevation angle. What matters is not that a unit can make two colours, but that the transition sits at a defined vertical viewing angle. The four units are then aimed at progressively different elevations, which turns a continuous change in aircraft height into an intuitive pattern of colours.

Side view (angles exaggerated): the aircraft crosses each unit's transition line as it moves up or down

Does every unit point at the same angle?

No. The four units have different vertical aiming settings, carefully related so the transitions occur at the required angular positions. Installation is therefore not "install four units in a row" but survey, elevation, spacing, alignment, individual aiming and optical verification.

Why one moved unit matters

If Unit 3 moves, it may still turn on and show white and red, yet its transition now happens at the wrong elevation, changing the indication an approaching aircraft sees. PAPI aiming is a safety-related engineering parameter, not just a mechanical adjustment.

What PAPI Is Not

What PAPI Does and Does Not Tell the Pilot

Angular reference

PAPI shows the angular relationship between the aircraft line of sight and the four beams. 2 White + 2 Red means approximately on the indicated path.

Not an altimeter

It does not give altitude, airspeed, distance or attitude. The same indication occurs at different altitudes depending on distance from the runway.

Colour logic

White and red are used for vertical approach-path indication only. Runway and taxiway lighting use a wider colour set for their own functions.

Saying "PAPI can always be seen from X kilometres" is too simplistic. Range depends on intensity, atmospheric visibility, background, aircraft and pilot eye position, runway environment, design and operational requirements.
Geometry

Approach Slope, TCH and MEHT

Threshold Crossing Height (TCH) relates the visual approach path to aircraft height over the threshold. Minimum Eye Height over Threshold (MEHT) is the associated eye-height characteristic of the approved PAPI and runway geometry. Both belong to the larger approach geometry, so a PAPI cannot be designed by just choosing a four-light unit.

Is PAPI always 3°?

No. 3.00° is common, but the correct value is the one set by the approved runway design and current aeronautical information. Indian AAI eAIP entries publish the PAPI side, approach angle and MEHT per runway direction, with examples at 3.00° and at 3.25°. Check the current eAIP for any runway.

Why location matters

Location depends on threshold position, runway elevation, approach slope, TCH/MEHT, obstacles, aircraft considerations and runway geometry. Moving a PAPI without recalculating changes its relationship to the intended path. Resurfacing, extension, re-thresholding or threshold relocation should trigger a PAPI engineering review.

Approach lightingVisual reference extending before the thresholdDifferent information; both work together in the visual approach environment

It is useful at night and may contribute in reduced visibility where the approved procedure permits, alongside approach lighting, runway lighting, markings, weather/RVR and airport procedures.

Photometry

PAPI Photometry: Why "ON" Is Not Enough

Visual inspection tells you a unit is on. Photometry tells you how much light it produces and where it goes. A PAPI can be electrically functional but photometrically defective because of a dirty lens, optical deterioration, incorrect aiming, a damaged optical component, an LED/driver problem or mechanical displacement.

Intensity

A low result can come from contamination, optical degradation, electrical problems, LED/driver degradation or wrong test geometry. Verify the measurement before replacing equipment.

Colour and chromaticity

Colour depends on optical components, filters, lens condition, LED performance, contamination and correct replacements. AAI field-photometry specifications include colour and chromaticity for AGL and PAPI testing.

Beam alignment

Aiming errors come from foundation movement, impact, loose hardware, construction, incorrect adjustment or mechanical degradation, so verify aiming periodically.

Maintenance

PAPI Maintenance and Testing After Work

CategoryCheck
OpticalLens, colour, intensity, beam coverage, beam alignment
MechanicalFoundation, mounting, horizontal alignment, vertical aiming, unit relationship
ElectricalSupply, cable, connector, transformer, insulation, earthing
FunctionalAll units on, colour indication, control, brightness, final indication
FinalRetest and documentation

After maintenance follow this order: alignment check → electrical check → photometry → functional test → retest → documentation, so there is evidence that the work restored intended performance. AAI maintenance specifications give examples of this integrated approach, including physical inspection, lens cleaning, PAPI angle checks, calibration, earthing, insulation and transformer and connector checks.

Flight inspection

Ground maintenance and photometry are not formal airborne verification. Where the approved procedure requires it, PAPI is evaluated from the air. An AAI document describes ground calibration before flight calibration, with checks such as PAPI setting angles, brilliancy control and compatibility with the ILS glide path. The current airport and authority procedure decides when it is required.

India

PAPI in India

PAPI is widely deployed across Indian aerodromes. AAI eAIP material gives runway-specific data (PAPI side, approach angle and MEHT), so installed values differ between aerodromes and runway directions. For Indian projects the basis should include current DGCA requirements, AAI or project specifications, approved aerodrome information and manufacturer requirements, and the work chain runs survey, design review, civil foundation, installation, electrical integration, aiming, photometry, testing, commissioning and maintenance.

Standards

PAPI and ICAO

ICAO Annex 14 Volume I is the principal international aerodrome standard framework, and ICAO's Aerodrome Design Manual Part 4 (Visual Aids) gives engineering guidance on visual approach slope indicators. The ICAO Store lists Annex 14 Vol. I as the 9th Edition (July 2022) as amended, including Amendment 18. Corrigenda have followed, so confirm the latest corrigendum or edition and your State's implementation before relying on any requirement.

FAQ

How PAPI Works: FAQ

Q1How does PAPI work?

PAPI uses four optical units with different vertical aiming settings. Each has a controlled white/red transition, and the combination the pilot sees shows whether the aircraft is above, approximately on, or below the intended approach path.

Q2What does PAPI stand for?

Precision Approach Path Indicator.

Q3How many lights are in a PAPI?

A conventional PAPI uses four light units.

Q4Why does PAPI use red and white?

The two colours give a simple visual indication of the aircraft's relationship to the intended approach slope.

Q5What does 2 white and 2 red mean?

The aircraft is approximately on the intended PAPI approach path.

Q6What does 3 white and 1 red mean?

The aircraft is slightly above the indicated approach path.

Q7What does 1 white and 3 red mean?

The aircraft is slightly below the indicated approach path.

Q8What does 4 red mean on PAPI?

The aircraft is significantly below the indicated visual approach path.

Q9Does 4 white always mean the same altitude?

No. PAPI gives an angular visual reference, not a fixed altitude. Actual altitude depends on distance and position relative to the runway.

Q10Is every PAPI set to 3 degrees?

No. 3.00° is common, but designs can differ. Indian AAI publications show examples at both 3.00° and 3.25°.

Q11What is PAPI TCH?

Threshold Crossing Height: the relationship between the approach path and aircraft height over the threshold.

Q12What is PAPI MEHT?

Minimum Eye Height over Threshold, associated with the approved PAPI and runway approach geometry and published per runway direction in Indian AAI aeronautical information.

Q13Is PAPI the same as ILS?

No. PAPI is a visual approach-slope indicator and ILS is a radio-navigation system. They can be used together.

Q14Is PAPI the same as approach lighting?

No. They provide different visual information and work together in the approach environment.

Q15Does PAPI need photometric testing?

It may, during commissioning, inspection, maintenance or acceptance per the applicable programme. Measurements can include intensity, colour, chromaticity and beam characteristics.

Q16Can PAPI work without ILS?

Yes. It can give visual approach-slope guidance on appropriately equipped runways, subject to the applicable design and operational requirements.

Q17Can PAPI be on but still defective?

Yes. It can operate electrically while having incorrect aiming, low intensity, damaged optics or poor colour.

Q18Why is PAPI aiming important?

The white/red transition is angular, so changing a unit's physical aiming changes where the transition is seen.

Q19What happens if one PAPI unit moves?

Its transition moves relative to the other three units, potentially changing the indication presented to the pilot.

Q20Does PAPI need maintenance?

Yes. Maintenance should cover optical, mechanical, electrical, aiming, photometric and functional performance.
