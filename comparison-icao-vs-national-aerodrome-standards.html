Standards & Compliance Comparison

ICAO VS National Standards

Annex 14, Volume I vs the national aerodrome regulations of a given State — ICAO provides the international baseline for aerodrome design and operations, while National Aerodrome Standards convert, adopt, or supplement those requirements for a particular country. For AGL, the practical rule is simple: ICAO is the international baseline, and the national standard is the legally/regulatorily applicable requirement in that country.

02 · ICAO's Role

The International SARPs Framework

ICAO

ICAO develops Standards and Recommended Practices (SARPs). For aerodromes, the principal reference is ICAO Annex 14, Volume I — Aerodrome Design and Operations. ICAO's purpose is international harmonization.

Annex 14, Vol. I covers

  • Aerodrome physical characteristics
  • Runways, taxiways & visual aids
  • Markings, lighting & signs
  • PAPI & obstacle-related visual aids
  • Electrical systems, secondary power & maintenance

National Standard

Each State establishes its own regulatory framework that implements, reproduces, or supplements ICAO requirements. The national authority may adopt requirements directly, reproduce them nationally, add its own requirements, modify them where permitted, or set procedures for certification and oversight.

Examples of national authorities

  • India → DGCA
  • United States → FAA
  • United Kingdom → CAA
  • Australia → CASA
  • Canada → Transport Canada

03 · Key Difference

International Baseline vs Applicable Requirement

The easiest way to remember the difference between ICAO and a National Aerodrome Standard:

ICAO

International baseline — what aerodrome visual aids need to achieve, harmonized worldwide

National Standard

Legally/regulatorily applicable requirement in that country — how it is implemented and enforced

Common misunderstanding: "If the airport complies with ICAO Annex 14, it automatically complies with the country's aviation regulations." Not necessarily — the State is responsible for implementing applicable international obligations through its own national aviation framework.

08 · National Deviations

Never Assume ICAO Is the Complete Requirement

One of the most important concepts is differences from ICAO provisions. A State may have national differences or additional provisions relative to a given ICAO Standard.

ICAO Standard
Adopted unchanged
Nationally supplemented
Difference / variation

Never assume that an ICAO requirement is the complete national requirement. For a real project, check the current national regulation and applicable differences.

09 · Country Examples

Two National Implementation Structures

India

ICAO → Annex 14 → Indian aviation regulatory framework → DGCA → Airport/AAI requirements → Project specification → AGL equipment.

For an Indian AGL project, DGCA requirements and the airport/project specification must be checked in addition to ICAO Annex 14.

United States

ICAO → International framework → U.S. national aviation framework → FAA → FAA AC / specifications / regulations → Airport.

FAA requirements should not be treated simply as an "alternative version" of ICAO Annex 14 — they are the U.S. national implementation and technical framework.

Three Standards, Three Different Functions

For example, an AGL transformer could involve an ICAO aerodrome electrical-system requirement, a national authority's regulatory requirement, IEC 61823's technical transformer requirements, and a project specification's exact contractual requirement — each performing a different function.

14 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: "ICAO compliant" = nationally approved
Fact

Not necessarily. National authority approval, certification and project-specific requirements must still be verified.

Myth: The national standard replaces ICAO
Fact

Not necessarily — the national standard may implement ICAO requirements rather than replace them entirely.

Myth: IEC compliant = aerodrome compliant
Fact

No. IEC addresses technical/electrical requirements, not the complete aviation operational requirement.

Myth: FAA and ICAO are equivalent
Fact

No. They are different regulatory/technical frameworks, and the same applies to any national authority compared against ICAO.

15 · Best Compliance Statement

How to Phrase Compliance Correctly

Not sufficient

"Equipment complies with ICAO."

Better

"Equipment complies with the applicable national aerodrome requirements and project specification, with the relevant ICAO Annex 14 requirements identified in the compliance matrix."

Indian project example: "Equipment complies with applicable DGCA requirements, airport/project specifications and relevant ICAO Annex 14 provisions. Applicable IS/IEC standards are identified separately."

16 · Engineering Verdict

National Regulation First, ICAO as Baseline

Engineering Verdict

ICAO establishes the international aerodrome baseline; the National Aerodrome Standard establishes how that baseline is implemented and regulated within a particular country. For airport ground lighting, the safest compliance approach is national regulation first, then project specification, then ICAO baseline, then applicable technical standards, then manufacturer evidence.

Do not use "ICAO compliant" as a substitute for national regulatory approval. For an Indian airport, for example, DGCA plus Airport/AAI requirements plus ICAO Annex 14 plus applicable IS/IEC standards should be mapped item by item for the runway lights, taxiway lights, PAPI, CCR, transformers, cables, connectors, secondary power, photometry, FAT/SAT and maintenance.

ICAOInternational aerodrome baseline — harmonized SARPs referenced worldwide.
National StandardCountry-specific implementation with direct legal/regulatory force — usually decisive.

17 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Does complying with ICAO Annex 14 automatically satisfy a country's aviation regulations?

Not necessarily. The State is responsible for implementing applicable international obligations through its own national aviation framework, and the national standard governs what is actually mandatory.

Can a national authority add requirements beyond ICAO?

Yes. A national authority may specify detailed approval procedures, equipment certification, inspection frequency, calibration procedure, reporting requirements, local environmental considerations, operational restrictions and national deviations.

Is ICAO-compliant photometry sufficient for national approval?

Not on its own. A fixture may have ICAO-compliant photometry while the national authority or airport still specifies additional requirements that must also be satisfied for final acceptance.

Which requirement governs on an Indian aerodrome project?

DGCA requirements and the airport/project specification must be checked in addition to ICAO Annex 14 — DGCA and the airport/project documents are usually decisive.

Are FAA requirements just a U.S. version of ICAO Annex 14?

No. FAA requirements should not be treated simply as an "alternative version" of ICAO Annex 14 — they are the U.S. national implementation and technical framework, developed through its own regulations, ACs and specifications.

What is the correct way to phrase an AGL compliance statement?

State compliance with the applicable national aerodrome requirements and project specification, with the relevant ICAO Annex 14 requirements identified in a compliance matrix, rather than a bare "complies with ICAO" statement.

18 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual airport project, verify the current applicable editions of the relevant standards before using this comparison for project design.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — the international baseline for visual aids, lighting characteristics, colour, intensity, location, secondary power and maintenance performance objectives.

National Aerodrome Regulations

Issued by the national aviation authority (e.g. DGCA, FAA, CAA, CASA, Transport Canada) — direct regulatory relevance and usually decisive for project compliance.

Airport / Project Specification

May impose requirements more restrictive than the national minimum — governs equipment, testing and documentation details for the specific project.

IS / IEC Technical Standards

Technical/equipment requirements (e.g. IEC 61823 for transformers) — address electrical/technical performance, not the complete aviation operational requirement.

Final Engineering Note

Never assume an ICAO requirement is the complete national requirement — check the current national regulation and applicable differences for the specific project country.

ICAO = international aerodrome baseline. National Aerodrome Standard = the legally/regulatorily applicable requirement in that country. Do not use "ICAO compliant" as a substitute for national regulatory approval — map each requirement item by item.
