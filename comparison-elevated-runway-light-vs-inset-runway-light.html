Runway Lighting Comparison

Elevated Runway Light VS Inset Runway Light

Above the Surface vs Within the Surface — an elevated runway light is a fixture mounted above the pavement or ground, while an inset runway light sits within the pavement with its upper surface close to pavement level. The core difference is physical installation, aircraft clearance and mechanical protection; both can provide the required runway visual guidance when correctly designed and installed.

02 · The Core Idea

Above the Surface vs Within It

The fundamental difference is where the fixture sits relative to the pavement. An elevated light is physically visible above the surface, mounted on a base or support. An inset light is installed inside the pavement, with its top kept close to the surrounding surface so aircraft can pass over or near it without obstruction.

03 · Each Fixture Explained

What Each Light Is

Elevated Runway Light

Mounted above the pavement or ground on a base or support, so the fixture is physically visible above the surface.

Commonly used where

  • The fixture is outside the aircraft wheel path
  • Adequate obstacle clearance is available
  • Easy maintenance access is desirable
  • Pavement cutting is to be minimised

Inset Runway Light

Installed inside the pavement, with its top kept close to the surface so aircraft can pass over or near it without creating an obstruction.

Commonly used where

  • An elevated fixture would create an obstruction
  • The location is exposed to aircraft wheel loading
  • A low physical profile is required
  • The fixture's approved load rating supports the traffic area
04 · Biggest Difference: Aircraft Wheel Path

Elevated Stays Clear · Inset Is Built to Take the Load

This is one of the most important differences between the two. An elevated fixture generally cannot be placed where aircraft wheels are expected to pass directly over it. An inset fixture is specifically suited to pavement locations exposed to aircraft movement and wheel loading, subject to its approved load rating and installation requirements.

This is why a typical runway arrangement often uses elevated lights at the edges — outside the wheel path — and inset lights along the centreline and in the touchdown zone, where aircraft wheels pass directly over the fixture.

05 · Typical Applications

Where Each Type Is Used

Elevated Lights

Often used for runway edge lighting and other positions where the fixture can safely remain above ground, outside areas of direct aircraft wheel contact.

Inset Lights

Common for runway centreline and touchdown zone lighting, and other aircraft movement areas where a low physical profile is required.

Threshold, wing-bar and runway-end lights can be either type: they are typically elevated where they sit outside the pavement and inset where they fall on it (for example across a displaced threshold), as the approved airfield lighting design specifies.

06 · Installation Comparison

What Each Installation Involves

Elevated Light

  • Base installation
  • Mounting support
  • Fixture installation
  • Cable connection
  • Alignment

Inset Light

  • Pavement recess/core cutting
  • Base can/pot or approved mounting arrangement
  • Cable connection
  • Fixture installation
  • Pavement sealing and level adjustment

This generally makes inset installation more complex than elevated installation.

07 · Drainage

Why Drainage Matters More for Inset Lights

Drainage is particularly important for inset fixtures because water can accumulate around the unit within the pavement structure. Poor drainage can lead to water accumulation, corrosion, electrical problems, reduced reliability, and freezing issues in cold environments. Elevated lights generally have fewer water-accumulation concerns since they sit above the pavement structure rather than within it.

Design implication: inset fixture installations need properly detailed drainage provisions around the base can/pot — this is a critical, not optional, part of the design.

08 · Maintenance

Access and Upkeep

Elevated Runway Light

Easy access, easier replacement, easier visual inspection, and less pavement work involved when servicing the fixture.

Inset Runway Light

Maintenance may require removing the fixture from its base, cleaning the optical window, checking seals, inspecting for water ingress, checking fixture-to-pavement level, and inspecting mounting hardware.

09 · Physical Protection

Exposure and Risk of Impact

An elevated fixture is more exposed to vehicle impact, ground equipment, jet blast and general physical damage, since it stands above the surface. Elevated aeronautical ground lights are made frangible: ICAO Annex 14 specifies that they break away on impact and are mounted low enough to clear propellers and engine pods, and FAA elevated fixtures carry a designed yield point at the coupling. An inset fixture has a low profile and reduced obstruction, making it better suited to aircraft operating surfaces — but it is instead exposed to aircraft wheel loads, rubber deposits, pavement contamination, water ingress and surface damage.

10 · Electrical Arrangement

Mounting Type vs Electrical Principle

Both elevated and inset lights can be part of the same airport ground lighting system, typically supplied through a CCR, series circuit and isolation transformer to the light unit. The mounting type does not fundamentally determine the electrical supply principle.

Key point: elevated vs inset describes the physical installation, not necessarily the electrical supply system. The same series-circuit and isolation-transformer arrangement can feed either type.

11 · Myth vs Fact

Common AGL Terminology Confusion

Myth: Elevated lights are always brighter than inset lights
Fact

The mounting type alone does not determine photometric performance. Both can meet the required output when correctly designed — performance depends on fixture photometry, light source, optical design, intensity setting and installation geometry, not on whether the fixture is elevated or inset.

Myth: Inset lights are always cheaper because they're low-profile
Fact

The opposite is generally true. Inset installation involves pavement cutting, base cans, sealing and drainage provisions, making initial installation cost generally higher than elevated fixtures.

Myth: Elevated and inset lights use different electrical systems
Fact

Not necessarily. The mounting type describes the physical installation, not the electrical supply — both can be fed through the same CCR, series-circuit and isolation-transformer arrangement.

12 · Engineering Verdict

Elevated Runway Light vs Inset Runway Light

Engineering Verdict

The choice comes down to aircraft clearance and location, not performance. Where a fixture can safely sit above the surface outside the wheel path, elevated lighting gives lower installation complexity and easier maintenance. Where the location is exposed to aircraft wheel loading — centreline, touchdown zone, other movement areas — an inset fixture with an approved load rating is required to avoid creating an obstruction.

Both fixture types can provide the required photometric performance; the decision is fundamentally about installation position, mechanical loading and civil works, not light output.

ElevatedAbove surface + easier access + lower installation complexity.
InsetWithin surface + low profile + built for aircraft wheel loading.

Easy memory: Elevated → above, easy maintenance. Inset → inside, built for aircraft clearance and wheel-load capability.

13 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between an elevated and an inset runway light?

Physical installation position. An elevated light is mounted above the pavement or ground on a base or support. An inset light is installed within the pavement, with its top kept close to the surrounding surface.

Why can't elevated lights be used in the aircraft wheel path?

Because they sit above the surface, an elevated fixture generally cannot be placed where aircraft wheels are expected to pass directly over it without creating an obstruction hazard.

Do elevated runway lights have to be frangible?

Yes. Elevated runway lights are designed to break away cleanly if struck and are mounted low enough to preserve clearance for aircraft propellers and engine pods. Inset lights avoid the protrusion issue by sitting flush with the surface, but their housings must carry wheel loads instead.

Are inset lights dimmer than elevated lights?

Not inherently. Light output depends on fixture photometry, light source, optical design, intensity setting and installation geometry — not on whether the fixture is elevated or inset.

Why is drainage more critical for inset lights?

Because inset fixtures sit within the pavement structure, water can accumulate around the unit. Poor drainage can lead to corrosion, electrical problems, reduced reliability and freezing issues, so drainage provisions are a critical part of inset fixture design.

Which type is used for centreline and touchdown zone lighting?

Inset lights are the typical choice for centreline and touchdown zone lighting, since these locations are directly exposed to aircraft wheel loading and need a low, flush profile.

Do elevated and inset lights use different electrical circuits?

Not fundamentally. Both mounting types can be part of the same series-circuit, CCR and isolation-transformer arrangement — the mounting type describes physical installation, not the electrical supply principle.

14 · Standards & Reference Basis

Governing Documents

For an actual project design, always verify the current edition of the applicable documents.

ICAO Annex 14, Volume I

Aerodrome Design and Operations — runway light siting, frangibility, and requirements applicable to elevated and inset fixtures. Verify the amendment level adopted by your State (Amendment 18 became applicable on 27 November 2025).

ICAO Aerodrome Design Manual (Doc 9157)

Part 4 (Visual Aids), Part 5 (Electrical Systems) and Part 6 (Frangibility) — design guidance on aerodrome lighting, its electrical supply and frangible mounting.

FAA AC 150/5345-46

Specification for Runway, Taxiway, Heliport, and Vertiport Light Fixtures (current edition: 150/5345-46F, September 2024) — the equipment spec both elevated and inset runway fixtures must meet, including load testing of in-pavement units.

FAA AC 150/5345-42

Specification for Airport Light Bases, Transformer Housings, Junction Boxes, and Accessories — the bases and mountings used with both elevated and inset fixtures.

FAA AC 150/5340-30

Design and Installation Details for Airport Visual Aids — siting and installation details for airfield lighting. Equipment performance (photometrics, load testing, frangibility) is set by the fixture and base specifications, AC 150/5345-46 and AC 150/5345-42.

DGCA / AAI Requirements

For Indian airport projects, also verify applicable DGCA requirements, AAI requirements, airport-specific technical specifications, project drawings and contract specifications.

Final Engineering Note

Do not select elevated or inset fixture type from a generic web diagram. The final installation drawing should always be developed from the current governing standard, approved airport design and project-specific requirements, including the fixture's approved wheel-load rating where applicable.

Elevated = above the surface, easier to maintain, kept outside the wheel path. Inset = within the surface, low profile, built for aircraft wheel loading in the movement area.
