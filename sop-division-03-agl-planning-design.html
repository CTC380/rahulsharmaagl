  • Terrain information
  • Operational requirements
  • Future expansion requirements
  • Applicable standards and specifications

  2. Identify affected movement areas.
  3. Determine required visual aids.
  4. Review existing AGL infrastructure.
  5. Identify deficiencies.

  7. Develop preliminary circuit architecture.
  8. Identify CCR requirements.
  9. Establish cable routing philosophy.
  10. Develop preliminary equipment quantities.
  11. Identify interfaces with other systems.
  12. Assess maintainability.
  13. Prepare preliminary layouts.

SOP-78 Design Change Control
Purpose

To ensure that changes to approved AGL designs are technically evaluated, documented and controlled.

Examples of Design Changes
  • Fixture relocation
  • Cable route change
  • CCR replacement
  • Circuit modification
  • Transformer rating change
  • Signage modification
  • PAPI relocation
  • Approach light modification
  • Equipment substitution
  • BOQ quantity change
Procedure

Step 1 — Identify Change: document the existing design, proposed change, reason for change and originator.

Step 2 — Technical Assessment: assess impact on safety, electrical design, photometry, operations, civil works, maintenance, cost, programme and documentation.

Step 3 — Approval: obtain approval from the designated technical/engineering authority.

Step 4 — Update Documentation: update affected drawings, BOQ, equipment schedule, cable schedule, circuit schedule, calculations, specifications, test procedures and as-built information.

Step 5 — Implementation: only the approved revision shall be issued for construction.

Step 6 — Verification: confirm that the installed change matches the approved change.

Step 7 — Closeout: record the change reference, date, reason, technical assessment, approval, implementation status and updated documents.

Division-Wide Requirements

Master workflow, quality-control principles and the review checklist that apply across all 28 Division 03 procedures.

A. Master AGL Design Workflow

The recommended engineering workflow is:

PROJECT REQUIREMENTS SITE / EXISTING DATA DESIGN BASIS AGL SYSTEM PLANNING RWY/TWY/APCH/PAPI/SIGN PLANNING CIRCUIT ARCHITECTURE CCR & EQUIPMENT SELECTION LOAD CALCULATION VOLTAGE / CIRCUIT VERIFICATION LAYOUT DRAWINGS CABLE ROUTING EQUIPMENT SCHEDULE CABLE SCHEDULE BOQ TECHNICAL SUBMITTAL REVIEW DESIGN COMPLIANCE REVIEW APPROVED FOR CONSTRUCTION CONSTRUCTION / INSTALLATION SITE VERIFICATION AS-BUILT VERIFICATION FINAL DOCUMENTATION

B. Design Quality Control Principles

Principle 01 — One Source of Truth

The latest approved design revision shall be the controlling engineering document.

Principle 02 — Drawing-to-BOQ Traceability

Every major BOQ item should be traceable to the approved drawings and specifications.

Principle 03 — Circuit-to-Equipment Traceability

Every AGL circuit should be traceable to: CCR → Cable → Transformer → Fixture/Sign → Control/Monitoring.

Principle 04 — Design-to-As-Built Traceability

The final installed system shall be traceable back to the approved design, with all authorized deviations documented.

Principle 05 — Independent Verification

Critical calculations and design decisions should undergo independent technical review.

Principle 06 — Design for Maintainability

  • Accessibility
  • Fault isolation
  • Testability
  • Replacement
  • Spare requirements
  • Cable identification
  • Equipment identification
  • Maintenance access
  • Future expansion

Principle 07 — Safety by Design

Safety shall be considered during design rather than added after installation.

Division 03 — Golden Rule

"No AGL installation should begin from an unverified design. Plan the operational requirement, define the circuit architecture, select compatible equipment, verify the electrical and photometric design, cross-check the BOQ and drawings, control every design change, and ensure the final as-built system accurately represents what was installed."

Division 03 — SOP Approval Principle

Each project should adapt these SOPs to its own approved aerodrome requirements, applicable regulatory requirements, operational concept, aircraft movement areas, declared distances, obstacle environment, electrical system characteristics, maintainability criteria and approved project specifications.

Local project specifications, airport standards and authorized engineering instructions shall take precedence wherever they impose more restrictive requirements.

Design Hold Points

  • HP-01 — Design Basis — Standards, aerodrome data and operational requirements frozen.
  • HP-02 — Layout — Layout checked against ICAO and national geometry.
  • HP-03 — Calculations — Circuit load, CCR sizing, voltage drop and spare capacity verified.
  • HP-04 — Equipment — Fixture, transformer, cable and connector types confirmed as compliant or approved.
  • HP-05 — Coordination — Civil, power, ALCMS and operational interfaces coordinated.
  • HP-06 — Independent Check — Design independently reviewed.
  • HP-07 — Change Control — Design changes approved before issue.
  • HP-08 — Issue — Drawings and schedules issued for construction only after all comments are closed.

Reference Basis

Technical references behind the procedures in this division:

  • ICAO Annex 14, Vol. I — Aerodrome Design and Operations, Chapter 5 and Appendices 1 and 2 — visual aids, aeronautical ground light colours and light characteristics (isocandela requirements).
  • ICAO Annex 14, Vol. I — Aerodrome Design and Operations, Chapter 8 (Electrical systems) — power supply, secondary power and switch-over times, light circuit design and monitoring.
  • ICAO Doc 9157, Aerodrome Design Manual, Part 4 (Visual Aids) and Part 5 (Electrical Systems) — design guidance for layouts, circuits and power systems.
  • ICAO Doc 9476 (SMGCS) and Doc 9830 (A-SMGCS) — taxiway guidance, stop bar and routing design.
  • FAA AC 150/5340-30 and AC 150/5300-13 — design and installation details for visual aids, and airport design.
  • FAA AC 150/5345 series and AC 150/5345-53 — equipment specifications, and the Airport Lighting Equipment Certification Program for approved equipment.
  • IEC 61822 (constant current regulators) and IEC 61823 (series transformers) — ratings used in load, capacity and loading calculations.
  • UK CAA CAP 637 (Visual Aids Handbook) and CAP 168 — supplementary national practice on visual aids and licensing.
  • ISO 9001 — design control, design review, verification and change control.

References are given at document or chapter level for technical orientation. Confirm the current edition, amendments and national adoption (for example DGCA CAR Section 4, FAA Advisory Circulars or EASA material) for each project. Approved project specifications, manufacturer instructions and the airport's own procedures take precedence, as stated in the precedence note above.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about AGL Planning & Design SOPs in the AGL SOP Library.

Q1What is Division 03 — AGL Planning & Design SOPs?

Division 03 is a set of 28 engineering procedures (SOP-51 to SOP-78, Rev 0) covering work before physical installation. It includes AGL system planning for runway, taxiway, approach, PAPI and signage; circuit architecture; CCR and equipment selection; load and voltage calculations; layout drawings; cable and equipment schedules; BOQ preparation; submittal and compliance review; as-built verification and design change control.

Q2Which standards should design decisions be verified against?

These SOPs are an engineering framework. Final design criteria, photometric requirements, spacing, tolerances, equipment characteristics and installation details must be verified against the current applicable ICAO, FAA, DGCA India and Indian Standards requirements, airport standards, project specifications and the manufacturer's approved data.

Q3What is the master AGL design workflow?

It runs from project requirements, site and existing data, and the design basis through AGL system planning, circuit architecture, CCR and equipment selection, load calculation and circuit verification. Then come layout drawings, schedules, BOQ, technical submittal review and design compliance review, approval for construction, site verification, as-built verification and final documentation.

Q4What does an AGL load calculation include?

SOP-66 considers fixture and transformer quantity and rating, fixture and auxiliary load, cable losses, maximum circuit load and design margin. The result is compared against CCR, transformer, cable and distribution capacity, protection settings and future expansion, and all calculations must be traceable to approved equipment data.

Q5How should voltage drop be verified?

Voltage performance is verified for the complete circuit, not only individual cable sections (SOP-67). The calculation considers cable length and impedance, circuit current, and conductor, transformer and fixture characteristics, reviews the worst-case operating condition and is documented.

Q6What traceability does the design require?

Every major BOQ item should trace to approved drawings and specifications, and every AGL circuit should trace from CCR to cable to transformer to fixture or sign to control and monitoring. The installed system must trace back to the approved design, with authorized deviations documented.

Q7How are design changes controlled?

SOP-78 requires identifying the change with its reason and originator, assessing its impact on safety, electrical design, photometry, operations, civil works, maintenance, cost, programme and documentation, and obtaining approval from the designated technical authority. Affected drawings, BOQ and documents are then updated.

Q8What is verified in as-built drawings?

SOP-77 compares approved design with the installed system: fixture, transformer, pit, CCR and sign locations, cable routes and recorded lengths, circuit and equipment IDs, control interfaces, modifications and deviations. Deviations are physically verified, drawings and schedules updated, and engineering review completed before controlled as-built documents are issued.
