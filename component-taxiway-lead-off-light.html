Taxiway Lead-off Light

A specialized taxiway guidance light that provides directional guidance to pilots transitioning from the runway onto the taxiway after landing. Taxiway lead-off lights are typically installed in pairs on either side of the taxiway centreline at the exit point from the runway, forming a visual funnel that guides the pilot safely off the runway and onto the correct taxiway route.

Overview

What Is It?

A Taxiway Lead-off Light is a specialized taxiway guidance light that provides directional guidance to pilots transitioning from the runway onto the taxiway after landing. These lights are typically installed in pairs on either side of the taxiway centreline at the exit point from the runway, forming a visual funnel that guides the pilot safely off the runway and onto the correct taxiway route. Lead-off lights are essential for ensuring efficient runway occupancy and safe aircraft movement during the post-landing phase.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

Lead-off lights exist for the specific moment right after touchdown when a pilot's priority shifts from decelerating on the runway to committing to the correct exit — a transition that has to happen quickly, often at speed, and without ambiguity about which direction the taxiway curves away from the runway. The paired, funnel-shaped arrangement gives an instant, unmistakable visual cue for the turn direction before the pilot's eyes have even fully registered the taxiway centreline itself.

Getting this transition right is also an airfield capacity issue: an aircraft that lingers on the runway while the crew works out where the exit goes delays the next arrival or departure behind it. By making the direction of travel obvious from the moment the aircraft reaches the exit point, lead-off lights help keep runway occupancy time short and traffic flowing safely behind the exiting aircraft.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Taxiway lead-off lights are installed at runway exits where taxiways connect to the runway. They are used on:

  • Precision approach runways (CAT II and III) where efficient runway exit is critical
  • Busy airports with high traffic volumes requiring rapid runway clearance
  • Rapid exit taxiways where high-speed exits are required
  • Complex taxiway layouts where accurate guidance is essential
  • Low visibility operations where visual guidance is critical
Placement

Installation & Location

Fixtures are set out along the curved exit path from the runway centreline to the parallel taxiway centreline, using the standard inset base can recessed into the pavement at each survey point through the turn, with spacing following the exit curve's geometry rather than a fixed interval. Because these fixtures sit within the runway strip and exit curve where colour must change progressively from runway yellow to taxiway green, they are wired on the exit's own lighting circuit (kept separate from the parent runway centreline circuit) so the colour transition can be switched independently of the runway lights; cabling runs in duct beneath the pavement back to the isolating transformers and exit lighting controller, with setting-out tied to the runway exit's geometric design survey.

Function

How Does It Work?

Taxiway lead-off lights operate as part of the taxiway and runway guidance system:

  • Installation: Lead-off lights are installed in pairs on either side of the taxiway centreline at the runway exit point, typically at 15-30 metre intervals from the runway edge to the taxiway.
  • Lighting: The lights are typically green or yellow, providing directional guidance to the pilot. The lights may be steady burning or flashing, depending on the application.
  • Guidance: The visual funnel created by the paired lights guides the pilot to safely exit the runway and enter the taxiway.
  • Integration: Lead-off lights are integrated with taxiway centreline lights, edge lights, and other taxiway guidance systems.
Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

Taxiway lead-off lights are wired into the same type of constant current series circuit as the runway lighting they connect to and, like runway edge lights, are not individually addressable. The tower or an automated visibility routine — at advanced aerodromes, an A-SMGCS route-lighting controller deciding which taxiway segment should be lit for a given routing — issues the on/off or brightness-step command for the exit circuit. This command reaches the CCR, which regulates the entire loop to a single fixed current; every fixture on that loop, including both members of a lead-off pair, draws through its own isolating transformer and therefore switches and dims in lockstep with the rest of the circuit. Because the funnel effect only works when the paired fixtures stay matched, the control system catches an outright lamp failure — a voltage rise at constant current, reported as a loop fault — but has no way of detecting a fixture that has merely drifted out of alignment: it still draws normal current and produces no fault at all, so misalignment (see Field Notes) must be caught by physical inspection rather than the control system.

Tower / A-SMGCS → CCR → Exit Circuit Loop → Isolating Transformer → Lead-off Pair (with loop-level feedback)

Construction

Physical Construction

The fixture body is a die-cast aluminium alloy housing, machined and anodised or powder-coated for corrosion resistance against runway de-icing fluids, fuel spillage, and UV exposure. A toughened prismatic lens, tinted green or yellow to match the fixture's assigned colour, caps the optical assembly and is gasket-sealed to the housing with an O-ring or silicone seal to achieve an IP67/IP68 rating against water and dust ingress. Flush-mounted units seat into an L-852 base can recessed into the pavement, with the top surface load-rated for aircraft wheel loading; elevated units instead bolt to a frangible coupling engineered to shear cleanly under aircraft impact loads, seating on an L-861 base can set flush with the surrounding grade. The optics are oriented to project the visual funnel back toward the exiting aircraft, guiding it away from the runway centreline. Internal wiring terminates at L-823 primary connectors, sealed splice connectors rated for direct burial and continuous immersion, which mate to the secondary leads of the isolating transformer housed in its own base can nearby. Stainless-steel fasteners are used throughout to resist corrosion, and the base can assembly includes a drainage provision to prevent water pooling around the transformer and cable entry points.

Civil

Excavate and compact the sub-grade to design depth before setting the L-852 (inset) or L-861 (elevated) base can, holding the can top flush and level with the surrounding pavement or grade.

Civil

Set out fixture pairs along the exit curve from the runway exit's geometric design survey, following the curve's own geometry rather than a fixed interval, at the design 15–30 m spacing.

Drainage Provision

Provide a weep hole or drainage path at the base can to prevent water pooling around the transformer and cable entry.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Lens Contamination

Lead-off lights are exposed to rubber deposits from aircraft tyres and de-icing fluids. Regular cleaning with approved solvents and soft brushes is required to maintain light output and visibility.

Misalignment

Lead-off lights can become misaligned due to pavement settlement or impact damage, altering the visual funnel and reducing guidance effectiveness. Regular alignment checks using a digital level or specialized alignment jig are recommended.

LED Driver Failure

LED drivers in lead-off lights can fail due to thermal stress or power surges. Regular inspection and replacement of faulty drivers is essential to maintain system reliability.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Single Fixture Dark, Rest of Circuit Normal
Likely Cause
Failed LED driver or LED array, or an open isolating transformer (blown primary fuse-wire link or failed secondary winding).
Diagnostic Steps
Confirm series loop continuity is unbroken through the fixture's transformer primary; megger the transformer; check secondary voltage at the connector with the CCR energised.
Corrective Action
Replace the LED driver/array if the transformer checks healthy; otherwise swap the isolating transformer and re-seal the L-823 connectors before backfill.
Entire Series Circuit Dark / CCR Will Not Regulate
Likely Cause
Open-circuit fault in the series loop (severed cable, corroded L-823 splice, or a failed transformer acting as an open point).
Diagnostic Steps
Check the CCR fault/open-circuit indication first; loop-resistance test from the CCR output to isolate the faulted segment; sectionalise and megger cable runs to narrow down the break.
Corrective Action
Excavate and repair or replace the faulted cable section and connector; re-test insulation resistance before reinstating the circuit.
CCR Trips on Overcurrent or Ground Fault
Likely Cause
Moisture ingress at a fixture or connector has degraded insulation resistance, creating a short or a partial ground fault on the series loop.
Diagnostic Steps
Megger the full circuit to identify the low-resistance segment; visually inspect suspect fixtures for cracked gaskets, condensation, or damaged secondary cable jackets.
Corrective Action
Dry out or replace the affected fixture/connector, renew O-rings and gaskets, and confirm insulation resistance is back above the acceptable minimum before re-energising.
Reduced or Uneven Light Output
Likely Cause
Rubber deposits, tyre debris, or de-icing fluid residue built up on the lens is blocking or scattering light output.
Diagnostic Steps
Visually inspect the lens for contamination; run a photometric intensity check against ICAO/FAA thresholds to quantify the loss.
Corrective Action
Clean the lens with approved solvents and a soft brush; re-verify intensity with a follow-up photometric reading.
Guidance Funnel Misaligned
Likely Cause
Pavement settlement or impact damage has shifted the fixture's aim off the designed visual funnel toward the exit path.
Diagnostic Steps
Check fixture aim against the design angle using a digital level or alignment jig; inspect the flush-mounted frame and surrounding pavement for settlement or cracking.
Corrective Action
Re-align the fixture to the specified funnel angle and address any frame or subgrade settlement to prevent recurrence.
Fixture Flickering or Intermittent
Likely Cause
Loose or corroded primary/secondary crimp connections, or an LED driver cycling under thermal stress.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect and re-torque all cable connections at the fixture base can; check for corrosion on crimps; monitor driver output and temperature once the CCR has warmed up.
Corrective Action
Re-terminate corroded connections with new crimps and sealant, or replace the LED driver if thermal cycling is confirmed under load.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

LED Module / Lamp Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Solder-joint fatigue from repeated thermal cycling, moisture ingress through a compromised LED board seal, or ordinary end-of-life lumen depreciation.
Effect
Single fixture goes dark or drops below minimum intensity while the rest of the series circuit continues to operate normally, weakening the visual guidance funnel toward the exit.
Detection Method
Nighttime visual patrol, periodic photometric intensity survey, and continuity/secondary-voltage checks at the fixture connector.
Isolating Transformer Winding Failure
High Severity
Root Cause
Moisture ingress leading to insulation breakdown, a manufacturing defect in the winding, or a blown primary fuse-wire link from a transient surge.
Effect
Open-circuit point in the series loop; can darken the single fixture or, if the open extends to the loop itself, take down the entire circuit.
Detection Method
Megger test of the transformer, secondary voltage check with the CCR energised, and loop-resistance testing from the CCR output.
L-823 Connector / Splice Degradation
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Improper seating or crimping during installation, gasket failure allowing groundwater ingress, or corrosion building up on the pins over time.
Effect
Intermittent operation, flickering, or a partial ground fault that degrades circuit insulation resistance.
Detection Method
Insulation resistance (megger) testing of the circuit and visual inspection of connectors at the fixture base can during maintenance.
Series Cable Insulation Breakdown
High Severity
Root Cause
Cable jacket damage from nearby excavation or rodent activity, age-related insulation degradation, or prolonged standing water in the duct run.
Effect
Ground fault or short circuit that trips the CCR or darkens the entire series loop.
Detection Method
Full-circuit megger test to flag the low-resistance segment, followed by sectionalised testing or time-domain reflectometry to pinpoint the fault location.
Flush-Mount Housing Seal Failure
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Gasket compression set from repeated aircraft and vehicle wheel loading, cracked top housing from impact, or sealant failure at the frame-to-pavement joint.
Effect
Water tracks into the base can, accelerating connector corrosion and insulation breakdown, and can lead to a cracked lens under continued wheel loading.
Detection Method
Visual inspection for cracked housings or perimeter gaps, and insulation resistance testing to catch moisture ingress before it darkens the fixture.
Lens Contamination / Optical Degradation
Low Severity
Root Cause
Rubber deposits and tyre debris from turning traffic, de-icing fluid residue, and prolonged UV exposure on the outer lens.
Effect
Reduced or unevenly distributed light output that blurs the bidirectional colour cue guiding aircraft off the runway.
Detection Method
Scheduled photometric intensity testing and visual inspection of the lens during routine cleaning.
CCR Regulation Fault
High Severity
Root Cause
Internal component failure (control board or output stage), a load mismatch after circuit modification, or a firmware/setpoint fault.
Effect
Incorrect brightness step, failure to hold regulated current across the loop, or nuisance tripping on healthy circuits.
Detection Method
CCR self-diagnostic/fault indication, output current verification, and a load-bank test to confirm regulation across all intensity steps.
Base Can / Pavement Settlement
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Poor sub-grade compaction at installation, frost heave, or drainage failure allowing water to pool and erode the surrounding pavement fill.
Effect
Fixture sinks below or rises above the flush pavement tolerance, creating an FOD/wheel-loading hazard and stressing cable connections at the base can.
Detection Method
Visual and level inspection of the fixture against surrounding pavement, and a check of drainage paths during annual maintenance.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Circuit Isolation
Electrical Safety

Lead-off circuits are wired separately from the parent runway or taxiway circuit so the colour transition can switch independently; confirm which circuit is live and lock out the correct CCR before opening a base can or handling connectors.

Runway Strip Work Coordination
Operational

These fixtures sit within the runway strip and exit curve; work here requires ATC coordination and a NOTAM where lights will be dark, with personnel and vehicles kept clear of the runway environment.

Flush Fit & Wheel-Load Tolerance
Mechanical

The inset fixture must sit precisely flush with the pavement through the exit curve; a proud edge or depression becomes a wheel-impact point during high-speed runway exit.

Colour Transition Accuracy
Compliance

Confirm the correct lens colour is fitted at each point along the exit curve (runway yellow through the exit, transitioning to taxiway green) before closing out, since an incorrect transition point misleads the pilot on whether they are still within the runway environment.

Insulation Test Before Re-Energisation
Electrical Safety

After any cable, connector, or transformer work, megger test the affected loop before restoring power, since a residual fault at re-energisation can trip the CCR or darken the exit funnel.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Do not leave a fixture dark, out of position, or with the wrong colour after maintenance; verify flushness, colour, and photometric conformance before closing out and cancelling the associated NOTAM.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Colour Transition Timing Is a Controller Setting, Not Just a Lens Colour
Operational

The progressive change from runway yellow to taxiway green along the exit is switched by the exit lighting controller on its own circuit, so a colour-transition complaint should be checked against controller timing and sequencing first, rather than assumed to be a lens or lamp fault at a single fixture.

Funnel Orientation Is Aim-Critical at Speed
Optical

The paired funnel arrangement has to give an instant, unambiguous turn cue to a pilot still decelerating from landing speed, so even a small optical misalignment at one fixture reads as a confusing or contradictory cue at exactly the moment the pilot has the least time to interpret it. Re-verify aim against the exit's geometric design survey after any base can disturbance, not just a visual on/off check.

Survey Off the Exit Curve Geometry
Civil

Like other curve-following fixture types, lead-off lights are set out from the runway exit's actual geometric design rather than a fixed interval. A resurfacing or exit-geometry change since original design needs a fresh survey tie-in, not a like-for-like reinstatement at the old positions.

Independent Circuit Means Independent Fault Isolation
Electrical

The exit's lighting circuit is kept separate from the parent runway centreline circuit specifically so the colour transition can be switched independently, so a lead-off light fault won't necessarily show up as a runway centreline loop fault and vice versa. Check the exit's own circuit and controller status directly rather than inferring it from the runway circuit's health.

High-Speed, Post-Touchdown Wheel Loading
Mechanical

Fixtures in the exit curve take wheel loading at higher speed and steeper turning angle than a mid-taxiway centreline light, closer in severity to the runway environment than the taxiway they lead to. Weight inspection toward wheel-load fatigue signs accordingly, rather than the lighter-duty assumptions that apply further along the parallel taxiway.

Trend Loop Readings on the Exit-Specific Circuit
Troubleshooting

Because the exit circuit and controller are dedicated to this one function, a slow insulation-resistance drift on it won't be masked by averaging across a larger taxiway-wide circuit the way it might be elsewhere. Trend it on its own rather than letting a healthy reading from the wider system hide a developing fault here.
