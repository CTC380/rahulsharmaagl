Electrical Safety Inspection

Standardized inspection methodology for identifying electrical safety hazards associated with AGL equipment, electrical panels, CCRs, control systems, cables, enclosures, maintenance areas, and portable electrical equipment — covering isolation, LOTO, earthing, bonding, warning signage, and safe-access verification.

Initial Issue

Critical control — This division is an inspection and verification framework only. Actual isolation, switching, LOTO application/removal, electrical testing, and energized-work controls shall be performed only by authorized/competent personnel in accordance with the applicable airport safety procedures, electrical safety rules, and approved risk assessment. A visually healthy CCR, panel, transformer, or AGL circuit must not be assumed electrically safe merely because no external defect is visible.

INS-ESI-001 Equipment-Earthing Inspection
What's Inspected

Inspection of protective earthing connections associated with AGL electrical equipment, including protective-earth conductors, earth terminals, connection points, and applicable bonding to the protective earthing system.

Inspection Points
  • Protective-earth conductor condition (damage, disconnection, corrosion, improper termination)
  • Earth terminals & connection points
  • Equipment earth-bond
  • Earth connection security — loose, damaged, or corroded
  • Earth-test record review, where required
Classify As
Normal Defective Unserviceable Immediate Action Required
INS-ESI-002 Bonding Inspection
What's Inspected

Inspection of equipotential bonding arrangements between metallic components, electrical cabinets, doors, panels, frames, equipment, and associated structures where bonding is applicable.

Inspection Points
  • Cabinet bonding — doors, panels, frames to earth system
  • Equipment-to-structure bonding
  • Bonding conductor condition — damage, corrosion, looseness, disconnection
  • Bonding connection security
Classify As
Normal Defective Unserviceable Immediate Action Required
INS-ESI-003 Electrical-Isolation Inspection
What's Inspected

Verification that applicable electrical isolation arrangements, isolation points, isolation devices, and isolation procedures/instructions are correctly identified, in good condition, and available to authorized personnel.

Inspection Points
  • Isolation-point identification
  • Isolation device condition — switches, breakers, isolators, disconnectors
  • Isolation accessibility to authorized personnel
  • Isolation status identification — clearly identifiable where required
  • Isolation procedure/instruction availability
Scope Boundary

This is a verification check only. Actual isolation, switching, and de-energization shall be carried out solely by authorized/competent personnel under an approved isolation procedure and risk assessment.

Classify As
Normal Defective Unserviceable Immediate Action Required
INS-ESI-004 Warning-Label Inspection
What's Inspected

Inspection of electrical hazard labels, warning signs, high-voltage warnings, and equipment/circuit identification labels for presence, legibility, visibility, and correspondence with the actual equipment or circuit.

Inspection Points
  • Electrical hazard sign presence
  • High-voltage/high-energy warnings
  • Equipment identification labels
  • Circuit identification labels — breakers, isolators, equipment
  • Label legibility — faded, damaged, missing
  • Label accuracy — correspondence with actual equipment/circuit
Classify As
Normal Defective Unserviceable Immediate Action Required
INS-ESI-005 LOTO-Device Inspection
What's Inspected

Inspection of lockout/tagout locks, tags, hasps, and associated lockout devices for correct identification, physical condition, availability, and storage arrangements.

Inspection Points
  • LOTO identification — equipment, isolation point, responsible person
  • LOTO device condition — damaged, missing, illegible, unsuitable
  • LOTO availability at designated locations
  • LOTO storage arrangements — accessibility, organization, completeness
  • LOTO procedure/instruction availability & currency
  • LOTO application-status — correspondence with the authorized isolation in progress
Scope Boundary

Application or removal of LOTO devices shall be performed only by authorized/competent personnel under an approved isolation procedure. This check verifies device condition and correspondence, not the isolation itself.

Classify As
Normal Defective Unserviceable Immediate Action Required

What's Inspected

Inspection of electrical cable protection against mechanical, environmental, and electrical hazards, including cable glands, entries, seals, routing, support, and temporary cable installations.

Inspection Points
  • Cable gland/entry seals & strain-relief
  • Cable routing safety
  • Cable abrasion/damage — sharp edges, movement, heat, chemicals
  • Cable support & mechanical loading
  • Temporary cable installations — routing, protection, identification, suitability
Classify As
Normal Defective Unserviceable Immediate Action Required
INS-ESI-007 Panel-Access Inspection
What's Inspected

Verification that electrical panels can be safely accessed by authorized personnel and that unauthorized access is appropriately prevented, together with the physical, electrical, mechanical, and identification condition of the panel.

Inspection Points
  • Panel condition — physical, electrical, mechanical, environmental
  • Panel doors, hinges, locks, handles
  • Panel covers, barriers, blanking plates
  • Access control — unauthorized access prevention
  • Internal barriers/protection where accessible for authorized inspection
  • Panel identification & circuit designation
  • Panel damage — cracks, deformation, corrosion, overheating
Classify As
Normal Defective Unserviceable Immediate Action Required
INS-ESI-008 Electrical-Enclosure Inspection
What's Inspected

Inspection of electrical equipment enclosures for integrity and protection, including doors, seals, gaskets, and evidence of water ingress, contamination, or corrosion.

Inspection Points
  • Enclosure door/seal & gasket condition
  • Water-ingress evidence — penetration, moisture accumulation
  • Dust/contamination — dirt, oil, chemicals, insects
  • Corrosion & deterioration
  • Mechanical integrity — cracks, deformation, impact damage
  • Exposed conductors, terminals, or missing covers within the enclosure

Verification of required working and access clearances around electrical equipment, including panel working space, separation from other hazards, and emergency access routes.

Inspection Points
  • Panel working-space in front of panels/equipment
  • Equipment access clearance — not obstructed
  • Separation from water, fuel, combustible materials, mechanical equipment
  • Emergency access clearance — routes remain clear

INS-ESI-011 Electrical-Room Safety Inspection
What's Inspected

Inspection of the overall condition of electrical rooms and equipment areas, including access control, housekeeping, water ingress, ventilation, lighting, emergency equipment, and signage.

Inspection Points
  • Electrical-room access control
  • Housekeeping — unnecessary materials, waste, combustible items
  • Water ingress — leaks, flooding, condensation
  • Ventilation — unobstructed and suitable
  • Lighting — adequate for safe access and inspection
  • Emergency equipment & emergency access arrangements
  • Warning, identification, emergency, and access-control signage

Normal Defective Unserviceable Immediate Action Required
INS-ESI-012 Portable-Electrical-Equipment Inspection
What's Inspected

Inspection of portable electrical tools and equipment used for AGL maintenance, including power cords, plugs, earthing, enclosures, test/inspection records, and storage arrangements.

Inspection Points
  • Power cord/cable condition — cuts, exposed conductors, abrasion
  • Plugs, pins, housings & strain-relief
  • Applicable protective-earthing arrangements
  • Equipment housing/enclosure condition
  • Inspection, test, or calibration record verification
  • Damage, unauthorized modification, or unsafe condition
  • Storage — cable damage, moisture exposure, unauthorized use prevention

Division-Wide Requirements

Common classification scheme, workflow, records, and safety boundaries that apply across all 12 Division 11 inspection topics.

A. Standard Inspection Classification

Every observation should be assigned an appropriate status:

NormalEquipment/condition satisfactory and operating as expected.

DefectiveAbnormality or defect identified but equipment remains operational or condition does not require immediate withdrawal.

UnserviceableEquipment or system is not available for its intended function.

Immediate Action RequiredCondition presents an electrical, safety, or operational risk requiring immediate escalation to authorized personnel.

B. Typical Inspection Workflow

The following sequence applies across all Division 11 topics:

IDENTIFY EQUIPMENT IDENTIFY HAZARDS VERIFY ISOLATION VERIFY LOTO CHECK EARTHING/BONDING INSPECT PANELS/ENCLOSURES CLASSIFY RECORD ESCALATE CORRECT VERIFY CLOSE

C. Minimum Electrical Safety Inspection Record

Each inspection record should include:

  • Date and inspection time
  • Inspector name and designation
  • Equipment/system inspected
  • Equipment identification/location
  • Isolation/LOTO status at time of inspection
  • Inspection observation
  • Status classification
  • Defect description, where applicable
  • Immediate action taken
  • Person/department notified
  • Corrective-action reference
  • Closure date/time
  • Inspector's verification/sign-off

D. Scope Boundary

Electrical safety inspection is intended to

  • Identify visible electrical hazards, unsafe conditions, and inadequate protective measures at the earliest possible stage.

It does not replace

  • Isolation, switching, or de-energization by authorized personnel
  • LOTO application/removal
  • Electrical testing or earth-resistance testing
  • Insulation-resistance testing
  • Energized-work risk assessment and controls
  • Detailed engineering assessment

E. Key Distinctions to Verify at Every Inspection

A visually healthy CCR, panel, transformer, or AGL circuit must not be assumed electrically safe merely because no external defect is visible. Every inspection should distinguish between:

Equipment condition Electrical isolation status Protective measures Access/control arrangements Work-area safety PPE availability & condition

Any condition involving exposed energized parts, unidentified isolation points, damaged electrical insulation, compromised earthing/bonding, unsafe access, water ingress into electrical equipment, damaged LOTO arrangements, or inadequate electrical clearances should be treated according to the applicable airport electrical-safety procedure and escalated to authorized personnel.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about electrical safety inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook.

Q1What is Division 11 — Electrical Safety Inspection in the AGL Inspection Handbook?

Division 11 contains 12 published topics (Level 2, safety-critical and scheduled). It gives a standardized methodology for identifying electrical safety hazards associated with AGL equipment, electrical panels, CCRs, control systems, cables, enclosures, maintenance areas and portable electrical equipment.

Q2What does the electrical safety inspection cover?

It covers isolation, LOTO, earthing, bonding, warning signage and safe-access verification.

Q3What is the typical electrical safety inspection workflow?

The sequence identifies the equipment and hazards, verifies isolation and LOTO, checks earthing and bonding, and inspects panels and enclosures. It then classifies, records, escalates, corrects, verifies and closes.

Q4Can equipment be assumed electrically safe if no defect is visible?

No. The division states that a visually healthy CCR, panel, transformer or AGL circuit must not be assumed electrically safe merely because no external defect is visible.

Q5What status classifications are used in this division?

Every observation is assigned Normal, Defective, Unserviceable or Immediate Action Required. Immediate Action Required means the condition presents an electrical, safety or operational risk requiring immediate escalation to authorized personnel.

Q6Does the electrical safety inspection replace isolation, LOTO or testing?

No. It does not replace isolation, switching or de-energization by authorized personnel, LOTO application or removal, electrical or earth-resistance testing, insulation-resistance testing, energized-work risk assessment and controls, or detailed engineering assessment.

Q7What should an electrical safety inspection record include?

Alongside the usual date, inspector, observation, status, action and sign-off fields, the record includes the equipment inspected, its identification and location, and the isolation and LOTO status at the time of inspection.

Q8Do airport procedures take precedence over this handbook?

Yes. This handbook is an uncontrolled educational reference, not an approved procedure. The airport's approved inspection schedule, SMS procedures, ATC instructions, NOTAM procedures and local aerodrome regulations always take precedence, and the current revision of any official document should be verified before use.

Q9Can I download a printable checklist for each inspection topic?

Yes. Each inspection topic has a Download as Word option that produces an uncontrolled Word checklist template (description, a tick-box inspection table and an inspector sign-off block) for personal reference and internal training, to be reviewed and adopted under the user’s own document control.
