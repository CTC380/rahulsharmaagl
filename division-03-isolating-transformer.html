IT-121 Transformer Open Circuit

Symptom — A fixture on the series circuit is completely dark and initial testing shows no continuity through the isolating transformer as a whole — neither winding is confirmed conducting yet, and it is not yet established whether the fault lies in the primary or the secondary.

Equipment / System

Series-circuit isolating transformer (encapsulated/epoxy-potted, single-lamp type), typically 1:1 ratio, 6.6A primary, various VA ratings (30/45/65/100W), installed in an L-867 light base or below-grade pit.

Possible Causes
HighPrimary winding open-circuited internally
HighSecondary winding open-circuited internally
MediumInternal lead/connection broken at the potting compound interface, common after thermal cycling or a manufacturing defect
MediumPrimary or secondary L-823 connector failed open — corroded pins, a cracked connector body, or a pulled-apart crimp
LowerLightning or switching surge damage causing an internal open fault
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meter (loop-rated)Isolating transformer bench test setInsulation resistance tester (5kV)MultimeterL-823 connector test leadsFixture wiring diagram
Safety Precautions
  • Never open or disconnect an isolating transformer while the CCR is running — isolate the CCR and apply LOTO first, since the series loop can present hazardous induced voltage at any break point.
  • Follow airside work permit and NOTAM procedures before accessing runway/taxiway light bases.
  • Assume the pit/light base may contain standing water — inspect before kneeling or placing tools inside.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the CCR is delivering rated loop current at the commanded step (per CCR-021/CCR-031 in Division 01) before concluding the fault is local to this transformer rather than a CCR-side issue.
  2. MeasurementWith the CCR isolated and LOTO applied, disconnect the transformer's primary and secondary L-823 connectors and bench-test the unit using an isolating transformer test set.
  3. MeasurementCheck primary winding continuity/resistance against the manufacturer's nameplate value.
  4. MeasurementCheck secondary winding continuity/resistance against the manufacturer's nameplate value.
  5. DecisionPrimary winding open → proceed to IT-122 for primary-specific diagnosis, noting the fail-safe shorting device implication for loop continuity. Secondary winding open → proceed to IT-123. Both windings show continuity on the bench but the fixture was still dark in the field → suspect a failed L-823 connector rather than the transformer itself.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the primary winding, the secondary winding, or an external connector at the transformer's plug interface.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific open point through the winding-specific diagnostic procedure (IT-122 or IT-123).
Test Points
Primary winding resistance (bench test)
Secondary winding resistance (bench test)
Primary L-823 connector pins
Secondary L-823 connector pins
Root Cause Verification

After replacement or repair, bench-test the transformer to confirm both windings show correct resistance and turns ratio, then re-install and confirm the fixture illuminates correctly at every commanded intensity step.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the transformer if either winding is confirmed open.
  • Repair or replace a failed L-823 connector if the windings themselves test healthy.
  • Re-terminate a broken internal lead only if the manufacturer's repair procedure permits field repair of a potted unit — otherwise replace.
Preventive Action
  • Bench-test spare isolating transformers before storage so failures are caught before installation, not after.
  • Include a periodic sample bench-test programme for transformers pulled during other pit works.
  • Track transformer replacement history by circuit to identify recurring failure locations.

IT-122 Transformer Primary Open Circuit

Symptom — The isolating transformer's primary winding is confirmed open. Because the primary sits in series within the loop, the field presentation depends entirely on whether the transformer's internal fail-safe shorting device operated: if it did, only this one fixture goes dark while the rest of the circuit runs normally; if it did not, the entire series circuit loses continuity and the CCR raises an open-circuit/no-load alarm.

Equipment / System

Isolating transformer primary winding and its internal fail-safe shorting device (where fitted) — a component specifically designed to short the primary on winding failure so a single transformer fault does not take down the whole series circuit.

Possible Causes
HighPrimary winding failed open due to age, thermal stress, or an internal manufacturing defect, with the fail-safe shorting device operating correctly — only this fixture affected
HighPrimary winding failed open and the fail-safe shorting device also failed to operate — the entire series circuit loop is open, escalate immediately per CCR-021/CCR-030 in Division 01
MediumPrimary-side L-823 connector corroded or pulled apart, presenting as an open primary without an actual winding fault
LowerSurge/lightning damage to the primary winding and, in some cases, to the fail-safe device itself
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterIsolating transformer bench test setMultimeterInsulation resistance tester (5kV)L-823 connector test leads
Safety Precautions
  • If the entire circuit is open (fail-safe did not operate), treat the loop as carrying an elevated open-circuit voltage at the break point — apply LOTO on the CCR before touching any part of the loop.
  • Do not assume a single dark fixture with the rest of the circuit healthy is automatically safe to handle — verify zero energy at the specific transformer before disconnecting it.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationDetermine which scenario applies: is only this one fixture dark with the rest of the circuit operating normally, or has the CCR raised an open-circuit/no-load alarm for the whole loop?
  2. DecisionWhole-loop open-circuit alarm present → the fail-safe device did not operate; this is now a full-circuit fault, proceed per CCR-021/CCR-030 (Division 01) to locate the break, which will be at this transformer's primary.
  3. MeasurementIf only the single fixture is affected, with the CCR isolated and LOTO applied, disconnect the transformer and bench-test the primary winding and fail-safe device continuity.
  4. MeasurementCheck the primary-side L-823 connector for corrosion, looseness, or physical damage as an alternative to a true winding fault.
  5. DecisionPrimary winding confirmed open with the fail-safe correctly shorted → replace the transformer as a routine single-fixture repair. Connector fault found with the winding itself healthy → repair/replace the connector only.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the primary winding and fail-safe device, or to the primary-side connector.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific failure by bench test before returning the transformer to service or scrapping it.
Test Points
Primary winding resistance
Fail-safe shorting device continuity/operation
Primary-side L-823 connector
Root Cause Verification

After replacement, confirm loop continuity and rated current at the CCR, then re-energize and confirm the repaired fixture illuminates correctly alongside the rest of the circuit.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the transformer if the primary winding is confirmed open.
  • Repair or replace the primary-side L-823 connector if the winding tests healthy.
  • Escalate to a full loop continuity investigation per CCR-021/CCR-030 if the fail-safe device did not operate.
Preventive Action
  • Verify fail-safe device function as part of the bench-test acceptance criteria for spare/replacement transformers.
  • Track which circuits have experienced a fail-safe non-operation event, since it indicates the device population on that circuit may be ageing together.
  • Include primary winding resistance trending in periodic sample testing.

IT-123 Transformer Secondary Open Circuit

Symptom — The fixture served by this isolating transformer is dark, but the CCR shows normal loop current and no open-circuit alarm — the primary loop is intact, and the fault is isolated to the secondary winding or the secondary-side connection to the lamp/LED driver.

Equipment / System

Isolating transformer secondary winding and its output connector/lead to the fixture.

Possible Causes
HighSecondary winding open-circuited internally
HighSecondary-side L-823 connector corroded, loose, or pulled apart at the lamp base connection
MediumSecondary lead broken at the potting compound interface
LowerSecondary-side surge protection device (where fitted) failed in an open-circuit mode
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterIsolating transformer bench test setMultimeterL-823 connector test leadsFixture wiring diagram
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO on the CCR before disconnecting the transformer, even though the primary loop is confirmed healthy, since the secondary can still carry a hazardous voltage while the loop is energized.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the CCR shows normal loop current and no open-circuit alarm, isolating the fault to this transformer's secondary side or the fixture itself rather than the loop.
  2. MeasurementWith the CCR isolated and LOTO applied, disconnect the transformer's secondary connector and bench-test secondary winding continuity.
  3. MeasurementInspect the secondary-side L-823 connector and lamp base connection for corrosion or looseness.
  4. DecisionSecondary winding confirmed open on the bench → replace the transformer. Winding tests healthy but connector/lead is damaged → repair or replace the connector/lead only, redirect to CCR-031 (Division 01) if the fixture itself (lamp/LED driver) is then found to be the actual fault.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the secondary winding, the secondary connector, or the fixture's own wiring.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the specific open point by bench test and connector inspection.
Test Points
Secondary winding resistance
Secondary L-823 connector pins
Secondary lead at potting interface
Root Cause Verification

After replacement or repair, bench-test the transformer to confirm secondary winding continuity, then re-install and confirm the fixture illuminates correctly at every commanded intensity step.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the transformer if the secondary winding is confirmed open.
  • Repair or replace the secondary-side connector or lead.
  • Replace a failed secondary surge protection device if fitted and confirmed as the open point.
Preventive Action
  • Include secondary winding resistance in the bench-test acceptance criteria for spares.
  • Inspect secondary connectors for corrosion whenever a fixture is opened for lamp/LED driver replacement.
  • Track fixture-level dark-lamp reports by transformer age to spot a batch nearing end of life.

IT-124 Low Secondary Voltage

Symptom — With rated primary current confirmed flowing through the transformer, the secondary output voltage measured at the fixture connector reads below the expected value for the unit's rated transformation ratio — the lamp or LED fixture runs dim.

Equipment / System

Isolating transformer winding and core, secondary connector/lead to the fixture.

Possible Causes
HighInternal winding degradation increasing effective winding resistance or reducing effective coupling, reducing secondary output for a given primary current
HighCore degradation (lamination damage, partial saturation) reducing magnetic coupling efficiency between windings
MediumSecondary connector or lead has a high-resistance connection, dropping voltage before it reaches the fixture even though the transformer itself is healthy
MediumAn incorrect transformer (wrong VA rating or ratio) was installed at a previous replacement, undersized for the fixture's actual load
LowerBench test measurement error — voltage measured under a load current that does not match the actual field primary current
Required Tools
Isolating transformer bench test set (with turns-ratio function)True-RMS clamp meterMultimeterFixture wiring diagram / transformer schedule
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO on the CCR before disconnecting the transformer for bench testing.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. MeasurementBench-test the transformer at its rated primary current and measure secondary output voltage against the nameplate value.
  2. MeasurementPerform a turns-ratio test to compare the actual primary:secondary ratio against the nameplate ratio.
  3. ObservationVerify the installed transformer's part number/rating against the design transformer schedule for this fixture type.
  4. MeasurementWith the transformer confirmed healthy, check the secondary connector and lead for a high-resistance connection dropping voltage under load.
  5. DecisionRatio test confirms a genuine deviation from nameplate → internal winding/core degradation, replace the transformer. Ratio and bench voltage both correct but field voltage at the fixture is still low → connector/lead fault, not the transformer. Wrong-rating transformer confirmed installed → correct with the design-specified unit.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the transformer's internal winding/core, the secondary connector/lead, or an incorrect unit having been installed.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause through bench ratio testing and physical rating verification.
Test Points
Secondary output voltage at rated primary current (bench)
Turns ratio (bench)
Secondary connector/lead voltage drop under load
Installed unit's nameplate rating
Root Cause Verification

After correction, bench-test the transformer (or its replacement) to confirm secondary voltage and turns ratio match nameplate values, then re-install and confirm correct fixture brightness in the field.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the transformer if winding or core degradation is confirmed.
  • Repair or replace the secondary connector/lead.
  • Install the design-specified transformer rating if a wrong unit is found.
Preventive Action
  • Verify transformer part number against the design transformer schedule whenever a unit is replaced.
  • Include a turns-ratio test in periodic sample testing, not just a resistance check.
  • Maintain an accurate, current transformer schedule by fixture type and circuit.

IT-125 High Secondary Voltage

Symptom — With rated primary current confirmed flowing through the transformer, the secondary output voltage reads above the expected value for the unit's rated transformation ratio, risking over-driving the lamp filament or LED driver and causing premature fixture failure.

Equipment / System

Isolating transformer winding and core, secondary load condition.

Possible Causes
HighAn incorrect transformer (wrong ratio, intended for a different fixture type or wattage) was installed at a previous replacement
HighInternal winding fault (partial inter-turn short on the primary side) effectively reducing primary turns and raising the ratio, elevating secondary voltage for the same primary current
MediumSecondary circuit lightly loaded or partially disconnected, with the reading taken close to an unloaded/no-load condition rather than true rated load — a measurement artefact rather than a genuine fault
LowerBench test performed at an incorrect primary current, not matching the actual field loop current
Required Tools
Isolating transformer bench test set (with turns-ratio function)True-RMS clamp meterMultimeterFixture wiring diagram / transformer schedule
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO on the CCR before disconnecting the transformer for bench testing.
  • Do not leave a confirmed over-voltage condition connected to an LED driver or lamp longer than necessary — it accelerates fixture degradation.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. MeasurementBench-test the transformer at its correct rated primary current, with the secondary correctly loaded to the fixture's rated load, and measure secondary voltage against nameplate.
  2. MeasurementPerform a turns-ratio test to compare the actual ratio against nameplate.
  3. ObservationVerify the installed transformer's part number/rating against the design transformer schedule.
  4. DecisionRatio confirmed deviated from nameplate under correctly loaded test conditions → internal winding fault, replace the transformer. Ratio and bench voltage correct once tested under proper load → the original field reading was a measurement artefact from an unloaded/lightly loaded condition, not a genuine fault. Wrong-rating transformer confirmed installed → correct with the design-specified unit.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the transformer's internal winding, an incorrect installed unit, or a measurement condition issue.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause through a correctly loaded bench ratio test.
Test Points
Secondary output voltage under correct rated load (bench)
Turns ratio (bench)
Installed unit's nameplate rating
Root Cause Verification

After correction, bench-test under correct rated load to confirm secondary voltage and turns ratio match nameplate values, then re-install and monitor the fixture for correct brightness with no signs of over-drive.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the transformer if a genuine winding fault is confirmed under a correctly loaded test.
  • Install the design-specified transformer rating if a wrong unit is found.
  • Correct the bench test procedure/loading if the original reading is confirmed to be a measurement artefact.
Preventive Action
  • Always bench-test isolating transformers under correct rated load, not open-circuit, to avoid misleading voltage readings.
  • Verify transformer part number against the design schedule whenever a unit is replaced.
  • Include turns-ratio testing in periodic sample testing.

IT-126 Incorrect Transformation Ratio

Symptom — A turns-ratio test on the isolating transformer returns a value that does not match its nameplate-rated primary:secondary ratio, identified during bench testing, acceptance testing of a new/repaired unit, or while investigating a voltage complaint (IT-124/IT-125).

Equipment / System

Isolating transformer winding assembly — turns-ratio relationship between primary and secondary.

Possible Causes
HighInternal winding fault — a partial inter-turn short on either winding altering the effective number of active turns
HighWrong transformer model/part number was supplied or installed, with a genuinely different rated ratio than intended for this fixture type
MediumBench test set calibration error or an incorrect test procedure (wrong excitation voltage/current) producing a false ratio reading
LowerManufacturing defect present since new, not caught at incoming inspection
Required Tools
Isolating transformer bench test set (with turns-ratio function)Calibrated reference transformer or known-good unit for cross-checkMultimeterTransformer schedule / nameplate reference
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO on the CCR before removing any transformer for bench testing.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. MeasurementRepeat the turns-ratio test carefully following the bench test set's specified procedure, to rule out a test error.
  2. MeasurementCross-check the test set itself against a known-good reference transformer with a verified correct ratio.
  3. ObservationConfirm the transformer's nameplate part number and rated ratio against the design transformer schedule for this fixture type.
  4. DecisionTest set confirmed accurate against the reference unit and the deviation persists → genuine winding fault or wrong unit supplied. Test set itself found inaccurate → recalibrate/repair the test set and re-test the transformer before drawing further conclusions.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to the transformer's winding, an incorrect part supplied, or the test equipment.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause and reject or accept the unit accordingly.
Test Points
Turns ratio (bench)
Reference transformer ratio (for test set cross-check)
Transformer nameplate part number
Root Cause Verification

Confirm the turns-ratio test result matches nameplate on a verified-accurate test set before accepting the transformer into service, or confirm the correct-rated replacement unit tests within tolerance.

Corrective Action
  • Reject and replace a transformer confirmed to have a genuine winding fault.
  • Correct a wrong-part-number installation with the design-specified unit.
  • Recalibrate or repair the bench test set if it is confirmed to be the source of the error.
Preventive Action
  • Include turns-ratio testing as a mandatory incoming-inspection step for new and repaired transformers, not just resistance checks.
  • Maintain a calibration schedule for the bench test set itself.
  • Keep the transformer schedule current and clearly cross-referenced to fixture type.

IT-127 High Primary Current

Symptom — During a rated-voltage bench excitation test (with the transformer off the series loop and energized from a dedicated AC test source), the primary draws current noticeably above the normal value for the applied test voltage — indicating the unit is absorbing more power than a healthy transformer would at that excitation level.

Equipment / System

Isolating transformer primary winding and core, as evaluated during off-loop bench excitation testing (not a loop current measurement, since loop current is fixed by the CCR's constant-current regulation).

Possible Causes
HighPartial winding short (inter-turn fault) on the primary or secondary, increasing current draw for a given test voltage
HighCore lamination damage or degraded core steel increasing eddy current/hysteresis losses
MediumMoisture or insulation degradation within the winding creating a partial leakage path that adds to normal excitation current
LowerTest procedure error — excitation voltage applied above the correct bench test value for this transformer's rating
Required Tools
Isolating transformer bench test setTrue-RMS clamp meterInsulation resistance tester (5kV)Manufacturer bench test procedure/reference values
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO on the CCR before removing the transformer for bench testing.
  • Do not exceed the manufacturer's specified bench test excitation voltage — an incorrect over-voltage test can itself cause or worsen a winding fault, confusing the diagnosis.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the exact bench test excitation voltage applied matches the manufacturer's specified value for this transformer's rating.
  2. MeasurementRe-run the excitation test at the correct voltage and record primary current draw.
  3. MeasurementMegger the winding insulation resistance to check for a moisture-related leakage contribution.
  4. MeasurementCross-check the reading against a known-good reference unit of the same rating under identical bench conditions.
  5. DecisionExcitation voltage confirmed correct and current draw remains elevated versus the reference unit → genuine winding/core fault, reject the transformer. Test voltage found incorrect → repeat the test correctly before drawing conclusions. Insulation resistance also found low → treat as a combined winding/insulation fault (see IT-130).
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to a genuine winding/core fault, an insulation-related leakage contribution, or a test procedure error.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause through correctly-conducted, reference-compared bench testing.
Test Points
Primary current at rated bench excitation voltage
Winding insulation resistance
Reference (known-good) unit comparison reading
Root Cause Verification

Confirm any replacement unit draws primary current within the manufacturer's normal range at the correct rated bench excitation voltage before returning it to service.

Corrective Action
  • Reject and replace a transformer confirmed to have a genuine winding or core fault.
  • Correct the bench test procedure/voltage if a test error was the actual cause.
  • Address insulation degradation per IT-130/IT-131 if that is found to be the contributing factor.
Preventive Action
  • Standardise and document the correct bench excitation voltage for every transformer rating used on site.
  • Keep a known-good reference unit of each common rating available for comparison testing.
  • Include insulation resistance testing alongside every excitation current test as standard practice.

IT-128 Abnormal No-Load Current

Symptom — With the secondary open-circuited (no lamp/LED load connected) and rated voltage applied to the primary during a bench test, the measured magnetizing (no-load) current falls outside the manufacturer's specified acceptance range — either significantly above or, less commonly, implausibly low.

Equipment / System

Isolating transformer core and primary winding, evaluated specifically under the standard no-load magnetizing current acceptance test.

Possible Causes
HighCore lamination damage or degraded core material increasing magnetizing current beyond the normal range
HighPartial winding short reducing effective turns, which increases no-load current for the same applied voltage
MediumTest connection error — secondary not genuinely open-circuited (a partial load or a shorted secondary during the test), invalidating the no-load reading
LowerManufacturer's published acceptance range applied incorrectly for this specific transformer rating
Required Tools
Isolating transformer bench test setTrue-RMS clamp meterManufacturer no-load current acceptance criteria (by rating)
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO on the CCR before removing the transformer for bench testing.
  • Confirm the secondary is genuinely open before applying primary voltage — an inadvertently shorted secondary during a no-load test can damage the transformer or the test set.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the secondary is genuinely open-circuited (verified with a meter, not just visually) before starting the test.
  2. MeasurementApply rated primary voltage and record the no-load current.
  3. ObservationCompare the result against the manufacturer's published no-load current acceptance range for this specific rating, not a generic figure.
  4. DecisionSecondary confirmed genuinely open and the reading still falls outside the acceptance range → genuine core/winding issue, reject the transformer. Secondary found not fully open during the original test → repeat the test correctly before concluding a fault.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate to a genuine core/winding fault or a test setup error.
  6. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause through a correctly conducted, rating-specific no-load test.
Test Points
No-load (magnetizing) current at rated primary voltage
Secondary open-circuit verification
Root Cause Verification

Confirm any replacement unit's no-load current falls within the manufacturer's published acceptance range for its rating before returning it to service.

Corrective Action
  • Reject and replace a transformer confirmed to have a genuine core or winding fault.
  • Repeat the test correctly if a setup error (secondary not truly open) was the actual cause.
  • Update the site's acceptance-criteria reference sheet if the wrong range was being applied.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain a rating-specific no-load current acceptance table for every transformer type used on site.
  • Verify secondary open-circuit condition with a meter, not visual inspection alone, before every no-load test.
  • Include no-load current testing as a standard incoming-inspection step for new and repaired units.

IT-129 Excessive Load Current

Symptom — During a bench test with the secondary connected to its rated fixture load (or an equivalent test load), the secondary current draw exceeds the fixture's rated current for the applied primary excitation — indicating the transformer, fixture, or connected load is drawing more current than designed.

Equipment / System

Isolating transformer secondary circuit and its connected lamp/LED fixture load.

Possible Causes
HighAn incorrect lamp/LED fixture (higher wattage than the circuit and transformer are rated for) has been fitted
HighA partial short within the fixture wiring or lamp base is drawing additional current, reflected back through the transformer
MediumThe transformer's own winding fault is presenting as excess loading rather than a true fixture-side cause — cross-check against IT-126/IT-127
LowerTest load used on the bench does not match the actual field fixture's rated current, giving a misleading result
Required Tools
Isolating transformer bench test setTrue-RMS clamp meterMultimeterFixture/lamp rating referenceTransformer schedule
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO on the CCR before removing the transformer or fixture for bench testing.
  • Sustained excessive secondary current can overheat the transformer — do not leave a confirmed over-current condition connected for extended bench testing.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the exact test load used on the bench matches the fixture's rated current specification.
  2. MeasurementWith the correct test load connected, measure secondary current at rated primary excitation and compare against the fixture's rated value.
  3. ObservationInspect the actual field-installed lamp/LED fixture for correct wattage/rating against the design specification.
  4. MeasurementIf the fixture rating is confirmed correct, bench-test the transformer alone (per IT-126/IT-127) to rule out an internal transformer fault presenting as excess loading.
  5. DecisionWrong-rated fixture confirmed installed → correct with the design-specified fixture. Fixture rating correct but current still excessive → inspect fixture wiring for a partial short. Transformer itself found faulty in isolation testing → replace the transformer.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to an incorrect fixture, a fixture-side wiring fault, or the transformer itself.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause by testing the transformer and fixture independently.
Test Points
Secondary current under rated test load
Field-installed fixture wattage/rating
Fixture wiring insulation and continuity
Root Cause Verification

After correction, confirm secondary current at rated primary excitation matches the fixture's design current, both on the bench and after re-installation in the field.

Corrective Action
  • Replace an incorrectly rated fixture with the design-specified unit.
  • Repair fixture wiring or lamp base if a partial short is confirmed.
  • Replace the transformer if it is confirmed faulty independent of the fixture.
Preventive Action
  • Verify fixture wattage/rating against the design specification whenever a lamp or LED driver is replaced.
  • Bench-test transformer and fixture separately when investigating an over-current complaint, to isolate which component is responsible.
  • Maintain a current fixture and transformer rating schedule by circuit.

IT-130 Low Insulation Resistance

Symptom — A megger test of the isolating transformer (winding-to-winding or winding-to-earth) returns a value below the acceptable threshold (commonly a minimum of 100 MΩ at 500V/1000V per site standard), without yet indicating an outright breakdown — an early-warning finding rather than a confirmed failure.

Equipment / System

Isolating transformer winding insulation system — potting compound, winding varnish/insulation, and the winding-to-core/earth barrier.

Possible Causes
HighMoisture ingress into the potting compound or winding insulation, gradually reducing resistance (see IT-134/IT-135)
HighAge-related insulation degradation from long-term thermal cycling and service life
MediumContamination on the transformer case/terminals providing a surface leakage path, mistaken for internal insulation degradation
MediumTest performed on a wet/damp unit immediately after removal from a flooded pit, giving an artificially low reading that may partially recover once dried
LowerTest equipment/lead fault giving a false low reading
Required Tools
Insulation resistance tester (5kV, or per site standard voltage)Contact cleaner and lint-free clothIsolating transformer bench test setSite insulation resistance acceptance criteria
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO on the CCR before removing the transformer for testing.
  • Handle a unit with confirmed low insulation resistance as a potential shock hazard until it is either repaired/replaced or confirmed safe — do not return it to service pending investigation.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the test is being performed at the correct test voltage per the site's insulation testing standard, and record the exact reading.

Drainage design adequacy for the section affected

After replacement of non-recoverable units and drying/re-testing of marginally affected units, confirm all units in the previously flooded section show stable, acceptable insulation resistance before re-energising the section.

Corrective Action
  • Replace any transformer confirmed to have been fully or near-fully submerged — do not attempt to return a submerged unit to service.
  • Attempt manufacturer-permitted drying only for units with mild, partial exposure and confirm recovery before return to service.
  • Investigate and correct drainage design for any section that experiences flooding, rather than only addressing individual transformers.
Preventive Action
  • Review and improve drainage design for low-lying circuit sections identified as flood-prone.
  • Maintain a flood-event log by circuit section to identify recurring flood-prone areas.
  • Establish a standard post-flood inspection and test procedure covering the full affected section, not just the initially reported fixture.

IT-159 Transformer Failure Under High Load

Symptom — A transformer fails, or a related symptom (overheating, excessive current, tripped protection) appears specifically when the circuit is commanded to a high-intensity step, while the same unit performs acceptably at lower intensity steps — indicating the fault is load-dependent rather than a constant condition.

Equipment / System

Isolating transformer winding and secondary circuit, evaluated specifically across CCR intensity steps rather than at a single fixed load point.

Possible Causes
HighA developing winding or inter-turn fault (see IT-138/IT-139) that is only thermally or electrically significant at the higher current level of a high-intensity step
HighThe transformer or fixture is correctly rated for a lower intensity step but undersized relative to the actual current at the highest commanded step for this circuit
MediumA marginal connection (terminal, connector, or lead) that only develops enough resistance/heating to cause a symptom at higher current
LowerA CCR-side issue specific to the high-intensity step (see Division 01) rather than a transformer fault — verify against other transformers on the same circuit before concluding this unit is at fault
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterIsolating transformer bench test setInfrared thermal cameraTransformer schedule (VA rating by fixture type)
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO on the CCR before removing the transformer for bench testing.
  • Do not repeatedly command a circuit to a high-intensity step to reproduce a suspected fault without adequate monitoring — a developing fault under test can progress to a more severe failure.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the symptom's correlation with CCR intensity step, noting at which step(s) it appears and whether it is absent at lower steps.
  2. MeasurementWith appropriate monitoring, observe secondary current and/or case temperature across the full range of intensity steps to characterise the load-dependency.
  3. MeasurementBench-test the transformer's winding resistance, insulation resistance, and no-load current to check for a developing internal fault.
  4. ObservationVerify the transformer's VA rating is adequate for the actual current at the highest commanded step on this circuit, per the design schedule.
  5. DecisionBench test reveals a developing winding/insulation fault → replace the transformer. Bench test normal but the unit is confirmed undersized for the actual high-step current → correct with the design-specified rating. Other transformers on the same circuit show the same step-dependent symptom → redirect to a Division 01 CCR-side investigation.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to a load-dependent internal transformer fault, an undersized rating, a marginal connection, or a CCR-side cause common to the circuit.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause through step-correlated measurement and bench testing.
Test Points
Secondary current and/or case temperature across intensity steps
Winding resistance, insulation resistance, and no-load current (bench)
Transformer VA rating vs. actual current at highest commanded step
Symptom presence on other transformers on the same circuit
Root Cause Verification

After correction, confirm the fixture operates normally with no recurrence of the symptom across the full range of intensity steps, including the highest step.

Corrective Action
  • Replace a transformer confirmed to have a load-dependent internal fault.
  • Install the design-specified VA rating if the unit is confirmed undersized for the highest commanded step.
  • Correct a marginal connection identified as the cause.
  • Redirect to Division 01 if the cause is confirmed to be CCR-side and common to the circuit.
Preventive Action
  • Verify transformer VA rating against the actual highest-step current for the circuit, not just the fixture's nominal rating.
  • Include a full-range intensity-step check as part of commissioning and periodic testing, not only a single fixed-load check.
  • Track units with any step-dependent symptom for closer monitoring even if the fault has not yet caused an outage.

IT-160 Transformer Failure During High Intensity Operation

Symptom — A transformer fails outright (open, short, or thermal failure) while the circuit is actively operating at a sustained high-intensity step — for example, during a period of low visibility requiring maximum runway/taxiway light output — distinguished from IT-159 by being a confirmed in-service failure event during high-intensity operation rather than a reproducible step-correlated symptom found during testing.

Equipment / System

Isolating transformer and its associated fixture, at the point of an in-service failure occurring during sustained high-intensity operation.

Possible Causes
HighA pre-existing developing fault (winding, insulation, or core) reached its failure point under the sustained thermal and electrical stress of high-intensity operation
HighThe transformer or fixture was undersized for sustained operation at the highest intensity step, having previously only been exposed to shorter or less frequent high-step periods
MediumSustained high-intensity operation coincided with an environmental stressor (high ambient temperature, restricted pit ventilation, or recent moisture exposure) that compounded normal thermal stress
LowerThe failure is unrelated to the high-intensity operation and coincidentally occurred during that period — verify against the unit's prior test/service history before concluding load was the precipitating factor
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterIsolating transformer bench test setInsulation resistance tester (5kV)Infrared thermal cameraTransformer schedule (VA rating by fixture type)
Safety Precautions
  • Apply LOTO on the CCR before removing the failed transformer, coordinating with ATC/airside operations given the operational significance of a failure during low-visibility high-intensity operation.
  • Handle a unit that failed under sustained high load as a potential thermal hazard — allow it to cool and confirm temperature before handling.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationRecord the operational context of the failure — intensity step, duration of sustained operation prior to failure, and any concurrent environmental conditions.
  2. MeasurementBench-test the failed transformer for winding resistance, insulation resistance, and no-load current to characterise the failure mode.
  3. ObservationReview the unit's prior test/service history for any pre-existing developing fault indicator (declining insulation trend, marginal no-load current, prior thermal survey flag) that preceded this failure.
  4. ObservationVerify the transformer's VA rating and duty expectation are adequate for sustained operation at the highest intensity step used on this circuit, not just intermittent exposure.
  5. DecisionBench test confirms a specific failure mode consistent with a pre-existing developing fault → treat as that fault progressing to failure under sustained load (cross-reference the specific IT code for the failure mode found, e.g. IT-136/IT-137/IT-138). No pre-existing indicator found and the unit is confirmed undersized for sustained high-step duty → correct with the design-specified rating for sustained operation. Environmental stressor confirmed as a compounding factor → address the environmental condition in addition to replacing the unit.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific winding/insulation/core failure mode revealed by bench testing, in the context of sustained high-intensity operation.
  7. Root CauseConfirm whether the precipitating factor was a pre-existing fault reaching its limit, an inadequate duty rating, or a compounding environmental stressor.
Test Points
Winding resistance, insulation resistance, and no-load current (bench, post-failure)
Prior service/test history for pre-existing fault indicators
Operational context — intensity step, duration, and environmental conditions at time of failure
Transformer VA/duty rating vs. sustained high-step requirement
Root Cause Verification

After replacement, confirm the new unit's bench test results are within acceptance criteria, and monitor performance during subsequent sustained high-intensity operations to confirm no recurrence.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed transformer, addressing the underlying failure mode identified by bench testing (see the relevant specific IT code).
  • Install a transformer with an adequate duty rating for sustained high-intensity operation if the failed unit is confirmed undersized for that duty.
  • Address any compounding environmental stressor (ventilation, moisture, ambient temperature) identified as a contributing factor.
Preventive Action
  • Include sustained high-intensity duty capability as an explicit acceptance criterion for transformer specification, not just peak current rating.
  • Trend insulation resistance, no-load current, and thermal survey results for units on circuits with frequent low-visibility (high-intensity) operation, to catch developing faults before they fail in service.
  • Prioritise proactive replacement of units showing any developing-fault indicator ahead of anticipated high-intensity-demand periods (e.g., seasonal low-visibility conditions).
