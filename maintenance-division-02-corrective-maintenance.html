Corrective Maintenance

The defect-driven counterpart to Division 01. Corrective maintenance begins when an inspection, failure, test result or abnormal condition identifies a defect requiring diagnosis, repair, replacement, testing and restoration — carried out to return AGL equipment to full, verified service.

Philosophy: a corrective-maintenance job is not complete simply because the defective component has been physically replaced. The full cycle establishes that the original defect is corrected, the equipment is correctly reassembled and connected, it functions correctly, the work area is restored, any associated defect is addressed, and records are completed.

Corrective Maintenance Priority

Every defect is classified consistently with the handbook's established framework, and the classification drives the response.

PriorityMeaningTypical Action
CriticalImmediate operational / safety significanceImmediate action
MajorSignificant degradation / failurePriority corrective action
MinorLimited impactPlanned repair
ObservationCondition requiring monitoringMonitor / trend

Defect Initiation

Corrective maintenance can originate from five sources, each feeding into the same assessment and repair cycle.

Routine Inspection

  • Defect found during scheduled, calendar-driven inspection
e.g. runway edge light found unserviceable

Detailed Inspection

  • Condition deterioration identified during a detailed asset inspection
e.g. isolation transformer insulation deterioration

Failure

  • Equipment fails during normal operation
e.g. CCR output fault

Test Result

  • An electrical or functional test returns a non-conforming result
e.g. cable test indicates unacceptable condition

Special Inspection

  • Lightning
  • Flooding
  • Severe weather
  • Accident / incident
  • Construction
  • Major maintenance

Defect Assessment Record

  • Asset ID, location, circuit, equipment type
  • Defect description and failure symptom
  • Date/time detected and source of defect
  • Operational and safety impact, required action, classification

Root-Cause Consideration

A major improvement for this division is to avoid "replace and close" maintenance. For recurring failures, the diagnostic chain below is worked through before the job is closed.

What failed?
Why did it fail?
Was the component itself defective?
Was installation a factor?
Was moisture involved?
Was overheating involved?
Was electrical stress involved?
Was mechanical damage involved?
Was environmental exposure involved?
Is the failure recurring?
If recurring: the defect is escalated into condition assessment / corrective-action review.

Key Principle for Division 02

A corrective-maintenance job is not closed when the component is replaced. It is closed only when the full chain below is complete — making Division 02 complementary to Division 01, and consistent with the Inspection Handbook, where inspection identifies and classifies the condition while maintenance performs the corrective action.

Defect Identified → Cause Assessed → Repair Completed → Equipment Tested → Function Verified → Area Restored → Returned to Service → Record Completed → Defect Closed

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about corrective maintenance, based on the topics in this division.

8 QUESTIONS
Q1What is corrective maintenance in AGL?

Corrective maintenance is the defect-driven counterpart to preventive maintenance. It begins when an inspection, failure, test result or abnormal condition identifies a defect that needs diagnosis, repair, replacement, testing and restoration, so that AGL equipment is returned to full, verified service.

Q2What does Division 02 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 02 (Corrective Maintenance) contains 34 topics in four groups: Group A Lighting Fixtures (10 topics), Group B Electrical Equipment (8 topics), Group C AGL Systems (10 topics) and Group D Restoration (6 topics).

Q3Is a corrective-maintenance job finished once the defective component is replaced?

No. The job is closed only when the full chain is complete: defect identified, cause assessed, repair completed, equipment tested, function verified, area restored, returned to service, record completed and defect closed. The full cycle establishes that the original defect is corrected, the equipment is reassembled and connected correctly, and any associated defect has been addressed.

Q4What is the standard corrective-maintenance sequence?

Every task follows the same ten-step sequence regardless of asset type: Detect, Assess, Diagnose, Plan, Isolate, Repair/Replace, Test, Restore, Monitor and Close.

Q5How are corrective-maintenance defects prioritised?

Every defect is classified as Critical (immediate operational or safety significance, immediate action), Major (significant degradation or failure, priority corrective action), Minor (limited impact, planned repair) or Observation (a condition needing monitoring, monitor and trend). The classification drives the response.

Q6Where do corrective-maintenance jobs come from?

Five sources feed the same assessment and repair cycle: routine inspection, detailed inspection, equipment failure, a non-conforming test result, and special inspection after events such as lightning, flooding, severe weather, an accident or incident, construction or major maintenance.

Q7What does 'replace and close' mean, and how does the handbook avoid it?

It describes replacing a failed part and closing the job without asking why it failed. For recurring failures the handbook works through a root-cause chain first: what failed, why, whether the component was defective, and whether installation, moisture, overheating, electrical stress, mechanical damage or environmental exposure was involved. If the failure is recurring, it is escalated into a condition assessment or corrective-action review.

Q8What should a defect assessment record contain?

It should capture the asset ID, location, circuit and equipment type; the defect description and failure symptom; the date and time detected and the source of the defect; and the operational and safety impact, required action and classification.
