Product Overview

Approach Crossbar Light

What It Is

Approach crossbar lights form an important transverse visual reference within an airport approach lighting system. The centreline gives the principal longitudinal reference; crossbars extend across it to give a clear horizontal and lateral reference during approach.

Centreline + Crossbar + Side Row creates a geometric pattern for the pilot.

The Pattern Assists With

  • Runway alignment
  • Lateral position
  • Roll perception
  • Approach orientation
  • Visual transition from instrument to visual flight

Selected According To

  • Approach category and lighting system type
  • Runway geometry and threshold position
  • Centreline and side-row arrangement
  • Crossbar location, length and fixture spacing
  • Light colour, optical direction, intensity
  • Mounting arrangement and support structure
  • Electrical circuit and control requirements
  • Project technical specification

The final crossbar configuration should be taken from the approved approach-lighting layout and applicable aerodrome standard.

Function

A Transverse Reference Across the Centreline

Helps the Pilot Interpret

  • Horizontal position and runway alignment
  • Roll attitude
  • Approach geometry
  • Perspective and distance
  • Aircraft relationship to the centreline

Centreline + Crossbar

Centreline = longitudinal reference. Crossbar = transverse reference. Together: longitudinal guidance + horizontal reference. The crossbar is a geometric element, not simply an additional group of lamps.

ICAO Simple Approach

ICAO Simple Approach System

Geometry

Crossbar lights are arranged as nearly as practicable in a horizontal straight line at right angles to and bisected by the centreline.

Spacing

ICAO notes spacings of 1 m to 4 m are in use. With a 30 m crossbar, gaps may be provided either side of the centreline, each not exceeding 6 m.

Where sufficient centreline length cannot be physically achieved, ICAO provides for shortening the centreline while retaining the crossbar where practicable.

Precision Category I

Precision Approach Category I

ICAO Specifies

  • Approach centreline extending wherever possible to 900 m from the threshold
  • A 30 m crossbar
  • Located 300 m from the threshold

Precision Category II / III

Category II / III Crossbars

ICAO System

  • Centreline row wherever possible 900 m
  • Two side rows extending 270 m (240 m where serviceability is demonstrated)
  • Crossbar at 150 m
  • Crossbar at 300 m

Visual Geometry

The crossbars are an important part of the complete Category II/III visual geometry, working with side rows and centreline as one field.

150 m Crossbar

  • ICAO: fills the gaps between centreline and side-row lights
  • Completes the transverse reference close to the threshold
  • Check against centreline spacing, side-row gauge, threshold location, TDZ gauge and side-row position

300 m Crossbar

The principal wider transverse reference. ICAO: extends on both sides of the centreline to 15 m from the centreline — a total nominal extent of approximately 30 m.

Crossbar Geometry

Geometry, Spacing and Length

Crossbar Lights Should Be

  • As nearly as practicable in a horizontal straight line
  • At right angles to the centreline
  • Bisected by the centreline
  • Spaced to produce a linear visual effect

Accurate survey and installation are important.

Spacing Is Determined By

  • Crossbar length and number of light sources
  • Fixture/barrette configuration
  • Applicable approach system and authority requirement
  • Approved project drawing

Do not present one universal spacing value for all approach systems.

Light Colour

Colour Depends on the Approach System

ICAO & FAA

For conventional ICAO systems the crossbar is part of the centreline/crossbar visual field and uses the applicable approach-system colour. Annex 14 specifies fixed white lights for the approach centreline and crossbars; side rows are red and threshold lights are green. For FAA systems the arrangement depends on MALS, MALSF, MALSR, ALSF-1, ALSF-2 or another approved configuration.

Example: FAA ALSF-2

High-intensity white approach lights, green threshold lights and red side-row lights.

Do not specify the crossbar only as a generic “white crossbar light” without identifying the complete approach-lighting system.

System Relationships

Side Rows, TDZ, Centreline and Flashers

Side-Row Lighting

In Category II/III the crossbars and side rows work as a coordinated field: side rows give lateral, crossbars transverse, and the centreline longitudinal alignment.

TDZ Lighting

ICAO ties the gauge between the innermost side-row lights to the TDZ-light gauge. Review crossbar, side-row and TDZ arrangements together for gauge, alignment, threshold position, visual continuity and perspective.

Centreline

The crossbar is not designed independently. The centreline determines the crossbar station, bisecting point, light orientation and approach axis.

Sequenced Flashers

FAA ALSF-2 combines steady-burning elements with 15 sequenced flashers flashing from the outer approach area toward the threshold. Keep crossbar lights visually distinct from flashers, centreline, side-row and threshold lights.

Intensity Control

Visually Balanced at Every Intensity

Depending on the project the system may provide high, medium, low and multiple intensity steps or system-specific control. All centreline, side-row and crossbar components should remain visually balanced at each applicable operating intensity.

Mechanical Installation

Supports, Plane and Environment

Crossbar Assemblies May Use

  • Elevated or low-level supports
  • Light bars and barrettes
  • Frangible structures
  • Modular support frames

Consider support height, horizontal alignment, frangibility, wind loading, structural stability, drainage, cable routing and maintenance access. FAA maintains a separate specification for Low-Impact Resistant (LIR) Structures. ICAO requires elevated approach lights and their supports to be frangible; beyond 300 m from the threshold, where a support is taller than 12 m, frangibility applies to the top 12 m only (Annex 14, Chapter 5 — confirm against your edition).

Approach-Light Plane

ICAO requires the simple approach system to lie as nearly as practicable in the horizontal plane through the threshold, subject to obstacle and visibility provisions. Consider threshold, centreline and crossbar elevation, ground profile, support heights and the obstacle environment.

Environmental Exposure

  • Rain, dust, humidity
  • Temperature variation, UV, corrosion
  • Wind, vibration, wildlife, maintenance activities

Installation Alignment

  • Station from threshold and centreline reference
  • 90-degree relationship to centreline
  • Crossbar length, light spacing, elevation
  • Optical direction, side-row relationship, support alignment

Even a small angular error can distort the perceived horizontal reference. For the Cat II/III 300 m crossbar, verify the specified extent on both sides.

FAQ

Approach Crossbar Light — Frequently Asked Questions

What is an approach crossbar light?

Approach crossbar lights form a transverse visual reference within an approach lighting system. The centreline gives the principal longitudinal reference and the crossbars extend across it to give a clear horizontal and lateral reference. Together with the centreline and side rows they help the pilot judge runway alignment, lateral position, roll perception and approach orientation during the visual transition.

Where are approach crossbars located under ICAO?

For a simple approach system the crossbar is 18 m or 30 m long at 300 m from the threshold. For precision approach Category I it is a 30 m crossbar at 300 m from the threshold. For Category II/III there are crossbars at 150 m and 300 m. The approved project drawing controls the actual geometry.

How are crossbar lights arranged and spaced?

Crossbar lights are arranged as nearly as practicable in a horizontal straight line at right angles to and bisected by the centreline. ICAO notes spacings of 1 m to 4 m are in use, and with a 30 m crossbar gaps may be provided either side of the centreline, each not exceeding 6 m. Do not present one universal spacing value for all approach systems; it depends on crossbar length, number of light sources, the approach system and the approved drawing.

What do the 150 m and 300 m crossbars do in Category II/III?

The 150 m crossbar fills the gaps between the centreline and side-row lights and completes the transverse reference close to the threshold. The 300 m crossbar is the principal wider transverse reference: it extends on both sides of the centreline to 15 m from the centreline, a total nominal extent of approximately 30 m. Both work with the side rows and centreline as one visual field.

What colour are approach crossbar lights?

Annex 14 specifies fixed white lights for the approach centreline and crossbars, with red side rows and green threshold lights. For FAA systems the arrangement depends on MALS, MALSF, MALSR, ALSF-1, ALSF-2 or another approved configuration; ALSF-2 uses high-intensity white approach lights, green threshold lights and red side-row lights. Do not specify the crossbar only as a generic white crossbar light without identifying the complete approach lighting system.

How does a crossbar relate to the centreline, side rows and TDZ lights?

The crossbar is not designed independently: the centreline determines the crossbar station, bisecting point, light orientation and approach axis. In Category II/III the crossbars and side rows work as a coordinated field, with side rows giving lateral, crossbars transverse and the centreline longitudinal alignment. ICAO ties the gauge between the innermost side-row lights to the touchdown-zone light gauge, so review the crossbar, side-row and TDZ arrangements together.

How is a crossbar different from a sequenced flasher?

A crossbar is normally steady and provides a transverse, geometric horizontal and roll reference, while a sequenced flasher flashes in sequence and provides a dynamic lead-in cue along the approach path. FAA ALSF-2 includes both steady-burning approach elements and 15 sequenced flashers flashing from the outer approach area toward the threshold, so keep crossbar lights visually distinct from flashers, centreline, side-row and threshold lights.

How are crossbar lights supported and aligned?

Assemblies may use elevated or low-level supports, light bars and barrettes, frangible structures or modular support frames. ICAO requires elevated approach lights and their supports to be frangible (beyond 300 m from the threshold, where a support is taller than 12 m, frangibility applies to the top 12 m only), and the simple system lies as nearly as practicable in the horizontal plane through the threshold. Verify the station from the threshold and centreline reference, the 90-degree relationship to the centreline, crossbar length, spacing and elevation, because even a small angular error can distort the perceived horizontal reference.

How are crossbar lights powered and how is intensity controlled?

Potential arrangements include a series-circuit supply or CCR-fed circuit, a dedicated approach-light circuit, a local transformer or LED driver, a control cabinet and integrated flasher or control equipment where required. Depending on the project the system may provide high, medium, low and multiple intensity steps, and all centreline, side-row and crossbar components should remain visually balanced at each applicable operating intensity.

What are the common faults in approach crossbar lighting?

Typical symptoms are a crossbar light that is off, uneven crossbar output, crossbar misalignment, an incomplete crossbar and a poor horizontal reference. Troubleshoot in sequence: approach lighting control, CCR or power, primary circuit, transformer or driver, secondary connection, crossbar fixture, light source, optical alignment, crossbar geometry and photometry. For system-level problems also verify centreline, crossbar, side row, threshold and TDZ.

What should be confirmed before ordering approach crossbar lights?

Confirm the runway designation and threshold location, approach category, approach lighting system type, centreline arrangement, crossbar station and length, number of light sources and individual-light spacing, side-row arrangement, TDZ-light gauge, light colour, optical direction, intensity steps, light-bar or barrette configuration, support structure and frangibility, and electrical and control details. Request the crossbar layout drawing, photometric report, mounting drawing, frangibility documentation and compliance certificates.

Engineering Note
