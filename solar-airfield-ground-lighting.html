Solar Airfield Ground Lighting (SAGL)

A practical engineering guide to solar-powered runway, taxiway and airport visual lighting systems. SAGL is an airfield visual-lighting arrangement in which photovoltaic energy is converted into electrical energy, stored in batteries where required, and used to operate aviation lighting equipment. Unlike conventional centralized AGL — which commonly depends on an electrical distribution network, CCRs, series circuits and isolation transformers — solar AGL can distribute the energy source directly at or close to individual lighting units, making it especially useful for remote, small, off-grid, temporary or infrastructure-constrained applications.

Overview

What Is Solar Airfield Ground Lighting?

Solar AGL is an airfield-lighting system powered partly or entirely by photovoltaic energy. A typical unit integrates a solar panel, charge controller, rechargeable battery, LED driver, aviation light source and optical system — with additional components such as a radio receiver, remote controller, control/monitoring electronics, GPS or timing system, communication antenna, mounting structure and mechanical anchoring system.

The FAA is currently evaluating decentralized solar lighting at General Aviation airports, including runway and taxiway edge lights, obstruction lights, runway guard lights, windsocks and guidance signs — its research specifically evaluates reliability, safety, photometric and design compliance, and siting requirements.

A solar runway light is an equipment item. A solar AGL system is an integrated aviation visual-aid system. The engineering question is therefore not only "Does the solar lamp work?" — it is "Can the complete system reliably provide the required visual guidance for the intended operation?"

More Than a Solar Lamp

A solar AGL system is an integrated aviation visual-aid system — energy, storage, electronics, optics, geometry, control and environment must all work together.

Decentralized, Not Uncontrolled

Solar AGL can remove much of the conventional centralized distribution architecture — but "decentralized" does not mean the system can go unmonitored or unverified.

FAA Solar Research

The FAA's decentralized solar-lighting research evaluates reliability, safety, photometric compliance, design compliance, solar suitability and siting requirements across multiple visual-aid types.

Why It Matters

Why Solar AGL Is Important

Solar technology can reduce the need for extensive electrical infrastructure at locations where grid-connected AGL would be difficult or expensive. FAA airport technology research specifically identifies solar as an alternative for small, remote and off-grid airports.

Reduced cable infrastructure Reduced trenching Reduced dependence on centralized power Easier deployment in remote locations Independent operation of individual units Local energy storage Potentially lower infrastructure complexity
These advantages do not eliminate the need for aviation-grade design and verification.

Where It Fits Best

Remote Airfields & Small General Aviation Airports

Remote Airfields

Solar AGL is especially relevant where grid power is unavailable, cable installation is difficult, the runway is far from the electrical substation, terrain is difficult, infrastructure development is expensive, airport operations are intermittent, or independent lighting units are desirable. The site should nevertheless undergo proper engineering evaluation before deployment.

Small General Aviation Airports

The FAA is evaluating solar-powered lighting at General Aviation airports as part of its ongoing airport technology research. Its test arrays include several types of airfield visual aids — solar AGL should be viewed as a potential system architecture, not simply an alternative lamp technology.

Deployment Duration

Temporary vs Permanent Decentralized SAGL

Temporary Solar AGL

Used for construction, emergency restoration, temporary runway configuration, remote works, temporary taxi routes and short-duration operations. The designer should consider both the solar energy system and the approved temporary airfield layout.

Permanent Decentralized Solar AGL

A typical concept: individual light + integrated PV + battery + local control + wireless communication — removing much of the conventional centralized distribution architecture. However, "decentralized" does not mean "uncontrolled."

Solar AGL Reliability Chain

System reliability cannot be described using LED lifetime alone — every stage in the chain has its own failure possibility, and a fault anywhere breaks the visual guidance function.

Sun available → PV healthy → controller healthy → battery healthy → driver healthy → LED healthy → optics correct

LED lifetime is not equivalent to system lifetime — the complete solar light still contains a battery, electronics, seals, connectors, PV components and optical components, each with its own failure mode.
Energy Fundamentals

Photovoltaic Energy Conversion & Solar Radiation

Solar panels convert incident solar radiation into electrical energy. The actual energy available varies — therefore rated panel wattage alone is not sufficient for system sizing.

Solar irradiance Panel orientation Panel area Temperature Shading Dust Cloud cover Atmospheric conditions Seasonal variation Latitude Time of day Horizon obstruction Panel inclination
A site with strong annual sunlight may still have difficult periods during monsoon, winter, prolonged cloud cover or dust events — design around the worst-case period, not the annual average.

Every stage introduces losses, so PV capacity must be sized against the entire system energy requirement, not only the LED rating.

Daily PV Energy ≈ PV Capacity × Effective Solar Hours × System Efficiency

System efficiency accounts for losses in PV conversion, charge controller, battery charging, battery discharge, wiring and driver electronics. The actual design should use site-specific solar data.

LED Load — A Reminder

LED technology is highly useful for solar AGL because LEDs generally provide high luminous efficiency compared with older light-source technologies. However: low electrical power ≠ low engineering requirement. The LED still needs to produce the required intensity, colour, beam distribution, angular coverage and reliability.

Storage

Battery Storage & Autonomy

The battery provides energy when solar generation is unavailable, making it one of the most critical components in a solar AGL system.

CapacityVoltageChemistryCycle life TemperatureDepth of dischargeCharging characteristics Self-dischargeAgeMaintenanceReplacement interval

Battery Autonomy is the period for which the system can operate without meaningful solar charging.

Required autonomy = expected no-sun operating period + design reserve

The required value should be determined from the operational requirement, site climate, lighting duty cycle and applicable design criteria. Do not use a generic autonomy figure for every airport.

Worked Example

Energy Budget & Example Calculation

Daily energy consumption = lighting load + control load + communication load + monitoring load, then add allowances for charge losses, battery losses, temperature effects, aging, reserve capacity and seasonal solar availability.

Example: a unit uses a 10 W LED system for 10 hours/night → Lighting energy = 10 W × 10 h = 100 Wh/night. Once controller and battery losses are included, the required stored energy will be greater than 100 Wh. A simple "10 W × 10 hours" calculation is not enough for final system sizing.

Duty Cycle & Control

Duty Cycle, Pilot Control & Wireless Networking

Duty Cycle

Not every visual aid operates continuously — continuous operation, reduced intensity, standby mode, pilot activation, scheduled, flashing or event-driven operation each strongly affect energy consumption.

Pilot-Controlled SAGL

Pilot command → radio receiver → local controller → lighting units. The exact control arrangement must be designed and tested for the equipment and operational environment.

Wireless Networking

Used for ON/OFF commands, intensity control, status feedback, fault reporting, battery information, configuration and remote diagnostics via radio, proprietary RF, mesh, cellular or other site-specific technology.

Wireless Failure

Communication loss, interference, receiver failure, antenna damage, software fault, power loss or configuration error — the design should define what happens when communication is unavailable.

Local Autonomy

An individual light can potentially keep operating even if the central network or adjacent lights fail — but this behaviour is equipment-specific and must not be assumed without documentation.

Centralized vs Decentralized

Centralized: control centre → network → field equipment. Decentralized: local controller → individual unit. Hybrid: central control → wireless network → local autonomous control.

Each architecture has different failure modes. A distributed system can make single-unit failures easier to isolate, but whether a single failure is operationally acceptable depends on light function, location, pattern, required serviceability and applicable operating rules. Do not treat decentralization as automatic redundancy.

Failure Analysis

Single, Multiple & Common-Cause Failure

Single Light Failure

Depends on light function, location, pattern, required serviceability, applicable operating rules and failure distribution — not automatically acceptable just because it's an isolated unit.

Multiple-Light Failure

The more important question is often "what happens when several adjacent lights fail?" — possible causes include severe weather, battery depletion, communication failure, charging failure or control malfunction.

Common-Cause Failure

Poor solar siting, extended cloud cover, incorrect configuration, firmware problems, radio interference, excessive dust or a battery design problem can affect many individual units simultaneously.

Redundancy Strategy

Conventional AGL uses multiple feeders, separate transformers, backup power and central monitoring. Solar AGL instead uses distributed units, individual storage, multiple independent power sources, wireless redundancy and local autonomous operation.

Siting

Panel Orientation, Integrated Geometry & Shading

Solar Panel Orientation

Geographic location, solar path, panel angle, seasonal performance, horizon obstruction and airfield geometry all affect energy production. The best solar orientation and the best aviation-light orientation are not necessarily the same.

Integrated Solar-Light Geometry

Panels may be mounted directly on the light housing, or use separate panels, adjustable brackets, multiple panels, vertical PV surfaces or remote PV modules. The mechanical arrangement should maintain required aviation visibility while maximizing energy collection.

Shading

Buildings, trees, vehicles, aircraft, fences, signs, structures, terrain and even other solar units can shade a panel — a panel partially shaded for part of the day may significantly reduce energy production.

Airfield Shading Study — Check Throughout the Day

A site that appears unobstructed at noon may have significant morning or evening shading — inspect across the full expected solar path and seasonal changes

Environmental Factors

Soiling, Temperature & Battery Chemistry

Solar Panel Soiling & Cleaning

Dust accumulates from aircraft movement, construction, unpaved shoulders, wind and vehicle traffic. A maintenance plan should specify inspection frequency, cleaning method, water requirements, cleaning safety and damage inspection — never use aggressive cleaning methods that can damage PV surfaces.

Temperature Effects

PV and battery performance both depend on temperature — high daytime temperature, low nighttime temperature, seasonal changes and enclosure temperature all matter. Thermal design is therefore important in hot climates.

Battery Chemistry

Lead-acid, AGM, gel, lithium-based and other rechargeable chemistries each differ in temperature tolerance, cycle life, weight, maintenance, safety, cost, replacement availability and charging strategy.

Battery Management System

Lithium-based systems commonly require cell monitoring, overcharge protection, over-discharge protection, over-temperature protection, current protection and balancing — the exact architecture depends on battery chemistry.

Charge Controller & MPPT

The controller manages charge regulation, overcharge protection, load management, low-voltage disconnect and battery monitoring, using MPPT or PWM. MPPT can extract more usable energy under changing solar conditions where panel and battery voltages differ significantly.

LED Driver & Intensity Control

The driver must maintain appropriate current, voltage, regulation, protection and dimming behaviour — a poor driver can reduce optical stability even when battery and PV are healthy. Higher intensity means higher energy demand, so intensity, battery capacity and PV capacity should be designed together.

Photometry

Photometry Remains Essential

A solar light is still an aviation light. Solar technology changes the energy source, not the fundamental visual-aid requirement.

Do not determine compliance only by asking "Can I see the light?" — a light can be visibly bright and still have an incorrect photometric distribution.

Application-Specific Engineering

Solar Light Types & Their Particular Considerations

EDGE

Runway & Taxiway Edge

Verify runway/taxiway geometry, light spacing, colour, intensity, orientation, mechanical stability and power endurance — particular attention at intersections, holding positions, curves, aprons and construction zones.

THRESHOLD

Threshold & Runway End

Correct colour, quantity, spacing and orientation, and a clear relationship with runway edge lights, are essential for correct runway-end communication to approaching/departing aircraft.

REIL

Runway End Identifier

Evaluate flashing pattern, intensity, beam direction, synchronisation, control and visibility. Do not assume a generic solar beacon can perform the function of an aviation REIL.

APPROACH

Approach Lighting

Considerably more complex than edge lighting — needs defined geometry, accurate positioning, correct visual pattern, stable power, appropriate optical output, suitable mechanical supports and operational approval.

PAPI

Solar PAPI

PAPI is not merely a lamp — precise geometry, elevation, aiming angles, unit relationship, surveying, photometric characteristics and approach-path considerations all apply. A solar power supply does not simplify these requirements.

OBSTRUCTION

Obstruction Lighting

Particularly useful for isolated obstructions where grid connection is difficult — solar sizing and battery autonomy are especially important because maintenance access may be limited.

SIGNS

Airfield Guidance Signs

Consider sign brightness, day/night control, LED load, battery capacity, solar panel sizing, communication and inspection access.

WINDSOCK

Windsock Lighting

Consider required operating period, windcone location, battery autonomy, environmental exposure, visibility and maintenance access.

GUARD

Runway Guard Lighting

Part of the runway-incursion prevention arrangement — evaluate location, control, fail-safe response and monitoring, not as a simple replacement lamp.

Critical Rule

Solar AGL, CCRs & Isolation Transformers

A standalone solar lighting unit usually does not use the conventional CCR → 6.6 A series circuit architecture — instead it commonly uses PV → battery → LED driver. This is one of the biggest conceptual differences between conventional and decentralized solar AGL. Standalone solar units generally do not require the isolation-transformer architecture used in a conventional constant-current series circuit.

Never connect a solar unit to a conventional 6.6 A series circuit without equipment-specific technical justification. Any hybrid system connected to conventional AGL infrastructure must be specifically engineered.
Hybrid Systems

Hybrid Solar AGL

A hybrid system can combine solar, battery, grid and generator sources to improve operational flexibility.

Solar + Grid Charging

A hybrid controller can allow batteries to be charged from solar PV, a grid AC/DC system, or a generator — such systems require suitable power-management logic and protection.

Solar + Generator

A generator can serve as backup when solar generation is insufficient, battery state of charge is low, prolonged bad weather occurs, or emergency operations extend beyond normal design assumptions. Automatic generator integration requires additional control engineering.

"Solar powered" does not necessarily mean 100% energy independent under all conditions — the system remains dependent on solar availability, battery health, electronics, panel condition and environmental conditions.
Design Margin

Worst-Case Conditions, Reserve Margin & Sizing Sequence

System design should consider the period when energy demand is high and solar energy availability is low — this is typically more important than designing around average annual sunlight. Before designing, obtain site-specific data for global irradiation, seasonal variation, effective peak sun hours, cloud conditions, temperature and historical weather. A site-specific energy model is preferable to generic internet-based assumptions.

A solar AGL system should not normally be designed so that daily solar generation ≈ daily load with no reserve — weather varies, panels soil, batteries age, temperatures change and system losses vary.

Mechanical & Safety

Environmental Protection, Anchorage & Aircraft Clearance

Environmental Exposure

Rain, dust, UV, heat, humidity, salt, wind, jet blast, wildlife and insects — selected equipment should be suitable for the environmental conditions of the installation.

Corrosion & Water Ingress

Corrosion can affect mounting hardware, battery terminals, connectors, PV structures, housing and fasteners. Water ingress points include compartments, cable glands, connectors, housing joints, switches, antennas and PV connections — often causing intermittent or delayed failure.

Mechanical Protection & Anchorage

Consider impact, wind, jet blast, ground movement, vibration, maintenance activity and vehicle exposure. After strong wind or jet-blast exposure, inspect for movement, tilt, foundation movement, bracket damage and cable displacement.

Safety

Electrical protection, battery protection, mechanical stability, aircraft clearance, personnel safety, fire considerations, cable safety and maintenance access — battery technologies with specific thermal or chemical hazards need additional protection.

Aircraft Clearance

Solar panels can be physically larger than conventional fixtures — check against runway/taxiway strip, obstacle-free areas, clearance surfaces, wingtip clearance, propeller clearance and jet blast exposure.

Frangibility

Where frangibility is required, the complete assembly — light, solar panel, support and foundation — must be evaluated. A compliant lamp attached to an unsuitable support does not automatically make the complete installation compliant.

Deployment Type

Portable vs Fixed Solar AGL

Portable Solar AGL

Designed primarily for rapid deployment, temporary use, emergency response and construction. See our Portable Airfield Ground Lighting (PAGL) guide for the complete portable-lighting engineering picture.

Fixed Solar AGL

Designed for longer-term deployment, remote infrastructure, dedicated airfield applications and permanent or semi-permanent installations. Applicable design and certification requirements can differ between portable and fixed solar equipment.

Standards

Standards & Regulatory References

FAA

AC 150/5345-50B (portable runway/taxiway lights and REIL for temporary use) and AC 150/5345-46F (Specification for Runway, Taxiway, Heliport, and Vertiport Light Fixtures) are both currently listed as active. The FAA has also published research material on solar-powered lighting and airport solar technology, with its current airport-technology program evaluating decentralized solar lighting to inform future guidance.

FAA Solar Research

Current research evaluates reliability, safety, photometric compliance, design compliance, solar suitability and siting requirements — across multiple visual-aid types, showing solar AGL is being assessed as an integrated airport infrastructure concept, not only a runway-edge product.

ICAO

Solar technology does not replace the applicable aerodrome visual-aid requirements. The actual applicable ICAO, national and airport-specific requirements must be established for the particular project.

National

DGCA / State aviation authority requirements, approved airport drawings and specifications, and airport operational procedures and approvals as applicable.

There is an important distinction between solar technology requirements and aviation visual-aid requirements. The solar system provides the energy; the airfield visual-aid requirements determine what the resulting aviation lighting system must achieve. The designer should identify the applicable visual-aid specification, photometric requirement, equipment specification, aerodrome design requirements, national aviation authority requirements and airport-specific requirements.

Engineering Model

The Final Solar AGL Engineering Principle

Solar AGL is not "AGL without cables." It is a different system architecture with a different set of engineering challenges. The engineer must simultaneously manage solar energy, battery storage, LED electronics, optics, geometry, control, communication, environmental conditions, mechanical stability and operational requirements.

The most important design question is not "How many watts is the solar panel?" It is: "Can this complete system reliably deliver the required aviation visual guidance throughout the intended operating conditions?"

Precision AGL Academy takeaway: the sun provides the energy, the battery provides continuity, the electronics control the light, and photometry proves what the pilot actually receives.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is solar airfield ground lighting (SAGL)?

SAGL is an airfield-lighting system powered partly or entirely by photovoltaic energy, converted to electrical energy, stored in batteries where required and used to operate aviation lighting equipment. A typical unit integrates a solar panel, charge controller, rechargeable battery, LED driver, aviation light source and optical system.

Q2How does solar AGL differ from conventional AGL?

Conventional AGL uses utility or generator power, centralized backup, series-circuit distribution, CCRs, isolation transformers and extensive underground cabling with centralized control. Solar AGL uses solar energy with local batteries, decentralized units, minimal or local wiring and local, wireless or hybrid control. CCRs and isolation transformers are usually not required for standalone units.

Q3Is a solar runway light the same as a solar AGL system?

No. A solar light is an equipment item, while a solar AGL system is an integrated aviation visual-aid system. The engineering question is whether the complete system can reliably provide the required visual guidance for the intended operation.

Q4Why is solar AGL important?

It can reduce the need for extensive electrical infrastructure where grid-connected AGL would be difficult or expensive, and FAA airport technology research identifies solar as an alternative for small, remote and off-grid airports. Benefits include reduced cabling and trenching, easier deployment in remote locations, independent unit operation and local energy storage.

Q5Where can solar AGL be used?

Applications include runway and taxiway edge lighting, threshold and runway end lighting, REIL, obstruction lighting, runway guard lighting, airfield guidance signs, windsock lighting, and temporary and remote lighting. Suitability depends on the exact operational requirement and applicable standards.

Q6Where does solar AGL fit best?

Remote airfields where grid power is unavailable or cable installation is difficult, and small general aviation airports. The site should still undergo proper engineering evaluation before deployment.

Q7What is the FAA researching in solar airfield lighting?

The page says the FAA is evaluating decentralized solar lighting at General Aviation airports, including runway and taxiway edge lights, obstruction lights, runway guard lights, windsocks and guidance signs. The research evaluates reliability, safety, photometric and design compliance, solar suitability and siting requirements.

Q8Does decentralized solar AGL mean uncontrolled?

No. Solar AGL can remove much of the conventional centralized distribution architecture, but decentralized does not mean the system can go unmonitored or unverified.

Q9What is the difference between temporary and permanent solar AGL?

Temporary solar AGL is used for construction, emergency restoration, temporary runway configurations, remote works, temporary taxi routes and short-duration operations, and must consider both the solar energy system and the approved temporary layout. Permanent decentralized solar AGL typically combines an individual light with integrated PV, battery, local control and wireless communication.

Q10Do the advantages of solar remove the need for aviation-grade design?

No. These advantages do not eliminate the need for aviation-grade design and verification. Energy, storage, electronics, optics, geometry, control and environment must all work together.
