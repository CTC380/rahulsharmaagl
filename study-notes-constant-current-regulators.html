WHAT IS A CCR?

The Constant Current Regulator (CCR) is one of the most important electrical components in an AGL system. In a conventional series circuit it supplies a controlled current through the complete primary loop, and maintains that current despite changes in circuit load, within the regulator's operating range. It is the main power-control device for an AGL series circuit.

A CCR converts the available supply into a controlled output suitable for an AGL series circuit. Unlike an ordinary voltage-regulated supply, it is primarily concerned with controlling output current.

Purpose
  • Maintain the required current in the airfield lighting series circuit
  • Provide the required lighting intensity levels

WHY A CCR IS REQUIRED

A lighting circuit may contain many fittings, isolation transformers, cables and other loads, and its electrical characteristics change with operating conditions. The CCR compensates by controlling the circuit current.

Conditions that change the circuit

  • Number of lamps operating
  • Transformer loading
  • Cable impedance and length
  • Faults and open secondary circuits
  • Temperature
  • Circuit configuration
  • Equipment ageing

Without proper current regulation

  • Incorrect light intensity
  • Excessive current
  • Reduced light output
  • Unstable operation
  • Equipment stress
  • Poor photometric performance

Why it is called a series circuit

The primary sides of the isolation transformers are connected in series, so the same current passes through every one. A CCR output of 6.6 A means the designed operating current of the loop is approximately 6.6 A RMS, subject to the applicable regulator specification and operating conditions.

4

CURRENT IS CONTROLLED, VOLTAGE VARIES

This is one of the most important concepts for an AGL engineer. An ordinary supply is described as, say, 230 V constant. A CCR is operated as 6.6 A constant; the output voltage is whatever the regulator must produce, within its capability, to drive that current through the connected circuit.

Current = controlled
Voltage = varies according to circuit requirement

V ≈ I × Z
  V = circuit voltage   I = regulated current   Z = effective circuit impedance

Real AGL circuits contain transformers, cables and reactive effects, so design calculations must use the actual equipment and circuit characteristics.

7

6.6 A AND 20 A CIRCUITS

6.6 A (Class 1)

A very important AGL configuration. The transformer primary is chosen to suit the series current and the connected fitting. Transformer arrangements met in practice include 6.6 A / 6.6 A and 20 A / 6.6 A. Select from the circuit and fixture requirements, never from wattage alone.

20 A (Class 2)

Some installations use a 20 A series circuit. AC 150/5345-10H five-step nominal currents: 8.5 → 10.3 → 12.4 → 15.8 → 20.0 A. Isolation transformers, cables, connectors, fittings, control equipment and protective devices must all be designed for that current.

INPUT, OUTPUT AND VOLTAGE

Input: receives utility or airport power. AC 150/5345-10H lists standard input ratings of 208, 220, 240 and 480 V at a standard frequency of 60 Hz. Other voltages, frequencies or sizes may be supplied for local conditions when all the requirements of the specification are met. On 50 Hz systems, as in India, the CCR, isolation transformers (FAA L-831 is the 50 Hz type; L-830 is 60 Hz) and fittings must be specified and tested for 50 Hz operation (see Note 08); confirm supply voltage and frequency in the project specification and do not assume a 60 Hz FAA catalogue item is suitable.

Output: feeds the AGL series circuit at the selected regulated current. The output voltage is not a fixed value; it depends on the connected series load.

Environment: the specification defines temperature, humidity, altitude and continuous-operation requirements, for example an equipment range of approximately −40 °C to +55 °C for covered equipment. Project specifications may add requirements.

10

CCR LOAD, SIZING AND POWER RATING

CCR sizing is not simply number of lights × lamp wattage. A proper load assessment considers transformer ratings, fixture power, number of fittings, cable and transformer losses, control components, power factor, expected maximum and minimum load, circuit configuration and spare capacity. FAA visual-aid guidance warns that changing loads can affect CCR efficiency and stability, so design for both the lowest and highest possible loading.

Selection sequence

  1. Identify the AGL circuit.
  2. Determine the series current.
  3. Count fittings and transformers.
  4. Determine transformer ratings.
  5. Calculate the circuit load.
  6. Determine the required CCR kW / kVA capacity.
  7. Check voltage capability.
  8. Check brightness steps.
  9. Check control and monitoring.
  10. Check the standard and project specification.

CCR capacity is commonly expressed as output power in kW. AC 150/5345-10H lists standard sizes of 1, 2, 4, 7.5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30 kW, with larger legacy sizes also identified; some manufacturers also offer intermediate sizes (e.g. 5 kW), so confirm the size table of the edition in force. The selected size must come from the project design and manufacturer's calculations.

Total requirement = Load + system losses + required operating margin

Losses: isolation transformers · cables · equipment · control components · other connected loads
Never select a CCR only from the number of lights, and do not pick the next standard rating blindly. Confirm with the approved design calculation and manufacturer data.

Worked outline: 100 fittings on a 6.6 A circuit

Number of fittingsTransformer ratingsConnected powerCable lossesTransformer lossesCircuit voltageCCR kW rating
Illustrative example only (assumed values). 100 fittings, each 45 W lamp load = 4.5 kW. Add isolation-transformer losses (assume about 8–10 % of lamp load ≈ 0.4 kW) and primary-cable I²R loss (assume 5 km loop at about 1.3 Ω/km = 6.5 Ω; 6.6² × 6.5 ≈ 0.28 kW). Total ≈ 5.2 kW. A 4 kW CCR is too small; a 7.5 kW CCR would be about 70 % loaded, which leaves margin for growth without being dangerously light. Always repeat the calculation with the real transformer, cable and fitting data, and check the lowest-load condition too.
11

EFFICIENCY AND POWER FACTOR

Efficiency

Efficiency (%) = Output power ÷ Input power × 100. AC 150/5345-10H sets minimum efficiency for its covered ratings (commonly about 90 % minimum at the highest step with full resistive load for typical sizes); take the exact figure for each kW rating from the table in the edition in force. Field efficiency varies with load, power factor, step, temperature and equipment condition.

Power factor

Power factor = Real power ÷ Apparent power. A low power factor means the system carries more apparent power for the same useful real power. AC 150/5345-10H requires at least 0.90 for CCRs of 10 kW or less and at least 0.95 for CCRs larger than 10 kW, measured at maximum step and full resistive load.

12

MONITORING, LOCAL AND REMOTE CONTROL

A monitored CCR does more than regulate. It detects abnormal circuit conditions and reports faults to the airport control system. The FAA specification identifies monitoring of:

  • Loss of AC input power
  • Regulator shutdown by protection
  • Significant reduction in delivered circuit VA
  • Failure to deliver the selected output current
  • Failure of a specified number of lamps

Local control

The operator controls the CCR from the equipment itself.

Remote control

The CCR takes commands from an external system: ON/OFF, brightness selection, fault status and monitoring. Interfaces include an AGL control panel, relay system, ALCMS, SCADA or airport control system.

The CCR is the bridge between the control system and the high-power series circuit. AC 150/5345-10H references AC 150/5345-56 for the airport lighting control and monitoring system.

14

FAILURES: PRIMARY VS SECONDARY

A secondary-side open circuit may stop one fitting while the primary series circuit stays continuous through the isolation transformer. That is why a single failed fixture does not take down the CCR circuit.

A break in the primary series circuit interrupts the only current path, so every fitting on that circuit goes out, not only those beyond the break. The CCR sees an open circuit and its open-circuit protection normally shuts it down; the break must be located and repaired before re-energising. Fault finding must always separate primary faults from secondary / fixture faults.

Light loading caution. FAA guidance notes that low CCR loading combined with many open secondary isolation transformers can, for some CCR designs, reduce efficiency or cause instability.

Protection functions

  • Overcurrent
  • Overvoltage
  • Open-circuit protection (output opened or primary loop broken)
  • Short-circuit conditions
  • Thermal overload
  • Input abnormalities
  • Internal component faults
  • Surge conditions

Protection philosophy and settings vary by manufacturer and specification. Do not treat the CCR as an ordinary industrial motor controller or generic power supply.

15

WAVEFORM, ENCLOSURE AND LOCATION

Output waveform

Consider RMS current, waveform distortion, stability, regulation and switching characteristics. AC 150/5345-10H includes output-current, waveform and certification-testing requirements.

Enclosure

Normally a dedicated equipment enclosure protecting power components, control electronics, terminals, transformers / reactors, monitoring equipment and protection devices. The specification includes grounding and physical-arrangement requirements.

Location

Commonly the AGL lighting vault or electrical equipment room, allowing safe access, ventilation / thermal management, cable entry, inspection, maintenance, isolation, control connection and fire / electrical safety.

Terminals

Input (AC power), output (series circuit), control (ON/OFF, brightness, remote, status) and ground. Designations vary by manufacturer.

FAT outline

  • Document verification: datasheet, GA drawing, wiring diagram, test and calibration certificates, compliance documents
  • Electrical: input, output current, steps, voltage, efficiency, power factor
  • Functional: local and remote control, alarms, monitoring, interlocks
  • Safety: insulation, dielectric withstand, earthing, protection

SAT / commissioning

Test the complete chain (CCR, primary cable, isolation transformers, secondary cable, fixtures) under actual installed conditions. Verify correct circuit, current, brightness levels, loading, transformer configuration, fixture operation, remote indication and fault monitoring.

Output current check

For a nominal 6.6 A setting, measured RMS current must fall within the equipment tolerance. AC 150/5345-10H specifies 6.50–6.70 A RMS for the applicable Class 1 steps, with similar tolerances for other steps. Use an appropriate calibrated true-RMS method and the approved procedure. Measure with a correctly rated clamp or CT around the insulated primary conductor; never break the series loop to insert a meter while energised.

Safety first: a series circuit is lethal. Even at 6.6 A the primary loop can reach thousands of volts, and an open series circuit can produce a dangerous voltage across the break. Never open, disconnect or touch an energised primary cable, connector or transformer. Switch off, isolate at the CCR, apply lock-out/tag-out, prove dead, and follow the airport permit-to-work. Do not work on any series circuit alone, and never leave the loop open while the CCR is energised.
Do not repeatedly reset a tripped CCR without identifying the cause. Repeated resets can turn a circuit fault into an equipment fault.

COMMON CCR MISTAKES

1. Treating a CCR as a normal transformer

It is a controlled power-electronic / electrical system.

2. Measuring only voltage

The primary controlled parameter is current.

3. Selecting only by kW

Also verify current, circuit voltage, load, brightness steps, controls and monitoring.

4. Ignoring circuit loading

Very light or abnormal loading can affect certain CCR designs.

5. Mixing 6.6 A and 20 A components

CCR, transformer, connectors and fittings must match the actual series circuit.

6. Ignoring monitoring

An L-829-type monitored arrangement provides functionality an L-828 does not.

7. Repeatedly resetting a tripped CCR

Identify the fault first.

Exam & Interview

EXAM / INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

What does CCR stand for?

Constant Current Regulator.

What is the main function of a CCR?

To provide and maintain controlled current in an AGL series lighting circuit.

Why is constant current required in AGL?

AGL series circuits are designed around controlled current, allowing compatible fittings to operate at defined intensity levels.

Does a CCR regulate voltage?

Its primary function is to regulate current. Output voltage varies as the connected series circuit requires, within the regulator's capability.

What is a 6.6 A CCR?

A CCR designed for a nominal 6.6 A output class (FAA Class 1), subject to the applicable specification.

What is a 20 A CCR?

A regulator designed for a nominal 20 A output class (FAA Class 2).

What is L-828?

A CCR without monitoring under FAA AC 150/5345-10H.

What is L-829?

A CCR with monitoring under FAA AC 150/5345-10H.

What is L-827?

A regulator monitor under the FAA classification.

What is Style 1? Style 2?

Style 1 has three brightness steps; Style 2 has five.

What is the 6.6 A five-step current sequence?

2.8 → 3.4 → 4.1 → 5.2 → 6.6 A.

What is the difference between a CCR and an isolation transformer?

The CCR regulates the complete series-circuit current; the isolation transformer electrically isolates and supplies the individual fitting.

What happens if the primary series cable opens?

The series loop is broken, so all fittings on that circuit go out and the CCR's open-circuit protection normally shuts it down.

Why is CCR monitoring important?

It lets faults and abnormal operating conditions be detected and reported.

Final Study Takeaway

FINAL STUDY TAKEAWAY

The CCR is the heart of the electrical side of a conventional AGL series circuit. Remember five things: current is the controlled parameter; it supplies the series circuit; current settings give brightness levels; a monitored CCR provides monitoring; and it must be considered as part of the whole system of cable, isolation transformer, connector, fixture, control system and photometry.

AGL CCR knowledge = Current + Load + Voltage + Brightness + Monitoring + Control + Testing + Safety.
