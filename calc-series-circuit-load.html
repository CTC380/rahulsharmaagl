Calculator #13

Series Circuit Load

Total connected load on a series AGL circuit — fixtures, isolating transformer losses and primary cable I²R loss, with an approximate CCR output voltage and loading check.

About this calculator

A series circuit is driven by a constant current regulator (CCR), so the CCR sees the sum of every fixture's power, the isolating transformer losses that come with it, and the loss in the primary cable itself. This tool adds those three parts, reports kW and kVA, and compares the result with a standard CCR size.

Method & Reference

How the load is built up

Every step is shown so the result can be checked by hand.

1. Fixture load

Sum of quantity times rated watts for each fixture type at full brightness.

P_fixtures = Σ ( n × W )

2. Isolating transformer losses

Each fixture is fed through an isolating transformer. Dividing by efficiency gives the power the primary circuit must supply.

P_tr = P_fixtures ÷ η

The default 90 % is an editable planning value. Use the transformer data sheet at the actual load, because efficiency falls when a transformer is lightly loaded.

3. Primary cable loss

The cable carries the full series current, so its loss is I² times total loop resistance, corrected for temperature.

R = R₂₀ × [1 + 0.00393 × (T − 20)] × L ÷ 1000
P_cable = I² × R

4. Total, kVA and voltage

P = P_tr + P_cable    S = P ÷ PF    V ≈ S ÷ I

The voltage is an approximation for quick checking. It ignores secondary cable and connector losses and assumes the power factor entered.

What this tool does not do

It does not size cable for voltage or insulation limits, model brightness steps, or account for secondary cable, connectors or lamp-out conditions. Dedicated CCR capacity, circuit voltage and cable tools are on the toolkit roadmap.

FAQ

Series Circuit Load Calculator: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on total connected load on a series AGL circuit and the CCR check.

What does the Series Circuit Load calculator do?

It adds up the total connected load on a series AGL circuit, which is the fixtures, the isolating transformer losses and the primary cable I squared R loss. It reports kW and kVA, gives an approximate CCR output voltage, and compares the result with a standard CCR size.

How is the load built up?

The fixture load is the sum of quantity times rated watts at full brightness. The transformer losses come from dividing the fixture load by the transformer efficiency, and the default of 90 percent is an editable planning value. The cable loss is the current squared times the loop resistance, corrected for temperature. The total real power is the transformer-side load plus the cable loss.

How are kVA and the output voltage worked out?

The apparent power is the total real power divided by the load power factor, and the approximate output voltage is that kVA divided by the circuit current. The voltage is an approximation for quick checking. It ignores secondary cable and connector losses and assumes the power factor entered.

How does the CCR check work?

The tool compares the circuit kVA with the CCR nameplate rating, which is the conservative reading, and finds the smallest CCR that fits at your maximum loading. The maximum loading is your project's own design rule, not a standard value. FAA AC 150/5345-10H lists CCR sizes of 1, 2, 4, 7.5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 50 and 70 kW, where 50 and 70 kW are replacement-only. The 2.5 and 5 sizes are IEC and manufacturer sizes rated in kVA.

Which circuit current and cable length should I enter?

Enter the full-brightness series current, which is 6.6 A for a standard FAA Class 1 CCR and 20 A for Class 2 circuits. The cable length is the full series loop, out and back, which is the total primary cable in the circuit. Leave it at 0 to ignore cable loss.

Can you give a worked example?

100 fixtures of 45 W give 4,500 W, and at 90 percent transformer efficiency the load is 5,000 W. Over 4,000 m of #8 AWG at 20 degrees C the cable resistance is 8.244 ohms and the loss at 6.6 A is 359 W. The total is 5,359 W, which is 5.64 kVA at a power factor of 0.95, with an approximate output voltage of 855 V. On a 7.5 kVA CCR the loading is 75 percent.

What does the tool not do?

It does not size cable for voltage or insulation limits, model brightness steps, or account for secondary cable, connectors or lamp-out conditions.

Is the result a final design load?

No. It is a calculated load at full brightness and not a final design load. Lightly loaded transformers, such as those on low-wattage LED fixtures, are less efficient than the default. Final design shall be verified against manufacturer data for the fixtures, isolating transformers, cable and CCR selected for the project.
