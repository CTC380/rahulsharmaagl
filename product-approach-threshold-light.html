Product Overview

Approach Threshold Light

What It Is

Approach threshold lights form the threshold-light element of an airport approach lighting system, giving a clear visual indication of the runway threshold where the approach-light field meets the runway.

The threshold is where the aircraft transitions from the approach lighting environment to the runway visual environment, so the array is an important reference alongside approach centreline, crossbar, side-row and runway lighting.

Selected According To

  • Runway width and threshold configuration
  • Approach category and lighting system type
  • Centreline, crossbar and side-row arrangement
  • Threshold geometry and fixture spacing
  • Light colour, optical direction, intensity
  • Mounting arrangement
  • Electrical circuit and control arrangement
  • Project technical specification

The final configuration should be based on the approved approach-lighting system design and applicable aerodrome requirements.

System Context

Approach Threshold vs Runway Threshold Light

Approach Threshold Light

The threshold-light element associated with a configured approach-lighting system.

Runway Threshold Light

The runway visual-aid component identifying the threshold within the runway lighting system.

On many airports these functions are physically integrated and use the same green array — FAA MALSR places the threshold array within the approach light field. The product specification should therefore identify the system in which the threshold lights are used.

FAA MALSR

FAA MALSR Threshold Light

MALSR Incorporates

  • Threshold lights
  • Steady-burning white light bars
  • Sequenced flashers

The threshold array consists of 18 to 33 aviation-green steady-burning lights, depending on runway width, in a line at and parallel to the threshold.

Place in the MALSR

The threshold array and the seven centreline light bars together form the approach light field. The five sequenced flashers continue outward at 200 ft intervals, starting at 1,600 ft from the threshold.

Runway width and system configuration are key selection parameters. Take the exact requirement from the current approved FAA system drawing and specifications.

FAA ALSF-2

FAA ALSF-2 Threshold Light

ALSF-2

A high-intensity approach lighting system for Category II/III instrument approaches. The threshold array is integrated with green threshold lights, white steady-burning centreline elements, red side-row lights and sequenced flashers.

Typical System

FAA describes a typical ALSF-2 system with 49 green threshold lights in the complete array.

Select the threshold array as part of the approved ALSF-2 configuration, not as a standalone product.

System Relationships

Centreline, Crossbar, Side Rows and Runway Threshold

Approach Centreline

The centreline gives longitudinal alignment; the threshold array gives threshold identification and transition. Keep them visually balanced at all applicable intensity settings.

Approach Crossbar

Approach Crossbar → Centreline → Threshold Light → Runway. The threshold array is the final major transverse reference before the runway visual environment.

Side-Row Lighting

In Category II/III systems the side rows and threshold lights form one visual pattern (ALSF-2: green threshold, red side rows, white centreline, sequenced flashers). Coordinate with side-row and centreline geometry.

Runway Threshold Light

Where one physical fixture serves both approach and runway threshold functions, the specification should identify the applicable system and standard.

Standards & References

Governing Standards

ICAO

The ICAO Store lists Annex 14, Volume I, 9th Edition, July 2022, including Amendment 18 (effective 4 August 2025; aerodrome-design and visual-aids provisions applicable from 27 November 2025, ground handling from 26 November 2026, obstacle limitation surfaces from 21 November 2030). Annex 14 specifies threshold lights as fixed unidirectional green in the approach direction. Evaluate the threshold-light arrangement as part of the applicable approach-lighting system, coordinated with centreline, crossbar, side-row and runway threshold provisions. ICAO also sets minimum threshold-light quantities and spacing by runway category; take these from Annex 14 Chapter 5 rather than from this page.

FAA

MALSR: threshold array of 18 to 33 aviation-green steady-burning lights, depending on runway width, at and parallel to the threshold.

ALSF-2: green threshold lights, red side-row lights, white high-intensity approach lights and sequenced flashers.

AC 150/5345-46F is the active FAA specification for runway and taxiway light fixtures; its L-850E is the in-pavement unidirectional threshold light for use with a medium-intensity approach lighting system. FAA Order JO 6850.2C (Visual Guidance Lighting Systems, August 2022) gives the installation criteria for approach lighting systems such as MALSR and ALSF-2. Derive the exact fixture, electrical configuration, spacing, photometry and approvals from the specified approach system and current FAA project documentation.

FAQ

Approach Threshold Light — Frequently Asked Questions

What is an approach threshold light?

Approach threshold lights form the threshold-light element of an approach lighting system, giving a clear visual indication of the runway threshold where the approach-light field meets the runway. The threshold is where the aircraft transitions from the approach lighting environment to the runway visual environment, so the array is an important reference alongside the approach centreline, crossbar, side-row and runway lighting.

What colour are approach threshold lights?

Approach threshold lights are aviation green, and FAA MALSR and ALSF-2 both use green threshold lights. Annex 14 specifies threshold lights as fixed unidirectional green in the approach direction. Evaluate the fixture for green colour and chromaticity, intensity and beam distribution, visibility and uniformity across the threshold, and compatibility with the approach lighting system.

What is the FAA MALSR threshold arrangement?

The threshold array consists of 18 to 33 aviation-green steady-burning lights, depending on runway width, in a line at and parallel to the threshold. Together with the seven centreline light bars it forms the approach light field, and the five sequenced flashers continue outward at 200 ft intervals, starting at 1,600 ft from the threshold. Take the exact requirement from the current approved FAA system drawing and specifications.

What is the FAA ALSF-2 threshold arrangement?

ALSF-2 is a high-intensity approach lighting system for Category II/III instrument approaches. Its threshold array is integrated with green threshold lights, white steady-burning centreline elements, red side-row lights and sequenced flashers, and FAA describes a typical ALSF-2 system with 49 green threshold lights in the complete array. Select the threshold array as part of the approved ALSF-2 configuration, not as a standalone product.

How many threshold lights are needed?

There is no single universal number: runway width plus approach system plus approved layout determines the threshold light quantity. FAA MALSR uses 18 to 33 green lights depending on runway width, and ALSF-2 uses 49 in a typical system. ICAO sets minimum threshold-light quantities and spacing by runway category, which should be taken from Annex 14 Chapter 5.

How does an approach threshold light differ from a runway threshold light?

An approach threshold light is the threshold-light element associated with a configured approach lighting system, while a runway threshold light is the runway visual-aid component identifying the threshold within the runway lighting system. On many airports these functions are physically integrated and use the same green array, since FAA MALSR places the threshold array within the approach light field, so the specification should identify the system in which the threshold lights are used.

Which FAA fixture type is used for approach threshold lights?

AC 150/5345-46F is the active FAA specification for runway and taxiway light fixtures, and its L-850E is the in-pavement unidirectional threshold light for use with a medium-intensity approach lighting system. FAA Order JO 6850.2C (August 2022) gives installation criteria for approach lighting systems such as MALSR and ALSF-2. Derive the exact fixture, electrical configuration, spacing, photometry and approvals from the specified approach system and current FAA project documentation.

How are approach threshold lights powered and controlled?

Potential configurations include a dedicated approach-lighting circuit or series circuit, a CCR-fed circuit with a local power supply, isolating transformers and LED drivers, and a control cabinet with intensity control and an ALCMS interface. Control functions include on/off and intensity selection, approach-system activation, remote control and centralized monitoring, and fault indication. FAA approach systems use coordinated approach-lighting controls, not an unrelated runway circuit.

How should the threshold array be installed and aligned?

The array should form a clean, consistent transverse reference. Verify alignment to the threshold line and centreline, fixture spacing and runway width, outer-light position, the relationship to the runway edge lights and approach centreline, and the optical axis, together with fixture elevation, frangibility where applicable and cable routing. Coordinate with runway pavement and threshold marking, drainage, cable trenches, junction boxes and foundations; approved civil and electrical drawings control all installation dimensions.

What are the common faults in approach threshold lighting?

Typical symptoms are the threshold array completely off, an individual light off, an uneven threshold appearance, the wrong colour and a misaligned array. Troubleshoot in sequence: approach control system, CCR or power, primary circuit, transformer or driver, secondary connection, threshold fixture, light source, optical assembly, alignment and photometry. For a system-level problem also verify the threshold line, runway centreline, runway edge, approach centreline, crossbar and side rows.

What should be confirmed before ordering approach threshold lights?

Confirm the runway designation and width, threshold location, approach category, approach lighting system and configuration (MALS, MALSF, MALSR, ALSF or ICAO), number of threshold lights, spacing and array length, green colour and light-source technology, intensity steps, optical distribution, the relationship to centreline, crossbar, side rows and runway edge, mounting and frangibility, and electrical and control details. Request the threshold-array drawing, photometric report, green chromaticity data and compliance documents.

Engineering Note

Approach threshold lights should be selected as part of the complete approach-lighting system, not simply as a generic green light.

For FAA systems the threshold arrangement differs materially between configurations such as MALSR and ALSF-2: MALSR uses an aviation-green steady-burning array whose quantity varies with runway width, while ALSF-2 uses a larger green array integrated with white centreline lighting, red side rows and sequenced flashers. For ICAO-based projects, the applicable approach category and Annex 14 configuration should control the final arrangement.

Select the final product from the approved approach-lighting layout, applicable governing standard, manufacturer technical data and project-specific requirements.
