Product Overview

Mandatory Instruction Sign

What They Are

Mandatory instruction signs are safety-critical visual aids communicating restrictions and holding requirements within the aircraft movement area. Under ICAO terminology they include runway designation signs, Category I/II/III holding-position signs, runway-holding position signs, road-holding position signs and NO ENTRY signs, all with a white inscription on a red background.

For FAA projects the equivalent family is Type L-858R under AC 150/5345-44L, the current active specification for lighted and unlighted runway and taxiway signs.

Design From the Approved Message

Design each sign from the approved sign message and location, not solely by overall physical dimensions. The configuration is determined by the applicable aerodrome standard, operational layout and approved sign schedule.

Sign Characteristics

Red Background, White Legend

ICAO Configuration

  • White inscription on red background
  • Appropriate mandatory instruction message
  • Positioned relative to the holding position or prohibited area
  • Illuminated where required by operating conditions

ICAO identifies the red background as the colour reserved for mandatory instruction signs on the movement area.

FAA L-858R Configuration

AC 150/5345-44L identifies L-858R signs as a white legend on a red background, with the applicable black outline configuration around the white legend.

Select exact size, style, class, electrical configuration and construction according to the FAA specification and project requirements.

Colour

Red Background, White Legend, Optional Black Outline

Colours

  • Background: red
  • Legend: white
  • Outline: black around the white inscription where required

Outline Provisions

ICAO provides for a black outline where environmental or other factors make enhanced conspicuity necessary. FAA L-858R signs specify a black outline around the white legend in the applicable configuration.

Illumination

Legibility at Night and in Poor Visibility

Illumination Matters During

  • Night operations
  • Poor visibility
  • Low ambient light
  • Reduced contrast conditions
  • Adverse weather

ICAO Guidance

Movement-area signs should be illuminated where the airport is designed for use in poor visibility, maintaining legibility and recognizable background colour. Follow the applicable standard and airport lighting design.

Retroreflective or Illuminated

When ICAO Requires an Illuminated Sign

ICAO Annex 14

Signs must be retroreflective, illuminated, or both, to the requirements of Annex 14. An illuminated sign is required where the runway is intended for use in RVR below 800 m, and at night in association with instrument runways, and with non-instrument runways of code number 3 or 4. Confirm the exact wording and the amendment in force for your project.

Brightness Steps

Illuminated signs on a series circuit are fed through an isolating transformer and follow the CCR brightness steps, so sign luminance tracks the runway and taxiway lighting setting. Colour, chromaticity and luminance of the sign face must meet the standard, because a bright sign with the wrong red is still non-compliant.

LED Illuminated Mandatory Signs

Internal Illumination Using LED Technology

Potential Advantages

  • Efficient electrical operation
  • Consistent illumination

Compliance Is Not Automatic

LED technology alone does not establish compliance. The complete sign must meet applicable requirements for optical performance, colour, luminance, electrical interface, environmental performance and construction.

Support height and position should maintain aircraft clearance and viewing geometry. ICAO guidance calls for lightweight, frangibly designed signs mounted low enough to preserve clearance with overhanging parts of the critical aircraft.

Installation Considerations

Location, Orientation, Viewing and Marking Coordination

Do not consider signage independently of the approved airfield marking and lighting layout.

Location

Install at the approved location relative to the holding position, runway, taxiway or prohibited area. Incorrect positioning reduces effectiveness even when the sign is correctly manufactured.

Orientation

Face the intended viewing direction; for runway holding positions, toward approaching aircraft.

Viewing Distance

  • Aircraft cockpit height
  • Taxiway geometry and sign location
  • Character height
  • Ambient illumination
  • Night and poor visibility

Marking Coordination

  • Runway-holding position marking
  • Taxiway centreline
  • Runway designation
  • Intermediate holding position
  • ILS/MLS protected-area markings

FAQ

Mandatory Instruction Sign — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a mandatory instruction sign?

Mandatory instruction signs are safety-critical visual aids that communicate restrictions and holding requirements within the aircraft movement area, marking locations beyond which movement is restricted unless the appropriate authorization or clearance has been received. Under ICAO terminology they include runway designation signs, Category I, II and III holding-position signs, runway-holding position signs, road-holding position signs and NO ENTRY signs, all with a white inscription on a red background.

What is an FAA L-858R sign?

For FAA projects the equivalent family is Type L-858R under AC 150/5345-44L, the specification for lighted and unlighted runway and taxiway signs. It identifies L-858R as a white legend on a red background, with the applicable black outline around the white legend. Sign layout and location rules are in AC 150/5340-18H, and the size, style, class and electrical configuration come from the current FAA specification.

What does ICAO Annex 14 say about mandatory instruction signs?

ICAO identifies the red background as the colour reserved for mandatory instruction signs, and provides for a black outline where environmental or other factors make enhanced conspicuity necessary. Sign location, inscription, dimensions, illumination and mounting are developed from Annex 14 Volume I Chapter 5 on signs, Appendix 4 on sign design, Appendix 1 on colours and Chapter 9 on siting of equipment on operational areas.

When must a mandatory instruction sign be illuminated?

ICAO Annex 14 requires an illuminated sign where the runway is intended for use in RVR below 800 m, at night in association with instrument runways, and at night with non-instrument runways of code number 3 or 4. Confirm the exact wording and the amendment in force for your project.

Where are mandatory instruction signs used?

Typical locations are runway holding positions, Category I, II and III holding positions, taxiway and runway intersections, runway and runway intersections, ILS/MLS protected-area holding positions, road-holding positions where vehicle roads meet movement areas, and NO ENTRY locations at the beginning of the prohibited area.

How are the legend and character height decided?

The message comes from the approved airport signage plan. Examples of message types are a runway designation such as 09-27, a category holding position such as 09 CAT II, a runway-holding position such as 27, and NO ENTRY. At an intermediate taxiway and runway intersection the sign shows both runway designators. Character height is never a universal value and depends on the governing standard, viewing distance, geometry and the approved sign schedule.

How is a mandatory sign powered?

Possible configurations are a series airfield lighting circuit through an isolating transformer, direct AC supply, or a project-specific supply with an integrated or remote power supply or LED driver. On a series circuit the sign follows the CCR brightness steps, so luminance tracks the runway and taxiway lighting setting. Colour, chromaticity and luminance must meet the standard, because a bright sign with the wrong red is still non-compliant. Never open, cut or disconnect an energised series circuit: de-energise at the regulator and use the site lock-out procedure first.

Do mandatory instruction signs need to be frangible?

Yes. ICAO guidance calls for lightweight, frangibly designed signs mounted low enough to preserve clearance with overhanging parts of the critical aircraft. The complete installation should satisfy the applicable frangibility requirements, which may involve frangible posts, frangible couplings, controlled breakaway interfaces and specified mounting arrangements.

How should mandatory instruction signs be installed?

Install at the approved location relative to the holding position, runway, taxiway or prohibited area, because incorrect positioning reduces effectiveness even when the sign is correctly manufactured. For runway holding positions the face should point toward approaching aircraft. Coordinate with the runway-holding position marking, taxiway centreline, runway designation, intermediate holding position and ILS/MLS protected-area markings.

What are the common problems with mandatory instruction signs?

A sign that is difficult to read at night may have reduced illumination, an LED failure, a dirty face or a driver fault, and uneven illumination can come from LED module failure or optical problems. An incorrect colour appearance should be checked against the approved configuration and colour requirements, and physical damage from vehicle impact, aircraft contact, weather or corrosion needs inspection of the housing, support and frangible components before return to service.

What should be provided for a mandatory instruction sign quotation?

Provide the airport and project, the governing standard, the sign type, the exact legend, the quantity, the character height and required sign size, whether it is illuminated or unlighted, the LED or specified light source, the electrical supply and series-circuit requirements, the support type and frangible requirements, the environmental requirements and the installation location. A sign drawing or approved sign schedule is particularly useful for custom or multi-panel configurations.
