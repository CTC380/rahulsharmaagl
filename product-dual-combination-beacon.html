Product Overview

Dual / Combination Beacon

What It Is

Dual or combination beacons combine multiple obstruction-lighting functions within a coordinated system, for towers, communication masts, industrial structures, tall infrastructure and other aviation obstacles.

A combination beacon may integrate different lighting characteristics into one assembly, simplifying equipment installation while maintaining the required obstruction-lighting functions. It can reduce layout, cabling and maintenance effort versus separate units where the project permits.

May Combine Different

  • Light intensities and light colours
  • Flash characteristics and operating modes
  • Day/night functions
  • Control states and monitoring functions

Obstacle Light Types

Mode and Colour Follow the Light Type

Light typeMode and colour (ICAO Annex 14 Ch. 6)Typical use
Low-intensity, Types A and BSteady (fixed) redLower levels of a structure, and low obstacles
Medium-intensity, Type AFlashing white (about 20,000 cd by day and twilight, 2,000 cd at night)Day, twilight and night marking of structures
Medium-intensity, Type BFlashing redNight marking; often paired with Type A as a dual system
Medium-intensity, Type CSteady redNight marking of extensive objects
High-intensity, Types A and BFlashing white (about 200,000 cd Type A or 100,000 cd Type B by day, 20,000 cd at twilight, 2,000 cd at night)Tall structures; day and twilight marking, reduced at night

Dual Lighting Systems

  • White flashing in day and twilight, red at night is a common dual arrangement (FAA uses red/white dual systems)
  • Changeover is made by a photocell or controller at defined ambient light levels
  • All lights on one structure should flash together unless the design states otherwise
  • Failure of a light should raise an alarm, and the authority should be told if it cannot be restored quickly

Engineering Note

Intensity values, number of levels and spacing come from the approved obstruction-lighting assessment. Medium and high-intensity levels differ between ICAO and FAA, so confirm the standard used for the project.

FAQ

Dual / Combination Beacon — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a dual or combination beacon?

A dual or combination beacon combines multiple obstruction-lighting functions in one coordinated assembly for towers, communication masts, industrial structures, tall infrastructure and other aviation obstacles. It may integrate different light intensities and colours, flash characteristics and operating modes, day and night functions, and control and monitoring functions. Where the project permits, it can reduce layout, cabling and maintenance effort compared with separate units.

What are the obstacle light types and their colours?

Under ICAO Annex 14 Chapter 6, low-intensity Types A and B are steady red. Medium-intensity Type A is flashing white, about 20,000 cd by day and twilight and 2,000 cd at night, Type B is flashing red and Type C is steady red. High-intensity Types A and B are flashing white, about 200,000 cd for Type A or 100,000 cd for Type B by day, 20,000 cd at twilight and 2,000 cd at night. The mode and colour follow the light type.

What is a dual lighting system?

A common dual arrangement is white flashing light in day and twilight and red at night, and the FAA uses red/white dual systems. The changeover is made by a photocell or controller at defined ambient light levels. All lights on one structure should flash together unless the design states otherwise, and the control philosophy should be established during project engineering.

How is the required intensity decided?

The approved aviation obstruction-lighting assessment determines it. It can depend on structure height and location, the aviation obstacle classification, day and night visibility requirements, the applicable regulations, surrounding lighting and the required conspicuity. Intensity should not be selected solely by structure height, and medium and high-intensity levels differ between ICAO and FAA, so confirm the standard used for the project.

Which structures use dual or combination beacons?

They are considered for communication towers and telecommunications masts, tall industrial structures, utility and transmission structures, airport-adjacent infrastructure, elevated platforms and major installations. They also suit towers needing multiple modes or coordinated intensity arrangements, new installations, replacement and modernization projects, and systems requiring integrated monitoring and control. The final configuration comes from the approved obstruction-lighting assessment and applicable aviation requirements.

How are flash and synchronization handled?

Flash characteristics, such as duration, interval and repetition rate, are configuration-dependent and should be confirmed during technical selection. Synchronization can coordinate multiple beacons, upper and intermediate lighting levels, separate obstruction-lighting assemblies, and local and central controllers. Depending on the project, it may use a dedicated timing arrangement or another approved reference.

What monitoring and automatic control can a combination beacon have?

Monitoring can cover light-source and individual lighting-mode status, power, controller, flashing and communication status, fault indication and remote alarm output, and it can be integrated with an existing SCADA, building-management or airport infrastructure system. Automatic control can include day/night switching with a photocell input, mode selection, flash control, synchronization, enable/disable, fault reporting and remote status indication. A failed light should raise an alarm, and the authority should be told if it cannot be restored quickly.

What electrical, mounting and environmental points need attention?

Consider the supply voltage, AC/DC configuration, power consumption, circuit, surge and lightning protection, cable routing, connectors, earthing and the control and monitoring interfaces, and identify the supply and control needs of each function. Mounting on tower platforms, mast tops, brackets or rooftop structures must hold the optical orientation under site wind and vibration and allow access for inspection and maintenance. Select enclosure, optics, fasteners and mounting materials for wind, rain, dust, solar radiation, temperature variation and, where relevant, coastal or industrial corrosion.

What are the common faults and checks?

If one lighting function is not operating, check its supply, controller and light source. If the whole beacon is out, check the supply, protection and controller. An incorrect operating mode points to mode-selection logic, incorrect flashing to the flash settings and unsynchronized beacons to the timing or communication system. Reduced optical output suggests a dirty or damaged optical surface, moisture inside the enclosure suggests a seal or cable-entry problem and mechanical movement suggests loose mounting hardware. An active monitoring alarm should be reviewed against the system status.

Can a combination beacon replace legacy obstruction-lighting equipment?

Modernization may consolidate separate legacy equipment, but a combination beacon should be selected only where its integrated functions satisfy the complete approved obstruction-lighting requirement. The retrofit assessment should consider the existing lighting classifications and beacon positions, required intensity, colour and flash characteristics, structure geometry, existing power supply and cable configuration, control system, monitoring interfaces, synchronization and current aviation requirements.

Which standards apply to combination beacons?

Review against ICAO Annex 14 Vol. I Chapter 6 on obstacle lighting and Doc 9157 Part 4, FAA AC 70/7460-1 (edition 1N issued 11 Aug 2026, cancelling 1M) together with AC 150/5345-43J on obstruction lighting equipment, and DGCA India CAR Section 4 and aviation authority NOC conditions. Also review the approved project documentation, current authority criteria and any national regulation, airport standard or engineering specification. Requirements vary by structure type, location, lighting classification and authority.

What information is needed to select a combination beacon?

Provide the structure type, height and location, the applicable aviation authority, the existing obstruction-lighting configuration, and the required lighting modes, intensity levels, colours, steady or flashing operation and flash characteristics. Also provide the number of beacon positions, the available power supply, any existing control system, synchronization and monitoring requirements, the mounting arrangement, environmental conditions and whether it is a new installation or a retrofit.
