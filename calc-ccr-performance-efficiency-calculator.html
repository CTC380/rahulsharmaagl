About this calculator

A constant current regulator is judged on what it takes in, what it delivers and how steady the output current stays. Enter the readings from the power analyser and the output test, set the limits from your specification, and this tool works out the rest, including heat released into the CCR room and the current deviation at each load point and brightness step.

Method & Reference

How the test readings are worked up

Every step is shown so the result can be checked by hand.

1. Input side

Apparent power comes from voltage and current, with a √3 factor on a three-phase supply. The power factor is real power over apparent power.

S_in = V × I (×√3) ÷ 1000    PF_in = P_in ÷ S_in

2. Output side

S_out = V_out × I_out ÷ 1000    P_out = S_out × PF_load

3. Efficiency and loss

η = P_out ÷ P_in    Loss = P_in − P_out

The loss all becomes heat in the CCR room. One kilowatt is about 3,412 BTU per hour, which is useful when sizing cooling.

4. Regulation and steps

Each measured current is compared with its nominal value. Pass or fail uses the tolerance in amperes, since the limit is an absolute current rather than a percentage. The spread is the gap between the highest and lowest load-point current, as a percentage of the rated current.

Deviation = (I_meas − I_nom) ÷ I_nom × 100

What this tool does not do

It does not measure harmonics, ripple or waveform shape, and it assumes the readings are true RMS. The limits are placeholders until you enter your specification values, and a CCR's rated size, step currents and test conditions come from its data sheet and the applicable standard.

FAQ

CCR Performance / Efficiency Calculator: Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on how the calculator works up CCR test readings for FAT and SAT.

What does the CCR Performance / Efficiency Calculator do?

It works up test readings from a constant current regulator for factory and site acceptance testing. From the input and output readings it calculates input and output kVA, power factor, efficiency, total loss, loading and load impedance. It also checks load regulation and brightness-step currents against limits you enter, and it produces a PDF test report.

How are CCR efficiency and loss calculated?

Output real power is output kVA times the load power factor, and efficiency is output real power divided by input real power. Loss is input real power minus output real power. The result depends on meter accuracy, waveform distortion and the load type, so record the instrument class with the test.

How is input power factor worked out?

Input apparent power is voltage times current divided by 1,000, multiplied by the square root of 3 on a three-phase supply. Power factor is the input real power from your power analyser or wattmeter divided by that apparent power. For a three-phase supply, enter the line-to-line voltage.

How does the load regulation and step current check work?

Each measured current is compared with its nominal value, and the result passes or fails against a tolerance in amperes, because the limit is an absolute current and not a percentage. FAA AC 150/5345-10 (edition F onward) allows plus or minus 0.1 A on a 6.6 A CCR and plus or minus 0.3 A on a 20 A CCR. Confirm this in the edition named in your specification. The regulation spread is the gap between the highest and lowest load-point current as a percentage of rated current.

Are the acceptance limits built in?

No. Minimum efficiency, minimum input power factor and output current tolerance are placeholders until you enter the figures from your specification or the CCR data sheet. Selecting a CCR class and step set loads the nominal step currents and the usual tolerance, which you can edit if your specification differs.

Can you give a worked example?

A 10 kW CCR fed at 230 V and 45 A single-phase draws 10.35 kVA and 9.85 kW, a power factor of 0.952. With 1,400 V at 6.6 A and a power factor of 1.00, the output is 9.24 kW. That gives an efficiency of 93.8 percent, a loss of 0.61 kW, which is about 2,081 BTU per hour of heat into the room at 3,412 BTU per kW, and a loading of 92.4 percent.

What does the calculator not cover?

It does not measure harmonics, ripple or waveform shape, and it assumes the readings are true RMS. A CCR's rated size, step currents and test conditions come from its data sheet and the applicable standard. The nameplate rating in kW is treated as kVA.

Can I use the result as the final acceptance decision?

No. It is an engineering reference tool. Acceptance shall be judged against the project specification and the CCR manufacturer's test procedure. The acceptance summary and PDF report record the checks and the limits you entered, so those limits must come from your own documents.
