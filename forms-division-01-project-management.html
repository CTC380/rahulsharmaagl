Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about the 25 Division 01 project management forms, how they are grouped, and how to use them.

Q1What is Division 01 — Project Management Forms in the AGL Engineering Forms & Templates library?

Division 01 is a set of 25 project management forms (PMG-01 to PMG-25) that record an Airfield Ground Lighting (AGL) project from first kickoff to final handover. The forms are grouped into three categories: Project Initiation, Project Execution, and Risk & Planning. Every form follows the same 15-field skeleton used across the library, so a form filled on one project reads the same way on the next.

Q2Which forms are included in Project Initiation?

Project Initiation has 10 forms: Project Information Sheet (PMG-01), Project Kickoff Form (PMG-02), Project Scope Form (PMG-03), Project Objective Sheet (PMG-04), Project Organization Chart (PMG-05), Project Contact Register (PMG-06), Project Responsibility Matrix (PMG-07), Project Document Register (PMG-08), Project Milestone Register (PMG-09), and Project Schedule (PMG-10). Together they set the baseline: who is involved, what is in and out of scope, the objectives, the documents, the milestones and the programme.

Q3Which forms cover Project Execution and progress reporting?

Project Execution has 10 forms: Material Approval Form (PMG-11), Inspection Request (PMG-12), Site Visit Report (PMG-13), Daily Progress Report (PMG-14), Weekly Progress Report (PMG-15), Monthly Progress Report (PMG-16), Work Progress Tracker (PMG-17), Meeting Minutes (PMG-18), Action Item Register (PMG-19), and Project Communication Register (PMG-20). They record approvals, inspections, site progress, meetings, open actions and formal correspondence while the work is under way.

Q4What is the difference between the daily, weekly and monthly progress reports and the Work Progress Tracker?

The Daily Progress Report (PMG-14) is the daily record of manpower, equipment, activities and quantities executed on site. The Weekly Progress Report (PMG-15) rolls the daily reports up into a weekly summary of planned versus actual progress and issues. The Monthly Progress Report (PMG-16) is the client-facing summary of overall progress, safety, quality, finance and forecast. The Work Progress Tracker (PMG-17) is different in kind: it is the live, activity-level register showing planned quantity versus executed quantity and percent complete for every WBS item.

Q5What is the difference between a Risk Assessment, a Method Statement and a Work Permit Request?

The Risk Assessment (PMG-21) identifies the hazards of an activity, rates the risk, and records the controls applied before work is authorized. The Method Statement (PMG-22) is the step-by-step how-to for the activity, covering sequence, resources, applicable standards and safety precautions, and it references the related risk assessment. The Work Permit Request (PMG-23) is the site-level permit that authorizes a specific activity for a specific time window, including hot work, electrical, height, confined space and airside work.

Q6What does the Site Readiness Checklist confirm before an activity starts?

The Site Readiness Checklist (PMG-24) is a go or no-go check confirming that everything needed is in place before an activity starts. Its items cover access or security clearance, material availability, equipment and tools availability, permits in place, manpower mobilized, drawings and method statement available, and safety arrangements, ending in a go / no-go decision.

Q7What must be completed before an AGL project can be formally handed over?

The Project Handover Checklist (PMG-25) is the final gate before the project is closed. It confirms that the as-built drawings, O&M manuals, test and commissioning certificates, spares handed over, training completed, punch list closure, warranty documents and final acceptance sign-off are all complete.

Q8Can the forms in Division 01 be downloaded and filled in?

Yes. Each form has a Download as Word button that produces an uncontrolled sample Word template with letterhead, a document-control block, ruled entry fields and a three-tier approval table (Prepared By, Reviewed By, Approved By) as layout only, to be adapted under your own document-control system before use. Checklist-style forms such as PMG-24 and PMG-25 download with a checkbox and a verified-by line for each item. The forms can also be searched by keyword or form ID.

Q9How does Division 01 connect to the SOP Library and to other divisions?

The SOP Library tells the engineer what to do, while this division tells them what to record while doing it. Several registers here, including the Document Register, Risk Assessment and Work Permit Request, are also referenced from the airside safety and quality-control SOPs, so a record filled once can be reused wherever it is called for. Related divisions are Division 09 (Site Safety Forms), Division 20 (Documentation & Records Control), Division 22 (Audit & Management Review Forms) and Division 23 (Project Handover & Closeout Forms).
