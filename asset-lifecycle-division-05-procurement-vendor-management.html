Procurement & Vendor Management

The commercial and supply-chain gateway between lifecycle design and the physical acquisition of AGL assets. Division 04 establishes what the asset should be designed to achieve over its lifecycle; Division 05 ensures that the equipment actually procured can meet those lifecycle expectations through product selection, technical specification, supplier qualification, quality control, warranty provisions, spare-parts availability, documentation, factory testing, lifecycle-cost evaluation and vendor performance management.

Initial Issue

The question this division answers — Can the equipment actually procured meet the lifecycle expectations set in Division 04, through appropriate product selection, vendor and manufacturer evaluation, quality control, warranty provisions, spare-parts strategy, documentation, factory testing and lifecycle-cost evaluation?

Where Division 05 Sits in the Lifecycle

Division 05 stays focused on acquisition and supplier accountability — it does not decide the design basis (Division 04) and it does not run installation, commissioning or maintenance (Division 06 onward). It is the natural transition from design intent to actual equipment acquisition, and the contractual foundation for every division that depends on the equipment being fit for its full service life.

PRO-01 Lifecycle-Based Procurement
Purpose

Lifecycle-based procurement means selecting and purchasing AGL assets based on their complete expected lifecycle, rather than simply selecting the lowest initial purchase price.

Procurement Should Consider
  • Initial purchase cost
  • Installation requirements
  • Expected service life
  • Reliability
  • Maintainability
  • Energy consumption
  • Spare-parts requirements
  • Maintenance cost
  • Warranty
  • Vendor support
  • Obsolescence
  • Replacement cost
  • Disposal implications
Basic Principle
Procure for lifecycle performance, not simply procurement price
Connection

The procurement decision should connect directly to the lifecycle requirements established in Division 04 — Design.

PRO-02 Product Selection
Purpose

Product selection is the technical process of determining which AGL product is suitable for the intended application.

Selection Should Consider
  • Required function
  • Technical performance
  • Applicable standards
  • Environmental suitability
  • Service life
  • Reliability
  • Maintainability
  • Compatibility
  • Interchangeability
  • Spare-parts availability
  • Manufacturer support
  • Obsolescence
  • Lifecycle cost
AGL Examples

Product selection may involve: runway lights, taxiway lights, approach lights, PAPI, airfield signs, CCRs, isolation transformers, connectors, primary cables, secondary cables and ALCMS equipment.

Important Principle

The selected product should satisfy the approved design basis, not merely appear technically similar.

PRO-03 Technical Specification
Purpose

The technical specification converts the lifecycle requirements into procurement requirements that suppliers must satisfy.

Specification Should Address
  • Equipment description
  • Functional requirements
  • Performance requirements
  • Electrical requirements
  • Mechanical requirements
  • Environmental requirements
  • Interface requirements
  • Reliability requirements
  • Maintainability requirements
  • Service-life expectations
  • Testing requirements
  • Documentation requirements
  • Warranty requirements
  • Spare-parts requirements
  • Obsolescence requirements
Important Principle

A specification should be sufficiently clear that suppliers can be evaluated against objective technical requirements.

PRO-04 Expected Service Life
Purpose

Expected service life should be included as a procurement requirement where applicable.

Procurement Documentation Should Establish
  • Minimum expected service life
  • Manufacturer's design-life statement
  • Major component life
  • Operating assumptions
  • Maintenance assumptions
  • Replacement assumptions
  • Supporting evidence
Verification

The procurement team should not rely solely on a supplier's general statement such as “Long service life.” Evidence may include manufacturer technical documentation, product lifecycle information, historical performance data, reference installations, product testing and manufacturer declaration.

PRO-05 Reliability Requirements
Purpose

Reliability requirements establish the expected ability of the equipment to perform its function consistently.

Procurement Evaluation May Consider
  • Reliability history
  • Failure rates
  • MTBF, where applicable
  • Product maturity
  • Reference installations
  • Failure modes
  • Component quality
  • Environmental performance
  • Manufacturer quality systems
  • Warranty/failure history
Supplier Evidence

The supplier may be required to provide reliability data, test evidence, product history, reference installations, failure information and reliability commitments where contractually appropriate.

Connection

Reliability requirements should be linked back to the criticality assessment established in Division 03.

PRO-06 Maintainability Requirements
Purpose

The procurement should establish how easily the asset can be inspected, tested, maintained and repaired.

Consider
  • Access to components
  • Replaceable components
  • Diagnostic capability
  • Test points
  • Maintenance intervals
  • Special tools
  • Component availability
  • Repair procedures
  • Maintenance documentation
  • Technician training
Procurement Question
Can the airport's maintenance team realistically maintain this product throughout its expected service life?
Caution

A technically excellent product may still be unsuitable if maintenance support is impractical.

PRO-07 Vendor Evaluation
Purpose

Vendor evaluation determines whether the supplier is capable of delivering and supporting the required equipment.

Evaluation Criteria
  • Technical capability
  • Product experience
  • AGL experience
  • Quality capability
  • Manufacturing capability

  • Warranty support

Each vendor should be assessed using a controlled evaluation process rather than relying only on price.

PRO-08 Manufacturer Evaluation
Purpose

Manufacturer evaluation focuses specifically on the organization responsible for producing the equipment.

Assess
  • Manufacturing experience
  • Product history
  • Quality systems
  • Production capability
  • Product development
  • Testing facilities
  • Engineering support
  • After-sales support
  • Global/local support
  • Spare-parts capability
  • Product lifecycle management
  • Obsolescence policy
Important Distinction

Vendor may be the organization selling/supplying the equipment. Manufacturer is the organization producing it. Both may require separate evaluation.

PRO-09 Supplier Qualification
Purpose

Supplier qualification determines whether a supplier is approved to provide the required AGL equipment.

Qualification May Include
  • Company information
  • Technical capability
  • Quality system
  • Relevant experience
  • Product approvals
  • Manufacturing capability
  • Testing capability
  • Supply capacity
  • Financial/commercial assessment
  • References
  • Warranty capability
  • Spare-parts capability
  • Technical support
Qualification Status

A controlled system could identify suppliers as: Approved, Conditionally Approved, Under Evaluation, Not Approved or Suspended. Supplier qualification should be periodically reviewed.

PRO-10 Product Quality Assessment
Purpose

Product quality assessment verifies that the proposed product is capable of meeting the required quality level.

Assess
  • Manufacturing quality
  • Material quality
  • Component quality
  • Assembly quality
  • Electrical quality
  • Mechanical quality
  • Optical quality
  • Environmental performance
  • Testing
  • Traceability
  • Quality-control processes
Evidence

Possible evidence includes quality certificates, test reports, inspection records, factory QA records, product qualification reports and factory acceptance testing.

Objective

The objective is to avoid accepting a product based solely on catalogue specifications.

PRO-11 Warranty Requirements
Purpose

Warranty requirements should be established before procurement, not negotiated only after failure.

Procurement Should Define
  • Warranty duration
  • Warranty start date
  • Covered equipment
  • Covered components
  • Defect definition
  • Labour coverage
  • Replacement obligations
  • Repair obligations
  • Transportation responsibility
  • Response time
  • Restoration requirements
  • Warranty exclusions
  • Claim process
AGL-Specific Consideration

For critical AGL equipment, warranty requirements should be aligned with asset criticality, failure consequence, expected service life and availability requirements.

PRO-13 Spare-Parts Availability
Purpose

Spare-parts availability should be assessed before purchasing the equipment.

Procurement Should Identify
  • Critical spares
  • Routine spares
  • Consumables
  • Long-lead parts
  • Manufacturer-specific parts
  • Strategic spares
  • Emergency replacement components
Questions
  • Is the part currently available?
  • What is the normal lead time?
  • What is the emergency lead time?
  • How long will the manufacturer support the part?
  • Is an alternative available?
  • Can the part be repaired?
  • Is local stock available?
Connection

This connects directly with the later Spare Parts & Inventory Lifecycle division.

PRO-14 Obsolescence Risk
Purpose

Procurement should evaluate whether the product is likely to remain supportable throughout its expected lifecycle.

Assess
  • Product age
  • Manufacturer support policy
  • Product roadmap
  • Component availability
  • Software support
  • Hardware support
  • Replacement availability
  • Communication protocols
  • Vendor dependency
  • End-of-life declarations
High-Risk Indicators
  • Discontinued product family
  • Manufacturer withdrawal
  • Proprietary obsolete components
  • Limited spare availability
  • No documented upgrade path
  • Unsupported software
  • Single-source dependency
Connection

This connects to a dedicated Obsolescence Management division covering product discontinuation, spare-part obsolescence, technology/software obsolescence and alternative-product evaluation.

Recommended AGL Procurement Evaluation Structure

For a major AGL procurement, evaluate suppliers through five sequential gates rather than price alone.

Gate 1 — Technical Compliance · Does the product meet the required specification?
Gate 2 — Lifecycle Performance · Does it meet service-life, reliability and maintainability expectations?
Gate 3 — Supply & Support · Can the supplier provide spares, documentation, warranty and technical support?
Gate 4 — Lifecycle Economics · Is the total cost of ownership acceptable?
Gate 5 — Lifecycle Risk · Are obsolescence, supply-chain, vendor and technology risks acceptable?

Most Important Design Principle

Division 05 should remain the commercial and supply-chain gateway. It converts the lifecycle design intent set in Division 04 into a supportable, procured asset — not the design basis itself, and not the installation, commissioning or maintenance activity that follows once the equipment arrives on site.

The procurement decision must acquire not merely an AGL product, but a supportable lifecycle asset. Technical compliance, expected service life, reliability, maintainability, quality, spares, warranty, supplier support, documentation, obsolescence control, lifecycle cost and vendor performance together define a lifecycle-ready procurement — this is the contractual foundation for every division that follows, covering installation/handover, commissioning, maintenance, spare parts, warranty, obsolescence, lifecycle cost and vendor performance.

Division 04 — DESIGN
Division 05 — PROCURE & MANAGE VENDORS
Division 06 onward — INSTALL → COMMISSION → OPERATE → MAINTAIN → MONITOR → OPTIMIZE → REPLACE → DISPOSE

This is why the framework places Division 05 immediately after Division 04: Division 04 decides what the asset must be designed to achieve; Division 05 ensures the equipment actually acquired — and the supplier behind it — can deliver on that promise for the asset's full service life.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Procurement & Vendor Management in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide.

Q1What is Division 05 — Procurement & Vendor Management in the AGL Asset Lifecycle Guide?

Division 05 is the commercial and supply-chain gateway between lifecycle design and physical acquisition. It covers 20 topics on lifecycle-based procurement, product selection, technical specification, expected service life, reliability, maintainability, vendor and manufacturer evaluation, supplier qualification, product quality, warranty requirements and management, spare-parts availability, obsolescence risk, supplier support, technical documentation, factory testing, procurement lifecycle cost, total cost of ownership and vendor performance monitoring.

Q2What is lifecycle-based procurement for AGL equipment?

It means selecting and purchasing AGL assets based on their complete expected lifecycle rather than simply the lowest initial purchase price. It weighs purchase cost, installation requirements, service life, reliability, maintainability and energy use.

Q3What should an AGL technical specification include?

The technical specification converts lifecycle requirements into procurement requirements that suppliers must satisfy. It covers the equipment description and functional, performance, electrical and mechanical requirements, and should also state expected service life, reliability and maintainability requirements where applicable.

Q4How do vendor evaluation, manufacturer evaluation and supplier qualification differ?

Vendor evaluation determines whether the supplier can deliver and support the required equipment. Manufacturer evaluation focuses on the organization that produces the equipment, including its quality systems and product history. Supplier qualification determines whether a supplier is approved to provide the required AGL equipment.

Q5Why should warranty requirements be set before procurement?

Warranty requirements should be established before procurement rather than negotiated only after a failure. They define duration, start date, covered equipment and components, defect definition and labour cover, and once equipment is purchased the warranty must be actively managed.

Q6How does procurement address spare parts and obsolescence?

Spare-parts availability should be assessed before buying the equipment, covering critical spares, long-lead parts and manufacturer-specific parts. Procurement should also evaluate whether the product is likely to remain supportable throughout its expected lifecycle, considering product age, manufacturer support policy, roadmap and component availability.

Q7Why are technical documentation and factory testing procurement deliverables?

Technical documentation such as datasheets, manuals and wiring diagrams should be a contractual procurement deliverable. Factory testing then verifies that equipment meets specified requirements before shipment or installation, including functional, electrical, protection, control and alarm performance.

Q8What is total cost of ownership in AGL procurement?

Total Cost of Ownership provides a broader financial assessment of an asset than the purchase price. It includes initial procurement, installation, energy, planned and corrective maintenance and spares. Vendor performance should also keep being tracked after the purchase order is awarded.
