Standards & Certification Comparison

FAA VS IEC

FAA standards are aviation-specific requirements for airport lighting equipment and systems, while IEC standards are international electrical/engineering standards covering the design, construction, testing and safety of electrical equipment. For AGL, they are complementary, not substitutes: FAA asks "is this equipment suitable for airport lighting application?" while IEC asks "is this electrical equipment designed and tested to the applicable international technical standard?"

IEC 61823, for example, specifically covers AGL series transformers for 6.6 A series circuits, up to 5 kV service voltage and CCR applications up to 30 kVA.

02 · Definitions

What Is FAA & IEC?

FAA

FAA has a dedicated airport-lighting document structure built around Advisory Circulars and equipment specifications. AC 150/5340 covers design and installation, AC 150/5345 covers equipment specifications, Engineering Briefs address specific technical topics, and AC 150/5345-53 covers equipment certification.

Main functions

  • Airport / aeronautical application suitability
  • Photometric performance requirements
  • Equipment-specific specifications
  • Certification via ALECP

IEC

IEC standards cover electrical equipment, transformers, cables, connectors, insulation, environmental testing, EMC, safety, protection, measurement, lighting and electronic equipment. The IEC system is much broader than aviation, though an important exception is that IEC does have standards written specifically for aerodrome lighting, such as IEC 61823 for AGL series transformers.

Main functions

  • Electrical/technical design standardization
  • Insulation, isolation and safety testing
  • International conformity framework
  • Component-level technical performance
03 · Key Difference

Application vs Technology

The easiest way to remember the difference between FAA and IEC:

FAA

Application-specific — "Is this suitable for airport lighting?"

IEC

Technology/equipment-specific — "Does this electrical equipment meet the applicable technical requirements?"

Engineering point: Equipment flows into both an IEC technical standard and an FAA aviation application requirement, and both feed the final project compliance requirement.

04 · IEC Is Broader

Where IEC Standards Apply

IEC standards cover areas well beyond aviation, including:

  • Electrical equipment
  • Transformers
  • Cables
  • Connectors
  • Insulation
  • Environmental testing
  • EMC
  • Safety
  • Protection
  • Measurement
  • Lighting
  • Electronic equipment

An important exception within this broad scope is that IEC does have standards written specifically for aerodrome lighting, such as IEC 61823 for AGL series transformers.

05 · Example: Isolation Transformer

Where FAA and IEC Work Together

IEC 61823 specifies characteristics of AGL series transformers used in 6.6 A series circuits. It addresses their use with CCR-fed AGL circuits and the isolation between primary and secondary circuits. FAA, on the other hand, may specify the transformer or associated airport-lighting equipment according to the applicable FAA/project requirements.

Layered requirement: IEC 61823 (transformer technical standard) + FAA requirement (airport application) + project specification (exact compliance requirement).

06 · Example: Fixture, Connector & CCR

Where FAA Standards Are Primary

Runway light fixture

FAA has a specific equipment specification — AC 150/5345-46F, Specification for Runway, Taxiway, Heliport, and Vertiport Light Fixtures — establishing photometric, mechanical, electrical and environmental requirements. An IEC standard may address particular electrical, environmental, EMC or safety characteristics, but IEC compliance alone does not establish FAA airport-lighting compliance.

L-823 connector

FAA has a dedicated specification — AC 150/5345-26E — covering connectors used with underground airport-lighting power cables, isolation-transformer leads and fixture leads. A connector can satisfy a general IEC electrical connector standard and still not automatically qualify as an FAA L-823 connector.

CCR

FAA also has a dedicated specification — AC 150/5345-10H — for Constant Current Regulators and Regulator Monitors, covering output current, control, protection and monitoring. IEC standards may be used for individual electrical components, insulation, EMC and safety, but the AGL system requirements still need to be satisfied.

07 · Photometry

FAA Has the Advantage

For airport lighting, photometry is highly application-specific — candela, beam distribution, horizontal and vertical distribution, colour and required aviation performance. FAA specifications define the required photometric characteristics for particular airport fixtures.

For runway/taxiway light photometry, FAA requirements should be checked directly where FAA compliance is specified. A generic IEC lighting standard should not be assumed to replace the FAA photometric requirement.

08 · Electrical Testing

IEC Has a Strong Role

IEC standards are particularly useful for detailed electrical engineering, including:

  • Insulation
  • Dielectric strength
  • Temperature rise
  • EMC
  • Environmental testing
  • Protection
  • Component safety

Where the applicable IEC standard covers the equipment, it can provide a robust technical testing framework. But the final acceptance requirement should be based on the project's specified standard hierarchy.

09 · Certification

FAA Certification vs IEC Test Reports

FAA has the Airport Lighting Equipment Certification Program (ALECP), which allows FAA-approved third-party certifiers to evaluate and certify airport-lighting equipment and license suppliers to mark products meeting FAA specifications.

IEC test certification and FAA equipment qualification are not the same thing. An IEC test report demonstrating IEC standard compliance does not automatically equal FAA qualification. If the project requires FAA-certified equipment, the appropriate FAA qualification/certification route must be followed.

11 · Worked Example

Specifying a 6.6/6.6 A Transformer

Suppose you are specifying a 6.6/6.6 A isolation transformer. A strong technical specification could read:

AGL series isolation transformer shall comply with the applicable requirements of IEC 61823 and the project/FAA requirements applicable to the airport lighting system.

IEC 61823 specifically addresses AGL series transformers used in 6.6 A series circuits and their electrical isolation function. This is much stronger than simply stating "transformer shall be IEC compliant" because it identifies the specific AGL IEC standard.

12 · Worked Example

LED Runway Light Compliance

An LED runway light's compliance rests on several layers working together:

  • FAA fixture specification
  • FAA photometry
  • FAA installation requirements
  • IEC electrical tests
  • IEC EMC/environmental tests
  • Project-specific requirements

FAA also maintains Engineering Brief 67 for light sources other than incandescent and xenon in airport and obstruction lighting fixtures. An LED fixture's compliance should be demonstrated against the actual applicable FAA specification/EB and project requirements, not merely by presenting an IEC LED test report.

13 · Working Together

Can FAA and IEC Both Be Used?

Yes — in fact, this is often the best approach. For an AGL transformer, IEC 61823 covers transformer technical performance, the FAA requirement covers airport application, and the project specification governs final acceptance.

Combined result: International technical standard + aviation-specific requirement + project requirement.

14 · Common Misconceptions

What You Should Not Do

Incorrect: "IEC compliant, therefore FAA compliant"
Fact

IEC compliance addresses the technical/electrical standard; it does not by itself establish FAA airport-lighting application compliance.

Incorrect: "FAA compliant, therefore IEC compliant"
Fact

FAA compliance addresses aviation application suitability; it does not by itself establish compliance with a specific IEC technical standard.

Incorrect: "FAA/IEC certified" without naming the exact document
Fact

A credible submittal names the exact standard — e.g. "Complies with FAA AC 150/5345-46F and applicable IEC standards specified in the project requirements" — not just "FAA" or "IEC."

18 · Engineering Verdict

Which Standard Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

FAA compliance and IEC compliance should be treated as complementary requirements unless the project specification explicitly makes one standard sufficient. FAA tells you whether the equipment is suitable for the required airport-lighting application; IEC tells you whether the equipment meets the applicable international technical/electrical standard.

The most important point for technical documentation is to name the exact standard — not just "FAA" or "IEC."

FAAApplication-specific — is this equipment suitable for airport lighting?
IECTechnology-specific — does this equipment meet the applicable international standard?

19 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is IEC compliance the same as FAA compliance?

No. IEC compliance addresses the technical/electrical standard for the equipment; FAA compliance addresses whether the equipment is suitable for the specific airport-lighting application. IEC compliance alone does not establish FAA compliance.

Can equipment be both FAA and IEC compliant?

Yes, and this is often the best approach — for example, an AGL transformer complying with IEC 61823 for its technical performance and with the applicable FAA/project requirements for its airport application.

Does an IEC test report satisfy FAA certification requirements?

Not automatically. IEC test certification and FAA equipment qualification are not the same thing. Where FAA-certified equipment is required, the appropriate FAA qualification/certification route (such as ALECP) must be followed.

Does IEC have any AGL-specific standards?

Yes. IEC 61823 specifically covers AGL series transformers for 6.6 A series circuits, addressing electrical characteristics, insulation, isolation and performance/testing.

Which standard governs photometry for runway and taxiway lights?

FAA specifications should be checked directly for photometric requirements where FAA compliance is specified. A generic IEC lighting standard should not be assumed to replace the FAA photometric requirement.

Is it acceptable to submit equipment as simply "FAA/IEC certified"?

No. A credible technical submittal names the exact FAA document and the exact IEC standard rather than making a generic claim of certification.

Governing Documents

Airport-lighting design/installation and equipment specifications, including fixtures, CCRs, L-823 connectors, L-824 cable, bases and transformer housings.

IEC 61823 & Related IEC Standards

AGL series transformers for 6.6 A series circuits, plus general IEC standards for electrical components, insulation, EMC, safety and environmental testing.

FAA ALECP

The Airport Lighting Equipment Certification Program — FAA-approved third-party certification and product marking for airport lighting equipment.
