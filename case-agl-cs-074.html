AGL-CS-074 — Photometry Failure After Maintenance

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A photometric investigation where routine maintenance introduced a beam alignment error — from electrical verification through optical assembly inspection, lens orientation assessment, torque verification, and corrective reassembly.

AGL-CS-074

Case Type

Engineering Simulation

Primary Skill

Important: This is an engineering simulation created for training. It is not presented as an actual airport incident. All readings, values, dates and outcomes are illustrative, and no airport, organisation, person or manufacturer is identified or implied.

1Case Study Overview

A runway edge light underwent routine preventive maintenance, including replacement of the optical module, cleaning of the optical chamber, seal replacement, and fixture reassembly.

The fixture was electrically tested after maintenance and returned to service successfully.

During the next scheduled photometric verification test, the fixture failed the required ICAO photometric intensity and beam-distribution requirements.

The fixture had been fully compliant before maintenance.

A detailed investigation was initiated covering optical assembly installation, lens orientation, reflector positioning, mechanical alignment, torque application, and post-maintenance beam verification.

The investigation identified incorrect optical assembly positioning during maintenance, resulting in beam-axis displacement and photometric non-compliance.

This case demonstrates that maintenance activities can create photometric failures even when electrical functionality is fully restored.

  • Improve maintenance quality-control procedures.

3System Background

Maintenance activities affecting photometry include:

  • Optical module replacement.
  • Lens replacement.
  • Reflector replacement.
  • Seal replacement.
  • Housing reassembly.
  • Fixture leveling.
  • Optical cleaning.
  • Mechanical adjustment.

Common maintenance-induced errors include:

  • Incorrect lens orientation.
  • Reflector misalignment.
  • Optical-module rotation.
  • Improper seal compression.
  • Uneven tightening.
  • Fixture tilt.
  • Beam-axis displacement.

Photometric performance depends on optical geometry.

4Problem Statement

The reported condition was:

  • Maintenance completed.
  • Electrical operation normal.
  • CCR healthy.
  • Photometric test failed.
  • Beam distribution changed.
  • Pre-maintenance records acceptable.

7Maintenance-Induced Photometric Failure Principle

Correct maintenance → Correct optical geometry → Correct beam → Correct intensity → ICAO compliance
Incorrect maintenance → Optical misalignment → Beam displacement → Reduced measured intensity → Photometric failure

Maintenance quality directly affects beam direction and distribution.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The photometry failure after maintenance was caused by incorrect optical assembly installation, including:

  • Optical-module rotation.
  • Incorrect lens orientation.
  • Reflector misalignment.
  • Uneven fastener torque.

The electrical system remained healthy.

The primary failure mechanism was maintenance-induced optical geometry error.

11Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Removed the optical assembly.
  2. Reinstalled the optical module correctly.
  3. Corrected lens orientation.
  4. Realigned the reflector.
  5. Applied specified torque.
  6. Verified optical reference marks.
  7. Performed beam-alignment verification.
  8. Conducted photometric testing.
  9. Updated maintenance procedures.
  10. Implemented post-maintenance photometric verification.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: Fixture maintained successfully. Electrical operation normal. Photometric test failed immediately after maintenance. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace the CCR.
B. Increase current.
C. Verify optical assembly orientation, lens installation, reflector positioning, fastener torque, beam alignment, and repeat photometric testing.
D. Replace the transformer.
Correct answer: C. A photometric failure immediately after maintenance with healthy electrical operation points to a maintenance-induced optical geometry problem. Optical assembly verification and realignment will identify the cause without unnecessary component replacement.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about maintenance-induced photometric failure and post-maintenance verification.

Q1What is AGL-CS-074?

AGL-CS-074 is an engineering training case study. It covers a runway edge light that passed electrical checks after maintenance but then failed photometric verification because of incorrect optical assembly installation. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2Can maintenance itself cause a photometric failure?

Yes. The fixture was compliant before maintenance and electrically healthy afterwards, yet failed the next photometric test because the optical geometry was installed incorrectly.

Q3What changed between pre- and post-maintenance testing?

Peak intensity fell from 52,600 cd to 41,800 cd. The beam centre shifted, horizontal distribution narrowed and vertical distribution became depressed.

Q4What was wrong with the optical assembly?

The optical module was rotated about 2°, the locator pin was partially seated, and the reference mark was misaligned. The lens had the wrong rotational orientation and the reflector was slightly displaced with uneven seating.

Q5Did fastener torque matter?

Yes. Three fasteners were at or near the specified 8 Nm, but fastener C measured only 4.2 Nm, giving uneven mounting pressure.

Q6Why did the fixture pass electrical checks but fail photometry?

Electrical tests confirm the light operates, not that the beam points correctly. The failure was a maintenance-induced optical geometry error, not an electrical fault.

Q7How was the cause confirmed?

A controlled reassembly with correct installation raised intensity from 41,800 cd to 52,300 cd, a 25.1% increase, and returned the beam distribution to the required envelope.

Q8What corrective actions were taken?

The team reinstalled the optical module correctly, corrected lens orientation, realigned the reflector and applied specified torque. They verified reference marks and beam alignment, performed photometric testing and implemented post-maintenance photometric verification.

Q9What is the key lesson from this case?

After optical maintenance, verify photometry as well as electrical operation. Follow reference marks, locator pins and torque specifications exactly.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

Photometry failure after maintenance is often a maintenance-quality problem caused by incorrect optical assembly installation, lens orientation errors, reflector misalignment, and mechanical reassembly defects, and electrical functionality alone cannot confirm restoration of ICAO-compliant photometric performance.

Systematic optical assembly verification, alignment checks, torque verification, beam-distribution testing, and post-maintenance photometric acceptance testing can prevent maintenance-induced photometric failures and ensure reliable airfield lighting performance.

In AGL engineering, maintenance is complete only when both the current and the beam are correct.
