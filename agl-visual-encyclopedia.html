Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about using the AGL Visual Encyclopedia.

Q1What is the AGL Visual Encyclopedia?

A diagram-first reference to Airfield Ground Lighting, where each entry covers a single engineering concept — drawn, labelled and cross-linked to the rest of the Knowledge Hub. It's organized into 28 divisions and a planned 776 topics.

Q2How many entries are live right now?

776 entries are published so far, covering airfield layout, runway and taxiway lighting, approach lighting, visual guidance, signage, series circuits, CCR and power supply, isolation transformers, installation, maintenance, testing and commissioning, safety, fixture construction, photometry, airfield lighting control, asset lifecycle, troubleshooting, airfield civil interface, standards and compliance, future AGL technology, engineering concepts, inspection and product comparison and selection. The remaining topics are shown as planned (unlit) entries across the divisions, including new divisions on obstruction lighting, beacons and weather sensing, navaids and airfield sensors, and heliport lighting.

Q3What does the 'Lit' and 'Unlit' status mean?

A lit entry is published and can be opened. An unlit entry is planned but not yet written.

Q4How do I find a specific topic?

Use the search box to search across all 776 planned topics, or toggle 'Live entries only' to show just the 776 published entries. You can also browse by division.

Q5Is the Visual Encyclopedia a substitute for the actual ICAO or FAA standards?

No. The diagrams are original explanations of the requirements for learning purposes, and fixture counts, spacing and layouts shown are illustrative rather than to scale. Verify against the current ICAO, FAA or applicable national standard for any real project.

Q6How is the Visual Encyclopedia different from the Component Library or Glossary?

The Encyclopedia is the visual layer, explaining how a system works through a diagram. The Component Library covers individual parts and specifications, the Glossary covers terminology, and other Knowledge Hub references go deeper on comparisons, failures and paperwork.
