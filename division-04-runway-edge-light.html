EDG-161 Runway Edge Light Not Working

Symptom — One or more runway edge lights fail to illuminate when the runway edge circuit is energised and the CCR confirms normal output current at the commanded intensity step. Entry-point triage topic used to establish scope before proceeding to EDG-162/163/164.

Equipment / System

Elevated omnidirectional or inset unidirectional/bidirectional runway edge light, isolating transformer (typically 6.6/6.6A or 200W/45W series-circuit type), series circuit loop fed from the runway edge CCR.

Possible Causes
HighFailed lamp/LED module within the affected fixture(s)
HighFailed or open-circuit isolating transformer serving the affected fixture
MediumBroken or corroded primary/secondary cable connector at the transformer
MediumOpen or high-resistance joint in the series circuit loop upstream of the affected fixture(s)
LowerFixture internal wiring damaged by moisture, heat, or mechanical impact
LowerFixture disconnected or removed for maintenance and not yet reinstated on record
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meter (series circuit rated)Insulation resistance tester (5kV)Series circuit loop test setFixture wiring diagramInsulated hand toolsTorch/inspection light
Safety Precautions
  • Runway edge series circuits operate at high voltage (up to several kV) under load — never open a transformer connector or fixture while the circuit is energised.
  • Coordinate with ATC/airside operations before approaching the runway for inspection; obtain a runway inspection/works clearance.
  • Apply LOTO at the CCR before any circuit continuity or insulation testing.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationFrom the CCR/ALCMS, confirm the series circuit is energised and the regulated current matches the commanded intensity step — this confirms the circuit itself is live.
  2. ObservationConduct a visual runway inspection (or review the latest daily/periodic inspection record) to identify exactly which fixture(s) are dark and their position/circuit number.
  3. DecisionSingle fixture dark → proceed to EDG-162 (Single Edge Light Failure). Multiple fixtures in a run dark → proceed to EDG-163 (Multiple Edge Lights Failure). Entire circuit/runway dark → proceed to EDG-164 (Runway Edge Lights Completely Off).
  4. Fault LocationRecord the fixture position, circuit/loop identifier, and cable route reference to support the follow-on specific topic.
Test Points
CCR output current at commanded step
ALCMS lighting status feedback
Runway inspection log / fixture position record
Root Cause Verification

This topic is a triage entry point; verification is completed under the specific follow-on topic (EDG-162/163/164) once the fault scope has been confirmed.

Corrective Action
  • No corrective action at this stage — route to the applicable specific topic based on the observed scope of the failure.
Preventive Action
  • Maintain an up-to-date fixture position and circuit-loop reference map to speed scope triage.
  • Ensure daily/periodic runway inspection records capture exact fixture identifiers, not just general location.
Related References

EDG-162 Single Edge Light Failure

Symptom — Exactly one runway edge light in an otherwise normally illuminated series circuit fails to light, while adjacent fixtures on the same loop operate normally.

Equipment / System

Individual elevated or inset runway edge fixture and its dedicated isolating transformer, fed in series from the runway edge circuit loop.

Possible Causes
HighFailed LED module or lamp within the fixture
HighFailed isolating transformer (open primary or secondary winding)
MediumCorroded or disconnected primary connector (bayonet/plug-in type) at the transformer
MediumWater-damaged secondary cable or connector between transformer and fixture
LowerFailed LED driver (if fixture is LED-converted) with the lamp/module itself intact
LowerFixture base/riser internal wiring fault
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterInsulation resistance tester (5kV)Primary loop current injection test setTorque wrench (fixture base bolts)Spare isolating transformer (known-good)Spare LED module/lamp (known-good)
Safety Precautions
  • De-energise and LOTO the runway edge circuit at the CCR before disconnecting any primary connector.
  • Verify zero energy at the transformer primary with a proven meter before handling — series circuits can retain induced voltage.
  • Obtain airside/runway access clearance before working at the fixture location.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm only the single fixture is affected and adjacent fixtures on the same loop are operating normally, ruling out a loop-level fault.
  2. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated and LOTO applied, measure the isolating transformer secondary output using a known-good primary current injection test set or a portable series circuit tester.
  3. MeasurementIf the transformer output is present but the fixture does not light, test the LED module/lamp and driver (if fitted) directly.
  4. MeasurementIf the transformer output is absent, disconnect the primary connector and inspect for corrosion, moisture, or a broken pin; megger the transformer primary and secondary windings.
  5. DecisionTransformer output present, fixture dead → lamp/LED/driver fault. Transformer output absent, primary connector sound → transformer fault. Transformer output absent, primary connector damaged → connector/cabling fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the transformer, the primary connector, or the fixture lamp/LED/driver assembly.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific failed component by substitution with a known-good spare before closing out.
Test Points
Isolating transformer primary connector
Isolating transformer secondary output
Fixture lamp/LED module terminals
LED driver output (if fitted)
Fixture base wiring insulation resistance
Root Cause Verification

After replacement, re-energise the circuit and confirm the fixture lights at all intensity steps, and remains stable through a full step-up/step-down cycle.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed LED module/lamp.
  • Replace the failed isolating transformer.
  • Clean, dry, and reseat the primary connector, or replace if corroded/damaged.
  • Replace the failed LED driver.
  • Repair or replace damaged fixture base wiring.
Preventive Action
  • Include primary connector inspection and megger testing in the periodic runway edge light PM programme.
  • Maintain isolating transformer and LED module spares at site stores for rapid single-fixture restoration.
  • Log individual fixture/transformer failure history to identify recurring problem locations.
Related References

SOP: SOP-EDG-03 (Single Fixture Fault Isolation), SOP-EDG-05 (Isolating Transformer Test Procedure)

EDG-163 Multiple Edge Lights Failure

Symptom — Several runway edge lights in the same section of the series circuit are dark simultaneously, while the CCR still shows normal regulated output current for the loop overall.

Equipment / System

Runway edge circuit loop section, primary cable run, and multiple isolating transformer/fixture assemblies in that section.

Possible Causes
HighOpen or high-resistance joint in the primary series cable upstream of the affected fixtures, reducing/interrupting current to the downstream section
HighDamaged primary cable (cut, chafed, or water-ingressed splice) affecting a section of the loop
MediumCoincidental multiple independent transformer/fixture failures (statistically less likely but must be ruled out)
MediumFaulty or corroded L-connector/T-connector joint feeding the affected section
LowerPartial short or leakage path diverting current away from the affected section
LowerExcavation or trenching works in the vicinity that may have disturbed the primary cable
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterInsulation resistance tester (5kV) / cable fault locatorTime-domain reflectometer (TDR) if availableSeries circuit as-built/loop drawingCable route/duct drawingLOTO kit
Safety Precautions
  • De-energise and LOTO the full runway edge circuit before any cable joint or splice pit work.
  • Treat all affected cable sections as potentially live until proven de-energised, given possible partial short conditions.
  • Coordinate with airside operations and any concurrent civil works crews before excavation-related fault investigation.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationMap the exact fixtures affected and confirm they form a contiguous downstream section of the loop rather than being randomly distributed — this points toward a shared upstream cause.
  2. MeasurementWith the loop isolated, measure primary cable continuity and insulation resistance section by section, working from the last known-good fixture toward the first affected fixture.
  3. MeasurementWhere accessible, inspect splice pits/handholes in the affected section for water ingress, corrosion, or a physically damaged joint.
  4. MeasurementIf a TDR is available, use it to localise a suspected open or short in the primary cable to a specific cable length from a known reference point.
  5. DecisionOpen/high-resistance joint found → cable/joint fault at that location. Continuity and insulation normal at all joints → investigate individual transformers as coincidental multiple failures.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific splice pit, joint, or cable section responsible for interrupting the downstream section.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the physical cause — corrosion, water ingress, mechanical damage, or manufacturing defect — at the identified joint or cable section.
Test Points
Primary cable continuity, section by section
Splice pit/handhole joint condition
Insulation resistance at each accessible joint
TDR trace (if used)
Individual transformer output, if coincidental failure suspected
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-energise the loop and confirm all previously affected fixtures illuminate correctly at every intensity step, and re-megger the repaired section to confirm insulation resistance is within baseline.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the damaged primary cable section.
  • Remake the faulty splice/joint using an approved airfield-rated jointing kit.
  • Pump out and reseal a water-ingressed splice pit.
  • Replace any individual transformers confirmed faulty in addition to the cable fault.
Preventive Action
  • Include periodic insulation resistance testing of the full series circuit loop, section by section, in the PM programme.
  • Inspect and reseal splice pit lids/gaskets on a scheduled basis to prevent water ingress.
  • Coordinate with civil/works contractors before any excavation near known cable routes.
  • Maintain an accurate as-built cable route drawing, updated after any repair.
Related References

SOP: SOP-EDG-06 (Series Circuit Loop Insulation Testing), SOP-EDG-07 (Splice Pit Inspection Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30, FAA AC 150/5345-46, IEC 60364

EDG-164 Runway Edge Lights Completely Off

Symptom — All runway edge lights on a runway are dark; the fault scope extends to the entire series circuit rather than a single fixture or section.

Equipment / System

Runway edge CCR and its complete series circuit loop, including main isolator, primary feeder cable, and the full run of fixtures.

Possible Causes
HighCCR not producing output — refer to Division 01 (CCR Troubleshooting) for CCR-specific diagnosis
HighMain series circuit loop open — a complete break in the primary cable anywhere in the loop, interrupting current to the entire circuit
MediumRunway edge circuit not selected/energised from ALCMS or the local control panel
MediumCCR output breaker/isolator open or tripped downstream of the CCR itself
LowerCatastrophic loop-side short causing the CCR to trip on overcurrent/earth fault protection before reaching steady output
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meterInsulation resistance tester (5kV)ALCMS/local control panel accessSeries circuit as-built drawingLOTO kit
Safety Precautions
  • De-energise and LOTO the CCR output isolator before any loop-level testing.
  • A complete loop-off condition with a tripped CCR protection relay may indicate a hazardous short — do not repeatedly reset the CCR without first confirming loop insulation resistance is within safe limits.
  • Coordinate with ATC to confirm the runway lighting status and any operational impact before commencing fault-finding.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCheck the CCR front panel/ALCMS for run status, output current, and any active alarm or trip indication.
  2. DecisionCCR shows no output/fault indication → refer to Division 01 (CCR Troubleshooting) for the specific CCR fault topic before proceeding further.
  3. MeasurementIf the CCR confirms normal regulated output but the entire runway remains dark, measure current at the CCR output terminals and again at the first fixture in the loop to confirm current is actually leaving the CCR cabinet.
  4. MeasurementWith the loop isolated, megger the full series circuit loop to check for a low-resistance short that may be preventing normal circulation of current.
  5. DecisionOutput confirmed leaving the CCR but not reaching the first fixture → break in the feeder between CCR and the runway. Output present at the first fixture but the rest of the loop dark → loop break further along the run.
  6. Fault LocationProgressively test loop continuity moving along the runway from the CCR end to localise the specific break point.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the exact cable section, joint, or connector responsible for the complete loop interruption.
Test Points
CCR output terminals
CCR output isolator/breaker
Feeder cable from CCR to first fixture
Series circuit loop insulation resistance (full loop)
Progressive loop continuity at intermediate splice pits
Root Cause Verification

After repair, re-energise the full circuit under supervision and confirm normal current regulation and full runway illumination at every intensity step before returning the runway to service.

Corrective Action
  • Refer to and close out the applicable Division 01 CCR topic if the root cause is CCR-side.
  • Repair the specific cable break/joint identified in the loop.
  • Reset and re-energise the runway edge circuit from ALCMS/local panel once the fault is cleared.
  • Replace a failed CCR output isolator/breaker.
Preventive Action
  • Include full-loop insulation resistance testing as a scheduled PM activity, not only a reactive one.
  • Maintain clear escalation procedure between CCR-side and loop-side fault diagnosis to avoid delay in a total-outage scenario.
  • Ensure redundancy/backup CCR arrangements (where provided) are tested periodically.
Related References

SOP: SOP-EDG-08 (Total Runway Edge Outage Response), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR Isolation & LOTO Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30, FAA AC 150/5345-46

EDG-165 Edge Light Dim

Symptom — One or more runway edge lights appear visibly dimmer than adjacent fixtures at the same commanded intensity step, without being completely off.

Equipment / System

Runway edge fixture (LED module or lamp), isolating transformer, and the associated primary series circuit connection.

Possible Causes
HighDegraded LED module (partial LED string failure or end-of-life lumen depreciation) with the fixture still partially functional
HighPartially failed isolating transformer delivering reduced secondary output
MediumHigh-resistance primary connector causing a voltage/current drop to that fixture only
MediumDirty or hazed lens/optical surface reducing effective light output — see EDG-171/EDG-183/EDG-184 if optical fouling is confirmed
LowerIncorrect intensity step selected locally versus the commanded ALCMS step (fixture correctly reproducing a lower step than intended)
LowerAgeing lamp (legacy PAR56-type fixture) nearing end of rated life
Required Tools
Light output/luminous intensity meter or calibrated lux meterTrue-RMS clamp meterInsulation resistance testerReference known-good fixture for comparisonFixture wiring diagram
Safety Precautions
  • De-energise and LOTO before opening the fixture or transformer enclosure for internal inspection.
  • Use appropriate eye protection when taking close-range light output readings on an energised fixture from outside the enclosure.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationVisually compare the affected fixture against immediately adjacent fixtures at the same intensity step, ideally at night or in a darkened hangar test setup.
  2. MeasurementTake a luminous intensity/lux reading at a fixed distance and angle from the affected fixture and compare against a known-good reference fixture at the same step.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, measure isolating transformer secondary output and compare against nameplate rating.
  4. ObservationInspect the lens/optical surface for dirt, haze, or discolouration that could account for reduced output even with normal electrical supply.
  5. DecisionTransformer output normal, lens clean, output still low → LED module/lamp degradation. Transformer output below nameplate → transformer fault. Lens dirty/hazed → optical fouling, not an electrical fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the LED module/lamp, the isolating transformer, or the optical surface as the source of reduced output.
  7. Root CauseConfirm by substitution — swap in a known-good LED module or transformer and re-measure.
Test Points
Fixture luminous intensity output
Isolating transformer secondary output
Primary connector condition
Lens/optical surface condition
Root Cause Verification

After corrective action, re-measure luminous intensity output and confirm it is within the manufacturer-specified tolerance of a known-good reference fixture at the same intensity step.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the degraded LED module/lamp.
  • Replace the underperforming isolating transformer.
  • Clean the lens/optical surface if fouling is the confirmed cause.
  • Clean and reseat a high-resistance primary connector.
Preventive Action
  • Include periodic luminous intensity spot-checks against known-good reference fixtures in the PM programme.
  • Track LED module lumen output trend over time to plan proactive replacement before it drops below tolerance.
  • Include lens cleaning on a defined maintenance interval based on local environmental (dust/pollution) conditions.
Related References

SOP: SOP-EDG-09 (Light Output Verification Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30, FAA AC 150/5345-46, IES LM-79 (LED photometric testing reference)

EDG-166 Edge Light Too Bright

Symptom — One or more runway edge lights appear noticeably brighter than adjacent fixtures at the same commanded intensity step, or brighter than the expected output for that step.

Equipment / System

Runway edge fixture (LED module), isolating transformer, and local/step selection wiring where fitted.

Possible Causes
HighFixture responding to a higher intensity step than commanded, due to a local step-selection or transformer tap fault
MediumIncorrect isolating transformer tap setting (mismatched to the intended intensity step) fitted during a previous repair
MediumLED driver fault causing it to drive the LED module above its rated current
LowerWrong-rated LED conversion module fitted (higher output than the fixture/circuit was designed for) during a previous retrofit
LowerPerceived-only brightness difference due to a genuinely dim neighbouring fixture, not an actual fault on the reported fixture — cross-check against EDG-165
Required Tools
Light output/luminous intensity meter or calibrated lux meterTrue-RMS clamp meterFixture and transformer nameplate/spec referenceReference known-good fixture for comparison
Safety Precautions
  • De-energise and LOTO before opening any enclosure to check tap settings or driver configuration.
  • An LED module driven above its rated current can overheat — handle with care immediately after de-energisation.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the observation by comparing the reported fixture against multiple adjacent fixtures, not just one, to rule out the neighbouring-fixture-is-dim scenario.
  2. MeasurementTake a luminous intensity reading on the reported fixture and compare against the expected value for the commanded intensity step per manufacturer photometric data.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, check the isolating transformer tap setting/wiring against the design step assignment for that fixture.
  4. MeasurementIf an LED driver is fitted, measure driver output current and compare against the LED module's rated current.
  5. DecisionTransformer tap mismatched to design → tap/wiring error. Driver output above rated LED current → driver fault. Correct module/tap but still bright → wrong-rated module fitted previously.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the transformer tap/wiring, the LED driver, or an incorrectly specified LED module.
  7. Root CauseConfirm against the original design intensity-step assignment and the correct part number for that fixture position.
Test Points
Fixture luminous intensity output at commanded step
Isolating transformer tap/terminal configuration
LED driver output current
LED module part number/rating plate
Root Cause Verification

After correction, re-measure luminous intensity output at the commanded step and confirm it matches manufacturer photometric data within tolerance, and matches adjacent fixtures.

Corrective Action
  • Correct the isolating transformer tap setting to match the design intensity-step assignment.
  • Replace a faulty LED driver with a correctly rated unit.
  • Replace an incorrectly specified LED module with the correct part number.
Preventive Action
  • Verify transformer tap settings against the design record whenever a transformer is replaced.
  • Maintain a controlled parts list for LED conversion modules to prevent incorrect substitutions during maintenance.
  • Include intensity-step verification in post-maintenance commissioning checks.
Related References

SOP: SOP-EDG-09 (Light Output Verification Procedure), SOP-EDG-10 (Isolating Transformer Tap Verification)

EDG-167 Edge Light Flickering

Symptom — A runway edge light exhibits rapid, repeated fluctuation in light output (flicker) rather than a steady, stable illumination at the commanded intensity step.

Equipment / System

Runway edge fixture (LED module and driver), isolating transformer, and associated primary connections.

Possible Causes
HighLED driver fault or instability — failing internal components causing an unstable output to the LED module
HighLoose or intermittent primary connector making poor, fluctuating contact under vibration or thermal cycling
MediumLoose internal wiring connection within the fixture or transformer
MediumIsolating transformer with a developing internal fault (partial winding short) producing an unstable secondary output
LowerUpstream CCR output instability — refer to Division 01 if flicker is observed across multiple fixtures simultaneously
LowerMoisture ingress causing an intermittent leakage path
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meter with min/max/holdOscilloscope (for driver waveform analysis, if available)Insulation resistance testerTorque screwdriver setFixture wiring diagram
Safety Precautions
  • De-energise and LOTO before tightening or inspecting any connector suspected of causing intermittent contact.
  • Treat flicker accompanied by any unusual smell, heat, or discolouration as a potential fire risk — isolate immediately and inspect before re-energising.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm whether the flicker is isolated to a single fixture or observed across multiple fixtures on the same loop — the latter points toward a CCR-side cause (Division 01) rather than a local fixture fault.
  2. MeasurementWith the fixture accessible and circuit isolated, gently flex/wiggle the primary connector and internal wiring (while de-energised) to check for looseness, then re-energise briefly under controlled conditions to observe if flicker correlates with connector position.
  3. MeasurementIf an LED driver is fitted, check driver output for ripple/instability using a clamp meter with min/max hold or an oscilloscope if available.
  4. MeasurementMeasure isolating transformer secondary output stability under load.
  5. DecisionFlicker correlates with connector movement → loose connection. Driver output unstable independent of connections → driver fault. Transformer output unstable → transformer fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific loose connector, the LED driver, or the isolating transformer.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the failure mode — mechanical looseness, component degradation, or developing insulation fault — before closing out.
Test Points
Primary connector contact integrity
Internal fixture/transformer wiring terminations
LED driver output stability
Isolating transformer secondary output stability
Root Cause Verification

After corrective action, observe the fixture through at least one full intensity-step cycle and confirm stable, flicker-free operation with no recurrence over a sustained observation period.

Corrective Action
  • Clean, tighten, or replace the loose primary connector.
  • Re-terminate loose internal wiring connections.
  • Replace a faulty LED driver.
  • Replace a faulty isolating transformer.
Preventive Action
  • Include connector torque checks in the periodic PM programme, particularly for fixtures in high-vibration areas (near touchdown zones).
  • Track intermittent-fault fixtures across multiple reporting cycles to catch developing faults early.
  • Use vibration-rated, locking-type connectors where specified for the fixture type.
Related References

SOP: SOP-EDG-03 (Single Fixture Fault Isolation), SOP-EDG-11 (Intermittent Fault Investigation)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30, FAA AC 150/5345-46

EDG-168 Edge Light Intermittent

Symptom — A runway edge light operates normally most of the time but periodically goes fully off and back on, or fails only under certain conditions (e.g., temperature, vibration, weather), rather than exhibiting continuous flicker.

Equipment / System

Runway edge fixture and isolating transformer, together with the primary series circuit connection and cable route to that fixture.

Possible Causes
HighThermally sensitive connection — a marginal joint that opens as it heats up under load and recloses on cooling
HighWater ingress into the transformer or fixture causing an intermittent leakage/short that trips or interrupts the local supply under certain moisture conditions
MediumDeveloping LED driver fault that fails intermittently under thermal stress
MediumCable insulation damage causing an intermittent short/open depending on cable movement or moisture
LowerLoose fixture base connection affected by ground vibration (e.g., from aircraft movement) or frost heave
Required Tools
True-RMS clamp meter with data-logging capabilityInsulation resistance testerPortable data logger for extended monitoringThermal imaging camera (if available)Fixture and cable route drawing
Safety Precautions
  • De-energise and LOTO before any physical inspection of a suspected intermittent connection.
  • If water ingress is suspected as the intermittent trigger, do not attempt repeated re-energising to "test" the fault — investigate and dry the enclosure first.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationGather all available failure occurrence data — time of day, weather conditions, temperature, and any correlation with aircraft/vehicle movement — from maintenance logs or ALCMS event history.
  2. MeasurementInstall a data logger on the affected fixture circuit to capture current/voltage behaviour over an extended period, ideally spanning the conditions under which the fault has previously occurred.
  3. ObservationInspect the fixture and transformer for any sign of past moisture ingress (staining, corrosion, condensation residue) even if currently dry.
  4. MeasurementPerform insulation resistance testing under both dry and (if safely possible) simulated damp conditions to check for a moisture-dependent leakage path.
  5. DecisionFault correlates with temperature rise under load → thermal connection issue. Fault correlates with rain/high humidity → moisture ingress. No clear correlation → investigate driver/component-level intermittent fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific connection, moisture entry point, or component identified through the correlation analysis.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the root cause using the logged data and physical inspection findings together, rather than relying on a single test.
Test Points
Logged current/voltage over the observation period
Connector and joint condition (thermal and visual)
Insulation resistance, dry and (if applicable) damp condition
Fixture/transformer enclosure seal integrity
Root Cause Verification

After corrective action, continue data-logging monitoring across at least one full cycle of the conditions previously associated with the fault (e.g., a full day/night thermal cycle, or a rain event) to confirm no recurrence.

Corrective Action
  • Remake the thermally sensitive connection with a correctly rated, properly torqued terminal.
  • Dry, inspect, and reseal the enclosure if moisture ingress is confirmed; replace any moisture-damaged components.
  • Replace a developing-fault LED driver.
  • Repair or replace damaged cable insulation.
Preventive Action
  • Include thermal imaging of fixture/transformer connections in the periodic PM programme to catch marginal joints before they fail.
  • Verify enclosure gasket and seal condition on a defined maintenance interval.
  • Encourage systematic logging of intermittent fault conditions (time, weather) at first report to speed future correlation analysis.
Related References

EDG-169 Edge Light Incorrect Color

Symptom — A runway edge light displays a colour that does not match the expected white output (e.g., visibly yellow, blue-shifted, pink, or otherwise off-colour), or does not correctly show yellow in the final 600m caution zone where applicable.

Equipment / System

Runway edge fixture LED module (or, in older installations, lamp with a coloured filter/lens), fixture lens/filter assembly.

Possible Causes
HighDegraded or ageing LED module exhibiting colour shift as it nears end of life (common LED failure mode distinct from a full failure)
HighIncorrect LED module or filter fitted for that fixture position — e.g., a white module fitted in a caution-zone position requiring yellow, or vice versa
MediumDiscoloured or contaminated lens/filter (yellowing of the plastic from UV/heat exposure) altering the perceived colour
MediumPartial LED string failure within a multi-colour or multi-die module causing a colour imbalance rather than a simple brightness drop
LowerLED driver operating outside specification, affecting colour temperature on some driver/LED combinations
Required Tools
Colorimeter or chromaticity meter (if available)Reference known-good fixture at the same position typeFixture position/zone reference drawing (to confirm correct colour requirement)True-RMS clamp meter
Safety Precautions
  • De-energise and LOTO before removing the lens/filter or LED module for inspection or replacement.

HighImpact from a vehicle, mower, or aircraft ground equipment (elevated fixtures are particularly exposed to this risk)
HighImpact from a snowplough or snow-removal equipment during winter operations
MediumJet/prop blast-driven debris impact
MediumAircraft wheel loading directly over an inset fixture not rated for the applicable loading, causing frame deformation
LowerManufacturing defect or material fatigue leading to structural failure under normal operational loading
Required Tools
Torque screwdriver set / hand tools appropriate to fixture fixingReplacement fixture body/housing or complete fixture assemblyCamera for damage documentationStraightedge/level for frame flatness check (inset fixtures)
Safety Precautions
  • De-energise and LOTO before handling a damaged fixture — structural damage may have also compromised internal wiring insulation.
  • Treat a damaged elevated fixture as a FOD and safety hazard — assess whether immediate removal/temporary marking is required pending repair, per site safety procedure.
  • For inset fixtures with pavement-level damage, assess and coordinate any pavement surface hazard with airfield operations before leaving the location.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationDocument the extent, location, and likely cause of the body damage, including any associated pavement or surrounding surface damage.
  2. ObservationWith power isolated, inspect internal wiring and components for damage or displacement resulting from the impact, not just the visible external damage.
  3. MeasurementPerform insulation resistance testing on the fixture's internal wiring to rule out damage-induced insulation degradation even where internal components appear undamaged.
  4. ObservationFor inset fixtures, check frame flatness/level against the surrounding pavement to confirm correct seating is still achievable, or whether frame replacement is required.
  5. DecisionBody damage cosmetic/minor with sound internals and insulation → assess for repair versus replacement based on structural integrity. Body damage affecting fixture mounting, sealing, or internal component safety → replace the fixture body/complete assembly.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific damaged component — body/housing, mounting frame, or riser — and confirm whether the damage is repairable or requires replacement.
  7. Root CauseConfirm and record the likely cause to inform relevant preventive/coordination action (e.g., with mowing contractors or snow-removal operations).
Test Points
Extent and location of body/housing damage
Internal wiring/component condition and insulation resistance
Frame flatness/seating (inset fixtures)
Mounting/fixing point integrity
Root Cause Verification

After repair or replacement, confirm the fixture is securely mounted/seated, insulation resistance is within acceptable limits, and the fixture operates correctly through a full intensity-step cycle before returning to service.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the damaged fixture body/housing or complete fixture assembly.
  • Repair minor cosmetic damage where structural integrity and sealing are confirmed unaffected.
  • Replace damaged internal wiring identified during inspection.
  • Re-level/re-seat an inset fixture frame where pavement seating has been affected.
Preventive Action
  • Coordinate fixture location awareness with mowing, snow-removal, and ground-equipment operators to reduce impact risk.
  • Include structural condition as a specific item in routine visual inspection, particularly after any known ground equipment incident.
  • Consider more impact-resistant fixture types for locations with a history of repeated body damage.

EDG-188 Fixture Corrosion

Symptom — Visible corrosion (rust, oxidation, or pitting) is present on the fixture housing, mounting hardware, or internal metallic components, potentially affecting structural integrity, electrical continuity, or sealing performance.

Equipment / System

Fixture housing, mounting hardware, internal metallic brackets/terminals, and (for inset fixtures) the frame/base assembly.

Possible Causes
HighProlonged exposure to moisture (see EDG-177/178/179) combined with inadequate protective coating or galvanising
HighDe-icing/anti-icing fluid exposure, particularly glycol-based fluids which can be corrosive to certain metals over repeated exposure
MediumDissimilar-metal (galvanic) corrosion where incompatible metal fixing hardware has been used in contact with the fixture body
MediumCoastal/high-salinity airfield environment accelerating corrosion beyond the design assumption for the fixture's protective coating
LowerCoating/plating damage during a previous installation or maintenance visit, exposing base metal to the environment
Required Tools
Wire brush / corrosion removal toolsApproved anti-corrosion treatment/coatingInsulation resistance testerReplacement hardware (correct material grade)Camera for documentation
Safety Precautions
  • De-energise and LOTO before working on a corroded fixture, particularly where corrosion is near electrical terminals or wiring.
  • Use appropriate PPE when removing corrosion products, which may include fine particulates.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationAssess the extent and location of corrosion — surface-only, or affecting structural sections, mounting hardware, or electrical terminals.
  2. ObservationIdentify the fixing/mounting hardware material and check for evidence of dissimilar-metal contact that could indicate galvanic corrosion.
  3. MeasurementWhere corrosion is near electrical terminals, measure contact resistance/insulation resistance to confirm whether the corrosion has affected electrical performance.
  4. ObservationAssess whether corrosion is consistent with the general environmental exposure at this location (e.g., near a de-icing pad, coastal airfield) or appears localised/premature.
  5. DecisionSurface corrosion only, no structural/electrical impact → clean and treat. Corrosion affecting structural integrity or electrical terminals → component/hardware replacement required in addition to treatment.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific components affected and the level of severity — surface, structural, or electrical.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the corrosion mechanism (moisture, de-icing fluid, galvanic, coating damage) to guide the appropriate preventive/material selection response.
Test Points
Extent and depth of corrosion on housing/hardware
Electrical terminal contact resistance where corrosion is present near terminals
Fixing hardware material compatibility
General environmental exposure assessment for the location
Root Cause Verification

After treatment/replacement, confirm mechanical integrity of the affected component, and confirm electrical contact resistance/insulation resistance is within acceptable limits where terminals were involved.

Corrective Action
  • Remove surface corrosion and apply an approved anti-corrosion treatment/coating.
  • Replace structurally compromised housing or mounting components.
  • Replace corroded electrical terminals/hardware and re-terminate.
  • Replace dissimilar-metal fixing hardware with a compatible material grade.
Preventive Action
  • Specify corrosion-resistant materials/coatings appropriate to the local environment (coastal, de-icing exposure) for fixture and hardware replacement.
  • Include corrosion assessment as a specific PM checklist item, particularly for fixtures in known high-exposure areas.
  • Address the underlying moisture-ingress cause (EDG-177/178/179) promptly to limit ongoing corrosion risk.

EDG-189 Fixture Overheating

Symptom — The runway edge fixture housing, lens, or an internal component runs noticeably hotter than comparable fixtures, evidenced by discolouration, warping, a burning odour, or elevated temperature readings, without necessarily having failed outright yet.

Equipment / System

LED module/driver, lamp (legacy fixtures), heat sink/thermal management system, and fixture housing.

Possible Causes
HighBlocked or degraded heat sink preventing effective heat dissipation from the LED module/driver
HighLED driver configured to overdrive the module beyond its rated current, generating excess heat (see also EDG-172, EDG-174)
MediumPoor thermal interface (degraded or incorrectly applied thermal paste/pad) between the LED module and heat sink
MediumInadequate ventilation due to debris, dirt, or vegetation blocking the fixture's designed airflow path
LowerAmbient ground/ballast temperature at the fixture location running unusually high (e.g., dark pavement in direct summer sun) compounding an otherwise marginal thermal design
Required Tools
Thermal imaging camera (if available) or non-contact infrared thermometerTrue-RMS clamp meterFixture technical manual with rated operating temperature rangeCleaning tools for heat sink/ventilation path
Safety Precautions
  • De-energise and LOTO before handling a fixture confirmed or suspected to be overheating, and allow it to cool before close physical inspection.
  • Treat a fixture showing discolouration/burning odour as a potential fire risk — isolate immediately and do not re-energise until fully investigated.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationTake a surface/component temperature reading (thermal camera or infrared thermometer) and compare against a known-good reference fixture under the same operating and ambient conditions.
  2. ObservationWith power isolated and the fixture cooled, inspect the heat sink for blockage, debris, or physical damage.
  3. ObservationInspect the thermal interface between the LED module and heat sink for degradation, gaps, or incorrect application.
  4. MeasurementMeasure LED driver output current and compare against the LED module's rated current to rule out overdrive as a contributing cause.
  5. DecisionHeat sink blocked/degraded thermal interface found → thermal management fault, correct directly. Driver output above rated current → driver configuration/fault, correct per EDG-174. Heat sink and driver both normal → investigate ambient/environmental contribution.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to heat sink condition, thermal interface, driver overdrive, or ambient/environmental factors.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the dominant contributing factor(s) — overheating is frequently multi-causal (e.g., a marginal thermal interface combined with high ambient temperature).
Test Points
Component surface/case temperature versus reference fixture
Heat sink condition and airflow path
Thermal interface material condition
LED driver output current versus module rated current
Root Cause Verification

After corrective action, re-measure temperature under representative operating conditions (including peak ambient temperature if practical) and confirm the fixture runs within the manufacturer's rated operating temperature range.

Corrective Action
  • Clean and clear the heat sink and ventilation path of debris/blockage.
  • Replace degraded thermal interface material with correctly applied, correctly rated material.
  • Correct LED driver configuration to the module's rated current.
  • Consider a fixture/location-specific mitigation (e.g., shading, alternate part rating) where ambient conditions are a significant contributing factor.
Preventive Action
  • Include heat sink/ventilation path condition in the periodic PM programme.
  • Verify driver current settings match the LED module rating whenever either is replaced.
  • Track fixtures with recurring overheating reports to identify a location-specific or batch-related thermal issue.
Related References

SOP: SOP-EDG-16 (LED Driver Replacement Procedure), SOP-EDG-25 (Thermal Inspection Procedure)

EDG-190 Fixture Misalignment

Symptom — The runway edge fixture beam is not correctly aimed relative to the runway centreline/design orientation — the fixture is physically tilted, rotated, or otherwise out of its specified alignment, resulting in the light being visible from an incorrect angle or with reduced effective intensity along the intended sightline.

Equipment / System

Fixture mounting/base (elevated fixture riser and frangible coupling, or inset fixture frame/leveling system) and its aiming/orientation reference.

Possible Causes
HighImpact or vehicle/mower contact causing the fixture to tilt or rotate on its mounting, particularly for elevated fixtures on a frangible coupling designed to give way under load
HighSettlement or frost heave of the surrounding ground/pavement altering the fixture base level over time
MediumIncorrect aiming/orientation set during a previous installation or fixture replacement, not correctly referenced to the design aiming diagram
MediumLoosened mounting/base fixing allowing the fixture to shift under normal vibration or wind loading
LowerFrangible coupling partially failed/sheared without the fixture having fully separated, leaving it in a displaced but still-connected position
Required Tools
Inclinometer/levelFixture aiming template or design aiming diagram/drawingTorque wrench for mounting/base fixingsReplacement frangible coupling (if applicable)
Safety Precautions
  • De-energise and LOTO before adjusting or working on the fixture mounting.
  • For elevated fixtures on a frangible coupling, inspect the coupling condition before applying force to straighten/realign the fixture — a partially sheared coupling may fail completely under load.
  • Obtain airside/runway access clearance before working at the fixture location.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationVisually assess the fixture's tilt/rotation relative to adjacent, correctly aligned fixtures and the runway centreline.
  2. MeasurementUse an inclinometer/level and the design aiming diagram to quantify the deviation from the specified elevation and azimuth angle for that fixture position.
  3. ObservationFor elevated fixtures, inspect the frangible coupling for damage or partial shearing before attempting realignment.
  4. ObservationFor inset fixtures, check the surrounding pavement/frame level for settlement or heave that may be the underlying cause rather than a simple loose fixing.
  5. DecisionFrangible coupling intact, fixing loose → re-align and re-torque. Frangible coupling damaged/partially sheared → replace coupling as part of the correction. Ground/pavement settlement confirmed → broader pavement/foundation correction may be required beyond the fixture itself.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to a loose fixing, a damaged frangible coupling, or underlying ground/pavement movement.
  7. Root CauseConfirm whether the misalignment is an isolated mounting issue or symptomatic of a broader ground/pavement condition affecting multiple fixtures in the area.
Test Points
Fixture elevation and azimuth angle versus design aiming diagram
Frangible coupling condition (elevated fixtures)
Mounting/base fixing tightness
Surrounding ground/pavement level (inset fixtures)
Root Cause Verification

After realignment, re-measure elevation and azimuth angle against the design aiming diagram to confirm the fixture is within tolerance, and visually confirm consistency with adjacent fixtures at night.

Corrective Action
  • Re-align the fixture to the design aiming angle and re-torque mounting/base fixings.
  • Replace a damaged or partially sheared frangible coupling.
  • Coordinate broader pavement/foundation correction where ground settlement or heave is confirmed as the underlying cause.
Preventive Action
  • Include fixture aiming/alignment verification in the periodic PM programme, not only after a reported incident.
  • Coordinate with mowing/vehicle operators on fixture locations to reduce impact-related misalignment risk.
  • Monitor known settlement-prone areas for recurring alignment drift and address the underlying ground condition proactively.
Related References

SOP: SOP-EDG-26 (Fixture Aiming & Alignment Verification Procedure)
