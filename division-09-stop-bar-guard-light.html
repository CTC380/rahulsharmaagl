Stop Bar / Guard Light Troubleshooting

Stop Bar (SPB) and Runway Guard Light (GRD) fault diagnosis — from a dark or partially illuminated stop bar through to flicker, wrong-colour and intensity compliance, local control, safety interlock and remote command faults, series circuit, connector and transformer failure on the stop bar side; and guard light illumination, colour, intermittent, and flash-controller faults on the runway guard light side. Each topic follows the full engineering diagnostic record: symptom → observation → measurement → decision → fault location → root cause → verification → corrective action → preventive action.

SPB-256 Stop Bar Not Illuminated

Symptom — A stop bar (or the full set of fixtures spanning the taxiway at the CAT II/III holding position) is commanded ON from ALCMS/ATC but fails to illuminate.

Equipment / System

Stop bar fixtures — in-pavement or elevated red L-852E lights spanning the taxiway at the CAT II/III holding position, fed via a dedicated stop bar series circuit and isolation transformers, commanded ON/OFF from the ALCMS/ATC control panel.

Possible Causes
HighCCR feeding the stop bar circuit not energised or at zero output
HighPrimary series cable fault on the loop feeding the bar, taking down the full fixture set
MedControl command from ALCMS not reaching the CCR/contactor (route to SPB-261 or SPB-263 if isolated there)
MedInterlock logic incorrectly holding the bar OFF due to a centreline circuit sensing fault
LowMultiple fixture-level transformer failures coinciding across the bar
LowALCMS displaying a stale or mismatched status not reflecting the true field state
Required Tools
ALCMS diagnostic terminalClamp-on series circuit ammeterTrue-RMS multimeter1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Stop bar circuit as-built drawingBase-can lifting tool and PPE
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate airside access with ATC given the stop bar's location at an active holding position.
  • LOTO the circuit before work.
  • Confirm with ATC that the stop bar will be treated as out of service, with a compensating procedure in place, before beginning work.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompare the ALCMS commanded state against the actual field state observed at the fixtures.
  2. MeasurementCheck the CCR output for the stop bar circuit to confirm whether power is reaching the loop.
  3. ObservationCheck the interlock status of the associated taxiway centreline circuit, since the interlock can legitimately hold the stop bar OFF.
  4. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger a sample of fixture transformers to rule out a coincidental multi-fixture failure.
  5. DecisionCCR output absent → source-side fault, route to Division 01. CCR output correct but bar still dark → cable fault or interlock fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the CCR/source, the primary cable, the interlock logic, or a coincidental multi-fixture failure.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific cause before restoring the circuit to automatic control.
Test Points
CCR output at the panel
Interlock relay/logic state
Sample fixture transformer output
ALCMS commanded state vs. field state
Root Cause Verification

Confirm the stop bar illuminates correctly at commanded ON, and confirm the interlock correctly inhibits the downstream centreline lighting while the bar is lit, before returning to automatic control.

Corrective Action
  • Restore CCR output if the fault is source-side.
  • Repair the identified primary cable fault.
  • Correct the interlock fault causing the false OFF state.
  • Replace failed fixture transformers if a coincidental multi-fixture failure is confirmed.
Preventive Action
  • Include periodic testing of stop bar and interlock function in the PM cycle.
  • Verify ALCMS status accuracy against a field check on a defined schedule.
  • Trend CCR output for the stop bar circuit to catch a developing source-side fault early.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SPB-01 (Stop Bar Fault Isolation Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I (stop bar requirements), FAA AC 150/5340-30, FAA AC 150/5345-46 (L-852E)

SPB-257 Stop Bar Partially Illuminated

Symptom — One or more individual fixtures within an otherwise correctly energised stop bar fail to illuminate, while the remainder of the bar lights normally.

Equipment / System

Individual stop bar fixture positions within the bar, and the series circuit segment feeding them.

Possible Causes
HighIndividual fixture transformer or lamp/LED failure at one or more positions
HighConnector fault isolated to a specific fixture
MedLocalised cable segment fault between two adjacent fixtures within the bar
MedBase can water ingress affecting a specific fixture
LowPhysical damage at a specific fixture position from a vehicle strike
LowUneven circuit loading from an incorrectly rated transformer installed at a previous replacement
Required Tools
Clamp-on series circuit ammeterTrue-RMS multimeter1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Series circuit fault locator / TDRStop bar fixture layout drawingBase-can lifting tool and PPE
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before work.
  • Coordinate access at the active holding position with ATC.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationIdentify exactly which fixture(s) within the bar are dark or affected via direct inspection.
  2. MeasurementConfirm the overall circuit current is at the correct value for the commanded step, which rules out a full circuit-level fault.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the transformer and test the connector at each affected fixture position.
  4. ObservationInspect the base can at each affected position for water, debris, or physical damage.
  5. DecisionFault isolated to one or two specific fixtures → fixture-level repair. Fault pattern suggests a cable segment issue between fixtures → investigate the intervening cable.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the specific fixture(s) affected or to the cable segment between them.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific failed component at each affected position before replacement.
Test Points
Affected fixture transformer
Connector continuity at affected positions
Cable segment between affected fixtures
Overall circuit current
Root Cause Verification

Re-energise and confirm every fixture in the bar illuminates uniformly at the commanded intensity step.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed transformer, lamp/LED, or connector at each affected fixture.
  • Repair the identified local cable fault.
  • Dry and reseal an affected base can.
Preventive Action
  • Include fixture-level inspection of the full stop bar in the periodic maintenance cycle.
  • Track individual fixture failure history to identify a chronically weak position.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SPB-01 (Stop Bar Fault Isolation Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5345-46

SPB-258 Stop Bar Flickering

Symptom — One or more stop bar fixtures flicker, pulse, or vary in brightness instead of holding a steady red output at the commanded state.

Equipment / System

Stop bar fixture, isolation transformer, connector, and the interlock relay/logic governing the circuit.

Possible Causes
HighLoose or intermittent L-823 connector, worsened by vehicle traffic vibration at the holding position
HighA partially failed or overheating isolation transformer with an intermittent internal short
MedLamp/LED driver instability or a failing LED module nearing end of life
MedIntermittent cable fault (chafed insulation making and breaking contact)
LowInterlock relay chatter causing rapid ON/OFF cycling — a cause specific to the interlocked nature of the stop bar circuit
LowCCR output instability rather than a fixture-level fault
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeter with min/max holdOscilloscope or power quality logger for waveform captureThermal imaging cameraL-823 connector test adaptorInterlock relay test equipment
Safety Precautions
  • Treat intermittent faults as live until proven otherwise.
  • LOTO the circuit before opening any base can or control cabinet.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm whether the flicker is on a single fixture or several, and whether it correlates with a particular commanded step or interlock transition.
  2. MeasurementLog CCR output current over a period spanning the reported flicker to rule out a source-side instability.
  3. MeasurementCheck the interlock relay for chatter or contact bounce as a distinct possible cause given the circuit's interlocked design.
  4. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, inspect and re-torque the L-823 connector, and megger the isolation transformer under light mechanical flexing.
  5. DecisionFlicker follows CCR current logging → CCR/source-side issue, route to Division 01. Flicker correlates with interlock transitions → investigate interlock relay. Flicker isolated to the fixture → connector or transformer fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the connector, transformer, lamp/LED module, cable, or interlock relay.
  7. Root CauseConfirm by replacing the suspect component and re-testing under conditions that previously produced the flicker.
Test Points
L-823 connector contact resistance
Isolation transformer under mechanical flex
Interlock relay operation
CCR output current trend log
Root Cause Verification

Monitor the repaired fixture over several operating cycles, including interlock transitions, before closing out.

Corrective Action
  • Clean, re-grease, and properly seat the L-823 connector.
  • Replace a transformer confirmed to have an intermittent internal fault.
  • Replace a failing lamp/LED module.
  • Service or replace the interlock relay if chatter is confirmed.
Preventive Action
  • Torque-check connectors as a standard PM step given holding-position traffic vibration.
  • Include interlock relay condition in the periodic maintenance schedule.
  • Include thermal imaging of connectors and transformers in the PM cycle.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SPB-01 (Stop Bar Fault Isolation Procedure), SOP-SPB-03 (Interlock Function Test Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5345-46

SPB-259 Stop Bar Wrong Color

Symptom — A stop bar fixture displays a colour other than red, creating a safety-critical guidance discrepancy at a CAT II/III holding position.

Equipment / System

Stop bar fixture lens/filter assembly and lamp/LED colour source.

Possible Causes
HighIncorrect lens/filter installed at a previous replacement (red specified per ICAO Annex 14 for stop bars)
HighLED module fitted with the wrong colour source or an incorrect part number
MedLens cracked or missing, letting the underlying white lamp/LED source show through uncoloured
MedFixture confused with an adjacent green or yellow fixture during a bulk parts replacement given the mixed colour inventory at a holding position
LowUV degradation shifting the red lens toward an orange or faded hue
LowSurface contamination causing an apparent colour shift without an actual component fault
Required Tools
Colour coordinate/chromaticity meterFixture parts catalogue and specification sheetStop bar as-built drawingReplacement lens/filter stock
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before fixture access.
  • Treat a confirmed wrong-colour stop bar fixture as an immediate safety-critical discrepancy; notify ATC/airfield operations and consider taking the stop bar out of service until corrected, given its role in runway incursion prevention.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the reported colour against the specification — red for stop bar fixtures per ICAO Annex 14.
  2. MeasurementInspect the fixture's lens/filter and lamp/LED part number against the maintenance specification.
  3. ObservationCheck lens integrity (cracking, missing lens) and confirm the lamp/LED source colour independent of the lens.
  4. DecisionWrong lens/filter part installed → replace with the correct red part. Correct part but fixture confused with an adjacent colour zone during installation → verify against the as-built drawing.
  5. Fault LocationIsolate the discrepancy to the lens/filter, the lamp/LED source, or an installation mix-up.
  6. Root CauseVerify chromaticity meets the ICAO Annex 14 Vol I colour boundary for red after correction.
Test Points
Chromaticity coordinates vs. ICAO Annex 14 Vol I red colour boundary
Installed lens/filter part number
Lamp/LED source colour independent of lens
Fixture position vs. stop bar as-built drawing
Root Cause Verification

Re-check chromaticity after correction and confirm visually against the other fixtures in the same stop bar at night.

Corrective Action
  • Replace with the correct red lens/filter or fixture assembly.
  • Notify airfield operations if the discrepancy affected the stop bar's operational status.
Preventive Action
  • Verify part numbers against the stop bar specification before any bulk lens/filter replacement.
  • Segregate stop bar (red) parts stock from taxiway (green) and lead-on/off (yellow) parts stock to prevent mix-ups.
  • Include colour verification in the periodic inspection.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SPB-04 (Colour Verification Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I (colour specifications), FAA AC 150/5345-46

SPB-260 Stop Bar Intensity Failure

Symptom — A photometric survey or reported observation finds the stop bar, or a section of it, below the minimum required intensity for its operating category.

Equipment / System

Stop bar fixture photometric output as measured against ICAO Annex 14 Appendix 2 / FAA AC 150/5340-30 specifications.

Possible Causes
HighCumulative lens contamination across multiple fixtures reducing transmitted intensity below the compliance threshold
HighAggregate fixture ageing (lamp/LED lumen depreciation) across the bar approaching or past rated service life
MedCCR output intensity step below the value required for the stop bar's operating category
MedA batch of fixtures with a systematic transformer/lamp mismatch installed during a previous bulk replacement
LowSeries circuit voltage drop from cable resistance or route length affecting the far end of the bar
LowSurvey/measurement methodology or equipment calibration issue producing an apparent non-compliance
Required Tools
Calibrated mobile photometric survey unit or handheld luminance/intensity meterColour coordinate measurement equipmentICAO/FAA photometric compliance reference tablesFixture and transformer inventory records
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the circuit before any fixture-level intervention following the survey.
  • Conduct photometric surveys per the applicable procedure and calibration schedule to ensure results are valid before acting on them.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm the survey methodology and equipment calibration are valid before treating the result as a genuine non-compliance.
  2. ObservationIdentify whether non-compliance is isolated to specific fixtures, a specific installation batch, or the bar generally, by cross-referencing survey results against inventory and installation dates.
  3. MeasurementPhysically inspect a representative sample of non-compliant fixtures for lens contamination, lamp/LED condition, and transformer rating.
  4. MeasurementConfirm the CCR output intensity step matches the requirement for the stop bar's operating category.
  5. DecisionNon-compliance traces to contamination → clean and re-survey before replacing components. Non-compliance traces to CCR intensity step → correct the CCR setting.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate the dominant contributing cause across the affected fixtures.
  7. Root CauseConfirm compliance is restored with a follow-up photometric re-survey after corrective action.
Test Points
Measured intensity vs. ICAO/FAA minimum per fixture
CCR output intensity step
Lens condition across the affected sample
Fixture/transformer age and specification records
Root Cause Verification

Conduct a follow-up photometric survey of the corrected fixtures, and cross-check a sample of unaffected fixtures to confirm the survey and correction are consistent.

Corrective Action
  • Clean lenses across the affected section as a first step where contamination is implicated.
  • Replace lamp/LED modules that have reached end-of-life lumen depreciation.
  • Correct the CCR intensity step setting.
  • Correct a transformer/lamp mismatch found across an installation batch.
Preventive Action
  • Schedule photometric compliance surveys on a defined recurring interval.
  • Track fixture installation dates and expected lumen-depreciation curves to plan proactive replacement.
  • Verify transformer/lamp specification consistency across every bulk procurement or replacement batch.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SPB-05 (Photometric Compliance Survey Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I Appendix 2, FAA AC 150/5340-30

SPB-261 Stop Bar Control Failure

Symptom — The stop bar's local control system fails to correctly execute an ON/OFF command, or reports a status to ALCMS that does not match the actual field state.

Equipment / System

Stop bar local control system — the CCR-integrated or standalone controller/PLC card, selector relays, and status feedback circuitry that executes ON/OFF commands and reports stop bar status to ALCMS.

Possible Causes
HighFailed controller/PLC card or relay driving the stop bar contactor
HighStatus feedback circuit reporting an incorrect field state to ALCMS while the commanded state is actually correct
MedLocal selector switch stuck or faulty at the CCR cabinet
MedFirmware or logic fault in the controller
LowPower supply fault to the local controller card
LowWiring fault between the controller and the CCR/contactor
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeterALCMS diagnostic terminalController/PLC diagnostic softwareSpare controller cardLocal control cabinet wiring diagram
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the control cabinet before card or relay replacement.
  • Verify with ATC before taking the stop bar control system out of service.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCompare the ALCMS commanded state against the physical state of the stop bar fixtures.
  2. MeasurementCheck the controller/PLC card status LEDs and diagnostics.
  3. MeasurementTest the selector switch and relay operation manually at the CCR cabinet.
  4. MeasurementCheck the status feedback wiring from the controller to ALCMS.
  5. DecisionField state correct but ALCMS status wrong → feedback circuit fault. Field state does not follow the command → controller/relay/selector switch fault.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the controller/PLC card, selector switch/relay, status feedback wiring, or controller power supply.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific failed component before replacement.
Test Points
Controller/PLC card diagnostics
Selector switch/relay operation
Status feedback signal to ALCMS
Control cabinet power supply
Root Cause Verification

Command the stop bar ON/OFF several times from ALCMS and confirm both the actual field state and the reported status match on each cycle.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the faulted controller/PLC card.
  • Repair or replace the selector switch/relay.
  • Correct the status feedback wiring.
  • Restore the controller power supply.
Preventive Action
  • Include controller diagnostics in the periodic maintenance cycle.
  • Keep a spare controller card on hand given the stop bar's safety-critical function.
  • Verify status feedback accuracy during routine testing.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SPB-06 (Stop Bar Control System Test Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

SPB-262 Stop Bar Interlock Failure

Symptom — The safety interlock between the stop bar and the downstream taxiway centreline lighting fails to prevent both from being illuminated simultaneously, or fails to allow the centreline lighting to activate when the stop bar is correctly switched OFF.

Equipment / System

Stop bar / taxiway centreline interlock logic — the control circuitry (relay-based or PLC-based) that ensures the stop bar and the taxiway centreline lighting beyond it are never illuminated simultaneously, per ICAO Annex 14 Vol I requirements for CAT II/III holding positions.

Possible Causes
HighInterlock relay contact welded or mechanically stuck, allowing both circuits to be commanded together
HighA maintenance bypass or override left engaged after previous work
MedInterlock logic firmware or programming fault
MedSensing circuit reporting incorrect stop bar or centreline status to the interlock logic
LowWiring fault between the two circuits' status feedback and the interlock controller
LowPower supply fault to the interlock controller
Required Tools
True-RMS multimeterInterlock logic diagnostic tool/PLC softwareRelay test equipmentInterlock wiring diagramALCMS diagnostic terminal
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the interlock cabinet before work.
  • Treat any interlock discrepancy as safety-critical and notify ATC/airfield operations immediately — do not leave the stop bar in automatic operation until the interlock is verified correct.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationCommand the stop bar and centreline circuits through a full test cycle and verify only one is ever illuminated at a time.
  2. MeasurementCheck interlock relay contacts for welding or mechanical failure.
  3. MeasurementCheck the sensing/status feedback from both circuits into the interlock logic.
  4. ObservationConfirm no maintenance override or bypass is engaged.
  5. DecisionRelay contact welded/stuck → replace relay. Bypass found engaged → remove and re-secure. Feedback wiring or logic fault → correct wiring or reprogram logic.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the interlock relay, the bypass/override, the status feedback wiring, or the interlock controller logic.
  7. Root CauseConfirm with a full interlock test cycle that the fault is resolved before returning to automatic control.
Test Points
Interlock relay contact condition
Status feedback from stop bar and centreline circuits
Bypass/override switch position
Interlock controller power supply
Root Cause Verification

Run a complete interlock test cycle — stop bar ON/centreline OFF, then stop bar OFF/centreline ON — and confirm correct mutually exclusive operation before returning to automatic control.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the faulted interlock relay.
  • Correct the interlock logic/programming.
  • Repair the status feedback wiring.
  • Remove and re-secure a left-engaged bypass.
Preventive Action
  • Include a full interlock test cycle in the periodic maintenance schedule.
  • Log and formally close out any maintenance override/bypass use.
  • Verify interlock logic after any control system firmware update.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SPB-03 (Interlock Function Test Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I (stop bar interlock requirements), FAA AC 150/5340-30

SPB-263 Stop Bar Remote Command Failure

Symptom — A command issued from the ATC tower or ALCMS workstation to select the stop bar ON or OFF fails to reach or be executed by the field control system.

Equipment / System

Remote command link between the ATC tower/ALCMS workstation and the stop bar's local control system — the communication network (serial/Ethernet/SCADA), protocol converters, and the field RTU/controller receiving the command.

Possible Causes
HighCommunication link failure (cable/fibre break, network switch fault) between ALCMS and the field controller
HighRTU or protocol converter fault at the field end
MedALCMS server/workstation software fault or a hung command queue
MedIncorrect addressing or configuration following a system change
LowPower failure at an intermediate communication node
LowElectromagnetic interference affecting a wireless or poorly shielded link segment
Required Tools
Network diagnostic tools (cable tester, ping/traceroute from the ALCMS network)ALCMS system log reviewRTU/protocol converter diagnostic softwareNetwork architecture drawing
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate with ATC before testing since the stop bar may be under active operational control.
  • Do not disable remote control without an agreed fallback (local manual control) in place.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm from the ALCMS system log whether the command was sent and acknowledged.
  2. MeasurementCheck the communication link status between ALCMS and the field RTU/controller.
  3. MeasurementTest the physical link (cable, fibre, network switch) for continuity or faults.
  4. MeasurementVerify RTU/protocol converter configuration and diagnostics.
  5. DecisionCommand not acknowledged and link shows a fault → communication link/hardware fault. Command acknowledged but not executed → route to SPB-261 (local control system).
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the communication link, the RTU/protocol converter, or the ALCMS server/software.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific failed link segment or component before repair.
Test Points
ALCMS command log and acknowledgement status
Communication link continuity
RTU/protocol converter diagnostics
Field controller local status vs. ALCMS commanded state
Root Cause Verification

Issue a series of test commands from ALCMS and confirm each is correctly received, executed, and acknowledged with accurate status feedback.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the faulted communication link segment.
  • Replace the RTU/protocol converter.
  • Correct addressing/configuration.
  • Restore power to an intermediate communication node.
Preventive Action
  • Include communication link testing in the periodic maintenance cycle.
  • Maintain an accurate network architecture drawing.
  • Ensure a documented local manual-control fallback procedure is available and tested.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SPB-07 (Remote Command Link Test Procedure)

Standards: ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, ALCMS/SCADA vendor documentation

SPB-264 Stop Bar Circuit Failure

Symptom — A circuit-level fault affects the full stop bar series loop, taking out the entire bar or a large section of it, distinct from an isolated fixture-level fault.

Equipment / System

Stop bar series circuit — the CCR-fed series loop supplying the full stop bar fixture set, including the primary cable route and all in-line isolation transformers.

Possible Causes
HighCCR failure or fault at the source
HighPrimary series cable fault (cut, chafed, or moisture-degraded insulation) taking down the whole bar
MedA single shorted isolation transformer collapsing available voltage to the rest of the loop
MedDamaged or crushed duct/cable from recent excavation or heavy vehicle loading near the holding position
LowStorm or lightning-related surge damage to circuit components
LowA section isolator or tie point left open after previous maintenance, if the bar is circuit-segmented
Required Tools
Series circuit fault locator / TDR1000V/2500V insulation resistance testerClamp-on ammeterFull circuit as-built layout drawingExcavation permit and utility locate request if digging is required
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the CCR before any circuit work.
  • Obtain excavation clearance and utility locates before opening pavement if the fault requires digging.
  • Coordinate the stop bar outage with ATC given its role in runway incursion prevention — a compensating procedure is typically required.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm at the CCR/ALCMS whether the fault is a complete loss of output (source-side) or a partial dark section with the CCR still delivering rated current (downstream fault).
  2. ObservationCross-reference the extent of the fault against the circuit layout drawing to identify candidate cable segments.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, megger the primary cable in segments from accessible pull boxes or transformer base cans.
  4. MeasurementUse a TDR or fault locator to estimate fault distance if a direct segment test is inconclusive.
  5. DecisionInsulation resistance fails on a specific segment → cable/joint fault in that segment. All segments clear but a specific transformer reads shorted → that transformer is loading down the loop.
  6. Fault LocationPhysically locate and expose the faulted cable segment or confirm the single shorted transformer.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the cause of the cable fault (mechanical damage, water tracking, age-related insulation breakdown) once exposed.
Test Points
CCR output current and voltage
Primary cable insulation resistance by segment
Suspect transformer primary-to-secondary and to-earth
Section isolator continuity, if applicable
Root Cause Verification

After repair, megger the full circuit end-to-end, re-energise, and confirm every fixture illuminates correctly and the interlock function still operates correctly.

Corrective Action
  • Repair or replace the faulted cable segment.
  • Replace the shorted isolation transformer.
  • Restore duct/cable protection where mechanical damage was the cause.
Preventive Action
  • Schedule periodic full-loop insulation resistance testing and trend results over time.
  • Maintain accurate as-built records of every joint, pull box, and repair splice.
  • Coordinate with civil works teams before any excavation near the holding position.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SPB-01 (Stop Bar Fault Isolation Procedure), SOP-CCR-04 (CCR LOTO Procedure)

Standards: IEC 60364 (insulation testing), ICAO Annex 14 Vol I, FAA AC 150/5340-30

SPB-265 Stop Bar Connector Failure

Symptom — An L-823 connector at a stop bar fixture position is found faulted, contributing to an outage, intermittent fault, or a failed insulation reading at that position.

Equipment / System

L-823 connectors and base cans at individual stop bar fixture positions.

Possible Causes
HighMoisture ingress into the connector body degrading the seal
HighCorroded or pitted connector pins from age or de-icing chemical exposure
MedPlug not fully seated or locked after a previous maintenance visit
MedMechanical damage from vehicle or tug wheel contact given the connector's proximity to the holding position traffic path
LowAn incorrectly rated connector installed at a prior repair
LowContact plating worn from repeated connect/disconnect cycling
Required Tools
L-823 connector test adaptor1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Contact resistance meterDielectric greaseReplacement connector stock
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO before disconnecting or inspecting a connector.
  • Treat a wet connector as energised until confirmed isolated.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationIsolate the circuit and inspect the connector body, pins, and boot for moisture, corrosion, or damage.
  2. MeasurementMeasure contact resistance across the connector.
  3. ObservationConfirm the connector part number/rating matches the specification.
  4. ObservationCheck that the plug is fully seated and locked.
  5. DecisionContact resistance elevated with visible corrosion → clean/replace connector. Correct connector but not fully seated → reseat and re-torque/lock.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate the fault to the connector body, pins, boot, or seating.
  7. Root CauseConfirm restored contact resistance and insulation resistance after correction.
Test Points
Connector contact resistance
Insulation resistance across the connector
Pin condition
Seating/locking engagement
Root Cause Verification

Re-energise and confirm the fixture illuminates correctly at all commanded states, and re-check the connector after a subsequent maintenance interval.

Corrective Action
  • Clean, re-grease, and reseat the connector.
  • Replace a corroded or damaged connector.
  • Correct an incorrectly rated connector with the specified part.
Preventive Action
  • Apply dielectric grease at every reconnection.
  • Include connector inspection in the periodic maintenance cycle given the holding-position traffic exposure.
  • Standardise connector part numbers across the stop bar circuit.
Related References

SOP: SOP-SPB-08 (Connector and Transformer Inspection Procedure)

Standards: FAA AC 150/5345-46, ICAO Annex 14 Vol I

Rev 0 — Initial issue
SPB-266 Stop Bar Transformer Failure

Symptom — An individual isolation transformer feeding a stop bar fixture is found faulted during investigation of an outage, dim output, or a failed insulation reading.

Equipment / System

Individual isolation transformers feeding stop bar fixtures off the series circuit.

Possible Causes
HighOpen primary or secondary winding from age-related insulation breakdown
HighA partial internal short developing into an intermittent or hard fault

Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationIsolate the circuit and disconnect the transformer.
  2. MeasurementMegger the primary winding, secondary winding, and primary-to-secondary insulation.

Root Cause Verification

Re-energise and confirm the fixture illuminates correctly at the commanded intensity step, and re-megger the replacement transformer as a baseline reading.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed transformer with the correctly rated part.
  • Correct a mismatched wattage/tap installed previously.
Preventive Action
  • Verify transformer rating against the fixture specification at every replacement.
  • Include transformer megger testing in the periodic maintenance cycle.

GRD-267 Guard Light Failure

Symptom — A Runway Guard Light (RGL) fixture, or a pair/row of fixtures, fails to illuminate or flash when the installation is energised.

Equipment / System

Runway Guard Lights (RGL) — elevated Configuration A paired yellow flashing fixtures or in-pavement Configuration B row of yellow flashing fixtures, installed at the taxiway holding position to alert of proximity to the runway, fed via a dedicated series or parallel circuit and flash controller.

Possible Causes
HighFlash controller/synchroniser failure (route to GRD-270 if isolated to the controller)
HighLamp/LED module failure in one or both fixtures
MedIsolation transformer failure (in-pavement Configuration B) or ballast/power supply failure (elevated Configuration A)
MedConnector or base can fault
LowCircuit-level fault (source or cable) affecting the full RGL installation
LowFixture physically damaged by vehicle strike — elevated fixtures are particularly exposed
Required Tools
Clamp-on ammeterTrue-RMS multimeter1000V insulation resistance tester (megger)Flash controller diagnostic toolRGL as-built circuit drawingBase-can or elevated-fixture access tools and PPE
Safety Precautions
  • Coordinate airside access given the holding-position location.
  • LOTO the circuit before work.
  • Use appropriate fall protection/access equipment for elevated fixture work.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm from ALCMS/field inspection whether the fault is isolated to one fixture, both fixtures of a pair, or the full installation.
  2. MeasurementCheck the flash controller status and output for the affected fixture(s).
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, test the affected fixture's lamp/LED and transformer/ballast.
  4. ObservationInspect the connector/base can for damage or moisture.
  5. DecisionController output present but fixture dark → fixture-level fault (lamp, transformer/ballast, connector). Controller output absent for the channel → route to GRD-270.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the fixture (lamp/LED, transformer/ballast, connector) or to the flash controller.
  7. Root CauseConfirm the specific failed component before replacement.
Test Points
Flash controller output
Fixture lamp/LED condition
Transformer/ballast output
Connector/base can condition
Root Cause Verification

Re-energise and confirm both fixtures (or the full row) flash correctly in the specified alternating or sequential pattern before closing out.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed lamp/LED module.
  • Replace a failed transformer/ballast.
  • Repair or replace the connector.
  • Replace a physically damaged fixture.
Preventive Action
  • Include RGL fixture and flash pattern verification in the periodic night inspection.
  • Prioritise elevated-fixture condition checks given vehicle-strike exposure.
  • Track flash controller service history.

Equipment / System

RGL fixture, connector, transformer/ballast, and the flash controller output feeding that fixture.

Possible Causes
HighLoose or intermittent connector connection, worsened by vehicle vibration at the holding position
HighA partially failed transformer/ballast with an intermittent internal fault
MedA flash controller output channel developing an intermittent fault
MedAn intermittent cable fault (chafed insulation making and breaking contact)
LowLamp/LED driver instability nearing end of life

Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationConfirm whether the intermittent behaviour is isolated to one fixture or affects the flash controller output generally.
  2. MeasurementLog the flash controller output over a period spanning the reported fault.
  3. MeasurementWith the circuit isolated, inspect the connector and megger the transformer/ballast under mechanical flex.
  4. MeasurementCheck the flash controller channel diagnostics for the affected fixture.
  5. DecisionFault reproduced by flexing the connector/transformer → component-level intermittent fault. Fault correlates with the controller channel → route to GRD-270.

Flash controller channel output
Cable segment continuity

  • Replace the transformer/ballast.

  • Replace the flash controller channel/module if confirmed faulty.

GRD-270 Guard Light Control Failure

Symptom — The Runway Guard Light flash controller/synchroniser fails to produce the correct flash pattern — showing no flash, a steady state, or an incorrect rate/sequence.

Equipment / System

RGL flash controller/synchroniser — the device generating and timing the alternating (Configuration A) or sequential (Configuration B) flash pattern for the runway guard lights, and any interface to ALCMS for status/command.

Possible Causes
HighFailed controller card or timing circuit producing no flash, a steady-on/steady-off state, or an incorrect flash pattern
HighPower supply fault to the controller
MedFirmware or logic fault following a configuration change
MedWiring fault between the controller and the fixture outputs
LowLoss of synchronisation between paired Configuration A fixtures
LowALCMS interface fault reporting incorrect controller status
Required Tools
Flash controller diagnostic softwareTrue-RMS multimeterOscilloscope for flash-timing verificationController wiring diagramSpare controller card/module
Safety Precautions
  • LOTO the control cabinet before card replacement.
  • Coordinate with ATC/airfield operations if the RGL will be out of service during correction, given its role as a runway-proximity warning.
Diagnostic Procedure
  1. ObservationObserve the actual flash pattern in the field and compare against the specified pattern (alternating or sequential, correct rate).
  2. MeasurementCheck the controller's diagnostic status and output channels.
  3. MeasurementVerify the controller's power supply.
  4. MeasurementCheck wiring between the controller and each fixture.
  5. DecisionController diagnostics show a channel fault → replace controller card/module. Power supply fault confirmed → restore power. Wiring fault confirmed → repair wiring.
  6. Fault LocationIsolate to the controller card/module, its power supply, the output wiring, or a synchronisation fault between paired fixtures.
  7. Root CauseConfirm with an on-site flash pattern check that the corrected pattern matches the specification.
Test Points
Controller diagnostic status
Flash timing/rate at each output channel
Controller power supply
ALCMS status feedback, where applicable
Root Cause Verification

Confirm the corrected flash pattern matches the specification at the correct rate and synchronisation, observed directly at the fixture during a night check.

Corrective Action
  • Replace the failed controller card/module.
  • Restore the controller power supply.
  • Repair the wiring fault.
  • Reconfigure the controller after a logic/firmware fault.
Preventive Action
  • Include flash pattern verification in the periodic night inspection.
  • Keep a spare controller card on hand given the RGL's safety function.
  • Verify controller configuration after any firmware update.

Q2What information does each topic include?

Every topic follows the same diagnostic record: the symptom, equipment/system, possible causes ranked by likelihood (high, medium or low), required tools, safety precautions, diagnostic procedure, test points, root cause verification, corrective action, preventive action and related references (SOPs, standards, diagrams and case studies).

Each procedure starts with observation before any intervention. For example, SPB-256 (Stop Bar Not Illuminated) begins: Compare the ALCMS commanded state against the actual field state observed at the fixtures.

Each topic lists its own precautions. For example, SPB-256 (Stop Bar Not Illuminated) starts with: Coordinate airside access with ATC given the stop bar's location at an active holding position.
