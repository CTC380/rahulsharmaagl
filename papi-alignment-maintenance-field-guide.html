PAPI Alignment, Testing & Maintenance Field Guide

How to install, level, align, aim, test and maintain a PAPI: site survey, foundation, unit spacing, aiming angles, electrical installation, photometry, maintenance checklists, records and troubleshooting. A PAPI that is electrically operational can still be aimed wrongly, and one that passed commissioning can later move.

Overview

What a Complete PAPI Installation Involves

A Precision Approach Path Indicator (PAPI) gives pilots visual information about their position relative to the desired approach path. Correct performance depends on far more than installing and switching on the units: the system must be sited, positioned, levelled, aligned, aimed, connected, tested and maintained.

Siting

Site Survey, Siting and Threshold Crossing Height

Site survey should establish

  • Threshold, runway centreline and edge
  • Longitudinal and transverse runway profile
  • Proposed PAPI location, ground and PAPI elevation
  • Aircraft group, TCH and desired approach angle
  • Obstacles, ILS or other approach guidance
  • Utilities, cable routes, drainage, maintenance access

Siting and TCH

Location comes from an approved siting calculation, never a convenient distance from the threshold. PAPI location, PAPI elevation, threshold elevation, approach angle and aircraft characteristics together give the approved geometry, and the intended TCH must suit the aircraft and runway operation.

Do not use one universal distance. Aircraft, runway geometry, ILS relationships and obstacles change the answer.

Foundation

Foundation, Elevation and Drainage

Stable reference

The foundation provides stable support, accurate position, mechanical rigidity, protection against settlement and maintenance access. Build it from controlled survey information, not estimates from ground conditions.

Elevation matters

Beam geometry depends on physical unit elevation. A wrong foundation elevation gives incorrect TCH, effective approach angle and beam geometry, plus excess adjustment at the mounts.

Drainage

Standing water causes deterioration, corrosion, cable and connector problems, movement and electrical faults. Provide drainage suited to local conditions.

Unit Installation

Unit Position, Spacing and Runway Offset

A standard four-unit PAPI is four units in a horizontal row, installed to the approved layout. Check sequence, spacing, runway offset, elevation, azimuth, orientation, mounting stability, adjustment access and frangibility where applicable, then survey the final positions.

Installation and frangibility

Where frangibility requirements apply, install the approved breakaway arrangement. Do not introduce unapproved rigid supports, excessive structure, improper foundations or non-approved mounting modifications. Consider the unit, mounting arrangement and foundation together; the applicable airport design standard controls the final arrangement.

Level & Alignment

Levelling, Mounting and Horizontal Alignment

Levelling

The aim is not for each unit to "look level" but to establish the required physical relationship between all four optical systems. Check unit elevation and tilt, housing level, mounting-leg adjustment, foundation settlement and unit-to-unit difference.

Mounting legs

FAA AC 150/5345-28H requires at least three adjustable mounting legs for levelling, unless an alternative shows equivalent rigidity and levelling capability. Take the final measurement after the hardware is secured.

Electrical

Electrical Installation, Power Supply and Wiring

Installation

Follow the approved electrical design and equipment documents. Equipment can include AC supply, power control unit, CCR/series circuit, isolation transformer, secondary connections, local and remote control, monitoring and photocell brightness control.

Before energizing

Verify supply voltage, frequency, circuit identification, protective devices, grounding, cable insulation, connections, transformer rating and control wiring. Never assume a replacement unit can be connected to an existing circuit without checking its electrical characteristics.

Wiring inspection

Inspect supply cable, primary and secondary connections, control and monitoring wiring, grounding, cable glands and connectors. Look for loose terminals, damaged insulation, moisture, corrosion, mechanical strain and incorrect connections.

Verification

Functional Test and Light Indication Test

Power and operation

All units receive the required supply and operate correctly.

Colour and brightness

Red/white indication appears correctly and the intended intensity setting operates.

Controls, monitoring, interlocks

Local and remote control, alarms, status signals and any runway-lighting interlock work as designed.

Aircraft positionExpected indication
Too high4 White
Slightly high3 White + 1 Red
On path2 White + 2 Red
Slightly low1 White + 3 Red
Too low4 Red
The functional and indication checks verify electrical and operational behaviour. Use a controlled observation method, and do not treat them as a substitute for measured alignment and photometry.
Photometry

Photometric Testing and Equipment Calibration

PAPI photometry is broader than brightness. Depending on the requirement it verifies intensity, beam pattern, colour/chromaticity, red/white transition, horizontal and vertical distribution and full-intensity performance. FAA AC 150/5345-28H contains the photometric and chromaticity requirements and requires calibrated test equipment.

Equipment

PAPI photometer, calibrated photometric sensor, angular measurement system, alignment equipment, data acquisition, and colour/chromaticity equipment where required. Check identification, certificate, validity, condition, power, settings and reference configuration before testing, and retain a traceable certificate with the records.

Test conditions

The procedure may specify full intensity, measurement distance and angles, beam position, horizontal coverage setting, vertical reference, temperature and test sequence. Keep conditions controlled.

Colour and red/white transition

A unit can look bright yet have a colour problem from filter degradation, optical damage, LED performance, contamination, incorrect optical assembly or temperature. A poor transition gives an unclear indication, inconsistent unit-to-unit behaviour and a wrong perceived approach position, so test the whole optical system, not just centre-beam intensity.

Two Levels of Assessment

Alignment vs Photometry, Visual Inspection vs Measurement

Alignment asks

Is the unit physically aimed in the correct direction?

Photometry asks

Does the optical system produce the required light output, colour and beam pattern?

A unit can have correct aiming with poor photometry, good photometry with wrong aiming, or both faults at once. Commissioning verifies both.

Maintenance

PAPI Maintenance: Daily, Monthly and Optical, Mechanical, Electrical Checks

Base maintenance on the airport programme, manufacturer instructions, authority requirements, operational experience, environment and defect history. FAA AC 150/5340-26C gives PAPI preventive-maintenance procedures.

Daily (FAA 150/5340-26C)

Confirm the PAPI is operating with equal brightness, and keep spare lamps and, where applicable, spare bypass fuses. For LED systems follow the manufacturer procedure rather than incandescent practice. Also check for visible damage, obstruction, contamination and signs of misalignment.

Monthly (FAA 150/5340-26C)

Control operation, photocell brightness control, runway-lighting interlock, radio and remote control, damage from mowing or snowplows, cleaning lamps and filters, mechanical parts, burned wires or connections, and cracked insulators, lamps or filters. Adapt to the installed equipment.

Optical

Clean lens, filter, protective window, housing, heater, ventilation; check moisture. Dirt, dust, insects, salt and oil reduce output, and the equipment standard addresses rain, snow, dew, frost and ice on lens surfaces.

Mechanical

Housing, mounting legs, fasteners, foundation, level, alignment, frangible parts, corrosion. Ask: has anything changed the geometry since commissioning?

Electrical

Supply voltage, connections, fuses and protection, cable, transformer, control circuit, remote control, monitoring, grounding. Look for burned terminals, water ingress and heating.

Avoid These

Common PAPI Installation Mistakes

1

Using a generic PAPI distance

Distance comes from the approved siting process.

2

Aiming all four units at one angle

The units need different aiming angles.

3

Ignoring unit elevation

Small elevation differences change PAPI geometry.

4

Checking only visual appearance

Visual inspection cannot replace measurement.

5

Forgetting foundation movement

An unstable foundation causes recurring aiming problems.

6

Ignoring horizontal alignment

Correct vertical aiming is not enough if azimuth is wrong.

7

No photometric retest

Aiming alone does not prove optical compliance.

8

No records

Without baseline aiming and photometry records, maintenance gets much harder.

Final Engineering Perspective

Do not maintain PAPI only by looking at it. Maintain the geometry, optics, electrical system and measured performance.

Install accurately. Aim precisely. Test objectively. Maintain systematically.

FAQ

PAPI Installation and Maintenance FAQ

Q1How is a PAPI installed?

Site survey, approved siting, foundation construction, unit positioning, spacing, levelling, horizontal alignment, vertical aiming, electrical connection, functional testing and photometric verification.

Q2How do you align PAPI lights?

Establish the runway reference, position the units, level them, set azimuth and adjust each unit to its approved vertical aiming angle.

Q3Are all PAPI units aimed at the same angle?

No. Each unit is aimed at a different elevation relative to the selected approach path.

Q4What are the standard PAPI aiming angles?

They depend on the selected approach path and the applicable design standard. FAA guidance gives four-unit offsets of 30 minutes above, 10 above, 10 below and 30 below the pre-selected glide path for the standard installation.

Q5What is the spacing between PAPI units?

For FAA L-880/L-881 installations the cited spacing is 20 to 30 ft (6 to 9 m) centre-to-centre with a maximum variation of ±1 ft (0.3 m). Other authorities may specify different values.

Q6How far should PAPI be from the runway?

It depends on the approved siting calculation, runway geometry, TCH, aircraft characteristics and applicable standards. FAA guidance gives specific lateral and longitudinal siting requirements.

Q7What is PAPI elevation?

The physical elevation of the light units relative to the runway and the approved reference geometry.

Q8Why is PAPI levelling important?

The vertical orientation of each unit determines its effective beam angle and therefore the guidance given to pilots.

Q9What is PAPI photometry?

Measurement of optical performance, including applicable intensity, beam distribution and colour characteristics.

Q10When should PAPI photometry be performed?

During commissioning and whenever the inspection, maintenance, authority or project procedure requires it, especially after optical adjustment, unit replacement or major maintenance.

Q11What equipment is used for PAPI photometry?

A calibrated PAPI photometer, angular measurement equipment and, where required, colour or chromaticity measurement equipment.

Q12Does PAPI maintenance require alignment checks?

Per the approved maintenance programme and whenever there is evidence of movement, impact, civil work or an abnormal visual indication.

Q13What should be checked daily on PAPI?

FAA maintenance guidance includes confirming the PAPI is operating with equal brightness. For LED systems follow the airport and manufacturer procedure.

Q14What should be checked during periodic PAPI maintenance?

Controls, optical cleanliness, mechanical condition, wiring, connectors, mounting, alignment and applicable photometric performance.

Q15What causes PAPI misalignment?

Mechanical impact, loose mounting, foundation movement, incorrect installation, maintenance error or nearby civil works.

Q16What causes unequal PAPI brightness?

Light-source degradation, optical contamination, electrical faults, incorrect intensity control or internal equipment problems.

Q17Can PAPI be visually inspected without photometry?

Visual inspection is useful for routine maintenance but does not replace objective photometric verification where it is required.

Q18What should be done after replacing a PAPI light unit?

Verify position, level, horizontal alignment, vertical aiming, electrical connection and the required operational and photometric performance.

Important Notice, Disclaimer & Limitation of Liability

Please read before relying on any content on this page or on any record downloaded from it.

Specific to this page: PAPI, APAPI and docking-guidance systems are safety-critical visual aids: a unit that is wrongly sited, aimed or calibrated can give pilots or marshalling staff misleading guidance. This page explains principles. It does not authorise the siting, installation, aiming, adjustment, calibration or return to service of any unit. Angles, settings, tolerances, distances and checklists are generic and indicative; use the approved design, the manufacturer’s instructions and the current regulatory requirements. Commissioning or modifying such a system requires the approvals, and where required a flight check, set by the competent authority. Nothing here is a calibration certificate, certification or finding of compliance, and interactive demonstrations must never be used to set a real unit.
