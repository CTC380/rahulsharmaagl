PREVENTIVE VS PREDICTIVE

Two maintenance philosophies for AGL systems — preventive maintenance is performed at planned time or usage intervals to reduce the probability of failure, while predictive maintenance uses actual equipment-condition data and trend analysis to determine when maintenance is genuinely needed. Preventive asks "when does the schedule say to service it?" Predictive asks "what does the equipment's condition say?"

Preventive Maintenance

05 · Why Preventive Maintenance Is Used

Simple, Schedulable, Low-Complexity

Preventive maintenance is the practical baseline for most AGL systems because it is simple to plan, easy for maintenance teams to implement, and does not depend on sensors or monitoring infrastructure. The main trade-off is that equipment may be serviced earlier than actually necessary.

  • Simple to schedule and budget
  • Lower monitoring and data-collection cost
  • Easy for maintenance teams to implement consistently
  • Lower technical complexity and initial investment
06 · Why Predictive Maintenance Is Used

Targeted, Data-Driven, Condition-Aware

Predictive maintenance becomes valuable where CCRs, LED fixtures, control systems, or other critical equipment can provide sufficient condition data to identify degradation before it becomes an operational failure. It generally reduces unnecessary replacement and makes better use of remaining equipment life, at the cost of higher technical complexity.

  • Maintenance can be better targeted to actual need
  • Reduced unnecessary replacement of serviceable parts
  • Better use of equipment life
  • Potentially lower unplanned downtime

11 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: Predictive maintenance replaces preventive maintenance entirely
Fact

Preventive maintenance remains the practical baseline for most AGL equipment. Predictive maintenance is generally layered on top for critical equipment where sufficient condition data is available, not used as a wholesale replacement.

Myth: Preventive maintenance is an outdated approach
Fact

Preventive maintenance is simple, low-cost, and easy to implement consistently. It remains appropriate wherever monitoring infrastructure is not justified or available.

Myth: Predictive maintenance is always cheaper overall
Fact

Predictive maintenance can reduce unnecessary replacement and unplanned downtime, but it carries a higher initial investment in sensors, monitoring, and trained personnel for data analysis.

Myth: Every piece of AGL equipment needs predictive maintenance
Fact

Predictive maintenance becomes valuable specifically where equipment can provide sufficient condition data to identify degradation trends — not as a default requirement across every component.

14 · Engineering Verdict

Preventive vs Predictive Maintenance

Engineering Verdict

Preventive maintenance is planned maintenance performed at defined time, usage, or operating intervals to reduce the probability of failure.

Predictive maintenance is condition-based maintenance using measurements, monitoring, and trend analysis to identify when maintenance is actually needed. For AGL systems, preventive maintenance is generally the practical baseline, while predictive maintenance becomes valuable where CCRs, LED fixtures, control systems, or other critical equipment provide sufficient condition data to identify degradation before an operational failure occurs.

Preventive"Service it before the scheduled failure risk becomes high."
Predictive"Monitor its condition and service it when the data shows deterioration."

Predictive maintenance does not eliminate the need for preventive maintenance — most AGL maintenance programs combine both, applying preventive scheduling as the baseline and predictive monitoring where condition data justifies it.

15 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fundamental difference between preventive and predictive maintenance?

Preventive maintenance is performed at planned time or usage intervals regardless of actual condition, while predictive maintenance uses measured equipment condition and trend analysis to decide when maintenance is needed.

Does predictive maintenance always reduce cost?

Not necessarily overall. It can reduce unnecessary replacement and unplanned downtime, but it also requires a higher initial investment in sensors, monitoring systems, and trained analysis personnel.

Is predictive maintenance the same as condition-based maintenance?

Predictive maintenance is a condition-based maintenance approach — it uses condition and trend data rather than a calendar to determine when maintenance is needed.

Can preventive and predictive maintenance be used together?

Yes. Most practical AGL maintenance programs use preventive maintenance as the baseline schedule and add predictive monitoring for critical equipment where sufficient condition data is available.

Which AGL equipment typically benefits most from predictive maintenance?

Equipment that can provide continuous or periodic condition data — such as CCRs, LED fixtures, and control systems — is best positioned to benefit, since trend analysis depends on having that data.

Does preventive maintenance guarantee equipment won't fail between services?

No. Preventive maintenance reduces the probability of failure but is not condition-aware, so a fault developing between scheduled intervals may not be caught until the next inspection or until it causes an operational issue.
