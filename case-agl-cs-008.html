AGL-CS-008 — Cable Joint Failure in an AGL Circuit

The AGL Knowledge Hub | Engineering Case Study Series. A progressive underground cable-joint failure caused by moisture ingress and incomplete sealing — from maintenance history review through insulation testing and joint excavation to final repair.

Case ID

AGL-CS-008

Category

Series Circuit & Cable Joint Faults

Level

Advanced

Case Type

Engineering Simulation

Primary Skill

Cable-joint failure diagnosis & underground investigation

Important: This is an engineering simulation created for training. It is not presented as an actual airport incident. All readings, values, dates and outcomes are illustrative, and no airport, organisation, person or manufacturer is identified or implied.

1Case Study Overview

During a scheduled night inspection, a section of the runway edge lighting circuit was found inoperative. The Constant Current Regulator (CCR) remained energized and attempted to maintain the selected current, but one section of the field circuit remained unavailable.

Initial inspection of the affected lights showed no visible fixture damage. Previous maintenance records indicated that a cable joint had been repaired approximately 18 months earlier in the affected section.

The investigation ultimately identified progressive failure of an underground cable joint caused by moisture ingress and incomplete sealing during a previous repair.

This case demonstrates why cable-joint quality is one of the most important long-term reliability factors in AGL systems.

2Learning Objectives

After completing this case study, the learner should be able to:

  • Understand common cable-joint failure mechanisms.
  • Recognize symptoms of underground joint deterioration.
  • Apply systematic cable-joint investigation procedures.
  • Interpret insulation resistance and continuity test results.
  • Identify moisture-related insulation failure.
  • Evaluate installation-quality issues.
  • Perform post-repair verification of underground cable joints.

3System Background

The AGL primary series circuit contains underground cable joints that connect cable sections between field equipment locations.

A typical arrangement is:

CCR → Primary Cable → Cable Joint → Cable → Isolating Transformer → Light → Return

A cable joint must provide:

  • Electrical continuity.
  • Mechanical strength.
  • Moisture protection.
  • Insulation integrity.
  • Long-term environmental resistance.

Failure of a cable joint can produce:

  • Open circuits.
  • High-resistance faults.
  • Intermittent faults.
  • Insulation breakdown.
  • Partial discharge.
  • Short circuits.

4Problem Statement

The reported condition was:

  • One runway edge lighting section OFF.
  • CCR energized.
  • Circuit voltage abnormal.
  • Recent rainfall.
  • Previous cable repair in the same area.
  • No visible excavation damage.
Engineering Question

Has the previous cable joint repair failed, or is another field component responsible?

The affected lights formed a continuous section, suggesting a common upstream fault.

The circuit drawing showed that all affected lights were supplied through the cable joint repaired during the previous maintenance activity.

This significantly narrowed the investigation area.

9Investigation

Step 1: Verify CCR Operation

Selected current 6.6 A
Output voltage Elevated

The CCR was operating correctly.

Step 2: Circuit Isolation

The field circuit was isolated. Continuity testing localized the interruption to the cable section containing the previous repair.

Step 3: Insulation Resistance Testing

Results:

CCR to Joint Normal
Joint to Downstream Section Low
Adjacent Cable Section Normal

The abnormal insulation condition was concentrated around the cable joint.

Step 4: Excavation and Joint Inspection

The cable joint was excavated and opened. Inspection revealed:

  • Water inside the joint enclosure.
  • Incomplete sealing.
  • Corroded conductor connections.
  • Oxidized joint surfaces.
  • Insulation deterioration.
  • Evidence of electrical tracking.

The joint had clearly lost its environmental protection.

Step 5: Joint Construction Review

Detailed examination showed:

  • Improper heat-shrink application.
  • Inadequate adhesive sealing.
  • Moisture path along the cable sheath.
  • Incomplete conductor preparation.
  • Reduced insulation overlap.

The failure mechanism was consistent with installation-quality deficiencies.

10Root Cause

Training-Case Finding

The cable joint failed because moisture entered through an incompletely sealed heat-shrink termination.

Progressive water ingress caused:

  • Conductor corrosion.
  • Insulation deterioration.
  • Electrical tracking.
  • Increased resistance.
  • Eventual circuit interruption.

The failure originated from poor joint workmanship during the previous repair.

11Corrective Action

The maintenance team:

  1. Isolated the circuit.
  2. Removed the failed cable joint.
  3. Cut back damaged cable insulation.
  4. Verified conductor integrity.
  5. Installed a new approved cable joint kit.
  6. Applied correct heat-shrink installation procedures.
  7. Verified complete environmental sealing.
  8. Performed insulation resistance testing.
  9. Conducted continuity testing.
  10. Restored and verified the circuit.

14Why This Case Was Important

Cable joints are often hidden underground for many years.

A joint may appear satisfactory immediately after installation but fail later due to:

  • Inadequate sealing.
  • Poor conductor preparation.
  • Moisture ingress.
  • Thermal cycling.
  • Ground movement.
  • Mechanical stress.

This makes installation quality critical for long-term reliability.

16Engineer's Decision Point

You observe: A continuous section of runway lights OFF. Recent rainfall. Previous cable repair in the affected area. CCR operating normally. What should you investigate first?

A. Replace all light fittings.
B. Replace all transformers.
C. Investigate the previous cable joint.
D. Increase CCR current.
Correct answer: C. The combination of a continuous failure pattern and a history of previous repair in that section strongly indicates a cable-joint issue.

19Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about underground cable joint failure, based on this case study.

Q1What is AGL-CS-008?

AGL-CS-008 is an engineering training case study on a progressive underground cable joint failure, caused by moisture ingress and incomplete sealing, that left a section of runway edge lights inoperative. It is an engineering simulation created for training, not a report of an actual airport incident.

Q2Why did the maintenance history matter in this case?

Records showed a cable joint in the affected section had been repaired about 18 months earlier. Combined with a continuous section outage and recent rainfall, that pointed the investigation toward the previous joint.

Q3What was the root cause?

Moisture entered through an incompletely sealed heat-shrink termination. The failure originated from poor joint workmanship during the previous repair.

Q4How did the joint fail progressively?

Water ingress caused conductor corrosion, insulation deterioration, electrical tracking and increased resistance, leading to eventual circuit interruption.

Q5Which test equipment was used?

The investigation used a true-RMS multimeter, insulation resistance tester, cable continuity tester, approved AGL circuit test equipment, a cable fault locator, joint inspection tools, circuit drawings and historical maintenance records.

Q6How was the circuit repaired?

The team isolated the circuit, removed the failed joint, cut back damaged insulation and verified conductor integrity. They installed a new approved joint kit using correct heat-shrink procedures, verified complete environmental sealing, tested insulation resistance and continuity, and restored the circuit.

Q7Why does joint workmanship matter for AGL reliability?

Cable-joint workmanship directly affects circuit reliability. An incompletely sealed joint can pass initial checks yet fail progressively months later.

Q8What should you investigate first if a continuous section is OFF after rain and the area had a previous cable repair?

Investigate the previous cable joint. The continuous failure pattern combined with prior repair history strongly indicates a cable-joint issue, so replacing all fittings or transformers is not the right first step.

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist

Jointing Quality Is a Process, Not Just a Part

Workmanship

Clean surfaces, correct preparation and complete sealing matter as much as the kit. Follow the manufacturer's steps and record who made the joint.

Check Heat-Shrink Sealing at Both Ends

Sealing

Voids and uneven shrinkage let water track in. Inspect the finished joint for full adhesive flow before backfilling.

Localise With Insulation Tests Before Excavating

Fault Location

Sectional testing narrows the dig. Excavation without localisation is expensive and may damage healthy cable.

Keep Joint Records on the As-Built

Records

Joint location, date and method on the drawings help future teams focus inspection where workmanship risk is highest.

20Case Study Conclusion

Key Engineering Principle

Cable-joint failures are often progressive reliability problems caused by moisture ingress, installation-quality deficiencies, and long-term insulation deterioration.

Systematic continuity testing, insulation resistance measurement, joint excavation, and construction-quality assessment can identify the true root cause of underground circuit failures.

In AGL engineering, the quality of a cable joint is often more important than the speed of the repair that created it.
