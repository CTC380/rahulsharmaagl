6.6A Isolating Transformer

The industry‑standard isolating transformer for 6.6A AGL series circuits — the most widely used primary current rating in airfield ground lighting. Available in power ratings from 15W to 500W, this transformer provides reliable galvanic isolation and constant‑current transfer for runway, taxiway, and approach lighting fixtures worldwide.

Overview

What Is It?

The 6.6A Isolating Transformer is a single‑phase, fully encapsulated transformer designed for use in 6.6A constant‑current AGL series circuits. With a primary current rating of 6.6A, this is the most common and widely used isolating transformer in airfield ground lighting systems worldwide. It is available in a comprehensive range of secondary power ratings — from 15W for low‑intensity signage to 500W for the most demanding approach lighting and obstruction applications.

The transformer provides essential galvanic isolation between the high‑voltage series loop (primary) and the low‑voltage lamp circuit (secondary), ensuring safety, fault containment, and the ability to remove individual fixtures without interrupting the series current. Its robust construction, fully encapsulated design, and adherence to international standards make it the backbone of AGL power distribution.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

The 6.6A isolating transformer exists as the standard building block of the entire AGL series-circuit approach, because 6.6A is the current level most runway and taxiway lighting circuits worldwide are designed around. Its purpose is to make that constant-current architecture work at the individual-fixture level: taking the shared 6.6A loop current and stepping it down to whatever voltage each specific lamp or LED module needs, while keeping every fixture electrically isolated from its neighbours.

Because it spans secondary ratings from 15W to 500W, this same fundamental component can serve everything from a low-intensity taxiway sign to a high-intensity approach light on the same underlying circuit philosophy, which is why it functions as the backbone of AGL power distribution rather than just one option among several.

Application

Where Is It Used?

The 6.6A isolating transformer is the default choice for the vast majority of AGL applications, including:

  • Runway Lighting: Edge lights, centreline lights, threshold lights, end lights, and touchdown zone lights on precision and non‑precision runways.
  • Taxiway Lighting: Edge lights, centreline lights, stop bars, and clearance bars.
  • Approach Lighting Systems: ALSF‑1, ALSF‑2, MALSR, and other approach lighting configurations.
  • Obstruction Lighting: Low‑, medium‑, and high‑intensity obstruction lights on structures of all heights.
  • Illuminated Airfield Signs: Mandatory instruction signs, information signs, direction signs, and destination signs.
  • Airport Beacons: Rotating beacons and heliport beacons.

The transformer is typically installed in handholes, concrete pits, or direct‑buried enclosures adjacent to each fixture, using standard L‑823 primary connectors and secondary cabling. Its widespread use makes it the most commonly stocked and replaced AGL transformer.

Placement

Installation & Location

Being the default series-circuit transformer, the 6.6A unit is installed at essentially every fixture position along the loop, in a handhole, concrete pit, or direct-buried enclosure sited immediately adjacent to the light it feeds. The primary winding is spliced into the series circuit using L-823 primary connectors sealed against moisture, with the secondary leads run a short distance to the fixture's own L-823 connector. The enclosure is set to finished grade or just below it, deep enough to clear frost and mechanical loading but shallow enough for the primary loop cable to maintain a consistent bend radius as it passes from one transformer to the next along the runway or taxiway edge.

Function

How Does It Work?

The 6.6A transformer operates on the principle of electromagnetic induction. The primary winding is connected in series with the 6.6A constant‑current loop. The primary current creates a magnetic flux in the core, which induces a secondary voltage proportional to the turns ratio. The secondary voltage is selected to match the lamp's rated voltage at the rated current — for example, a 45W lamp at 6.6A requires approximately 6.8V, while a 150W lamp requires approximately 22.7V.

Key performance features of the 6.6A design include:

  • Optimised Turns Ratio: Designed to deliver the correct secondary voltage at 6.6A primary current, ensuring maximum light output at full intensity.
  • Low Core Loss: High‑grade silicon steel laminations minimise no‑load losses, improving efficiency across the entire dimming range (2.8A to 6.6A).
  • Excellent Voltage Regulation: Maintains secondary voltage within ±5% over the full current range, ensuring consistent light output at all brightness levels.
  • High Thermal Capacity: The fully encapsulated design provides effective heat dissipation to the surrounding soil or concrete, with power ratings up to 500W.
  • Robust Short‑Circuit Protection: The transformer's leakage impedance limits secondary fault currents, protecting the loop and minimising the risk of CCR tripping.
  • Galvanic Isolation: Complete electrical separation between primary and secondary circuits ensures safety and prevents fault propagation.
Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

The 6.6A transformer occupies the same position in the control chain as any isolating transformer, regardless of the loop it sits on: an Airfield Lighting Control and Monitoring System (ALCMS) selects the required brightness step based on prevailing RVR, ATC instruction, or pilot-controlled lighting input, and the CCR regulates the whole loop to the corresponding fixed current (one of 5 steps between 2.8A and 6.6A). The transformer has no addressable identity of its own — it converts whatever current the loop is carrying at that moment into the secondary voltage its fixture needs, and cannot be commanded independently of the rest of the loop. Because 6.6A is the standard loop current used across the vast majority of AGL installations, a fault detected by the CCR's voltage monitoring typically points to one transformer among many similar units on a runway, taxiway, or sign circuit rather than to a large specialised array; the choice between a 6.6A and a 20A loop for a given installation, and what that implies for the scale of a single fault, is covered in the loop current selection logic below.

Construction

Physical Construction

The transformer core and windings are fully encapsulated in a resin compound, typically epoxy or polyurethane, cast within a cylindrical or oval-section housing to exclude moisture entirely and achieve the IP68 submersible rating required for direct burial. The primary and secondary windings are wound on a laminated silicon-steel core, insulated to Class F or Class H depending on the power rating, with the encapsulant also serving to dampen vibration and provide mechanical protection against soil movement and impact during installation. Primary leads terminate in factory-moulded L-823 connectors, sealed splice connectors designed for continuous immersion, allowing the transformer to be spliced directly into the series circuit loop without a separate junction enclosure; secondary leads are similarly sealed and sized to the connecting fixture's cable entry. The unit sits within its own base can, an L-867 or L-868 base recessed into the ground beneath or beside the fixture it serves, with a drainage provision at the base of the can to prevent water pooling around the connectors. Larger power ratings use a proportionally larger encapsulated body and heavier-gauge windings to manage increased heat dissipation, and the exterior casing is moulded from a UV-stable, chemical-resistant polymer to withstand prolonged exposure to soil chemistry, de-icing fluids, and fuel spillage over decades of service.

Testing & Commissioning

Insulation Resistance (Megger) Test
Pre-Energisation

Megger-test primary and secondary windings against earth and against each other before backfill, confirming insulation resistance clears the acceptable minimum across the full 15W–500W power range.

Winding Resistance Check
Pre-Energisation

Measure primary and secondary winding resistance and compare against the manufacturer's factory figures for the specific power rating supplied, flagging any deviation before installation.

Dielectric (Hi-Pot) Withstand Test
Pre-Energisation

Apply the rated 3kV primary-secondary / 2kV primary-earth test voltage to confirm the transformer withstands its rated dielectric strength without breakdown.

Loop Continuity Test
Pre-Energisation

Verify 6.6A series loop continuity through the transformer's primary winding and confirm no unintended breaks exist before the circuit is first energised.

5-Step Current / Secondary Voltage Verification
Energisation

With the CCR energised, step through the 2.8A–6.6A dimming range and confirm secondary voltage at each step matches the transformer's rated ratio for the connected load.

CCR Step Response Check
Energisation

Cycle the CCR through every intensity step and confirm the transformer's secondary output tracks each step correctly, ruling out a marginal winding or loose connection.

Connector Sealing & Encapsulation Check
Pre-Energisation

Inspect L-823 connector seals and epoxy/potting encapsulation for damage, confirming IP68 integrity before the unit is buried or backfilled.

Documentation & As-Built Sign-off
Final Acceptance

Record insulation resistance, winding resistance, dielectric, and secondary voltage readings for the installed power rating in the commissioning dossier for handover.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Primary Connection Corrosion

Primary terminals (L‑823 connectors) are susceptible to corrosion in wet or salty environments. Use dielectric grease and ensure proper torque to maintain a low‑resistance connection. Periodic inspection and cleaning are recommended.

Incorrect Transformer Sizing

Selecting a transformer with the wrong power rating (e.g., using a 65W transformer for a 45W lamp) can cause over‑voltage and premature lamp failure, or under‑voltage and reduced light output. Always match the transformer rating to the fixture's power requirement.

Moisture Ingress

Although IP68 rated, damage to the encapsulation or cable entries can allow moisture ingress, leading to insulation degradation and premature failure. Insulation resistance testing (≥ 2 MΩ at 500V) is recommended during routine maintenance.

Secondary Short Circuit

Damaged secondary cables or fixture faults can create a short, causing excessive current draw and potential CCR tripping. Ensure secondary cable insulation is intact and use proper cable glands to prevent chafing.

Retrofit Compatibility with LED

When upgrading from halogen to LED, ensure the LED driver accepts the transformer's secondary voltage and that the total power does not exceed the transformer's rating. Some LED retrofits may require a different secondary voltage (e.g., 12V instead of 6.6V).

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Downstream Lights Dark, Loop Otherwise Continuous
Likely Cause
Open-circuit primary winding, or a corroded/loose L-823 primary connection at this transformer, breaking the 6.6A series loop to every fixture fed beyond it.
Diagnostic Steps
Confirm series loop continuity through the transformer primary; megger the primary winding; check the L-823 connectors on both sides for corrosion or looseness and proper torque.
Corrective Action
Re-terminate corroded or loose connectors with dielectric grease and correct torque, or replace the transformer if the primary winding has failed.
CCR Trips on Overcurrent or Ground Fault
Likely Cause
Moisture ingress from a damaged encapsulation or cable entry has degraded insulation resistance below the acceptable minimum.
Diagnostic Steps
Megger the transformer (target ≥ 2 MΩ at 500V); visually inspect the encapsulation and cable entries for cracking or damage.
Corrective Action
Replace the transformer if insulation resistance is below minimum; ensure the replacement is correctly sealed at the cable entries before backfill.
Fixture Over- or Under-Voltage / Premature Lamp Failure
Likely Cause
The transformer's power rating doesn't match the connected fixture's requirement (for example, a 65W transformer feeding a 45W lamp, or vice versa).
Diagnostic Steps
Check the fixture's rated power and secondary voltage against the transformer's nameplate; measure actual secondary voltage under load with the CCR energised.
Corrective Action
Replace the transformer with one correctly matched to the fixture's power and voltage requirement.
Secondary Short Circuit / CCR Overcurrent Trip
Likely Cause
Damaged secondary cable insulation or a fixture fault is creating a short, drawing excessive current through the transformer.
Diagnostic Steps
Disconnect the fixture and test the transformer secondary in isolation; inspect secondary cable jackets for chafing or damage near the cable gland.
Corrective Action
Repair or replace the damaged secondary cable with proper cable glands, and repair or replace the faulted fixture before re-energising.
LED Retrofit Fixture Flickers or Fails to Start
Likely Cause
The transformer's secondary voltage doesn't match the LED driver's input range — some retrofits require a different secondary voltage (e.g., 12V instead of 6.6V) than the original halogen lamp used.
Diagnostic Steps
Check the LED driver's rated input voltage against the transformer's actual secondary output under load; confirm total connected load does not exceed the transformer's rating.
Corrective Action
Fit a transformer with the secondary voltage matched to the LED driver, or select an LED-optimised isolating transformer for the retrofit.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

Primary Winding Open Circuit
High Severity
Root Cause
Wire fatigue from repeated thermal cycling, a manufacturing defect in the winding, or a blown internal fuse-wire link from a lightning-induced transient.
Effect
Opens the series loop, darkening every fixture downstream of the transformer on that circuit.
Detection Method
CCR loop-continuity/open-circuit indication, and megger or continuity testing at the transformer primary.
Insulation Breakdown from Moisture Ingress
High Severity
Root Cause
Cracked or degraded encapsulation, a failed cable gland or seal, or prolonged submersion in a flooded handhole or pit.
Effect
Reduced insulation resistance leading to a partial or full ground fault that can trip the CCR.
Detection Method
Megger testing against the ≥2 MΩ minimum, and visual inspection of the encapsulation and cable entries.
Secondary Winding Short or Breakdown
High Severity
Root Cause
Internal insulation failure between secondary turns, often accelerated by sustained overtemperature operation.
Effect
Loss of secondary voltage regulation, fixture dimming or failure, and possible primary overcurrent.
Detection Method
Secondary voltage measurement under load, compared against the transformer's rated output.
Overload / Core Saturation from Rating Mismatch
Medium Severity
Root Cause
A CCR step or connected fixture load exceeding the transformer's rated primary current or secondary power.
Effect
Increased core losses, overheating, and accelerated insulation ageing.
Detection Method
Verification of the CCR step current against the transformer's rating plate, and thermal inspection where accessible.
L-823 Connector / Termination Corrosion
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Improper seating during installation, gasket failure allowing moisture in, or galvanic corrosion building up at the connector pins over time.
Effect
Intermittent contact, flicker, or a high-resistance joint that generates localised heat.
Detection Method
Visual inspection at scheduled maintenance, and continuity or resistance checks across the joint.
Encapsulation Cracking / Case Damage
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Freeze-thaw cycling, mechanical impact during installation or maintenance, or age-related degradation of the potting compound.
Effect
Exposes the winding to moisture, accelerating insulation breakdown.
Detection Method
Visual inspection during handhole or pit access, and a drop in insulation resistance on megger testing.
Thermal Degradation from Sustained Overcurrent
High Severity
Root Cause
Repeated or prolonged operation above rated current, often from an incorrectly set CCR step or a transformer undersized for the installed load.
Effect
Progressive insulation embrittlement that shortens service life and raises the risk of an eventual winding failure.
Detection Method
Rating verification against actual loop current, and insulation resistance trending across successive maintenance cycles.
Output Voltage Drift / Regulation Loss
Low Severity
Root Cause
Gradual winding degradation or core ageing that shifts the transformer's turns-ratio performance away from its original specification.
Effect
Fixture brightness drifts slightly from the intended intensity step without an outright fault.
Detection Method
Photometric intensity survey and secondary voltage measurement compared against commissioning records.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Series Circuit Isolation
Electrical Safety

The isolating transformer sits on a constant-current series loop that can carry several kV. De-energise and lock out the CCR, then verify zero energy at the transformer before opening a handhole, pit, or connector housing.

Confirm Correct Transformer in Circuit
Operational

On multi-transformer series circuits, positively identify the correct unit before isolating work, since opening the wrong handhole can leave live conductors exposed while the intended fixture stays dark.

Insulation Test Before Re-Energisation
Electrical Safety

After any cable, connector, or transformer work, megger test the primary and secondary windings against the minimum insulation resistance before restoring power, since a residual fault at re-energisation can trip the CCR or damage healthy transformers on the loop.

Rating Verification Before Replacement
Compliance

Confirm the replacement transformer's rated primary current and secondary power match the CCR step and connected fixture before installing it; an incorrectly rated unit will saturate, overheat, or under-drive the fixture.

PPE & Grounding
Personnel Safety

Use insulated tools and appropriate PPE when working on series-circuit connections, and confirm the transformer case and cable shielding are bonded/grounded per the electrical design before reconnecting the loop.

FOD Control at the Handhole
Operational

Keep tools, fasteners, and removed gaskets accounted for at all times when a handhole or pit is open, and secure or remove any loose material before reinstating the cover, to prevent foreign object debris on the movement area.

Hot Surface Contact
Personnel Safety

Allow the transformer case to cool before handling after an extended active period, since sustained loop current can leave the encapsulation and connectors noticeably warm to the touch.

Restore to Compliant State
Compliance

Do not leave a transformer with a degraded insulation reading, a loose connection, or a mis-seated cover after maintenance; verify insulation resistance and secure closure before closing out the work order.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
Same Primary Current, Very Different Secondary Loads
Compliance

Every wattage in this family shares the same 6.6A primary rating, so a wrong-wattage swap won't trip anything obviously. Verify the wattage label matches the fixture's actual requirement, not just that “it's a 6.6A transformer.”

Standardise on a Few Wattages per Site, Not the Full Catalogue
Operational

Stocking every wattage from 15W to 500W at one airport multiplies the chance of an installer grabbing the wrong one from the store. Most airfields only need three or four wattages in practice.

Label the Handhole, Not Just the Transformer
Maintenance

A legible wattage/rating tag inside the handhole cover saves a technician from having to dig out and read a corroded nameplate just to confirm what's installed.

6.6A Doesn't Mean Interchangeable With 20A Systems
Electrical

Despite the shared naming convention, a 6.6A-rated transformer must never be substituted into a 20A circuit or vice versa. Confirm circuit current rating before pulling a replacement off the shelf.

Undersizing Shows Up as Heat Long Before It Shows Up as Failure
Troubleshooting

A 15W or 25W transformer forced to carry a higher-draw retrofit fixture will run hot for a long time before it actually fails. A warm-to-touch unit during a routine check is worth investigating even with no fault reported.

Baseline Insulation Readings at Commissioning Pay Off Later
Inspection

Record the as-installed megger reading for every 6.6A transformer at commissioning, so later readings have something specific to trend against rather than only ever comparing to the bare minimum standard.
