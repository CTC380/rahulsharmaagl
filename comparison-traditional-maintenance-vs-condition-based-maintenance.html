Traditional Maintenance VS Condition-Based Maintenance

Traditional maintenance is mainly driven by fixed schedules, inspections and failures; condition-based maintenance (CBM) uses the actual condition of equipment — measured through inspections, monitoring, alarms and trends — to decide when maintenance is needed. For AGL, this applies to CCRs, series circuits, isolation transformers, runway/taxiway fixtures, PAPI, signs, cables and control systems.

02 · Traditional Maintenance

A Predetermined Schedule

Traditional maintenance generally follows a predetermined schedule — for example, "inspect AGL fixtures every 3 months" or "replace a particular lamp after a defined operating period." The equipment may be perfectly healthy, but maintenance is performed because the scheduled interval has arrived.

03 · Condition-Based Maintenance

What Is the Actual Condition of the Equipment?

CBM asks a different question: what is the actual condition of the equipment? Maintenance is initiated when the measured condition indicates that intervention is required.

Equipment
↓
Inspection / Sensors
↓
Condition Data → Assessment
↓
Condition Acceptable? YES → Continue · NO → Maintenance
04 · AGL Example — CCR

Scheduled Check vs Continuous Trend

Traditional

CCR → scheduled inspection → check current → check alarms → record result. The inspection occurs according to a fixed maintenance schedule.

Condition-Based

CCR → continuous monitoring → current/voltage/temperature/alarms → trend analysis → abnormal condition → maintenance decision, based more heavily on actual operating condition.

05 · Example — CCR Temperature Trend

Catching Deterioration Before Failure

Suppose a Smart CCR normally operates at a stable temperature. Traditional maintenance checks the CCR during scheduled maintenance and finds temperature normal. A monitoring system, by contrast, can observe a developing trend:

06 · Example — LED Fixture, Series Circuit, PAPI & Signage

The Same Pattern Across AGL Assets

Traditional

  • LED fixture: every 3 months → visual inspection → clean/repair if required
  • Series circuit: scheduled inspection → electrical testing → circuit condition
  • PAPI: scheduled inspection → alignment check → photometric check → correct if necessary
  • Signage: physical condition, sign face, mounting, wiring, illumination checked at scheduled intervals

Condition-Based

  • LED fixture: temperature, output, driver alarms, abnormal electrical behaviour or repeated faults → investigation → repair/replacement
  • Series circuit: circuit monitoring → current/voltage behaviour → trend → abnormal condition → investigation
  • PAPI: driver/power/alarm information → abnormal condition → investigation
  • Signage: driver status, electrical condition, temperature, fault alarms, operating status monitored where supported

Remote circuit monitoring does not necessarily identify the exact failed fixture, and electronic condition monitoring does not replace required PAPI alignment and photometric verification.

07 · Traditional Does Not Mean "Bad"

Some Activities Must Stay Scheduled

Traditional scheduled maintenance remains very useful. Some activities must be performed periodically regardless of whether the equipment reports a normal condition:

  • Physical inspection
  • Cleaning
  • Alignment checks
  • Electrical safety testing
  • Field photometry where required
  • Mechanical inspection

CBM complements scheduled maintenance; it does not eliminate every scheduled inspection or test.

08 · CBM vs Preventive Maintenance

Not the Same Thing

Preventive maintenance

Maintenance is performed before failure, usually according to a schedule or known maintenance interval. Time interval → maintenance.

Condition-based maintenance

Maintenance is performed based on measured/observed condition. Condition → threshold/assessment → maintenance.

All CBM is preventive in intent, but not all preventive maintenance is condition-based.

09 · CBM vs Predictive Maintenance

Also Not Identical

Condition-based

Current condition → condition unacceptable → maintenance.

Predictive

Historical + real-time data → trend/model → predicted deterioration/failure → maintenance before failure.

Predictive maintenance is generally a more advanced application of condition information.

10 · Maintenance Decision Model

Not Every Alarm Means Immediate Replacement

Condition
↓
Normal → Continue Operation  |  Abnormal → Assess Severity
↓
Maintenance Required? NO → Monitor · YES → Repair

This prevents every alarm from automatically becoming an immediate replacement.

11 · Advantages & Disadvantages

Weighing the Two Approaches

Traditional Maintenance

Advantages

  • Simple, easy to plan, predictable workload
  • Low technology requirement
  • Doesn't require continuous monitoring
  • Familiar to maintenance teams

Disadvantages

  • May perform maintenance too early
  • May miss failures between inspections
  • More manual effort, limited condition history
  • Less optimized for large asset populations

Condition-Based Maintenance

Advantages

  • Maintenance based on actual condition
  • Early detection of deterioration
  • Potentially fewer unnecessary interventions
  • Better equipment history & planning; supports remote monitoring

Disadvantages

  • Requires monitoring/inspection data
  • Higher initial investment, more complex data management
  • Requires trained personnel
  • Sensors/monitoring systems require maintenance themselves; poor-quality data can lead to poor decisions

15 · Important Distinction

Monitoring ≠ CBM

Simply installing a monitoring system does not automatically create condition-based maintenance.

This is monitoring

Temperature sensor → temperature displayed.

This is CBM

Temperature sensor → condition assessment → defined criterion → maintenance decision.

Data becomes CBM only when it is used systematically to make maintenance decisions based on equipment condition.

16 · Common Misconceptions

Myths vs Facts

Myth: Installing sensors automatically means CBM
Fact

A displayed reading is monitoring. It only becomes CBM once condition data is systematically assessed against a defined criterion to trigger a maintenance decision.

Myth: CBM eliminates scheduled inspections
Fact

Physical inspection, cleaning, alignment checks, electrical safety testing and field photometry still need to happen periodically regardless of reported condition.

Myth: CBM and predictive maintenance are the same thing
Fact

CBM reacts to current condition against a threshold. Predictive maintenance goes further, using historical and real-time data to model and forecast deterioration before it happens.

18 · Engineering Verdict

Which Approach Applies Where?

Engineering Verdict

Traditional maintenance asks, "When is the next maintenance interval?" whereas condition-based maintenance asks, "What is the actual condition of this equipment, and does it now require maintenance?"

For a modern Smart AGL, the ideal strategy is usually scheduled preventive maintenance + continuous/periodic condition monitoring + corrective maintenance when required + predictive techniques where sufficient historical data exists.

TraditionalSimple, predictable and still necessary for physical inspection, cleaning and formal testing.
CBMStrong complement to scheduled maintenance, especially where Smart CCR/remote monitoring is available.

19 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Does condition-based maintenance replace traditional scheduled maintenance?

No. CBM complements scheduled maintenance; it does not eliminate every scheduled inspection or test — some activities like physical inspection and electrical safety testing must still happen periodically.

Is CBM the same as preventive maintenance?

No. All CBM is preventive in intent, but not all preventive maintenance is condition-based — preventive maintenance can also be purely schedule-driven.

Is CBM the same as predictive maintenance?

No. Predictive maintenance is generally a more advanced application of condition information, using historical and real-time data to forecast deterioration before it happens.

Does installing a monitoring system automatically create CBM?

No. A displayed sensor reading is monitoring. Data becomes CBM only when it is used systematically to make maintenance decisions based on equipment condition against a defined criterion.

Does remote CCR monitoring identify the exact failed fixture in the field?

Not necessarily. Remote circuit monitoring does not necessarily identify the exact failed fixture, and electronic condition monitoring does not replace required alignment and photometric verification for equipment like PAPI.

Is CBM always cheaper than traditional maintenance?

Not necessarily at the outset. CBM generally has a higher initial system cost and requires trained personnel and data management, though it can potentially reduce unnecessary maintenance and unexpected failures over time.
