Post-Maintenance Testing & Restoration

The formal technical verification and return-to-service division of the handbook. Maintenance is not considered complete merely because a repair has been physically finished — this division is the bridge that carries a completed repair through testing, verification, restoration and final inspection before it is authorized to return to service.

Core rule: no AGL asset shall be returned to service following maintenance until the applicable testing, functional verification, restoration and final inspection requirements have been satisfied. Not every activity requires every test in this division — selection depends on the equipment, the defect, the work performed, and the applicable approved requirements.

Return-to-Service Requirements

Four requirement groups must be closed out before the responsible authorized person can formally confirm return to service.

Testing Complete

  • Electrical tests satisfactory
  • Functional tests satisfactory
  • Required photometric / optical verification satisfactory
  • Test results compared against acceptance criteria

Restoration Complete

  • Work area restored, FOD removed
  • Pit covers and pavement restored
  • Cable route restored, markers reinstated
  • Equipment identification restored

Final Inspection

  • Equipment, electrical, and operational checks complete
  • No exposed electrical hazard
  • Earthing / bonding restored
  • Area confirmed safe

Documentation & Authorization

  • Required documentation complete
  • Operational coordination / authorization satisfied
  • Responsible authorized person confirms return to service
  • Maintenance record and test records attached

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about post-maintenance testing & restoration, based on the topics in this division.

7 QUESTIONS
Q1What is Post-Maintenance Testing & Restoration?

It is the formal technical verification and return-to-service division. Maintenance is not complete merely because a repair is physically finished; this division carries a completed repair through testing, verification and restoration to formal return to service.

Q2What does Division 23 of the AGL Maintenance Handbook cover?

Division 23 (Post-Maintenance Testing & Restoration) contains 33 topics in four groups: Group A Electrical Testing (8 topics), Group B Functional Testing (12 topics), Group C Photometry (4 topics) and Group D Restoration (9 topics).

Q3When can an AGL asset be returned to service after maintenance?

Not until the applicable testing, functional verification, restoration and final inspection requirements have been satisfied. Not every activity requires every test, and the actual tests required are determined by the applicable approved maintenance and test procedure and the work performed.

Q4What is the post-maintenance verification sequence?

Maintenance, Testing, Verification, Restoration, Final Inspection, Return to Service and Closure, across four areas: electrical testing, functional testing, photometry and restoration.

Q5What is the Post-Maintenance Test Matrix?

A planning aid that maps each maintenance activity, such as fixture replacement, LED or module replacement, connector replacement, cable or joint repair, transformer replacement, CCR, PAPI, sign and ALCMS maintenance, and base-can or civil work, to the electrical test, functional test, photometry and restoration that may apply. The actual tests are set by the approved procedure and the work performed.

Q6What must be closed out before return to service?

Four requirement groups. Testing complete means electrical, functional and any required photometric or optical checks are satisfactory and compared against acceptance criteria. Restoration complete means the work area is restored and FOD removed, pit covers, pavement and cable route are restored, markers reinstated and identification restored. Final inspection means equipment, electrical and operational checks are complete, there is no exposed electrical hazard, earthing and bonding are restored and the area is safe. Documentation and authorization means required documentation is complete, operational coordination is satisfied, the responsible authorized person confirms return to service, and the maintenance and test records are attached.

Q7What is the core sequence from finished work to closed record?

Eight steps: maintenance completed, electrical testing, functional testing, photometric verification where required, area restoration, final inspection, return to service confirmed by the responsible authorized person, and documentation closure of the maintenance record, test records and asset history.
