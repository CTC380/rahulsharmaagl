01 · Maintenance

Airport AGL Maintenance & Technical Support

AGL maintenance involves much more than replacing a defective light. An AGL system contains interconnected electrical, lighting and control components, so a fault in one part of the chain can affect a single fixture or an entire circuit. Maintenance should therefore consider the complete system rather than only the visible fixture.

AGL chain: CCR → Primary Cable → Primary Connector → Isolating Transformer → Secondary Connector → Light Fixture.

02 · Objectives

What AGL Maintenance Maintains

AGL maintenance is the planned inspection, testing, servicing, repair and replacement of airport lighting equipment and associated infrastructure. The objective is to maintain:

Equipment availabilityLighting performanceElectrical integritySystem serviceabilityCorrect fixture alignmentReliable control & monitoringOperational readiness

The exact maintenance programme depends on airport configuration, operational category, installed equipment, airport procedures, applicable regulations, manufacturer requirements, project specifications and operational requirements.

04 · Categories

AGL Maintenance Types

A practical maintenance programme can be divided into several categories. The actual strategy should follow the airport's established procedures.

Preventive Maintenance

Planned activities performed before a failure occurs.

  • Scheduled inspection
  • Cleaning
  • Connector & transformer inspection
  • Cable & CCR checks
  • Alignment & photometric checks

Corrective Maintenance

Work carried out after a defect or failure is identified.

  • Failed fixture / lamp / LED replacement
  • Transformer & connector replacement
  • Cable repair
  • CCR & control-system fault correction

Airport Lighting Breakdown Maintenance

Immediate investigation and restoration following an unexpected failure.

  • Fault report & circuit ID
  • Fault isolation & testing
  • Defective component ID
  • Repair / replace & return to service

Airport Lighting Emergency Maintenance

Urgent work on a failure that affects operations, such as lost lights on an active runway, taxiway or approach circuit.

  • Follows the breakdown workflow
  • Coordinated with airport operations and ATC
  • Temporary measures until the system is restored

Condition-Based Maintenance

Planned around indicators of equipment condition.

  • Repeated failures
  • Reduced lighting performance
  • Insulation / connector / transformer deterioration
  • Equipment alarms & physical damage

07 · Connectors & Transformers

Connector & Isolating Transformer Inspection

Connectors and isolating transformers are critical points in the series-circuit system, and both should form part of systematic maintenance.

Connector Inspection

A defective connector can result in:

  • Intermittent operation
  • High-resistance connection
  • Open circuit
  • Fixture failure / circuit problems

Isolating Transformer Inspection

Checks may include:

  • Physical & connection condition
  • Insulation & environmental condition
  • Signs of water ingress
  • Rating verification & primary/secondary connection

Where a fixture repeatedly fails, the transformer should also be considered during fault investigation.

08 · Circuit & Cable

Primary Series-Circuit & Cable Testing

Series-Circuit Maintenance

The primary AGL series circuit connects the CCR to multiple lighting units.

  • Circuit & connector checks
  • Cable & transformer testing
  • Insulation & continuity testing
  • Fault location & performance verification

Cable Testing

Potential cable-related problems:

  • Open / short circuit
  • Insulation deterioration
  • Joint / termination failure
  • Physical & water-related damage

A systematic approach helps determine whether a lighting failure is local to one fixture or associated with the wider circuit. Exact test procedures and acceptance criteria should follow the applicable airport maintenance and testing requirements.

12 · Failure Patterns

Multiple & Intermittent Faults

Multiple Light Failures

Investigation should begin at the common parts of the system: CCR, primary series circuit, cable, common connector, control system, power supply.

  • Are the failed fixtures on the same circuit or common equipment?

Intermittent Faults

More difficult to identify since equipment may operate normally during a basic inspection.

  • Loose connection / connector condition
  • Cable joint & transformer connection
  • Insulation deterioration
  • CCR behaviour & control-system communication

25 · Standards & Support

AGL Maintenance Standards & Support Scope

AGL maintenance requirements in India can involve ICAO, DGCA, AAI / airport maintenance procedures, airport-specific technical specifications, BIS / Indian Standards, manufacturer instructions and project-specific maintenance requirements. BIS documentation identifies IS 12309 Part 2 for maintenance of aerodrome lighting fittings — current applicable requirements should always be verified before carrying out maintenance activities.

Runways

Edge, threshold, runway end, centreline, touchdown zone

Taxiways

Edge, centreline, stop bars, runway guard lights

Electrical

CCR, transformers, cables, connectors

Control

Control, monitoring, ALCMS interfaces where applicable

FAA Airport Lighting Maintenance

FAA maintenance guidance, such as AC 150/5340-26C on maintenance of airport visual aid facilities, may be referred to where FAA-type equipment is used or the project specification calls for it. At Indian airports the governing procedures are normally ICAO, DGCA, AAI and BIS based, so confirm which document applies and its current edition. See ICAO vs FAA.

Airport Lighting Maintenance Technician

An airport lighting maintenance technician needs a working knowledge of series circuits, CCR operation, isolating transformers, connectors, test instruments and the airport's maintenance procedures. Safe working matters, because a series circuit can still carry current when the lights look off. Training builds these skills; see the AGL Academy.

A failed light may be caused by a component located several steps upstream — a defective connector, transformer, cable, CCR or control fault, not the fixture itself. AGL maintenance should therefore combine inspection, electrical testing, fault diagnosis, equipment knowledge and functional verification as a single system activity.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is AGL maintenance important at Indian airports?
AGL maintenance is essential for maintaining availability and supporting airport operations, including fault diagnosis, component replacement and photometric testing.
What maintenance activities are typically carried out?
Visual, fixture, connector and transformer inspection, series-circuit checks, cable testing, CCR checks, fault diagnosis, component replacement, photometric testing and post-maintenance verification.
Why does serviceability matter more for CAT II/III operations?
For low-visibility operations, serviceability of critical AGL facilities becomes particularly important, with Indian AIP material documenting specific serviceability and inspection requirements for LVP and CAT II/III operations.
Why does a light stay off even after the fixture is replaced?
A failed light may be caused by a component several steps upstream — the secondary connector, isolating transformer, primary connector, cable, CCR or control system — not the fixture itself. Diagnosis should trace the complete AGL chain before replacing components.
What should be checked for an LED retrofit project?
Beyond the new LED fixtures, the existing CCR, isolating transformers, cables, connectors and control/monitoring system should all be assessed for compatibility before replacement, testing, photometry and commissioning.

Keyword Reference

AGL Maintenance Contracts in India

Airfield lighting maintenance company

An airfield lighting maintenance company provides trained staff, test equipment and spares to keep the AGL system serviceable. It should know the airport's circuits, CCRs and control system and work to the airport's procedures.

AGL maintenance contract

An AGL maintenance contract defines the inspections, tests, response times, spares, reporting and responsibilities for the airfield lighting system. A clear scope and measurable service levels make performance easy to review.

Airfield lighting annual maintenance contract

An airfield lighting annual maintenance contract covers planned maintenance and fault attendance over a year. It normally lists the schedule of inspections, what is included and excluded, and how records are handed to the airport.
