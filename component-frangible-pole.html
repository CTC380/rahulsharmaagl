Frangible Pole

A purpose‑designed, breakaway support structure for elevated AGL fixtures that shears cleanly upon aircraft impact, minimising damage to the aircraft and ensuring pilot and passenger safety. Engineered to meet strict frangibility requirements while maintaining structural integrity under normal operational loads.

Overview

What Is It?

A Frangible Pole (also known as a breakaway pole, shear pole, or frangible mast) is a specially engineered support structure designed to hold elevated AGL light fixtures, signs, or other airfield equipment at the required height above the pavement, while incorporating a deliberate weak point that causes the pole to break away cleanly upon impact by an aircraft. This frangible feature is a critical safety requirement specified by ICAO Annex 14 and FAA standards, aimed at minimising damage to the aircraft and protecting lives in the event of a runway or taxiway excursion.

Key characteristics of frangible poles include:

  • Controlled Breakaway: The pole is designed with a frangible coupling or a weakened section that fractures in a predictable manner when subjected to a defined impact force, typically at or near the base.
  • Structural Integrity: Under normal operating conditions (wind, vibration, fixture weight), the pole behaves as a rigid, stable structure, supporting the fixture securely.
  • Corrosion Protection: Hot‑dip galvanised or stainless steel construction ensures long‑term durability in the harsh airfield environment.
  • Fixture Compatibility: Designed to accommodate standard AGL fixtures and mounting hardware, with appropriate bolt patterns and cable routing.

Frangible poles are used in the following applications:

  • Elevated Runway Edge Lights: Supporting runway edge light fixtures, especially on curves and intersections.
  • Elevated Taxiway Edge Lights: Supporting taxiway edge lights where flush mounting is not feasible.
  • Approach Lighting: Supporting elevated approach light fixtures and barrettes.
  • Obstruction Lighting: Supporting obstruction lights on poles within the airfield.
  • Airfield Signage: Supporting elevated mandatory instruction signs and direction signs.

The frangible pole is typically mounted on a base plate or base can, and is designed to break at a specific height above the pavement (usually 25‑50mm) when subjected to a lateral impact force of a defined magnitude, ensuring that the aircraft sustains minimal damage.

Purpose

Purpose & Function

Certain fixtures — approach lights, elevated edge lights, obstruction lights — only work if they are raised above the pavement or surrounding terrain, but any object raised into an aircraft's operating envelope is, by definition, a potential obstacle. The frangible pole's purpose is to let a fixture keep the height it needs for correct photometric coverage while removing the danger that height would otherwise create. Under normal conditions the pole must behave as an ordinary rigid structure, holding the fixture steady against wind and vibration so its beam stays correctly aimed; the moment it is struck by an aircraft, that same structure is designed to fail at a known, predictable point and load, breaking away cleanly rather than absorbing the impact rigidly. This is what allows AGL to place fixtures in positions — close to runway edges, on approach paths, at low elevations near the aircraft's flight path — that would otherwise be unacceptable safety risks.

Application

Where Is It Used?

Frangible poles are installed in areas where elevated AGL fixtures are required and where aircraft may potentially impact them, including:

  • Runway Edge Lighting: Supporting elevated runway edge light fixtures on the runway strip, especially on curves, runway intersections, and where flush mounting is not possible due to snow or terrain.
  • Taxiway Edge Lighting: Supporting elevated taxiway edge lights, particularly on curves, at intersections, and on taxiway shoulders.
  • Approach Lighting Systems: Supporting elevated approach light fixtures, barrettes, and flashing light units in the approach area.
  • Obstruction Lighting: Supporting obstruction lights on poles located within the runway strip, taxiway strip, or approach areas.
  • Airfield Signage: Supporting elevated mandatory instruction signs, direction signs, and information signs that are located within aircraft movement areas.
  • Sensor Mounting: Supporting anemometers, wind cones, and other instrumentation in aircraft movement areas.

The poles are designed to meet the frangibility requirements specified in ICAO Annex 14, which mandates that any fixture or structure located within the runway strip or taxiway strip must break away or yield upon impact to prevent aircraft damage. The poles are available in various heights (typically 300mm to 1500mm) and diameters to accommodate different fixture types and operational requirements.

Placement

Installation & Location

The pole's frangible coupling seats on a foundation set flush with or just below grade — typically the same base can (L-867 type) that carries the fixture's electrical connection — so the break point sits at the designed low-fracture-force height rather than at ground level where it would be less predictable. Foundation position is set out from the runway/taxiway centreline and edge survey, kept within the required setback from the pavement edge while staying clear of the graded strip's load-bearing width, and the pole's fracture height is checked against the obstacle-limitation surface for that runway category so the fitting on top stays within the allowable envelope. Cabling from the base can up to the fixture runs inside the pole itself, sized with enough slack at the coupling to allow reconnection after a frangible break without needing to re-run the underground cable.

Function

How Does It Work?

The frangible pole functions as a support structure that provides the required height for the fixture while incorporating a controlled failure mechanism that activates upon impact:

  • Normal Operation: Under normal conditions, the pole acts as a rigid, stable column, supporting the fixture's weight and resisting wind loads, vibration, and thermal expansion. The frangible coupling or weakened section is designed to withstand all expected operational loads without failure.
  • Impact Activation: When an aircraft contacts the pole (typically during a runway excursion or taxiway incursion), the impact force is transmitted through the pole to the frangible section. The frangible section is designed to fracture at a specific force threshold, typically at the base of the pole (25‑50mm above the mounting plate).
  • Clean Breakaway: The pole shears cleanly at the frangible point, allowing the fixture and the upper portion of the pole to separate from the base. The design ensures that the pole does not become a projectile or cause additional damage to the aircraft.
  • Energy Absorption: The frangible coupling is often designed with a shear‑type mechanism that absorbs some of the impact energy, further reducing the force transmitted to the aircraft.

The frangible coupling is typically a precision‑machined component that incorporates shear pins, a reduced‑diameter section, or a notched design that ensures a predictable fracture. The coupling is designed to meet specific frangibility criteria:

  • Shear Force Threshold: The force required to break the pole is typically set between 2 kN and 10 kN (depending on the pole size and fixture type), ensuring that the pole will break before causing significant aircraft damage.
  • Breakaway Height: The pole is designed to break at a height of 25‑50mm above the pavement, leaving a low‑profile base that does not pose a hazard to aircraft.
  • Directional Performance: The frangible coupling is designed to break regardless of the direction of impact, providing protection from any angle.

The pole is typically installed on a base plate or base can, with the frangible coupling positioned between the base and the pole shaft. The fixture is mounted on top of the pole, with the electrical cable routed through the hollow pole or along its exterior.

Architecture

System Architecture & Control Logic

The frangible pole carries no electrical or control function — it is a support structure with a deliberately engineered breakaway point, positioned between the foundation and whatever it elevates (an approach light, a sign, or other equipment requiring height). Under normal wind and service loads it behaves as a rigid structural member; under an impact load above a defined threshold, it is designed to fail cleanly at the engineered point rather than transmitting the impact energy into the foundation or into whatever struck it. As with the breakable coupling used on lower fixtures, everything below the frangible point — the base and foundation — is sized so it is never the weak point during that failure event.

A structural member up to a defined load, then a deliberately engineered failure — the foundation is never the weak point.

Construction

Physical Construction

The pole is a hollow tubular section of structural steel or stainless steel, hot-dip galvanised for corrosion protection, sized in diameter and wall thickness to resist the specified wind loading while still yielding predictably under aircraft impact. Frangibility is built in near the base, either through a shear-pin coupling, a deliberately reduced-diameter neck, or a notched circumferential groove, engineered so the pole shears cleanly at the design threshold rather than bending or embedding fragments, minimising secondary hazard to an errant aircraft. A flat top plate with a standard AGL bolt pattern is welded or bolted to the pole's upper end to accept the light fixture, while the base is designed to mount into a below-grade footing or base can via a matching bolt circle. The galvanised or stainless finish is applied after all machining and welding to ensure full corrosion protection at the frangible section, since any localised weakening there would compromise the calibrated shear threshold.

Upkeep

Inspection & Maintenance

Frangible Coupling Check
Annual

Inspect the shear pin, reduced-diameter section, or notched design at the breakaway point for corrosion, cracking, or fatigue that could shift the 2–10 kN shear threshold.

Pole Straightness & Alignment
Semi-annual

Check the pole remains vertical and undamaged; a bent or leaning pole can concentrate stress unevenly on the frangible coupling.

Corrosion / Coating Inspection
Annual

Inspect galvanised or stainless surfaces, particularly at ground level and the coupling interface, for coating breakdown or pitting.

Top Plate / Fixture Mounting Check
Annual

Verify the top mounting plate and bolt holes are undamaged and torqued to spec so the fixture stays securely and correctly aligned.

Breakaway Height Verification
Annual

Confirm the breakaway point sits within the specified 25–50 mm above pavement height, adjusting for any resurfacing or settlement.

Wall Thickness / Impact Damage Check
Annual

Inspect for dents, cracks, or wall thinning from prior minor impacts that haven't triggered full breakaway but may have weakened the structure.

Wind Load Resistance Review
Annual

Confirm the pole shows no permanent deflection after high-wind events, verifying it still meets its rated ≥150 km/h wind resistance without yielding.

Post-Impact Replacement
As needed

Replace the pole and coupling assembly after any breakaway event rather than reinstalling the fractured section, per frangibility design intent.

Field Notes

Common Field Issues

Corrosion of Frangible Coupling

The frangible coupling is a critical safety component that must remain functional over the life of the pole. Corrosion can alter the shear force threshold, potentially preventing the pole from breaking on impact. Regularly inspect the coupling for rust, pitting, or damage. Replace if corrosion is present.

Incorrect Shear Force Setting

If the frangible coupling is not properly calibrated or has been modified (e.g., using incorrect shear pins), the shear force threshold may be too high (pole won't break) or too low (pole breaks under normal loads). Always use manufacturer‑supplied replacement parts and verify the shear force rating.

Misalignment of Frangible Coupling

If the pole is not installed vertically, the frangible coupling may experience bending loads in addition to shear, which can affect its breakaway performance. Ensure the pole is plumb and the mounting base is level.

Improper Fixture Mounting

Over‑tightening the fixture mounting bolts can deform the frangible coupling or the pole, affecting its strength and frangibility. Use a torque wrench and follow the manufacturer's torque specifications.

Damage from Snowploughs or Maintenance Vehicles

While frangible poles are designed to break on impact with aircraft, they can also be damaged by snowploughs or maintenance vehicles. Use protective covers or markers to increase visibility, and inspect poles after any known contact.

Inadequate Cable Routing

The secondary cable is often routed through the hollow pole or along its exterior. If the cable is not properly secured or protected, it can be pinched or damaged when the pole breaks, causing a fault. Use cable glands and ensure the cable has sufficient slack to allow the pole to break without pulling the cable.

Galvanic Corrosion with Base Plate

If the pole and base plate are made of dissimilar metals, galvanic corrosion can occur at the interface. Use anti‑seize compound, isolation gaskets, or ensure both components are of the same material.

Diagnostics

Troubleshooting Guide

Pole Fails to Break Away on Impact
Likely Cause
Corrosion on the frangible coupling has altered its shear force threshold, requiring a higher-than-designed load to break.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect the coupling for rust, pitting, or surface damage; compare its condition and age against the manufacturer's inspection and replacement schedule.
Corrective Action
Replace the corroded coupling with a manufacturer-supplied part rated for the correct shear force, and re-verify the pole's breakaway rating.
Pole Breaks Under Normal Wind or Service Loads
Likely Cause
An incorrect or modified shear pin/coupling assembly has set the breakaway threshold too low for normal operating loads.
Diagnostic Steps
Verify the installed coupling and shear pins against the manufacturer's specification for this pole and fixture weight.
Corrective Action
Replace with manufacturer-supplied parts matched to the correct shear force rating, and confirm the rating before reinstating the pole.
Coupling Shows Uneven Wear or Unexpected Bending Stress
Likely Cause
The pole was not installed plumb, or the mounting base is not level, subjecting the frangible coupling to bending loads in addition to shear.
Diagnostic Steps
Check the pole's verticality with a level, and inspect the base mounting for unevenness.
Corrective Action
Re-level the base and re-plumb the pole, then re-inspect the coupling for any damage caused by the prior misalignment.
Coupling or Pole Shows Deformation Near the Fixture Mount
Likely Cause
Fixture mounting bolts were over-tightened, deforming the frangible coupling or the pole body.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect the coupling and pole for deformation near the mounting point, and check bolt torque against the manufacturer's specification.
Corrective Action
Loosen and re-torque the mounting bolts to the correct value using a torque wrench, replacing any component found to be deformed.
Pole Damaged Without an Aircraft Contact Event
Likely Cause
A snowplough or maintenance vehicle has struck the pole outside of its intended breakaway function.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect the pole for impact damage and cross-check against known snow-clearing or maintenance vehicle activity in the area.
Corrective Action
Replace the damaged pole, and add or improve protective covers or markers to increase its visibility to ground vehicles.
Cable Fault Occurs When the Pole Breaks Away
Likely Cause
The secondary cable inside or along the pole was not secured or given enough slack, so it was pinched or damaged during a breakaway event.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect the cable routing and any cable glands for damage following a breakaway event; check for adequate slack at the break point.
Corrective Action
Re-route and re-secure the cable with proper glands and sufficient slack to allow the pole to break without pulling or pinching the cable.
Corrosion Found at the Pole-to-Base-Plate Interface
Likely Cause
The pole and base plate are made of dissimilar metals, causing galvanic corrosion at their interface.
Diagnostic Steps
Inspect the interface for corrosion products, and confirm the metals in use for both the pole and base plate.
Corrective Action
Apply anti-seize compound or isolation gaskets at the interface, or replace with matching materials to prevent further galvanic action.
Reliability

Failure Modes & Root Causes

Frangible Coupling Corrosion / Threshold Shift
High Severity
Root Cause
Rust or pitting at the shear pin, reduced-diameter neck, or notched groove from long-term moisture exposure and coating breakdown at the breakaway point.
Effect
Shear force threshold rises above its 2–10 kN design rating, so the pole may fail to break cleanly on aircraft contact and instead bend or remain embedded, defeating the frangibility safety function.
Detection Method
Annual visual and physical inspection of the coupling for rust, pitting, or surface damage, checked against the manufacturer's inspection and replacement schedule.
Incorrect or Non-Genuine Coupling Hardware
High Severity
Root Cause
A prior repair used non-manufacturer shear pins or a modified coupling assembly not matched to the pole height and fixture weight it supports.
Effect
Breakaway threshold is set too high, so the pole won't shear on impact, or too low, so it breaks under normal wind or service loads.
Detection Method
Verification of installed coupling and shear pins against the manufacturer's specification during commissioning and periodic maintenance audits.
Out-of-Plumb Installation / Base Settlement
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Pole was not set vertically at installation, or the mounting base has settled or heaved unevenly since, pre-stressing the frangible section with bending loads it wasn't designed to carry.
Effect
Coupling experiences uneven wear and altered breakaway performance, and fixture aim drifts out of tolerance.
Detection Method
Plumb and level check with the pole standing, and visual inspection of the surrounding base or pavement for settlement or heave.
Fixture Mounting Bolt Over-Torque
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Fixture mounting bolts on the top plate were tightened beyond the manufacturer's torque specification during installation or fixture replacement.
Effect
Localised deformation of the frangible coupling or pole body near the mount, altering the calibrated shear threshold.
Detection Method
Visual inspection for deformation near the mounting point and bolt torque verification with a calibrated torque wrench.
Non-Aircraft Impact Damage
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Contact from a snowplough, mower, or maintenance vehicle outside the pole's intended aircraft-impact breakaway function.
Effect
Dents, cracks, or wall thinning that weaken the pole without triggering a full breakaway, leaving latent structural damage in service.
Detection Method
Post-event visual inspection following known ground vehicle activity, and routine wall thickness / impact damage checks during scheduled maintenance.
Secondary Cable Damage at Breakaway Point
Medium Severity
Root Cause
Secondary cable routed through or along the pole was not secured with proper glands or left with insufficient slack near the breakaway point.
Effect
Cable is pinched or severed when the pole breaks away, producing a circuit fault at the same moment as the mechanical failure.
Detection Method
Post-breakaway inspection of cable routing and glands, and pre-emptive slack/routing checks during annual maintenance.
Galvanic Corrosion at Base Plate Interface
Low Severity
Root Cause
Dissimilar metals used for the pole and base plate create a galvanic couple that corrodes preferentially at the mating interface.
Effect
Progressive material loss at the interface that can loosen the base connection and, over time, migrate toward the frangible section.
Detection Method
Annual corrosion/coating inspection of the base plate interface, and confirmation of pole and base plate material compatibility during maintenance review.
Precautions

Safety & Operational Notes

Frangibility Not to Be Defeated
Mechanical

Never guy, brace, weld, or otherwise stiffen a frangible pole beyond its design, and never substitute its coupling with a rigid fitting; the pole must break away cleanly at the specified load on aircraft impact.

Series Circuit Isolation
Electrical Safety

De-energise and lock out the circuit and verify zero energy before working on the pole base, cabling, or the fixture it supports.

Working at Height
Personnel Safety

Use appropriate fall protection and stable access equipment when working on taller poles, and account for wind loading on the pole and any ladder or platform in use.

Environmental Load Awareness
Structural

Avoid installation or maintenance work in high wind, ice, or storm conditions, since the pole's slender frangible design is more vulnerable to lateral loading while partially secured or under repair.

Airside Work Coordination
Operational

Coordinate with ATC/airside and raise a NOTAM where the fixture will be dark or out of tolerance during pole work, and keep vehicles and personnel clear of the runway or taxiway strip.

FOD Control
Operational

Account for all fasteners and tools used at height, and clear the base area completely before reopening it to traffic.

Verify Shear Threshold After Any Work
Compliance

Confirm coupling torque and hardware match the manufacturer's specification exactly after any repair, since deviations can raise or lower the pole's designed breakaway force.

Field Insight

Engineering & Expert Notes

RS
By Rahul Sharma
Airfield Ground Lighting Specialist
The Coupling, Not the Pole Material, Usually Defines the Break Threshold
Design

It's a common misconception that pole material alone determines frangibility; in most designs the engineered coupling is the actual weak point, and the pole itself may be quite robust. Verify frangibility performance at the coupling specifically, not by assessing the pole material in isolation.

A Straight, Undamaged Pole Can Still Have a Degraded Coupling
Inspection

Visual inspection of the pole's structural condition tells you little about the coupling's current shear characteristics, which can drift with age and environmental exposure. Keep coupling verification on its own inspection cycle.

Wind-Induced Sway Fatigues Connections Before It Bends the Pole
Alignment

Repeated wind-induced sway over years tends to fatigue bolted or welded connections at the pole base and fixture mount before it produces any visible bend in the pole itself; include connection-point fatigue in the inspection scope, not just gross structural condition.

Verify Internal Cable Routing Accommodates Full Breakaway Travel
Electrical

Cabling routed up through a frangible pole needs slack matching the coupling's designed travel distance; a taut internal cable can prevent the clean breakaway the pole is designed to provide, or damage the cable and create a secondary hazard on impact.

Internal Condensation Can Corrode From the Inside of a Hollow Pole
Durability

Hollow frangible poles can accumulate internal condensation from daily thermal cycling; check for adequate drainage or venting at the base rather than assuming external weatherproofing alone protects the structure.

Never Field-Weld or Reinforce a Frangible Pole to 'Fix' Wobble
Safety

Adding reinforcement to reduce pole sway addresses a cosmetic symptom while potentially defeating the pole's designed breakaway function entirely; any modification to a frangible structure needs engineering review, not a field workaround.
