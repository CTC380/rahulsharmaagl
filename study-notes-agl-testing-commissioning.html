WHAT IS AGL COMMISSIONING?

Commissioning proves that an installed AGL system is installed correctly, electrically safe, functionally operational, optically compliant, correctly controlled and properly documented. It is not just switching on the CCR and seeing lamps light. The whole chain is in scope:

PowerControlCCRPrimary circuitIsolation transformerSecondary circuitConnectorFixtureOptical output

plus signs, PAPI, ALCMS, emergency power and the marking interface. Testing measures one parameter (insulation, continuity, CCR current, fixture intensity); commissioning combines the tests with functional operation, integration, control, monitoring, alarms, final settings, documentation and operational acceptance.

Memory
  • TEST = CHECK · COMMISSION = PROVE THE SYSTEM
  • Objectives: safety, correct installation, electrical and optical performance, control, monitoring, documentation

Main FAA references: AC 150/5340-26C (maintenance, safety, test equipment, series-circuit troubleshooting) and AC 150/5340-30J (design and installation), with separate equipment specifications for CCRs, transformers, connectors, fixtures, PAPI and signs.

2

TYPES OF TESTING: FAT, SAT, PRE-COMMISSIONING

StageWhere / whenFocus
FATFactory, before shipmentEquipment compliance: CCR, transformers, fixtures, signs, PAPI, panels
SATSite, after installationWiring, power, function, control, circuit performance, integration
Pre-commissioningBefore energisationFind mistakes before live operation
CommissioningWhole systemFinal proof of performance
FAT ≠ SAT. A unit that passes FAT can still fail on site through wrong cable, transformer or connector, wrong circuit connection, damage or bad control wiring.

The full sequence is set by the project ITP (Inspection & Test Plan) and method statements: material receiving, FAT, pre-installation, pre-commissioning, SAT, electrical, functional, photometric, control and monitoring, integrated, standby power, final inspection, performance verification and handover.

3

PREPARATION, DOCUMENTS, EQUIPMENT

Documents needed

Approved drawings and layout, single-line diagrams, cable, circuit, transformer and fixture schedules, sign schedule, PAPI drawings, CCR datasheets, manuals, ITP, method statements, risk assessment, FAT reports, calibration certificates, approved redlines.

Material inspection

Make, model, part number, quantity, rating, certification, condition, accessories. CCR current/class, transformer wattage/current/frequency, connector Type/Class/Style, fixture model.

Traceability

Manufacturer, serial, batch, part number, circuit number, location, test report number, especially for transformers, CCRs, PAPI and signs.

Calibration

Unique instrument ID, valid certificate, calibration and next-due dates, traceability. Do not base acceptance on uncalibrated instruments where calibrated ones are required.

Typical tools: true-RMS multimeter, clamp meter, insulation tester, continuity and voltage testers, AGL circuit tester, cable fault locator, TDR, PAPI aiming tool, level or survey equipment, photometric equipment, torque wrench, thermal camera and service laptop.

4

CABLE, CONTINUITY AND INSULATION TESTING

Pre-commissioning inspection covers electrical (panels, CCR, terminations, transformers, connectors, earthing), mechanical (fixtures, bases, frangible fittings, sign supports), optical (lens, filters, orientation) and civil (pits, foundations, drainage, cable routes). Before energising, confirm the numbering: CCR circuit = cable circuit = transformer circuit = fixture circuit.

Continuity

Finds open conductors, broken cable, open connectors or transformer windings. Continuity PASS ≠ circuit PASS: poor insulation, moisture or high-resistance joints can still be present.

Insulation resistance

Primary to ground, cable sections, transformers, secondary circuits. AC 150/5340-26C says there is no single ideal value; readings depend on length, age, equipment, installation, weather and moisture, so use consistent conditions.

Trending

Example only: 500 → 220 → 35 MΩ over three years is significant even before a limit is crossed. A trend can beat a single reading.

Acceptance values

AC 150/5340-26C notes that a series circuit below 1 MΩ generally points to rapid failure, but this is not a universal acceptance limit. Use the project specification, manufacturer requirement and standard.

De-energiseIsolateLockout / tagoutVerify no voltageDisconnect sensitive equipmentTestDischargeVerify safe
Cables can store dangerous energy after testing. Leave the tester connected (or use its automatic discharge) until the circuit has fully discharged, following AC 150/5340-26C and the instrument manual, before touching leads. Test off → discharge → verify → touch. Do not megger LED drivers, control modules, monitoring boards or communication equipment without the approved procedure.

Cable faults: open, short, ground fault, high-resistance joint, intermittent (moisture, temperature, movement). AC 150/5340-26C has procedures for ground faults, open circuits and interconnected series-circuit faults. Interrelated notes: Note 09.

Fixtures: AC 150/5345-46F covers runway, taxiway, heliport and vertiport light fixtures. Also check pits and junction boxes (water, drainage, cable arrangement, covers, sealing), and verify torque where specified for CCR and panel terminals, fixture bolts, grounding and sign connections.

6

CCR TESTING AND ENERGISATION

Before energising, check input supply, output circuit, current setting, circuit identification, protection, control wiring, monitoring, earthing and interlocks. Then test each step: selected step, measured current, output voltage, load or power, alarm, remote indication. AC 150/5340-26C gives tolerance tables for 3-step and 5-step regulators.

Local and remote

Local OFF/ON and each step; remote ON/OFF, intensity, status, fault, reset. Confirm commands at both control room and CCR.

Monitoring and faults

Use the manufacturer-approved method to simulate faults. Do not create live faults unsafely.

Loading

Check load, current, output voltage and regulation on the installed circuit. Open-secondary transformers can alter inductive load and CCR behaviour; never improvise this on an operational circuit.

Thermal and torque

Thermal imaging finds hot terminals and connector heating (a diagnostic aid, not a substitute for electrical tests).

Circuit isolatedPre-check donePersonnel clearControl room notifiedCCR onFirst stepObserveStep up

Do not energise the whole circuit at maximum intensity at once; follow the airport's approved procedure. At each brightness step check light on, colour, apparent intensity, flicker, heating, fault indications and transformer or connector abnormality. See Note 07.

PAPI

Commissioning is more than "all 4 boxes ON": supply and transformer/PCU/CCR arrangement, foundation, level, alignment, red/white transition, intensity, beam and aiming. Check each unit against its approved design angle, never a generic angle from another runway; verify horizontal alignment, vertical angle, level, spacing, elevation and tilt protection (Note 11).

Signs

Check legend against the approved sign schedule and signage drawing (taxiway and runway designation, arrow, mandatory instruction, destination, location), brightness, uniform illumination, no dark areas, correct circuit, control and status. One wrong letter is an operational problem even when the sign is electrically perfect (Note 13).

8

CONTROL, ALCMS, STANDBY POWER AND EARTHING

ALCMS

Communication, device and circuit status, ON/OFF, brightness, alarms, faults, event logging, local/remote status. Test the full command-and-feedback loop: control room → CCR → field lights → status back.

Local vs remote

Priority logic, interlocks, indication, safe recovery. Examples: wrong circuit selection blocked, safety interlock prevents operation, maintenance mode restricts remote command.

Alarms and cause-effect

Match a cause-and-effect matrix: CCR fault, circuit fault, power or communication failure, sign fault, PAPI fault, standby status. Record expected vs actual and PASS/FAIL.

Control networks

Hardwired, fibre, Ethernet, serial or field bus: continuity, insulation, termination, shield bonding, addressing, signal integrity. Fibre: link status, connector cleanliness, optical loss, redundancy. PLC/SCADA: I/O, interlocks, HMI, event log, time sync.

Standby power

Transfer operation, backup availability, CCR restart, lighting continuity, alarm, return to normal. Measure the transfer time against the required switch-over time (ICAO Annex 14 Table 8-1: generally 15 s, or 1 s for key CAT II/III lights; see Note 05). Never interrupt power uncontrolled on an operational runway.

Earthing and lightning

CCR, panel, sign, PAPI and structure bonding, with measured values where required; check surge devices and arresters for burn marks and inspect equipment after lightning.

9

PHOTOMETRY AND INSPECTIONS

Photometry verifies intensity, colour, beam distribution, alignment and uniformity. Visual inspection says "the light appears to work"; photometry says "measured output meets the requirement". ON ≠ COMPLIANT. Electrical PASS does not automatically mean photometric PASS.

Photometric levelScope
FixtureIndividual fixtures
CircuitPerformance across a circuit
Runway surveyInstalled system at defined points
PAPIOptical performance and beam geometry

Before testing: equipment ID, valid calibration, sensor condition, verified distance and geometry. Record date, time, weather, visibility, temperature, setup, brightness step and instrument ID. The measurement grid and acceptance limits come from the standard or project specification.

Night inspection suits actual light operation; FAA guidance expects daily twilight or night patrols for dim, burnt-out or misaligned lights and lens cleanliness. Record each defect by location + fixture ID + circuit ID. Daytime inspection suits physical condition: pits, signs, foundations, pavement interface. Coordinate any operational runway test with operations and ATC; low-visibility configurations, centerline guidance, stop bars, sign operation, PAPI, control logic and emergency arrangements are tested only under the approved safety procedure.

11

PUNCH LIST, NCR AND RETEST

Punch list

Electrical (labels, terminals, insulation, wrong connection), mechanical (alignment, sign damage, foundations), optical (low intensity, colour, PAPI aim), documentation (missing certificates, expired calibration, drawings).

Categories

A: safety or operationally critical. B: important technical defect. C: minor, documentation or cosmetic. Follow the contract quality procedure.

NCR

Raise when an installed or tested item misses the approved requirement. Never alter a record to make it pass.

Retest

Repair → retest → record → close NCR. A replaced component is not a closed failure until retested.

Never hide a failure. FAIL → record actual value → find cause → rectify → retest → record new result → close.

Accept on the approved requirement, not "it looks okay": drawing, project specification, ITP, manufacturer manual, FAA specification, ICAO or national requirement. Practical hierarchy: law or regulator → airport requirement → project specification → drawing/ITP → manufacturer requirement → test procedure → measured result. Resolve conflicts formally.

A typical ITP defines H hold points, W witness points and R review, e.g. cable IR test (W/R), photometry (W/W), PAPI aiming (W/W), final integrated test (hold for client). The actual ITP governs. Keep raw instrument files, photometric data, ALCMS logs and calibration certificates, and make records traceable, dated, signed, linked to equipment and protected from alteration.

13

HANDOVER AND BASELINE

Final acceptance needs all tests done, failures rectified and retested, photometry and control accepted, documentation complete, punch list closed and airport approval. The handover dossier includes: as-built and circuit drawings, sign schedule, equipment datasheets and manuals, FAT, SAT, electrical, CCR, transformer, cable, photometry, PAPI, sign, ALCMS and standby-power reports, NCRs and punch-list closure, calibration certificates, O&M manuals, spares list (by installed equipment, criticality and manufacturer advice), training records, warranties and asset register. Redlines become final as-built drawings showing the actual installed system.

Commissioning baseline
  • Initial insulation resistance, CCR current, voltage and step values
  • Photometric output and fixture alignment
  • PAPI aiming and photometry
  • Sign illumination and electrical condition
  • ALCMS configuration, alarm list and communication status
  • Example: 600 MΩ at commissioning vs 60 MΩ later is visible immediately

Commissioning proves a new or modified system is correct; maintenance proves it stays correct. Train O&M staff (architecture, CCR, circuits, transformers, connectors, fixtures, PAPI, signs, ALCMS, fault-finding, safety) and operators (control, brightness, alarms, emergency operation, local/remote mode, escalation). Maintenance schedules follow the airport's approved program with manufacturer manuals and applicable ACs.

14

SAFETY, FOD AND WEATHER

IdentifyIsolateLockout / tagoutVerifyTestDischargeWork

Series circuits can carry hazardous voltages. Near an operational runway, coordinate with ATC, airport operations, airside safety and maintenance control, using work and vehicle permits, radio, FOD controls and the lighting isolation procedure; never assume a runway is unavailable because a test team is on it. Control tools and cable offcuts, remove packing and inspect the work area. Record weather (rain, moisture, temperature, fog, dust, lightning, wind) because it affects insulation readings, optical measurements and safety.

15

GOLDEN RULES AND MISTAKES

1

Never energise before pre-commissioning is complete.

2

Never rely on continuity alone.

3

Never use uncalibrated equipment for acceptance decisions.

4

Never insulation-test sensitive electronics without the approved procedure.

5

Never accept "light ON" as photometric compliance.

6

Never change PAPI aiming without the approved design.

7

Never repeatedly reset a tripped CCR without identifying the fault.

8

Record actual values, not assumed values; retest after every significant correction.

Final acceptance = technical result + documentation + operational approval.

Exam & Interview

EXAM / INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

What is AGL commissioning?

Verifying that an installed AGL system is safe, correctly installed, functional, optically compliant, correctly controlled, documented and ready for service.

What is FAT? SAT?

Factory Acceptance Test before shipment; Site Acceptance Test after installation at the airport.

Is FAT sufficient?

No. Site installation and system integration must also be verified.

What is the difference between testing and commissioning?

Testing checks individual parameters; commissioning proves the complete integrated system.

Why test before energising?

To find installation errors, opens, insulation problems and wrong connections before live operation.

Why is insulation testing important?

It verifies insulation integrity and finds leakage or fault conditions.

Is continuity testing enough?

No. A circuit can have continuity and poor insulation, moisture or high-resistance joints.

Why does insulation-resistance trending matter?

Moisture and ageing change values over time; a trend shows deterioration before failure.

What must you do after an insulation test before touching the leads?

Let the circuit discharge fully through the tester or an approved discharge method, verify it is safe, then disconnect. Follow AC 150/5340-26C and the instrument manual.

What secondary-power switch-over times does ICAO Annex 14 set?

Up to 15 s for most lights on CAT I runways. For CAT II/III, key lights such as the inner 300 m of the approach lighting, runway centreline, touchdown zone and stop bars need 1 s. Confirm in Annex 14 Table 8-1 (see Note 05).

Why is photometry required?

A light being on does not prove the optical output meets the requirement.

Why is calibration important?

It makes acceptance measurements traceable and reliable.

Why record actual values?

They are acceptance evidence and the future maintenance baseline.

What should you check if one light is off?

Fixture, secondary connector and cable, transformer, then primary connection.

What if many lights are off?

Control, CCR, primary circuit, primary connectors, transformer section.

CCR trips on energising: what do you check?

Fault indication, input, protection, primary circuit, ground or short fault, circuit loading, diagnostics.

Why test PAPI separately?

It needs specific geometry, aiming, transition and photometric performance.

Why test ALCMS after the field works?

A field circuit can work locally while the control-room command or indication path is wrong.

What is an NCR?

A non-conformance report raised when an item does not meet the approved requirement.

What is a commissioning baseline?

The verified initial condition used for future maintenance comparison.

What are H, W and R in an ITP?

Hold point, witness point and review.

Final Study Takeaway

FINAL STUDY TAKEAWAY

Successful commissioning is not proof that the lights switch on. It is documented evidence that the complete installed system performs to the approved design, applicable standards, equipment requirements and airport operational needs.

AGL commissioning = safety + installation + electrical + functional + control + monitoring + photometry + integration + documentation = ready for acceptance.

Agreed values and methods in advance prevent disputes and make results interpretable. Record who witnessed each test.
